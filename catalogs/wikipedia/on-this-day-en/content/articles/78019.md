The **Sea of Azov**[^cite_note-3] is an inland [shelf sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf#Shelf_seas) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) connected to the [Black Sea](3386.md) by the narrow (about 4 km (2.5 mi)) [Strait of Kerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Kerch), and sometimes regarded as a northern extension of the Black Sea.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The sea is bounded by Russia on the east, and by [Ukraine](31750.md) on the northwest and southwest (the parts of Ukraine bordering the sea have been [under Russian occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine) since 2022). It is an important access route for [Central Asia](6742.md), from the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) via the [Volga–Don Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal).

The sea is largely affected by the inflow of the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_\(river\)), [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_\(river\)), and other rivers, which bring sand, [silt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt), and shells, which in turn form numerous bays, [limans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman_\(landform\)), and narrow [spits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spit_\(landform\)). Because of these deposits, the sea bottom is relatively smooth and flat, with the depth gradually increasing toward the middle. Because of the river inflow, water in the sea has low [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) and a high amount of [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) (such as [green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_algae)) that affects the water colour. Abundant [plankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plankton) result in unusually high fish productivity. The sea shores and spits are low; they are rich in vegetation and bird colonies. The Sea of Azov is the shallowest sea in the world, with the depth varying between 0.9 and 14 metres (3 and 46 ft).[^cite_note-k65-1][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] There is a constant outflow of water from the Sea of Azov to the Black Sea.

## Names

The name is likely to derive from the settlement of an area around [Azov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov), whose name comes from the [Kipchak Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipchak_language) *asak* or *azaq* 'lowlands'.[^cite_note-10] A Russian [pseudo-etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_etymology), however, instead derives it from an [eponymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) [Cuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumans) prince named "Azum" or "Asuf", said to have been killed defending his town in 1067. A formerly common spelling of the name in English was the Sea of Azoff.[^cite_note-eb9-11]

In [antiquity](252905.md), the sea was known as the [Maeotis Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maeotis_Swamp) ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Μαιῶτις λίμνη,[^cite_note-12] *hē Maiōtis límnē*; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Palus Maeotis*),[^cite_note-13] from the marshlands to its northeast. It remains unclear whether it was named for the nearby [Maeotians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maeotians) or viceversa if that name was an [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym), broadly applied to various peoples who happened to live beside it.[^cite_note-digrog-14] Other names included Lake Maeotis or Maeotius (*_Mæotius_* or *_Mæotis Lacus_*);[^cite_note-15] the Maeotian or Maeotic Sea (*_Mæotium_* or *_Mæoticum Æquor_*);[^cite_note-16] the Cimmerian or Scythican Swamps (*_Cimmeriae_*[^cite_note-17] or *Scythicæ Paludes*);[^cite_note-ovy-18] and the Cimmerian or Bosporic Sea (*_Cimmericum_* or *_Bosporicum Mare_*).[^cite_note-19] The Maeotians themselves were said by [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) to call the sea Temarunda (alternative spellings Temarenda and Temerinda), meaning "Mother of Waters".[^cite_note-20]

The medieval Russians knew it as the Sea of Surozh after the adjacent city now known as [Sudak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudak).[^cite_note-k65-1][^cite_note-bse1-21] It was known in [Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language) as the *Balük-Denis* ("Fish Sea") from its high productivity.[^cite_note-eb9-11]

## History

### Prehistory

Main article: [Black Sea deluge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_deluge_theory)

There are traces of [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) settlement in the area now covered by the sea.

In 1997, William Ryan and [Walter Pitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Pitman_\(geologist\)) of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) published a theory that a massive flood through the [Bosporus](3705.md) occurred in ancient times. They claim that the Black and [Caspian Seas](19653787.md) were vast freshwater lakes, but in about 5600 BC the [Mediterranean](19006.md) spilled over a rocky [sill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sill_\(geology\)) at the Bosporus, creating the current link between the Black and Mediterranean Seas. Subsequent work has been done both to support and to discredit this theory, and archaeologists still debate it. This has led some to associate this catastrophe with prehistoric [flood myths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluge_\(mythology\)).[^cite_note-22]

### Antiquity

Main article: [Bosporan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporan_Kingdom)

The [Maeotian marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maeotian_Swamp) around the mouth of the [Tanais River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanais_River) (the present-day [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River,_Russia)) were famous in [antiquity](252905.md), as they served as an important check on the migration of nomadic people from the [Eurasian steppelands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_steppe). The [Maeotians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maeotians) themselves lived by fishing and farming, but were avid warriors able to defend themselves against invaders.[^cite_note-straystray-23] Misled by its strong currents,[^cite_note-eb9-11] [ancient geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_geography#Antiquity) had only a vague idea of the extent of the sea, whose fresh water caused them to typically label it a "swamp" or a "lake". [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) (5th century BC) judged it as large as the [Black Sea](3386.md), while the [Pseudo-Scylax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_Pseudo-Scylax) (4th century BC) thought it about half as large.[^cite_note-digrog-14]

It was long believed to provide direct communication with the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean).[^cite_note-eb9-11] [Polybius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polybius) (2nd century BC) confidently expected that the strait to the Sea of Azov would close in the near future, due to ongoing deposition of sediments from rivers.[^cite_note-24] In the 1st century, [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) reckoned the distance from the Cimmerian Bosporus (the [Strait of Kerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Kerch)) to the mouth of the Tanais at 2200 [stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_\(unit_of_length\)), a roughly correct figure,[^cite_note-26] but did not know that its width continuously narrows.[^cite_note-digrog-14]

[Milesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miletus) [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_colonization) began in the 7th century BC. The [Bosporan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporan_Kingdom) was named for the Cimmerian Bosporus rather than for the more famous [Bosporus](3705.md) at the other end of the [Black Sea](3386.md). Briefly annexed by [Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pontus) from the late 2nd century BC, it stretched along both southern shores of the Sea of Azov from the time of [Greek colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_colonization) to the end of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), serving as a [client kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_kingdom) which exported wheat, fish, and [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_antiquity) in exchange for Greek and Roman manufactures and luxuries. Its later history is uncertain but probably the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns), after defeating the [Alans people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans) who had settled in the region from central Asia, overran it in the late 4th century.[^cite_note-27]

### Azov campaigns of 1695–96 and 1736–37



Capture of Azov 1696, painting by [Robert Ker Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ker_Porter).

Main article: [Azov campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_campaigns)

The Sea of Azov was frequently the scene of military conflicts between Russia, pursuing naval expansion to the south, and the major power in the region, [Turkey](22278.md). During the [Russo-Turkish War (1686–1700)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1686%E2%80%931700\)), there were two campaigns in 1695–96 to capture the then Turkish fortress of Azov defended by a garrison of 7,000. The campaigns were headed by [Peter I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia) and aimed to gain Russian access to the Sea of Azov and Black Sea. The first campaign began in the spring of 1695. The Russian army consisted of 31 thousand men and 170 cannons and included selected trained regiments and [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks). It reached Azov on 27–28 June and besieged it by land by 5 July. After two unsuccessful assaults on 5 August and 25 September, the siege was lifted.[^cite_note-bse95-28]

The second campaign involved both ground forces and the Azov fleet, which was built in Moscow Oblast, Voronezh, Bryansk and other regions between winter 1695 and spring 1696. In April 1696, the army of 75,000 headed by [Aleksei Shein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Shein) moved to Azov by land and by ship via the Don River to Taganrog. In early May, they were joined by another fleet led by Peter I. On 27 May, the Russian fleet blocked Azov by sea. On 14 June, the Turkish fleet tried to break the blockade but, after losing two ships, retreated to the sea. After intensive bombardment of the fortress from land and sea, on 17 July the Russian army broke the defense lines and occupied parts of the wall. After heavy fighting, the garrison surrendered on 17 July. After the war, the Russian fleet base was moved to Taganrog and Azov, and 215 ships were built there between 1696 and 1711. In 1711, as a result of the [Russo-Turkish War (1710–1711)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1710%E2%80%931711\)) and the [Treaty of the Pruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Pruth), Azov was returned to Turkey and the Russian Azov fleet was destroyed.[^cite_note-bse95-28][^cite_note-bsefleet-29] The city was recaptured by Russia in 1737 during the [Russo-Austrian-Turkish War (1735–1739)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Austrian-Turkish_War_\(1735%E2%80%931739\)). However, as a result of the consequent [Treaty of Niš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ni%C5%A1_\(1739\)), Russia was not allowed to keep the fortress and military fleet.[^cite_note-30]

### Crimean War 1853–1856



Gravure showing the first attack on Taganrog.

Main articles: [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) and [Siege of Taganrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Taganrog)

Another major military campaign on the Sea of Azov took place during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) of 1853–56. A naval and ground campaign pitting the allied navies of [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and France against Russia took place between May and November 1855. The British and French forces besieged [Taganrog](1288065.md), aiming to disrupt Russian supplies to Crimea. Capturing Taganrog would also result in an attack on Rostov, which was a strategic city for Russian support of their Caucasian operations. On 12 May 1855, the allied forces easily captured Kerch and gained access to the Sea of Azov, and on 22 May they attacked Taganrog. The attack failed and was followed by a siege. Despite the vast superiority of the allied forces (about 16,000 soldiers against fewer than 2,000), the city withstood all attempts to capture it, which ended around August 1855 with the retreat of the allied army. Individual coastal attacks continued without success and ceased in October 1855.[^cite_note-31]

### 21st century

In December 2003, Ukraine and the Russian Federation agreed in a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_Between_the_Russian_Federation_and_Ukraine_on_Cooperation_in_the_Use_of_the_Sea_of_Azov_and_the_Kerch_Strait) to treat the sea and the Kerch Strait as shared internal waters.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In September 2018, Ukraine announced the intention to add [navy](1590554.md) ships and further ground forces along the coast of the Sea of Azov, with the ships based at [Berdiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdiansk). The military posturing was exacerbated following the construction of the [Crimean Bridge](22563317.md), which is too low to allow passage of [Panamax](504868.md) ships into Ukraine's port.[^cite_note-2018-09-24_stratfor-34] Late that September, two Ukrainian vessels departed from the [Black Sea](3386.md) port [Odesa](55545.md), passed under the Crimean Bridge, and arrived in [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol).[^cite_note-35] Tensions increased further after the [Kerch Strait incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait_incident) in November 2018, when Russia seized three Ukrainian Navy vessels attempting to enter the Sea of Azov.[^cite_note-36]

Control of the western shore of the Sea is vital to the economy of Ukraine but it is also of immense strategic importance to Russia, as a land route to Crimea as well as it is for passage by Russian marine traffic.[^cite_note-37]

On December 10, 2021, the Ukrainian Navy announced that Russia had blocked off nearly 70 percent of the Sea of Azov, issuing navigation warnings, ostensibly to conduct artillery fire exercises on the sea ..."near Mariupol, Berdyansk and Henichesk."[^cite_note-aggression-38] It raised apprehension regarding a potential Russian invasion since it had begun amassing tens of thousands of troops near the southeast Ukraine border and had begun a propaganda war against the Kyiv government.[^cite_note-aggression-38] The Russians seized three Ukrainian military vessel as the boats were trying to cross the strait, and captured 24 sailors who were finally released after months of negotiations.[^cite_note-aggression-38]

On February 24, 2022, Russian forces began shelling Mariupol at the start of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] By May, with the end of the [siege of Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mariupol), Russia fully captured the city and blocked off Ukraine's access to the sea by controlling the entire north [Pryazovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pryazovia).[^cite_note-41]

The [International Hydrographic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hydrographic_Organization) defines the limit of the Sea of Azov in the [Kertch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Kerch)  \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*] as "The limit of the [Black Sea](3386.md)", which is itself defined as "A line joining Cape Takil and Cape Panaghia (45°02'N)".[^cite_note-42]

The sea is considered an internal sea of Russia and Ukraine, and its use is governed by an agreement between these countries ratified in 2003.[^cite_note-43] The sea is 360 kilometres (220 mi) long and 180 kilometres (110 mi) wide and has an area of 39,000 square kilometres (15,000 sq mi); it is the smallest sea within the countries of the former Soviet Union.[^cite_note-kki-44] The main rivers flowing into it are the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River,_Russia) and [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_River); they ensure that the waters of the sea have comparatively low salinity and are almost fresh in places, and also bring in huge volumes of [silt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt) and sand. Accumulation of sand and shells results in a smooth and low coastline, as well as in numerous spits and sandbanks.[^cite_note-bse1-21]

The Sea of Azov is the shallowest sea in the world with an average depth of 7 metres (23 ft) and maximum depth of 14 metres (46 ft);[^cite_note-k65-1] in the bays, where silt has built up, the average depth is about 1 metre (3 ft). The sea bottom is also relatively flat with the depth gradually increasing from the coast to the centre.[^cite_note-seas-45] The Sea of Azov is an internal sea with passage to the Atlantic Ocean going through the Black, [Marmara](28863.md), [Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea) and Mediterranean seas. It is connected to the Black Sea by the Strait of Kerch, which at its narrowest has a width of 4 kilometres (2.5 mi) and a maximum depth of 15 metres (49 ft).[^cite_note-k65-1] The narrowness of the Kerch Strait limits the water exchange with the Black Sea. As a result, the salinity of the Sea of Azov is low; in the open sea it is 10–12 on the [Practical Salinity Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_Salinity_Unit) (PSS), about one third of the salinity of the oceans; it is even lower (2–7 (PSS)) in the Taganrog Bay at the northeast end of the Sea. The long-term variations of salinity on the PSS are low, and are mostly caused by changes in humidity and precipitation.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

Although more than 20 rivers flow into the sea, mostly from the north,[^cite_note-seas-45] two of them, the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)) and [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_River) rivers, account for more than 90% of water inflow. The contribution of the Don is about twice that of the Kuban.[^cite_note-seas-45] The Kuban delta is located at the southeast, on the east side of the Kerch Strait. It is over 100 km long and covers a vast flooded area with numerous channels. Because of the spread, the delta has low contrast in satellite images, and is hardly visible in the map. The Don flows from the north into the large [Taganrog Bay](3032433.md). The depth there varies between 2 and 9 metres, while the maximum depth is observed in the middle of the sea.[^cite_note-k66-48]

Typical values of the annual inflow and outflow of water to the sea, averaged over the period from 1923 to 1985, are as follows: river inflow 38.6 km<sup>3</sup>, precipitation 15.5 km<sup>3</sup>, evaporation 34.6 km<sup>3</sup>, inflow from the Black Sea 36–38 km<sup>3</sup>, outflow 53–55 km<sup>3</sup>.[^cite_note-k67-49] Thus, about 17 km<sup>3</sup> of fresh water is outflowing from the Azov Sea to the Black Sea.[^cite_note-bse1-21] The depth of Azov Sea is decreasing, mostly due to the river-induced deposits.[^cite_note-kki-44] Whereas the past hydrological expeditions recorded depths of up to 16 metres, more recent ones could not find places deeper than 13.5–14 metres.[^cite_note-kki-44] This might explain the variation in the maximum depths among different sources. The water level fluctuates by some 20 cm over the year due to the snow melts in spring.[^cite_note-k67-49]

The [Taman Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Peninsula) has about 25 [mud volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_volcano), most of which are active. Their eruptions are usually quiet, spilling out mud, and such gases as methane, carbon dioxide and [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide), but are sometimes violent and resemble regular volcanic eruptions. Some of those volcanoes are under water, near the shores of the peninsula. A major eruption on 6 September 1799, near Golubitskaya [stanitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanitsa), lasted about 2 hours and formed a mud island 100 metres in diameter and 2 metres in height; the island was then washed away by the sea. There were similar eruptions in 1862, 1906, 1924, 1950 and 1952.[^cite_note-kki-44]

The current vertical profile of the Sea of Azov exhibits oxygenated surface waters and [anoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoxic_waters) bottom waters, with the anoxic waters forming in a layer 0.5 to 4 metres (2–13 ft) in thickness. The occurrence of the anoxic layer is attributed to seasonal eutrophication events associated with increased sedimentary input from the Don and Kuban Rivers. This sedimentary input stimulates biotic activity in the surface layers, in which organisms photosynthesise under aerobic conditions. Once the organisms expire, the dead organic matter sinks to the bottom of the sea where bacteria and microorganisms, using all available oxygen, consume the organic matter, leading to anoxic conditions. Studies have shown that in the Sea of Azov, the exact vertical structure is dependent on wind strength and [sea surface temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature), but typically a 'stagnation zone' lies between the oxic and anoxic layers.[^cite_note-50]

## Coastal features and major population centres

See also: [Spits of the Sea of Azov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spits_of_the_Sea_of_Azov)



Major spits of the Sea of Azov: 1. Arabat 2. Fedotov 3. Obitochna 4. Berdyansk 5. Belosaraysk 6. Krivaya 7. Beglitsk 8. Glafirovsk (east) and Yeysk (west) 9. Dolgaya 10. Kamyshevatsk 11. Yasensk 12. Achuevsk 13. Chushka

Many rivers flowing into the Sea of Azov form bays, [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) and [limans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman_\(landform\)). The sand, silt and shells they bring are deposited in the areas of reduced flow, that is the sides of the bays, forming narrow sandbanks called [spits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spit_\(landform\)). Typical maximum depth in the bays and limans is a few metres. Because of shallow waters and abundant rivers, the spits are remarkably long and numerous in the sea – the [Arabat Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabat_Spit) stretches over 112 kilometres (70 mi) and is one of the world's longest spits; three other spits, Fedotov Spit, Achuevsk Spit and Obitochna Spit, are longer than 30 km. Most spits stretch from north to south and their shape can significantly change over just several years.[^cite_note-r2-51][^cite_note-b1-52]

A remarkable feature of the Sea of Azov is the large complex of shallow lagoons called [Syvash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syvash) or "Rotten Sea". Their typical depth is only 0.5–1 metres with a maximum of 3 metres. They cover an area of 2,560 square kilometres (990 sq mi) in the northeastern [Crimea](163045.md) which is separated from the sea by the Arabatsk Spit. North of the spit lies the city of [Henichesk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henichesk) (population 22,500) and south of it is the [Bay of Arabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Arabat).[^cite_note-arabat-53] Syvash accepts up to 1.5 km<sup>3</sup> of Azov water per year. Because of the lagoons' wide extent and shallowness, the water rapidly evaporates, resulting in the high salinity of 170 on the [practical salinity scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity). For this reason Syvash has long had an important salt-producing industry.[^cite_note-k66-48]



Population centres on the Sea of Azov

North of the Arabat Spit is the [Molochnyi Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molochnyi_Liman) with the associated Fedotov Spit (45 km long) which are formed by the [Molochna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molochna_River). Farther north, between the Fedotov Spit and Obytochna Spit (30 km long), lies Obytochny Bay. Further north, between Obytochna Spit and Berdyansk Spit (23 km long), is Berdyansk Bay with two cities, [Berdyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdyansk) (population 112,000) and [Primorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prymorsk) (population 13,900). Further north again lies Belosaraysk Bay with Belosaraysk Spit, formed by the river [Kalmius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmius). The major city in the area is [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol) (population 491,600). Then, approaching the [Taganrog Bay](3032433.md) and very close to [Taganrog](1288065.md), are the Mius Liman and Krivaya Spit formed by the [Mius River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mius_River).[^cite_note-b1-52]

With an area of about 5,600 square kilometres (2,200 sq mi), [Taganrog Bay](3032433.md) is the largest bay of the Sea of Azov. It is located in the north-eastern part of the Sea and is bounded by the Belosaraysk and [Dolgaya Spits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgaya_Spit). The [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)) flows into it from the north-east. On its shores stand the two principal cities of the Sea of Azov, [Taganrog](1288065.md) (population 257,600) and [Azov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov) (population 83,200). South-east of the bay is Yeysk Liman. It lies entirely on the continent, entering the Taganrog Bay through the Yeysk and Glafirovsk Spits, and is the mouth of the [Yeya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeya_River). Yeysk Spit is part of [Yeysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeysk) city, which has a population of 87,500. It extends into the prominent Yeysk peninsula, which is tipped in the north-west by the [Dolgaya Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgaya_Spit). South of it, also enclosed by the continent, lies Beisug Liman, which is restricted by the Yasensk Spit and is fed by the [Beysug River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beysug_River). South-west of the liman, the 31 km long Achuevsk Spit runs along the coastline. Between the Achuevsk spit and Beisug Liman stands [Primorsko-Akhtarsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsko-Akhtarsk) with 32,165 inhabitants.[^cite_note-r2-51][^cite_note-b1-52]



A spit in the Sea of Azov

In the south, the Sea of Azov is connected to the Black Sea via the [Strait of Kerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Kerch), which is bordered to the west by the Kerch peninsula of the [Crimea](163045.md) and to the east by the Russian Taman peninsula in [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md). The city of [Kerch](47638.md) (population 151,300) is located on the Kerch peninsula, and the Taman peninsula contains the delta of the [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_\(river\)), a major Russian river. The strait is 41 kilometres long and 4 to 15 kilometres wide. Its narrowest part lies on the Sea of Azov side, restricted by the [Chushka Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chushka_Spit) which faces southwards in consequence of the [outflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)) from the Azov to the Black Sea.[^cite_note-54]

The Strait of Kerch is spanned by the [Crimean Bridge](22563317.md), which was opened in May 2018. This is a major geopolitical issue since shipping vessels over a certain size can not pass under the span.[^cite_note-55] Since then Russia has been accused of interdicting shipping through the Kerch Strait.[^cite_note-56]

## Climate



Beach in [Shchyolkino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shcholkine) in [Crimea](163045.md)

The sea is relatively small and nearly surrounded by land. Therefore, its climate is continental with cold winters and hot and dry summers. In autumn and winter, the weather is affected by the [Siberian Anticyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_High) which brings cold and dry air from [Siberia](29639.md) with winds of 4–7 m/s, sometimes up to 15 m/s. Those winds may lower the winter temperatures from the usual −1 to −5 °C to below −30 °C. The mean mid-summer temperatures are 23–25 °C with a maximum of about 40 °C.[^cite_note-k66-48] Winds are weaker in summer, typically 3–5 m/s.[^cite_note-seas-45] [Precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) varies between 312 and 528 mm/year and is 1.5–2 times larger in summer than in winter.[^cite_note-bse1-21]

Average water temperatures are 0–1 °C in winter (2–3 °C in the Kerch Strait) and 24–25 °C in summer, with a maximum of about 28 °C on the [open sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_sea) and above 30 °C near the shores. During the summer, the sea surface is usually slightly warmer than the air.[^cite_note-seas-45] Because of the [shallow](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/shallow) character of the sea, the temperature usually lowers by only about 1 °C with depth, but in cold winters, the difference can reach 5–7 °C.[^cite_note-seas-45][^cite_note-k69-57]

The winds cause frequent storms, with the waves reaching 6 metres in the Taganrog Bay, 2–4 metres near the southern shores, and 1 metre in the Kerch Strait. In the open sea, their height is usually 1–2 metres, sometimes up to 3 metres. Winds also induce frequent [seiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiche) – [standing waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave) with an amplitude of 20–50 cm and lasting from minutes to hours. Another consequence of the winds is [water currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_current). The prevailing current is a [counterclockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterclockwise) [swirl](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/swirl) due to the westerly and south-westerly winds. Their speed is typically less than 10 cm/s, but can reach 60–70 cm/s for 15–20 m/s winds. In the bays, the flow is largely controlled by the inflow of the rivers and is directed away from the shore.[^cite_note-k67-49] In the Kerch Strait, the flow is normally toward the Black Sea due to the predominance of northern winds and the water inflow from the rivers; its average speed is 10–20 cm/s, reaching 30–40 cm in the narrowest parts.[^cite_note-k68-58] Although the sea is not subject to tidal variation, there are seasonal changes in the observed sea level of up to 10–20 inches (250–510 mm), caused by seasonal variations in river outflows.[^cite_note-59]



An [icebreaker](483060.md) on the Sea of Azov.

The shallowness and low [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) of the sea make it vulnerable to freezing during the winter. [Fast ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_ice) bands ranging from 7 km in the north to 1.5 km in the south can occur temporarily at any time from late December to mid-March. Several ships were trapped in ice in 2012 when it froze over.[^cite_note-brit12-60] The ice thickness reaches 30–40 centimetres (12–16 in) in most parts of the sea and 60–80 cm in the Taganrog Bay.[^cite_note-k68-58] The ice is often unstable and piles up to the height of several metres. Before the introduction of [icebreakers](483060.md), navigation was halted in the winter.[^cite_note-k69-57]

## Flora and fauna

Historically, the sea has had rich marine life, both in variety, with over 80 fish and 300 invertebrate species identified, and in numbers. Consequently, fishing has long been a major activity in the area. The annual catch of recent years was 300,000 tonnes, about half of which are valuable species (sturgeon, [pike-perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sander_\(fish\)), [bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bream), [sea-roach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_roach), etc.).[^cite_note-k76-61] This was partly due to extremely high biological productivity of the sea, which was stimulated by the strong supply of nutrients from numerous rivers feeding the sea, low water salinity, ample heating due to shallow waters and long vegetation period. However, diversity and numbers have been reduced by artificial reduction of river flow (construction of dams), over-fishing and water-intense large-scale cultivation of cotton, causing increasing levels of [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution). Fish hauls have rapidly decreased and in particular [anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchovy) fisheries have collapsed.[^cite_note-k65-1][^cite_note-k76-61][^cite_note-k86-62][^cite_note-63]

### Plankton and benthos



[Green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_algae) (and other plankton species) are mostly responsible for the colour of the Sea of Azov waters.

Because of the shallow waters, the development of aquatic life in the Sea of Azov is more characteristic of a lagoon, and the plankton patterns are rather similar in the open sea and near the shores. Despite its shallowness, the water has low transparency, so bottom plants are poorly developed and most algae are of planktonic type. The sea is characterised by high concentrations of organic matter and long blooming periods. Another specific feature of the sea is the variable salinity – low in the large bays and higher in the open sea, especially near the Kerch Strait. Therefore, the plankton species are distributed inhomogeneously in the Sea of Azov. Although many additional species are brought in from the saltier Black Sea, most of them cannot adjust to the variable salinity of the Sea of Azov, except for the [euryhaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euryhaline) species.[^cite_note-k77-64] About 600 species of planktonic algae are known in the Sea of Azov.[^cite_note-k76-61] The number of species is dominated by [diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom) and [green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_algae); [blue-green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria) and pyrophites are significant, and [euglena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euglena) and [yellow-green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow-green_algae) form only 5% of the species. Green algae are mostly responsible for the colour of the sea in the satellite images (see photos above).[^cite_note-k77-64]

Regarding [zooplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooplankton), the fresh waters of the Tanganrog Bay are inhabited by [cladocera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladocera), [copepoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copepod) and [rotifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotifer), such as *[Brachionus plicatilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachionus_plicatilis)*, *Keratella curdata* and *Asplanchna*. Western part of the sea, which is more saline, hosts three forms of *[Acartia clausi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acartia_clausi)*, as well as *Centropages ponticus*, [meroplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meroplankton) and larvae of [gastropoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropoda), [bivalvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivalvia) and [polychaete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete).[^cite_note-k78-65]

[Benthos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthos) species reside mostly at the sea bottom and include worms, [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean), bottom [protists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist), [coelenterata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coelenterata), and [mollusks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusca). Mollusks account for 60–98% of the invertebrate biomass at the Sea of Azov bottom.[^cite_note-k78-65]



[Sturgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon) are among the major and most valuable commercial fish species of the Sea of Azov.

[^cite_note-k76-61]

### Fish

There are 183 ichthyofauna species from 112 genera and 55 families in the Sea of Azov region. Among them, there are 50 rare and 19 endangered species, and the sturgeon *[Acipenser nudiventris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastard_sturgeon)* is probably extinct in the region.[^cite_note-k79-66]

The fauna of the freshwater Taganrog Bay is much poorer – it consists of 55 species from 36 genera and 16 families; among them, three species are rare and 6 are endangered.[^cite_note-k81-67]

### Flora



Lotus.

The shores of the Sea of Azov contain numerous [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) and marshes and are dominated by reeds, [sedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperaceae), *[Typha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typha)* and *[Sparganium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparganium)*. Typical submerged plants are [Charales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charales), [pond weed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potamogeton), [hornworts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceratophyllum) and [water lilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphaeaceae). Also common is [sacred lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelumbo).[^cite_note-kki-44] The number of species is large; for example, the Belosaraysk and Berdyansk spits alone contain more than 200 each. Some spits are declared national [nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve), such as Beglitsk,[^cite_note-68] Belosaraysk,[^cite_note-eco-69] Krivaya[^cite_note-eco-69] and Berdyansk Spits.[^cite_note-b1-52][^cite_note-osnova-70][^cite_note-berd-71]

### Fauna



[Great cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_cormorant) and seagulls on the Belosaraysk Spit.

Estuaries and spits of the sea are rich in birds, mostly waterfowl, such as wild geese, ducks and seagulls. Colonies of [cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_cormorant) and [pelicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelican) are common. Also frequently observed are swans, herons, [sandpipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpipers) and many birds of prey. Mammals include foxes, wild cats, hares, hedgehogs, [weasels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weasel), [martens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marten) and wild boar.[^cite_note-berd-71] [Muskrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskrat) were introduced to the area in the early 20th century and are hunted for their fur.[^cite_note-kki-44]

### Migrating and invading species



*[Rapana venosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veined_rapa_whelk)* from the Black Sea.

Some ichthyofauna species, such as [anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchovy), [garfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfish), Black Sea [whiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlangius_merlangus) and [pickerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esox), visit the Sea of Azov from the Black Sea for spawning. This was especially frequent in 1975–77 when the salinity of the southern Sea of Azov was unusually high, and additional species were seen such as [bluefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluefish), [turbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbot), chuco, [spurdog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spurdog), [Black Sea salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmo_labrax), [mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackerel) and even [corkwing wrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corkwing_wrasse), rock hopper, [bullhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_bullhead) and [eelpout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eelpout). Unlike the Black Sea plankton which does not adapt well to the low salinity of the Sea of Azov and concentrates near the Kerch Strait, fishes and invertebrates of the Black Sea adjust well. They are often stronger than the native species, are used to the relatively low temperatures of the Black Sea and survive winter in the Sea of Azov well.[^cite_note-k84-72]

*[Balanus improvisus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanus_improvisus)* is the first benthos species which spread from the Black Sea in the early 20th century and settled in the Sea of Azov. Its current density is 7 kg/m<sup>2</sup>. From 1956, *[Rapana venosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veined_rapa_whelk)* is observed in the Sea of Azov, but it could not adjust to low salinity and therefore is limited to the neighborhood of the Kerch Strait. Several Sea of Azov mollusks, such as [shipworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipworm) (*Teredo navalis*), [soft-shell clam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft-shell_clam) (*Mya arernaria*), [Mediterranean mussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_mussel) (*Mytilus galloprovincialis*) and *Anadara inaequivalvis*, originate from the Black Sea. Another example of invading species is the Dutch crab *[Rhithropanopeus harrisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhithropanopeus_harrisii)* which is observed both in saline and freshwater parts.[^cite_note-k84-72]

Formerly three types of dolphins, [short-beaked common dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-beaked_common_dolphin), [common bottlenose dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_bottlenose_dolphin) and [harbour porpoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_porpoise), regularly visited the Sea of Azov from the Black Sea although the common dolphin usually avoided the basin and Kerch Strait due to low salinity.[^cite_note-73] One sub-species of harbour porpoise, *[Phocoena phocoena relicta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phocoena_phocoena_relicta)*, used to live in the Sea of Azov and was therefore called "Azov dolphin" (азовка, azovka) in the Soviet Union. Nowadays, dolphins are rarely observed in the Sea of Azov. This is attributed to shallowing of the sea, increasing navigation activities, pollution, and reduction in the fish population.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

Various species of [pinnipeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped) and [belugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beluga_whale)[^cite_note-76] were introduced into Black Sea by mankind and later escaped either by accidental or purported causes. Of these, [grey seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_seal) has been recorded within Kerch Strait and Sea of Azov.[^cite_note-77] [Mediterranean monk seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_monk_seal) became extinct in the [Black Sea](3386.md) in 1997,[^cite_note-iucn-78] and the historic presence of large whales such as [minke whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minke_whale) in the Black Sea is recorded,[^cite_note-GreekCetacea-79][^cite_note-BlackSeaWhales-80] although it is unclear whether these mammals historically occurred in the Azov basin.

## Economy and ecology

For centuries, the Sea of Azov has been an important waterway for the transport of goods and passengers. The first modern ironworks in Imperial Russia were located upstream on the [Kalmius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmius) River at [Donetsk](562951.md), originally named *Hughesovka* ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Юзовка). It was also important for the transportation of [iron ores](145352.md) from the mines of the Kerch peninsula to the processing plant of Azovstal in [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol) (formerly Zhdanov), Ukraine; this activity stopped after the closure of the mines in the 1990s.[^cite_note-81] Navigation increased after the construction in 1952 of the [Volga–Don Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal) which connected the Sea of Azov with the [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) – the most important riverine transport route in the central Russia – thus connecting major cities such as Moscow, [Volgograd](32585.md) and [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan).[^cite_note-kki-44] Currently, the major ports are in [Taganrog](1288065.md), [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol), [Yeysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeysk) and [Berdyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdyansk).[^cite_note-bse1-21][^cite_note-brit-82]

Increasing navigation rates have resulted in more pollution and even in ecological disasters. On 11 November 2007, a strong storm sank four ships in the [Kerch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait), in the Russian [Port of Kavkaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Kavkaz). The ships were the Russian [bulk carriers](1324895.md) *Volnogorsk*; *Nakhichevan*; and *Kovel*, and the Georgian *Haji Izmail* with a Turkish crew. Six other ships were driven from their anchors and grounded. The tanker [*Volgoneft-139*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgoneft-139) was broken in two, and the tanker *Volgoneft-123* was damaged. As a result, about 1,300 tons of fuel oil and about 6,800 tons of sulfur entered the sea.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

Another traditional activity in the sea is fishing. The Sea of Azov used to be the most productive fishing area in the Soviet Union: typical annual fish catches of 300,000 tonnes converted to 80 kg per hectare of surface. (The corresponding numbers are 2 kg in the Black Sea and 0.5 kilograms (1.1 lb) in the Mediterranean Sea.) The catch has decreased in the 21st century, with more emphasis now on [fish farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farming), especially of [sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon).

Traditionally much of the coastline has been a zone of health resorts.[^cite_note-seas-45]

The irrigation system of the Taman Peninsula, supplied by the extended delta of the Kuban River, is favorable for agriculture and the region is famous for its vines. The area of the Syvash lagoons and Arabat Spit was traditionally a centre of a salt-producing industry. The Arabat Spit alone produced about 24,000 tonnes/year in the 19th century.[^cite_note-kki-44][^cite_note-arabat-53]
