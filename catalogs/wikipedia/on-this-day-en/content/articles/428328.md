The **United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit** (in [case citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_citation), **6th Cir.**) is a [federal court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_court) with [appellate jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_jurisdiction) over the [district courts](70235.md) in the following [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_judicial_district):

- [Eastern District of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Kentucky)

- [Western District of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Kentucky)

- [Eastern District of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Michigan)

- [Western District of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Michigan)

- [Northern District of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Northern_District_of_Ohio)

- [Southern District of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_Ohio)

- [Eastern District of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Tennessee)

- [Middle District of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Middle_District_of_Tennessee)

- [Western District of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Tennessee)

The court is composed of sixteen judges and is based at the [Potter Stewart U.S. Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter_Stewart_United_States_Courthouse) in [Cincinnati, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati). It is one of 13 [United States courts of appeals](70228.md).

The United States federal courts were divided into six circuits in 1801, but a circuit court of appeals was not established until the passage of the [Judiciary Act of 1891](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_of_1891).[^cite_note-1]

[William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft), the only person ever to serve as both [President](24113.md) and [Chief Justice of the United States](31739.md), once served on the Sixth Circuit. Four other judges of the Sixth Circuit have been elevated to serve on the Supreme Court, the last being [Potter Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter_Stewart) in 1958.

Chief judges have administrative responsibilities with respect to their circuits, and preside over any panel on which they serve, unless the circuit justice (the [Supreme Court](31737.md) justice responsible for the circuit) is also on the panel. Unlike the Supreme Court, where one justice is specifically nominated to be chief, the office of chief judge rotates among the circuit judges.&#x20;

To be chief, a judge must have been in active service on the court for at least one year, be under the age of 65, and have not previously served as chief judge. A vacancy is filled by the judge highest in seniority among the group of qualified judges, with seniority determined first by commission date, then by age. The chief judge serves for a term of seven years, or until age 70, whichever occurs first. If no judge qualifies to be chief, the youngest judge over the age of 65 who has served on the court for at least one year shall act as chief until another judge qualifies. If no judge has served on the court for more than a year, the most senior judge shall act as chief. Judges can forfeit or resign their chief judgeship or acting chief judgeship while retaining their active status as a circuit judge.[^cite_note-5]

When the office was created in 1948, the chief judge was the longest-serving judge who had not elected to retire, on what has since 1958 been known as [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status), or declined to serve as chief judge. After August 6, 1959, judges could not become or remain chief after turning 70 years old. The current rules have been in operation since October 1, 1982.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

## Succession of seats

The court has 16 seats for active judges, numbered in the order in which they were initially filled. Judges who assume [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status) enter a kind of retirement in which they remain on the bench but vacate their seats, thus allowing the [U.S. President](24113.md) to appoint new judges to fill their seats.
