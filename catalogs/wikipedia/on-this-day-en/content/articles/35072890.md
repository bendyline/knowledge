# Humanitarian corridor

For Italian refugee-aid partnership, see [Humanitarian Corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_Corridors).

A **humanitarian corridor** is a type of temporary [demilitarized zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarized_zone) intended to allow the safe transit of [humanitarian aid](434679.md) in, and/or [refugees](45547.md) out of a [crisis](1251700.md) region. Such a corridor can also be associated with a [no-fly zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-fly_zone) or [no-drive zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-drive_zone).[^cite_note-1]

Various types of "humanitarian corridors" have been proposed in the [post–Cold War era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93Cold_War_era), put forward either by one or more of the warring parties, or by the [international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_community) in the case of a [humanitarian intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_intervention). Humanitarian corridors were used frequently during the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md).

## United Nations Safe Areas

Main article: [United Nations Safe Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Safe_Areas)

[United Nations Safe Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Safe_Areas) (UN Safe Areas) were humanitarian corridors established in 1993 in the territory of [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) during the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina#Bosnian_War) by several resolutions of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md).
