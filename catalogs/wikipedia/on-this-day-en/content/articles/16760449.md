The **International Ice Hockey Federation** (**IIHF**; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Fédération internationale de hockey sur glace*; [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Internationale Eishockey-Föderation*) is a worldwide [governing body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_governing_body) for [ice hockey](14790.md).[^cite_note-1] It is based in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), Switzerland, and has 84 member countries.

The IIHF maintains the [IIHF World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Ranking) based on international ice hockey [tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournament). Rules of play for IIHF events differ from hockey in [North America](21139.md) and the rules of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL). Decisions of the IIHF can be [appealed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) through the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport) in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland. The IIHF maintains its own hall of fame for international ice hockey. The [IIHF Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Hall_of_Fame) was founded in 1997, and has been located within the [Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame) since 1998.

Previously, the IIHF also managed the development of [inline hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_in-line_hockey), but in June 2019, the IIHF announced that they would no longer govern inline hockey or organize the [Inline Hockey World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Inline_Hockey_World_Championship).[^cite_note-2]

## Functions



IIHF Headquarters in Zurich (Villa Freigut, [Enge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enge_\(Z%C3%BCrich\))).

The main functions of the IIHF are to govern, develop and organize hockey throughout the world. Another duty is to promote friendly relations among the member national associations and to operate in an organized manner for the good order of the sport.[^cite_note-3] The federation may take the necessary measures in order to conduct itself and its affairs in accordance with its [statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute), [bylaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bylaw) and regulations as well as in holding a clear jurisdiction with regards to ice hockey at the international level. The IIHF is the body responsible with arranging the [sponsorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponsor_\(commercial\)), license rights, advertising and merchandising in connection with all IIHF competitions.

Another purpose of the federation is to provide aid in the young players' development and in the development of [coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(ice_hockey\)) and [game officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_\(ice_hockey\)). On the other hand, all the events of IIHF are organized by the federation along with establishing and maintaining contact with any other sport federations or sport groups. The IIHF is responsible for processing the international players' transfers. It is also the body that presides over [ice hockey at the Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_Olympic_Games) as well as over all levels of the [IIHF World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Hockey_World_Championships).[^cite_note-4] The federation works in collaboration with local committees when organizing its 25 World Championships, at five different categories.

The IIHF is also responsible for the organization of European club competitions such as the [Champions Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champions_Hockey_League) or the [Continental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Continental_Cup).

The federation is governed by the legislative body of the IIHF which is the General Congress along with the executive body, which is the council. The Congress is entitled to make decisions with regard to the game's rules, the statutes and bylaws in the name of the federation. It is also the body that elects the president and the council or otherwise known as board.[^cite_note-5] The president represents the IIHF's interests in external matters and enforces the federation's statutes and regulations. The president is assisted by the general secretary, the highest-ranked IIHF employee.

## History

Main article: [History of the International Ice Hockey Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_International_Ice_Hockey_Federation)

The International Ice Hockey Federation was founded on 15 May 1908 at 34 [Rue de Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_de_Provence) in Paris, France, as Ligue Internationale de Hockey sur Glace (LIHG).[^cite_note-6] The [1920 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) were the first to integrate hockey into their program.



The [Poland men's national ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) debuted at [the 1928 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1928_Winter_Olympics).

The [1928 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1928_Winter_Olympics), which also served as the World and European Championship for the year, saw a record 11 countries participate.[^cite_note-IIHF1914-7]

The [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956) which had caused [Hungary](13275.md) to be occupied by the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Army), led to a boycott of the [1957 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_World_Ice_Hockey_Championships), which were being staged in [Moscow](19004.md). Canada and the United States led the boycott, and were joined by [Norway](21241.md), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), [Italy](14532.md), and [Switzerland](26748.md).[^cite_note-IIHF1957-8]

The [1962 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_World_Ice_Hockey_Championships), hosted by the American cities of [Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado) and [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), was boycotted by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), which led to a further boycott by the other [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries. At issue was the boycott of the 1957 championships in Moscow by Canada and the United States, and the Americans refusal of East German passports in reaction to the building of the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall) by [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany).[^cite_note-IIHF1975-9]

For the 1965–66 season, the IIHF created the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_European_Cup), a tournament consisting of the top club teams from around Europe. The competition was originated by [Günther Sabetzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCnther_Sabetzki), based on the [Association football](10568.md) European Cup (now [UEFA Champions League](44220.md)). In 1968 the IIHF organized the [European U19 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_European_Junior_Championships), a junior competition for players aged 19 and under. The age limit was later reduced to 18 in 1977.[^cite_note-IIHF1957-8]

During the 1980s Canada stopped boycotting the World Championships and Olympic Games. The Canadians had boycotted these tournaments between 1970 and 1976 after the IIHF had refused to allow them to roster professional players at the World Championships from NHL teams that had not qualified for the [Stanley Cup playoffs](10490910.md). President Sabetzki found a compromise that resulted in the return of Canada to international events beginning in 1977. The pro players whose teams had been eliminated from the playoffs were allowed to compete and in exchange, Canada agreed to participate in the World Championships. They also waived their right to host any World Championships. The creation of the [Canada Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Cup) (a competition organized by the NHL in Canada every four years) was also part of the new agreement between the IIHF and North American professional hockey.[^cite_note-IIHF1975-9]

The IIHF continued to grow in numbers during the 1980s and 1990s, both due to political events and the continued growth of hockey worldwide. The [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) saw its membership transferred to [Russia](25391.md), and the addition of four ex-[Soviet republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union); [Azerbaijan](746.md), [Belarus](3457.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md) to the federation. In addition, the memberships of [Estonia](28222445.md), [Latvia](17514.md), and [Lithuania](17675.md) - all of which had initially joined the IIHF in the 1930s but were expelled following their annexation by the Soviet Union - were renewed. The [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia) also resulted in an increase in membership. [Croatia](5573.md) and [Slovenia](27338.md) joined as new members, while the membership of the old [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) was transferred to [FR Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro) (which later became known as Serbia and Montenegro and still later dissolved into the independent republics of [Serbia](29265.md) and [Montenegro](20760.md)). When [Czechoslovakia broke up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia), its membership rights were transferred to the [Czech Republic](5321.md), and [Slovakia](26830.md) was admitted as a new member. The influx of new members resulted in the IIHF increasing the size of the Group A tournament. It expanded from 8 teams to 12 in 1992 and from 12 to 16 in 1998.[^cite_note-IIHF1990-10]

The IIHF celebrated its 100th anniversary in 2008. As part of the celebrations, the [2008 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_IIHF_World_Championship) was held in Canada for the first time (the tournament was co-hosted by the cities of [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Regional_Municipality) and [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City)).[^cite_note-Timeline-11]

The number of members grew in the 21st century: [Chile](5489.md) (2000), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) (2001), [Liechtenstein](17810.md) (2001), [North Macedonia](23564616.md) (2001), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (2001), [Macau](19068.md) (2005), [Malaysia](3607937.md) (2006), [Moldova](19260.md) (2008; presumably expelled from IIHF membership in 2023), [Georgia](48768.md) (2009), Kuwait (2009; had originally joined in 1985, but was expelled in 1992), [Morocco](19291.md) (2010), [Kyrgyzstan](170131.md) (2011), [Jamaica](15660.md) (2012), [Qatar](8238258.md) (2012), [Oman](22316.md) (2014), [Turkmenistan](198149.md) (2015), [Indonesia](14579.md) (2016), [Nepal](171166.md) (2016), the [Philippines](23440.md) (2016), [Algeria](358.md) (2019), [Colombia](5222.md) (2019), [Iran](14653.md) (2019), [Lebanon](17771.md) (2019), [Uzbekistan](31853.md) (2019), [Tunisia](30188.md) (2021), [Puerto Rico](23041.md) (2022), [Bahrain](18933277.md) (2024), [Kenya](188171.md) (2024).[^cite_note-IIHF1990-10]

The IIHF received international criticism for holding the [2014 Men's Ice Hockey World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Men's_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) in [Belarus](3457.md), because of the poor [human rights](13831.md) record of the country. Several human rights organisations launched the "Don't play with the dictator!" boycott campaign[^cite_note-12] and there were appeals from the [US Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Congress), the [German Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Parliament), and the [European Parliament](9581.md).[^cite_note-13]

The IIHF again received criticism for planning to partly hold the [2021 Men's Ice Hockey World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Men's_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) in Belarus.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In January 2021, the IIHF withdrew the 2021 World Championship from [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) due to safety and security issues during the political unrest, besides the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and decided to solely hold the tournament in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), Latvia.[^cite_note-16]

On 23 May 2021, civilian [Ryanair Flight 4978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryanair_Flight_4978), which was en route from Athens to Vilnius, was forced to land in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) and a passenger of that flight was detained. In protest, Latvian officials replaced the [Belarusian state flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Belarus) in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) with the former flag associated with the Belarusian opposition groups, including at the 2021 IIHF World Championship display of flags. This was by order of Mayor of Riga [Mārtiņš Staķis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81rti%C5%86%C5%A1_Sta%C4%B7is) and Minister of Foreign Affairs of Latvia [Edgars Rinkēvičs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgars_Rink%C4%93vi%C4%8Ds). The IIHF issued a statement protesting the replacement of the flag, and IIHF president [René Fasel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Fasel) asked the mayor to remove the IIHF name, its flag and its symbols from such sites, or to restore the flag, insisting that the IIHF is an "apolitical sports organization".[^cite_note-17] In response, Staķis said he would remove the IIHF flags.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

On 28 February 2022, the IIHF suspended the memberships of the Russian and Belarusian ice hockey federations until further notice due to [the countries' invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-20]

Still, non-Russian players in Russian clubs are according to IIHF rules bound by their contracts, and cannot leave their clubs and Russia until their contracts expire or are terminated by their club. If players leave anyway they can be sued and would be blocked from playing for other clubs.[^cite_note-21]

On 22 March 2023, the IIHF excluded Russian and Belarusian national and club teams from IIHF competitions during the 2023–24 season, based on safety considerations.[^cite_note-22]

## Honors and awards

The IIHF awards ceremony is held annually on the second last day of each Ice Hockey World Championship, and its hall of fame induction ceremony on the final day of the championships. Prior to 2024, all awards were presented during one ceremony on the final day of the championships.[^cite_note-Aykroyd_25_May_2024-23]

### Hall of Fame



The Hockey Hall of Fame in Toronto has hosted the IIHF Hall of Fame since 1998.

Main articles: [IIHF Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Hall_of_Fame), [Torriani Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torriani_Award), and [List of members of the IIHF Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_IIHF_Hall_of_Fame)

Prior to the establishment of the IIHF Hall of Fame, the IIHF displayed a collection of historical artifacts from World Championships and the Olympic Games in temporary exhibits. From 1992 to 1997, the IIHF loaned its exhibits to the [International Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Hockey_Hall_of_Fame) in [Kingston, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario), Canada.[^cite_note-hockey-archives-24]

The first step taken by the IIHF to create its own hall of fame was a proposal made in 1996, which was later ratified at the 1997 IIHF summer congress to host the museum in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich).[^cite_note-hockey-archives-24] The approval came exactly 89 years from the foundation of the IIHF, with the purpose of honoring former international [ice hockey](14790.md) players, builders (administrators) and officials.[^cite_note-iihf-archives-25] The annual induction ceremony takes place on the medal presentation day of the Ice Hockey World Championships.[^cite_note-hockey-archives-24][^cite_note-iihf-archives-25] The IIHF agreed with the [National Hockey League](21809.md) to transfer its exhibits to the [Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame) in [Toronto](64646.md), Canada, as of 29 July 1998.[^cite_note-hockey-archives-24]

In 2015, the IIHF created the Torriani Award for "players with an outstanding career from non-top hockey nations". The award was named for [Bibi Torriani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibi_Torriani), who played internationally for the [Switzerland men's national ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_men's_national_ice_hockey_team).[^cite_note-26] The IIHF includes the recipients of the Torriani Award in the list of Hall of Fame inductees.[^cite_note-27]

### Paul Loicq Award

Main article: [Paul Loicq Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Loicq_Award)

The Paul Loicq Award was established in 1998.[^cite_note-IIHF-awards-overview-28] It is presented annually to honor a person who has made "outstanding contributions to the IIHF and international ice hockey".[^cite_note-29] Named after [Paul Loicq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Loicq), who was president of the IIHF from 1922 until 1947, it is the highest personal recognition given by the IIHF.[^cite_note-30]

### Centennial All-Star Team

Main article: [IIHF Centennial All-Star Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Centennial_All-Star_Team)

The IIHF Centennial All-Star Team is an all-star team of hockey players from international tournaments. The team was chosen based on the players' "impact in international ice hockey over a period of at least a decade," with a requirement that they must have performed "at the highest possible level (Olympics, the IIHF World Championship or the Canada Cup/World Cup tournaments)." The selection was named in 2008. All six players were already members of the IIHF Hall of Fame.[^cite_note-31]

### Milestone Award

### All-Time Teams

Main article: [IIHF All-Time Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_All-Time_Teams)

The IIHF chose all-time teams for the 16 countries that would have participated at the [2020 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_IIHF_World_Championship), to honor the 100-year anniversary of the Ice Hockey World Championships.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

### Player of the year awards

Main articles: [IIHF Male Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Male_Player_of_the_Year) and [IIHF Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Female_Player_of_the_Year)

The IIHF established male and female player of the year awards in 2023, to be given annually in recognition of the player who "best exemplifies exceptional skill, determination, team success, and sporting character on and off the ice during the preceding season".[^cite_note-eligibility-42] It is selected by a panel of media and representatives drawn from IIHF member states.[^cite_note-43] To be eligible, a player must have competed in at least one of four IIHF tournaments (the [Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_Winter_Olympics), [IIHF World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Championships), [IIHF World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Junior_Championships), or [IIHF World U18 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_U18_Championships)) as well as in a national domestic league "of the highest caliber for that country," with "the combined performances of which were deemed superior to all other players".[^cite_note-eligibility-42]

### Johan Bollue Award

The Johan Bollue Award was established in 2023, and named for Johan Bollue (1964–2021) who served as the sports development director for the [Royal Belgian Ice Hockey Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Belgian_Ice_Hockey_Federation), and was an organizer at [Youth Olympic Games](11315968.md), and a mentor coach at IIHF development camps.[^cite_note-Aykroyd_25_May_2024-23][^cite_note-44] The award is given to an individual or a group who have made significant contributions to growth and development in [youth ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_ice_hockey).[^cite_note-45] Recipients of the award include [Markus Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Graf) in 2024,[^cite_note-Aykroyd_25_May_2024-23] [Jim Aldred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Aldred) in 2025,[^cite_note-2025-IIHF-contributors-38] and [Aleksandrs Cicurskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandrs_Cicurskis?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 2026.[^cite_note-IIHF-contributors-2026-35]

### Media Award

The IIHF Media Award was established in 2024, given to an individual who made outstanding contributions to international hockey through television, print, and radio.[^cite_note-IIHF-awards-overview-28] The inaugural honoree was [Al Michaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Michaels), whose call of "Do you believe in Miracles?" described the [Miracle on Ice](295721.md) victory by the [United States men's national team](945923.md) in [ice hockey at the 1980 Winter Olympics](1400841.md).[^cite_note-Podnieks_15_January_2024-33] In 2025, [Paul Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_\(television_producer\)) was recognized for a career covering IIHF events with [The Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network).[^cite_note-2025-IIHF-contributors-38] The third recipient was Czech journalist [Pavel Barta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Barta?action=edit\&redlink=1), honored in 2026.[^cite_note-IIHF-contributors-2026-35]

## IIHF World Ranking

The IIHF World Ranking is a tool to reflect the long-term quality of the countries' national team program.[^cite_note-58] The IIHF World Ranking is released following each IIHF Ice Hockey World Championship and the Olympic Ice Hockey Tournament.
