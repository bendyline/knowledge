***Hayabusa2*** ([Japanese](15606.md): はやぶさ2; lit. '[Peregrine falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon) 2') is an [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) [sample-return mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample-return_mission) operated by the Japanese state space agency [JAXA](716184.md). It is a successor to the *[Hayabusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayabusa)* mission, which returned asteroid samples for the first time in June 2010.[^cite_note-Zukerman_2010-10] *Hayabusa2* was launched on 3 December 2014 and [rendezvoused in space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_rendezvous) with [near-Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object) asteroid [162173 Ryugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/162173_Ryugu) on 27 June 2018.[^cite_note-arrival-11] It surveyed the asteroid for a year and a half and took samples. It left the asteroid in November 2019 and returned the samples to Earth on 5 December 2020 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time).[^cite_note-Departure-8][^cite_note-NYT-20201205-12][^cite_note-BBC-20201206-13] Its mission was extended through at least 2031, during which it flew by the asteroid [98943 Torifune](46319186.md) in July 2026, and has a planned rendezvous with the small, rapidly rotating asteroid [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26) in July 2031.

*Hayabusa2* carries multiple science payloads for remote sensing and sampling, and four small rovers to investigate the asteroid surface and analyze the environmental and geological context of the samples collected.

Asteroid [162173 Ryugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/162173_Ryugu) (formerly designated 1999 JU<sub>3</sub>) is a primitive [carbonaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-type_asteroid) [near-Earth asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object). Carbonaceous asteroids are thought to preserve the most pristine, untainted materials in the [Solar System](26903.md), a mixture of minerals, ice, and [organic compounds](22203.md) that interact with each other.[^cite_note-Tachibana_2014-14] Studying it is expected to provide additional knowledge on the origin and evolution of the inner planets and, in particular, the origin of water and organic compounds on [Earth](9228.md),[^cite_note-Tachibana_2014-14][^cite_note-Tsuda_2013-15] all relevant to the [origin of life](19179706.md) on Earth.[^cite_note-newscientist-20100818-16]

Initially, launch was planned for 30 November 2014,[^cite_note-jaxa-report-20140521-17][^cite_note-ryChl-18][^cite_note-yoshikawa2011-19] but was delayed to 3 December 2014 at 04\:22\:04 UTC (3 December 2014, 13\:22\:04 local time) on a [H-IIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-IIA) launch vehicle.[^cite_note-sfn-20141203-20] *Hayabusa2* launched together with [PROCYON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROCYON) asteroid flyby space probe. PROCYON's mission was a failure. *Hayabusa2* arrived at Ryugu on 27 June 2018,[^cite_note-arrival-11] where it surveyed the asteroid for a year and a half and collected samples.[^cite_note-Tachibana_2014-14] It departed the asteroid in November 2019 and returned the samples to Earth in December 2020.[^cite_note-yoshikawa2011-19]

Compared to the previous *[Hayabusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayabusa)* mission, the spacecraft features improved [ion engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_thruster), guidance and navigation technology, antennas, and [attitude control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft_attitude_control) systems.[^cite_note-sfn-20120129-21] A kinetic penetrator (a high-explosive shaped charge) was shot into the asteroid surface to expose pristine sample material which was later collected for return to Earth.[^cite_note-Tsuda_2013-15][^cite_note-yoshikawa2011-19]

## Funding and history

Following the initial success of *Hayabusa*, JAXA began studying a potential successor mission in 2007.[^cite_note-OKEup-22] In July 2009, Makoto Yoshikawa of JAXA presented a proposal titled "Hayabusa Follow-on Asteroid Sample Return Missions". In August 2010, JAXA obtained approval from the Japanese government to begin development of *Hayabusa2*. The cost of the project estimated in 2010 was 16.4 billion yen ([US\$](18717338.md)149 million).[^cite_note-Zukerman_2010-10][^cite_note-sfn-20100811-23]

*Hayabusa2* was launched on 3 December 2014, arrived at asteroid Ryugu on 27 June 2018, and remained stationary at a distance of about 20 km (12 mi) to study and map the asteroid. In the week of 16 July 2018, commands were sent to move to a lower hovering altitude.[^cite_note-jaxa-20180725-24]

On 21 September 2018, the *Hayabusa2* spacecraft ejected the first two rovers, Rover-1A (HIBOU)[^cite_note-WAZzh-25] and Rover-1B (OWL), from about a 55 m (180 ft) altitude that dropped independently to the surface of the asteroid.[^cite_note-Rincon_20Sep-26][^cite_note-First_Drop-27] They functioned nominally and transmitted data.[^cite_note-space20180922-28] The MASCOT rover deployed successfully on 3 October 2018 and operated for about 16 hours as planned.[^cite_note-kGvEA-29]

The first sample collection was scheduled to start in late October 2018, but the rovers encountered a landscape with large and small boulders but no surface soil for sampling. Therefore, it was decided to postpone the sample collection plans to 2019 and further evaluate various options for the landing.[^cite_note-sampling_postponed-30][^cite_note-Mynavi190109-31] The first surface sample retrieval took place on 21 February 2019. On 5 April 2019, *Hayabusa2* released an impactor to create an artificial crater on the asteroid surface. However, *Hayabusa2* initially failed on 14 May 2019 to drop reflective markers necessary onto the surface for guiding the descent and sampling processes,[^cite_note-Marker-32] but later it successfully dropped one from an altitude of 9 m (30 ft) on 4 June 2019.[^cite_note-Ryan_June5-33] The sub-surface sampling took place on 11 July 2019.[^cite_note-Kyoto_July_2019-34] The spacecraft departed the asteroid on 13 November 2019 (with departure command sent at 01\:05 UTC on 13 November 2019). It successfully delivered the samples back to Earth on 6 December 2020 ([JST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Standard_Time)), dropping the contents by parachute in a special container at a location in [southern Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Australia). The samples were retrieved the same day for secure transport back to the JAXA labs in Japan.[^cite_note-Departure-8][^cite_note-1RTmx-35][^cite_note-2Y9yD-36]

After *Hayabusa2* completed its primary mission, its mission was extended, and it continued orbiting the Sun using its remaining propellant so that it could visit more asteroids along its flight path. The probe successfully flew-by asteroid [98943 Torifune](46319186.md) in July 2026, and it will flyby the micro-asteroid [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26) in July 2031.

The design of *Hayabusa2* is based on the first *Hayabusa* spacecraft, with some improvements.[^cite_note-Tachibana_2014-14][^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] It has a mass of 600 kilograms (1,300 lb) including fuel,[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] and electric power is generated by two sets of [solar arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panels_on_spacecraft) with an output of 2.6 kW at 1 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit), and 1.4 kW at 1.4 AU.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] The power is stored in eleven inline-mounted 13.2 Ah [lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

Propulsion

The spacecraft features four solar-electric ion thrusters for propulsion called μ10,[^cite_note-Kazutaka_2017-37] one of which is a backup. These engines use [microwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) to convert [xenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon) into [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_\(physics\)) ([ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion)), which are accelerated by a voltage applied by the [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel) and ejected out the back of the engine. The simultaneous operation of three engines generates thrusts of up to 28 mN.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] Although this thrust is very small, the engines are also extremely efficient; the 66 kg (146 lb) of [xenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon)[^cite_note-Kazutaka_2017-37] [reaction mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_mass) can [change the speed of the spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta-v) by up to 2 km/s.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

The spacecraft has four redundant [reaction wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_wheel) and a chemical [reaction control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_control_system) featuring twelve thrusters for [attitude control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft_attitude_control) (orientation) and orbital control at the asteroid.[^cite_note-Kazutaka_2017-37][^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] The chemical thrusters use [hydrazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrazine) and [MON-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_oxides_of_nitrogen), with a total mass of 48 kg (106 lb) of chemical propellant.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

Communication

The primary contractor [NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEC) built the 590 kg (1,300 lb) spacecraft, its [Ka-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka_band) communications system and a [mid-infrared camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermographic_camera).[^cite_note-sfn-20120129-21] The spacecraft has two [high-gain directional antennas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directional_antenna) for [X-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_band) and [K<sub>a</sub>-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka_band).[^cite_note-Kazutaka_2017-37] Bit rates are 8 bit/s to 32 kbit/s.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] The ground stations are the [Usuda Deep Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuda_Deep_Space_Center), [Uchinoura Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uchinoura_Space_Center), [NASA Deep Space Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Deep_Space_Network) and [Malargüe Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malarg%C3%BCe_Station) ([ESA](10363.md)).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

Navigation

The optical navigation camera telescope (ONC-T) is a telescopic framing camera with seven colors to optically navigate the spacecraft.[^cite_note-641rx-40] It works in synergy with the optical navigation camera wide-field (ONC-W2) and with two [star trackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_tracker).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

In order to descend to the asteroid surface to perform sampling, the spacecraft released one of five target markers in the selected landing zones as artificial guide marks, with highly reflective outer material that is recognized by a strobe light mounted on the spacecraft.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39] The spacecraft also used its laser altimeter and ranging ([LIDAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar)) as well as Ground Control Point Navigation (GCP-NAV) sensors during sampling.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

## Firsts

The *Hayabusa2* spacecraft was the first to deploy operating rovers on an asteroid.

## Science payload



*Hayabusa2* instrument inventory

The *Hayabusa2* payload is equipped with multiple scientific instruments:[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39][^cite_note-jaxa20180614-41]

- [Remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing): Optical Navigation Camera (ONC-T, ONC-W1, ONC-W2), Near-Infrared Camera (NIR3), Thermal-Infrared Camera (TIR), Light Detection And Ranging (LIDAR)

- Sampling: Sampling device (SMP), Small Carry-on Impactor (SCI), Deployable Camera (DCAM3)

- Four rovers: Mobile Asteroid Surface Scout (MASCOT), Rover-1A, Rover-1B, Rover-2.

### Remote sensing

The Optical Navigation Cameras (ONCs) were used for spacecraft navigation during the asteroid approach and proximity operations. They also remotely imaged the surface to search for [interplanetary dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_dust) around the asteroid. ONC-T is a telephoto camera with a 6.35° × 6.35° field of view and several [optical filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_filter) carried in a carousel. ONC-W1 and ONC-W2 are wide angle (65.24° × 65.24°) [panchromatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchromatic) (485–655 nm) cameras with [nadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadir) and oblique views, respectively.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

The Near-Infrared Spectrometer (NIRS3) is a [spectrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrograph) operating at a wavelength of 1.8–3.2 μm. NIRS3 was used for analysis of surface mineral composition.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

The Thermal-Infrared Imager (TIR) is a [thermal infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_infrared) camera working at 8–12 μm, using a two-dimensional [microbolometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbolometer) array. Its spatial resolution is 20 m at 20 km distance or 5 cm at 50 m distance (70 ft at 12 mi, or 2 in at 160 ft). It was used to determine surface temperatures in the range −40 to 150 °C (−40 to 302 °F).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

The Light Detection And Ranging ([LIDAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar)) instrument measured the distance from the spacecraft to the asteroid surface by measuring the reflected laser light. It operated over an altitude range between 30 m and 25 km (100 ft and 16 mi).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

When the spacecraft was closer to the surface than 30 m (98 ft) during the sampling operation, the Laser Range Finders (LRF-S1, LRF-S3) were used to measure the distance and the attitude (orientation) of the spacecraft relative to the terrain.[^cite_note-Oabul-42][^cite_note-zEAt1-43] The LRF-S2 monitored the sampling horn to trigger the sampling projectile.

LIDAR and ONC data are being combined to determine the detailed [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) (dimensions and shape) of the asteroid. Monitoring of a radio signal from Earth allowed measurement of the asteroid's [gravitational field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_field).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

## Rovers

*Hayabusa2* carried four small rovers to explore the asteroid surface *[in situ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_situ)*,[^cite_note-Keane-44] and provide context information for the returned samples. Due to the minimal gravity of the asteroid, all four rovers were designed to move around by short hops instead of using normal wheels. They were deployed at different dates from about 60 m (200 ft) altitude and fell freely to the surface under the asteroid's weak gravity.[^cite_note-okada2017-45] The first two rovers, called HIBOU (previously Rover-1A) and OWL (previously Rover-1B), landed on asteroid Ryugu on 21 September 2018.[^cite_note-space20180922-28] The third rover, called MASCOT, was deployed 3 October 2018. Its mission was successful.[^cite_note-plansoc-20181005-46] The fourth rover, known as Rover-2 or [MINERVA-II-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MINERVA_\(spacecraft\)), failed before release from the orbiter. It was released on 2 October 2019 to orbit the asteroid and perform gravitational measurements before being allowed to impact the asteroid a few days later.

### MINERVA-II

Main article: [MINERVA-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MINERVA-II)



The first photograph from the surface of an asteroid, taken by *HIBOU* on 22 September 2018 during one of its "hops".



Model of MINERVA-II-1 Rover 1B

MINERVA-II is a successor to the [MINERVA lander carried by *Hayabusa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayabusa#MINERVA_mini-lander). It consists of two containers with 3 rovers.

MINERVA-II-1 is a container that deployed two rovers, **Rover-1A** (**HIBOU**) and **Rover-1B** (**OWL**), on 21 September 2018.[^cite_note-MINERVA-II-1_Landings-47][^cite_note-jaxa-20181213-48] It was developed by JAXA and the [University of Aizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aizu). The rovers are identical having a cylindrical shape, 18 cm (7.1 in) diameter and 7 cm (2.8 in) tall, and a mass of 1.1 kg (2.4 lb) each.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39][^cite_note-otsuka20160328-49] They move by hopping in the low gravitational field, using a [torque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torque) generated by rotating masses within the rovers.[^cite_note-VOjg4-50] Their scientific payload is a [stereo camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereo_camera), [wide-angle camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-angle_camera), and [thermometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermometer). [Solar cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell) and [double-layer capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-layer_capacitor) provide the electrical power.[^cite_note-nssdc-2][^cite_note-0bknv-51] The MINERVA-II-1 rovers were successfully deployed 21 September 2018. Both rovers performed successfully on the asteroid surface, sending images and video from the surface. Rover-1A operated for 113 asteroid days (36 Earth days) returning 609 images from the surface, and Rover-1B operated for 10 asteroid days (3 Earth days) returning 39 images from the surface.[^cite_note-MINVERA-II_results-52]

The MINERVA-II-2 container held the **ROVER-2** (sometimes referred to as MINERVA-II-2), developed by a consortium of universities led by [Tohoku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohoku_University) in Japan. This was an [octagonal prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagonal_prism) shape, 15 cm (5.9 in) diameter and 16 cm (6.3 in) tall, with a mass of about 1 kg (2.2 lb). It had two cameras, a thermometer and an [accelerometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerometer). It was equipped with optical and ultraviolet [LEDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode) to illuminate and detect floating dust particles. ROVER-2 carried four mechanisms to move around using short hops.[^cite_note-nssdc-2] Rover-2 had problems prior to deployment from the orbiter but was released on 2 October 2019 to orbit the asteroid and perform gravitational measurements. It was then crashed onto the asteroid surface a few days later on 8 October 2019.

### MASCOT

"MASCOT" redirects here. For other uses, see [Mascot (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascot_\(disambiguation\)).



Mission overview

The **Mobile Asteroid Surface Scout** (**MASCOT**) was developed by the [German Aerospace Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Aerospace_Center) (DLR) in cooperation with the French space agency [CNES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNES).[^cite_note-xqxVq-53] Its dimensions measure 29.5 cm × 27.5 cm × 19.5 cm (11.6 in × 10.8 in × 7.7 in) and it weighs 9.6 kg (21 lb).[^cite_note-7Nxk8-54] MASCOT carries four instruments: an [infrared spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_spectroscopy) (MicrOmega), a [magnetometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetometer) (MASMAG), a [radiometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometer) (MARA), and a camera (MASCAM) that imaged the small-scale structure, distribution and texture of the regolith.[^cite_note-rDLBG-55] The rover is capable of tumbling once to reposition itself for further measurements.[^cite_note-Keane-44][^cite_note-NASASpaceFlight.com-56] It collected data on the surface structure and mineralogical composition, the thermal behaviour and the magnetic properties of the asteroid.[^cite_note-2gQrq-57] It has a non-rechargeable battery that allowed for operations for approximately 16 hours.[^cite_note-Safe-58][^cite_note-MASCOT_17h-59] The infrared radiometer on the *[InSight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InSight)* Mars lander, launched in 2018, is based on the MASCOT radiometer.[^cite_note-euK8J-60][^cite_note-xoRpN-61]

MASCOT was deployed on 3 October 2018, with a successful landing and ensuing surface mission. Two papers were published describing the results from MASCOT in the scientific journals *[Nature Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Astronomy)*[^cite_note-sS1gN-62] and *[Science](193513.md)*.[^cite_note-0zRP7-63] One finding of the research was that [C-type asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-type_asteroid) consist of more porous material than previously thought, explaining a deficit of this [meteorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorite) type. Meteorites of this type are too porous to survive the entry into the [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere) of planet Earth. Another finding was that [Ryugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/162173_Ryugu) consists of two different almost black types of rock with little internal [cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohesion_\(geology\)), but no dust was detected.[^cite_note-0XIe4-64][^cite_note-EzEUK-65] A third paper describing results from MASCOT was published in the *[Journal of Geophysical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Geophysical_Research)* and describes the [magnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism) properties of Ryugu, showing that Ryugu does not have a magnetic field on a boulder scale.[^cite_note-EAmKz-66]

## Sampling

| Sampling | Date |
| --- | --- |
| 1st surface sampling | 21 February 2019 |
| Sub-surface sampling | SCI impactor: 5 April 2019 Target marker: 5 June 2019[^cite_note-Ryan_June5-33] Sampling: 11 July 2019[^cite_note-Kyoto_July_2019-34] |
| 2nd surface sampling | Optional;[^cite_note-Press_conference_5_March_2019-70] was not done. |



Artistic rendering of *Hayabusa* collecting a surface sample.

The original plan was for the spacecraft to collect up to three samples: 1) surface material that exhibits traits of hydrous minerals; 2) surface material with either unobservable or weak evidence of aqueous alterations; 3) excavated sub-surface material.[^cite_note-Shogo_Tachibana_2013-71]

The first two surface samples were scheduled to start in late October 2018, but the rovers showed large and small boulders and insufficient surface area to sample, so the mission team decided to postpone sampling to 2019 and evaluate various options.[^cite_note-sampling_postponed-30] The first surface sampling was completed on 22 February 2019 and obtained a substantial amount of topsoil,[^cite_note-Press_conference_5_March_2019-70][^cite_note-First_sampling-72] so the second surface sampling was postponed and was eventually cancelled to decrease the risks to the mission.[^cite_note-Press_conference_5_March_2019-70]

The second and final sample was collected from material that was dislodged from beneath the surface by the kinetic impactor (SCI impactor) shot from a distance of 300 m (980 ft).[^cite_note-9ToPV-73][^cite_note-Mission_schedule-74] All samples are stored in separate sealed containers inside the [sample return capsule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample-return_mission) (SRC).

### Surface sample



Sampler horn

*Hayabusa2*'s sampling device is based on *Hayabusa*'s. The first surface sample retrieval was conducted on 21 February 2019, which began with the spacecraft's descent, approaching the surface of the asteroid. When the sampler horn attached to *Hayabusa2*'s underside touched the surface, a 5 g (0.18 oz) [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum) projectile (bullet) was fired at 300 m/s (980 ft/s) into the surface.[^cite_note-First_sampling-72] The resulting ejected materials were collected by a "catcher" at the top of the horn, which the ejecta reached under their own momentum under microgravity conditions.[^cite_note-reentry-75]

### Sub-surface sample



Animation illustrating SCI deployment and subsequent sampling from the resulting crater.

The sub-surface sample collection required an impactor to create a crater in order to retrieve material under the surface, not subjected to [space weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weathering). This required removing a large volume of surface material with a powerful impactor. For this purpose, *Hayabusa2* deployed on 5 April 2019 a free-flying gun with one "bullet", called the **Small Carry-on Impactor** (**SCI**); the system contained a 2.5 kg (5.5 lb) [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) projectile, shot onto the surface with an explosive propellant charge. Following SCI deployment, *Hayabusa2* also left behind a deployable camera (**DCAM3**)[^cite_note-a0Esa-76] to observe and map the precise location of the SCI impact, while the orbiter maneuvered to the far side of the asteroid to avoid being hit by debris from the impact.

It was expected that the SCI deployment would induce seismic shaking of the asteroid, a process considered important in the resurfacing of small airless bodies. However, post-impact images from the spacecraft revealed that little shaking had occurred, indicating the asteroid was significantly less cohesive than was expected.[^cite_note-ZTDX9-77]

The touchdown on and sampling of Ryugu on 11 July, 2019

Approximately 40 minutes after separation, when the spacecraft was at a safe distance, the impactor was fired into the asteroid surface by detonating a 4.5 kg (9.9 lb) [shaped charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaped_charge) of plasticized [HMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMX) for acceleration.[^cite_note-NASASpaceFlight.com-56][^cite_note-XQHiG-78] The copper impactor was shot onto the surface from an altitude of about 500 m (1,600 ft) and it excavated a crater of about 10 m (33 ft) in diameter, exposing pristine material.[^cite_note-Tsuda_2013-15][^cite_note-Marker-32] The next step was the deployment on 4 June 2019 of a reflective target marker in the area near the crater to assist with navigation and descent.[^cite_note-Ryan_June5-33] The touchdown and sampling took place on 11 July 2019.[^cite_note-Kyoto_July_2019-34]

## Sample return



Replica of *Hayabusa*'s sample-return capsule (SRC) used for re-entry. *Hayabusa2*'s capsule is of the same size, measuring 40 cm (16 in) in diameter and using a parachute for touchdown.

The spacecraft collected and stored the samples in separate sealed containers inside the sample-return capsule (SRC), which is equipped with [thermal insulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_insulation). The container is 40 cm (16 in) external diameter, 20 cm (7.9 in) in height, and a mass of about 16 kg (35 lb).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

At the end of the science phase in November 2019,[^cite_note-Departure-8] *Hayabusa2* used its ion engines to change orbit and return to Earth.[^cite_note-reentry-75] Hours before *Hayabusa2* flew past Earth in late 2020, it released the capsule, on 5 December 2020 at 05\:30 UTC.[^cite_note-AZkX3-79] The capsule was released spinning at one revolution per three seconds, and re-entered the Earth's atmosphere at 12 km/s (7.5 mi/s). Deploying a radar-reflective parachute at an altitude of about 10 km (6.2 mi), it ejected its heat shield while transmitting a position beacon signal.[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39][^cite_note-reentry-75] The sample capsule landed at the [Woomera Test Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAAF_Woomera_Range_Complex) in Australia.[^cite_note-BBC-20201206-13][^cite_note-8oP6P-80] The total flight distance was 5.24×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>9</sup> km (35.0 AU).[^cite_note-Fact_Sheet_Hayabusa2-39]

Any volatile substances will be collected before the sealed containers are opened.[^cite_note-Shogo_Tachibana_2013-71] The samples will be curated and analyzed at JAXA's [Extraterrestrial Sample Curation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterrestrial_Sample_Curation_Center),[^cite_note-R2YaY-81] where international scientists can request a small portion of the samples. The spacecraft brought back a capsule containing carbon-rich [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) fragments that scientists believe could provide clues about the ancient delivery of water and organic molecules to Earth.[^cite_note-JF0Z7-82][^cite_note-qEEGX-83]



One of the facility-to-facility transfer containers (FFTC) of *Hayabusa2* returned samples given to NASA by JAXA.

JAXA is sharing a portion of these samples with NASA. In exchange, NASA is providing JAXA a percentage of a sample from asteroid Bennu, after the agency's OSIRIS-REx spacecraft returned to Earth from the space rock on 24 September 2023.[^cite_note-FDGVz-84]

A November 2024 study published in *[Meteoritics & Planetary Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteoritics_&_Planetary_Science)* examined a sample returned from the asteroid Ryugu by the *Hayabusa2* mission. Despite stringent contamination control measures, terrestrial microorganisms rapidly colonized the sample. Researchers concluded that the observed microbial growth originated from Earthly contamination during sample preparation rather than being indigenous to the asteroid. This finding underscores the difficulty of preventing microbial contamination, even in highly controlled laboratory environments.[^cite_note-ZR9BG-85][^cite_note-JZbcZ-86]

## Mission extension (Hayabusa2♯)

Animation of *Hayabusa2* orbit – extended mission\
&#x20; *Hayabusa2* **·**   [162173 Ryugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/162173_Ryugu) **·**   [Earth](9228.md) **·**   [Sun](26751.md) **·**   [98943 Torifune](46319186.md) **·**   [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26)

With the successful return and retrieval of the sample capsule on 6 December 2020 ([JST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Standard_Time)), Hayabusa2 is planned to use its remaining 30 kg (66 lb) of [xenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon) propellant (from the initial 66 kg (146 lb)) to extend its service life and fly out to explore new targets.[^cite_note-Mission_extension-87] JAXA announced a mission extension for *Hayabusa2* in 2020.[^cite_note-UT-2020-88] This extended mission, titled the *Small Hazardous Asteroid Reconnaissance Probe* (SHARP or ♯), tasked the spacecraft to visit two more near-Earth asteroids: [98943 Torifune](46319186.md) in July 2026 and a rendezvous with 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> in July 2031.[^cite_note-Tsuda2025-89] These asteroids were chosen because they required a low amount of fuel (Δv < 1.6 km/s) to reach within 10 years.[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2021-90] JAXA presented the initial plan in July 2020 with the choice of visiting either 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> or [2001 AV43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_AV43),[^cite_note-sorae-2020-91][^cite_note-S\&T-2020-92] but ultimately selected 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> in September 2020.[^cite_note-UT-2020-88][^cite_note-Hirabayashi2021-90]

Selected EAEEA (Earth → Asteroid → Earth → Earth → Asteroid) scenario:[^cite_note-Haya220200915-93]

- December 2020: Extension mission start

- 2021 until July 2026: cruise operation

- July 2026: S-type asteroid [98943 Torifune](46319186.md) high-speed fly-by

- December 2027: Earth swing-by

- June 2028: Second Earth swing-by

- July 2031: Target body ([1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26)) rendezvous

### Torifune flyby

*Hayabusa2* successfully [flew by](48936466.md) the [S-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-type_asteroid) asteroid [98943 Torifune](46319186.md) in July 2026. The encounter and observation of the asteroid were conducted at a high relative speed (5 km/s), however the probe was able to successfully image the asteroid in great detail despite this limitation. The probe revealed that Torifune is a [rubble pile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubble_pile) and [contact binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_binary_\(small_Solar_System_body\)) strewn with boulders of different sizes.[^cite_note-cI5Yw-94][^cite_note-qitEn-95]

### Future 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> rendezvous and landing



Artist's impression of the *Hayabusa2* spacecraft landing on [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26)

When *Hayabusa2* arrives, [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26) will become the smallest asteroid ever visited by a spacecraft.[^cite_note-planetary-2026-96] The exploration of 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> will provide insights into the properties of decameter-sized asteroids, which will aid [planetary defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_defense) efforts.[^cite_note-Santana-Ros-2025-97][^cite_note-UT-2020-88][^cite_note-planetary-2026-96] During its rendezvous, *Hayabusa2* will slowly approach 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> and then [enter orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_insertion) around the asteroid.[^cite_note-Pedros-Faura-2025-98][^cite_note-Kikuchi2023-99][^cite_note-planetary-2026-96] The spacecraft will attempt to create an [impact crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_crater) on 1998 KY<sub>26</sub> by deploying its final remaining target marker and projectile.[^cite_note-Kikuchi2023-99][^cite_note-Santana-Ros-2025-97] *Hayabusa2* will then attempt to land on the surface of 1998 KY<sub>26</sub>.[^cite_note-Kikuchi2023-99][^cite_note-planetary-2020-100] Due to the asteroid's rapid rotation, landing on its surface would be challenging;[^cite_note-planetary-2026-96] the safest place for *Hayabusa2* to land would be the asteroid's poles, where centrifugal forces are minimal.[^cite_note-Kikuchi2023-99][^cite_note-Pedros-Faura-2025-98]
