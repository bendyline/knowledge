**Birni-N'Konni** (also **Birnin-Konni** or shortened to **Konni**/**Bkonni**) is a town in the [Tahoua Region](2000318.md) of [Niger](21373.md), lying immediately north of the [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger-Nigeria_border) of [Nigeria](21383.md) and west of seasonal [Maggia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggia_River_\(Niger\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). It is an important [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) and [transport hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub) and as of the 2012 census had a population of 63,169.[^cite_note-city-1] The town is the historic centre of the small pre-colonial Hausa state of [Konni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konni,_Niger). The name comes from the [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_language) for "*Walled Town of Konni*", and many Hausa towns (such as [Zinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinder)) designate the old citadel neighbourhood the "Birni".[^cite_note-2]

Konni is known for its [vernacular architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular_architecture) including traditional [granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granary) and is regarded as a centre for [smuggling](28645.md) between Niger and Nigeria. Birni-N'Konni's contemporary importance rests on the surrounding agricultural lands and its place on the [N1 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_highway_\(Niger\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), the main east-west highway linking Niger's capital [Niamey](57061.md) and the nation's main eastern cities of [Maradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maradi,_Niger) and [Zinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinder). It also sits astride one of several main routes between the Hausa populated territories of southern Niger and northern Nigeria, with the [Nigerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian) transport and trade centre of [Illela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illela,_Nigeria), [Sokoto State](2227660.md) just 18 km to the south.

The town was also the site of a major [massacre](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/massacre) on 8 May 1899 when the French [Voulet–Chanoine Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voulet%E2%80%93Chanoine_Mission) attacked the region.[^cite_note-3] On 27 October 2020, an American citizen was kidnapped in the area.[^cite_note-\:0-4] No group claimed responsibility for the abduction.[^cite_note-\:0-4] He was freed on 31 October.[^cite_note-5]
