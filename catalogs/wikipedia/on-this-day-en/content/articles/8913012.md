**Wembley Stadium**, branded as **Wembley Stadium connected by EE** for sponsorship reasons, is an [association football](10568.md) stadium in [Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley), London, England. It opened in 2007 on the site of the [original Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)), which had stood from 1923 until 2000.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-10] The stadium is England's [national football stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_stadiums), and thus hosts the majority of the [England national team](9904.md) home matches and the [FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_Final) – the final of England's primary domestic club football competition. It is considered one of the most well known football stadiums in the world, and is considered a hub for the English game. Wembley Stadium is owned by the governing body of English football, [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association), whose headquarters are in the stadium, through its subsidiary Wembley National Stadium Ltd (WNSL). With 90,000 seats, it is the [largest stadium in the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_in_the_United_Kingdom_by_capacity) and [the second-largest stadium in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_stadiums_by_capacity), behind [Barcelona](68187.md)'s [Camp Nou](222488.md).[^cite_note-11]

Designed by [Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_\(company\)) and [Foster and Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_and_Partners), the stadium is crowned by the 134-metre-high (440 ft) Wembley Arch which serves aesthetically as a landmark across London as well as structurally, with the arch supporting over 75% of the entire roof load.[^cite_note-12] The stadium was built by Australian firm [Multiplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplex_\(company\)) at a cost of £798 million (£1.41 billion today).[^cite_note-13] Two partially [retractable roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retractable_roof) structures over the east and west ends of the stadium can be opened to allow sunlight and aid pitch growth.

In addition to England home games and the FA Cup final, the stadium also hosts other major games in English football, including the season-opening [FA Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield), the finals of lower tiered cup competition – the [FA Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Trophy), the [FA Vase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Vase), finals of the [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) and [EFL Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy), the [FA Cup semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_semi-finals), and the promotion play-off finals of the [tiers two, three, four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_play-offs), and [five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(division\)) of the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system). The stadium also hosts the [Women's FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_FA_Cup) finals and an increasing number of home games of the [England women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_football_team).

A [UEFA category four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_stadium_categories) stadium, Wembley hosted the [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UEFA_Champions_League_final), [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_UEFA_Champions_League_final) and the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_Champions_League_final) Champions League Finals, eight games at [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) (including [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_final) and both of the semi-finals),[^cite_note-14] and hosted [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2022_final) of the [UEFA Women's Euro 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2022).[^cite_note-15] The stadium hosted the gold-medal matches at the [2012 Olympic Games football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics). The stadium also hosts the [Rugby Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_League)'s [Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenge_Cup), [Women's Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Challenge_Cup), and [1895 Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFL_1895_Cup) finals, in addition to various music concerts. The stadium also hosts [NFL London Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_London_Games) and was the temporary home of [Premier League](11250.md) football club [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur) between August 2017 and March 2019, while [White Hart Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Hart_Lane) was being demolished and their [new stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_Stadium) was constructed. Wembley has also been chosen as one of the host stadia for [UEFA Euro 2028](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2028).

In 2014, Wembley Stadium entered into a six-year sponsorship agreement with mobile provider [EE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EE_\(telecommunications\)), under which it provides technology and infrastructure services for the venue. Under the agreement, the facility is officially referred to as "Wembley Stadium connected by EE".[^cite_note-16] The deal was renewed in 2019 and again in 2025 for a further four years, through to 2028.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Stadium



Wembley Stadium interior

Wembley was designed by architects [Foster + Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_+_Partners) and [HOK Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOK_Sport) (now Populous) and with engineers Mott Stadium Consortium, who were a collection of three structural engineering consultants in the form of [Mott MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott_MacDonald), [Sinclair Knight Merz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinclair_Knight_Merz) and [Aurecon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurecon). The design of the building services was carried out by Mott MacDonald. The construction of the stadium was managed by Australian company [Multiplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplex_\(company\)) and funded by [Sport England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_England), WNSL (Wembley National Stadium Limited), [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association), the [Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Digital,_Culture,_Media_and_Sport) and the [London Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Development_Agency). It is one of the [most expensive stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_megaprojects#Sports_and_culture_projects) ever built at a cost of £798 million in 2007,[^cite_note-19] and has the largest roof-covered [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) in the world. [Nathaniel Lichfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Lichfield) and Partners was appointed to assist Wembley National Stadium Limited in preparing the scheme for a new stadium and to obtain planning and listed building permission for the development.[^cite_note-20]



Aerial view of the stadium in the day

The all-seater stadium is a bowl design with a capacity of 90,000, protected from the elements by a sliding roof that does not completely enclose it.[^cite_note-21] The stadium's signature feature is a circular section lattice arch of 7 m (23 ft) internal diameter with a 315 m (1,033 ft) span, erected some 22° off true, and rising to 133 m (436 ft). It supports all the weight of the north roof and 60% of the weight of the retractable roof on the southern side.[^cite_note-22] The arch is the world's longest unsupported roof structure.[^cite_note-23]

A platform system was designed in order to temporarily convert the stadium to athletics use, but its use would decrease the stadium's capacity to approximately 60,000.[^cite_note-24] No athletics events (track and field) have taken place at the stadium; the conversion for athletics use was a condition of part of the lottery funding the stadium received, but to convert it would take weeks of work and cost millions of pounds.[^cite_note-25] Instead, with the awarding of the [2012 Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympic_Games) to London in 2005, the [London Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Olympic_Stadium) has been used for major athletics events since 2012.

### Construction



The stadium in its very early stages of construction c. August 2003

The initial plan for the reconstruction of [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)) was for demolition to begin before Christmas 2000, and for the new stadium to be completed some time during 2003, but this work was delayed by a succession of financial and legal difficulties. In 2004, London Mayor [Ken Livingstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Livingstone) and Brent Council also announced wider plans for the [regeneration of Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneration_of_Wembley), taking in the arena and the surrounding areas as well as the stadium, to be implemented over two or three decades. Demolition officially began on 30 September 2002, with the [Twin Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Towers_\(Wembley\)) being dismantled in December 2002.[^cite_note-26]

Delays to the construction project started as far back as 2003. In December 2003, the constructors of the arch, subcontractors [Cleveland Bridge & Engineering Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Bridge_&_Engineering_Company) of [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington), warned Multiplex about rising costs. Cleveland Bridge withdrew from the project and were replaced by Dutch firm Hollandia, with all the attendant problems of starting over. 2004 also saw errors, most notably a fatal accident involving carpenter Patrick O'Sullivan for which construction firm PC Harrington Contractors were fined £150,000 in relation to breaches of health and safety laws.[^cite_note-27]

In October 2005, Sports Minister [Richard Caborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Caborn) announced: "They say the [Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup_Final) will be there, barring six feet of snow or something like that". By November 2005, WNSL were still hopeful of a handover date of 31 March, in time for the [cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FA_Cup_Final) on 13 May. However, in December 2005, the builders admitted that there was a "material risk" that the stadium might not be ready in time for the final.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] In February 2006 these worries were confirmed, with the FA moving the game to [Cardiff](5882.md)'s [Millennium Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Stadium).[^cite_note-30]



Construction of the new Wembley, looking east, taken January 2006

On 20 March 2006, a steel rafter in the roof of the new development fell by 1+1⁄2 ft (46 cm), forcing 3,000 workers to evacuate the stadium and raising further doubts over the completion date which was already behind schedule.[^cite_note-31] On 23 March 2006, [sewers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitary_sewer) beneath the stadium buckled due to ground movement. [GMB Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMB_Union) leader Steve Kelly said that the problem had been caused by the pipes not being properly laid, and that the repair would take months. A spokesman for developers [Multiplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplex_\(company\)) said that they did not believe this would "have any impact on the completion of the stadium", which was then scheduled to be completed on 31 March 2006.[^cite_note-32]

On 30 March 2006, the developers announced that Wembley Stadium would not be ready until 2007.[^cite_note-33] All competitions and concerts planned were to be moved to suitable locations. On 19 June 2006, it was announced that the turf had been laid. On 19 October 2006 it was announced that the venue was now set to open in early 2007 after the dispute between [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) and Multiplex had finally been settled. WNSL was expected to pay around £36m to Multiplex, on top of the amount of the original fixed-price contract. The total cost of the project (including local transport infrastructure redevelopment and the cost of financing) was estimated to be £1 billion.[^cite_note-34]

For the new stadium the level of the pitch was lowered. During excavation of the new playing field, mechanical diggers unearthed a buried obstruction: the concrete foundations of [Watkin's Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watkin's_Tower), a failed attempt to construct a rival to the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower) in London. Only the base of the tower was ever built before being abandoned and demolished in 1907; the site was later used as the location for the first Wembley Stadium.[^cite_note-delisle-35]

### Handover and opening



[Statue of Bobby Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Bobby_Moore,_Wembley), England's [1966 FIFA World Cup](61629.md) winning captain, stands outside the stadium entrance looking down [Olympic Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Way).

The new stadium was completed and handed over to the FA on 9 March 2007. The official Wembley Stadium website had announced that the stadium would be open for public viewing for local residents of Brent on 3 March 2007, however this was delayed by two weeks and instead happened on 17 March.[^cite_note-36]

While the stadium had hosted football matches since the handover in March, the stadium was officially opened on Saturday 19 May, with the staging of the [2007 FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FA_Cup_Final). Eight days before that on Friday 11 May, the [statue of Bobby Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Bobby_Moore,_Wembley) had been unveiled by his former England team-mate [Sir Bobby Charlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Bobby_Charlton) outside the stadium entrance, as the "finishing touch" to the completion of the stadium. The twice life-size bronze statue, sculpted by [Philip Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Jackson_\(sculptor\)), depicts England's [1966 World Cup winning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) captain [Bobby Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Moore), looking down [Olympic Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Way).[^cite_note-WNSL10May2007-37][^cite_note-WNSL11May2007-38][^cite_note-39]

### Pitch



Wembley Stadium pitch during [England](9904.md) [friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_match) against [Germany](250204.md) in August 2007

The pitch size, as lined for association football, is 115 yd (105 m) long by 75 yd (69 m) wide, slightly narrower than the old Wembley, as required by the [UEFA stadium categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_stadium_categories) for a category four stadium, the top category.

In a period after the completion of the new Wembley, the pitch came into disrepute. It was described as being "no good" and "not in the condition that Wembley used to be known for" by [Slaven Bilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaven_Bili%C4%87) before a game between [England](9904.md) and the team he managed, [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), in November 2007.[^cite_note-46] The pitch was cut up during the game, which was blamed by some[^cite_note-47] as the reason England did not qualify for [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008).[^cite_note-48] The Football Association admitted in April 2009, after the [FA Cup semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_semi-finals), that improvements were needed to the Wembley pitch, after criticism of the surface by coaches [Alex Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Ferguson), [Arsène Wenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars%C3%A8ne_Wenger) and [David Moyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Moyes).[^cite_note-49]

In March 2010, the surface was relaid for the tenth time since opening. In April 2010, the pitch was again criticised following the [FA Cup semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_FA_Cup), during which the players found it difficult to keep their footing and the surface cut up despite the dry conditions. The then [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur) boss, [Harry Redknapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Redknapp), labelled it a "disgrace" after his side's semi-final defeat to [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.).[^cite_note-50] After the [2010 FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FA_Cup_Final), [Chelsea](7473.md) captain [John Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Terry) said: "The pitch ruined the final. It's probably the worst pitch we've played on all year. It was not good enough for a Wembley pitch."[^cite_note-51] The stadium was then relaid with a Desso [GrassMaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GrassMaster) semi-artificial pitch, ahead of the 2010 community shield game between [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Football_Club) and [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United). [Michael Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Owen), who previously criticised the pitch for causing him injury, said that it was much improved.[^cite_note-52]

In 2023, the stadium switched to a carpet lay and play system.[^cite_note-53]

Wembley has been used for [American football](18951490.md) matches in the [National Football League](21211.md)'s [International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series). Tottenham Hotspur hosted [Manchester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City) in a [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Premier_League) match on 29 October 2018, a day after Wembley hosted the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles) and [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars). Due to the short turnaround, faded gridiron markings and the NFL logo were clearly visible on the pitch along with worn grass along the centre of the pitch and the touch-lines. Tottenham were forced to hold the match at Wembley due to construction delays to [their new ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_Stadium).[^cite_note-54] Despite the pitch's condition, UEFA allowed a [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Champions_League) leg to be played at Wembley on 6 November 2018 with Tottenham hosting [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Football_Association) side [PSV Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSV_Eindhoven).[^cite_note-55]

### Covering



Closeup of the arch

The stadium roof has an area of 40,000 m<sup>2</sup> (430,000 ft<sup>2</sup>), of which 13,722 m<sup>2</sup> (147,700 ft<sup>2</sup>) is movable.[^cite_note-CONSTRUCTIONFACTS-42] The primary reason for the sliding roof was to avoid shading the pitch, as grass demands direct sunlight to grow effectively.[^cite_note-56] The sliding roof design minimises the shadow by having the roof pulled back on the east, west and south.[^cite_note-57] Angus Campbell, the chief architect, also said that an aim was for the pitch to be in sunlight during matches played between 3 pm and 5 pm from the beginning of May to the end of June, when the FA and World Cups would be played. However, it was mentioned during live commentary of the mid-May 2007 FA Cup Final that the pitch was in partial shade at the start at 3 pm and also during the match.[^cite_note-BBC_text_commentary_FA_Cup_Final_2007-58]

The stadium roof rises to 52 metres (171 ft) above the pitch and is supported by an arch rising 133 m (436 ft) above the level of the external concourse. With a span of 315 m (1,033 ft), the arch is the longest single-span roof structure in the world.[^cite_note-Wembley_Stadium_Facts_and_Figures-41]

### Safety and security

Safety and security at Wembley Stadium are managed in partnership with the Metropolitan Police, Brent Council and other agencies, with event day operations including extensive pre event planning and coordination. Measures introduced at the venue have included enhanced [turnstiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnstile), a new command and control room, additional [CCTV](87179.md) coverage and strengthened perimeter doors and fencing, together with a Public Space Protection Order in the surrounding area that restricts street drinking, ticket touting and the use of [pyrotechnics](187584.md) on event days. Stadium information and spectator guides describe the use of stewards and security staff, controlled entry points, bag checks and limits on prohibited items such as flares and smoke devices.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

Following serious disorder at the [UEFA](32332.md) [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_2020) final between England and Italy, when numbers of ticketless supporters breached cordons and entered Wembley, an independent review led by Baroness Cassey described the day as "a source of national shame" and recommended stronger physical and operational safeguard. [Brent Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_London_Borough_Council) subsequently approved plans to reinforce the security line around the stadium, including a higher perimeter fence and other measures intended to prevent similar [incursions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incursions).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

In May 2026, the [FA Cup](11237.md) final at Wembley took place on the same day as a [pro-Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pro-Palestinian_protests_in_the_United_Kingdom) [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba) Day march and the "Unite the Kingdom" rally in [central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London). As part of a wider "significant public order policing operation," officers monitored [CCTV](87179.md) feeds from the FA Cup final to identify supporters travelling towards the demonstrations. [The Metropolitan Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Metropolitan_Police) reported 22 arrests at the FA Cup final itself, alongside 43 arrests connected to the two marches, and said four officers were assaulted and six were subjected to hate crime offences.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

### Litigation

The Australian firm Multiplex, which was the main contractor on Wembley Stadium, made significant losses on the project.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] In an attempt to recoup some of those losses, the firm initiated a number of legal cases against its sub-contractors and consultants.[^cite_note-67] The largest of these – the largest construction claim in UK legal history – was a claim for £253 million against the structural engineering consultants [Mott MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott_MacDonald).[^cite_note-guardian.co.uk-68]

In preliminary hearings the two architecture practices which worked for Multiplex on the project were ordered to allow Multiplex access to their records for them to build a case. The practices, [Foster + Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_+_Partners) and [Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_\(architects\)), estimated the costs of providing access and answering Multiplex's queries at £5 million.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] Mott MacDonald issued a counter-claim for unpaid fees of £250,000.[^cite_note-guardian.co.uk-68]

The dispute between Multiplex and Mott MacDonald was settled out of court in June 2010, ahead of a January 2011 trial. The terms of the settlement not disclosed, but it was reported that Multiplex "would not be out of pocket".[^cite_note-71]

Multiplex also took the original steel contractor, Cleveland Bridge, to court claiming £38 million[^cite_note-72] compensation for costs resulting from Cleveland Bridge withdrawing from the project. Cleveland Bridge, in turn, claimed up to £15 million from Multiplex. The case was resolved in September 2008 with Cleveland Bridge ordered to pay £6.1 million in damages and 20% of Multiplex's costs, after the court found against Cleveland Bridge. The judge criticised both sides for allowing the case to reach court, pointing out that total costs were £22 million, including £1 million for photocopying.[^cite_note-73] Multiplex's ultimate bill is estimated to be over £10 million. In 2007, Multiplex also contested a claim from its concrete contractor, PC Harrington, that Multiplex owes £13.4 million to PC Harrington.[^cite_note-74]

### Bid to buy

In April 2018, [Shahid Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Khan), the owner of [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) and the [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars), put forward an offer to purchase Wembley Stadium from the FA. The deal included not only the purchase of the stadium, but also providing the FA full rights to keep control of the Club Wembley business.[^cite_note-75] On 18 July 2018, a parliamentary select committee was held to discuss the possible sale, with evidence being given by former player, [Gary Neville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Neville), and Katrina Law of the [Football Supporters' Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Supporters'_Federation).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] The offer was withdrawn on 17 October 2018.[^cite_note-78]

## Sport

Further information: [List of international sports matches held at Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_sports_matches_held_at_Wembley_Stadium)



Logo of the governing body of English football, the FA, as displayed on the exterior of Wembley Stadium

Given the ownership of the stadium by [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) (the governing body of English football), the [England national football team](9904.md) is a major user of Wembley. In 2007, the [League Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) moved back to Wembley from Cardiff following the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_final) and [FA Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield). Other showpiece football matches that were previously staged at Wembley, such as the [Football League promotion play-offs](14419588.md) and the [Football League Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Trophy) final, have returned to the stadium. In addition, the [Conference National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_National) (now National League) play-off final is held at Wembley since 2007, and the [FA Women's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Women's_Cup) final since 2015.



Wembley Stadium during the [London 2012 Olympic Games football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics)

The new Wembley was a significant part of the plan for the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) in London; the stadium was the site of several games in both the men's and women's [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics) tournaments, with the finals being held there.[^cite_note-79] The FA offices at Wembley Stadium, with social areas and boardroom, were designed by architects Gebler Tooth – who were also responsible for Team GB House at the London 2012 Olympics.

Additionally, the Rugby League [Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenge_Cup) Final returned to Wembley Stadium in 2007, and the stadium also hosted both semi-finals of the [2013 Rugby League World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Rugby_League_World_Cup). Wembley was one of the 13 venues for the [2015 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup).

The [Race of Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_of_Champions) staged their [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Race_of_Champions) and [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Race_of_Champions) events at the stadium.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

[Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur) agreed with the operators (Wembley National Stadium Ltd) to use the stadium for all of their European fixtures during the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C._season), before using the stadium for the entire [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C._season).[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] They also played most of their home games of the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C._season) at Wembley and continued until April 2019 when they moved to [their new stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_Stadium).

### Football



Fans of the [England football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_football_team) create the [St George's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's_Cross).

The first match at the stadium was a game played behind closed doors between Multiplex and Wembley Stadium staff.[^cite_note-88] The first game in front of spectators was between the Geoff Thomas Foundation Charity XI and the Wembley Sponsors Allstars on 17 March 2007. The Geoff Thomas Foundation Charity XI won 2–0 (scorers [Mark Bright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bright) and [Simon Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Jordan)).[^cite_note-89] The first official match involving professional players was [England U21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_U21s) vs [Italy U21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_under-21_football_team) on 24 March 2007, which finished 3–3. Official attendance was 56,700 (although all of the 60,000 tickets that were made available were sold in advance).[^cite_note-England_U21s_draw_Wembley_cracker-90] The first player to score in a [FIFA](11049.md)-sanctioned match was Italian striker [Giampaolo Pazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giampaolo_Pazzini) after 28 seconds of the same game; he also scored the first [hat-trick](411541.md) at Wembley. The first English player to score in a full-scale match was [David Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bentley) with a free kick in the same game.[^cite_note-England_U21s_draw_Wembley_cracker-90]



[Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur) celebrate winning the [Football League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Cup) against [Chelsea](7473.md) in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Football_League_Cup_Final).

The first club game, competitive game, and cup final held at the new Wembley took place on 12 May 2007 when [Kidderminster Harriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidderminster_Harriers) met [Stevenage Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevenage_Borough) in the [FA Trophy final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FA_Trophy_Final).[^cite_note-91] Kidderminster striker [James Constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Constable) was the first player to score a goal in a final at the new Wembley. Kidderminster became the first team to play at both the old and new stadium. Stevenage Borough were the first team to win a final at the new Wembley beating Kidderminster 3–2, despite trailing 2–0 at half time. The first players to play at both the old and new Wembley Stadiums were [Steve Guppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Guppy) (for Stevenage Borough) and [Jeff Kenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Kenna) (for Kidderminster Harriers). Ex-[England](9904.md) international Guppy is also the first player to win a final at both stadia (with [Wycombe Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycombe_Wanderers) and [Leicester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_City) in the old Wembley, then Stevenage Borough in the new one). [Ronnie Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Henry) is the first ever player to lift a competitive club trophy at the new Wembley.[^cite_note-Henry_the_first-92]



[Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.) fans celebrate winning the [FA Cup](11237.md) against [Cardiff City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_City) in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_FA_Cup_Final). The first player to score international goals at both the old and new stadia was Michael Owen when he scored for England against Israel. On 22 August, [Germany](250204.md) beat England 2–1 to become the first team to beat them in the new stadium. England's first competitive defeat at the new stadium was on 21 November 2007 when [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) won 3–2. This match cost England qualification to [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) and head coach [Steve McClaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_McClaren) his job.

In 2015, a second statue was erected outside Wembley, depicting Rugby League legends [Martin Offiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Offiah), [Alex Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Murphy_\(rugby_league\)), [Eric Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Ashton), [Gus Risman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Risman) and [Billy Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Boston).[^cite_note-112]

In 2011, international rugby league returned to Wembley for the first time since 1997 when [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_league_team) lost to [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_rugby_league_team) 0–36[^cite_note-113] and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Kangaroos) beat host nation [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_rugby_league_team) 36–20[^cite_note-114] in the [2011 Rugby League Four Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Rugby_League_Four_Nations). The semi-finals of the [2013 Rugby League World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Rugby_League_World_Cup) were played at Wembley Stadium where [defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Rugby_League_World_Cup) New Zealand beat England 20–18, and eventual tournament champions Australia defeated [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji_Bati) 64–0. The double header drew 67,575 fans to Wembley, the second highest crowd for an international rugby league game at either the original or the new stadium.

### Rugby union



Wembley in rugby union formation, with posts up before [Saracens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracens_F.C.) played [Worcester Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_Warriors) in 2010

The first top level [rugby union](25405.md) match was a non-cap match between the [Barbarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_F.C.) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_union_team) on 3 December 2008.[^cite_note-115]

Between 2009 and 2017, the stadium was used regularly by [Saracens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracens_FC) for some major [Aviva Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviva_Premiership) and [Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup) matches. Their Aviva Premiership clash with [Harlequins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlequin_F.C.) in 2012 was played before a crowd of 83,761, a world record for a rugby union club match. The same two teams set further records of 83,889 spectators in 2014,[^cite_note-116] and 84,068 in 2015.[^cite_note-117]

The stadium was used during the [2015 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup), during which it hosted two pool matches. The 89,019 crowd for the New Zealand versus Argentina game set a new record attendance for a Rugby World Cup game.[^cite_note-rwc-118] The Ireland versus Romania match one week later improved this record again to 89,267.[^cite_note-rwc2-119] Although the 90,000 seat Wembley was the largest stadium used during the 2015 Rugby World Cup, the World Cup Final was held at the 82,000 seat [Twickenham Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham_Stadium), the traditional home of the tournament's host, England's [Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_Union).

### American football

Main articles: [NFL International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series) and [Union Jack Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack_Classic)



Build up to the 2010 game between [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos) and [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers)

Wembley has had a long association with [American football](18951490.md). A [United States Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Football_League) game was staged there in 1984,[^cite_note-120] and between 1986 and 1993 the old Wembley Stadium hosted eight [National Football League](21211.md) exhibition games featuring 13 different NFL teams.[^cite_note-121] Between the opening of the new stadium in 2007 and 2019, Wembley hosted games during the NFL regular season. As a result of this, [NFL commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_commissioner) [Roger Goodell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Goodell) stated in October 2009 that "he expected the NFL will start playing multiple regular-season games in Britain in the next few years, an expansion that could lead to putting [a franchise in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_NFL_franchise)."[^cite_note-122]

On 28 October 2007, in front of 81,176 fans, the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_New_York_Giants_season) defeated the [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Miami_Dolphins_season) by a score of 13–10 in the first [NFL regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_regular_season) game ever to be played in Europe, and the first outside of North America.[^cite_note-123] The first touchdown scored at Wembley was on a run by Giants' quarterback [Eli Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Manning).

On 20 January 2012, the league announced that the [St. Louis Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Rams) would become a temporary tenant of Wembley, playing an annual game at the stadium every year from 2012 to 2014; part of the reason the Rams were chosen was that the team is owned by [Stan Kroenke](5336231.md), who also is majority shareholder in a local Premier League team, [Arsenal](2174.md).[^cite_note-124] However, the Rams later cancelled their 2013–2014 games,[^cite_note-125] leading to the [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars) becoming new temporary tenants and agreeing to host games in London from 2013 to 2016.[^cite_note-NFL-126][^cite_note-127]

On 16 October 2012, the NFL announced there were to be two NFL regular season games played at Wembley during the 2013 season. The [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Pittsburgh_Steelers_season) at [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Minnesota_Vikings_season) on 29 September 2013, and the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_San_Francisco_49ers_season) at [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Jacksonville_Jaguars_season) on 27 October 2013. This is an attempt by the NFL to strengthen the NFL fanbase in London and internationally. Future plans to have a permanent NFL team in London have been suggested.[^cite_note-128]

Another first was recorded in 2014 as three regular season NFL games were played at Wembley. The [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Oakland_Raiders_season) hosted the [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Miami_Dolphins_season) on 28 September at 6 pm BST, the [Atlanta Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Atlanta_Falcons_season) hosted the [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Detroit_Lions_season) on 26 October at 1\:30 pm GMT and the [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Jacksonville_Jaguars_season) hosted the [Dallas Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Dallas_Cowboys_season) on 9 November at 6 pm GMT.[^cite_note-129] At 9\:30 am ET, the Detroit-Atlanta game was the earliest kick off in NFL history and gave fans a unique four game window on this day.[^cite_note-130] In 2015, another first occurred as the first ever divisional match took place at Wembley between the [American Football Conference – Eastern Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_East)'s [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Miami_Dolphins_season) and [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_New_York_Jets_season).

On 30 October 2016, for the first time in an NFL game played outside the US, the game carried into overtime and subsequently [ended in a tie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NFL_tied_games) (another first for both Wembley and a [London Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series)) in a week 8 match between the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Washington_Redskins_season) and the [Cincinnati Bengals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Cincinnati_Bengals_season). The final score was 27–27.

The Jaguars' deal was extended to 2020 and they were to become the first team to host two games in London in 2020.[^cite_note-NFL_extend_Wembley_deal-131] The two Jacksonville Jaguars games which were scheduled to be played in autumn 2020, were cancelled in May 2020 meaning that the Jaguars played consecutive annual games at Wembley only until 2019.[^cite_note-132] A new agreement was reached in 2022 which would see Jacksonville continue to host annual games for three years until 2024.[^cite_note-133] The game between the Jaguars and the New England Patriots on 20 October 2024 saw a record crowd for an NFL game at Wembley, with 86,651 in attendance.[^cite_note-134]

Wembley Stadium is slated to host its first [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) game in 2026 between [Arizona State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_Sun_Devils_football) and [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Jayhawks_football). The contest, as part of the new [Union Jack Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack_Classic) series, will be the first college football match in Britain since 1988.[^cite_note-135]

### Boxing

On 31 May 2014, Wembley Stadium hosted its first boxing event, featuring [the rematch between Carl Froch and George Groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Froch_vs._George_Groves_II) for the [WBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Association) and [IBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBF) [super-middleweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-middleweight) titles.[^cite_note-136] The contest was held in front of a crowd of 80,000, a British post-war attendance record for a boxing event, surpassing the crowd at the [City of Manchester Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium) when it hosted [Ricky Hatton vs. Juan Lazcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Hatton_vs._Juan_Lazcano) in May 2008.[^cite_note-137]

The [WBA (Super)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBA_\(Super\)) and IBF heavyweight championship fight, [Anthony Joshua vs. Wladimir Klitschko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs._Wladimir_Klitschko), broke the attendance record on 29 April 2017, with an attendance of approximately 90,000.[^cite_note-138]

In 2018, Joshua returned to Wembley and defeated [Alexander Povetkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs_Alexander_Povetkin) for the WBA (Super), IBF and [WBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBO) heavyweight titles in front of 80,000.[^cite_note-139]

The [WBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Council) and *[The Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ring_\(magazine\))* heavyweight title fight, [Tyson Fury vs. Dillian Whyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyson_Fury_vs._Dillian_Whyte), took place on 23 April 2022 in a sold-out Wembley Stadium with 94,000. Fury emerged victorious with a 6th-round TKO.[^cite_note-140]

The stadium hosted [Anthony Joshua](33343240.md) vs [Daniel Dubois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Dubois) on 21 September 2024 as part of the [Riyadh Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Season) with Dubois emerging victorious by knockout in front of 85,644.

[Oleksandr Usyk vs. Daniel Dubois heavyweight boxing rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Usyk_vs._Daniel_Dubois_II) for the Undisputed Championship was held on 19 July 2025 at the stadium. Usyk emerged victorious by knockout in front of 90,000.

## Music

[Dua Lipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua_Lipa) headlined two sold-out shows on 20 and 21 June 2025 during the European leg of her [Radical Optimism Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Optimism_Tour).[^cite_note-169]

[Blackpink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpink) performed at Wembley as the final stop of the European leg of their [Deadline World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_World_Tour) on 15 and 16 August 2025.[^cite_note-170]

British [rock](25423.md) band [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay) hold the record for most performances at Wembley, with 22 shows.[^cite_note-171] The first two were on the [Viva la Vida Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viva_la_Vida_Tour) in 2009, followed by a four-night stint on the [Head Full of Dreams Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Head_Full_of_Dreams_Tour) in 2016. They became the first act to stage ten concerts in a single year, as part of the [Music of the Spheres World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Spheres_World_Tour) between August and September 2025.[^cite_note-SAVAGE-172] This residency was preceded by six dates in August 2022, making the tour's 16 gigs another record.[^cite_note-SAVAGE-172]

As of 28 January 2026, singer [Harry Styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Styles)' brand new tour "[Together, Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together,_Together)" has surpassed [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay)'s 10 concerts in a single year, with a total 12 concerts confirmed for summer 2026 announced during this tour's presale.[^cite_note-173]

In 2026, country singer [Luke Combs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Combs) played three sold-out nights on 31 July and 1 and 2 August for his [My Kinda Saturday Night Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Kinda_Saturday_Night_Tour). Combs was the first country solo artist to headline Wembley and the first artist of his genre to sell out multiple nights there.

In August 2026, [The Weeknd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weeknd) performed over three nights at the stadium during the 2026 extension of his [After Hours til Dawn Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_Hours_til_Dawn_Tour), breaking records in attendance.

On 12 September 2026, [Diljit Dosanjh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diljit_Dosanjh) became the first Punjabi artist to headline Wembley during his Aura World Tour.[^cite_note-174]

## Transport connections

&#x20;The stadium is described as a "public transport destination"[^cite_note-175] for which parking is available on a very limited basis. To alleviate the impact of vehicular traffic on the local residents and businesses, [Brent Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Council) have introduced a number of measures in relation to on street parking and to access restrictions of roads that surround the stadium. The "Wembley Stadium Protective Parking Scheme" sets a boundary in which parking on street is restricted to only those that hold an event day parking permit. Road closures are in force from 10\:00 am on the event day until midnight and apply to Fulton Road, Engineers Way and South Way.[^cite_note-176]

### Onsite parking

The onsite parking facility is shared with Wembley Arena, essentially being the open air surface parking surrounding the eastern flank of Wembley Stadium and the [multi-storey car park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-storey_car_park). These are called Green Car Park and Red Car Park, respectively. There is disabled parking available onsite, at the Green Car Park, at a reduced rate but on a first come first served basis. On some football event dates, opposing team supporters have been separated into the two different car parks.
