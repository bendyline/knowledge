The **Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan** (PDKI; [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): حیزبی دێموکراتی کوردستانی ئێران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kurdish): *Hizbi Dêmukrati Kurdıstani Êran*, HDKA; [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): حزب دموکرات کردستان ایران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Ḥezb-e Demokrāt-e Kordestān-e Īrān*), also known as the **Kurdish Democratic Party of Iran** (KDPI), is a [militant](310790.md) [leftist](18499.md) [Kurdish](3821779.md) party in [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-Hassaniyan-17][^cite_note-18]

The PDKI operates as an armed group and is based in the [Kurdistan Region of Iraq](3949386.md), with political branch offices in Europe.[^cite_note-WRI-19] It is the oldest Kurdish political party advocating [self-determination](29269.md) for [Kurdish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) in [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-merip-20] It is banned and declared a [terrorist organization](63578.md) by Iran and thus not able to operate openly in the country.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Over the years, the group has shifted its focus from seeking an independent Kurdish [nation-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state) to advocating [autonomy](191145.md)[^cite_note-23] or the implementation of a [federal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_system) in Iran,[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-stratfor-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-APS-16] and it espouses [democratic socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism).[^cite_note-27]

Since the 20th century, PDKI has waged a persistent [guerrilla war](12720.md) against Iran as part of the [Kurdish separatist movement in Iran](35032860.md). This included the [1967 Kurdish revolt in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Kurdish_revolt_in_Iran), [1979–1989 Kurdish insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Kurdish_rebellion_in_Iran), the [1989–1996 insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDPI_insurgency_\(1989%E2%80%9396\)), and [recent clashes in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\)).[^cite_note-WRI-19]

In recent years, the PDKI has played a significant role in supporting [widespread protests against the Iranian regime](81976180.md). The party, alongside other Kurdish opposition groups, has called for regime change and the establishment of a democratic government that guarantees freedom and rights for all citizens. Despite harsh government crackdowns on demonstrators, the PDKI has organized solidarity actions such as strikes and public protests within [Kurdistan Province](448112.md).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] During the protests, the party joined other Kurdish groups to form the [Coalition of Political Forces of Iranian Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_Political_Forces_of_Iranian_Kurdistan).[^cite_note-\:4-30]

## History

### Early years

On 16 August 1945, [Qazi Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazi_Muhammad) founded the party under the name Kurdistan Democratic Party (PDK) in [Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran).[^cite_note-Chaliand1993-31] The organization emerged partly as a response to discriminatory state policies toward non-Persian and non-Shiʿite communities in Iran.[^cite_note-Hassaniyan-17] The party replaced the Society for Kurdish Resurrection ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): *Komalay Ziyanaway Kurd*), which had been founded a few years earlier by some of the future founding members of the PDK.[^cite_note-32]

On 22 January 1946, the [Republic of Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Kurdistan) would be declared, of which Muhammad formally became president. The republic lasted less than a year before the [USSR's occupation of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran) ended and the [Imperial Iranian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Iranian_Army) under [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](20065598.md) conquered the short-lived Kurdish state on 15 December 1946.[^cite_note-McDowall2004-240-33] After the fall of the republic, many PDK members were arrested and executed by the Iranian Government, including Muhammad, nearly ending the party.[^cite_note-Tamadonfar2015-34] The KDPI has been a central actor in the Iranian Kurdish national movement since 1947, particularly after the collapse of the Republic of Kurdistan.[^cite_note-Hassaniyan-17]

### After Qazi Muhammad's death

After the collapse and death of leader Qazi Muhammad the party experienced a long and difficult recovery process.

The collapse led to the PDK becoming reliant on the [Tudeh Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudeh_Party_of_Iran), until the party saw a short revival under the anti-[Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah) administration of [Mohammad Mosaddegh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Mosaddegh) (1951–53), but was again weakened when Shah [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](20065598.md) took over the country in the [1953 Iranian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Iranian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-\:0-35]

The party's first conference after the fall of the Republic of Kurdistan was held in 1955 and was a key step towards restoring its independence and popular support. The conference insisted on the independence of the party from other organizations, effectively ending relations with the Tudeh. [Jalil Gadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalil_Gadani?action=edit\&redlink=1), a long-time party member recalls that, during the conference, the word "Iran" was added, leading to the party being renamed the PDKI.[^cite_note-\:0-35]

In 1958, the PDKI was on the verge of unifying with the Iraqi [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) (KDP), but failed after it was stopped by the [SAVAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAVAK) secret police. The remnants of the PDKI continued to support the KDP until the Shah began aiding the KDP in its fight against the Iraqi government, which had previously overthrown the [Hashemite dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemites). In return for the Shah's aid, the KDP decreased its cooperation with the PDKI.[^cite_note-McDowall2004-249-36]

Later the PDKI reorganised itself, marginalising its pro-KDP leader Abdullah Ishaqi, letting new [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) and [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) members join, and forming the Revolutionary Committee to continue the fight against the Iranian government. The Committee began an unsuccessful [revolt in March 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Kurdish_revolt_in_Iran), which ended after 18 months of fighting and forced the party to retreat into [exile](9855.md) in [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md).[^cite_note-Chaliand1993-31][^cite_note-Tamadonfar2015-34][^cite_note-McDowall2004-249-36]

After reforms by charismatic leader, [Abdulrahman Ghassemlou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahman_Ghassemlou), the PDKI renewed its struggle and fought alongside [Islamist](15012.md) and [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism) movements against the Shah, culminating in the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) of 1979.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-McDowall2004-249-36]

### During the Islamic Republic of Iran

[Khomeini's](26232.md) new [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_republic), however, refused the PDKI demands for human rights and further oppressed the PDKI and other Kurdish parties, even going so far as to proclaim a ‘[holy war](16203.md)’ against the Kurds of Iran. This resulted in the deaths of over 10,000 Kurds.[^cite_note-38] The PDKI continued its activities mainly in exile, hoping to achieve Kurdish national rights within a "[Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) Federal [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) of Iran."[^cite_note-Tamadonfar2015-34]

In 1981, during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), [Ba'athist Iraq](26215470.md) supported the party in the Eastern Kurdish cities of [Nowdesheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowdesheh) and [Qasr-e Shirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasr-e_Shirin) by providing weapons.[^cite_note-mei.edu-39] With that, the Iraqi government under [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein), hoped to instrumentalize the party against the Iranians. The PKDI hopes were to establish an autonomous Kurdish entity in Iran. However, the Iranian forces staged a series of debilitating attacks against the KDPI, leaving them a "marginal military factor during much of the Iran–Iraq War."[^cite_note-mei.edu-39]

#### Vienna assassination

See also: [Abdul Rahman Ghassemlou § Assassination and funerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahman_Ghassemlou#Assassination_and_funerals)

On 13 July 1989, the then PDKI leader Abdulrahman Ghassemlou arrived in [Vienna](55866.md) with his delegation to have talks with Iranian diplomats regarding the terms of reconciliation between the central government in [Tehran](57654.md) and the Kurds. Those were not the only talks planned with Iran held in Vienna. After they entered the conference hall and the talks started, the Iranian "diplomats" took out [automatic firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_firearm) and murdered all of the members of the Kurdish delegation, including Ghassemlou.[^cite_note-40]

#### Mykonos restaurant assassinations

Main article: [Mykonos restaurant assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos_restaurant_assassinations)

[Sadeq Sharafkandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadeq_Sharafkandi)'s murder became an international incident between Germany and Iran. On 17 September 1992, PDKI leaders Sadeq Sharafkandi, Fettah Abduli, Humayûn Erdelan and their translator Nûri Dêkurdi were assassinated at the [Mykonos Greek restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos_restaurant_assassinations) in [Berlin](3354.md), [Germany](11867.md).[^cite_note-41] In the Mykonos trial, the courts found [Kazem Darabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazem_Darabi), an [Iranian](14653.md) national who worked as a grocer in Berlin, and [Lebanese](17771.md) Abbas Rhayel, guilty of murder and sentenced them to life in prison. Two other Lebanese, Youssef Amin and Mohamed Atris, were convicted of being accessories to murder. In its 10 April 1997 ruling, the court issued an international arrest warrant for Iranian intelligence minister [Hojjat al-Islam Ali Fallahian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fallahian)[^cite_note-42] after declaring that the assassination had been ordered by him with knowledge of Ayatollah [Ali Khamenei](385653.md) and President [Ayatollah Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Rafsanjani).[^cite_note-43]

#### 1996 unilateral ceasefire

In 1996, the PDKI had to declare a unilateral ceasefire, after being hard hit by relentless Iranian attacks.[^cite_note-\:2-44]

### Modern era

In 1997, the party's call for abstaining the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Iranian_presidential_election) remained largely ignored by Kurdish citizens in Iran and amid a high turnout in [Kurdistan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Province), a large number voted for [Mohammad Khatami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Khatami).[^cite_note-45]

From 1996-2004, Abdullah Hassanzadeh was the [secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-general) for the PDKI. In 2004 [Mustafa Hijri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Hijri) took charge. During Hijri's time as the secretary-general several members all over the world and in [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md) expressed their dissatisfaction with his work. In the 13th congress in November 2006 party members told Hijri to back down from his position, but he rejected, which ultimately led to led to weeks of intra-party negotiations aimed at avoiding a split and reaching a compromise with him. Hijri rejected all offers and on 1 December 2006 the [Kurdistan Democratic Party (HDK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party_\(Iran\)) was founded after it split from the Hijri's PDKI.

**PDKI TV Channel**

Shortly after the party split the PDKI was fast to establish their own TV Channel called [TISHK TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tishk_TV) while HDK some months later established Kurd Channel. But during the reunification between the two sides in August 2022 the PDKI changed their TV Channel as a part of the reunification to [Kurd Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurd_Channel?action=edit\&redlink=1).

#### 2016 renewing of the armed struggle



PDKI [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga) in 2014.

Main article: [Western Iran clashes (2016–2023)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\))

In 2016, the organization announced it was reviving its armed struggle following the [death of Farinaz Khosravani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad#Islamic_Republic_of_Iran) and subsequent [Mahabad riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad_riots).[^cite_note-iranianimperialism-46] The [Kurdistan Freedom Party](49010325.md) and [Komala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komala_Party_of_Iranian_Kurdistan) declared their support for the announcement - which led to the [Western Iran Clashes in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\)).[^cite_note-\:2-44] In wake of the clashes, secretary-general Hijri, called Iran a '[Shia ISIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_and_the_Islamic_State)'.[^cite_note-47]

In the same year an [Iranian agent](501536.md) planted a bomb near the party's headquarter, the [Democrat Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Castle?action=edit\&redlink=1), which led to the deaths of 6 [HDK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party_\(Iran\)) and KDPI members.

#### 2018 missile attack on the party's headquarter

Main article: [2018 Koy Sanjaq missile strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Koy_Sanjaq_missile_strike)

On 8 September 2018 the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Aerospace Force](5613577.md) launched seven [Fateh-110](4603470.md) missiles at the party's headquarters, the Democrat Castle in [Koya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koy_Sanjaq), [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md), [Iraq](7515928.md) while a HDK meeting was underway. The missiles got a direct hit on where the meeting was taking place at the Democrat Castle killing a total of 18 HDK and KDPI members. 50 HDK/KDPI members were injured, including HDK leaders Xalıd Ezizi and Mustafa Mewlûdi. And all the HDK members that were killed was, Mohammed Hassanpour, Nasrin Hedad, Rahman Piroti, Sohila Qaderi, Braim Zewei, Karim Mahdavi, Hasim Azizi, Osman Osmani, Kak Karsaz, Karim Rasoulzadeh, Peshawar Sayed Umaz, Jamal Akbari and Mansour Akbarpour.&#x20;

#### 2022 attack on Koya



The party's headquarters, the Democrat Castle, in [Koy Sanjaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koy_Sanjaq), [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), 2022. On the right side of the building, there is a mural of party founder and legendary leader [Qazi Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazi_Muhammad).

Main article: [September–October 2022 attacks on Iraqi Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September%E2%80%93October_2022_attacks_on_Iraqi_Kurdistan)

In 2022, the IRGC and the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) launched [ballistic missiles](38025.md) and several [Shahed drones](75153890.md) which attacked a school at Azadi Settlement in the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md). The ballistic missiles nearly hit close to a school, which led to the killing of 17 teachers and parents, and 1 child. After the attacks on the school Iran attacked the headquarters of the [Komala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komala_Party_of_Iranian_Kurdistan) Parties in Zrgwez, [Sulaymaniyah Governorate](752619.md).[^cite_note-48] Similar attacks continued the following days and casualties had increased to 18 deaths and 62 injuries, as of October 4, 2022.[^cite_note-49] On November 14, Iranian airstrikes on Koya and the PDKI's party headquarters continued, killing at least two people and injuring 10 other KDPI members.[^cite_note-50] As a result of the attacks 72 civilians and KDPI members were injured and 37 civilians and KDPI members were killed. Further [Iranian](14653.md) missile strikes on 21 November 2022 destroyed more houses in [Koy Sanjaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koy_Sanjaq).[^cite_note-51]

#### Reunification

Both groups, the PDKI and HDK, reunited on August 21, 2022 and resumed their political and military activities under the name of the PDKI.[^cite_note-52]

#### New leading team

The party's leadership is the Executive Board. The board consists of 12 members and is being led by Mustafa Hijri.[^cite_note-53]

#### 2026 Kurdish–Iranian crisis

Further information: [2026 Kurdish–Iranian crisis](82575260.md)

The PDKI expressed support for the [nationwide protests](81976180.md) that erupted in Iran in late 2025. The protests, which initially focused on economic grievances, later expanded to include broader opposition to the Islamic Republic’s leadership.[^cite_note-54] The PDKI stated its solidarity with the protest movement and condemned the government’s response. According to statements published on the party’s official website, the PDKI also called for international protection for demonstrators, particularly in Iran’s Kurdish regions, citing reports of lethal force used by security forces.[^cite_note-55] The PDKI participated in a joint meeting with other Iranian Kurdish political parties to discuss the ongoing protests and the broader political situation in Iran and the Kurdistan region. During the meeting, Kurdish opposition groups reiterated their support for the protest movement and emphasized the importance of political coordination and cooperation in response to the crisis.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] On January 11, PDKI representative Amir Babakhani called for the fall of Iran's regime in favor of a free and democratic country, and the party welcomed international condemnations of the crackdown on protests.[^cite_note-58]

On 22 February 2026, following the protests, the PDKI joined other Iranian Kurdish parties in creating the [Coalition of Political Forces of Iranian Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_Political_Forces_of_Iranian_Kurdistan). On 3 March 2026, [United States](3434750.md) President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) spoke to KDPI Secretary-General [Mustafa Hijri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Hijri), in an attempt to instigate a U.S.– and [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga)–backed ground offensive in western Iran by Iranian Kurdish opposition forces amid the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-59]

## Military wing



PDKI Peshmerga in 2013

The military wing of the PDKI are called Peshmerga, like the [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga) of the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md), or PDKI Peshmerga. Hyeran Jo of [Texas A\&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_University) classifies the KDPI fighters as "compliant rebels", i.e. rebels that kill fewer than 100 people and refrain from killing for more than half of their operating years.[^cite_note-\:1-60]

### Ethics

According to Jo, in order to gain domestic and international legitimacy, the KDPI denounces violence against civilians, claiming commitment to the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) and [Geneva Convention Article 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions#Common_Article_3_relating_to_non-international_armed_conflict), and as of 2007 is one of the signatories to the [Geneva Call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Call)'s ban on anti-personnel mines.[^cite_note-\:1-60]

## PDKI congresses

The PDKI has held eighteen congresses. These occurred in 1945, 1964, 1971, 1980, 1982, 1984, 1985, 1988, 1992, 1995, 1997, 2000, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2018, **2022 (reunification)** and 2025.[^cite_note-61]

During the 20th Congress of the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International), held at the [headquarters of the United Nations](188955.md) in [New York City](645042.md) (9–11 September 1996), the PDKI was given the status of *observer member*. In 2005, the PDKI's membership was elevated to *consultative member* status.
