**Basel**[^cite_note-2] is a city in northwestern [Switzerland](26748.md) on the river [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), at the transition from the [High Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Rhine) to the [Upper Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Rhine).[^cite_note-bs.ch-3] Basel is [Switzerland's third most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Switzerland) (after [Zurich](40334603.md) and [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva)), with 177,595 inhabitants within the city municipality limits.[^cite_note-4]

Basel is commonly considered to be the cultural capital of Switzerland[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] and the city's [museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museums_in_Basel) include the [Kunstmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstmuseum_Basel), which was the first collection of art accessible to the public in the world (1661) and the largest museum of [art in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_art), the [Fondation Beyeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fondation_Beyeler) (located in [Riehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riehen)), the [Museum Tinguely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Tinguely) and the [Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art_\(Basel\)), which was the first public museum of contemporary art in Europe.[^cite_note-7] Forty museums are spread throughout the canton, making Basel one of the largest cultural centres in relation to its size and population in Europe.[^cite_note-8] It is the hometown of the [Art Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Basel) international art fair, showcasing modern and contemporary works from leading galleries and attracting top collectors, artists, and enthusiasts globally.

The [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel), Switzerland's oldest university (founded in 1460),[^cite_note-9] and the city's centuries-long commitment to [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism), have made Basel a safe haven at times of political unrest in other parts of Europe for such notable people as [Erasmus of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_of_Rotterdam),[^cite_note-10] the [Holbein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Holbein_the_Younger) family, [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche),[^cite_note-11] [Carl Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jung), and in the 20th century also [Hermann Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Hesse)[^cite_note-12] and [Karl Jaspers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Jaspers).[^cite_note-13]

Basel was the seat of a [Prince-Bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Basel) starting in the 11th century, and joined the [Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) in 1501.[^cite_note-14] The city has been a commercial hub and an important cultural centre since the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance),[^cite_note-15] and has emerged as a centre for the [chemical](58721.md) and [pharmaceutical industries](560876.md) in the 20th century.[^cite_note-16] In 1897, Basel was chosen by [Theodor Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl) as the location for the first [World Zionist Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Zionist_Congress), and altogether the congress was held there ten times over a time span of 50 years, more than in any other location.[^cite_note-17] The city is also home to the world headquarters of the [Bank for International Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_for_International_Settlements).[^cite_note-18]

Other notable institutions in Basel include the [Basel Committee on Banking Supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Committee_on_Banking_Supervision), which published banking standards such as [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III);[^cite_note-19] [Art Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Basel);[^cite_note-20] and [FC Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Basel).[^cite_note-21]

Basel is Switzerland's main centre for the pharmaceutical industry, hosting both [Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis) and [Roche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche).[^cite_note-22] In 1938, the world-renowned chemist [Albert Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Hofmann) discovered [LSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSD) in Basel, where he spent most of his life. The discovery is celebrated internationally as [Bicycle Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_Day_\(psychedelic_holiday\)). Other influential and renowned figures such as [Roger Federer](262376.md), [Paracelsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus), [Matthäus Merian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matth%C3%A4us_Merian_the_Elder), [Michel von Tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_von_Tell) and [Stephan Remmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephan_Remmler) are closely associated with the city or were born there. In 1734, the so-called "[Basel Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)" was solved in the city, which is regarded as one of the most important achievements in mathematics.

Basel was ranked the tenth most liveable city in the world by [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_\(consulting_firm\)) in 2019.[^cite_note-23]

## Name

The name of Basel is first recorded as *Basilia* in the 3rd century (237/238), at the time referring to the [Roman castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_oppidum). This name is mostly interpreted as deriving from the personal name *Basilius*, from a toponym *villa Basilia* ("[estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_villa) of Basilius") or similar.[^cite_note-24]

Another suggestion derives it from a name *Basilia* attested in northern France as a development of *basilica*, the term for a public or church building (as in [Bazeilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazeilles)), but all of these names reference early church buildings of the 4th or 5th century and cannot be adduced for the 3rd century attestation of *Basilia*.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Kristol-26]

By popular etymology, or simple assonance, the [basilisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilisk) becomes closely associated with the city, used as [heraldic supporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_supporter) from 1448, represented on coins minted by the city, and frequently found in ornaments.

The [Middle French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_French) form *Basle* was adopted into English, but this form has fallen gradually out of use although it continues to be used with the modern French pronunciation in some sections of [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English),[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] at least informally. Currently, the spelling *Basel* is most often used, to match the official German spelling.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] In French *Basle* was still in use in the 18th century, but was gradually replaced by the modern French spelling *Bâle*. In Icelandic, the city is recorded as *Buslaraborg* in the 12th century itinerary *[Leiðarvísir og borgarskipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei%C3%B0arv%C3%ADsir_og_borgarskipan)*.

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Basel).

### Early history



The [Roman theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_theatre_\(structure\)) in Augusta Raurica, one of the most important Roman archaeological sites in Switzerland

There are traces of a settlement at the nearby [Rhine knee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_knee) from the early [La Tène period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_period) (5th century BC). In the 2nd century BC, there was a village of the [Raurici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raurici) at the site of *Basel-Gasfabrik* (to the northwest of the Old City, and likely identical with the town of *Arialbinnum* that was mentioned on the *[Tabula Peutingeriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabula_Peutingeriana)*).[^cite_note-32] The unfortified settlement was abandoned in the 1st century BC in favour of an *[oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_oppidum)* on the site of [Basel Minster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Minster), probably in reaction to the [Roman invasion of Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallic_War).

In [Roman Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Gaul), [Augusta Raurica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Raurica) was established some 20 km (12 mi) from Basel as the regional administrative centre, while a *[castrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra)* (fortified camp) was built on the site of the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_nations) *[oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppidum)*. In AD 83, the area was incorporated into the [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Germania Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_Superior). The [Roman Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate) [Munatius Plancus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Munatius_Plancus) is known as the traditional founder of Basel since the Renaissance.[^cite_note-Werthmüller-2013-33] Roman control over the area deteriorated in the 3rd century, and Basel became an outpost of the *[Provincia Maxima Sequanorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincia_Maxima_Sequanorum)* formed by [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian). *[Basilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_oppidum)* is first named by the [Ammianus Marcellinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus) in his Res Gestae[^cite_note-Werthmüller-2013-33] as part of the Roman military fortifications along the Rhine in the late 4th century.

The Germanic confederation of the [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) attempted to cross the Rhine several times in the 4th century, but were repelled; one such event was the [Battle of Solicinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Solicinium) (368). However, in the great invasion of AD 406, the Alemanni appear to have crossed the Rhine a final time, conquering and then settling what is today [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace) and a large part of the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau).

The [Duchy of Alemannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Alemannia) fell under [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francia) rule in the 6th century. The Alemannic and [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) settlement of Basel gradually grew around the old Roman castle in the 6th and 7th century. It appears that Basel surpassed the ancient regional capital of [Augusta Raurica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Raurica) by the 7th century; based on the evidence of a gold *[tremissis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremissis)* (a small gold coin with the value of a third of a *[solidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidus_\(coin\))*) with the inscription *Basilia fit*, Basel seems to have minted its own coins in the 7th century.[^cite_note-34]

Basel at this time was part of the [Archdiocese of Besançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Besan%C3%A7on). A separate [bishopric of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric_of_Basel), replacing the ancient bishopric of [Augusta Raurica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Raurica), was established in the 8th century. Under bishop [Haito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haito) (r. 806–823), the first cathedral was built on the site of the Roman castle[^cite_note-35] (replaced by a Romanesque structure consecrated in 1019).

At the partition of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire) through the [Treaty of Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Verdun) in 843, Basel was first given to [West Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Francia) and became its German exclave.[^cite_note-Werthmüller-2013-33] It passed to [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia) with the [Treaty of Meerssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Meerssen) of 870. Basel was destroyed by the [Magyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_invasion) in 917.[^cite_note-Werthmüller-2013-33] The rebuilt town became part of [Upper Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Burgundy), and as such was incorporated into the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1032.

### Prince-Bishopric of Basel

Main article: [Prince-Bishopric of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Basel)



[Basel Minster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Minster), built between 1019 and 1500

From the donation by [Rudolph III of Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_III_of_Burgundy)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood2006285–286,_313-36] of the [Moutier-Grandval Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moutier-Grandval_Abbey) and all its possessions to Bishop [Adalbero II of Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalbero_II_of_Metz) in 999 until the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), Basel was ruled by [Prince-Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Basel).[^cite_note-37]

In 1019, the construction of the [cathedral of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Minster) (known locally as the *Münster*) began under [Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).[^cite_note-38]

In the 11th to 12th centuries, Basel gradually acquired the characteristics of a medieval [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City). The main market place is first mentioned in 1091. The first city walls were constructed around 1100 (with improvements made in the mid-13th and in the late 14th century). A city council of nobles and burghers is recorded for 1185, and the first [mayor](101146.md), Heinrich Steinlin of Murbach, for 1253. The first bridge across the Rhine was built in 1225 under bishop [Heinrich von Thun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Thun?action=edit\&redlink=1) (at the location of the modern [Middle Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Bridge,_Basel)), and from this time the settlement of *Kleinbasel* gradually formed around the bridgehead on the far river bank. The bridge was largely funded by Basel's Jewish community who had settled there a century earlier.[^cite_note-habicht-39] For many centuries to come Basel possessed the only permanent bridge over the river "between [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance) and the sea". The first city [craft guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_guild) was the [furriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furriers), established in 1226. A total of about fifteen guilds were established in the course of the 13th century, reflecting the increasing economic prosperity of the city.[^cite_note-habicht-39] The [Crusade of 1267](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusade_of_1267) set out from Basel.

Political conflicts between the bishops and the burghers began in the mid-13th century and continued throughout the 14th century. By the late 14th century, the city was for all practical purposes independent although it continued to nominally pledge fealty to the bishops. The [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) attempted to gain control over the city. This was not successful, but it caused a political split among the burghers of Basel into a pro-Habsburg faction, known as *Sterner*, and an anti-Habsburg faction, the *Psitticher*.

The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) reached Basel in 1348. The [Jews were blamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Jews_during_the_Black_Death), and an estimated 50 to 70 Jews were executed by burning on 16 January 1349 in what has become known as the [Basel massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_massacre).[^cite_note-habicht-39] The [Basel earthquake of 1356](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1356_Basel_earthquake) destroyed much of the city along with a [number of castles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6tteln_Castle) in the vicinity.

&#x20;A riot on 26 February 1376, known as *Böse Fasnacht*, led to the killing of a number of men of [Leopold III, Duke of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_III,_Duke_of_Austria). This was seen as a serious [breach of the peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breach_of_the_peace), and the city council blamed "foreign ruffians" for this and executed twelve alleged perpetrators. Leopold nevertheless had the city placed under [imperial ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_ban), and in a treaty of 9 July, Basel was given a heavy fine and was placed under Habsburg control. To free itself from Habsburg hegemony, Basel joined the [Swabian League of Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_League_of_Cities) in 1385, and many knights of the pro-Habsburg faction, along with duke Leopold himself, were killed in the [Battle of Sempach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sempach) the following year. A formal treaty with Habsburg was made in 1393.

Basel had gained its de facto independence from both the bishop and from the Habsburgs and was free to pursue its own policy of territorial expansion, beginning around 1400.

&#x20;The unique representation of a bishops' [crozier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crozier) as the heraldic charge in the [coat of arms of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Basel) first appears in the form of a gilded wooden staff in the 12th century. It is of unknown origin or significance (beyond its obvious status of bishop's crozier), but it is assumed to have represented a relic, possibly attributed to [Saint Germanus of Granfelden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanus_of_Granfelden).[^cite_note-40] This staff (known as *Baselstab*) became a symbol representing the Basel diocese, depicted in bishops' seals of the late medieval period. It is represented in a heraldic context in the early 14th century, not yet as a heraldic charge but as a kind of heraldic achievement flanked by the heraldic shields of the bishop. The staff is also represented in the bishops's seals of the period. The use of the *Baselstab* in black as the coat of arms of the city was introduced in 1385. From this time, the *Baselstab* in red represented the bishop, and the same charge in black represented the city. The [blazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blazon) of the municipal coat of arms is *In Silber ein schwarzer Baselstab* (Argent, a staff of Basel sable). In 1400, Basel was able to purchase the towns of [Liestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liestal), [Homburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homburg,_Switzerland) and [Waldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldenburg,_Switzerland) with its surrounding territory.[^cite_note-Landolt-1998-41]



1493 woodcut of Basel, from the [Nuremberg Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle)

In 1412 (or earlier), the well-known [Gasthof zum Goldenen Sternen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasthof_zum_Goldenen_Sternen) was established. Basel became the focal point of western Christendom during the 15th century [Council of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Of_Basel) (1431–1449), including the 1439 election of [antipope Felix V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipope_Felix_V). In 1459, [Pope Pius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_II) endowed the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel), where such notables as [Erasmus of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_of_Rotterdam) and [Paracelsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus) later taught. At the same time the new craft of [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press) was [introduced to Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_spread_of_the_printing_press#Switzerland) by apprentices of [Johann Gutenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Gutenberg). In 1461, the land around [Farnsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsburg_Castle) became a part of Basel.[^cite_note-Landolt-1998-41]

The [Schwabe publishing house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwabe_publishing_house) was founded in 1488 by [Johannes Petri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Petri_\(printer\)) and is the oldest publishing house still in business. [Johann Froben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Froben) also operated his printing house in Basel and was notable for publishing works by Erasmus.[^cite_note-42] In 1495, Basel was incorporated into the [Upper Rhenish Imperial Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Rhenish_Circle); the Bishop of Basel was added to the Bench of the Ecclesiastical Princes of the [Imperial Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Diet_\(Holy_Roman_Empire\)). In 1500 the construction of the [Basel Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_M%C3%BCnster) was finished.

### As a member state in the Swiss Confederacy

Main article: [Canton of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Basel)



Map of Basel in 1642, [engraved](60125.md) by [Matthäus Merian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matth%C3%A4us_Merian), oriented with SW at the top and NE at the bottom

The city had remained neutral through the [Swabian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_War) of 1499 despite being plundered by soldiers on both sides. The [Treaty of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Basel_\(1499\)) ended the war and granted the Swiss confederates exemptions from the emperor Maximillian's taxes and jurisdictions, separating Switzerland *de facto* from the Holy Roman Empire.[^cite_note-Rappard-43]

On 9 June 1501, Basel joined the Swiss Confederation as its [eleventh canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Basel).[^cite_note-44] It was the only canton that was asked to join, not the other way round. Basel had a strategic location, good relations with [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) and [Mulhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulhouse), and control of the corn imports from Alsace, whereas the Swiss lands were becoming overpopulated and had few resources. A provision of the Charter accepting Basel required that in conflicts among the other cantons it was to stay neutral and offer its services for mediation.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-45][^cite_note-Bonjour-46]

In 1503, the new bishop [Christoph von Utenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_von_Utenheim) refused to give Basel a new constitution; whereupon, to show its power, the city began to build a new city hall.[^cite_note-habicht-39]

In 1529, the city became Protestant under [Oecolampadius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Oecolampadius) and the bishop's seat was moved to [Porrentruy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porrentruy). The bishop's crook was however retained as the city's coat of arms. For centuries to come, a handful of wealthy families collectively referred to as the ["Daig"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daig_\(Switzerland\)) played a pivotal role in city affairs as they gradually established themselves as a *de facto* [city aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy).

The first edition of *Christianae religionis institutio* (*[Institutes of the Christian Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutes_of_the_Christian_Religion)* – [John Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Calvin)'s great exposition of [Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism) doctrine) was published at Basel in March 1536.[^cite_note-47]

In 1544, Johann von Brugge, a rich Dutch Protestant refugee, was given citizenship and lived respectably until his death in 1556, then buried with honors. His body was exhumed and burnt at the stake in 1559 after it was discovered that he was the [Anabaptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabaptism) [David Joris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Joris).[^cite_note-habicht-39]

In 1543, *[De humani corporis fabrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_humani_corporis_fabrica)*, the first book on human anatomy, was published and printed in Basel by [Andreas Vesalius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Vesalius) (1514–1564).[^cite_note-48]

There are indications that [Joachim Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Meyer), author of the influential 16th-century [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts) text *Kunst des Fechten* ("The Art of Fencing"), came from Basel. In 1661 the *[Amerbaschsches Kabinett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerbach_Cabinet),* a vast collection of exotic artifacts, coins, medals and books was purchased by Basel.[^cite_note-49] It was to become to the first public museum of art.[^cite_note-50] Its collection became the core of the later [Basel Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstmuseum_Basel).

The Bernoulli family, which included important 17th- and 18th-century mathematicians such as [Jakob Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Bernoulli), [Johann Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Bernoulli) and [Daniel Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Bernoulli), were from Basel. The 18th-century mathematician [Leonhard Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler) was born in Basel and studied under Johann Bernoulli.

### Modern history

In 1792, the [Republic of Rauracia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauracian_Republic), a revolutionary [French client republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_client_republic), was created. It lasted until 1793.[^cite_note-51] After three years of political agitation and a short civil war in 1833 the disadvantaged countryside seceded from the Canton of Basel, forming the half canton of [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft).[^cite_note-52] Between 1861 and 1878 the city walls were [slighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slighting).[^cite_note-53]

On 3 July 1874, Switzerland's first zoo, the [Zoo Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo_Basel), opened its doors in the south of the city towards [Binningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binningen,_Switzerland).



First [World Zionist Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Zionist_Congress) in Basel, 1897 (Stadtcasino)

In 1897 the first [World Zionist Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Zionist_Congress) was held in Basel. Altogether the World Zionist Congress was held in Basel ten times, more than in any other city in the world.[^cite_note-54]

On 16 November 1938, the psychedelic drug [LSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSD) was first synthesized by Swiss chemist [Albert Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Hofmann) at [Sandoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandoz) Laboratories in Basel.

In 1967, the population of [Basel voted in favor of buying three works of art by painter Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Basel_Picasso_paintings_purchase_referendum) which were at risk of being sold and taken out of the local museum of art, due to a financial crisis on the part of the owner's family. Therefore, Basel became the first city in the world where the population of a political community democratically decided to acquire works of art for a public institution. [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso) was so moved by the gesture that he subsequently gifted the city with an additional three paintings.[^cite_note-55]

### Basel as a historical, international meeting place



Image of Basel in the*Topographie der Eidgenossenschaft* (Topography of the \[Swiss] Confederation) from 1761

Basel has often been the site of peace negotiations and other international meetings. The [Treaty of Basel (1499)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Basel_\(1499\)) ended the [Swabian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_War).[^cite_note-56] Two years later Basel joined the [Swiss Confederation](26748.md).[^cite_note-57] The [Peace of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Basel) in 1795 between the [French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic) and Prussia and Spain ended the [First Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Coalition) against France during the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars).[^cite_note-58] In more recent times, the [World Zionist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Zionist_Organization) held its first congress in Basel from 29 August through 31 August 1897. Because of the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars), the (Socialist) [Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International) held an extraordinary congress at Basel in 1912.[^cite_note-59] In 1989, the [Basel Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Convention) was opened for signature with the aim of preventing the export of [hazardous waste](38031.md) from wealthy to [developing nations](78449.md) for disposal.[^cite_note-60]

## Geography and climate

### Location

Basel is located in [Northwestern Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Switzerland) and is commonly considered to be the capital of that region. It is the centre of a trinational metropolitan region spanning Switzerland, France and Germany, with approximately 900,000 inhabitants in its wider urban area.[^cite_note-bs.ch-3] It is close to the point where the Swiss, French and German borders meet, and Basel also has suburbs in France and Germany.[^cite_note-61] As of 2016, the Swiss Basel agglomeration was the third-largest in Switzerland, with a population of 541,000[^cite_note-Agglomeration-62] in 74 municipalities in Switzerland (municipal count as of 2018).[^cite_note-63] The [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), called the [Trinational Eurodistrict of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinational_Eurodistrict_of_Basel) (TEB), consists of 62 suburban communes including municipalities in neighboring countries, and counted 829,000 inhabitants in 2007.[^cite_note-64]

### Topography



Basel (in the upper left corner) as seen from Bettingen (television tower St. Chrischona) facing France

Basel has an area, as of 2009, of 23.91 square kilometers (9.23 sq mi). Of this area, 0.95 km<sup>2</sup> (0.37 sq mi) or 4.0% is used for agricultural purposes, while 0.88 km<sup>2</sup> (0.34 sq mi) or 3.7% is forested. Of the rest of the land, 20.67 km<sup>2</sup> (7.98 sq mi) or 86.4% is settled (buildings or roads), 1.45 km<sup>2</sup> (0.56 sq mi) or 6.1% is either rivers or lakes.[^cite_note-BFS_land-65]

Of the built up area, industrial buildings made up 10.2% of the total area while housing and buildings made up 40.7% and transportation infrastructure made up 24.0%. Power and water infrastructure as well as other special developed areas made up 2.7% of the area while parks, green belts and sports fields made up 8.9%. Out of the forested land, all of the forested land area is covered with heavy forests. Of the agricultural land, 2.5% is used for growing crops and 1.3% is pastures. All the water in the municipality is flowing water.[^cite_note-BFS_land-65] The topography of the region is notably flat, as Basel is situated in a valley basin. The Rhine, one of Europe's largest and most significant rivers, flows directly through the heart of the city, dividing it into Grossbasel on the left bank and Kleinbasel on the right.

### Climate

Basel features a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*),[^cite_note-66] with cool, [overcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcast) winters and warm, humid summers.

The city averages 118.2 days of rain or snow annually and on average receives 842 mm (33.1 in) of [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)). The wettest month is May during which time Basel receives an average of 98 mm (3.9 in) of rain. The month with the most days of precipitation is also May, with an average of 11.7 days. The driest month of the year is February with an average of 45 mm (1.8 in) of precipitation over 8.4 days.[^cite_note-ClimateBB-67]

## Politics

The city of Basel functions as the capital of the [Swiss cantonhistorically](180752.md) called as [half-canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-canton) or nowadays as "cantons with half of a cantonal vote"-of [Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Stadt).

### Canton

The canton Basel-Stadt consists of three municipalities: [Riehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riehen), [Bettingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettingen), and the city Basel itself. The political structure and agencies of the city and the canton are identical.

The official language of Basel is [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language),[^cite_note-71] but the main spoken language is [Basel German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_German), the local variant of the [Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German) dialect.[^cite_note-72]

### Government

The city's and canton's executive, the Executive Council (*Regierungsrat*), consists of seven members for a mandate period of 4 years. They are elected by any inhabitant valid to vote on the same day as the parliament, but by means of a system of [Majorz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorz?action=edit\&redlink=1), and operates as a [collegiate authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_authority?action=edit\&redlink=1). The president (German: *Regierungspräsident(in)*) is elected as such by a public election, while the heads of the other departments are appointed by the collegiate. The current president is [Beat Jans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Jans). The executive body holds its meetings in the red [Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Town_Hall) (German: *Rathaus*) on the central *Marktplatz*. The building was built in 1504–14.

As of 2021, Basel's Executive Council is made up of three representatives of the SP ([Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland)) including the president, two LDP ([Liberal-Demokratische Partei of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal-Demokratische_Partei_of_Basel?action=edit\&redlink=1)), and one member each of [Green Liberals (glp)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland), and CVP ([Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland)).[^cite_note-Regrat-73] The last election was held on 25 October and 29 November 2020 and four new members have been elected.[^cite_note-Elect20-74]

The city's and canton's parliament, the [Grand Council of Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Council_of_Basel-Stadt) (**Grosser Rat**), consists of 100 seats, with members (called in German: *Grossrat/Grossrätin*) elected every 4 years. The sessions of the Grand Council are public. Unlike the members of the Executive Council, the members of the Grand Council are not politicians by profession, but they are paid a fee based on their attendance. Any resident of Basel allowed to vote can be elected as a member of the parliament. The delegates are elected by means of a system of [Proporz](23298.md).[^cite_note-76] The legislative body holds its meetings in the red Town Hall (*Rathaus*).

The last election was held on 25 October 2020 for the mandate period (*Legislatur*) of 2021–2025.[^cite_note-Elect20-74] As of 1 February 2021, the Grand Council consist of 30 (−5) members of the [Social Democratic Party (SP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland), 18 (+5) *Grün-Alternatives Bündnis (GAB)* (a collaboration of the [Green Party (GPS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Switzerland), its junior party, and [Basels starke Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basels_starke_Alternative?action=edit\&redlink=1) (BastA!)), 14 (−1) [Liberal-Demokratische Partei (LDP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal-Demokratische_Partei_of_Basel?action=edit\&redlink=1), 11 (−4) members of the [Swiss People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) (SVP), 8 (+5) [Green Liberal Party (glp)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland), 7 (−3) [The Liberals (FDP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDP.The_Liberals), 7 (-) [Christian Democratic People's Party (CVP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland), 3 (+2) [Evangelical People's Party (EVP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_People's_Party_of_Switzerland), and one each representative of the *Aktive Bettingen (AB)* and *Volks-Aktion gegen zuviele Ausländer und Asylanten in unserer Heimat* (VA).[^cite_note-77]

The left parties missed an absolute majority by two seats.

The canton of Basel (slightly more than the city itself) has a population (As of 2021) of 201,971, of whom 36.9% are resident foreign nationals.[^cite_note-89]

Over the 10 years of 1999–2009 the population has changed at a rate of −0.3%. It has changed at a rate of 3.2% due to migration and at a rate of −3% due to births and deaths.[^cite_note-SFSO-90]

Of the population in the municipality 58,560 or about 35.2% were born in Basel and lived there in 2000. There were 1,396 or 0.8% who were born in the same canton, while 44,874 or 26.9% were born somewhere else in Switzerland, and 53,774 or 32.3% were born outside of Switzerland.[^cite_note-STAT2000-91]

In 2008 there were 898 live births to Swiss citizens and 621 births to non-Swiss citizens, and in same time span there were 1,732 deaths of Swiss citizens and 175 non-Swiss citizen deaths. Ignoring immigration and emigration, the population of Swiss citizens decreased by 834 while the foreign population increased by 446. There were 207 Swiss men and 271 Swiss women who emigrated from Switzerland. At the same time, there were 1756 non-Swiss men and 1655 non-Swiss women who immigrated from another country to Switzerland. The total Swiss population change in 2008 (from all sources, including moves across municipal borders) was an increase of 278 and the non-Swiss population increased by 1138 people. This represents a [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth_rate) of 0.9%.[^cite_note-HDS_superweb-92]

As of 2000, there were 70,502 people who were single and never married in the municipality. There were 70,517 married individuals, 12,435 widows or widowers, and 13,104 individuals who are divorced.[^cite_note-STAT2000-91]

As of 2000 the average number of residents per living room was 0.59 which is about equal to the cantonal average of 0.58 per room.[^cite_note-SFSO-90] In this case, a room is defined as space of a housing unit of at least 4 m<sup>2</sup> (43 sq ft) as normal bedrooms, dining rooms, living rooms, kitchens and habitable cellars and attics.[^cite_note-EurostatSA1-93]<sup>: 18v </sup> About 10.5% of the total households were owner occupied, or in other words did not pay rent (though they may have a [mortgage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage_loan) or a [rent-to-own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-to-own) agreement).[^cite_note-EurostatSA1-93]<sup>: 17 </sup> As of 2000, there were 86,371 private households in the municipality, and an average of 1.8 persons per household.[^cite_note-SFSO-90] There were 44,469 households that consist of only one person and 2,842 households with five or more people. Out of a total of 88,646 households that answered this question, 50.2% were households made up of just one person and there were 451 adults who lived with their parents. Of the rest of the households, there are 20,472 married couples without children, 14,554 married couples with children There were 4,318 single parents with a child or children. There were 2,107 households that were made up of unrelated people and 2,275 households that were made up of some sort of institution or another collective housing.[^cite_note-STAT2000-91]

In 2000 there were 5,747 single family homes (or 30.8% of the total) out of a total of 18,631 inhabited buildings. There were 7,642 multi-family buildings (41.0%), along with 4,093 multi-purpose buildings that were mostly used for housing (22.0%) and 1,149 other use buildings (commercial or industrial) that also had some housing (6.2%). Of the single family homes 1090 were built before 1919, while 65 were built between 1990 and 2000. The greatest number of single family homes (3,474) were built between 1919 and 1945.[^cite_note-Housing-94]

In 2000 there were 96,640 apartments in the municipality. The most common apartment size was 3 rooms of which there were 35,958. There were 11,957 single room apartments and 9,702 apartments with five or more rooms. Of these apartments, a total of 84,675 apartments (87.6% of the total) were permanently occupied, while 7,916 apartments (8.2%) were seasonally occupied and 4,049 apartments (4.2%) were empty.[^cite_note-Housing-94] As of 2009, the construction rate of new housing units was 2.6 new units per 1000 residents.[^cite_note-SFSO-90]

As of 2003 the average price to rent an average apartment in Basel was 1118.60 [Swiss francs](361362.md) (CHF) per month (US\$890, £500, €720 approx. exchange rate from 2003). The average rate for a one-room apartment was 602.27 CHF (US\$480, £270, €390), a two-room apartment was about 846.52 CHF (US\$680, £380, €540), a three-room apartment was about 1054.14 CHF (US\$840, £470, €670) and a six or more room apartment cost an average of 2185.24 CHF (US\$1750, £980, €1400). The average apartment price in Basel was 100.2% of the national average of 1116 CHF.[^cite_note-BFS_rent-95] The vacancy rate for the municipality, in 2010, was 0.74%.[^cite_note-SFSO-90]

### Transport

#### Port

See also: [Merchant Marine of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Marine_of_Switzerland)



The Rhine in Basel as Switzerland's gateway to the sea

Basel has Switzerland's only cargo port, through which goods pass along the navigable stretches of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) and connect to ocean-going ships at the [port of Rotterdam](4410985.md).

#### Air transport

[EuroAirport Basel Mulhouse Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAirport_Basel_Mulhouse_Freiburg) is operated jointly by two countries, France and Switzerland, although the airport is located completely on French soil. The airport itself is split into two architecturally independent sectors, one half serving the French side and the other half serving the Swiss side; prior to [Schengen](179592.md) there was an immigration inspection point at the middle of the airport so that people could "emigrate" to the other side of the airport.

#### Railways



[Basel SBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_SBB_railway_station) station, built in 1907

Basel was the first Swiss city with a railway station and it has long held an important place as a rail hub.[^cite_note-98] Three main railway stations—those of the [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Switzerland), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_France) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Germany) networks—lie within the city, although the Swiss ([Basel SBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_SBB_railway_station)) and French ([Bâle SNCF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_SBB_railway_station)) stations are actually in the same complex, separated by customs and immigration facilities. The German [Basel Badischer Bahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Badischer_Bahnhof) (abbreviated to *Basel Bad Bf*) is on the northern side of the city. Two other, smaller stations within the city limits are [Basel Dreispitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Dreispitz_railway_station) and [Basel St. Johann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_St._Johann_railway_station). All stations are connected to the local [tram and bus network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basler_Verkehrsbetriebe) (BVB). Basel's regional rail services are supplied by the [Basel S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_S-Bahn), which links the city with destinations in Switzerland, France and Germany.

Most long-distance trains call at Basel SBB station, such as [EC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity), [TGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGV_Lyria), [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_Express), [IC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity_\(Switzerland\)) and [IR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterRegio#Switzerland) services. The ICE also calls at Basel Bad Bf. The new [high-speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) ICE railway line from [Karlsruhe to Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe%E2%80%93Basel_high-speed_railway) was completed in 2008 while phase I of the [TGV Rhine-Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGV_Rhin-Rh%C3%B4ne) line, opened in December 2011, has reduced travel time from Basel to Paris to about 3 hours.[^cite_note-99]

The largest goods railway complex of the country[^cite_note-100] is located just outside the city, spanning the [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Switzerland) of [Muttenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muttenz) and [Pratteln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratteln).

#### Roads

Basel is located on the [A3 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_motorway_\(Switzerland\)).

Within the city limits, five bridges connect Greater and Lesser Basel (downstream):

- Schwarzwaldbrücke (built 1972)

- Wettsteinbrücke (current structure built 1998, original bridge built 1879)

- [Mittlere Rheinbrücke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittlere_Rheinbr%C3%BCcke) (lit. 'Middle Rhine bridge', current structure built 1905, original bridge built 1225 as the first bridge to cross the Rhine)

- Johanniterbrücke (built 1967)

- Dreirosenbrücke (built 2004, original bridge built 1935)

#### Ferries

A somewhat anachronistic yet still widely used system of [reaction ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_ferry) boats links the two shores. There are four ferries, each situated approximately midway between two bridges. Each is attached by a cable to a block that rides along another cable spanning the river at a height of 20 to 30 metres (66 to 98 feet). To cross the river, the ferryman orients the boat around 45° from the current so that the current pushes the boat across the river. This form of transportation is therefore completely hydraulically driven, requiring no outside energy source. #### Public transport



Basel tram network

Basel has an extensive public transportation network serving the city and connecting to surrounding suburbs, including a large [tram network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Trams). Today, Basel has the largest tramway in terms of kilometers of rail tracks in Switzerland.[^cite_note-Appenzeller-101] Historically, only [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) had a larger one at some point.[^cite_note-Appenzeller-101]

The green-colored local [trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) and buses are operated by the [Basler Verkehrs-Betriebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basler_Verkehrs-Betriebe) (BVB). The yellow-colored buses and trams are operated by the [Baselland Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baselland_Transport) (BLT), and connect areas in the nearby half-canton of Baselland to central Basel. The BVB also shares commuter bus lines in cooperation with transit authorities in the neighboring Alsace region in France and [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden) region in Germany. The [Basel Regional S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Regional_S-Bahn), the commuter rail network connecting to suburbs surrounding the city, is jointly operated by [SBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schweizerische_Bundesbahnen), [SNCF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNCF) and [DB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn).

#### Border crossings

Basel is located at the meeting point of France, Germany, and Switzerland; because it sits on the Swiss national border and is beyond the [Jura Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Mountains), many within the [Swiss military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_military) reportedly believe that the city is indefensible during wartime.[^cite_note-mcphee19831031-102] It has numerous road and rail crossings between Switzerland and the other two countries. With Switzerland joining the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) on 12 December 2008, immigration checks were no longer carried out at the crossings. However, Switzerland did not join the [European Union Customs Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Customs_Union) (though it did join the [EU Single Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Single_Market)) and customs checks are still conducted at or near the crossings.

### Energy

Basel is at the forefront of a national vision to more than halve energy use in Switzerland by 2050. To research, develop and commercialise the technologies and techniques required for the country to become a [2000 Watt society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000-watt_society), a number of projects have been set up since 2001 in the Basel metropolitan area. These include demonstration buildings constructed to [Minergie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minergie) or *[Passivhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_house)* standards, electricity generation from [renewable energy](25784.md) sources,[^cite_note-104] and vehicles using natural gas, [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_economy) and [biogas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogas).[^cite_note-105] A building construction law was passed in 2002 also which stated that all new flat roofs must be greened leading to Basel becoming the world's leading green roof city. This was driven by an energy saving programme.[^cite_note-106]

A [hot dry rock geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_geothermal_systems) project was cancelled in 2009 since it caused [induced seismicity in Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_seismicity_in_Basel).

## Economy



[Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis) Campus Basel

The city of Basel, located in the north west of Switzerland, is one of the most dynamic economic regions of Switzerland.

As of 2016, Basel had an unemployment rate of 3.7%.[^cite_note-BSSKompakt-107] As of 2018, 19.3% of the working population was employed in the [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) and 80.6% was employed in the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy).[^cite_note-108] There were 82,449 residents of the municipality who were employed in some capacity, of which women made up 46.2% of the workforce.

In 2008 the total number of [full-time equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-time_equivalent) jobs was 130,988. The number of jobs in the primary sector was 13, of which 10 were in agriculture and 4 were in forestry or lumber production. The number of jobs in the secondary sector was 33,171 of which 24,848 or (74.9%) were in manufacturing, 10 were in mining and 7,313 (22.0%) were in construction. The number of jobs in the tertiary sector was 97,804. In the tertiary sector; 12,880 or 13.2% were in wholesale or retail sales or the repair of motor vehicles, 11,959 or 12.2% were in the movement and storage of goods, 6,120 or 6.3% were in a hotel or restaurant, 4,186 or 4.3% were in the information industry, 10,752 or 11.0% were the insurance or financial industry, 13,695 or 14.0% were technical professionals or scientists, 6,983 or 7.1% were in education and 16,060 or 16.4% were in health care.[^cite_note-109]

In 2000, there were 121,842 workers who commuted into the municipality and 19,263 workers who commuted away. The municipality is a net importer of workers, with about 6.3 workers entering the municipality for every one leaving. About 23.9% of the workforce coming into Basel are coming from outside Switzerland, while 1.0% of the locals commute out of Switzerland for work.[^cite_note-commuter-110] Of the working population, 49.2% used public transportation to get to work, and 18.7% used a private car.[^cite_note-SFSO-90]



Roche Tower, the tallest building in Switzerland

The first [Roche Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche_Tower), designed by [Herzog & de Meuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzog_&_de_Meuron), is 41 floors and 178 metres (584 ft) high, upon its opening in 2015 it has become the tallest building in Switzerland. Roche Tower-2 was opened in 2022, has 50 floors and is Switzerland's tallest building. Basel has also Switzerland's third tallest building ([Basler Messeturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basler_Messeturm), 105 m (344 ft)) and Switzerland's tallest tower ([St. Chrischona TV tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swisscom-Sendeturm_St._Chrischona), 250 m (820 ft)).

### Chemical industry

The Swiss [chemical industry](58721.md) operates largely from Basel, and Basel also has a large [pharmaceutical industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_industry_in_Switzerland). [Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis),[^cite_note-GoingtoBasel-111] [Syngenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngenta), [Ciba Specialty Chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciba_Specialty_Chemicals),[^cite_note-112] [Clariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clariant),[^cite_note-113] [Hoffmann-La Roche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoffmann%E2%80%93La_Roche),[^cite_note-GoingtoBasel-111] [Basilea Pharmaceutica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilea_Pharmaceutica), and [Actelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actelion) are headquartered there. Pharmaceuticals and specialty chemicals have become the modern focus of the city's industrial production.

Basel is a major European hub for Biotech and Biopharmaceuticals. There are plenty of start-ups.[^cite_note-114] The thriving [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) ecosystem further reinforces this trend.

### Banking

Banking is important to Basel:

- [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS) maintains central offices in Basel.

  [^cite_note-115]

- The [Bank for International Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_for_International_Settlements) (BIS) is located within the city and is the central banker's bank.

According to the BIS, "The choice of Switzerland for the seat of the BIS was a compromise by those countries that established the BIS: Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States. When consensus could not be reached on locating the Bank in London, Brussels or Amsterdam, the choice fell on Switzerland. An independent, neutral country, Switzerland offered the BIS less exposure to undue influence from any of the major powers. Within Switzerland, Basel was chosen largely because of its location, with excellent railway connections in all directions, especially important at a time when most international travel was by train."

[^cite_note-116]

Created in May 1930, the BIS is owned by its member [central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank). No agent of the Swiss public authorities may enter the premises without the express consent of the bank.

[^cite_note-117]

&#x20;The bank exercises supervision and police power over its premises. The BIS enjoys immunity from criminal and administrative jurisdiction.

[^cite_note-118]

The [Basel Committee on Banking Supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Committee_on_Banking_Supervision) usually meets at the BIS premises in Basel. It has produced recommendations such as [Basel I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_I), [Basel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_II) and [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III), based on consensus among its members which are central banks and [banking supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_supervisor).

- Basel also hosts the headquarters of the [Global Infrastructure Basel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Infrastructure_Basel_Foundation), which is active in the field of sustainable infrastructure (financing).

## Education

Besides [Humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism), the city of Basel has also been well known for its achievements in the field of mathematics. Among others, the mathematician [Leonhard Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler) and the [Bernoulli family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_family) have done research and been teaching at the local institutions for centuries. In 1910 the Swiss Mathematical Society was founded in the city and in the mid-twentieth century the Russian mathematician [Alexander Ostrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ostrowski) taught at the local university. In 2000 about 57,864 or (34.7%) of the population have completed non-mandatory [upper secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Switzerland#Secondary), and 27,603 or (16.6%) have completed additional higher education (either [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Switzerland) or a *[Fachhochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule)*). Of the 27,603 who completed tertiary schooling, 44.4% were Swiss men, 31.1% were Swiss women, 13.9% were non-Swiss men and 10.6% were non-Swiss women.[^cite_note-STAT2000-91]

In 2010 11,912 students attended the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) (55% female). 25% were foreign nationals, 16% were from canton of Basel-Stadt. In 2006, 6162 students studied at one of the nine academies of the FHNW (51% female).[^cite_note-BB-123]

As of 2000, there were 5,820 students in Basel who came from another municipality, while 1,116 residents attended schools outside the municipality.[^cite_note-commuter-110]

### Universities



Inauguration ceremony of the University of Basel, 1460

Basel hosts Switzerland's oldest university, the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel), dating from 1460. [Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desiderius_Erasmus), [Paracelsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus), [Daniel Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Bernoulli), [Leonhard Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler), [Jacob Burckhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Burckhardt), [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche), [Tadeusz Reichstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Reichstein), [Karl Jaspers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Jaspers), [Carl Gustav Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jung), and [Karl Barth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Barth) worked there. The University of Basel is currently counted among the 90 best educational institutions worldwide.[^cite_note-124]

In 2007, the [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Zürich) established the Department of Biosystems Science and Engineering (D-BSSE) in Basel. The creation of the D-BSSE was driven by a Swiss-wide research initiative SystemsX, and was jointly supported by funding from the ETH Zürich, the Swiss Government, the Swiss University Conference (SUC) and private industry.[^cite_note-125]

Basel also hosts several academies of the *Fachhochschule Nordwestschweiz (FHNW)*: the FHNW Academy of Art and Design, FHNW Academy of Music, and the FHNW School of Business.[^cite_note-126]

Basel is renowned for various scientific societies, such as the Entomological Society of Basel (*Entomologische Gesellschaft Basel*, EGB), which celebrated its 100th anniversary in 2005.[^cite_note-127]

### Volksschule

In 2005 16,939 pupils and students attended the *Volksschule* (the obligatory school time, including *Kindergarten* (127), primary schools (*Primarschule*, 25), and lower secondary schools (*Sekundarschule*, 10)),[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] of which 94% visited public schools and 39.5% were foreign nationals. In 2010 already 51.1% of all pupils spoke another language than German as their first language. In 2009 3.1% of the pupils visited special classes for pupils with particular needs. The average amount of study in primary school in Basel is 816 teaching hours per year.[^cite_note-BB-123]

### Upper secondary school

In 2010 65% of the youth finished their upper secondary education with a vocational training and education, 18% finished their upper secondary education with a [Federal Matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) at one of the five gymnasiums, 5% completed a *Fachmaturität* at the *FMS*, 5% completed a *Berufsmaturität* synchronously to their vocational training, and 7% other kind of upper secondary maturity. 14.1% of all students at public gymnasiums were foreign nationals. The Maturity quota in 2010 was on a record high at 28.8% (32.8 female, 24.9% male).[^cite_note-BB-123]



The Gymnasium Leonhard

Basel has five public [gymnasiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittelschule) (*[Gymnasium Bäumlihof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_B%C3%A4umlihof?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Gymnasium Kirschgarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_Kirschgarten?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Gymnasium am Münsterplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_am_M%C3%BCnsterplatz?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Gymnasium Leonhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_Leonhard?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Wirtschaftsgymnasium und Wirtschaftsmittelschule Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftsgymnasium_und_Wirtschaftsmittelschule_Basel?action=edit\&redlink=1)*), each with its own profiles (different focus on major subjects, such as visual design, biology and chemistry, Italian, Spanish, or Latin languages, music, physics and applied mathematics, philosophy/education/psychology, and economics and law) that entitles students with a successful [Matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) graduation to attend universities. ### Theatre and music



Metro decor for the [Eurovision Song Contest 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025)

Basel is the home of the [Schola Cantorum Basiliensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schola_Cantorum_Basiliensis), founded in 1933, a worldwide centre for research on and performance of music from the Medieval through the Baroque eras. [Theater Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Basel), chosen in 1999 as the best stage for German-language performances and in 2009 and 2010 as "Opera house of the year" by German opera magazine *[Opernwelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opernwelt)*,[^cite_note-138] presents a busy schedule of plays in addition to being home to the city's opera and ballet companies. Basel is home to the largest orchestra in Switzerland, the [Sinfonieorchester Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinfonieorchester_Basel). It is also the home of the [Basel Sinfonietta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Sinfonietta) and the [Kammerorchester Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kammerorchester_Basel), which recorded the complete symphonies of [Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven) for the [Sony](26989.md) label, led by its music director [Giovanni Antonini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonini). The Schola Cantorum and the Basler Kammerorchester were both founded by the conductor [Paul Sacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sacher), who went on to commission works by many leading composers. The Paul Sacher Foundation, opened in 1986, houses a major collection of manuscripts, including the entire [Igor Stravinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Stravinsky) archive. The baroque orchestras La Cetra and Capriccio Basel are also based in Basel.

In May 2004, the fifth European Festival of Youth Choirs (Europäisches Jugendchorfestival, or EJCF) opened; this Basel tradition started in 1992. Host of the festival is the local [Basel Boys Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Boys_Choir).

In 1997, Basel contended to become the "[European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture)", though the honor went to [Thessaloniki](40471.md).

In 2025, Basel hosted the [69th edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025) of the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) at [St. Jakobshalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jakobshalle), becoming the third Swiss city to host the song competition after [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano) in [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1956) and [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne) in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1989).[^cite_note-139]

### Events

The city of Basel is a centre for numerous fairs and events all year round. One of the most important fairs for contemporary art worldwide is the [Art Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Basel) which was founded in 1970 by Ernst Beyeler and takes place in June each year. [Baselworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baselworld), the watch and jewellery show (*Uhren- und Schmuckmesse*) one of the biggest fairs of its kind in Europe was held every year as well, and attracted a great number of tourists and dealers to the city, last happened in 2019 and is now cancelled. Live marketing company and fair organizer [MCH Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCH_Group) has its head office in Basel.



Top Secret Drum Corps at the 2009 Basel Tattoo

The [carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival) of the city of Basel (*[Basler Fasnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_of_Basel)*) is a major cultural event in the year. The carnival is the biggest in Switzerland and attracts large crowds every year, despite the fact that it starts at exactly four o'clock in the morning (*Morgestraich*) on a winter Monday. The Fasnacht asserts Basel's Protestant history by commencing the revelry five days after [Ash Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Wednesday) and continuing exactly 72 hours. Almost all study and work in the old city cease. Dozens of fife and drum clubs parade in medieval guild tradition with fantastical masks and illuminated lanterns.

[Basel Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Tattoo), founded in 2006 by the local [Top Secret Drum Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Secret_Drum_Corps), has grown to be the world's second largest military tattoo in terms of performers and budget after the [Edinburgh Military Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Edinburgh_Military_Tattoo).[^cite_note-swissinfo-159] The Basel Tattoo annual parade, with an estimated 125,000 visitors, is considered the largest event in Basel. The event is now sponsored by the [Swiss Federal Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sport (DDPS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Defence,_Civil_Protection_and_Sport), making it the official [military tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tattoo) of Switzerland.

### Cuisine

There are a number of culinary specialties originating in Basel, including *[Basler Läckerli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basler_L%C3%A4ckerli)* cookies and *[Mässmogge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4ssmogge)* candies. Being located in the meeting place between Switzerland, France and Germany the culinary landscape as a whole is very varied and diverse, making it a city with a great number of restaurants of all sorts.

### Zoo



The [Antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope) House at Zoo Basel

[Zoo Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo_Basel) is, with over 1.7 million visitors per year,[^cite_note-2011_visitors-160] the most visited tourist attraction in Basel and the second most visited tourist attraction in Switzerland.[^cite_note-visitors-161]

Established in 1874, Zoo Basel is the oldest zoo in Switzerland and, by number of animals, the largest. Through its history, Zoo Basel has had several breeding successes, such as the first worldwide [Indian rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rhinoceros) birth[^cite_note-Panzernashornhaus-162] and [Greater flamingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_flamingo) hatch[^cite_note-163] in a zoo. These and other achievements led *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* Travel to rank Zoo Basel as one of the fifteen best zoos in the world in 2008.[^cite_note-Forbes-164]

Despite its international fame, Basel's population remains attached to Zoo Basel, which is entirely surrounded by the city of Basel. Evidence of this is the millions of donations money each year, as well as Zoo Basel's unofficial name: locals lovingly call "their" zoo "*Zolli*" by which is it known throughout Basel and most of Switzerland.

### Sport



[St. Jakob-Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jakob-Park)

Amongst its major sports venues, Basel features a large [football stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jakob-Park) that has been awarded four stars by [UEFA](32332.md), a modern ice hockey arena, and a sports hall.

The [football](10568.md) club [FC Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Basel) is successful and in recognition of this the city was one of the Swiss venues for the [2008 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), along with [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich) and [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern). The championships were jointly hosted by Switzerland and [Austria](26964606.md). [BSC Old Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSC_Old_Boys) and [Concordia Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_Basel) are the other football teams in Basel.

Among the most popular sports in Switzerland is [ice hockey](14790.md).[^cite_note-165] Basel is home to [EHC Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EHC_Basel), who play in the [Swiss League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_League) (SL), the second tier of the Swiss ice hockey league system. They play their home games in the 6,700-seater [St. Jakob Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jakob_Arena). The team previously played in the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(ice_hockey\)) but filed for bankruptcy after the [2013–14 Swiss League season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013-14_National_League_B_season).

Basketball has a very small but faithful fan base. The top division, called the [SBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Basketball_League), is a semi-professional league and has one team from the Basel region, the "Birstal Starwings". As in most European countries, but unlike the U.S., Switzerland has a club-based rather than a school-based competition system.[^cite_note-166] The [Starwings Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starwings_Basel) are the only first division basketball team in [German-speaking Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-speaking_Switzerland).[^cite_note-167]

A large indoor tennis event takes place in Basel every October. Some of the best [ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals)-professionals play every year at the [Swiss Indoors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidoff_Swiss_Indoors), previously including Switzerland's biggest sporting hero [Roger Federer](262376.md), a Basel native who describes the city as "one of the most beautiful cities in the world".

In July 2022, the women's water polo players of the WSV Basel secured their 11th national championship title.[^cite_note-168]

[Basel GAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_GAA), a [Gaelic games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_games) club, is also located in Basel. [Basel Dragons AFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Dragons_AFC) have been playing [Australian Football](2403.md) in the [AFL Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Switzerland) league since 2019.

The annual [Basel Rhine Swim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Rhine_Swim) draws several thousand visitors to the city to swim in or float on the Rhine.[^cite_note-169]

The headquarters of the [IHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Handball_Federation) (International Handball Federation) is located in Basel.
