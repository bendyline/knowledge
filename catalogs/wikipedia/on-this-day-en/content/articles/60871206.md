**Ersin Tatar** (born 7 September 1960) is a Turkish Cypriot politician who served as the fifth [president of Northern Cyprus](7887833.md) (*de facto* state) from 2020 to 2025. He previously served as the fourth [Prime Minister of Northern Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Northern_Cyprus) following the collapse of [Tufan Erhürman](56342144.md)'s coalition government in 2019, and remained so until his election as president.[^cite_note-1] He was also the leader of the [National Unity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_Party_\(Northern_Cyprus\)) (UBP) and [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition) from 2018 until his election.[^cite_note-2]

## Early life

Tatar comes from a family of [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) who left [Crimea](163045.md) in the 19th century and eventually moved to Cyprus.[^cite_note-3] Tatar was born in [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia) on 7 September 1960, the son of Canev and Rüstem Tatar.[^cite_note-Milliyet-4] His father was also a politician.[^cite_note-Barron's-5] He was sent to England to attend [Forest School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_School,_Walthamstow), a private boarding school in [Walthamstow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walthamstow),[^cite_note-Milliyet-4][^cite_note-Sunday_Telegraph-6] then earned an economics degree from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University)'s [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge) in 1982.[^cite_note-auto-7][^cite_note-8]

## Pre-politics career

From 1982 to 1986, Tatar worked as a chartered accountant for [PriceWaterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PriceWaterhouse) in London. From 1986 to 1991, he worked for [Polly Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polly_Peck), and was the company's assistant treasurer when it collapsed with debts of £1.3 billion.[^cite_note-BBC_News-9] In the resulting trial that led to CEO [Asil Nadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asil_Nadir) receiving a 10-year jail term, it was alleged that Tatar had "assisted Mr. Nadir in the dishonest movement of money from PPI and enjoyed a close working relationship with the Polly Peck boss".[^cite_note-BBC_News-9] When Tatar visited the UK in 2019, for the first time since 1991, there was concern that he might be arrested for his role in the scandal; however, the government's [Serious Fraud Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serious_Fraud_Office_\(United_Kingdom\)) said that this was "no longer in the public interest".[^cite_note-T-Vine-10]

In 1991, Tatar moved to [Ankara](802.md), where he worked at FMC Nurol Defense Industry Co. until 1992. From 1992 to 2001, he was the general coordinator of [Show TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_TV), a Turkish television channel owned by [Ciner Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciner_Media_Group).[^cite_note-Barron's-5] In 1996, he founded the television channel Kanal T in Nicosia.[^cite_note-Barron's-5] He was also an active member of the Cypriot diaspora community in Turkey, and was chair of the Istanbul Turkish Cypriot Cultural Association from 1997 to 2001.[^cite_note-auto-7][^cite_note-auto1-11]

## Politics

Tatar entered politics in 2003, joining the [National Unity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_Party_\(Northern_Cyprus\)) (UBP). He was first elected to Parliament in 2009, serving as [Finance Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_of_Northern_Cyprus) under [Derviş Eroğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dervi%C5%9F_Ero%C4%9Flu) until his party's electoral defeat in 2013. He unsuccessfully ran for the UBP leadership in 2015, before running again and winning in 2018.[^cite_note-auto1-11] He voiced support for the [2019 Turkish offensive into north-eastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Turkish_offensive_into_north-eastern_Syria) and said that Turkish Cypriots "are always on Turkey's side".[^cite_note-12] He is a supporter of a [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution_\(Cyprus\)) to the [Cyprus dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_dispute).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

One week before the [2020 Northern Cyprus presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Northern_Cypriot_presidential_election), Tatar visited Turkey. After his arrival, he announced that he would be reopening the [Varosha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varosha,_Famagusta) beachfront with Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan)'s blessing. The move to open Varosha, which had been closed to the public and guarded by [Turkish soldiers](30205.md) since the 1974 [Turkish invasion of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Cyprus), attracted widespread condemnation both in Northern Cyprus and the [Republic of Cyprus](5593.md), as well as the international community.[^cite_note-15] His Deputy Prime Minister [Kudret Özersay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudret_%C3%96zersay) resigned, accusing of Tatar of stealing his idea; Özersay, who was the first to call for the reopening of Varosha under Turkish Cypriot control, said he was against Tatar's decision because he had turned the issue into a campaign matter ahead of the election.[^cite_note-auto2-16] Özersay was also a candidate in the election.[^cite_note-auto2-16] Özersay's resignation triggered a crisis that led to the government of Northern Cyprus dissolving just one week before the election. Attempting to deflect the accusations that the decision to reopen Varosha was not a campaign ploy on behalf of Turkey, Tatar said he made the decision in his capacity as Prime Minister and not as a candidate in the upcoming elections.[^cite_note-auto2-16]



Group photo at the [17th ECO Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_ECO_Summit) in [Khankendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khankendi), Azerbaijan, featuring Ersin Tatar, 4 July 2025

In the second and final round of the election on 18 October 2020, Tatar received 51.69% of the vote whilst incumbent President [Mustafa Akıncı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Ak%C4%B1nc%C4%B1) received 48.31%, the closest ever margin in the history of Northern Cyprus.[^cite_note-election1-17] Tatar took office on 23 October.[^cite_note-election1-17] The election was marred by accusations that Turkey had interfered on Tatar's behalf.[^cite_note-18]

Tatar was decisively defeated in the [2025 Northern Cypriot presidential election](78191958.md) on 19 October 2025, receiving 35.81% of the vote while former Prime Minister [Tufan Erhürman](56342144.md) received 62.76%.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

## Personal life

Tatar is married to Sibel Tatar, with whom he has two children.[^cite_note-auto1-11][^cite_note-20]
