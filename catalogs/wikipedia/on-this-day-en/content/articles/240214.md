The **Indiana Fever** are an American professional [basketball](3921.md) team based in [Indianapolis](57707.md). The Fever compete in the [Women's National Basketball Association](57721.md) (WNBA) as a member of the [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_\(WNBA\)). The team was founded for the [2000 WNBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_WNBA_season).[^cite_note-sportsbusinessjournal.com-8] The team is owned by [Herb Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Simon_\(real_estate\)), the founder of [Simon Property Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Property_Group).[^cite_note-9]

The Fever have qualified for the [WNBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_playoffs) in 14 of its 25 seasons in Indiana. In [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_WNBA_Finals), the Fever reached the [WNBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Finals) but fell short to the [Phoenix Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury) in game 5. In [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_WNBA_Finals), the Fever won the WNBA Finals with a 3–1 series victory over the [Minnesota Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Lynx). [Tamika Catchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Catchings) was named the [2012 Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award). In [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_WNBA_Finals), the Fever again reached the WNBA Finals but fell short to [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Lynx) in game 5.

Some of the players who have helped define the history of the Fever include [Tamika Catchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Catchings), [Katie Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Douglas_\(basketball\)), [Briann January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briann_January), [Natalie Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Williams), [Yolanda Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolanda_Griffith), [Shavonte Zellous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shavonte_Zellous), [Tully Bevilaqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tully_Bevilaqua), [Tammy Sutton-Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_Sutton-Brown), [Natasha Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Howard), [Candice Dupree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candice_Dupree), [Cappie Pondexter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappie_Pondexter), [Erica Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erica_Wheeler_\(basketball\)), [Kelsey Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelsey_Mitchell_\(basketball\)), [Lexie Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexie_Hull), [Aliyah Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_Boston), and [Caitlin Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark).

## History

### 2000: Early beginnings



Lobby of Gainbridge Fieldhouse

The Indiana Fever team was founded in 2000 following the WNBA's announcement on June 7, 1999, of four locations, including Indiana, having been granted an expansion franchise to coincide with the opening of [Gainbridge Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainbridge_Fieldhouse) (then Conseco Fieldhouse).[^cite_note-sportsbusinessjournal.com-8] Notably, the Fever's launch campaign was accompanied with the slogan "*In 49 states it's just basketball, but this is Indiana*," which has become a popular catchphrase regarding the sport's popularity in the state.[^cite_note-10] The league held an [expansion draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_WNBA_expansion_draft) for the Fever, [Miami Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Sol), [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire_\(2000%E2%80%932002\)), and [Seattle Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Storm) on December 15, 1999.[^cite_note-2000fever-11]

In their first two seasons, they were coached by women's basketball legends [Anne Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Donovan) and [Nell Fortner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nell_Fortner). Led by center [Kara Wolters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Wolters), the team posted a 9–23 record in their inaugural campaign.

### 2001–2016: Tamika Catchings era

The Fever drafted [University of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Lady_Volunteers_basketball) star [Tamika Catchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Catchings) in the 2001 WNBA Draft. The Fever went into the year with high expectations of a playoff berth, but Catchings [tore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterior_cruciate_ligament_injury) her [ACL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterior_cruciate_ligament) during a college game and missed the entire WNBA season. The Fever posted a 10–22 record in 2001.

After missing the entire 2001 season, the 2002 season proved to be the breakout season for Tamika Catchings and the Fever. Catchings came out strong and became one of the most versatile players in the WNBA, easily winning [Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Rookie_of_the_Year) honors as well as making the [WNBA All-Star team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_All-Star_Game). Her team competed well all year and posted a respectable 16–16 record, tying for the final playoff spot with the [Orlando Miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Miracle). Indiana won the tiebreaker and earned their first playoff appearance in franchise history. They drew the #1 seed in the playoffs, the [Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty); with the Fever losing two games to one.

The 2002–2003 offseason brought a lot of change for the Fever. The team added Olympian [Natalie Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Williams) and [Charlotte Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Sting) star [Kelly Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Miller_\(basketball\)) before the 2003 season. During the offseason, the original coach and GM, Nell Fortner, resigned. [Kelly Krauskopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Krauskopf) replaced Fortner as GM and immediately hired [Brian Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Winters) to be the head coach. On May 29, 2003, the Fever registered their first sellout of 18,345 and defeated the [Washington Mystics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Mystics) on national television. The team did better under the new coaching, but missed the playoffs, posting a 16–18 record.

The 2004 campaign was very similar to 2003's. The Fever finished with a 15–19 record. They missed the playoffs by one game in the Eastern Conference.

#### 2005–2007: Playoffs debut

In 2005, the Fever had their best season since joining the league, posting a 21–13 record and making the playoffs for just the second time. In the first round, the Fever swept the [New York Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty) two games to none, earning their first playoff series victory in franchise history. In the Eastern Conference Finals, the Fever faced the heavily favored [Connecticut Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Sun). Game 1 came down to the final seconds when [Katie Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Douglas_\(basketball\)) hit a crucial three to win the game for the Sun. Game 2 went into overtime, with the Sun winning, thus sweeping the Fever two games to none.

In the 2005–2006 offseason, the Fever acquired All-Star [Anna DeForge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_DeForge) from the [Phoenix Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury) in exchange for [Kelly Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Miller_\(basketball\)). Later that offseason, the Fever made another All-Star addition by signing free agent [Tamika Whitmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Whitmore) from the [Los Angeles Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Sparks). In the [2006 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_WNBA_draft) they selected athletic swing-forward [La'Tangela Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La'Tangela_Atkinson) from the [North Carolina Tar Heels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_women's_basketball) along with Kasha Terry from the [Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech_Yellow_Jackets_women's_basketball).



Outside Gainbridge Fieldhouse, home of the Fever

The Fever started the 2006 season off at 4–0 and jumped out to an early lead in the Eastern Conference standings. Teammates [Tamika Catchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Catchings) and [Tully Bevilaqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tully_Bevilaqua) led the league in steals, first and second, respectively, the first time teammates led the league in one statistical category. The Fever posted a 21–13 record, making the playoffs for the second year in a row. In the first round, the Fever faced archrival Detroit. Detroit won Game 1 in Indianapolis and held a one-game-to-nothing lead in the series. Game 2 in [Detroit](8687.md) was a high-scoring affair with Tamika Whitmore scoring a WNBA Playoff record 41 points. Detroit won in the end, 98–83, and won the series two games to none.

Going into the 2006–2007 off-season, the Fever looked to improve their post play. In the [Dispersal Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_WNBA_Draft#Dispersal_Draft), the Fever added veteran forward [Sheri Sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheri_Sam) from the [Charlotte Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Sting). Kelly Krauskopf and the front office then set their eyes on key Free Agent Center [Tammy Sutton-Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_Sutton-Brown), signing her on March 22, 2007. The Fever also selected 6–7 center [Alison Bales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Bales) from [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Blue_Devils_women's_basketball) in the [2007 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_WNBA_draft) to go along with Sutton-Brown.

Going into the 2007 season, the Fever had their eyes set on the WNBA Finals. They started the season strong, winning 16 of their first 20 games, the best 20-game start in Eastern Conference history. Then, on July 20, key player Tamika Catchings injured her foot, causing her to miss the rest of the regular season. The injury was later revealed as a partial tear of her [plantar fascia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantar_fascia). The Fever finished 5–9 without Catchings. They won the #2 seed in the Eastern Conference Playoffs, beating out the Connecticut Sun. They played the same Sun team in the first round of the playoffs. Game 1 in Connecticut was an epic battle, going into three overtimes, with the Sun hanging on to win 93–88. The Fever won Game 2 at home by double digits, forcing a decisive Game 3. In game 3, the Fever found themselves down by 22 points late halfway through the 3rd quarter. The Fever battled back to win Game 3 in overtime by the same score as Game 1, 93–88. The 22-point comeback was the largest comeback in WNBA Playoff history. In the Eastern Conference Finals, the Fever played their other hated rival, the [Detroit Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Shock). The Fever grabbed an early lead in the series, winning Game 1 at Conseco Fieldhouse. With the team just 1 game away from the [WNBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Finals) the Fever traveled to Detroit. In Game 2, the game was close until the 2nd quarter, when the Shock blew it wide open with a 14–0 run and easily won. With a spot in the Finals up for grabs in Game 3, the Fever started off the game with an early 17–3 lead. Then late in the first half Catchings went down with another injury; she had completely [torn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon_rupture) her [Achilles tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon). The Shock ended up winning Game 3, 85–61.

#### 2008: Head coach shakeup

On October 26, 2007, the Fever announced that they declined the option for head coach [Brian Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Winters), ending his four-year tenure in charge.[^cite_note-Winters-12] He compiled a 78–58 regular season record with a 5–7 playoff record. On December 12, 2007, assistant coach [Lin Dunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Dunn) was named his successor as head coach.[^cite_note-Dunn-13]

In one of the biggest trades in WNBA history, the Fever traded [Tamika Whitmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Whitmore) and their first-round pick in the [2008 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_WNBA_draft) for [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis,_Indiana) native, [Katie Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Douglas_\(basketball\)) on February 19, 2008.[^cite_note-Douglas-14]

The Fever were part of the [Liberty Outdoor Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Outdoor_Classic), which was the first regular-season professional basketball game played outdoors. It was played between the Fever and [New York Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty) at [Arthur Ashe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe_Stadium) in [Flushing, New York](267693.md) on July 19, 2008, with the Fever winning the game, 71–55.

During the 2008 season, the Fever struggled in comparison to the three previous seasons. They finished with a 17–17 record, good for fourth place in the playoffs. Meeting the [Detroit Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Shock) in the first round, the Fever lost in three games.

#### 2009–2012: Championship season

Barack Obama holding up a jersey, smiling, surrounded by the Indiana Fever

The Indiana Fever in 2013, visiting the [White House](33057.md) upon winning their first WNBA championship.

After a disappointing 2008 season, the Fever were looking to improve in 2009. [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) owners had said the Fever have been losing money. The only thing to save the Fever from folding in the near future, the owners inferred, was for the Fever to be successful on the court and at the box office. The Fever took the owners' ultimatum to heart and reached the playoffs as the No. 1 seed in the Eastern Conference with a franchise-best 22–12 record. In the first round, the Fever ousted the [Washington Mystics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Mystics) in a sweep, marking their return to the conference finals. In the East finals, again facing their rival [Detroit Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Shock), they reached their first ever [WNBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Finals) by defeating the Shock in three games. The Fever then lost in 5 games to the [Phoenix Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury) in the [WNBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Finals).

Because of the success in 2009, the Fever announced they would remain in [Indianapolis](57707.md) for 2010. Their 2011 season in Indianapolis was also confirmed as well.

In 2012, the Fever advanced to the 2012 WNBA Finals. Indiana then defeated the [Minnesota Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Lynx), 3 games to 1. It was the Fever's first WNBA title.

#### 2012–2016: Catchings' final years

After the Fever won the 2012 WNBA championship, the ownership group took notice of their management by promoting general manager Kelly Krauskopf to president of the franchise.[^cite_note-15] Krauskopf was given additional duties within the franchise as well as a pay increase.[^cite_note-16] The Fever competed for another title in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_WNBA_Finals), reaching the finals for the third time in franchise history, but fell short, losing to the Minnesota Lynx in five games. In [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_WNBA_season), the Fever made it to the playoffs for the [12th consecutive time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WNBA_franchise_post-season_streaks#WNBA_Playoffs_appearance_streaks_2) (the current WNBA record for most consecutive playoff appearances by a team).[^cite_note-17] However, the Fever were upset in the first round elimination game against the [Phoenix Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury) after the league's new playoff format was in effect. This game also marked the final game of [Tamika Catchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamika_Catchings)'s playing career; she had been known as the Fever's [franchise player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchise_player) since 2002.

### 2016–2023: Rebuilding

Two Indiana Fever players trying to get a ball with a Minnesota Lynx player among them

The Indiana Fever versus the [Minnesota Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Lynx) on June 9, 2023 with [Aliyah Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_Boston) blocking [Tiffany Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Mitchell_\(basketball\)).

On November 18, 2016, the Fever announced [Pokey Chatman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokey_Chatman) as their new head coach.[^cite_note-18] In February 2017, they traded for five-time all-star [Candice Dupree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candice_Dupree) along with the Mercury's 2017 first-round pick in a three-team deal that sent [Camille Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Little) and [Jillian Alleyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jillian_Alleyne) to the Mercury and the [Connecticut Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Sun) receiving the 8th overall pick in the [2017 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_WNBA_draft) along with [Lynetta Kizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynetta_Kizer) from the Fever.[^cite_note-19] Despite acquiring a veteran all-star forward and a new head coach, the Fever would have one of the most disappointing seasons in franchise history. On August 12, 2017, the Fever were defeated 111–52 by the Minnesota Lynx, the largest margin of defeat in WNBA history, and allowed a league record 37–0 scoring run.[^cite_note-20] The Fever finished with the second-worst record in the league of 9–25 in their first season after Catchings's retirement, ending a streak of 12 consecutive playoff seasons.[^cite_note-21]

Disappointment continued for the rebuilding Fever in 2018, as they finished in last place with a 6–28 record. On March 4, 2019, Pacers Sports & Entertainment announced [Allison Barber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Barber), as the new president and chief operating officer of the Fever, while Catchings was named vice president of Fever Basketball Operations.[^cite_note-22] They wound up with the third selection in the [2019 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WNBA_draft), selecting [Teaira McCowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaira_McCowan) from [Mississippi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_State_Bulldogs_women's_basketball). The 2019 season saw some improvement, and the team finished 13–21, two games out of the playoffs. After the season, coach and general manager [Pokey Chatman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokey_Chatman) was fired.[^cite_note-Chatman_fired-23]

On November 27, 2019, [Marianne Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Stanley) was introduced as the head coach of the Indiana Fever, making her the seventh coach in the franchise's 20-year WNBA history. In addition, it was also announced that Catchings would be promoted to general manager.[^cite_note-24]

Near the end of the 2019 season, the Fever announced that they would move their home games to [Butler University's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_Bulldogs) [Hinkle Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinkle_Fieldhouse) for the 2020 and 2021 seasons, plus at least part of the 2022 season, to accommodate renovations of Bankers Life Fieldhouse. The team planned to return to the renamed Gainbridge Fieldhouse upon project completion.[^cite_note-25] However, the [2020 WNBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_WNBA_season) was played in [IMG Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_Academy) in [Bradenton, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradenton,_Florida), due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). For the 2021 season, the Fever played their first four home games at Bankers Life Fieldhouse before playing the remaining home games at [Indiana Farmers Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Farmers_Coliseum).[^cite_note-26]

On February 14, 2022, Tamika Catchings stepped down as the vice president of basketball operations and general manager of the Indiana Fever.[^cite_note-27]

On February 24, 2022, former Fever coach Lin Dunn, who guided the team to a WNBA title in 2012, was introduced as the franchise's interim general manager and senior advisor for Fever basketball operations.[^cite_note-28]

The franchise generally struggled in 2022 and 2023, earning the top WNBA draft pick after both seasons. In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WNBA_draft), the Fever chose [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Gamecocks_women's_basketball)'s [Aliyah Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_Boston) with the number one pick; the next season, amidst the increasing popularity of collegiate [women's basketball](667977.md), the Fever chose [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes_women's_basketball)'s [Caitlin Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark) first overall. Drafting Clark led to a surge of interest in the team and the WNBA in general, with TV ratings for the [2024 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WNBA_draft) (at 2.5 million) surpassing the combined TV ratings for actual games played over the prior 25 seasons.[^cite_note-29]

### 2024–present: The Caitlin Clark era

See also: [Caitlin Clark effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark_effect)



[Caitlin Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark) during pregame at the 2024 season against the Lynx.

The Fever had a dramatic turnaround in the first season with Clark, going an even 20–20 during the [2024 regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indiana_Fever_season) and making the playoffs for the first time since 2016. Clark averaged 19.2 points, 5.7 rebounds, and 8.4 assists per game, becoming the first-ever true rookie to lead the WNBA in assists.[^cite_note-30] Teammates [Kelsey Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelsey_Mitchell_\(basketball\)) and [Aliyah Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_Boston) also displayed strong statistics.[^cite_note-31] In large part due to Clark, Fever home games drew an average of over 17,000 fans in 2024, actually beating their men's counterpart, the Pacers, on a per-game basis (though the Pacers play 20 more home games).[^cite_note-32] The Fever also drew strongly on the road[^cite_note-33] and set virtually all of the WNBA television records, recording figures oftentimes not seen since the early days of the league in the late 1990s and early 2000s.[^cite_note-34] The sixth-seeded Fever ended up falling to the [Connecticut Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Connecticut_Sun_season) in a two-game first-round sweep.

In August 2024, the WNBA announced that the Fever would host [2025 WNBA All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WNBA_All-Star_Game) and related events in July 2025 for the first time in franchise history.[^cite_note-35] Tickets for All-Star weekend events went on sale on April 29, 2025, and sold out within seven hours.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The Fever announced on October 27, 2024, that they had parted ways with head coach [Christie Sides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christie_Sides), after Sides' tenure went 33–47.[^cite_note-38] On November 1, 2024, the team announced that [Stephanie White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_White) would return to the organization as head coach.[^cite_note-39]

Before the [2025 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WNBA_season), the Fever announced on February 1, 2025, that they had acquired [Sophie Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Cunningham) from the [Phoenix Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury).[^cite_note-40] In the season, the team finished 24–20. Along the way they won the WNBA Commissioner's Cup with a record of 4–1 and they made the playoffs as No. 6 seed. They upset the No. 3 seed Atlanta Dream in three games in the quarterfinals and they took the No. 2 seed and eventual champion Las Vegas Aces to five games in the semifinals, falling only in overtime of the last game. They accomplished this despite a rash of player injuries. Star Caitlin Clark played only 13 games and missed the last half of the season. Regulars Sophie Cunningham and Sydney Colson suffered season-ending injuries, as did hardship replacements Aari McDonald and Chloe Bibby.[^cite_note-41]

The Fever acquired the tenth pick in the [2026 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_WNBA_draft) and, with only one lottery pick, selected [Raven Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raven_Johnson) from [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Gamecocks_women's_basketball).

## Logo and uniforms



Indiana Fever mascot Freddy Fever at a home game in 2026

### Logo

The logo was created in 1999 by designer Todd Radom.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Todd Radom has posted some sketches of the logo.[^cite_note-44] He has said the logo was inspired by a "decidedly retro vibe that references back to something like the 'Hoosiers' era in terms of basketball in Indiana".[^cite_note-45]

### Uniforms

- 2000–2006: The home jerseys were white with red, dark blue and yellow on the sides. On the front, Fever was written across. The road jersey were dark blue with red and yellow on the sides. Indiana in red letters trimmed with white on the chest.

- 2007–2010: Home jerseys are white with blue and yellow strips/trim from the neck down the side. Fever is written in red across the front and on the back the player name is written in all dark blue under the number also in red trimmed in dark blue. The new road jerseys are dark blue with yellow and white trim/strips. Indiana is written on the front in red with white trim and on the back the number is in red with white trim with the player's name underneath in all white. Each jersey has the Fever's alternate logo at the top on the back.

- 2011–2012: As part of the move to [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas)'s Revolution 30 technology, the Fever made subtle changes to the uniforms. Home uniforms remain white, but the 'Fever' wordmark is now written in navy with red and yellow trim. Numbers are now rounded and in red. Away uniforms remain unchanged save for the striping patterns and rounded numbers.

- 2013–2015: On October 19, 2012, the Fever announced that [Finish Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finish_Line,_Inc.) will be their uniform sponsor, beginning with the [2013 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_WNBA_season). The uniform stayed the same aside from a new number scheme and the addition of the uniform sponsor.

- 2016: As part of a league-wide initiative for its 20th season, all games featured all-color uniform matchups. Therefore, the Fever unveiled a yellow jersey as a light-colored uniform to complement its standard navy uniform.

- 2019: The Fever announced Salesforce as their uniform sponsor, replacing Finish Line. Along with all other WNBA team uniforms, the front of the jersey prominently features both Salesforce and AT\&T, a new partner for the WNBA. The number of the player is now just on the back of the jersey.

## Facilities

The Indiana Fever announced in 2026 that they were acquiring a new practice facility in Downtown Indianapolis.[^cite_note-46] The Fever have stated this facility will cost \$78 million.[^cite_note-47] The land was acquired via a no-bid process.[^cite_note-Springer-48] Some have criticized the project, arguing that affordable housing should have been built on the site.[^cite_note-49] However, Indianapolis Mayor Joe Hogsett argued that the project represents a “commitment to equitable investment in women’s sports.”[^cite_note-Springer-48]

### Trades

- **December 15, 1999**: The Fever acquired Monica Maxwell from the Washington Mystics in exchange for agreeing to select Nyree Roberts in the expansion draft.

- **December 15, 1999**: The Fever traded Sandy Brondello and a first-round pick in the 2000 Draft to the Miami Sol in exchange for Stephanie White.

  [^cite_note-61]

- **April 25, 2000**: The Fever traded Latina Davis and Renee Robinson to the Houston Comets in exchange for Latavia Coleman.

- **April 11, 2001**: The Fever traded Kara Wolters to the Sacramento Monarchs in exchange for a first-round pick in the 2001 Draft.

- **May 27, 2001**: The Fever traded a second-round pick in the 2002 Draft to the Detroit Shock in exchange for Olympia Scott and a third-round pick in the 2002 Draft.

- **December 5, 2001**: The Fever traded Angie Braziel to the Washington Mystics in exchange for Nikki McCray and the right to swap second- and fourth-round picks in the 2002 Draft.

- **March 4, 2002**: The Fever traded Gordana Grubin to the Phoenix Mercury in exchange for Bridget Pettis and a first-round pick in the 2002 Draft.

- **July 20, 2002**: The Fever traded Rita Williams to the Houston Comets in exchange for Coquese Washington.

- **May 1, 2003**: The Fever traded Sylvia Crawley and Gwen Jackson to the San Antonio Silver Stars in exchange for Coretta Brown and Natalie Williams.

- **February 5, 2004**: The Fever traded the 3rd overall and the 18th overall picks in the 2004 Draft to the Charlotte Sting in exchange for Kelly Miller and the 9th overall pick in the 2004 Draft.

- **February 3, 2006**: The Fever traded Kelly Miller to the Phoenix Mercury in exchange for Anna DeForge.

- **February 24, 2006**: The Fever traded Kelly Schumacher and the 12th overall pick in the 2006 Draft to the New York Liberty in exchange for the 9th overall pick in the 2006 Draft.

- **March 7, 2007**: The Fever traded Olympia Scott to the Phoenix Mercury in exchange for Ann Strother.

- **March 23, 2007**: The Fever traded La'Tangela Atkinson to the Sacramento Monarchs in exchange for a second-round pick in the 2008 Draft.

- **February 19, 2008**: The Fever traded Tamika Whitmore, the 9th overall pick in the 2008 Draft and the rights to Jessica Foley to the Connecticut Sun in exchange for Katie Douglas.

- **May 14, 2008**: The Fever traded K.B. Sharp to the Chicago Sky in exchange for Bernadette Ngoyisa.

- **July 4, 2008**: The Fever traded Alison Bales to the Atlanta Dream in exchange for Kristen Mann.

- **May 27, 2010**: The Fever traded a second-round pick in the 2011 Draft to the Tulsa Shock in exchange for Shavonte Zellous.

- **April 29, 2011**: The Fever traded second- and third-round picks to the Seattle Storm and the Washington Mystics in exchange for Erin Phillips and Seattle's third-round pick in the 2012 Draft.

- **March 1, 2012**: The Fever traded Tangela Smith to the San Antonio Silver Stars in exchange for Roneeka Hodges.

- **July 2, 2012**: The Fever traded Roneeka Hodges to the Tulsa Shock in exchange for Karima Christmas.

- **March 11, 2014**: The Fever traded Erin Phillips to the Phoenix Mercury in exchange for Lynetta Kizer.

- **May 12, 2014**: The Fever traded a second-round pick in the 2015 Draft to the Phoenix Mercury in exchange for Maggie Lucas.

- **March 12, 2015**: The Fever traded a first- and third-round picks in the 2015 Draft to the San Antonio Stars in exchange for Shenise Johnson and the second-round pick in the 2015 Draft.

- **February 2, 2016**: The Fever traded Natasha Howard to the Minnesota Lynx in exchange for Devereaux Peters.

- **February 21, 2017:** In a three-way trade, the Fever traded Lynetta Kizer and a first-round pick in the 2017 Draft to the Connecticut Sun. The Fever received Candice Dupree and a second-round pick in the 2017 Draft from the Phoenix Mercury.

  [^cite_note-62]

- **February 1, 2018:** The Fever acquired C [Kayla Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayla_Alexander) and 2019 WNBA Draft third-round pick via trade from [Las Vegas Aces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Aces) for 2019 WNBA Draft second-round pick.

  [^cite_note-63]

- **March 6, 2018:** The Fever acquired 8th overall pick in the 2018 WNBA Draft via trade from [Phoenix Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury) for [Briann January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briann_January).

  [^cite_note-64]

- **March 6, 2020:** The Fever acquired the 14th pick in the 2020 Draft and Minnesota's second-round pick in the 2021 Draft in exchange for Shenise Johnson and the 16th pick in the 2020 Draft.

  [^cite_note-65]

- **February 8, 2021:** The Fever acquired Lindsay Allen from Las Vegas and the 24th pick in the 2021 Draft in exchange for the 14th pick in the 2021 Draft.

- **February 15, 2021:** The Fever acquired the Minnesota Lynx's 1st- and 3rd-round picks in the 2022 Draft, Odyssey Sims and negotiating rights to Temi Fagbenle in exchange for the Fever's 2nd-round pick in the 2022 Draft.

- **February 20, 2022:** The Fever acquired Bria Hartley, the 7th pick in the 2022 WNBA Draft, Chicago's 2023 First Round Draft pick, and Phoenix's 2022 and 2023 Second Round Draft picks in exchange for Julie Allemand.

- **March 8, 2022:** The Fever acquired the 4th and 6th picks in the 2022 Draft and Dallas' 2023 First Round pick in exchange for Teaira McCowan, the 7th pick in the 2022 Draft and the 2023 First Round pick from Chicago.

- **July 4, 2023:** The Fever acquired Amanda Zahui B from Washington in exchange for Queen Egbo.

- **February 1, 2025:** The Fever acquired Sophie Cunningham (from Phoenix), Jaelyn Brown (from Dallas), and the 19th pick in the 2025 WNBA Draft (from Phoenix).
