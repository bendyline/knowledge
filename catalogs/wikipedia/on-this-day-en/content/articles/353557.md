# Airspace

Not to be confused with [Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace).



[Flight information regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_region) in [France](5843419.md)

**Airspace** is a portion of the [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere_of_Earth) controlled by a [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country) above its territory and [territorial waters](496983.md), which is under the country's sovereignty and regulatory control.&#x20;

Internationally, allocation and management of airspace is coordinated by the [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md) (ICAO), which was established through the 1944 [Chicago Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_International_Civil_Aviation) and came into being in 1947 to unify air traffic.[^cite_note-icao-doc7300-1] ICAO organizes airspace into [Flight Information Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_region) (FIRs) and provides classification guidelines. National authorities, such as the [FAA](11186.md), [CAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Authority_\(United_Kingdom\)), or [EASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency) implement rules and regulations for [air traffic control](48563.md), [special-use airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_use_airspace), and enforcement within their own FIRs.&#x20;

Airspace can be used for both [civilian flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation) and for defence or military operations, and countries are responsible for protecting their own airspace using measures such as [air policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_Reaction_Alert). Legal and technical challenges include the vertical boundary between airspace and outer space and temporary restrictions such as [NOTAMs](1517785.md) or [TFRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Flight_Restriction).&#x20;

## Legal framework and ownership

On 4 April 1947, the [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md) (ICAO) was established through the [Chicago Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_International_Civil_Aviation), aimed at unifying and coordinating international air travel.[^cite_note-icao-convention-2] ICAO provides regulations and guidance for countries concerning air navigation, its infrastructure, [flight inspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_inspection), prevention of unlawful interference, and facilitation of border-crossing procedures for international [civil aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation).[^cite_note-3]&#x20;

Sovereign airspace refers to airspace owned by a country. By law, a country has the right to airspace above its land and waters up to 12 miles from its coast (if present). Beyond 12 miles, airspace is considered international.[^cite_note-4] ICAO divides airspace into [Flight Information Regions (FIR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_region), which are sections of airspace dedicated to a specific country. Within its own FIR(s), a country is responsible for providing air traffic control services and regulating flight.[^cite_note-\:3-5][^cite_note-\:4-6] This is usually done using a national regulator, such as the [FAA](11186.md), [CAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Authority_\(United_Kingdom\)), or [EASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency), which are able to create their own regulations while still abiding by ICAO guidelines.[^cite_note-7] Some countries may have multiple FIRs, for example, the United States has sole jurisdiction in 24 FIRs.[^cite_note-8] A country may, by international agreement, assume responsibility for providing [air traffic control services](48563.md) in parts of international airspace, which then becomes part of their FIR.[^cite_note-9] Certain FIRs are dedicated oceanic sectors which support and control air traffic crossing large oceans such as the [North Atlantic](698.md), which is made up of 7 FIRs controlled by countries like the [UK](31717.md), [Ireland](14560.md), and [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-10]&#x20;

&#x20;There is no international agreement on the vertical extent of sovereign airspace,[^cite_note-Harris_&_Harris_2006-11] although most countries use the [Kármán Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A1rm%C3%A1n_Line), 100km (62mi), as the legal distinction between airspace and outer space.[^cite_note-Byers_&_Boley_2021-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-Dr._S._Sanz_Fernández_de_Córdoba-14] However, descending [Space Shuttles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) flew closer than 80 km (50 mi) over other nations, such as Canada, without requesting permission first.[^cite_note-15]

### Private air rights

The boundary between public airspace and private [air rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_rights) is defined by national or local law.&#x20;

In the United States, the 1946 Supreme Court decision *[United States v. Causby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Causby)* overturned the [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) doctrine that private property rights extend indefinitely upwards, instead ruling that they end 300 feet above the highest terrain (including buildings), the lower limit of the "public highway" defined by Congress in the [Air Commerce Act of 1926](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Commerce_Act_of_1926). The [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) regulates the construction and marking of structures taller than 200 feet near airports and 500 feet generally.[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

## Classification

Airspace is divided into classes, which dictate what services are provided in different areas. The classes are defined by ICAO, but countries are free to allocate different sections of airspace to different classes as they wish. There are 7 classes, A-G, with progressively less strict restrictions. This classification system was adopted by ICAO on 12 March 1990.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:1-18]&#x20;

### Controlled vs uncontrolled

#### Controlled airspace

Main article: [Controlled airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_airspace)



[United States](3434750.md) implementation of ICAO airspace classification

[Controlled airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_airspace)[^cite_note-22] exists where it is deemed necessary that [air traffic control](48563.md) has some form of positive executive control over aircraft flying in that airspace (however, air traffic control does not necessarily control traffic operating under [visual flight rules (VFR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_flight_rules)[^cite_note-23] within this airspace). Controlled airspace consists of [classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspace_class) A-E.[^cite_note-\:1-18] Special types of controlled airspace, such as Control Zones (CTR) and Aerodrome Traffic Zones (ATZ) exist to protect aircraft in the vicinity of an airport, typically when arriving or departing.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]&#x20;

##### Uncontrolled airspace

Main article: [Uncontrolled airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncontrolled_airspace)

[Uncontrolled airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncontrolled_airspace) is airspace in which an [air traffic control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Traffic_Control) (ATC) service is not deemed necessary or cannot be provided for practical reasons. Uncontrolled airspace consists of classes F and G.[^cite_note-\:1-18] A [flight information service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_service) may still be provided to participating aircraft in some areas, depending on practicality and availability.[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

### Special use airspace

Main article: [Special use airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_use_airspace)

[Special use airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_use_airspace) (SUA) is airspace designated for operations that may cause limitations on other, uninvolved aircraft and exist to allow countries to separate certain areas of airspace that could be dangerous to flights within them. SUA may be controlled or uncontrolled, and does not have any specific classes associated with it.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-\:0-28]&#x20;

SUA includes:&#x20;

- [Prohibited airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibited_airspace) is an area where flight is prohibited, usually for safety reasons.

- [Restricted airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_airspace) is an area where flight is restricted or subject to limitations, and may require permission to enter.

#### Temporary restrictions

Airspace may be temporarily restricted for safety. This may be via a [NOTAM](1517785.md) or [temporary flight restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Regulations) (TFR) for a variety of reasons, such as protecting major sporting events, natural disaster areas, air shows, or VIP movements.[^cite_note-\:8-29][^cite_note-\:7-30]&#x20;

In the UK, airspace can be temporarily restricted by using a prohibited area, restricted area, or danger area—which designates areas where dangerous activities to flight may be taking place.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]&#x20;

## Defense



A [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) on QRA takes off to conduct training intercepting a passenger aircraft.

In countries with an established [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military), airspace is usually defended to protect national sovereignty. While military flights have to share airspace with civilians, they may use [military training routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_training_route) or other special use airspace to protect civilian flights from dangerous activities.[^cite_note-33]&#x20;

### NATO Air policing

Further information: [Quick Reaction Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_Reaction_Alert)

[NATO](21133.md) conducts air policing missions to protect sovereign European airspace from both enemy military aircraft and civilian aircraft that fail to identify themselves, fail to communicate with air traffic control, or don't follow a flight plan.[^cite_note-NATO-1-34] They do this by keeping [fighter aircraft](10929.md) in a 24/7 state of [quick reaction alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_Reaction_Alert), ready to be scrambled at any time.[^cite_note-\:5-35]&#x20;

For example, the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) maintains four [Eurofighter Typhoons](167667.md) at 24/7 readiness: two at [RAF Coningsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Coningsby) and two at [RAF Lossiemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Lossiemouth).[^cite_note-\:5-35] Some NATO states do not have the capability to conduct QRA over their own territory, such as in the Baltic and Icelandic areas, so other NATO countries provide aircraft to cover instead.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In the United States, the quick reaction alert mission is referred to as Aerospace Control Alert, after having been renamed from Air Sovereignty Alert in 2011.[^cite_note-38] The majority of aerospace control alert missions in the U.S. are carried out by the [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard) using the [F-15 Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15_Eagle) and [F-16 Fighting Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcon) jets.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]&#x20;

### Air defense identification zones (ADIZ)

Main article: [Air defense identification zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_defense_identification_zone)

An [air defense identification zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_defense_identification_zone) is a region of airspace where a country identifies, locates, and controls aircraft to protect [national security](240468.md), which, in many cases, can cover airspace outside of the country's sovereign airspace.[^cite_note-Abeyratne-41][^cite_note-\:6-42] A country may impose limitations on aircraft within them, such as requiring a flight plan to be filed.&#x20;

In 2013, [China](5405.md) established an [ADIZ in the East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Defense_Identification_Zone_\(East_China_Sea\)), drawing criticisms from neighbouring countries and the United States, as it overlaps existing zones and imposes requirements on both civilian and military aircraft.[^cite_note-WSJNov13-43][^cite_note-GlobSec2-44][^cite_note-nytimes.com2-45] Since 2010, China has been planning to establish an ADIZ over the [South China Sea](74209.md), which would raise tensions in the [existing territorial disputes](32005536.md). However, as of 2025, no ADIZ has been created.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]&#x20;

[Russian](25391.md) aircraft are frequently tracked by the [United States](3434750.md) entering their [Alaska](624.md) ADIZ,[^cite_note-Abeyratne2-48] often triggering QRA responses of American fighters.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]&#x20;

### Violations and incidents

An airspace violation occurs when an aircraft enters controlled or national airspace without authorisation. Such incidents may be accidental, such as navigation or communication errors, or they may be deliberate, such as military reconnaissance or probing.[^cite_note-51]&#x20;

In the [Cold War](325329.md), aircraft such as [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007) were shot down due to misidentification as enemy military aircraft.[^cite_note-52] In 2023, a Chinese high altitude [balloon entered US airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chinese_balloon_incident),[^cite_note-53] allegedly for intelligence gathering, and was subsequently shot down by an American jet, increasing tensions between the two countries.[^cite_note-54] In August and September 2025, Russia [repeatedly flew drones and aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violations_of_non-combatant_airspaces_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)) into [NATO](21133.md) airspace, prompting NATO to launch [Operation Eastern Sentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Eastern_Sentry) after an [Article 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Article_4) declaration.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

## Modernization

### Performance-based navigation

Main article: [Performance-based navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance-based_navigation)

[Performance-based navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance-based_navigation) (PBN) is a type of navigation that allows aircraft to use GPS and other satellite navigation instead of only ground-based stations like [VORs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHF_omnidirectional_range) which improves airspace safety, capacity, and efficiency.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] ICAO and national authorities such as the FAA are gradually redesigning controlled and uncontrolled airspace to support [Required Navigation Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Required_navigation_performance) (RNP) and [Area Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_navigation) (RNAV) routes.[^cite_note-58] This changes allow for more efficient flight paths and reduce the need of maintaining specific physical sensors.[^cite_note-59] Projects led by service providers such as [Hughes Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Aircraft_Company) have demonstrated how PBN procedures can be used to optimize traffic flows and reduce noise in congested areas,[^cite_note-60] such as the [Naples Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Airport_\(Florida\)) in Florida.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]&#x20;
