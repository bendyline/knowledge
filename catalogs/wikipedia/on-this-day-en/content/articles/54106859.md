# Prisons in Pakistan

&#x20; **Prisons in Pakistan** and their administration, is a Provincial competency under the [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-1] Pakistan has the 23rd largest prison population in the world[^cite_note-2] and the 5th largest death row population.[^cite_note-3] Around 64.5% of prisoners are awaiting trial prisoners. 98.6% of prisoners are male, 1.7% are juveniles and 1.2% of those held are foreign citizens.[^cite_note-4] With a total inmate population of 102,026 distributed across 128 correctional facilities spanning the four provinces, [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md), and [Azad Jammu and Kashmir](2745.md), the country's prison system grapples with severe operational pressures, further exacerbated by the fact that over three-quarters of the prison population, 74,918 individuals, remain under trial.[^cite_note-EOS-5]

## Types of prisons

The most common and standard jail institutions are Central Jails, District Jails and Sub Jails. The other types of jail establishments are Women Jails, Borstal Schools, and Special Jails.

Rehabilitation and diversion programs remain largely neglected, with no community service as an alternative sentence. Vulnerable groups, women, juveniles, and individuals with psychosocial [disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disabilities_affecting_intellectual_abilities), lack adequate protections. Meaningful reform demands the urgent enforcement of revised Prison Rules and alignment with global standards, including the [Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandela_Rules), [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Rules), and [Beijing Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Rules), to foster a just and humane penal system.[^cite_note-EOS-5]

## List of Prisons

Pakistan's 128 prisons, designed for 65,811 inmates, remain severely overcrowded despite infrastructural expansions. Resource constraints and a punitive reliance on detention continue to define the system.[^cite_note-EOS-5]

Pakistan has four dedicated women's prisons—three in Sindh (Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur) and one in Punjab (Multan), while most female inmates are housed in separate barracks within male prisons. Juvenile offenders are accommodated in five facilities, including *Youth Offenders Industrial School* and *[Borstal Institutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borstal_Institution_and_Juvenile_Jail_Bahawalpur)*, with two in Punjab (Faisalabad, Bahawalpur) and three in Sindh (Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur).[^cite_note-EOS-5]

## Training

### Federal

Main article: [National Academy for Prisons Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_for_Prisons_Administration)

The National Academy for Prisons Administration is the [Federal Government's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_Pakistan) department for setting the standards of prison staff and is in charge of the training for all the prison staff in every provinces of Pakistan. It operates under [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_and_Narcotics_Control). The academy is based in [Lahore](125315.md).[^cite_note-13]

### Sindh

Government of Sindh runs a training institute at Sindh Prisons Staff Training Institute Nara Hyderabad.[^cite_note-sindh.gov.pk-6]

### Punjab

Government of Punjab runs Punjab Prisons Staff Training Institute in Lahore.[^cite_note-14]
