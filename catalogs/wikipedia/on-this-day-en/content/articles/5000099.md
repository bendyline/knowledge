The **Fars News Agency** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): خبرگزاری فارس) is a [news agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_agency) in Iran that is close to the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC),[^cite_note-z700-1][^cite_note-w972-2][^cite_note-k736-3] one of the three branches of the [Islamic Republic of Iran Armed Forces](14305018.md). While it describes itself as "Iran's leading independent news agency",[^cite_note-aboutus-4] it is widely described by Western news media to be a "semi-official" news agency of the [Government of Iran](50913538.md).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Fars has been described by the Iran Disinformation Project as the cornerstone of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' disinformation and propaganda campaign. Fars News' disinformation campaign "reaches across Iran's borders, spreading rumors and lies about dissidents, human rights, labor, political activists, and intellectuals."[^cite_note-irandisinfo-8] One propaganda technique is to consistently publish interviews with western pundits and analysts such as conspiracy theorist [James Fetzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Fetzer) who echo Tehran's propaganda.[^cite_note-9]

## History

The Fars News Agency was founded in 2003, a time when [IRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRNA) and [ISNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISNA_\(news_agency\)) were under [Mohammad Khatami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Khatami)'s [reformist government control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Mohammad_Khatami).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] According to Iran Disinformation Project, [Hamidreza Moghaddamfar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamidreza_Moghaddamfar), advisor to IRGC [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_commanders_of_the_Islamic_Revolutionary_Guard_Corps), was CEO of Fars from 2007 to 2011. He was replaced by Nizam-al-Din Mousavi, then-head of [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran)'s [Student Basij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_Basij) and IRGC-linked [*Javan* newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javan_\(newspaper\)). Under Mousavi, Fars "\[launched] the career of many Iranian conspiracy theorists", covering personalities like [Ali-Akbar Raefipour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali-Akbar_Raefipour) and [Hassan Abbasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Abbasi).[^cite_note-irandisinfo-8][^cite_note-12]

In addition to [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) reporting, the agency also provides news in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language),[^cite_note-13] [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language),[^cite_note-\:4-14] [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language),[^cite_note-15] and [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language).[^cite_note-16] Fars formerly published all content on its website under the [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Commons_license#Version_4.0).[^cite_note-\:4-14]

### Sanctions

The Fars News Agency [.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.com) domain has been blocked by sanctions applied by the US Treasury since 25 January 2020.[^cite_note-17] On 15 September 2023, the [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md) added the Fars News Agency to the [Office of Foreign Assets Control](231045.md) (OFAC)'s [Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Nationals_and_Blocked_Persons_List).[^cite_note-18]

## Controversial stories

### Interview with Egyptian president

In June 2012, Fars released an interview with [Egyptian president](335341.md) [Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Morsi) in which Morsi is said to have told Fars that he wanted to restore ties with Iran and wanted to "review" the [Egypt–Israel peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_peace_treaty).[^cite_note-\:0-19] Morsi later disputed the authenticity of the interview.[^cite_note-\:0-19] Fars responded by providing what it said was audio of the interview.[^cite_note-\:0-19] Arabic newscaster [Al Arabiya](321645.md) quoted unnamed experts who said it was not Morsi's voice.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

### Reposted story by *The Onion*

In September 2012, the agency picked up a story from *[The Onion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Onion)*, a satirical newspaper, about a supposed survey showing "an overwhelming majority of rural white Americans would rather vote for [Iranian President](233913.md) [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) than [U.S. President](24113.md) [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) in the upcoming U.S. elections".[^cite_note-Martinez-20][^cite_note-McGreal-21] The Iranian version copied the original word-for-word, even including a made-up quote from a fictional [West Virginia](32905.md) resident who says he would rather go to a baseball game with Ahmadinejad because "he takes national defense seriously, and he'd never let some gay protesters tell him how to run his country like Obama does."[^cite_note-Martinez-20][^cite_note-McGreal-21]

The Fars News Agency later apologized for its mistake, but claimed that a majority of Americans would prefer anyone outside of the American political system to President Obama and American statesmen.[^cite_note-22]

### Time machine story

In April 2013, the agency carried a story claiming a 27-year-old Iranian scientist had invented a [time machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel) that allowed people to see into the future. A few days later the story was removed, and replaced with a story quoting an Iranian government official that no such device had been registered.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Alien/extraterrestrial intelligence agenda reports

In January 2014, Fars posted a series of articles that suggested U.S. security policy was being driven by an "alien/extraterrestrial intelligence agenda".[^cite_note-\:1-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-\:2-27] The report said that proof was found in a [Federal Security Service](385990.md) report carried out by [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden).[^cite_note-\:1-25][^cite_note-\:2-27] The report said that the United States government had been secretly run by a "shadow government" of space aliens since 1945.[^cite_note-28]

### Salman Rushdie fatwa

In February 2016, Fars was one of 40 Iranian news agencies that pledged money toward the bounty against [Salman Rushdie](28722.md) in regards to [The Satanic Verses controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Satanic_Verses_controversy).[^cite_note-\:3-29] Fars promised \$30,000 for the killing of Rushdie.[^cite_note-\:3-29]

### Yemenite Jews

In November 2020, the Agency sparked controversy after it shared an article by *[Kayhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayhan)* editor [Hossein Shariatmadari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Shariatmadari) [denying the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial) and saying that the "real Holocaust" was against [Yemenite Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Yemen) by the Jewish [Himyarite Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himyarite_Kingdom) in 524 CE. Critics denounced the article as an [antisemitic](1078.md) piece seeking to dehumanize Jews and downplay the brutality of the Holocaust.[^cite_note-30]

### "Where to Kill Trump?" video

In July 2026, Fars published a video on [X](9988187.md) and [Telegram](41501305.md) titled "Where to Kill Trump?", purporting to show the route taken by the [presidential motorcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_motorcade) of [Donald Trump](4848272.md) to his [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) residence.[^cite_note-y625-31]
