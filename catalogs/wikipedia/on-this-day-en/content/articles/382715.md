**Kouvola** (Finnish pronunciation: [\[ˈkou̯ʋolɑ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Finnish)) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in [Finland](10577.md) and the administrative capital of [Kymenlaakso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymenlaakso). It is located in the southeastern interior of the country. The population of Kouvola is approximately 78,000. It is the 12th most populous [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Finland) in Finland, and the 17th most populous [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Finland_by_population) in the country.&#x20;

Kouvola is located along the [Kymijoki River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymijoki) in the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Finland) of [Kymenlaakso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymenlaakso), 62 kilometres (39 mi) kilometers east of [Lahti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahti), 87 kilometres (54 mi) west of [Lappeenranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lappeenranta) and 134 kilometres (83 mi) northeast of the capital, [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki). With [Kotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotka), Kouvola is one of the capital centers and is the largest city in the Kymenlaakso region.

The urban area of Kouvola in the [city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown) itself is home to about 47,000 people.[^cite_note-9] The city covers an area of 2,883.30 square kilometres (1,113.25 sq mi) of which 325.06 km<sup>2</sup> (125.51 sq mi) is water.[^cite_note-total_area-5] The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) is 30.35 inhabitants per square kilometre (78.6/sq mi). Kouvola is bordered by the municipalities of [Hamina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamina), [Heinola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinola), [Iitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iitti), Kotka, [Lapinjärvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapinj%C3%A4rvi_\(municipality\)), [Loviisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loviisa), [Luumäki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luum%C3%A4ki), [Miehikkälä](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miehikk%C3%A4l%C3%A4), [Mäntyharju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4ntyharju), [Pyhtää](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyht%C3%A4%C3%A4) and [Savitaipale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitaipale). Kouvola has over 450 lakes and, together with Mäntyharju, the Kouvola area includes the [Repovesi National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repovesi_National_Park).[^cite_note-helsinki-times-10]

Kouvola, which had population growth as late as the 1980s, has suffered a loss of population due to migration since the 1990s. Over time, the loss has only deepened, so that at the end of the 2010s Kouvola had the highest rate of population loss by migration in Finland. Natural demographics have also trended downward; in 2017, more than 450 more people died in the city than new ones were born.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-yle-kouvola-vakiluku-12][^cite_note-ylioppilaslehti-13] Currently, the population of Kouvola is decreasing annually by about 800 inhabitants.[^cite_note-14] The reasons for the emigration are thought to be largely due to job losses in the region.[^cite_note-yle-kouvola-vakiluku-12][^cite_note-ylioppilaslehti-13] The city has placed expectations for economic recovery on various projects, such as the [TikTok](56822861.md) data center planned for [Koria village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koria).[^cite_note-15]

## History

In the Middle Ages, the Kouvola region were borderlands between the provinces of [Uusimaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uusimaa_\(historical_province\)), [Häme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A4me) and [Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelia_\(historical_province\)). The [Kymijoki River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymijoki_River) served as the border river between [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) and the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) from [1743](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_%C3%85bo) to [1809](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fredrikshamn).

The village of Kouvola has been inhabited since the Middle Ages, and it has belonged alternately to the parishes of [Hollola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollola), [Iitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iitti) and [Valkeala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkeala). However, the actual development did not start until the 1870s when the [Riihimäki–Saint Petersburg railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riihim%C3%A4ki%E2%80%93Saint_Petersburg_railway) was built and Kouvola became a railway junction.[^cite_note-helsinki-times-10] In the next decade, the [Savonia railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savonia_railway) was built from Kouvola to the north and the [Kotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouvola-Kotka_railway) line to the south, resulting in Kouvola becoming one of the busiest railway junctions in Finland. Over time, Kouvola developed into an important [pulp-producing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_\(paper\)), [paper-milling](417020.md) and printing centre and even had a leading [industrial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering) sector.[^cite_note-helsinki-times-10]

In 1918, conflict between the Red and White factions raged heavily during the [Finnish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War). More than 200 people were killed in the area during the fighting.

As a result of the railway, Kouvola was heavily built. In 1922 it was separated from the municipality of Valkeala and gained commercial rights immediately the following year. The city of Kouvola was established in 1960. Kouvola was annexed to [Viipuri Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viipuri_Province) in 1922–1945 but in 1940 and 1944, most of Viipuri County was ceded to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and the remaining areas were formed into [Kymi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymi_Province) in 1945. Kouvola had also become an administrative center; As the capital of Kymen County, it operated from 1955 until the 1997 county reform.

In January 2009, the six municipalities of Kouvola, [Kuusankoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuusankoski), [Elimäki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elim%C3%A4ki), [Anjalankoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjalankoski), [Valkeala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkeala) and [Jaala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaala) were consolidated, forming the new municipality of Kouvola. Kouvola has also assumed the slogan *Kymijoen kaupunki* (the town of [Kymijoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymijoki)) previously used by Anjalankoski.[^cite_note-16]

In March 2026, [two Ukrainian drones fell](83132995.md#March_incursions) in Kouvola. Prime Minister [Petteri Orpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petteri_Orpo) told the media that the drones likely came from Ukraine, which [has been conducting attacks](70149799.md) on Russian territory along the [border with Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland%E2%80%93Russia_border).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Culture

- The language spoken in the Kouvola area is based on the Southeastern Häme dialect, which belongs to the [Häme dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A4me_dialects).

- The name itself derives from Old Finnish *kouvo*, meaning [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_bear).

  [^cite_note-\:0-19]

  &#x20;The arms are *Sable, an escarbuncle Or, base wavy Argent*.

- The nickname *Kouvostoliitto* ("Koviet Union") is a humorous, self-deprecating play on words used for Kouvola, combining the city's name with *Neuvostoliitto* (the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)); the nickname stems from the city's concrete architecture, which reminds many of the stark style characteristic of the Soviet era—namely, [Brutalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture).

  [^cite_note-yle-koivisto-2]

  [^cite_note-20]

- [Verla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verla) factory, which is a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md), is located near Kouvola.

  [^cite_note-helsinki-times-10]

- The newspapers *[Kouvolan Sanomat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouvolan_Sanomat)* and *[Keskilaakso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keskilaakso)* are published in Kouvola.

- The third biggest [Amusement park](137327.md) in Finland, called [Tykkimäki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tykkim%C3%A4ki) is located in Kouvola.

## Sports

Kouvola has multiple clubs that compete in the highest tier of various sports. [KooKoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KooKoo_\(ice_hockey\)) plays [ice hockey](14790.md) in the men's Finnish top league [SM-Liiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liiga). [Kouvolan Pallonlyöjät](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouvolan_Pallonly%C3%B6j%C3%A4t) (KPL) is considered one of the best [pesäpallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pes%C3%A4pallo) teams in the country, having won five championships in the Finnish men's top flight [Superpesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpesis). [Kouvot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouvot) is the city's [basketball](3921.md) club that has won four top flight championships and currently plays in the men's [Korisliiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korisliiga), while the women's team Kouvottaret plays in the second tier I divisioona. [Kuusankosken Puhti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuusankosken_Puhti?action=edit\&redlink=1) plays pesäpallo in the women's second tier [Ykköspesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ykk%C3%B6spesis).

[MyPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myllykosken_Pallo_%E2%88%9247) from the industrial village of Myllykoski now part of Kouvola achieved success in [football](10568.md) during the 1990s and 2000s winning the Finnish men's top league [Veikkausliiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veikkausliiga) once and the [Finnish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Cup) three times, and is now playing in the fourth tier of Finnish football [Kakkonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakkonen). [Kouvolan Jalkapallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouvolan_Jalkapallo?action=edit\&redlink=1) (KJP) is the biggest football youth team in Kouvola with over 800 members[^cite_note-21], while their adult men's and women's teams both play in the fifth tier, [Kolmonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmonen) and [Naisten Nelonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naisten_Nelonen?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-22] respectively. [Sudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudet) was a multi-sports club from [Vyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg) and [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) that moved to Kouvola in 1962. They had achieved success in both [bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy) and football prior to the move, winning the men's bandy championship 14 times[^cite_note-23] and the [Finnish football championship once](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Mestaruussarja_%E2%80%93_Finnish_League_Competition). The club is currently active in Kouvola only in men's floorball, playing in the Finnish lower leagues, but their namesake [Wiipurin Sudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiipurin_Sudet?action=edit\&redlink=1) is one of the top women's clubs in bandy, operating in [Lappeenranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lappeenranta). [Valkealan Kajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkealan_Kajo?action=edit\&redlink=1) is a multi-sports club from [Valkeala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkeala) that has achieved success in [volleyball](32558.md), winning the women's championship in 1998.[^cite_note-24]

Kouvola is active in winter sports; It has the Palomäki Ski Jump Center for [ski jumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_jumping), and multiple tracks for [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing) throughout the town during the winter.[^cite_note-25] [Orienteering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orienteering) is also popular in the summertime.

## Climate



[ UPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPM_\(company\)) Kymi ([Paper mill](417020.md))

Kouvola has [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfb*).The city has four distinct seasons, the amount of precipitation is relatively uniform throughout the year. The driest season is spring. Summers are generally relatively warm as the city is considered to be one of the hottest cities in Finland as it has had the most days with "helle" (temperatures above 25 °C or 77 °F) since 2000. Winters are colder than cities located in the coastal areas of Finland because of its location further inland, meaning the marine effect doesn't affect the city as much than those nearer the sea. This also means that the summers are warmer due to it being further inland as the sea effect is making the summer temperatures slightly cooler in coastal areas. The average annual temperature is 5.4 °C (41.7 °F). The highest ever recorded temperature in the station was 34.5 °C (94.1 °F), on July 28, 2010. The lowest ever recorded was −37.3 °C (−35.1 °F). The city has a significant rainfall throughout the year and even in the driest month the precipitation stays over 30 millimetres (1.2 in) a month. The average duration of sunshine per year is 1,658 hours. Kouvola Utti airport weather station has on average somewhat lower average temperatures than in the city.

Kouvola is a monolingual [Finnish-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language) municipality. The majority of the population, 72,506 persons (93.4%), spoke Finnish as their first language. In addition, the number of [Swedish speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish-speaking_population_of_Finland) was 275 persons (0.4%) of the population. Foreign languages were spoken by 6.2% of the population.[^cite_note-statistics-finland-population-2025-28] As [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Swedish](26689.md) are compulsory school subjects, functional bilingualism or trilingualism acquired through language studies is not uncommon.

At least 40 different languages are spoken in Kouvola. The most commonly spoken foreign languages are [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) (2.0%), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) (0.6%), [Estonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_language) (0.5%) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) (0.3%).[^cite_note-statistics-finland-population-2024-final-27]

The language spoken in the Kouvola area is based on the Southeast Häme dialect, which belongs to the [Tavastian dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavastian_dialects).

As of 2024, there were 4,731 persons with a foreign background living in Kouvola, or 6% of the population.[^cite_note-30] The number of residents who were born abroad was 4,608, or 6% of the population. The number of persons with foreign citizenship living in Kouvola was 3,008.[^cite_note-statistics-finland-population-2024-final-27] Most foreign-born citizens came from the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [Estonia](28222445.md), [Russia](25391.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-statistics-finland-population-2024-final-27]

The relative share of immigrants in Kouvola's population is below to the national average. However, the city's new residents are increasingly of foreign origin. This will increase the proportion of foreign residents in the coming years.

### Religion

In 2023, the [Evangelical Lutheran Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_of_Finland) was the largest religious group with 69.2% of the population of Kouvola. Other religious groups accounted for 2.0% of the population. 28.8% of the population had no religious affiliation.[^cite_note-31]
