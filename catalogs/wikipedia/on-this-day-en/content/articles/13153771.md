An [Islamist](15012.md) [insurgency](589841.md) is taking place in the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) region of [North Africa](21714.md), followed on from the end of the [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War) in 2002.[^cite_note-CTC_2021-47] The Algerian militant group [Salafist Group for Preaching and Combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafist_Group_for_Preaching_and_Combat) (GSPC) allied itself with [al-Qaeda](1921.md) to eventually become [al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb) (AQIM).[^cite_note-NYT-48] The Algerian and other Maghreb governments fighting the militants have worked with the United States and the United Kingdom since 2007, when [Operation Enduring Freedom – Trans Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom_%E2%80%93_Trans_Sahara) began.[^cite_note-Flintlock-5][^cite_note-af.reuters.com-49]

While the 2011 [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) affected support for the insurgency, it also presented military opportunities for the [jihadists](7826589.md).[^cite_note-Stanford-19][^cite_note-NYT-48][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] In 2012, AQIM and Islamist allies [captured the northern half of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Civil_War). They held the territory for almost a year, until being forced out of the urban areas during a [French-led foreign intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Serval), which was succeeded by the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel)-wide [Operation Barkhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barkhane).[^cite_note-CFR-1][^cite_note-Stanford-19] In [Libya](17633.md), the [Islamic State](9087364.md) was able to control some limited territory during the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War), amid allegations of local collaboration with its AQIM rival.[^cite_note-ibtimes-53][^cite_note-Stanford-19][^cite_note-aqimisil-54]

## Background

Main article: [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War)

With the decline of the [Armed Islamic Group of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Islamic_Group_of_Algeria) (GIA), the GSPC was left as the most active rebel group, with about 300 fighters in 2003.[^cite_note-55] It continued an [assassination campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_campaign) of police and army personnel in its area, and also managed to expand into the [Sahara](325363.md), where its southern division, led by [Amari Saifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amari_Saifi) (nicknamed "Abderrezak el-Para", the "paratrooper"), kidnapped a number of German tourists in 2003, before being forced to flee to sparsely populated areas of [Mali](19127.md), and later [Niger](21373.md) and [Chad](5488.md), where he was captured.

Some believe that el-Para actually works for the Algerian government. By late 2003, the group's founder had been supplanted by the even more radical [Nabil Sahraoui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabil_Sahraoui), who announced his open support for [al-Qaeda](1921.md), thus strengthening government ties between the U.S. and Algeria. He was reportedly killed shortly afterwards, and was succeeded by [Abu Musab Abdel Wadoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_Abdel_Wadoud) in 2004.[^cite_note-56]

The GSPC has declared its intention to attack Algerian, French, and American targets. It has been designated as a [Foreign Terrorist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._State_Department_list_of_Foreign_Terrorist_Organizations) by the [U.S. Department of State](31975.md), and similarly classed as a terrorist organization by the [European Union](9317.md).

## Overview

### Insurgency in Algeria

The conflict with the GSPC continued to result in a significant number of casualties in Algeria, with over 1,100 killed in clashes with Islamist rebels in 2002.[^cite_note-LAT03-38][^cite_note-freedom03-39][^cite_note-2002algeria-40] In 2003, a total 1,162 were killed in clashes in Algeria, followed by 429 killed in 2004, 488 killed in 2005, and 323 killed in 2006.[^cite_note-Bergner-41] In early 2006, the head of the Algerian national police claimed that terrorism had nearly been eliminated in the country, but significant attacks continued,[^cite_note-Bergner-41] and 2007 would eventually mark a height of suicide bombings and terrorist attacks in Algeria.[^cite_note-CFR-1]

### Broadening of conflict

In order to improve recruiting and funding, the GSPC aligned itself with al-Qaeda, and on 11 September 2006, al-Qaeda leader [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri) announced a union between the groups.[^cite_note-CFR-1] The GSPC rebranded itself as al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM) in January 2007, signaling the broadened aspirations of the group.[^cite_note-CFR-1]

The group now aimed to overthrow all North African governments deemed apostate, including those of Algeria, Libya, Mali, Mauritania, Morocco and Tunisia.[^cite_note-CFR-1] Operations were shifted into two broader "sectors", Northern Algeria and Tunisia allocated a "central emirate", and northern Mali, Niger, Mauritania and Libya a "Sahara emirate" led by [Djamel Okacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamel_Okacha).[^cite_note-Boeke-57] The strategic leadership of AQIM continued to be headquartered in the mountainous region of [Kabylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabylie) east of the Algerian capital [Algiers](1644.md), headed by a 14-member Shura council leadership.[^cite_note-Boeke-57]

As the Algerian counterterrorism campaign became largely successful in pushing AQIM out of the country, the group established new bases in Sahel countries such as Niger, Mauritania, Chad and Mali.[^cite_note-CFR-1] Attacks against government and military installations were frequently underreported by Western media.[^cite_note-Boeke-57] In 2007, the United States and United Kingdom launched the [Operation Enduring Freedom – Trans Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Juniper_Shield) in support of governments in the region.[^cite_note-Flintlock-5][^cite_note-af.reuters.com-49]

Frequent kidnappings of foreigners in 2008 [led the Dakar Rally to be cancelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Dakar_Rally) and permanently moved to South America.[^cite_note-Boeke-57]

### Mauritania

In the earliest major attack as direct spillover of the Algerian conflict, the Mauritanian army base at Lemgheity was [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemgheity_attack) by the GSPC in June 2005, killing 17 soldiers (and nine jihadists), and wounding another 17.[^cite_note-Boeke-57][^cite_note-Ibrahim-58] The attack in part led to [a coup d'état in October 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Mauritanian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) by [Mohamed Ould Abdel Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Ould_Abdel_Aziz), who made a campaign against jihadists a major part of his rule.[^cite_note-Ibrahim-58] Attacks continued in Mauritania until it was largely successful in thwarting attacks since 2011 following a major military boost and political openings for Islamists.[^cite_note-Ibrahim-58] AQIM has however continued to remain active in the eastern border-regions with Mali, with active support systems for logistics and information.[^cite_note-59] A lack of military resources, often due to turmoil and having little population in a very large country, caused Mauritania to rely on support from France, Morocco, and Algeria in order to defeat the AQIM.

### Tunisia

In December 2006 and again in January 2007, [Tunisian security forces engaged in clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_and_2007_Tunisia_clashes) with a group linked to the GSPC that had established training camps in mountainous areas near the capital Tunis, killing more than a dozen people.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] According to French daily *[Le Parisien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Parisien)* at least 60 people were killed in the clashes.[^cite_note-weekly-63] The clashes were the most serious terrorist activity in Tunisia since the [Ghriba synagogue bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghriba_synagogue_bombing) in 2002.[^cite_note-weekly-63]

Starting in 2012, AQIM along with [Ansar al-Sharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Sharia_\(Tunisia\)) and the [Uqba ibn Nafi Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uqba_ibn_Nafi_Brigade) active in the mountainous [Jebel ech Chambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_ech_Chambi) region outside [Kasserine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasserine) near the [Algeria–Tunisia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Tunisia_border) have been targeted by the Tunisian Army in the [Chaambi Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaambi_Operations).[^cite_note-ctc230613-17][^cite_note-64] In 2014, Uqba ibn Nafi Brigade militants [attacked two Tunisian military checkpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Chaambi_Mountains_attack), killing fourteen Tunisian soldiers and injuring twenty-five in what was the deadliest military skirmish in Tunisia since its independence in 1956.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Since 2015, Tunisia has simultaneously been targeted by an [IS insurgency campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_insurgency_in_Tunisia). In March 2016 over 50 militants were killed when IS fighters [attempted to seize Ben Guerdane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ben_Guerdane) near the [Libya–Tunisia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya%E2%80%93Tunisia_border).[^cite_note-reuters.com-67]

### Libyan Civil War

Since the [Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)) in 2011, south-western Libya has offered sanctuaries to AQIM which has dispatched cells to be established in the region.[^cite_note-Boeke-57] On 11 September 2012, members of [Ansar al-Sharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Sharia_\(Libya\)) and AQIM were responsible for [coordinated attacks against two United States government facilities in Benghazi](37018641.md), and Ansar al-Sharia was later involved in [clashes in Benghazi in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Benghazi_conflict). Drawing defectors from AQIM,[^cite_note-68] the rival [Islamic State](9087364.md) (IS) organization was later able to control some limited territory in the north in the [renewed civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)) from 2014. After initial official support from AQIM, allegations have continued of local collaboration between the otherwise rivalling groups.[^cite_note-ibtimes-53][^cite_note-Stanford-19][^cite_note-aqimisil-54] After [being pushed out of Derna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna_campaign_\(2014%E2%80%9316\)), the remaining IS stronghold of [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte) was [captured in late 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sirte_\(2016\)). Al-Qaeda-affiliated Islamists led by Ansar al-Sharia have at the same time continued to exert control in other places.
