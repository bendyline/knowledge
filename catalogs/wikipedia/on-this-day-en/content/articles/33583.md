The **World Health Organization** (**WHO**) is a [specialized agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_specialized_agencies_of_the_United_Nations) of the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) which coordinates responses to international [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) issues and [emergencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency).[^cite_note-The-Henry-J-Kaiser-Family-Foundation-2019-2] It is headquartered in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), Switzerland, and has six regional offices[^cite_note-3] and 150 field offices worldwide. Only sovereign states are eligible to join, and it is the largest intergovernmental health organization at the international level.[^cite_note-4]

The WHO's stated purpose is to achieve the highest possible level of health for all the world's people, defining health as "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity."[^cite_note-5] The main functions of the World Health Organization include promoting the control of [epidemic](66981.md) and [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(epidemiology\)) diseases; providing and improving the teaching and training in public health, the medical treatment of disease, and related matters; and promoting the establishment of international standards for biological products.

The [WHO was established](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Establishment_of_the_World_Health_Organization) on 7 April 1948, and formally began its work on 1 September 1948.[^cite_note-Ascher-1952-6] It incorporated the assets, personnel, and duties of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations)' Health Organization and the Paris-based [Office International d'Hygiène Publique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Office_of_Public_Hygiene), including the [International Classification of Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Classification_of_Diseases) (ICD).[^cite_note-7] The agency's work began in earnest in 1951 after a significant infusion of financial and technical resources.[^cite_note-8]

The WHO's official mandate is to promote health and safety while helping the vulnerable worldwide. It provides technical assistance to countries, sets international health standards, collects data on global health issues, and serves as a forum for scientific or policy discussions related to health.[^cite_note-The-Henry-J-Kaiser-Family-Foundation-2019-2] Its official publication, the *[World Health Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Report)*, provides assessments of worldwide health topics.[^cite_note-9]

The WHO has played a leading role in several public health achievements, most notably the [eradication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eradication_of_infectious_diseases) of [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), the near-[eradication of polio](9901652.md), and the development of an [Ebola vaccine](44003094.md). Its current priorities include [communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communicable_disease), such as [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS), [Ebola](40817590.md), [malaria](20423.md) and [tuberculosis](30653.md); [non-communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-communicable_disease) such as heart disease and [cancer](105219.md); [healthy diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthy_eating), nutrition, and [food security](216361.md); [occupational health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_health); and [substance abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse). The agency advocates for [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) coverage, engagement with the monitoring of [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) risks, coordinating responses to health emergencies, and promoting health and [well-being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-being) generally.[^cite_note-10]

The WHO is governed by the [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly) (WHA), which is composed of its 193 member states. The WHA elects and advises an executive board made up of 34 health specialists; selects the WHO's chief administrator, the director-general (currently [Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus](39712205.md) of [Ethiopia](187749.md));[^cite_note-11] sets goals and priorities; and approves the budget and activities. The WHO is funded primarily by contributions from member states (both assessed and voluntary), followed by private donors.

## History

### Origin and founding

[Arcot Ramaswamy Mudaliar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcot_Ramaswamy_Mudaliar), the first president of the UN Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) in 1946, played a central role in founding the World Health Organization (WHO). Under his presidency, ECOSOC initiated the international conference that established the WHO as a specialized UN agency.

The [International Sanitary Conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Sanitary_Conferences) (ISC), the first of which was held on 23 June 1851, were a series of conferences that took place until 1938, about 87 years.[^cite_note-SanCon-12] The first conference, in Paris, was almost solely concerned with [cholera](7591.md), which would remain the disease of major concern for the ISC for most of the 19th century. With the [cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_\(medicine\)), origin, and communicability of many epidemic diseases still uncertain and a matter of scientific argument, international agreement on appropriate measures was difficult to reach.[^cite_note-SanCon-12] Seven of these international conferences, spanning 41 years, were convened before any resulted in a multi-state international agreement. The seventh conference, in Venice in 1892, finally resulted in a convention. It was concerned only with the sanitary control of shipping traversing the [Suez Canal](29323.md), and was an effort to guard against importation of cholera.[^cite_note-SanCon7-13]<sup>: 65 </sup>

Five years later, in 1897, a convention concerning the [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) was signed by sixteen of the nineteen states attending the Venice conference. While [Denmark](76972.md), [Sweden–Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden%E2%80%93Norway), and the US did not sign this convention, it was unanimously agreed that the work of the prior conferences should be codified for implementation.[^cite_note-SanCon10-14] Subsequent conferences, from 1902 until the final one in 1938, widened the diseases of concern for the ISC, and included discussions of responses to [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever), [brucellosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brucellosis), [leprosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leprosy), [tuberculosis](30653.md), and [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid).[^cite_note-SanCon13-15] In part as a result of the successes of the Conferences, the [Pan-American Sanitary Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Sanitary_Bureau) (1902) and the [Office International d'Hygiène Publique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_International_d'Hygi%C3%A8ne_Publique) or "[International office of Public Hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Office_of_Public_Hygiene)" in English (1907) were soon founded. When the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) was formed in 1920, it established the Health Organization of the League of Nations. After [World War II](32927.md), the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) assembled all the other health organizations to form the WHO.[^cite_note-16]

The WHO played a crucial role in coordinating the global response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), providing essential guidelines on preventive measures, supporting research on vaccines, and facilitating vaccine distribution through initiatives like [COVAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVAX).[^cite_note-17]

### Establishment

During the 1945 United Nations Conference on International Organization, [Szeming Sze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeming_Sze), a delegate from the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), conferred with Norwegian and Brazilian delegates on creating an international health organization under the auspices of the new United Nations. After failing to get a resolution passed on the subject, [Alger Hiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alger_Hiss), the secretary general of the conference, recommended using a declaration to establish such an organization. Sze and other delegates lobbied and a declaration passed calling for an international conference on health.[^cite_note-Pitt_ULS-18] The use of the word "world", rather than "international", emphasized the truly global nature of what the organization was seeking to achieve.[^cite_note-bmj1948-19] The constitution of the World Health Organization was signed by all 51 countries of the United Nations, and by 10 other countries, on 22 July 1946.[^cite_note-chronicle_1947-20] It thus became the first specialized agency of the United Nations to which every member subscribed.[^cite_note-shimkin-21] Its constitution formally came into force on the first [World Health Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Day) on 7 April 1948, when it was ratified by the 26th member state.[^cite_note-chronicle_1947-20] The WHO formally began its work on 1 September 1948.[^cite_note-Ascher-1952-6]

When the first meeting of the [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly) ended on 24 July 1948, a budget of US\$5 million (then £1,250,000) had been secured for the 1949 year. [G. Brock Chisholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Brock_Chisholm) was appointed director-general of the WHO, having served as executive secretary and a founding member during the planning stages,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-bmj1948-19] while [Andrija Štampar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrija_%C5%A0tampar) was the assembly's first president. Its first priorities were to control the spread of [malaria](20423.md), [tuberculosis](30653.md) and [sexually transmitted infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexually_transmitted_infection), improve [maternal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_health) and [child health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_health), nutrition and environmental hygiene.[^cite_note-23] Its first legislative act was concerning the compilation of accurate statistics on the spread and morbidity of disease.[^cite_note-bmj1948-19] The logo of the World Health Organization features the [Rod of Asclepius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_of_Asclepius) as a symbol for healing.[^cite_note-24]

In 1959, the WHO signed Agreement WHA 12–40 with the [International Atomic Energy Agency](14984.md) (IAEA), which says:[^cite_note-Independence-for-WHO-25]

> whenever either organization proposes to initiate a programme or activity on a subject in which the other organization has or may have a substantial interest, the first party shall consult the other with a view to adjusting the matter by mutual agreement.

The nature of this statement has led some groups and activists including [Women in Europe for a Common Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Europe_for_a_Common_Future) to claim that the WHO is restricted in its ability to investigate the [effects on human health of radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_radiation_syndrome) caused by the use of [nuclear power](22153.md) and the continuing effects of [nuclear disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_nuclear_disasters_and_radioactive_incidents) in [Chernobyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) and [Fukushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_nuclear_disaster). They believe WHO must regain what they see as independence.[^cite_note-Independence-for-WHO-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Independent WHO held a weekly vigil from 2007 to 2017 in front of WHO headquarters.[^cite_note-28] However, as pointed out by Foreman[^cite_note-29] in clause 2, it states:

> In particular, and in accordance with the Constitution of the World Health Organization and the Statute of the International Atomic Energy Agency and its agreement with the United Nations together with the exchange of letters related thereto, and taking into account the respective co-ordinating responsibilities of both organizations, it is recognized by the World Health Organization that the International Atomic Energy Agency has the primary responsibility for encouraging, assisting and co-ordinating research and development and practical application of atomic energy for peaceful uses throughout the world without prejudice to the right of the World Health Organization to concern itself with promoting, developing, assisting and co-ordinating international health work, including research, in all its aspects.

The key text is highlighted in bold, the agreement in clause 2 states that the WHO is free to perform any health-related work.

In 1947, the WHO established an [epidemiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology) information service via [telex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telex).[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 5 </sup> Two years later, in 1949, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and its constituent republics quit the organization over its unwillingness to share the [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin) recipe; they did not return until 1956.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In 1950, a mass [tuberculosis](30653.md) inoculation campaign using the [BCG vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCG_vaccine) began.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 8 </sup>

The malaria eradication programme was launched in 1955, though its objectives were later revised so that in most regions the goal shifted from eradication to control.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 9 </sup> In 1958, [Viktor Zhdanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Zhdanov), Deputy Minister of Health of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), urged the [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly) to undertake a global initiative to eradicate smallpox, leading to the adoption of Resolution WHA11.54.[^cite_note-fenner3-33][^cite_note-Fenner2-34]<sup>: 366–371, 393, 399, 419 </sup>

The WHO's first report on [diabetes mellitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus) was issued in 1965, the same year that the [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer) was established.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 10–11 </sup> In 1966, the organization moved its headquarters from the Ariana wing at the [Palace of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Nations) to a new purpose-built facility in Geneva.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-WHO-2-30]

By 1967, the WHO intensified the global [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) eradication campaign, contributing \$2.4 million annually and adopting new [disease surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_surveillance) methods at a time when 2 million people were dying of smallpox each year.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] The main challenge was underreporting of cases, which WHO addressed by creating a network of consultants to help countries implement surveillance and containment. The organization also helped manage the last European outbreak, which occurred in Yugoslavia in 1972.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] After more than two decades of effort, a Global Commission declared in 1979 that smallpox had been eradicated—the first disease in history to be eliminated by human effort.[^cite_note-40]

In 1974, the WHO launched the [Expanded Programme on Immunization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_Programme_on_Immunization)[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 13 </sup> and also began its control programme for [onchocerciasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onchocerciasis), in partnership with the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) (FAO), the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) (UNDP), and the [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 14 </sup> The following year, it introduced the [Special Programme for Research and Training in Tropical Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_disease#Health_programmes) (TDR), co-sponsored by UNICEF, UNDP, and the World Bank, in response to a 1974 WHA resolution. The TDR aimed to coordinate international research into the diagnosis, treatment, and control of tropical diseases, while also building research capacity in endemic countries.[^cite_note-41]

&#x20;In 1976, the WHA passed a resolution on [disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability) prevention and [rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_medicine_and_rehabilitation), emphasizing community-driven care.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 16 </sup> This was followed in 1977 by the first list of [essential medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_medicines),[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 17 </sup> and in 1978 by the declaration of the ambitious goal of "[Health For All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_For_All)".[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 18 </sup>



Three former directors of the [Global Smallpox Eradication Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox#Eradication) read the news that smallpox had been globally eradicated, 1980.

1986: The WHO began its global programme on [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS).[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 20 </sup> Two years later preventing discrimination against patients was attended to[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 21 </sup> and in 1996 the [Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_United_Nations_Programme_on_HIV/AIDS) (UNAIDS) was formed.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 23 </sup>

1988: The [Global Polio Eradication Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Polio_Eradication_Initiative) was established.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 22 </sup>

1995: The WHO established an independent International Commission for the Certification of [Dracunculiasis Eradication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eradication_of_dracunculiasis) (Guinea worm disease eradication; ICCDE).[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 23 </sup> The ICCDE recommends to the WHO which countries fulfil requirements for certification. It also has role in advising on progress made towards elthe imination of transmission and processes for verification.[^cite_note-42]

1998: The WHO's director-general highlighted gains in child survival, reduced [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality), increased [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) and reduced rates of "scourges" such as smallpox and [polio](25107.md) on the fiftieth anniversary of WHO's founding. He, did, however, accept that more had to be done to assist maternal health and that progress in this area had been slow.[^cite_note-43]

2000: The [Stop TB Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_TB_Partnership) was created along with the UN's formulation of the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals).[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 24 </sup>

2001: The [measles](58911.md) initiative was formed, and credited with reducing global deaths from the disease by 68% by 2007.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 26 </sup>

2002: [The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Global_Fund_to_Fight_AIDS,_Tuberculosis_and_Malaria) was drawn up to improve the resources available.[^cite_note-WHO-2-30]<sup>: 27 </sup>

2005: The WHO revises [International Health Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Health_Regulations) (IHR) in light of emerging health threats and the experience of the [2002/3 SARS epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%932004_SARS_outbreak), authorizing WHO, among other things, to declare a health threat a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Health_Emergency_of_International_Concern).[^cite_note-44]

2006: The WHO endorsed the world's first official HIV/AIDS Toolkit for Zimbabwe, which formed the basis for global prevention, treatment, and support the plan to fight the [AIDS pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_pandemic).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

2006: The WHO launches the [Global action plan for influenza vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_action_plan_for_influenza_vaccines).

2016: The [Global action plan for influenza vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_action_plan_for_influenza_vaccines) ends with a report which concludes that *while substantial progress has been made over the 10 years of the Plan, the world is still not ready to respond to an [influenza pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influenza_pandemic).*

2016: Following the perceived failure of the response to the [West Africa Ebola outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_African_Ebola_virus_epidemic), the World Health Emergencies programme was formed, changing the WHO from just being a "normative" agency to one that responds operationally to health emergencies.[^cite_note-47]

2020: the World Health Organization announced that it had classified the [novel coronavirus outbreak](62750956.md) as a public health emergency of international concern. The [novel coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novel_coronavirus) was a new strain of [coronavirus](201983.md) that had never been detected in humans before. The WHO named this new coronavirus "[COVID-19](63030231.md)" or "[2019-nCov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2)".

2022: The WHO suggests formation of a Global Health Emergency Council, with a new global health emergency workforce, and recommends revision of the International Health Regulations.[^cite_note-48]

2024: WHO has declared the spread of [mpox](242702.md) (formerly monkeypox) in several African countries a [public health emergency](42694003.md) of international concern, marking the second such declaration in the last two years due to the virus's transmission.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] In September 2025, the director-general [Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus](39712205.md) announced that mpox is no longer an emergency.[^cite_note-52]

2026: The [United States](3434750.md) formally withdrew from the WHO, citing alleged failures in the agency's handling of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), after notice had been given by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) on his first day in office on 20 January 2025. The administration said it would not rejoin or participate as an observer and instead pursued bilateral cooperation on global health issues, while disputing that the US is required to pay approximately \$260 million in outstanding fees prior to withdrawal. The withdrawal triggered a financial crisis at the Organization, leading to major budget cuts, a halving of its management team, and plans to reduce staff by about one quarter. As the WHO's largest funder, contributing about 18% of its budget, the US exit created uncertainty over future cooperation, with Director-General Tedros stating that the withdrawal would make both the US and the rest of the world less safe.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-Rigby-54]

## Policies and objectives

### Overall focus

The WHO's Constitution states that its objective "is the attainment by all people of the highest possible level of health".[^cite_note-55] It also seeks to provide a CRVS ([civil registration and vital statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_registration_and_vital_statistics)) system to provide monitoring of vital events (birth, death, wedding, divorce).[^cite_note-56]

Since the late 20th century, the rise of new actors engaged in global health—such as the [World Bank](36752.md), the [Gates Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_Foundation), the U.S. President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief ([PEPFAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Emergency_Plan_for_AIDS_Relief)) and dozens of public-private partnerships for global health—have weakened the WHO's role as a coordinator and policy leader in the field; subsequently, there are various proposals to reform or reorient the WHO's role and priorities in public health, ranging from narrowing its mandate to strengthening its independence and authority.[^cite_note-57]

In line with a growing global trend, as documented by the [OECD](33853117.md)[^cite_note-58] and established at the EU,[^cite_note-59] the WHO has embraced increased public participation in health policymaking.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] This is in alignment with the [UN Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) (SDGs)[^cite_note-63] and other intergovernmental agreements, and means "empowering people, communities and civil society through inclusive participation in decision-making processes that affect health across the policy cycle and at all levels of the system."[^cite_note-64]

#### Communicable diseases

During the 1970s, WHO had dropped its commitment to a global malaria eradication campaign, as it was considered too ambitious; it retained a strong commitment to malaria control. WHO's Global Malaria Programme works to monitor [malaria](20423.md) cases and potential problems in malaria control schemes. As of 2012, the WHO was to report as to whether [RTS,S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTS,S)/AS01, were a viable [malaria vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria_vaccine). For the time being, [insecticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticide)-treated [mosquito nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_net) and insecticide sprays are used to prevent the spread of malaria, as are [antimalarial drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimalarial_drugs) – particularly to vulnerable people such as pregnant women and young children.[^cite_note-65]

In 1988, WHO launched the Global Polio Eradication Initiative to eradicate [polio](25107.md).[^cite_note-66] It has also been successful in helping to reduce cases by 99% since WHO partnered with [Rotary International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotary_International), the [US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) (CDC), the [United Nations Children's Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Children's_Fund) (UNICEF), and smaller organizations. As of 2011, it has been working to immunize young children and prevent the re-emergence of cases in countries declared "polio-free".[^cite_note-67] In 2017, a study was conducted as to why Polio Vaccines may not be enough to eradicate the Virus & conduct new technology. Polio is now on the verge of extinction, thanks to a Global Vaccination Drive. The World Health Organization (WHO) stated the eradication programme has saved millions from deadly disease.[^cite_note-68]

Between 1990 and 2010, WHO's support contributed to a 40% decline in tuberculosis deaths. Since 2005, over 46 million people have been treated, and an estimated 7 million lives have been saved through practices advocated by WHO. These include engaging national governments and their financing, early diagnosis, standardizing treatment, monitoring of the spread and effect of tuberculosis, and stabilizing the drug supply. It has also recognized the vulnerability of victims of HIV/AIDS to tuberculosis.[^cite_note-69]

In 2003, the WHO denounced the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia)'s health department's opposition to the use of [condoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condom), saying: "These incorrect statements about condoms and HIV are dangerous when we are facing a global pandemic which has already killed more than 20 million people, and currently affects at least 42 million".[^cite_note-70] As of 2009, the [Catholic Church remains opposed to increasing the use of contraception to combat HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church_and_AIDS).[^cite_note-71] At the time, the World Health Assembly president, [Guyana](17238691.md)'s Health Minister [Leslie Ramsammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Ramsammy), condemned [Pope Benedict's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) opposition to contraception, saying he was trying to "create confusion" and "impede" proven strategies in the battle against the disease.[^cite_note-72]

In 2007, the WHO organized work on pandemic [influenza vaccine](1045705.md) development through [clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial) in collaboration with many experts and health officials.[^cite_note-73] [A pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_swine_flu_pandemic) involving the [H1N1 influenza virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic_H1N1/09_virus) was declared by the then director-general [Margaret Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Chan) in April 2009.[^cite_note-74] Margret Chan declared in 2010 that the H1N1 has moved into the post-pandemic period.[^cite_note-75] By the post-pandemic period, critics claimed the WHO had exaggerated the danger, spreading "fear and confusion" rather than "immediate information".[^cite_note-76] Industry experts countered that the 2009 pandemic had led to "unprecedented collaboration between global health authorities, scientists and manufacturers, resulting in the most comprehensive pandemic response ever undertaken, with a number of vaccines approved for use three months after the pandemic declaration. This response was only possible because of the extensive preparations undertaken during the last decade".[^cite_note-77]

The 2012–2013 WHO budget identified five areas, among which funding was distributed.[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 5, 20 </sup> Two of those five areas related to [communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communicable_diseases): the first, to reduce the "health, social and economic burden" of communicable diseases in general; the second to combat [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS), [malaria](20423.md) and [tuberculosis](30653.md) in particular.[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 5, 26 </sup>

As of 2015, the World Health Organization has worked within the [UNAIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAIDS) network and strives to involve sections of society other than health to help deal with the economic and social effects of [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS).[^cite_note-79] In line with UNAIDS, WHO has set itself the interim task between 2009 and 2015 of reducing the number of those aged 15–24 years who are infected by 50%; reducing new HIV infections in children by 90%; and reducing HIV-related deaths by 25%.[^cite_note-80]

The World Health Organization's definition of eglected tropical disease has been criticized as restrictive (focusing only on communicable diseases) and described as a form of epistemic injustice, where conditions like snakebite are forced to be framed as a medical problem.[^cite_note-81]

#### Non-communicable diseases

One of the thirteen WHO priority areas is aimed at the prevention and reduction of "disease, disability and premature deaths from chronic [noncommunicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommunicable_disease), [mental disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_disorder), violence and [injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injury_prevention), and [visual impairment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_impairment) which are collectively responsible for almost 71% of all deaths worldwide".[^cite_note-WHO-78][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] The Division of Noncommunicable Diseases for Promoting Health through the Reproductive Health has published the magazine, *Entre Nous*, across Europe since 1983.[^cite_note-84]

WHO is mandated under two of the [international drug control conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_drug_control_conventions) ([Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs, 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Convention_on_Narcotic_Drugs) and [Convention on Psychotropic Substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Psychotropic_Substances), 1971) to carry out scientific assessments of substances for international [drug control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_policy). Through the [WHO Expert Committee on Drug Dependence (ECDD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Expert_Committee_on_Drug_Dependence_\(ECDD\)), it can recommend changes to scheduling of substances to the [United Nations Commission on Narcotic Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_on_Narcotic_Drugs).[^cite_note-85] The ECDD is in charge of evaluating "the impact of psychoactive substances on public health" and "their dependence producing properties and potential harm to health, as well as considering their potential medical benefits and therapeutic applications".[^cite_note-86]

#### Environmental health

The WHO estimates that 12.6 million people died as a result of living or working in an unhealthy environment in 2012 – this accounts for nearly 1 in 4 of total global deaths. Environmental risk factors, such as air, water, and [soil pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_pollution), chemical exposures, climate change, and ultraviolet radiation, contribute to more than 100 diseases and injuries. This can result in a number of [pollution-related diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pollution-related_diseases).

#### Life course and lifestyle

WHO works to "reduce [morbidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbidity) and [mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death) and improve health during key stages of life, including [pregnancy](1771587.md), [childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth), the [neonatal period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant), [childhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood) and [adolescence](83859.md), and improve [sexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_health) and [reproductive health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_health) and promote active and healthy aging for all individuals", for instance with the [Special Programme on Human Reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Programme_on_Human_Reproduction).[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 39–45 </sup>[^cite_note-88]

It also tries to prevent or reduce risk factors for "health conditions associated with use of [tobacco](30942.md), [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage), [drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugs) and other [psychoactive substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoactive_substance), [unhealthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unhealthy) diets and physical inactivity and [unsafe sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsafe_sex)".[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 50–55 </sup>[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

The WHO works to improve nutrition, [food safety](8053588.md) and [food security](216361.md) and to ensure this has a positive effect on [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) and [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development).[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 66–71 </sup>

In April 2019, the WHO released new recommendations stating that children between the ages of two and five should spend no more than one hour per day engaging in sedentary behaviour in front of a screen and that children under two should not be permitted any sedentary screen time.[^cite_note-91]

In January 2025, the WHO released a new guideline *Use of lower-sodium salt substitutes* *,*&#x77;hich strongly recommends reducing sodium intake to less than 2 g/day and conditionally recommends replacing regular table salt with lower-sodium salt substitutes that contain potassium. This recommendation is intended for adults (not children or pregnant women) in general populations, excluding individuals with kidney impairments or with other circumstances or conditions that might compromise potassium excretion.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

#### Surgery and trauma care

The World Health Organization promotes road safety as a means to reduce traffic-related injuries.[^cite_note-95] It has also worked on global initiatives in surgery, including emergency and essential surgical care,[^cite_note-96] trauma care,[^cite_note-97] and safe surgery.[^cite_note-safesurgery-98] The [WHO Surgical Safety Checklist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Surgical_Safety_Checklist) is in current use worldwide in the effort to improve patient safety.[^cite_note-safesurgery-98]

#### Emergency work

The World Health Organization's primary objective in natural and man-made emergencies is to coordinate with member states and other stakeholders to "reduce avoidable loss of life and the burden of disease and disability".[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 46–49 </sup>

On 5 May 2014, WHO announced that the spread of [polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliomyelitis) was a world health emergency – outbreaks of the disease in Asia, Africa, and the Middle East were considered "extraordinary".[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

On 8 August 2014, WHO declared that the spread of [Ebola](40817590.md) was a public health emergency; an outbreak which was believed to have started in Guinea had spread to other nearby countries such as Liberia and Sierra Leone. The situation in West Africa was considered very serious.[^cite_note-101]

##### Reform efforts following the Ebola outbreak

Following the [2014 Ebola outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Ebola_outbreak) in West Africa, the organization was heavily criticized for its bureaucracy, insufficient financing, regional structure, and staffing profile.[^cite_note-102]

An internal WHO report on the Ebola response pointed to underfunding and the lack of "core capacity" in health systems in developing countries as the primary weaknesses of the existing system. At the annual World Health Assembly in 2015, Director-General Margaret Chan announced a \$100 million Contingency Fund for rapid response to future emergencies,[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] of which it had received \$26.9 million by April 2016 (for 2017 disbursement). WHO has budgeted an additional \$494 million for its [Health Emergencies Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Emergencies_Programme_\(WHO\)) in 2016–17, for which it had received \$140 million by April 2016.[^cite_note-105]

The program was aimed at rebuilding WHO capacity for direct action, which critics said had been lost due to budget cuts in the previous decade that had left the organization in an advisory role dependent on member states for on-the-ground activities. In comparison, billions of dollars have been spent by developed countries on the 2013–2016 Ebola epidemic and 2015–16 Zika epidemic.[^cite_note-106]

##### Response to the COVID-19 pandemic

Main article: [World Health Organization's response to the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization's_response_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic)



Staffs of the World Health Organization Bangladesh involved in the COVID vaccine program in Dhaka

The WHO created an Incident Management Support Team on 1 January 2020, one day after Chinese health authorities notified the organization of a cluster of pneumonia cases of unknown aetiology.[^cite_note-Withholding-funding-from-the-World-Health-Organization-is-wrong-and-dangerous-2020-107][^cite_note-whopneumonia-108][^cite_note-Guardian-superpower-struggle-109] On 5 January the WHO notified all member states of the outbreak,[^cite_note-Amanda-Watts-and-Veronica-Stracqualursi-110] and in subsequent days provided guidance to all countries on how to respond,[^cite_note-Amanda-Watts-and-Veronica-Stracqualursi-110] and confirmed the first infection outside China.[^cite_note-SCMP-WHO-111] On 14 January 2020, the WHO announced that preliminary investigations conducted by Chinese authorities had found no clear evidence of human-to-human transmission of the novel coronavirus (2019-nCoV) identified in Wuhan.[^cite_note-112] The same day, the organization warned of limited human-to-human transmission, and confirmed human-to-human transmission one week later.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-reuterswarns-114][^cite_note-KesslerApr17-115] On 30 January the WHO declared a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Health_Emergency_of_International_Concern) (PHEIC),[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] considered a "call to action" and "last resort" measure for the international community and a [pandemic](24255.md) on 11 March.[^cite_note-Rull2015-119]

While organizing the global response to the COVID-19 pandemic and overseeing "more than 35 emergency operations" for cholera, measles and other epidemics internationally,[^cite_note-Withholding-funding-from-the-World-Health-Organization-is-wrong-and-dangerous-2020-107] the WHO has been criticized for praising China's public health response to the crisis while seeking to maintain a "diplomatic balancing act" between the United States and China.[^cite_note-Guardian-superpower-struggle-109][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] [David L. Heymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_L._Heymann), professor of infectious disease epidemiology at the [London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine), said that "China has been very transparent and open in sharing its data... and they opened up all of their files with the WHO present".[^cite_note-123]

The WHO faced criticism from the United States' [Trump administration](52231341.md) while "guid\[ing] the world in how to tackle the deadly" [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-Withholding-funding-from-the-World-Health-Organization-is-wrong-and-dangerous-2020-107] On 14 April 2020, United States president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) said that he would halt United States funding to the WHO while reviewing its role in "severely mismanaging and covering up the spread of the coronavirus."[^cite_note-\:0-124] World leaders and health experts largely condemned President Trump's announcement, which came amid criticism of his response to the outbreak in the United States.[^cite_note-\:1-125] WHO called the announcement "regrettable" and defended its actions in alerting the world to the emergence of COVID-19.[^cite_note-\:2-126] On 8 May 2020, the United States blocked a vote on a U.N. Security Council resolution aimed at promoting nonviolent international cooperation during the pandemic, and mentioning the WHO.[^cite_note-127] On 7 July 2020, President Trump formally notified the UN of his intent to withdraw the United States from the WHO.[^cite_note-\:3-128] However, Trump's successor, President [Joe Biden](145422.md), cancelled the planned withdrawal and announced in January 2021 that the U.S. would resume funding the organization.[^cite_note-\:4-129][^cite_note-\:5-130][^cite_note-AP_NEWS-2021-131]

In May 2023, the WHO announced that COVID-19 was no longer a world-wide health emergency.[^cite_note-132]

In January 2025, during his [second term](78298108.md), President Trump issued an executive order to withdraw the United States from the WHO, citing their alleged mishandling of the COVID-19 pandemic among other reasons.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-exec_order-134] The United States officially left the World Health Organization on 22 January 2026, marking the first time in the organization's history that a member-state had withdrawn from the WHO.[^cite_note-\:6-135][^cite_note-136]

#### Health policy

WHO addresses government [health policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_policy) with two aims: firstly, "to address the underlying social and economic determinants of health through policies and programmes that enhance health equity and integrate pro-poor, gender-responsive, and human rights-based approaches" and secondly "to promote a healthier environment, intensify primary prevention and influence public policies in all sectors so as to address the root causes of environmental threats to health".[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 61–65 </sup>

The organization develops and promotes the use of evidence-based tools, norms and standards to support member states to inform [health policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_policy) options. It oversees the implementation of the [International Health Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Health_Regulations), and publishes a series of [medical classifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_classification); of these, three are over-reaching "reference classifications": the [International Statistical Classification of Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Classification_of_Diseases) (ICD), the [International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Classification_of_Functioning,_Disability_and_Health) (ICF) and the [International Classification of Health Interventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Classification_of_Health_Interventions) (ICHI).[^cite_note-137] Other international policy frameworks produced by WHO include the [International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Code_of_Marketing_of_Breast-milk_Substitutes) (adopted in 1981),[^cite_note-138] [Framework Convention on Tobacco Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_Convention_on_Tobacco_Control) (adopted in 2003),[^cite_note-139] the [Global Code of Practice on the International Recruitment of Health Personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Human_Resources#Global_Code_of_Practice_on_the_International_Recruitment_of_Health_Personnel) (adopted in 2010)[^cite_note-140] as well as the [WHO Model List of Essential Medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines) and its [pediatric counterpart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines_for_Children). An international convention on pandemic prevention and preparedness is currently under active consideration.[^cite_note-141]

In terms of health services, WHO aims to improve "governance, financing, staffing and management," as well as the availability and quality of evidence and research to guide policy. It also strives to "ensure improved access, quality and use of medical products and technologies".[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 72–83 </sup> WHO – working with donor agencies and national governments – can improve their reporting about use of research evidence.[^cite_note-142]

#### Digital Health

On Digital Health topics, WHO has existing Inter-Agency collaboration with the [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union) (the UN specialized agency for [ICT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology)), including the Be Health, Be Mobile initiative and the ITU-WHO Focus Group on Artificial Intelligence for Health.

### Governance and support

The remaining two of WHO's thirteen identified policy areas relate to the role of WHO itself:[^cite_note-WHO-78]<sup>: 84–91 </sup>

- "to provide leadership, strengthen governance and foster partnership and collaboration with countries, the United Nations system, and other stakeholders in order to fulfil the mandate of WHO in advancing the global health agenda"; and

- "to develop and sustain WHO as a flexible, learning organization, enabling it to carry out its mandate more efficiently and effectively".

#### Partnerships

The WHO, along with the [World Bank](36752.md), constitute the core team responsible for administering the [International Health Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Health_Partnership) (IHP+). The IHP+ is a group of partner governments, development agencies, civil society, and others committed to improving the health of citizens in [developing countries](78449.md). Partners work together to put international principles for [aid effectiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_effectiveness) and development co-operation into practice in the health sector.[^cite_note-144]

The organization relies on contributions from renowned scientists and professionals to inform its work, such as the [WHO Expert Committee on Biological Standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Expert_Committee_on_Biological_Standardization),[^cite_note-145] the [WHO Expert Committee on Leprosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Expert_Committee_on_Leprosy),[^cite_note-146] and the [WHO Study Group on Interprofessional Education & Collaborative Practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interprofessional_education#WHO_Study_Group_on_Interprofessional_Education_&_Collaborative_Practice).[^cite_note-147]

WHO runs the [Alliance for Health Policy and Systems Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Health_Policy_and_Systems_Research), targeted at improving [health policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_policy) and [systems](60976.md).[^cite_note-148]

WHO also aims to improve access to health research and literature in developing countries, such as through the [HINARI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HINARI) network.[^cite_note-149]

WHO collaborates with [The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Global_Fund_to_Fight_AIDS,_Tuberculosis_and_Malaria), UNITAID, and the [United States](3434750.md) President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief[^cite_note-appswhoint-150] to spearhead and fund the development of HIV programs.

WHO created the Civil Society Reference Group on HIV,[^cite_note-appswhoint-150] which brings together other networks that are involved in policymaking and the dissemination of guidelines.

WHO, a United Nations sector, partners with UNAIDS[^cite_note-appswhoint-150] to support the development of HIV responses across different regions of the world.

WHO facilitates technical partnerships through the Technical Advisory Committee on HIV,[^cite_note-151] which they created to develop WHO guidelines and policies.

In 2014, WHO released the *Global Atlas of Palliative Care at the End of Life* in a joint publication with the [Worldwide Hospice Palliative Care Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldwide_Hospice_Palliative_Care_Alliance), an affiliated NGO working collaboratively with the WHO to promote [palliative care](275509.md) in national and international [health policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_policy).[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

#### Public health education and action

The practice of empowering individuals to exert greater control over and improve their health is known as health education, according to the WHO. It shifts away from an emphasis on personal behaviour and toward a variety of societal and environmental solutions.[^cite_note-154]

Each year, the organization marks [World Health Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Day) and other observances focusing on a specific [health promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_promotion) topic. World Health Day falls on 7 April each year, timed to match the anniversary of WHO's founding. Recent themes have been [vector-borne diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_\(epidemiology\)) (2014), healthy ageing (2012) and [drug resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_resistance) (2011).[^cite_note-155]

The other official global public health campaigns marked by WHO are [World Tuberculosis Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tuberculosis_Day), [World Immunization Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Immunization_Week), [World Malaria Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Malaria_Day), [World No Tobacco Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_No_Tobacco_Day), [World Blood Donor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Blood_Donor_Day), [World Hepatitis Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hepatitis_Day), and [World AIDS Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_AIDS_Day).

As part of the United Nations, the World Health Organization supports work towards the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals).[^cite_note-156] Of the eight Millennium Development Goals, three – reducing child mortality by two-thirds, to reduce maternal deaths by three-quarters, andhaltingt and beginingn to reduce the spread of HIV/AIDS – relate directly to the WHO's scope; the other five inte-relate and affect world health.[^cite_note-157]

### Data handling and publications

The World Health Organization works to provide the needed health and well-being evidence through a variety of data collection platforms, including the World Health Survey covering almost 400,000 respondents from 70 countries,[^cite_note-158] and the *[Study on Global Aging and Adult Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Study_on_Global_Ageing_and_Adult_Health)* (SAGE) covering over 50,000 persons over 50 years old in 23 countries.[^cite_note-159] The Country Health Intelligence Portal (CHIP), has also been developed to provide an access point to information about the health services that are available in different countries.[^cite_note-160] The information gathered in this portal is used by the countries to set priorities for future strategies or plans, implement, monitor, and evaluate it.

The WHO has published various tools for measuring and monitoring the capacity of national [health systems](60976.md)[^cite_note-161] and [health workforces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Human_Resources).[^cite_note-162] The Global Health Observatory (GHO) has been the WHO's main portal which provides access to data and analyses for key health themes by monitoring health situations around the globe.[^cite_note-163]

The *WHO Assessment Instrument for Mental Health Systems* (WHO-AIMS), the *WHO Quality of Life Instrument* (WHOQOL), and the *Service Availability and Readiness Assessment* (SARA) provide guidance for data collection.[^cite_note-164] Collaborative efforts between WHO and other agencies, such as through the [Health Metrics Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Metrics_Network), also aim to provide sufficient high-quality information to assist governmental decision making.[^cite_note-165] WHO promotes the development of capacities in member states to use and produce research that addresses their national needs, including through the [Evidence-Informed Policy Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-Informed_Policy_Network) (EVIPNet).[^cite_note-166] The [Pan American Health Organization](1246706.md) (PAHO/AMRO) became the first region to develop and pass a policy on research for health approved in September 2009.[^cite_note-167]

On 10 December 2013, a new WHO database, known as MiNDbank, went online. The database was launched on [Human Rights Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Day), and is part of WHO's QualityRights initiative, which aims to end human rights violations against people with mental health conditions. The new database presents a great deal of information about mental health, substance abuse, disability, human rights, and the different policies, strategies, laws, and service standards being implemented in different countries.[^cite_note-MiNDbank-168] It also contains important international documents and information. The database allows visitors to access the health information of WHO member states and other partners. Users can review policies, laws, and strategies and search for the best practices and success stories in the field of mental health.[^cite_note-MiNDbank-168]

The WHO regularly publishes a *[World Health Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Report)*, its leading publication, including an expert assessment of a specific [global health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_health) topic.[^cite_note-169] Other publications of WHO include the *[Bulletin of the World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_World_Health_Organization)*,[^cite_note-170] the *[Eastern Mediterranean Health Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean_Health_Journal)* (overseen by EMRO),[^cite_note-171] the *[Human Resources for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Resources_for_Health)* (published in collaboration with [BioMed Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioMed_Central)),[^cite_note-172] and the *[Pan American Journal of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Journal_of_Public_Health)* (overseen by PAHO/AMRO).[^cite_note-173]

In 2016, the World Health Organization drafted a global health sector strategy on HIV. In the draft, the World Health Organization outlines its commitment to ending the AIDS epidemic by 2030 with interim targets for the year 2020. To make achievements towards these targets, the draft lists actions that countries and the WHO can take, such as a commitment to universal health coverage, medical accessibility, prevention and eradication of disease, and efforts to educate the public. Some notable points made in the draft include tailoring resources to mobilized regions where the health system may be compromised due to natural disasters, etc. Among the points made, it seems clear that although the prevalence of HIV transmission is declining, there is still a need for resources, health education, and global efforts to end this epidemic.[^cite_note-174]

The WHO has a Framework Convention on Tobacco implementation database which is one of the few mechanisms to help enforce compliance with the FCTC.[^cite_note-175] However, there have been reports of numerous discrepancies between it and national implementation reports on which it was built. As researchers Hoffman and Rizvi report "As of July 4, 2012, 361 (32·7%) of 1104 countries' responses were misreported: 33 (3·0%) were clear errors (e.g., database indicated 'yes' when report indicated 'no'), 270 (24·5%) were missing despite countries having submitted responses, and 58 (5·3%) were, in our opinion, misinterpreted by WHO staff".[^cite_note-176]

WHO has been moving toward acceptance and integration of [traditional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_medicine) and [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) (TCM). In 2022, the new [International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Classification_of_Diseases_and_Related_Health_Problems), ICD-11, will attempt to enable classifications from traditional medicine to be integrated with classifications from [evidence-based medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_medicine). Though Chinese authorities have pushed for the change, this and other support of the WHO for traditional medicine has been criticized by the medical and scientific community, due to lack of evidence and the risk of endangering wildlife hunted for traditional remedies.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-Hunt-178][^cite_note-179] A WHO spokesman said that the inclusion was "not an endorsement of the scientific validity of any Traditional Medicine practice or the efficacy of any Traditional Medicine intervention".[^cite_note-Hunt-178]

#### International Agency for Research on Cancer

Further information: [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer)

The WHO sub-department, the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), conducts and coordinates research into the causes of [cancer](105219.md).[^cite_note-180] It also [collects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_registry) and publishes [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_surveillance) data regarding the occurrence of cancer worldwide.[^cite_note-181]

Its [Monographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) Programme identifies carcinogenic [hazards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard) and evaluates environmental [causes of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen) in humans.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

## Structure and governance

The World Health Organization is a member of the [United Nations Development Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Group).[^cite_note-184]

### Membership



Countries by World Health Organization membership status (2026): 192 WHO member states, 2 UN observer states (Palestine, Vatican), 3 invited delegations/associate members (Taiwan, Puerto Rico, Tokelau), 2 withdrawn (Argentina and the United States)

As of March 2026, the WHO has 192 member states: all member states of the United Nations except for [Argentina](18951905.md), [Liechtenstein](17810.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) (190 countries), plus the [Cook Islands](7067.md) and [Niue](21228.md) (non-UN members).[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-\:6-135] No new members have joined since [Tuvalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvalu) in 2023.[^cite_note-186] A state becomes a full member of the WHO by ratifying the treaty known as the Constitution of the World Health Organization. As of January 2026, it also had two associate members, [Puerto Rico](23041.md) and [Tokelau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokelau).[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-Members+AM-188] The WHO two-year budget for 2022–2023 is paid by its 194 members and 2 associate members.[^cite_note-Members+AM-188] Several other countries have been granted [observer status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status). [Palestine](241405.md) is an observer as a "national liberation movement" recognized by the [League of Arab States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Arab_States) under United Nations Resolution 3118. The [Sovereign Military Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Military_Order_of_Malta) (or *Order of Malta*) also attends on an observer basis. The [Holy See](13393.md) attends as an observer, and its participation as "non-Member State Observer" was formalized by an Assembly resolution in 2021.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-Burci-2004-190] The government of [Taiwan](25734.md) was allowed to participate under the designation "[Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei)" as an observer from 2009 to 2016, but has not been invited again since.[^cite_note-191] On 20 January 2025, U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) signed [Executive Order 14155](78942315.md) initiating the 12-month process of withdrawing the U.S. from the WHO, with its completion being certified on 22 January 2026.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-exec_order-134][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-\:6-135] On 5 February 2025, Argentinian president [Javier Milei](56599840.md) announced that Argentina would also be withdrawing from WHO, with the completion being certified on 17 March 2026.[^cite_note-194]

WHO member states appoint delegations to the [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly), the WHO's supreme decision-making body. All UN member states are eligible for WHO membership, and, according to the WHO website, "other countries may be admitted as members when their application has been approved by a simple majority vote of the World Health Assembly".[^cite_note-countries-195] The World Health Assembly is attended by delegations from all member states, and determines the policies of the organization.

The executive board is composed of members technically qualified in health and gives effect to the decisions and policies of the World Health Assembly. In addition, the UN observer organizations [International Committee of the Red Cross](5023832.md) and [International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies](9720597.md) have entered into "official relations" with WHO and are invited as observers. In the World Health Assembly, they are seated alongside the other NGOs.[^cite_note-Burci-2004-190]

#### Membership and participation of Taiwan

Further information: [China and the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_the_United_Nations)

See also: [Cross-Strait relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-Strait_relations) and [Political status of Taiwan](66555.md)

The [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) (ROC), whose [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) controlled [Mainland China](55079.md) from 1912 to 1949 and has controlled [Taiwan](25734.md) since the end of World War II, was a founding member of WHO, representing "China". With the adoption of [United Nations General Assembly Resolution 2758](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_2758) in 1971, representation of "China" was handed to the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC), and the Republic of China was expelled from the WHO and other UN bodies.[^cite_note-UN_ARES2758XXVI_page1-196] Per the [One-China policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-China_policy), both the ROC and PRC [lay claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Chinas) of sovereignty to each other's territory.[^cite_note-197]

In May 2009, the [Department of Health of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_\(Republic_of_China\)) was invited by the WHO to attend the 62nd [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly) as an observer under the name "[Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei)". This was the ROC's first participation at WHO meetings since 1971, as a result of the improved [cross-strait relations](2596399.md) since [Ma Ying-jeou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Ying-jeou) became the [president of the Republic of China](61155.md) a year before.[^cite_note-198] Its participation with WHO ended due to diplomatic pressure from the PRC following the [election in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Taiwanese_presidential_election) that brought the independence-minded [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party) back into power.[^cite_note-Kyodo_News_2020-199]

Political pressure from the PRC has led to the ROC being barred from membership of the WHO and other UN-affiliated organizations, and from 2017 to 2020, the WHO refused to allow Taiwanese delegates to attend the [WHO annual assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly).[^cite_note-200] According to Taiwanese publication *[The News Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_News_Lens)*, on multiple occasions Taiwanese journalists have been denied access to report on the assembly.[^cite_note-201]

In May 2018, the WHO denied Taiwanese media access to its annual assembly, reportedly at the PRC's request.[^cite_note-202] Later in May 172 members of the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md) wrote to the director-general of the World Health Organization to argue for Taiwan's inclusion as an observer at the WHA.[^cite_note-203]

[Taiwan's response to the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Taiwan), which was praised by a number of experts, led to increased pressure to allow the ROC to participate in the WHO.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] In early May 2020, [New Zealand Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(New_Zealand\)) [Winston Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Peters) expressed support for the ROC's bid to rejoin the WHO during a media conference.[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] Taiwan's exclusion from emergency meetings concerning the outbreak brought a rare united front from Taiwan's diverse political parties. Taiwan's main opposition party, the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (KMT, Chinese Nationalist Party), expressed their anger at being excluded, arguing that disease respects neither politics nor geography. China once again dismissed concerns over Taiwanese inclusion, with the foreign minister claiming that no one cares more about the health and well-being of the Taiwanese people than the central government of the PRC.[^cite_note-208] During the outbreak, Canadian Prime Minister [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau) voiced his support for Taiwan's participation in the WHO,[^cite_note-209] as did Japanese Prime Minister [Shinzo Abe](2634434.md).[^cite_note-Kyodo_News_2020-199] In January 2020 the [European Union](9317.md), a WHO observer, backed Taiwan's participation in WHO meetings related to the coronavirus pandemic as well as their general participation.[^cite_note-210] Government officials in the United States, Germany and Australia also expressed support for Taiwan's inclusion in the WHO.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

In a 2020 interview, Assistant Director-General [Bruce Aylward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Aylward) appeared to dodge a question from [RTHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTHK) reporter Yvonne Tong about Taiwan's response to the pandemic and inclusion in the WHO, blaming internet connection issues.[^cite_note-213] When the video chat was restarted, he was asked another question about Taiwan. He responded by indicating that they had already discussed China and formally ended the interview.[^cite_note-214] This incident led to accusations about the PRC's political influence over the international organization.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216]

On 9 May 2020, United States Congressmen [Eliot Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_Engel), the [Democratic](5043544.md) chairman of the [United States House Committee on Foreign Affairs](1267657.md), [Michael McCaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McCaul), the United States House Committee's ranking [Republican](32070.md) member, United States Senator [Jim Risch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Risch), the Republican chairman of the [United States Senate Committee on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations), and United States Senator [Bob Menendez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Menendez), the United States Senate Committee's ranking Democratic member, submitted a joint letter to nearly 60 "like-minded" countries including Canada, Thailand, Japan, Germany, the United Kingdom, Saudi Arabia, and Australia, urging them to support the ROC's participation in the WHO.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]

In November 2020, the word "Taiwan" was blocked in comments on a livestream on the WHO's [Facebook](7529378.md) page.[^cite_note-219]

#### Membership and participation of the United States

On 14 April 2020, United States president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) said that he would halt United States funding to the WHO while reviewing its role in "severely mismanaging and covering up the spread of the coronavirus".[^cite_note-\:0-124] World leaders and health experts largely condemned Trump's announcement, which came amid criticism of his response to the outbreak in the United States.[^cite_note-\:1-125] WHO called the announcement "regrettable" and defended its actions in alerting the world to the emergence of COVID-19.[^cite_note-\:2-126] On 7 July 2020, Trump formally notified the UN of his intent to withdraw the United States from the WHO.[^cite_note-\:3-128] However, Trump's successor, president [Joe Biden](145422.md), cancelled the planned withdrawal after his inauguration on 20 January 2021, announcing that the U.S. would resume funding the organization.[^cite_note-\:4-129][^cite_note-\:5-130][^cite_note-AP_NEWS-2021-131]

On 20 January 2025, [an executive order](78942315.md) was signed by Trump hours after his re-inauguration, formally notifying the United Nations of his intent to withdraw the United States from the WHO for a second time.[^cite_note-exec_order-134][^cite_note-220] The withdrawal took effect on 22 January 2026,[^cite_note-Rigby-54] a year after the United Nations received Trump's notification of the withdrawal.[^cite_note-221] On 23 January the U.S. state of [California](5407.md) announced it would join WHO's [Global Outbreak Alert and Response Network](52207602.md) becoming the first U.S. state to do so.[^cite_note-222]

### World Health Assembly and Executive Board



WHO headquarters in Geneva

The [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly) (WHA) is the legislative and supreme body of the WHO. Based in Geneva, it typically meets in May each year. It appoints the director-general every five years and votes on matters of policy and finance of WHO, including the proposed budget. It also reviews the executive board's reports and decides whether any areas of work require further examination.

The Assembly elects 34 members, technically qualified in the field of health, to the executive board for three-year terms. The main functions of the board are to implement the Assembly's decisions and policies, advise it, and facilitate its work.[^cite_note-governance-223] As of June 2023, the chair of the executive board is Dr. Hanan Mohamed Al Kuwari of Qatar.[^cite_note-224]

### Director-General

Main article: [Director-General of the World Health Organization](63815422.md)

The head of the organization is the director-general, elected by the [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly).[^cite_note-225] The term lasts for five years, and directors-general are typically appointed in May, when the Assembly meets. The current director-general is [Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus](39712205.md), who was appointed on 1 July 2017.[^cite_note-dgelec-2017-226]

### Personnel

The WHO employs 7,000 people in 149 countries and regions to carry out its principles.[^cite_note-229] In support of the principle of a tobacco-free work environment, the WHO does not recruit cigarette smokers.[^cite_note-230] The organization has previously instigated the [Framework Convention on Tobacco Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_Convention_on_Tobacco_Control) in 2003.[^cite_note-231]

### Goodwill Ambassadors

The WHO operates "[Goodwill Ambassadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization_Goodwill_Ambassador)"; members of the arts, sports, or other fields of public life aimed at drawing attention to the WHO's initiatives and projects. There are currently five Goodwill Ambassadors ([Jet Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Li), [Nancy Brinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Brinker), [Peng Liyuan](10462776.md), [Yohei Sasakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohei_Sasakawa) and the [Vienna Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Philharmonic_Orchestra)) and a further ambassador associated with a partnership project ([Craig David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_David)).[^cite_note-232]

On 21 October 2017, the director-general [Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus](39712205.md) appointed the then Zimbabwean president [Robert Mugabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mugabe) as a [WHO Goodwill Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization_Goodwill_Ambassador) to help promote the fight against non-communicable diseases. The appointment address praised Mugabe for his commitment to public health in Zimbabwe. The appointment attracted widespread condemnation and criticism in WHO member states and international organizations due to Mugabe's poor record on human rights and presiding over a decline in Zimbabwe's public health.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] Due to the outcry, the following day the appointment was revoked.[^cite_note-235]

#### Past

At the beginning of the 21st century, the WHO's work involved increasing collaboration with external bodies.[^cite_note-263] As of 2002, a total of 473 nongovernmental organizations (NGO) had some form of partnership with WHO. There were 189 partnerships with international NGOs in formal "official relations" – the rest were considered informal.[^cite_note-264] Partners include the [Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_and_Melinda_Gates_Foundation)[^cite_note-265] and the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation).[^cite_note-266]

As of 2022, the largest annual assessed contributions from member states came from the United States (\$109 million), China (\$57 million), Japan (\$41 million), Germany (\$29 million), France (\$22 million) and the United Kingdom (\$21 million).[^cite_note-267]

According to [The Associated Press](18935732.md), the WHO routinely spends about \$200 million a year on travel expenses, more than it spends to tackle mental health problems, [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS), [tuberculosis](30653.md), and [malaria](20423.md) combined. In 2016, [Margaret Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Chan), director-general of WHO from January 2007 to June 2017,[^cite_note-dgelec-2017-226] stayed in a \$1000-per-night hotel room while visiting West Africa.[^cite_note-268]

The biggest contributor used to be the United States, which gives over \$400 million annually.[^cite_note-The-New-York-Times-2020-269] U.S. contributions to the WHO are funded through the [U.S. State Department's](31975.md) account for Contributions to International Organizations (CIO). In April 2020, U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), with backing by members of his party,[^cite_note-270] announced that his administration would halt funding to the WHO.[^cite_note-271] Funds previously earmarked for the WHO were to be held for 60–90 days pending an investigation into the WHO's handling of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), particularly in respect to the organization's purported relationship with China.[^cite_note-272] The announcement was immediately criticized by world leaders including [António Guterres](471806.md), the secretary general of the United Nations; [Heiko Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiko_Maas), the German foreign minister; and [Moussa Faki Mahamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Faki), African Union chairman.[^cite_note-The-New-York-Times-2020-269] During the first two years of the pandemic, American funding of the WHO declined by a quarter, although it is expected to increase during 2022 and 2023.[^cite_note-273]

On 16 May 2020, the Trump administration agreed to pay up to the level of China's assessed contributions, which is less than about one-tenth of its previous funding. Biennium 2018–2019: China paid assessed contributions of US\$75,796K and specified voluntary contributions of US\$10,184K, for a total of US\$85,980K.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275]

### WHO Public Health Prizes and Awards

Main article: [WHO Public Health Prizes and Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Public_Health_Prizes_and_Awards)

World Health Organization Prizes and Awards are given to recognize major achievements in public health. The candidates are nominated and recommended by each prize and award selection panel. The WHO Executive Board selects the winners, which are presented during the World Health Assembly.[^cite_note-276]

### Country and liaison offices

The World Health Organization operates 150 country offices in six different regions.[^cite_note-peopleandoffices-279] It also operates several liaison offices, including those with the [European Union](9317.md), United Nations and a single office covering the [World Bank](36752.md) and [International Monetary Fund](15251.md). It also operates the [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer) in [Lyon](8638634.md), France, and the [WHO Centre for Health Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Centre_for_Health_Development) in [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe), Japan.[^cite_note-280] Additional offices include those in [Pristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pristina); the [West Bank](33209.md) and [Gaza](12047.md); the US-Mexico Border Field Office in [El Paso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas); the Office of the Caribbean Program Coordination in Barbados; and the Northern Micronesia office.[^cite_note-detailedinformation-281] There will generally be one WHO country office in the capital, occasionally accompanied by satellite-offices in the provinces or sub-regions of the country in question.

The country office is headed by a WHO Representative (WR). As of 2010, the only WHO Representative outside Europe to be a national of that country was for the [Libyan Arab Jamahiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) ("Libya"); all other staff was international. WHO Representatives in the Region termed the Americas are referred to as PAHO/WHO Representatives. In Europe, WHO Representatives also serve as head of the country office, and are nationals except for Serbia; there are also heads of the country office in Albania, the Russian Federation, Tajikistan, Turkey, and Uzbekistan.[^cite_note-detailedinformation-281] The WR is a member of the [UN system](216034.md) country team which is coordinated by the UN System [Resident Coordinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Coordinator).

The country office consists of the WR, and several health and other experts, both foreign and local, as well as the necessary support staff.[^cite_note-peopleandoffices-279] The main functions of WHO country offices include being the primary adviser of that country's government in matters of health and pharmaceutical policies.[^cite_note-282]

### Regional offices

The regional divisions of WHO were created between 1949 and 1952, following the model of the pre-existing [Pan American Health Organization](1246706.md#History),[^cite_note-Hanrieder-2015-283] and are based on article 44 of the WHO's constitution, which allowed the WHO to "establish a \[single] regional organization to meet the special needs of \[each defined] area". Many decisions are made at the regional level, including important discussions over WHO's budget, and in deciding the members of the next assembly, which are designated by the regions.[^cite_note-burci53-284]

Each region has a regional committee, which generally meets once a year, normally in the autumn. Representatives attend from each member or associative member in each region, including those states that are not full members. For example, [Palestine](241405.md) attends meetings of the [Eastern Mediterranean Regional Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean_Regional_office_of_World_Health_Organization). Each region also has a regional office.[^cite_note-burci53-284] Each regional office is headed by a director, who is elected by the Regional Committee. The board must approve such appointments, although as of 2004, it had never over-ruled the preference of a regional committee. The exact role of the board in the process has been a subject of debate, but the practical effect has always been small.[^cite_note-burci53-284] Since 1999, regional directors serve for a once-renewable five-year term, and typically take their position on 1 February.[^cite_note-285]

Each regional committee of the WHO consists of all the Health Department heads, in all the governments of the countries that constitute the Region. Aside from electing the regional director, the regional committee is also in charge of setting the guidelines for the implementation, within the region, of the health and other policies adopted by the [World Health Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Assembly). The regional committee also serves as a progress review board for the actions of WHO within the Region. The regional director is effectively the head of WHO for their region. The RD manages and/or supervises a staff of health and other experts at the regional offices and in specialized centres. The RD is also the direct supervising authority – concomitantly with the WHO Director-General – of all the heads of WHO country offices, known as WHO Representatives, within the region.

The strong position of the regional offices has been criticized in WHO history for undermining its effectiveness and led to unsuccessful attempts to integrate them more strongly within 'One WHO'.[^cite_note-Hanrieder-2015-283] Disease specific programmes such as the smallpox eradication programme[^cite_note-286] or the 1980s Global Programme on AIDS[^cite_note-287] were set up with more direct, vertical structures that bypassed the regional offices.
