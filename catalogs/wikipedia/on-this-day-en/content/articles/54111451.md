Accession of [Moldova](19260.md) to the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) is on the agenda for [future enlargement of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_enlargement_of_the_EU).

Following an application by Moldova in March 2022, Moldova was officially granted candidate status by the EU on 22 June 2022.

On 14 December 2023, the European Council decided to open accession negotiations with Moldova.[^cite_note-bbc1412-6] Accession negotiations officially began on 25 June 2024, simultaneously with those with Ukraine.[^cite_note-negopen-7][^cite_note-enter_formal-8] Moldova set a target date of 2028 for EU Accession.[^cite_note-9]

It is one of nine current EU candidate countries, together with [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Albania_to_the_European_Union), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Montenegro_to_the_European_Union), [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_North_Macedonia_to_the_European_Union), [Serbia](10038148.md), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Turkey_to_the_European_Union) and [Ukraine](36830143.md).

## History

### European perspective

The European Parliament passed a resolution in 2014 stating that "in accordance with Article 49 of the [Treaty on European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty), Georgia, Moldova and Ukraine, as well as any other European country, have a European perspective and can apply for EU membership in compliance with the principles of [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), respect for fundamental freedoms and [human rights](13831.md), minority rights and ensuring the rule of rights".[^cite_note-en.trend.az-10]

### Membership application

After the outbreak of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), President of Moldova [Maia Sandu](40796149.md) signed the application for EU membership on 3 March 2022, together with [Igor Grosu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Grosu), President of the Moldovan Parliament; and [Natalia Gavrilița](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Gavrili%C8%9Ba), Prime Minister of Moldova.[^cite_note-11]

### Questionnaire

On 11 April 2022, the [Minister of Foreign Affairs and European Integration of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_and_European_Integration_\(Moldova\)) [Nicu Popescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicu_Popescu) received a questionnaire from the [European Commission](9974.md) (EC) following a meeting with the [European Commissioner for Neighbourhood and Enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Neighbourhood_and_Enlargement) [Olivér Várhelyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliv%C3%A9r_V%C3%A1rhelyi) as a result of Moldova's application for candidacy.[^cite_note-12] The response to the first part of the questionnaire was submitted back to the EC through the [Delegation of the European Union to Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delegation_of_the_European_Union_to_Moldova)'s head Janis Mazeiks by the Prime Minister of Moldova [Natalia Gavrilița](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Gavrili%C8%9Ba) on 22 April,[^cite_note-13] while the responses to the second part were submitted on 12 May 2022.[^cite_note-14]

### Recommendation

On 17 June 2022, the European Commission formally recommended that the European Council grant the Republic of Moldova the perspective to become a member of the European Union and candidate status for accession, with a number of conditions for the opening of accession negotiations.[^cite_note-15]

In 2026, in a letter addressed to European Council President António Costa, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Cypriot President Nikos Christodoulides, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz argued that Moldova and the Western Balkan candidate states should be brought closer to the European Union through “innovative solutions” and an accelerated accession process. He proposed measures such as privileged access to the EU internal market, closer participation in EU institutions and gradual integration into specific EU policy areas prior to full membership.[^cite_note-16]

### Candidacy

On 23 June 2022, [Moldova](19260.md) received the status of candidate together with [Ukraine](31750.md). Both countries received candidate status under the commitment of structural reforms. Moldova's structural reforms include:

1. improving economic efficiency

2. reducing corruption

3. better enforcing property rights

4. reducing the size of state-owned enterprises

5. improving [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use)

6. improving the labour market

On 22 June 2023, EU Neighbourhood and Enlargement Commissioner [Olivér Várhelyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliv%C3%A9r_V%C3%A1rhelyi) provided an oral update on Moldova's progress toward the reforms.[^cite_note-June2023Reforms-17]

On 13 September 2023, during the [State of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_European_Union) Address, [President of the European Commission](24468.md), [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) stated that the future of Moldova is "in our Union".[^cite_note-18]

In its June 2024 EU enlargement report, the [European Commission](9974.md) expressed concern over the dismissal of [Octavian Armașu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian_Arma%C8%99u) as governor of [National Bank of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Moldova) as a risk to [good governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_governance) and [central bank independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank_independence) in Moldova.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] His dismissal was likewise scrutinised by the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

On 20 October 2024, Moldova narrowly voted to amend [its constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Moldova) to include the aim of becoming a European Union member state in the [2024 Moldovan European Union membership constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Moldovan_European_Union_membership_constitutional_referendum).

On 28 September 2025, the pro-European [Party of Action and Solidarity](50481813.md) retained power in the [parliamentary election](67524672.md).[^cite_note-23] This was welcomed by President von der Leyen as a vote for "Europe. Democracy. Freedom".[^cite_note-24]

On 11 July 2026, the new designated prime minister, [Vasile Tofan](83660480.md), stated that the goal of his cabinet would be to sign an agreement on EU accession by the end of 2028.[^cite_note-25]

## Negotiations

The 2023 enlargement package report was released by the [European Commission](9974.md) on 8 November 2023, including updates on Moldova's acquis alignment and a recommendation for the Council to open accession negotiations.[^cite_note-UkraineMoldovaNegotiationsrec-26][^cite_note-2023Report-27]

On 14 December 2023, the European Council decided to open accession negotiations with Moldova.[^cite_note-bbc1412-6] On 21 June 2024, the European Union agreed to start membership negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-negopen-7][^cite_note-28] Accession negotiations officially opened on 25 June 2024, at the same time as those with [Ukraine](36830143.md).[^cite_note-negopen-7][^cite_note-enter_formal-8] Accession negotiations on the first cluster, Fundamentals, were expected to be officially opened in April 2025.[^cite_note-29]

The screening process was completed on 22 September 2025. Informal opening of all chapters began on 17 March 2026 to bypass Hungary's veto, and the first cluster was officially opened on 15 June 2026.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-Fundamentals_opened-31]

During the Third Accession Conference with Moldova on 14 July 2026, Cluster 6 (External Relations) was formally opened along with the two chapters inside of it: Chapter 30 (External Relations) and Chapter 31 (Foreign, Security & Defence Policy).[^cite_note-Cluster_6_Open-32]

## Challenges

According to [Octavian Calmîc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian_Calm%C3%AEc), who served as [Moldova's Minister of Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_Minister_of_Economy) from 2016 to 2017, the accession of Moldova to the EU presents potential benefits but also risks. These include the loss of agricultural productivity due to the concentration of land ownership and business operations in multinational corporations, similar to what has occurred in other EU countries. Calmîc has said that the loss of agricultural productivity could lead to a reduction in domestic food production and job losses in rural areas. Additionally, Moldova's local industries face challenges, particularly the risk of decreased competitiveness in both domestic and international markets, which could result in plant closures and unemployment. Furthermore, the energy sector is vulnerable, as Moldova may face high energy prices and a lack of investment in energy infrastructure, according to Calmîc.[^cite_note-115]

## Treaties

### Association Agreement

Main article: [Moldova–European Union Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova%E2%80%93European_Union_Association_Agreement)

The [EU Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Association_Agreement) (AA) was initialed on 29 November 2013 in Brussels.[^cite_note-116] It was signed on 27 June 2014 and was being provisionally applied (in particular the DCFTA) starting 1 September 2014.

On 1 July 2016, the Association Agreement (AA) between the European Union and the Republic of Moldova fully came into force, following ratification by all 31 signatories.[^cite_note-117]

The parties committed to co-operate and converge economic policy, legislation, and regulation across a broad range of areas, including equal rights for workers, the exchange of information and staff in the area of justice, the modernisation of Moldova's energy infrastructure,[^cite_note-118] and access to the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank).[^cite_note-119] The parties committed to regular [summit meetings](3271361.md), and meetings among [ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Moldova), other officials, and experts.[^cite_note-120]

The Association Agreement commits Moldova to economic, judicial and financial reforms to converge its policies and legislation to those of the European Union.[^cite_note-121]

The 30 parties are Moldova, the EU and [Euratom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euratom) and the 27 EU members.[^cite_note-122]

#### Malta

The ratification was performed in accordance with article 4(2)(b) of the Maltese European Union Act,[^cite_note-malta1-169] which reads that:

> "Provided that with regard to treaties and international conventions which Malta may accede to as Member State of the European Union, and treaties and international conventions which Malta is bound to ratify in its own name or on behalf of the European Community by virtue of its membership within the European Union, these shall come into force one month following their being submitted in order to be discussed by the Standing Committee on Foreign and European Affairs."[^cite_note-malta2-170]

As the treaty was submitted to the Standing Committee on Foreign and European Affairs on 21 July 2014,[^cite_note-malta3-171] the treaty came into force as part of the Maltese legislation on 21 August 2014.

#### United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland

The ratification was based on The European Union (Definition of Treaties) (Association Agreement) (Moldova) Order 2015, made in accordance with section 1(3) of the European Communities Act 1972, after having been approved by a resolution of each House of Parliament.

The agreement applied to the United Kingdom as an EU-member state until [Brexit](41688778.md) on 31 January 2020. During the [transition period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit_withdrawal_agreement#Transition_period) that followed Brexit, the agreement until 31 December 2020, the agreement still applied to the UK. The UK and Moldova announced on 8 October 2020 an agreement replacing the EU-Moldova Association Agreement between them,[^cite_note-196] which was provisionally applied from 1 January 2021.[^cite_note-197]

### Free trade area

The agreement established a [Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_and_Comprehensive_Free_Trade_Area) (DCFTA) between the EU and Moldova, including "the removal of import duties for most goods traded between the EU and Moldova" and "broad mutual access to trade in services for both partners".[^cite_note-198]

## Public opinion

A poll in June 2018 found that 46% preferred that Moldova join the EU versus 36% that preferred to join the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md).[^cite_note-199] A March 2022 survey conducted by Magenta Consulting found that, after president [Maia Sandu](40796149.md) announced that her government had officially submitted an application for membership of the European Union, 61% of Moldovans (40% 'totally', 21% 'rather') were in favour of EU membership, up from 52% before the start of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-200]

In May 2022, a poll in Moldova found that 56.1% supported EU membership.[^cite_note-202]

Two July 2023 polls in Moldova showed respectively 63% support[^cite_note-203] and 59% support[^cite_note-204] for Moldova joining the EU.

A [referendum on changing the constitution to include EU accession as a goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Moldovan_European_Union_membership_constitutional_referendum) took place on 20 October 2024. There were no voting stations in Transnistria; however, residents there were free to travel to other areas of Moldova to vote.[^cite_note-205] A narrow majority supported the referendum.[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

According to the 2025 annual survey of opinion in the Republic of Moldova, almost nine out of ten Moldovans (86%) feel their country has good relations with the EU (a ten-point increase compared to 2024). Trust in the EU is also at its highest ever level, 71%. Overall, most Moldovans (55%) have a positive image of the EU, compared to 14% who have a negative image. 60% said they supported EU membership, and 63% would vote in favour of accession if a referendum was held, compared to 26% against.[^cite_note-209]

### Gagauzia

On 2 February 2014, the [Autonomous Territorial Unit of Gagauzia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Territorial_Unit_of_Gagauzia) held two referendums on European integration. In one, 98.4% voted in favour of joining the [Customs Union of Belarus, Kazakhstan, and Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_Union_of_Belarus,_Kazakhstan,_and_Russia), while in the second 97.2% opposed further integration with the EU. 98.9% also supported the proposition that Gagauzia could declare independence if [Moldova unified with Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Moldova_and_Romania).[^cite_note-210] There is concern in Gagauzia that Moldova's integration with the EU could lead to such a unification with EU member Romania, which is unpopular in the autonomous region.[^cite_note-211]

## Schengen visa liberalisation

Moldova citizens have been able to travel to the Schengen area from 28 April 2014 without a visa. However, since October 2025, Moldovans need to comply with the EU's [EES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entry/Exit_System) system before entering any of the Schengen member countries.[^cite_note-212] The application of the new [ETIAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETIAS) rule was postponed to at least April 2027.[^cite_note-213]

## Unification of Moldova and Romania

Main article: [Unification of Moldova and Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Moldova_and_Romania)

There is some support for the unification of [Moldova](19260.md) and [Romania](25445.md), which would incorporate the current territory of Moldova into Romania and thus into the EU.[^cite_note-214] About 44% of the Moldovans that were polled in 2021 supported such a scenario.[^cite_note-215]
