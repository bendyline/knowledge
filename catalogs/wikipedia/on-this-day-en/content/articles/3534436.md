The **attorney general of New York**, also known as the **New York attorney general** (**NYAG**), is the chief legal officer of the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [New York](8210131.md) and head of the Department of Law of the [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_\(state\)).[^cite_note-1] The office has existed in various forms since 1626, originally established under the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) colonial government of New Netherland.&#x20;

[Democrat](5043544.md) [Letitia James](1598829.md) currently serves as [attorney general](6065438.md), in office since January 1, 2019.[^cite_note-2]

## Functions

The attorney general advises the executive branch of state government as well as defends actions and brings proceedings on its behalf. The attorney general acts independently of the [governor of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_York). The department's regulations are compiled in title 13 of the *[New York Codes, Rules and Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Codes,_Rules_and_Regulations)* (NYCRR).

## Organization

The legal functions of the Department of Law are divided primarily into five major divisions: Appeals and Opinions, State Counsel, Criminal Justice, Economic Justice and Social Justice.

## Terms of office

- From 1684 to 1777, when [New York](8210131.md) was under the British colonial government, the attorney general was appointed by the [British crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain), or the colonial governor on its behalf. In 1693, the attorney general earned a salary of 50 [pounds](270673.md).

- From 1777 to 1822, the attorney general was appointed by the [Council of Appointment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Appointment).

- From 1823 to 1846, the attorney general was elected by the [New York State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Legislature) for a three-year term.

- Attorneys general have been [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Attorney_General_elections) by the voters since 1847.
