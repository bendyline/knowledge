# Ministry of foreign affairs

"Foreign office" redirects here. For the British government department, see [Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office](59653.md).



The office of the [French Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs) on the [Quai d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quai_d'Orsay) in Paris



British Foreign Secretary [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan) and Dutch Minister of Foreign Affairs [Max van der Stoel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_van_der_Stoel) in 1975

In many countries, the **ministry of foreign affairs** (abbreviated as **MFA** or **MOFA**) is the highest government department exclusively or primarily responsible for the state's [foreign policy](233404.md) and relations, [diplomacy](28165911.md), [bilateral](429154.md), and [multilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism) relations affairs as well as for providing [international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International) support, including consular services, for a country's citizens who are abroad.[^cite_note-1] The entity is usually headed by a **foreign minister** or **minister of foreign affairs** (the title may vary, such as a [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_state) who has the same functions).[^cite_note-2] The foreign minister typically reports to the head of government (such as prime minister or president).[^cite_note-3]

## Difference in titles

In some nations, such as [India](14533.md), the foreign minister is referred to as the [minister for external affairs](10302410.md); or others, such as [Brazil](3383.md) and the states created from the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), call the position the minister of external relations. In the United States, the [secretary of state](32293.md) is the member of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) who handles foreign relations. Other common titles may include minister of foreign relations. In many countries of Latin America, the foreign minister is colloquially called "[chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor#Foreign_minister_and_diplomatic_official)" (*canciller* in the Spanish-speaking countries and *chanceler* in the Portuguese-speaking Brazil).

Diplomats, themselves, and historians often refer to the foreign ministry by its local address, for example, the [Ballhausplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballhausplatz) in Vienna housed the [Foreign Ministry of Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Ministry_of_Austria-Hungary); the [Quai d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quai_d'Orsay) in [Paris](22989.md) for France's [Ministry of Europe and Foreign Affairs](250002.md); the [South Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Block) in [New Delhi](51585.md) for India's [Ministry of External Affairs](10302410.md); the [Necessidades Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessidades_Palace) in Lisbon for Portugal's [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Portugal\)); the [Wilhelmstraße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmstra%C3%9Fe), in Berlin, was the location of the [German Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Foreign_Office); and [Foggy Bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foggy_Bottom), a neighborhood of Washington, D.C., houses the [Department of State](31975.md). The [Ministry of External Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Brazil\)) of Brazil is often referred as the "Itamaraty" due to the two homonymous palaces that served as its headquarters, the original one in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) (1899–1970) and the present [Itamaraty Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itamaraty_Palace) (since 1970) in [Brasília](4752.md). Indonesians also often refer to their [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Indonesia\)#Ministers) as "Pejambon", since the ministry's main headquarters is located at Pejambon Street, [Central Jakarta](2728760.md). During the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), which lasted until 1917, the term used was the [Choristers' Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pevchesky_Bridge) in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md). In contrast, the Italian ministry was called the [Consulta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Consulta).[^cite_note-4]

## Powers of position

A foreign minister's powers vary from government to government. In a classic [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system), a foreign minister can potentially exert significant influence in forming [foreign policy](233404.md) but when the government is dominated by a strong [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister), the foreign minister may be limited to playing a more marginal or subsidiary role in determining policy. Similarly, the political powers invested in the foreign minister are often more limited in [presidential governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) with a strong [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch). Since the end of World War II, it has been common for both the foreign minister and [defense minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_minister) to be part of an inner cabinet (commonly known as a [national security council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_council)) in order to coordinate defense and [diplomatic policy](28165911.md). Although the 19th and early 20th centuries saw many heads of government assume the foreign ministry, this practice has since become uncommon in most [developed nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_nation).

In some countries, the foreign minister is typically among the highest profiles of cabinet positions. For instance, in the US, its foreign minister is the first member of cabinet in line for the presidential line of succession (with the vice president, speaker of the United States House of Representatives, and president pro-tempore of the United States Senate ahead of the foreign minister). The UK's foreign secretary belongs to the four Great Offices of State (along with the prime minister, chancellor of the exchequer, and home secretary).

## Responsibilities

Along with their political roles, foreign ministers are also traditionally responsible for many diplomatic duties, such as hosting foreign world leaders and going on [state visits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visits) to other countries. The foreign minister is generally the most well-traveled member of any cabinet.

- In the United Kingdom, the minister responsible for foreign policy (as well as the [British Overseas Territories](207114.md)) is the [Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(United_Kingdom\)) (Foreign Secretary). Before 1968, the [secretary of state for foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_\(UK\)) only handled relations with foreign (non-[Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations)) countries, while relations with Commonwealth countries and colonies were handled by the [secretary of state for Commonwealth affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Commonwealth_Affairs). For the same reason, in Commonwealth countries other than the United Kingdom, the ministers responsible for handling relations with both Commonwealth and non-Commonwealth countries were formerly usually designated ministers for external affairs.

- In the United States, the [secretary of state](32293.md) handles foreign policy and is the senior [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cabinet) officer. The name of the post comes from several domestic duties. Under the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation), the title was "Secretary of Foreign Affairs".

Although it is very rare for there to be any sub-national foreign minister post, sometimes there is a minor external relations position. The [European Union](9317.md) has dealt with external relations in certain areas since its inception (see [EU Trade Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Trade)) and has a [high representative](750942.md) as its chief diplomat. However, his or her duties are primarily to implement [EU foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy), rather than formulate it.
