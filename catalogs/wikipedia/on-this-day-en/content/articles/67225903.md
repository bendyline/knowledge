# Total intravenous anaesthesia

**Total intravenous anesthesia** (**TIVA**) refers to the [intravenous administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous_administration) of [anesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthetic) agents to induce a temporary loss of sensation or awareness. The first study of TIVA was done in 1872 using [chloral hydrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloral_hydrate),[^cite_note-\:3-1] and the common anesthetic agent [propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol) was licensed in 1986. TIVA is currently employed in various procedures as an alternative technique of [general anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_anaesthesia) in order to improve post-operative recovery.

TIVA is maintained by [intravenous infusion devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusion_pump) and assisted by [electroencephalography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography) (EEG) monitoring. These techniques facilitate the use of [propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol), [etomidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etomidate), [ketamine](16948.md), and other intravenous anesthetic agents. During or after TIVA, patients may be subjected to an elevated risk of [anesthesia awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesia_awareness), [hyperalgesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperalgesia) and [neurotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotoxicity).[^cite_note-\:4-2] Considering these risks, special consideration is given to obese, elderly and pediatric patients.

## History

In the mid-19th century, specific equipment was developed to enable intravenous anesthesia. Francis Rynd developed the hollow needle in 1845,[^cite_note-\:3-1] and Charles Gabriel Pravaz developed the [syringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syringe) in 1853,[^cite_note-\:3-1] which allowed drugs to be administered intravenously.

Using this new mode of delivery, many chemical compounds were tested as intravenous anesthetics. This was pioneered by Pierre-Cyprien Oré in 1872, who reported using [chloral hydrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloral_hydrate) as an intravenous anesthetic.[^cite_note-\:3-1] However, these early trials were associated with high mortality.[^cite_note-\:3-1] [Hedonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonal) was later developed in 1909 for general anesthesia, although with limited success due to its long duration of effect.[^cite_note-3] These insufficiencies encouraged the development of [paraldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraldehyde) by Noel & Souttar,[^cite_note-4] [magnesium sulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium_sulfate) by Peck & Meltzer[^cite_note-5] as well as [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) by Nakagawa[^cite_note-6] as intravenous anesthetic agents.

[Propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol) (di-isopropyl phenol) was synthesized by Glen and colleagues in the early 1970s,[^cite_note-7] but its first formulations were temporarily withdrawn due to a number of adverse reactions during clinical studies.[^cite_note-\:3-1] In 1983, a lipid emulsion formulation of propofol was available, which carried great potential during clinical trials.[^cite_note-8] It was licensed for use in Europe in 1986 and received [FDA](11632.md) approval in the US in 1989.[^cite_note-\:3-1] Propofol is now used worldwide with a well-defined pharmacological profile for a variety of medical uses.

## Medical uses

TIVA is used to induce [general anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_anaesthesia) while avoiding the [disadvantages of volatile anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhalational_anesthetic) (and traditional inhalation agents).[^cite_note-9] Intravenous anesthetic agents are titrated at safe doses to maintain [stage III surgical anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guedel's_classification) (unconsciousness, amnesia, immobility, and absence of response to noxious stimulation).[^cite_note-10] The use of TIVA is advantageous in cases where volatile anesthesia is of high risk or is impossible, such as cases involving [morbidly obese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbid_obesity) patients.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] TIVA has also been used for anesthetic delivery at sites of trauma such as serious accidents, disasters and wars.[^cite_note-\:3-1]

The overall goals of TIVA include:[^cite_note-13]

- Smooth induction of anesthesia

- Reliable and measurable maintenance of anesthesia

- Rapid emergence out of the effects of infused drugs as soon as the [infusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusion_fluid) is terminated.

Propofol-based TIVA significantly improves post-operative recovery profile and comfort, minimizes [nausea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nausea) and [vomiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vomiting), facilitates rapid recovery, greater [hemodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemodynamic) stability, preservation of [hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxic_pulmonary_vasoconstriction), reduction in intracerebral pressure, and reduces the risk of organ toxicity.[^cite_note-14] Despite these advantages, it accounts for a small proportion of general anesthetics due to the relatively expensive cost of preparation and maintenance.[^cite_note-15]

## Techniques

### Dosing considerations

The doses for intravenous [sedative-hypnotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedative_hypnotic) and [adjuvant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjuvant_therapy) agents vary individually. [Pharmacodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacodynamics) and [pharmacokinetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacokinetics) factors need to be considered for each patient (e.g. patients with impaired kidney or liver function, blood abnormalities and [myocardial dysfunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiomyopathy), etc.)[^cite_note-16] There are also risks of [adverse effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_effect) related to doses such as [hypotension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension) and [respiratory depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_depression).[^cite_note-\:0-17] In terms of adjuvant agents, the co-administration of anesthetic drugs from different classes often produce [synergistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergy) [hypnotic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnotic).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Is_synergy_the_rule?_A_review_of_an-19] This is especially common for agents acting on [gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-aminobutyric_acid_receptor_subunit_alpha-1) that are combined with drugs acting on different types of receptors.[^cite_note-Bowdle_2009_291–295-20][^cite_note-Jäntti_2008_77–93-21]

The drug interactions between sedative-hypnotic agents and adjuvant agents suggest that dosing regimens cannot be fixed.[^cite_note-Is_synergy_the_rule?_A_review_of_an-19] Instead, dosing should be based on adjusted body weight or estimated lean body weight, especially for obese patients. It is recommended that drug doses be titrated in brief intervals (around 20 to 60 seconds).[^cite_note-22]

### Equipment

&#x20;The delivery of intravenous anesthetics is dependent on different types of infusion devices. Examples of [infusion devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusion_pump) include smart pumps, [syringe pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syringe_pump) and [target-controlled infusion (TCI) devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_controlled_infusion).[^cite_note-Nimmo_211–224-23]



A smart pump used to deliver intravenous agents

Smart pumps are commonly used to administer potent anesthetics and various vasoactive drugs such as [vasopressors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasopressors), [inotropes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inotrope), [vasodilators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasodilation), which need to be continuously titrated in the operating room.[^cite_note-24] Smart pumps are advantageous since they administer safe doses with a programmed infusion rate within pre-existing limits based on the institutional standardized medication library.[^cite_note-25]

[Syringe pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syringe_pump) are smaller infusion pumps that allow the administration of small amounts of induction agents at a precise rate.[^cite_note-26] The accuracy of syringe pumps is dependent on the selection of syringes during pump programming. Most pumps are able to identify the size of the syringe automatically when the syringe manufacturer's name is input correctly.[^cite_note-27]

[Target-controlled infusion (TCI) systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_controlled_infusion) are assisted by computer systems that make use of [pharmacokinetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacokinetics) and [pharmacodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacodynamics) modelling to maintain a target concentration of anesthetic in the brain.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] TCI requires clinicians to input a target concentration for an anesthetic or other agents, from which the computer calculates the amount of agent required for the input concentration, then the infusion pump delivers the calculated [bolus dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolus_dose).[^cite_note-30] Subsequently, the computer continuously recalculates how much drug is in the system and influences the amount of drug required to maintain the desired concentration at the effect site.[^cite_note-31]



An EEG recording cap for monitoring awareness

### Maintenance

During TIVA, the continuous assessment of [heart rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_rate), [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure), and [state of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_consciousness) is essential when titrating anesthetic agents.[^cite_note-Bowdle_2009_291–295-20][^cite_note-Nimmo_211–224-23] Processed [electroencephalogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography) (EEG) monitoring is used to assess anesthetic depth.[^cite_note-32] However, there is 30 seconds of lag time between the subject's state of consciousness and the processed EEG signal. This limits its usefulness during the induction of anesthesia.[^cite_note-Bowdle_2009_291–295-20][^cite_note-Jäntti_2008_77–93-21]

## Intravenous agents

[Propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol), [etomidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etomidate) and [ketamine](16948.md) are common [intravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous_therapy) [sedative-hypnotic agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedative_hypnotic) for the induction of TIVA.[^cite_note-Is_synergy_the_rule?_A_review_of_an-19] Their highly lipophilic nature allows the rapid onset of anesthesia upon intravenous injection.[^cite_note-\:0-17] It also enables penetration through the [blood–brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier) and effective [perfusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfusion) to the brain. However, the rapid [redistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(pharmacology\)) of these agents from the brain to other muscle and fat tissues causes it to have a short duration of action. [Adjuvant agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjuvant_therapy) are typically administered in addition to sedative-hypnotic agents to supplement the induction of TIVA.[^cite_note-\:0-17]

### Sedative-hypnotic agents

#### Propofol

[Propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol) is usually the selected sedative-hypnotic agent to maintain general anesthesia through TIVA because of its rapid onset and offset, beneficial properties and few adverse effects.[^cite_note-\:6-33] Its rapid onset of action is due to its high [lipid-solubility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid-soluble), rapid redistribution from the brain to other parts of the body, and rapid [clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearance_\(pharmacology\)) (20 to 30 mL/kg/minute).[^cite_note-\:6-33] Most propofol is [conjugated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_\(pharmacokinetics\)) in the liver with pharmacologically inactive metabolites.[^cite_note-\:6-33] Although it has a long [terminal elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_\(pharmacology\)) half-life of 4 to 30 hours, [plasma concentrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_concentrations) remain low after the typical induction dose.[^cite_note-\:6-33]

Its advantages include “[antiemetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiemetic), [antipruritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipruritic), [bronchodilatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchodilator), and [anticonvulsant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticonvulsant) properties”,[^cite_note-34] which makes it suitable for patients with kidney or liver insufficiency.[^cite_note-35] Potential adverse effects of propofol include [hypotension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension) and [respiratory depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_depression) caused by inadequate dosing, pain on injection, and risk of contamination.[^cite_note-36]

#### Etomidate

[Etomidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etomidate) is suitable for patients with hemodynamic instability since it does not compromise blood pressure, cardiac output, or heart rate.[^cite_note-\:7-37][^cite_note-38] Its advantages include [anticonvulsant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticonvulsant) properties and hemodynamic stability. Potential adverse effects include a higher incidence of [postoperative nausea and vomiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postoperative_nausea_and_vomiting), [transient acute adrenal insufficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_adrenal_insufficiency), pain during injection, [involuntary myoclonic movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myoclonic_dystonia), absence of [analgesic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analgesic) and mild increases in [airway resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_resistance).[^cite_note-\:7-37][^cite_note-39]

#### Ketamine

Ketamine is suitable for hypotensive patients, or patients with risks of developing [hypotension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension) (e.g. those who have [hypovolemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypovolemia), [hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemorrhage), [sepsis](158400.md) or severe cardiovascular compromise).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] This is because ketamine is associated with increased blood pressure, heart rate and cardiac output.[^cite_note-42] Its advantages include profound [analgesic properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analgesic), [bronchodilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchodilator), and the ability to maintain [airway reflexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cough_reflex) and [respiratory drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_drive).[^cite_note-43] It could also be induced via the [intramuscular route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuscular_injection) if TIVA access gets lost. However, its potential adverse effects impact cardiovascular and neurological functions.

Potential adverse effects on cardiovascular activities are listed below:[^cite_note-\:2-44]

- Increase in myocardial [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) demand due to a rise in heart rate, blood pressure and cardiac output

- Increase in pulmonary arterial pressure, which could be fatal in patients with [ischemic heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischemic_Heart_Disease), [systemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_hypertension) or [pulmonary hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_hypertension)

- Increase in the toxicity of [cocaine](7701.md) and [tricyclic antidepressants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricyclic_antidepressant) on cardiovascular structures

- Exacerbates [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension), [tachycardia arrhythmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia) in [pheochromocytoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheochromocytoma)

- Though rare, direct mild [myocardial depressant effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_depressant_factor)

Potential adverse effects on neurological activities are listed below:[^cite_note-\:2-44]

- Higher incidence of [psychotomimetic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychotomimetic)

- Increase in [cerebral blood flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_circulation) and [intracranial pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracranial_pressure), which may increase the cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen

- Unique EEG effects might lead to misinterpretation of [processed EEG values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEG_analysis)

### Adjuvant agents

[Opioid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid), [lidocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidocaine) and [midazolam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midazolam) are [adjuvant agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjuvant_therapy) frequently administered to minimize pain during the injection of the induction agents.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] They are also used to lessen the sympathetic stress response, cough reflex during [laryngoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laryngoscopy) or [intubation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intubation), and supplement sedation by [synergistic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergy).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-\:9-48] The dose of sedative-hypnotic agents should be reduced due to the [synergistic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergy) when combined with adjuvant agents.[^cite_note-\:9-48]

Choice of specific adjuvant agents is dependent upon the patient and procedure-specific factors.[^cite_note-49] Opioid is a commonly administered adjuvant agent as the analgesic component of TIVA. However, when used with propofol, it might exacerbate the adverse hypotensive effects.[^cite_note-\:9-48] Other potential adverse effects include respiratory depression, [bradycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradycardia), [delirium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delirium) and potential for [acute tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_tolerance).[^cite_note-\:1-50]

## Risks and complications

### Accidental awareness during general anesthesia (AAGA)

Main article: [Anesthesia awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesia_awareness)

Patients under TIVA have a higher risk of AAGA. Unlike inhaled anesthetic agents, intravenous agents do not have an indicative [end-tidal anesthetic concentration (ETAC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesia_awareness) for the monitoring of administered drugs, so the determination of successful delivery is usually left to the anesthetist's clinical judgment.[^cite_note-\:4-2]

The high incidence of AAGA with TIVA can be attributed to several factors. Firstly, the target concentration of anesthetic agents required to maintain unresponsiveness is not well understood.[^cite_note-\:4-2] Although there have been studies aiming to establish the target concentration of propofol, there is a high degree of variability with the established dosing range.[^cite_note-\:5-51] Secondly, intravenous delivery may be impaired by lax monitoring of the [intravenous catheter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catheter) and the insertion site.[^cite_note-\:4-2] Thirdly, the use of [neuromuscular blockades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular-blocking_drug) is a risk factor of AAGA and also hinders communication of distress in the case of [accidental awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accidental_awareness).[^cite_note-\:4-2]

### Opioid-induced hyperalgesia

TIVA techniques which involve the continued administration of [opioids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid) (e.g. [remifentanil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remifentanil)) at high doses can cause [opioid-induced hyperalgesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid-induced_hyperalgesia).[^cite_note-52] This may lead to difficult postoperative pain control, as patients with [hyperalgesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperalgesia) experience increased chronic pain and require more [analgesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analgesic) following surgery.[^cite_note-\:5-51]

### Neurotoxicity

Prolonged anesthetic exposure can result in the death of neural cells and defective [synaptogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaptogenesis),[^cite_note-53] caused by increased expression of neurologically harmful substances.[^cite_note-\:4-2] The resulting neurologic injuries may lead to a persistent subtle decline of cognitive abilities, especially in elderly or very young patients.[^cite_note-\:4-2] Animal studies suggest that propofol may have similar neurotoxic properties as it is associated with apoptotic degeneration of [oligodendrocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligodendrocyte).[^cite_note-\:4-2]

## Special populations

### Obese patients

Obese patients present technical and physiological challenges to TIVA. Physical tasks such as [surgical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgery) positioning, intravenous insertion and [ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventilation_\(physiology\)) are complicated by excess fat.[^cite_note-\:10-54] Associated physiological and pharmacological changes include higher susceptibility to [hypoxemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxemia), decrease in [resting metabolic rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resting_metabolic_rate) and lower [cardiac output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_output) per kg body weight.[^cite_note-55] The use of dosing models derived from non-obese patients is therefore unsuitable for obese patients.[^cite_note-56]

Even within the obese population, the large variability between individuals limits the accuracy of pharmacokinetic models in predicting and informing [anesthetic titration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_anaesthesia).[^cite_note-\:10-54]

### Pediatrics

Infants differ from adults in the consideration of [pharmacokinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacokinetics), [pharmacodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacodynamics) and side effects.[^cite_note-\:11-57] In terms of pharmacokinetics, protein binding, organ function and body composition are significantly different.[^cite_note-58] Pharmacodynamic effects such as the capacity of target organs to respond to drugs are also changed.[^cite_note-\:11-57] Based on this knowledge, doses are adjusted to achieve optimal clinical response and avoid toxicity in pediatric patients.[^cite_note-59] Generally, [clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearance_\(pharmacology\)) (drug elimination from the body) is greater in children due to the nonlinear scaling between body size and function.[^cite_note-\:11-57]

### Elderly patients

Aging is associated with an increase in fat and a reduction in [lean body mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_body_mass) and [total body water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_body_water).[^cite_note-60] These factors increase the [volume of distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_of_distribution) of lipid-soluble drugs, lower their [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_plasma) concentration and delay [elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_\(pharmacology\)).[^cite_note-\:12-61] Aged patients typically have a higher sensitivity to drug action due to a reduction in the initial drug [clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearance_\(pharmacology\)), resulting in higher plasma concentration and hence greater initial drug effect.[^cite_note-\:12-61]
