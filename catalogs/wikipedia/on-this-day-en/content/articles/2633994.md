**Lesbos** or **Lesvos** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Λέσβος, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek): *Lésvos* [\[ˈlezvos\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek)) is a [Greek island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_island) located in the northeastern [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea). It has an area of 1,633 km<sup>2</sup> (631 sq mi),[^cite_note-stat01-2] with approximately 400 km (249 mi) of [coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline), making it the third largest island in [Greece](12108.md) and the [eighth largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Mediterranean#By_area) in the Mediterranean. It is separated from [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) by the narrow [Mytilini Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilini_Strait). On the southeastern coast is the island's capital and largest city, [Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilene) (Μυτιλήνη), whose name is also used for the island as a whole. Lesbos is a separate [regional unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_units_of_Greece) with the seat in Mytilene, which is also the capital of the larger [North Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Aegean) region. The region includes the islands of Lesbos, [Chios](17067.md), [Ikaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikaria), [Lemnos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemnos), and [Samos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samos). The total population of the island was 83,755 in 2021.[^cite_note-census21-1] A third of the island's inhabitants live in the capital, while the remainder are concentrated in small towns and villages. The largest are [Plomari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plomari), [Agia Paraskevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agia_Paraskevi,_Lesbos), [Polichnitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polichnitos), [Agiassos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agiassos), [Eresos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eresos), [Gera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gera,_Lesbos), and [Molyvos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molyvos) (the ancient Mythimna).&#x20;

According to later Greek writers, Mytilene was founded in the 11th century BC by the family Penthilidae, who arrived from [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly) and ruled the city-state until a popular revolt (590–580 BC) led by [Pittacus of Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittacus_of_Mytilene) ended their rule. In fact, the archaeological and linguistic records may indicate a late [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) arrival of Greek settlers, although references in Late Bronze Age Hittite archives indicate a likely Greek presence then. According to [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer)'s *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad)*, Lesbos was part of the kingdom of [Priam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priam), which ruled from [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy). In the Middle Ages, it was under [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and then [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) rule. Lesbos was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_conquest_of_Lesbos) by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1462. The Ottomans then ruled the island until the [First Balkan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Balkan_War) in 1912, when it became part of the Kingdom of Greece.&#x20;

## Names

The [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) name *Lesbos* (pronounced [/ˈlɛzbɒs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), also [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˈlɛzbəs, -boʊs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) [Λέσβος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%9B%CE%AD%CF%83%CE%B2%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek) (*Lésbos*).[^cite_note-OEtymD|lesbian-3] The name appears in [Late Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age) [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_language) texts as *Lazpa* ([Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_language): 𒆷𒊍𒉺 *Lāzpa*).[^cite_note-Beckman-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:0-7] The earliest reference to Lesbos in Greek texts comes from the [Homeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer) poems, where it is described as "well-built".[^cite_note-\:0-7] The etymology of the name is obscure,[^cite_note-8] but may have originally meant 'forested', 'wooded'.[^cite_note-OEtymD|lesbian-3]&#x20;

In [Modern Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Greek), the letter [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta) ⟨β⟩ is pronounced and transliterated as [\[v\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek), thus producing the alternative form *Lesvos*. An older name for the island that was maintained in [Aeolic Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolic_Greek) was Ἴσσα (*Íssa*). [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) also refers to the island with the names ἱμερτή (*himertḗ*, 'desirable') and Λασία (*Lasía*, often understood as 'shaggy').[^cite_note-\:0-7] In Greece, Lesbos is commonly referred to as Mytilene (Μυτιλήνη) after its capital.[^cite_note-9] Some suggest that the name derives from the [Anatolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_languages) root "muwa" meaning power,[^cite_note-\:0-7] while others have suggested a link to the ancient Greek word μυτίλος (*mytilos*), meaning [mussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussel), or a type thereof.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The ending *-ene* appears to be the common Greek place name suffix (*-enos* in [masculine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masculine_gender)) indicating provenance.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The island is also sometimes called the "Island of the Poets", alluding to renowned native poets like [Alcaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcaeus_of_Mytilene) and [Sappho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sappho).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-News.gr_2018-13]&#x20;

## History

### Prehistory

The oldest artifacts found on the island may date to the late [Paleolithic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_period).[^cite_note-14] Important archaeological sites on the island are the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) cave of [Kagiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kagiani\&action=edit\&redlink=1), probably a refuge for shepherds, the Neolithic settlement of [Chalakies](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chalakies\&action=edit\&redlink=1).&#x20;

### Early Bronze Age

Lesbos has been inhabited since at least 3000 BC. There is the extensive habitation of Thermi (3000–1000 BC). The largest habitation is found in Lisvori, dating back to 2800–1900 BC, part of which is submerged in shallow coastal waters.&#x20;

### Late Bronze Age

Lesbos (Lāzpa) is mentioned in two [Hittite texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_texts) from the [Late Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age), a period during which the island appears to have been a dependent of the [Seha River Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seha_River_Land). The [Manapa-Tarhunta letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manapa-Tarhunta_letter) recounts an incident in which a group of purple-dyers from Lesbos defected from the Sehan king.[^cite_note-AT7-15]&#x20;

### Ancient and Classical era



Coin of Lesbos under the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), c. 510–480 BC

According to Classical [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), Lesbos was the [patron god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron_god) of the island. [Macareus of Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macareus_of_Rhodes) was reputedly the first king whose many daughters bequeathed their names to some of the present larger towns. In Classical myth his sister, [Canace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canace), was killed to have him made king. The place names with female origins are claimed by some to be much earlier settlements named after local goddesses, who were replaced by gods; however, there is little evidence to support this. [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer) refers to the island as "*Macaros edos*," the seat of Macar. [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) records from the Late [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) name the island *Lazpa* and must have considered its population significant enough to allow the Hittites to "borrow their gods" (presumably idols) to cure their king when the local gods were not forthcoming. It is believed that emigrants from mainland Greece, mainly from [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly), entered the island and [Aeolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolis) in the opposite coast, in the Late Bronze Age, and bequeathed it with the [Aeolic dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolic_dialect) of the Greek language, whose written form survives in the poems of [Sappho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sappho), amongst others. In classical times, the cities of the island formed a [pentapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentapolis), comprising [Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilene), [Methymna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methymna), [Antissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antissa), [Eresos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eresos), and [Pyrrha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrha_\(Lesbos\)).[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] Pyrrha was destroyed in an earthquake in 231 BC, and Antissa by the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) in 168 BC.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16]&#x20;



[Sappho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sappho) listens as the poet [Alcaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcaeus_of_Mytilene) plays a [kithara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kithara). (Painting by [Lawrence Alma-Tadema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Alma-Tadema), 1881)

[^cite_note-17]

Two of the [nine lyric poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_lyric_poets) in the Ancient Greek canon, Sappho and [Alcaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcaeus_of_Mytilene), were from Lesbos. [Phanias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phanias_of_Eresus) wrote history. The seminal artistic creativity of those times brings to mind the myth of [Orpheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheus) to whom [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo) gave a [lyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre) and the [Muses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse) taught to play and sing. When Orpheus incurred the wrath of the god Dionysus he was dismembered by the Maenads and of his body parts his head and his lyre found their way to Lesbos where they have "remained" ever since. [Pittacus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittacus) was one of the [Seven Sages of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sages_of_Greece). In classical times, [Hellanicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellanicus_of_Lesbos) advanced historiography and [Theophrastus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophrastus), the father of botany, succeeded [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle) as the head of the Lyceum. Aristotle and [Epicurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicurus) lived there for some time, and it is there that Aristotle began systematic zoological investigations.[^cite_note-18]&#x20;



View of the Roman aqueduct

[Theophanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophanes_of_Mytilene), the historian who recorded [Pompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey)'s campaigns, was also from Lesbos. As the Greek novel *[Daphnis and Chloe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphnis_and_Chloe)* is set on Lesbos, the author, [Longus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longus), is usually assumed to be from the island. The abundant grey pottery ware found on the island and the worship of [Cybele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybele), the great mother-goddess of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), suggest the cultural continuity of the population from [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) times. When the Persian king [Cyrus the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_the_Great) defeated [Croesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croesus) (546 BC) the Ionic Greek cities of Anatolia and the adjacent islands became Persian subjects and remained such until the Persians were defeated by the Greeks at the [Battle of Salamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Salamis) (480 BC). The island was governed by an [oligarchy](22315.md) in [archaic times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_Greece), followed by quasi-democracy in [classical times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greece). Around this time, [Arion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arion) developed the type of poem called [dithyramb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dithyramb), the progenitor of tragedy, and [Terpander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terpander) invented the seven-note musical scale for the lyre. For a short period it was a member of the [Athenian confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delian_League), its apostasy from which is recounted by [Thucydides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thucydides) in the *[Mytilenian Debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilenian_Debate)*, in Book III of his *[History of the Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Peloponnesian_War)*. In [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic) times, the island belonged to various [Diadochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadochi) of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) until 79 BC, when it passed into [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) hands. Remnants of its Roman medieval history are three impressive castles. The cities of Mytilene and Methymna have been bishoprics since the 5th century. By the early 10th century, Mytilene had been raised to the status of a [metropolitan see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_see). Methymna achieved the same by the 12th century.[^cite_note-ODB-19]&#x20;

### Middle Ages and Byzantine era

During the Middle Ages, Lesbos belonged to the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). In 802, the Byzantine Empress [Irene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_of_Athens) was exiled to Lesbos after her deposition and died there.[^cite_note-ODB-19] The island served as a gathering base for the fleet of the rebel [Thomas the Slav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Slav) in the early 820s.[^cite_note-ODB-19] In the late 9th century, it was heavily raided by the [Emirate of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Crete). As a result, the inhabitants of Eresos abandoned their town and settled in [Mount Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos).[^cite_note-EI2-20] In the 10th century, it was part of the [theme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_\(Byzantine_district\)) of the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea_\(theme\)), while in the late 11th century it formed a *dioikesis* (fiscal district) under a *kourator* in Mytilene.[^cite_note-ODB-19] In c. 1089–1093, the island was briefly occupied by the [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) Turkish emir [Tzachas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzachas), ruler of [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna), but he was unable to capture Methymna, which resisted throughout.[^cite_note-ODB-19][^cite_note-EI2-20] In the 12th century, the island became a frequent target for plundering raids by the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice).[^cite_note-ODB-19]&#x20;

After the [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade) (1202–1204), the island passed to the [Latin Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Empire), but was reconquered by the [Empire of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Nicaea) sometime after 1224.[^cite_note-ODB-19] The notable [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greeks) general [Alexios Philanthropenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexios_Philanthropenos) was then named briefly governor of Lesbos in 1328, and again in 1336. He ruled the island thereafter, probably until his death in the 1340s.&#x20;



Denaro of [Francesco II Gattilusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_II_Gattilusio), lord of Lesbos (1384–1403)

In 1354, it was granted as a dowry and fief to the [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) [Francesco I Gattilusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_I_Gattilusio) by the Byzantine emperor [John V Palaiologos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_V_Palaiologos).[^cite_note-ODB-19][^cite_note-EI2-20] The [Gattilusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gattilusio) family ruled the island for over a century, engaging in fortifications at the [Castle of Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_of_Mytilene), Molyvos (ancient Methymna), and the fort of Agios Theodoros at the site of ancient Antissa.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16]&#x20;

### Ottoman era



Map of Lesbos by Giacomo Franco (1597)

After the [Fall of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople) in 1453, the Gattilusi continued to rule Lesbos as tributary vassals to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), until the island was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mytilene_\(1462\)) by Sultan [Mehmed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_II) in September 1462.[^cite_note-ODB-19][^cite_note-EI2-20] After the capture of Lesbos, the richer inhabitants were moved to [Constantinople](3391396.md) in order to repopulate the city, some boys and girls were taken away into imperial service, but the rest of the population remained. Mehmed II brought in Muslim settlers from [Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumelia) and [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), and encouraged his [Janissaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janissaries) to settle there and take local wives.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16][^cite_note-EI2-20] Among them was Yakub, the father of the pirate admiral [Hayreddin Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Barbarossa).[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] Named Midilli (مدللى) after its capital, Mytilene, the island became a *[sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak)* (province) of the [Eyalet of Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyalet_of_Rumelia), and after 1534 of the [Eyalet of the Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyalet_of_the_Archipelago).[^cite_note-EI2-20] Mytilene and Molova (the Turkish name for Molyvos/Methymna) became seats of [kadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadi_\(Ottoman_Empire\)). The cathedral of Mytilene was converted into a mosque. Otherwise, the organization of the local Orthodox church was not altered.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16]&#x20;

In 1464, as part of the [First Ottoman–Venetian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ottoman%E2%80%93Venetian_War), the Venetians under [Orsato Giustiniani](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orsato_Giustiniani\&action=edit\&redlink=1) occupied the fort of Agios Theodoros, but failed to capture the rest of the island, and destroyed the castle upon their withdrawal. Another attack occurred in 1474, when the Venetians under [Pietro Mocenigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Mocenigo) raided the island.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] During the [Second Ottoman–Venetian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Ottoman%E2%80%93Venetian_War), a Venetian-led fleet of 200 ships [besieged Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mytilene_\(1501\)), but the attack was defeated by [Şehzade Korkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Eehzade_Korkut). His father, Sultan [Bayezid II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_II), then reinforced the Castle of Mytilene with artillery bastions.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16]&#x20;

The large majority of the island's population remained Greek Christian, although there was a sizeable [Muslim](19541.md) community, formed from both immigrants and converts; from 7.4% of households in 1488, it rose to a peak of 19.45% in 1831 before starting to decline in relative terms, reaching 14% in 1892. The Islamization process peaked between 1602 and 1644.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] The Muslims lived throughout the island. Relations between the two communities were generally good, and Lesbians were often bilingual in both [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and [Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish).[^cite_note-EI2-20] During Ottoman rule, the compulsory [devshirme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devshirme) system was implemented into the island, where the locals including Muslim landowners and the state representatives negotiated enlisting their teenagers into the Ottoman military by preventing some boys from being levied and sneaking others into the levied groups. For example, in the winter between 1603 and 1604, 105 boys were levied from the island and Lesvos was the only Island that the levy was implemented on the levy of this period.[^cite_note-21]&#x20;

Lesbos prospered from trade, and Mytilene was considered the busiest Ottoman port in the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea). West European representatives are attested in the city already in 1700, acting as vice-consuls for the consulates in Smyrna. The island exported olives and olive oil, wheat, grapes, raisins and wine, figs, fish, dairy products, acorns, soap, leather and hides, pitch and livestock.[^cite_note-EI2-20] Mytilene itself increased five-fold in population during the Ottoman period. A number of new mosques were erected in the city, and Hayreddin Barbarossa built a [madrasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa), [dervish lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dervish_lodge\&action=edit\&redlink=1), and [imaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaret) erected in his hometown.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] Many of the early Ottoman buildings, as well as the city walls, were destroyed in the earthquake of 1867.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] [Mevlevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mevlevi) and [Bektashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bektashi) lodges are attested, since 1544 for the former, and since 1699 for the latter.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] Molyvos, which was the island's second city for most of the Ottoman period, also experienced growth, doubling in size; unlike Mytilene, the Muslim element came to predominate, and comprised over half the population by 1874. Mosques were built and fortifications were undertaken during the long [Cretan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_War_\(1645%E2%80%931669\)) with Venice. But during the 19th century, the town declined rapidly in importance and number of inhabitants, a decline which continued to modern times.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] In the mid-18th century, the castle and settlement of [Sigri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigri_\(village\)) were established to protect the western coast from pirate attacks.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16]&#x20;



European warships off [Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilene) during the 1905 incident.

The relative prosperity of the island—wealth was apparently concentrated among the Greek Christian bourgeoisie rather than the Muslim community[^cite_note-EI2-20]—contributed to the island not taking part in the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence) in 1821–1829.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] During the second half of the 19th century, this prosperity became evident in the construction of large and ornamented mansions and churches; the Muslims followed suit, employing the fashionable [Neo-Classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) and [Neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Gothic) styles in their own renovations of their mosques, especially after the destructive 1867 earthquake.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] The Ottoman writer and liberal politician [Namık Kemal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam%C4%B1k_Kemal) served in the local administration in 1877–1884.[^cite_note-TDVIA-16] In 1905, four European powers seized the customs and telegraph offices in the island to pressure the Ottoman government to accept their plan for an international commission that would supervise the provinces of [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(region\)).[^cite_note-EI2-20]&#x20;

### Modern era

See also: [Battle of Lesbos (1912)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lesbos_\(1912\)) and [History of modern Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_modern_Greece)

In 1912, the [First Balkan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Balkan_War) broke out between the kingdoms of [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria), [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia) and [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Montenegro), against the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) over the independence and expansion of Christian Balkan states. Under Rear Admiral [Pavlos Kountouriotis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlos_Kountouriotis), Greek naval forces landed at Lesbos on 21 November 1912, commencing the [Battle of Lesbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lesbos_\(1912\)). Kountouriotis sent an ultimatum to secure Mytilene under Greece, which Ottoman officials agreed to, before fleeing the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEΕπίτομη_Ιστορία121-22] The operation to annex the rest of the island was placed under Colonel Apollodoros Syrmakezis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEΕπίτομη_Ιστορία122–123-23] Syrmakezis led 3,175 troops towards an Ottoman camp in [Filia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Filia,_Lesbos\&action=edit\&redlink=1), reaching the outskirts of the city on 19 December, with an attack planned for the following morning. However, Ottoman military commanders approached Syrmakezis with a request for an armistice and Ottoman surrender was finalised on 21 December 1912, a month after the commencement of the battle.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEΕπίτομη_Ιστορία123–124-24] Nine Greek troops were killed and 81 were injured during the battle.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEΕπίτομη_Ιστορία125-25] The following year, the Ottoman Empire denied their previous agreement to cede Lesbos to Greece, until the [Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_\(1913\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHall200071,_101–102-26][^cite_note-TDVIA-16]&#x20;

In the [Greco-Turkish population exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_population_exchange) that followed [World War I](4764461.md) and the [Greco-Turkish War of 1919–1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_of_1919%E2%80%931922), the local Muslims left the island and Lesbos returned to a fully Greek Christian population. In 1922, many [Greek refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_refugees) from [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor_Greeks) settled in Lesbos as a result of the war and the concurrent [Greek genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide). These refugees were mostly women and children as the men were either fighting or had died in battle. A statue of a mother cradling her children named the "Statue of the Asia Minor Mother" was donated by the refugees and erected in Mytilene.[^cite_note-27] Twenty years later, during [World War II](32927.md), [Nazi Germany](21212.md) conducted an invasion of Greece and [Yugoslavia](34244.md), with both being defeated in 1941 and subsequently divided between the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers). Lesbos was occupied by Germany until 10 September 1944, when Greece was liberated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETomasevich197552–53-28][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERichter1998616-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-Hist-31]&#x20;

The poet [Odysseus Elytis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus_Elytis), the descendant of an old family of Lesbos, received the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md) in 1979.[^cite_note-32]&#x20;

## Tourism

The Roman Aqueduct at Mória



[Castle of Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_of_Mytilene)

Lesbos is known to be one of the Greek island touristic hotspots, especially during its tourism season of April, May, June, and July.[^cite_note-Tourism-33] [Mytilene airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilene_International_Airport) management recorded 47,379 tourists visiting Lesbos in its 2015 tourism season. The [refugee crisis](#Migrants) has since slowed down tourism to the island, with a 67.89% decrease rate from June 2015 to June 2016. 6,841 Europeans on 47 flights arrived in Lesbos during its 2016 tourism season, compared to July the previous year, which saw 18,373 Europeans fly to the island on 130 flights.[^cite_note-Tourism-33] 94 cruise ships full of tourists arrived in Lesbos in 2011 and only one in 2018.[^cite_note-Woo-34] Of the refugee crisis' impact on tourism, Maria Dimitriou, a local shop owner from [Mithymna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithymna), said, "2015 was a very good year for tourism and then, suddenly they started to arrive. The refugees began arriving in mid-July, when the hotels were full of tourists. There were refugees everywhere, lying down with all their trash. And after this, tourism stopped."[^cite_note-Woo-34]&#x20;

In 2019, the head of the Lesbos chamber of commerce, Vangelis Mirsinias, told *[The Jakarta Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jakarta_Post)* that the island's administration is trying to "woo back the tourists" and they "want to remind people of how beautiful" Lesbos is."[^cite_note-Woo-34] He advocated for the [European Union](9317.md) to help in advertising and also said, "The economy is still paying the impact of the crisis. It will need time and money to change this image."[^cite_note-Woo-34] Lesbos is also a hotspot for [Dutch tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) and one Dutch tourist said that tourism had halted because people "did not feel like seeing all this misery" of the refugees.[^cite_note-Woo-34] One local told the publication that residents had become "fed up" and "people are angry towards the government and towards Europe: they told us not to worry, the camps won't last. But it's still there", whilst another business owner explained that he had lost a third of his business and "blames all the negative media attention" for the lack of tourists.[^cite_note-Woo-34] *The Jakarta Post* also reported that tourists have increased in numbers in recent years, with 63,000 arriving in 2018.[^cite_note-Woo-34] The [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) has also damaged the island's tourism industry.[^cite_note-Lockdowned-35]&#x20;

In April 2022, the Greek government announced a dedication of €2 million in restoring tourism in Lesbos and four other islands.[^cite_note-36] In October 2022, it was announced that Lesbos would return to the cruise ship industry.[^cite_note-Cruise-37] Konstantinos Moutzouris, the governor of the North Aegean Region, which Lesbos is under, explained that the region's administration will run a study "in order to develop cruise tourism on the island."[^cite_note-Cruise-37] The deputy governor of tourism, Nikolaos Nyktas, believed that the cruise industry "suits the island and its culture", while the head of development for the project, Ioannis Bras, said that the island could "offer a lot to the cruise market".[^cite_note-Cruise-37]&#x20;

In English and most other European languages, including [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), the term *[lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian)* is commonly used to refer to homosexual women. This use of the term derives from the poems of [Sappho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sappho), who was born in Lesbos and who wrote with powerful emotional content directed toward other women.[^cite_note-38] Due to this association, the town of [Eresos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eresos), her birthplace, is visited frequently by [LGBTQ tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_tourism).[^cite_note-39]&#x20;

As of May 2027, [Jet2 Airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet2.com) will be flying from London Gatwick Airport directly to Lesbos Aiport, with the new route to form part of Jet2's expansion of its Greek destinations.[^cite_note-40]&#x20;

## Land transport

There are two national roads in Lesbos: the [EO36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_National_Road_36), a cross-island road from [Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilene) to [Kalloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalloni); and the [EO73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_National_Road_73), an east coast road from [Thermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Paralia_Thermis,_Lesbos\&action=edit\&redlink=1) to [Kratigos](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agrilia_Kratigou,_Lesbos\&action=edit\&redlink=1) via Mytilene.[^cite_note-egnatia-map-lesbos-41] The island is also served by 20 of 28 numbered provincial roads (the other eight being in [Lemnos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemnos#Transport)), created in 1956 while Lesbos was a prefecture: [Eresos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eresos) is accessible by provincial roads 14 or 15, via the EO36 from Kalloni.[^cite_note-ep-list-1956-42]&#x20;

## Geography



Topography of Lesbos



Detailed map of Lesbos



Mount Olympus’ peak rises 967 metres over Lesbos



[Agiasos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agiasos) village

Lesbos lies in the far east of the Aegean sea, facing the Turkish coast ([Gulf of Edremit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Edremit)) from the north and east; at the narrowest point, the [Mytilini Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilini_Strait) is about 5.5 km (3.4 mi) wide. In late Palaeolithic/Mesolithic times it was joined to the Anatolian mainland before the end of the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period).[^cite_note-43] The shape of the island is roughly triangular, but it is deeply intruded by the gulfs of [Kalloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalloni#Bay_and_lagoon), with an entry on the southern coast, and of [Gera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gera,_Lesbos#Bay_of_Gera), in the southeast.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;

The island is forested and mountainous with two large peaks, Mount Lepetymnos at 968 m (3,176 ft) and Mount Olympus at 967 m (3,173 ft) (not to be confused with [Mount Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus) in Thessaly on the Greek mainland), dominating its northern and central sections.[^cite_note-45] The island's volcanic origin is manifested in several [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring) and the two gulfs. Lesbos is verdant, aptly named *Emerald Island*, with a greater variety of flora than expected for the island's size. Eleven million [olive trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_tree) cover 40% of the island, together with other fruit trees. Forests of Mediterranean [pines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), chestnut trees and some [oaks](39696.md) occupy 20%, and the remainder is [scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrubland), [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland) or urban. The island is also one of the best in the world for bird watching.[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

## Climate

The island has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa* in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)). The mean annual temperature is 18 °C (64 °F), and the mean annual rainfall is 750 mm (30 in). Its exceptional sunshine makes it one of the sunniest islands in the Aegean Sea. Snow and very low temperatures are rare.&#x20;

## Geology



Petrified forest of Lesbos

The entire territory of Lesbos is "Lesvos [Geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopark)", which is a member of the [European Geoparks Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Geoparks_Network) (since 2000) and [Global Geoparks Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Geoparks_Network) (since 2004) on account of its outstanding geological heritage, educational programs and projects, and promotion of [geotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geotourism).[^cite_note-49]&#x20;

This geopark was enlarged from former "Lesvos Petrified Forest Geopark". Lesbos contains one of the few known [petrified forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrified_wood), called the [Petrified forest of Lesbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrified_forest_of_Lesbos), and it has been declared a [Protected Natural Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Protected_Natural_Monument\&action=edit\&redlink=1). Fossilised plants have been found in many localities on the western parts of the island. The fossilised forest was formed during the Late [Oligocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligocene) to Lower–Middle [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene), as determined by the intense [volcanic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_activity) in the area. Neogene [volcanic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_rock) dominate the central and western part of the island, comprising [andesites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite), [dacites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacite) and [rhyolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyolite), [ignimbrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignimbrite), [pyroclastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_rock), [tuffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff), and [volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash). The products of the volcanic activity covered the [vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetation) of the area and the [fossilization](10958.md) process took place during favourable conditions. The fossilized plants are silicified remnants of a [sub-tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-tropical) forest that existed on the northwest part of the island 20–15 million years ago.&#x20;

## Economy



The building of the former Lesbos Prefecture, and now of the Lesbos Regional Unit



A bottle of *[Ouzo Plomari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouzo_Plomari)* of Lesbos

The economy of Lesbos is essentially [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), with [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) being the main source of [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income). Tourism in [Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytilene), encouraged by its international airport and the coastal towns of [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra,_Lesbos), [Plomari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plomari), [Molyvos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molyvos) and [Eresos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eresos), contributes substantially to the island's economy. [Fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) and the [manufacture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) of [soap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap) and [ouzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouzo), the Greek national [liqueur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liqueur), are the remaining sources of income.&#x20;

## Migrants

Further information: [Pagani Detention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagani_Detention_Center)

Due to its proximity to the Turkish mainland, Lesbos is one of the Greek islands most affected by the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md) that started in 2015. [Refugees of the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_Civil_War) came to the island in multiple vessels every day.[^cite_note-54] As of June 2018, 8,000 refugees were trapped when a deal between Europe and Turkey removed their route to the continent in 2016. After that, living conditions deteriorated and the possibility of movement to Europe dimmed. [Moria Refugee Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moria_Refugee_Camp) was the largest of the refugee camps and held twice as many people as it was designed to accommodate.[^cite_note-55] By May 2020, Moria had 17,421 refugees living there.[^cite_note-Lockdowned-35]&#x20;

On 9 September 2020, thousands of migrants fled from the overcrowded Moria camp after a fire broke out. At least 25 firefighters, with 10 engines, were battling the flames both inside and outside the facility.[^cite_note-56] A smaller-scale facility, the [Pikpa camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikpa_camp) catered for a segment of the refugee population until its closure in October 2020, whereupon the occupants were transferred to the "old" [Kara Tepe Refugee Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Tepe_Refugee_Camp).[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

The Greek government maintains that the fires were started deliberately by migrants protesting that the camp had been put in lockdown due to a [COVID-19](63030231.md) outbreak amongst the migrants in the camp. On 16 September 2020, four Afghan men were formally charged with arson for allegedly starting the fire.[^cite_note-58] Two other migrants, both aged 17, which is below the age of full adult criminal responsibility in Greece, were also allegedly involved in starting the fire, and were held in police detention on the mainland.[^cite_note-59]&#x20;

After the closure of the Moria camp, a [temporary facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Kara_Tepe_Refugee_Camp) was rapidly set up at Kara Tepe.[^cite_note-60] The Greek government announced in November 2020 that a new closed reception centre will be built in the Vastria area near Nees Kydonies, on the border between Mytilene and Western Lesbos, and will be completed by late 2021.[^cite_note-61]&#x20;
