**Deputies** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *députés* and *députées*,[^cite_note-1] pronounced [\[depyte\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ec/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Eihel-d%C3%A9put%C3%A9s.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Eihel-d%C3%A9put%C3%A9s.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ec/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Eihel-d%C3%A9put%C3%A9s.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Eihel-d%C3%A9put%C3%A9s.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), also known in English as **members of Parliament** (MPs), are the [legislators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislator) who sit in the [National Assembly](299616.md), the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) of the [French Parliament](1009776.md).[^cite_note-2] The [17th and current legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_legislature_of_the_French_Fifth_Republic) of the [Fifth Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_French_Republic) has a total of [577 deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deputies_of_the_17th_National_Assembly_of_France), elected in single-member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_constituencies_of_the_National_Assembly_of_France) across [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) (539) and [overseas France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France) (27), as well as for [French residents overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies_for_French_residents_overseas) (11).

## Name

The term "deputy" is associated with the legislator's task to deputise for the people of his or her constituency.

## Current

Main article: [List of deputies of the 17th National Assembly of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deputies_of_the_17th_National_Assembly_of_France)

There are currently 577 legislative seats in the National Assembly. They are elected through the [two-round system](25932.md) in [single-member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district) [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_constituencies_of_the_National_Assembly_of_France).

## Numbers

Main article: [Number of parliamentarians in the Fifth French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_parliamentarians_in_the_Fifth_French_Republic)



Deputies in the National Assembly chamber in 2018

The number of deputies is codified in the [Constitution of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_France).

In 2019, it was reported that the [Government of France](3398091.md) wanted to cut the number of deputies by 25%. This reform was later abandoned due to a lack of support in the [Senate](567323.md).[^cite_note-3]

## Restrictions and privileges



Deputy [Ian Boucard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Boucard) wearing his official [sash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sash)

Deputies have [parliamentary immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_immunity_in_France). They enjoy total freedom of expression within the National Assembly chamber, although they can be sanctioned by a majority of deputies for not complying with the rules of procedure. The maximum penalty is a 15-day suspension from sitting in Parliament. As of 2023 and since 1958, the maximum penalty has been voted three times; the most recent was against [La France Insoumise](52520313.md) deputy [Thomas Portes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Portes), who pictured Labour Minister [Olivier Dussopt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Dussopt) decapitated on social media.[^cite_note-4] A deputy cannot be removed from office by other deputies.

Like senators and members of the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), deputies have to submit a declaration of interests and assets to the *[Haute Autorité pour la transparence de la vie publique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute_Autorit%C3%A9_pour_la_transparence_de_la_vie_publique)* (HATVP). Such declarations are then verified and made publicly accessible.[^cite_note-5] Like senators, deputies hold various privileges. They can inspect – without having to announce their visit prior to arrival – a number of sites managed on behalf the executive to verify compliance with laws voted by Parliament.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

It is common for deputies, wearing their distinctive [sash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sash), to place themselves at the front of demonstrations, with the aim of being recognisable to police forces and protecting individuals behind them. At a 2023 demonstration in [Sainte-Soline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Soline), [Deux-Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deux-S%C3%A8vres), deputies formed a line in front of police on site to allow medical teams to evacuate wounded participants who had clashed with police forces.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Wearing an official sash without the proper rights constitutes a punishable offence.

Deputies, like senators, can have a [dual mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_mandate) at the local level (most notably municipal, departmental, regional councillor) but a new law that entered in application in 2017 has limited the practice's extent by restricting national officials' ability to serve in local executives. Deputies are paid 5,782.66 euros per month.[^cite_note-10]

## Eligibility

See also: [Women in the French National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_French_National_Assembly)

Candidates can run for a seat in the National Assembly when they hold [French citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nationality_law), are at least 18 years old, as well as not have been declared incompetent in court or sentenced to a loss of civic rights.[^cite_note-11]
