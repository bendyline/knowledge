**Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.** (**AMD**) is an American multinational [semiconductor](612813.md) company headquartered in [Santa Clara, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara,_California). It develops [central processing units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AMD_processors) (CPUs), [graphics processing units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AMD_graphics_processing_units) (GPUs), [field-programmable gate arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field-programmable_gate_array) (FPGAs), [system-on-chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_on_a_chip) (SoCs), and [high-performance computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing) components. AMD serves a wide range of business and consumer markets, including [personal computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer) (PCs), gaming, [data centers](579730.md), and [embedded systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_system).

AMD's main products include [microprocessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AMD_microprocessors), [embedded processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_processor), and [graphics processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AMD_graphics_processing_units) for [servers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_\(computing\)), [workstations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workstation), PCs, and embedded system applications, as well as [chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipset) for [motherboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motherboard). The company has also expanded into new markets, such as data centers, gaming, and [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing).[^cite_note-2] AMD's processors are used in a wide range of computing devices, including PCs, servers, laptops, and gaming consoles. Initially manufacturing its own processors, the company [outsourced its manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabless_manufacturing) after [GlobalFoundries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlobalFoundries) was spun off in 2009. Through its [Xilinx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xilinx) acquisition in 2022, AMD offers [field-programmable gate array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field-programmable_gate_array) (FPGA) products.

AMD was founded in 1969 by [Jerry Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Sanders_\(businessman\)) and a group of his fellow [Fairchild Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Semiconductor) colleagues.[^cite_note-3] The company's early products were primarily memory chips and other computer components. In 1975, AMD entered the microprocessor market, competing with [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel), its main rival in the industry, by reverse-engineering Intel's [8080](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_8080). This led to the initial cross-license agreement allowing AMD to [build legal clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_source) of Intel processors, including the [x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86) family of processors from [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Personal_Computer).[^cite_note-\:1-4] The two companies fought a seven-year legal battle after Intel refused to share the blueprint of their [i386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I386) with AMD, ending in the latter's favor in 1994 but requiring AMD to develop their own x86 microprocessors in-house.[^cite_note-\:2-5] In the early 2000s, AMD experienced significant growth and success, partly due to its strong position in the PC market and its [Athlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon) and [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) processors. The company faced challenges in the late 2000s and early 2010s, as it struggled to compete with Intel.[^cite_note-6]

In the late 2010s, AMD regained market share by pursuing a [penetration pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetration_pricing) strategy[^cite_note-7] for its [Ryzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryzen) processors, offering a competitive performance and lower costs than Intel microprocessors.[^cite_note-8] In 2022, AMD surpassed Intel by market capitalization for the first time.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### Foundational years

Advanced Micro Devices was formally incorporated by [Jerry Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Sanders_\(businessman\)), along with seven of his colleagues from [Fairchild Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Semiconductor)—[Ed Turney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Turney); John Carey; Sven Simonsen; [Jack Gifford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Gifford_\(businessman\)), and three members of his team: Frank Botte, Jim Giles, and Larry Stenger, on May 1, 1969.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Sanders, an [electrical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineer) who was the director of marketing at Fairchild, had, like many of its executives, grown frustrated with the increasing lack of support, opportunity, and flexibility within the company. He later decided to leave to start his own [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) company,[^cite_note-13] following the footsteps of [Robert Noyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Noyce) (developer of the first silicon [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) at Fairchild in 1959)[^cite_note-14] and [Gordon Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Moore), who together founded the semiconductor company [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) in July 1968.[^cite_note-abc-15]

In September 1969, AMD moved from its temporary location in [Santa Clara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara,_California) to its new \$500,000 15,000sqft headquarters at 901 Thompson Place, [Sunnyvale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyvale,_California), which housed the entire company, including the 8-mask, 7 μm feature size, 2-inch diameter wafer production.[^cite_note-lojek220-16][^cite_note-17] To immediately secure a customer base, AMD initially became a [second source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_source) supplier of microchips designed by Fairchild (the 5 V [TTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor%E2%80%93transistor_logic) "9300" logic series) and [National Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Semiconductor).[^cite_note-directory-18][^cite_note-19] AMD first focused on producing logic chips.[^cite_note-20] The company guaranteed quality control to [United States Military Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-STD-883), an advantage in the early computer industry since unreliability in microchips was a distinct problem that customers – including [computer manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_manufacturer), the [telecommunications industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_industry), and instrument manufacturers – wanted to avoid.[^cite_note-directory-18][^cite_note-singer-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-svh-23]

In November 1969, the company manufactured its first product: the Am9300, a [4-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-bit_computing) [MSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit#SSI,_MSI_and_LSI) [shift register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift_register), which began selling in 1970.[^cite_note-svh-23][^cite_note-26] Also in 1970, AMD produced its first proprietary product, the Am2501 logic counter, which was highly successful.[^cite_note-lojek220-16][^cite_note-history-27] Its bestselling product in 1971 was the Am2505, the fastest [multiplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_multiplier) available.[^cite_note-lojek220-16][^cite_note-rodengen41-28]

In 1971, AMD entered the [RAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random-access_memory) chip market, beginning with the Am3101, a 64-bit bipolar RAM.[^cite_note-rodengen41-28][^cite_note-29] That year AMD also greatly increased the sales volume of its linear integrated circuits, and by year-end the company's total annual sales reached US\$4.6 million.[^cite_note-lojek220-16][^cite_note-30]

AMD went public in September 1972.[^cite_note-directory-18][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Chogath_Kog'sante-32] The company was a second source for Intel [MOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOSFET)/[LSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Scale_Integration) circuits by 1973, with products such as Am14/1506 and Am14/1507, dual 100-bit dynamic shift registers.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] By 1975, AMD was producing 212 products – of which 49 were proprietary, including the Am9102 (a [static](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_random-access_memory) [N-channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-type_semiconductor) 1024-bit RAM)[^cite_note-35] and three low-power [Schottky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schottky_barrier) [MSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-scale_integration) circuits: Am25LS07, Am25LS08, and Am25LS09.[^cite_note-rodengen55-36]

Intel had created the first [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor), its 4-bit [4004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_4004), in 1971.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] By 1975, AMD entered the microprocessor market with the [Am9080](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Am9080), a [reverse-engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) clone of the [Intel 8080](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_8080),[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-rodengen50-40][^cite_note-41] and the [Am2900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Am2900) [bit-slice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit-slice) microprocessor family.[^cite_note-rodengen50-40] When Intel began installing [microcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcode) in its microprocessors in 1976, it entered into a [cross-licensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-licensing) agreement with AMD, which was granted a copyright license to the microcode in its microprocessors and peripherals, effective October 1976.[^cite_note-rodengen55-36][^cite_note-\:3-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-justia1994-44][^cite_note-sec1996-45]

In 1977, AMD entered into a joint venture with [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), a German engineering conglomerate wishing to enhance its technology expertise and enter the American market.[^cite_note-malerba-46] Siemens purchased 20% of AMD's stock, giving the company an infusion of cash to increase its product lines.[^cite_note-malerba-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The two companies also jointly established Advanced Micro Computers (AMC), located in Silicon Valley and in Germany, allowing AMD to enter the [microcomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcomputer) development and manufacturing field,[^cite_note-malerba-46][^cite_note-rodengen60-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] in particular based on AMD's second-source [Zilog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilog) [Z8000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z8000) microprocessors.[^cite_note-freiberger-52][^cite_note-53] When the two companies' vision for Advanced Micro Computers diverged, AMD bought out Siemens' stake in the American division in 1979.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] AMD closed Advanced Micro Computers in late 1981 after switching focus to manufacturing second-source Intel x86 microprocessors.[^cite_note-freiberger-52][^cite_note-swaine-56][^cite_note-57]

Total sales in [fiscal year](5752099.md) 1978 topped \$100 million,[^cite_note-rodengen60-49] and in 1979, AMD debuted on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md).[^cite_note-history-27] In 1979, production also began on AMD's new [semiconductor fabrication plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_fabrication_plant) in [Austin, Texas](1998.md);[^cite_note-history-27] the company already had overseas assembly facilities in [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang) and [Manila](184334.md),[^cite_note-58] and began construction on a fabrication plant in [San Antonio](53848.md) in 1981.[^cite_note-HittC26-59] In 1980, AMD began supplying semiconductor products for telecommunications, an industry undergoing rapid expansion and innovation.[^cite_note-60]

### Intel partnership

[Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) had introduced the first [x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86) microprocessors in 1978.[^cite_note-61] In 1981, [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) created its [personal computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Personal_Computer), and wanted Intel's x86 processors, but only under the condition that Intel would also provide a [second-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-source) manufacturer for its patented x86 microprocessors.[^cite_note-singer-21] Intel and AMD entered into a 10-year technology exchange agreement, first signed in October 1981[^cite_note-swaine-56][^cite_note-\:1-4] and formally executed in February 1982.[^cite_note-sec1996-45] The terms of the agreement were that each company could acquire the right to become a second-source manufacturer of semiconductor products developed by the other; that is, each party could "earn" the right to manufacture and sell a product developed by the other, if agreed to, by exchanging the manufacturing rights to a product of equivalent technical complexity. The technical information and licenses needed to make and sell a part would be exchanged for a royalty to the developing company.[^cite_note-justia1994-44] The 1982 agreement also extended the 1976 AMD–Intel cross-licensing agreement through 1995.[^cite_note-justia1994-44][^cite_note-sec1996-45] The agreement included the right to invoke arbitration of disagreements, and after five years the right of either party to end the agreement with one year's notice.[^cite_note-justia1994-44] The main result of the 1982 agreement was that AMD became a second-source manufacturer of Intel's x86 microprocessors and related chips, and Intel provided AMD with database tapes for its [8086](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8086), [80186](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80186), and [80286](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80286) chips.[^cite_note-sec1996-45] However, in the event of a bankruptcy or takeover of AMD, the cross-licensing agreement would be effectively canceled.[^cite_note-62]

Beginning in 1982, AMD began volume-producing second-source Intel-licensed 8086, 8088, 80186, and 80188 processors, and by 1984, its own [Am286](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am286) clone of Intel's 80286 processor, for the rapidly growing market of IBM PCs and [IBM clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_clone).[^cite_note-singer-21][^cite_note-63] It also continued its successful concentration on proprietary [bipolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_junction_transistor) chips.[^cite_note-64]

The company continued to spend greatly on research and development,[^cite_note-65] and created the world's first 512K [EPROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPROM) in 1984.[^cite_note-66] That year, AMD was listed in the book *The 100 Best Companies to Work for in America*,[^cite_note-HittC26-59][^cite_note-67] and later made the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) list for the first time in 1985.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

By mid-1985, the microchip market experienced a severe downturn, mainly due to long-term aggressive trade practices ([dumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumping_\(pricing_policy\))) from Japan, but also due to a crowded and non-innovative chip market in the United States.[^cite_note-70] AMD rode out the mid-1980s crisis by aggressively innovating and modernizing,[^cite_note-71] devising the Liberty Chip program of designing and manufacturing one new chip or chipset per week for 52 weeks in [fiscal year](5752099.md) 1986,[^cite_note-HittC26-59][^cite_note-72] and by heavily lobbying the U.S. government until sanctions and restrictions were put in place to prevent predatory Japanese pricing.[^cite_note-73] During this time, AMD withdrew from the [DRAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DRAM) market,[^cite_note-74] and made some headway into the [CMOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMOS) market, which it had lagged in entering, having focused instead on bipolar chips.[^cite_note-75]

AMD had some success in the mid-1980s with the AMD7910 and AMD7911 "World Chip" [FSK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency-shift_keying) modem, one of the first multi-standard devices that covered both Bell and [CCITT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITU-T) tones at up to 1200 baud half duplex or 300/300 full duplex.[^cite_note-76] Beginning in 1986, AMD embraced the perceived shift toward [RISC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RISC) with its own [AMD Am29000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Am29000) (29k) processor;[^cite_note-77] the 29k survived as an [embedded processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_processor).[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] The company also increased its [EPROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPROM) memory market share in the late 1980s.[^cite_note-80] Throughout the 1980s, AMD was a second-source supplier of Intel x86 processors. In 1991, it introduced its 386-compatible [Am386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am386), an AMD-designed chip. Creating its own chips, AMD began to compete directly with Intel.[^cite_note-81]

In January 1996, AMD acquired [NexGen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NexGen) for \$857 million to develop what eventually became [AMD K6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K6). At the time, NexGen was working on its Nx686 microprocessor.[^cite_note-NexGen_Acq-82]

AMD had a large, successful [flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_memory) business, even during the [dotcom bust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotcom_bust).[^cite_note-83] In 2003, to divest some manufacturing and aid its overall cash flow, which was under duress from aggressive microprocessor competition from Intel, AMD spun off its flash memory business and manufacturing into [Spansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spansion), a joint venture with [Fujitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujitsu), which had been co-manufacturing flash memory with AMD since 1993.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] In December 2005, AMD divested itself of Spansion to focus on the microprocessor market, and Spansion went public in an IPO.[^cite_note-86]

### 2006–2019

On July 24, 2006, AMD announced its acquisition of the Canadian 3D graphics card company [ATI Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Technologies). AMD paid \$4.3 billion and 58 million shares of its [stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock), for approximately \$5.4 billion. The transaction was completed on October 25, 2006.[^cite_note-87] On August 30, 2010, AMD announced that it would retire the ATI brand name for its graphics chipsets in favor of the AMD brand name.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

In October 2008, AMD announced plans to spin off manufacturing operations in the form of [GlobalFoundries Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlobalFoundries), a multibillion-dollar joint venture with [Advanced Technology Investment Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Technology_Investment_Company), an investment company formed by the government of [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md). The partnership and spin-off gave AMD an infusion of cash and allowed it to focus solely on chip design.[^cite_note-90] To assure the Abu Dhabi investors of the new venture's success, AMD's CEO [Hector Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hector_Ruiz) stepped down in July 2008, while remaining executive chairman, in preparation for becoming chairman of GlobalFoundries in March 2009.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] President and COO [Dirk Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Meyer) became AMD's CEO.[^cite_note-93] Recessionary losses necessitated AMD cutting 1,100 jobs in 2009.[^cite_note-94]

In August 2011, AMD announced that former [Lenovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenovo) executive [Rory Read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Read) would be joining the company as CEO, replacing Meyer.[^cite_note-95] In November 2011, AMD announced plans to lay off more than 10% (1,400) of its employees from across all divisions worldwide.[^cite_note-96] In October 2012, it announced plans to lay off an additional 15% of its workforce to reduce costs in the face of declining sales revenue.[^cite_note-king2012-97] The inclusion of AMD chips into the [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4) and [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One) were later seen as saving AMD from bankruptcy.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

AMD acquired the low-power server manufacturer [SeaMicro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaMicro) in early 2012, with an eye to bringing out an [Arm64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm64) server chip.[^cite_note-vance2012-100]

On October 8, 2014, AMD announced that Rory Read had stepped down after three years as president and chief executive officer.[^cite_note-wsj.com-101] He was succeeded by [Lisa Su](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Su), a key lieutenant who had been chief operating officer since June.[^cite_note-102]

On October 16, 2014, AMD announced a new restructuring plan along with its Q3 results. Effective July 1, 2014, AMD reorganized into two business groups: Computing and Graphics, which primarily includes desktop and notebook processors and chipsets, discrete GPUs, and professional graphics; and Enterprise, Embedded, and Semi-Custom, which primarily includes server and embedded processors, dense servers, semi-custom SoC products (including [solutions for gaming consoles](#Partnerships)), engineering services, and royalties. As part of this restructuring, AMD announced that 7% of its global workforce would be laid off by the end of 2014.[^cite_note-103]

After the GlobalFoundries spin-off and subsequent layoffs, AMD was left with significant vacant space at 1 AMD Place, its aging Sunnyvale headquarters office complex. In August 2016, AMD's 47 years in Sunnyvale came to a close when it signed a lease with the [Irvine Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Company) for a new 220,000 sq. ft. headquarters building in Santa Clara.[^cite_note-DonatoWeinstein-104] AMD's new location at Santa Clara Square faces the headquarters of arch-rival Intel across the [Bayshore Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayshore_Freeway) and [San Tomas Aquino Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Tomas_Aquino_Creek). Around the same time, AMD also agreed to sell 1 AMD Place to the Irvine Company.[^cite_note-SVBJ-105] In April 2019, the Irvine Company secured approval from the Sunnyvale City Council of its plans to demolish 1 AMD Place and redevelop the entire 32-acre site into townhomes and apartments.[^cite_note-SVBJ-105]

### 2020–present

In October 2020, AMD announced that it was acquiring [Xilinx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xilinx), the market leader in [field-programmable gate arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field-programmable_gate_array) and [complex programmable logic devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_programmable_logic_device) (FPGAs and CPLDs) in an all-stock transaction. The acquisition was completed in February 2022, with an estimated acquisition price of \$50 billion.[^cite_note-\:4-106][^cite_note-\:5-107]

In January 2024, AMD announced it was discontinuing the production of all [complex programmable logic devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_programmable_logic_device) (CPLDs) acquired through Xilinx.[^cite_note-cpld_eol-108]

In March 2024, a rally in semiconductor stocks pushed AMD's valuation above \$300B for the first time.[^cite_note-109]

In July 2024, AMD announced that it would acquire the Finnish-based [artificial intelligence](1164.md) start-up company Silo AI in a \$665 million all-cash deal in an attempt to better compete with AI chip market leader [Nvidia](39120.md).[^cite_note-110]

In August 2024, AMD signed a deal to acquire ZT Systems for \$4.9 billion. The company creates custom computing infrastructure that is used for AI tasks.[^cite_note-111]

Since early 2024, AMD has made significant gains in the server CPU market, reducing the gap with Intel. Initial reports of a 50\:50 market split drew on incorrect data and were later revised. As of July 2025, Intel holds 63.3% of the server CPU market, while AMD has reached 36.5%, showing a strong upward trend for AMD despite Intel's continuing lead.[^cite_note-pcguide2025-112]

In June 2025, AMD unveiled the AI server for 2026, the MI400 series of chips, which will be the basis of a new server called "Helios".[^cite_note-113]

In October 2025, the company announced it had agreed a deal with [OpenAI](48795986.md) to sell six gigawatts of its AI processors over the next five years.[^cite_note-\:0-114] As part of the deal, OpenAI has the option to acquire a 10 per cent stake in AMD: 160 million shares at a token price of \$0.01 per share contingent on meeting undisclosed performance targets, the final tranche of options requiring AMD common stock to reach \$600 per share.[^cite_note-\:0-114]

#### IBM PC and the x86 architecture

Main articles: [Am286](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am286), [Am386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am386), [Am486](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am486), and [Am5x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am5x86)

In February 1982, AMD signed a contract with [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel), becoming a licensed second-source manufacturer of [8086](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_8086) and [8088](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_8088) processors. [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) wanted to use the Intel 8088 in its [IBM PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Personal_Computer), but its policy at the time was to require at least two sources for its chips (→ [Second source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_source)). AMD later produced the [Am286](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am286) under the same arrangement. In 1984, Intel internally decided to no longer cooperate with AMD in supplying product information to shore up its advantage in the marketplace, and delayed and eventually refused to convey the technical details of the [Intel 80386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_80386).[^cite_note-136] In 1987, AMD invoked arbitration over the issue, and Intel reacted by canceling the 1982 technological-exchange agreement altogether.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] After three years of testimony, AMD eventually won in arbitration in 1992, but Intel disputed this decision. Another long legal dispute followed, ending in 1994 when the [Supreme Court of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_California) sided with the arbitrator and AMD.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-justia1994-44]

In 1990, Intel countersued AMD, renegotiating AMD's right to use derivatives of Intel's [microcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcode) for its cloned processors.[^cite_note-140] In the face of uncertainty during the legal dispute, AMD was forced to develop [clean room designed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_room_design) versions of Intel code for its x386 and x486 processors, the former long after Intel had released its own x386 in 1985.[^cite_note-141] In March 1991, AMD released the [Am386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am386), its clone of the Intel 386 processor.[^cite_note-HittC26-59] By October of the same year it had sold one million units.[^cite_note-HittC26-59]

In 1993, AMD introduced the first of the [Am486](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am486) family of processors,[^cite_note-history-27] which proved popular with a large number of [original equipment manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_equipment_manufacturer), including [Compaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compaq), which signed an exclusive agreement using the Am486.[^cite_note-directory-18][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] The [Am5x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am5x86), another Am486-based processor, was released in November 1995, and continued AMD's success as a fast, cost-effective processor.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145]

Finally, in an agreement effective 1996, AMD received the rights to the microcode in Intel's x386 and x486 processor families, but not the rights to the microcode in the following generations of processors.[^cite_note-\:3-42][^cite_note-146]

#### K5, K6, Athlon, Duron, and Sempron

Main articles: [AMD K5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K5), [AMD K6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K6), [Athlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon), [Duron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duron), and [Sempron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sempron)

AMD's first in-house x86 processor was the [K5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K5), launched in 1996.[^cite_note-CPU-INFO_K5-147] The "K" in its name was a reference to [Kryptonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryptonite), the only substance known to harm comic book character [Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman). This itself was a reference to Intel's hegemony over the market, i.e., an anthropomorphization of them as Superman.[^cite_note-Forbes-Chip-Names-148] The number "5" was a reference to the fifth generation of x86 processors; rival Intel had previously introduced its line of fifth-generation x86 processors as [Pentium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentium) because the U.S. Trademark and Patent Office had ruled that mere numbers could not be trademarked.[^cite_note-newyorker-149]

In 1996, AMD purchased [NexGen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NexGen), specifically for the rights to its Nx series of x86-compatible processors. AMD gave the NexGen design team their own building, left them alone, and gave them time and money to rework the Nx686. The result was the [K6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K6) processor, introduced in 1997. Although it was based on [Socket 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socket_7), variants such as [K6-III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K6-III)/450 were faster than Intel's [Pentium II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentium_II) (sixth-generation processor).

The K7 was AMD's seventh-generation x86 processor, making its debut under the brand name [Athlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon) on June 23, 1999. Unlike previous AMD processors, it could not be used on the same motherboards as Intel's, due to licensing issues surrounding Intel's [Slot 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slot_1) connector, and instead used a [Slot A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slot_A) connector, referenced to the [Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEC_Alpha) processor bus. The [Duron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duron) was a lower-cost and limited version of the Athlon (64 KB instead of 256 KB L2 cache) in a 462-pin [socketed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU_socket) [PGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pin_grid_array) (socket A) or soldered directly onto the motherboard. [Sempron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sempron) was released as a lower-cost Athlon XP, replacing Duron in the [socket A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socket_A) PGA era. It has since been migrated upward to all new sockets, up to [AM3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM3).

On October 9, 2001, the [Athlon XP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_XP) was released. On February 10, 2003, the Athlon XP with 512 KB L2 Cache was released.[^cite_note-The_AMD_Athlon_XP_Processor_with_512_KB_L2_Cache-150]

#### Athlon 64, Opteron, and Phenom

Main articles: [Athlon 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64), [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron), and [Phenom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Phenom)

The K8 was a major revision of the K7 architecture, with the most notable features being the addition of a [64-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64-bit_computing) extension to the x86 instruction set (called [x86-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64), AMD64, or x64), the incorporation of an on-chip memory controller, and the implementation of an extremely high-performance point-to-point interconnect called [HyperTransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HyperTransport), as part of the [Direct Connect Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Connect_Architecture). The technology was initially launched as the [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) server-oriented processor on April 22, 2003.[^cite_note-151] Shortly thereafter, it was incorporated into a product for desktop PCs, branded [Athlon 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64).[^cite_note-152]

On April 21, 2005, AMD released the first [dual-core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-core_processor) [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron), an x86-based server CPU.[^cite_note-153] A month later, it released the [Athlon 64 X2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64_X2), the first desktop-based [dual-core processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-core_\(computing\)) family.[^cite_note-154] In May 2007, AMD abandoned the string "64" in its dual-core desktop product branding, becoming Athlon X2, downplaying the significance of [64-bit computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64) in its processors. Further updates involved improvements to the microarchitecture, and a shift of the target market from mainstream desktop systems to value dual-core desktop systems. In 2008, AMD started to release dual-core Sempron processors exclusively in China, branded as the Sempron 2000 series, with lower HyperTransport speed and smaller L2 cache. AMD completed its dual-core product portfolio for each market segment.

In September 2007, AMD released the first server Opteron [K10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K10) processors,[^cite_note-155] followed in November by the [Phenom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Phenom) processor for desktop. K10 processors came in dual-core, [triple-core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-core),[^cite_note-156] and [quad-core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-core) versions, with all cores on a single die. AMD released a new platform codenamed "[Spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Spider)", which used the new Phenom processor, and an R770 GPU and a 790 GX/FX chipset from the [AMD 700 chipset series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_700_chipset_series).[^cite_note-hothardware-157] However, AMD built the Spider at [65nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65nm), which was uncompetitive with Intel's smaller and more power-efficient [45nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45nm).

In January 2009, AMD released a new processor line dubbed [Phenom II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenom_II), a refresh of the original [Phenom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Phenom) built using the 45 nm process.[^cite_note-anandtech3512-158] AMD's new platform, codenamed "[Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Dragon)", used the new Phenom II processor, and an [ATI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_\(brand\)) R770 GPU from the [R700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radeon_R700) GPU family, and a 790 GX/FX chipset from the [AMD 700 chipset series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_700_chipset_series).[^cite_note-159] The Phenom II came in dual-core, triple-core and quad-core variants, all using the same die, with cores disabled for the triple-core and dual-core versions. The Phenom II resolved issues that the original Phenom had, including a low clock speed, a small L3 cache, and a [Cool'n'Quiet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cool'n'Quiet) bug that decreased performance. The Phenom II cost less but was not performance-competitive with Intel's mid-to-high-range [Core 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_2) Quads. The Phenom II also enhanced its predecessor's memory controller, allowing it to use [DDR3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDR3) in a new native socket [AM3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM3), while maintaining backward compatibility with [AM2+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM2+), the socket used for the Phenom, and allowing the use of the [DDR2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDR2_SDRAM) memory that was used with the platform.

In April 2010, AMD released a new Phenom II Hexa-core (6-core) processor codenamed "[Thuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenom_X6)".[^cite_note-160] This was a totally new die based on the hexa-core "Istanbul" [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) processor. It included AMD's "turbo core" technology, which allows the processor to automatically switch from 6 cores to 3 faster cores when more pure speed is needed.

The [Magny Cours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AMD_Opteron_microprocessors#Opteron_6100-series_%22Magny-Cours%22_\(45_nm\)) and [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AMD_Opteron_microprocessors#Opteron_4100-series_%22Lisbon%22_\(45_nm\)) server parts were released in 2010.[^cite_note-Opteron6100Series-161] The Magny Cours part came in 8 to 12 cores and the Lisbon part in 4 and 6 core parts. Magny Cours is focused on performance while the Lisbon part is focused on high performance per watt. Magny Cours is an MCM ([multi-chip module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-chip_module)) with two hexa-core "Istanbul" [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) parts. This will use a new [socket G34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socket_G34) for dual and quad-socket processors and thus will be marketed as Opteron 61xx series processors. Lisbon uses [socket C32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socket_C32) certified for dual-socket use or single socket use only and thus will be marketed as Opteron 41xx processors. Both will be built on a [45 nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45_nm) [SOI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_on_insulator) process.

#### Fusion becomes the AMD APU

Main articles: [AMD APU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_APU) and [AMD mobile platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_mobile_platform)

Following AMD's 2006 acquisition of Canadian graphics company [ATI Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Technologies), an initiative codenamed *Fusion* was announced to integrate a [CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU) and [GPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPU) together on some of AMD's microprocessors, including a built in [PCI Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCI_Express) link to accommodate separate PCI Express peripherals, eliminating the [northbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northbridge_\(computing\)) chip from the motherboard. The initiative intended to move some of the processing originally done on the CPU (e.g. [floating-point unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating-point_unit) operations) to the GPU, which is better optimized for some calculations. The Fusion was later renamed the [AMD APU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_APU) (Accelerated Processing Unit).[^cite_note-APU-162]

Llano was AMD's first APU built for laptops. Llano was the second APU released,[^cite_note-Hruska-163] targeted at the mainstream market.[^cite_note-APU-162] It incorporated a CPU and GPU on the same die, and northbridge functions, and used "[Socket FM1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socket_FM1)" with [DDR3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDR3) memory. The CPU part of the processor was based on the [Phenom II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenom_II) "Deneb" processor. AMD suffered an unexpected decrease in revenue based on production problems for the Llano.[^cite_note-164] More AMD APUs for laptops running Windows 7 and Windows 8 OS are being used commonly. These include AMD's price-point APUs, the E1 and E2, and their mainstream competitors with Intel's [Core i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_i)-series: The Vision A- series, the A standing for accelerated. These range from the lower-performance A4 chipset to the A6, A8, and A10. These all incorporate next-generation Radeon graphics cards, with the A4 utilizing the base Radeon HD chip and the rest using a Radeon R4 graphics card, with the exception of the highest-model A10 (A10-7300) which uses an R6 graphics card.

#### New microarchitectures

##### High-power, high-performance Bulldozer cores

Main articles: [Bulldozer microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulldozer_\(microarchitecture\)), [Piledriver microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piledriver_\(microarchitecture\)), [Steamroller microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamroller_\(microarchitecture\)), and [Excavator microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excavator_\(microarchitecture\))

Bulldozer was AMD's microarchitecture codename for server and desktop [AMD FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_FX) processors, first released on October 12, 2011. This [family 15h microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_4) is the successor to the [family 10h (K10)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_10h) microarchitecture design. Bulldozer was a clean-sheet design, not a development of earlier processors.[^cite_note-165] The core was specifically aimed at 10–125 W [TDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_Design_Power) computing products. AMD claimed dramatic performance-per-watt efficiency improvements in [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing) (HPC) applications with Bulldozer cores. While hopes were high that Bulldozer would bring AMD to be performance-competitive with Intel once more, most benchmarks were disappointing. In some cases the new Bulldozer products were slower than the K10 models they were built to replace.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

The [Piledriver microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piledriver_\(microarchitecture\)) was the 2012 successor to Bulldozer, increasing clock speeds and performance relative to its predecessor.[^cite_note-169] Piledriver would be released in AMD FX, APU, and Opteron product lines.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] Piledriver was subsequently followed by the [Steamroller microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamroller_\(microarchitecture\)) in 2013. Used exclusively in AMD's APUs, Steamroller focused on greater parallelism.[^cite_note-anandtech-kaveri-review-174][^cite_note-175]

In 2015, the [Excavator microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excavator_\(microarchitecture\)) replaced Piledriver.[^cite_note-PCWorld-176] Expected to be the last microarchitecture of the Bulldozer series,[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] Excavator focused on improved power efficiency.[^cite_note-179]

##### Low-power Cat cores

Main articles: [Bobcat microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobcat_\(microarchitecture\)), [Jaguar microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_\(microarchitecture\)), and [Puma microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_\(microarchitecture\))

The [Bobcat microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobcat_\(microarchitecture\)) was revealed during a speech from Henri Richard, AMD executive vice-president, in [Computex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computex) 2007, and was put into production during the first quarter of 2011.[^cite_note-Hruska-163] Based on the difficulty competing in the x86 market with a single core optimized for the 10–100 W range, AMD had developed a simpler core with a target range of 1–10 watts.[^cite_note-180] In addition, it was believed that the core could migrate into the hand-held space if the power consumption can be reduced to less than 1 W.[^cite_note-181]

[Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_\(microarchitecture\)) is a microarchitecture codename for Bobcat's successor, released in 2013, that is used in various APUs from AMD aimed at the low-power/low-cost market.[^cite_note-182] Jaguar and its derivates would go on to be used in the custom APUs of the [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4),[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-AMD_elaborate-184] [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One),[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-JaguarAnandtech-186] [PlayStation 4 Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4_Pro),[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188][^cite_note-Anandtech-189] [Xbox One S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One_S),[^cite_note-190] and [Xbox One X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One_X).[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-arstechnica.com-192] Jaguar would be later followed by the [Puma microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_\(microarchitecture\)) in 2014.[^cite_note-193]

##### ARM architecture-based designs

In 2012, AMD announced it was working on [ARM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture_family) products, both as a semi-custom product and server product.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] The initial server product was announced as the [Opteron A1100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron_A1100) in 2014, an 8-core [Cortex-A57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortex-A57)-based [ARMv8-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARMv8-A) [SoC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_on_a_Chip),[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] and was expected to be followed by an APU incorporating a [Graphics Core Next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_Core_Next) GPU.[^cite_note-199] However, the Opteron A1100 was not released until 2016, with the delay attributed to adding software support.[^cite_note-a1100_release-200] The A1100 was also criticized for not having support from major vendors upon its release.[^cite_note-a1100_release-200][^cite_note-\:6-201][^cite_note-202]

In 2014, AMD also announced the [K12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K12) custom core for release in 2016.[^cite_note-203] While being [ARMv8-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARMv8-A) [instruction set architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instruction_set_architecture) compliant, the K12 was expected to be entirely custom-designed, targeting the server, embedded, and semi-custom markets. While ARM architecture development continued, products based on K12 were subsequently delayed with no release planned. Development of AMD's x86-based Zen microarchitecture was preferred.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]

#### Zen-based CPUs and APUs

Main article: [Zen microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_\(microarchitecture\))

[Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_\(microarchitecture\)) is an architecture for x86-64 based [Ryzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryzen) series of CPUs and APUs, introduced in 2017 by AMD and built from the ground up by a team led by [Jim Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Keller_\(engineer\)), beginning with his arrival in 2012, and [taping out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tape-out) before his departure in September 2015.

One of AMD's primary goals with Zen was an [IPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructions_per_cycle) increase of at least 40%, however in February 2017 AMD announced that it had actually achieved a 52% increase.[^cite_note-206] Processors made on the Zen architecture are built on the 14 nm FinFET node and have a renewed focus on single-core performance and [HSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneous_System_Architecture) compatibility.[^cite_note-207] Previous processors from AMD were either built in the 32 nm process ("Bulldozer" and "Piledriver" CPUs) or the 28 nm process ("Steamroller" and "Excavator" APUs). Because of this, Zen is much more energy efficient.

The Zen architecture is the first to encompass CPUs and APUs from AMD built for a single socket (Socket AM4). Also new for this architecture is the implementation of [simultaneous multithreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_multithreading) (SMT) technology, something Intel has had for years on some of its processors with its proprietary [hyper-threading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyper-threading) implementation of SMT. This is a departure from the "[Clustered MultiThreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clustered_multi-thread)" design introduced with the Bulldozer architecture. Zen also has support for [DDR4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDR4_SDRAM) memory.

AMD released the Zen-based high-end Ryzen 7 "Summit Ridge" series CPUs on March 2, 2017,[^cite_note-208] mid-range Ryzen 5 series CPUs on April 11, 2017, and entry level Ryzen 3 series CPUs on July 27, 2017.[^cite_note-zen_ars-209] AMD later released the [Epyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epyc) line of Zen derived server processors for 1P and 2P systems.[^cite_note-210] In October 2017, AMD released Zen-based APUs as Ryzen Mobile, incorporating [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_Core_Next#GCN_5th_Generation_\(Vega\)) graphics cores.[^cite_note-RM_AT-211] In January 2018, AMD announced its new lineup plans, with Ryzen 2.[^cite_note-212] AMD launched CPUs with the [12nm Zen+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen+)[^cite_note-213] microarchitecture in April 2018, following up with the [7nm Zen 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_2) microarchitecture in June 2019, including an update to the Epyc line with new processors using the Zen 2 microarchitecture in August 2019, and Zen 3 slated for release in Q3 2020.

As of 2019, AMD's Ryzen processors were reported to outsell Intel's consumer desktop processors.[^cite_note-214] At CES 2020 AMD announced its Ryzen Mobile 4000, as the first 7 nm x86 mobile processor, the first 7 nm 8-core (also 16-thread) high-performance mobile processor, and the first 8-core (also 16-thread) processor for ultrathin laptops.[^cite_note-215] This generation is still based on the Zen 2 architecture. In October 2020, AMD announced new processors based on the Zen 3 architecture.[^cite_note-216] On PassMark's Single thread performance test the Ryzen 5 5600x bested all other CPUs besides the Ryzen 9 5950X.[^cite_note-217]

In April 2020, AMD launched three new SKUs which target commercial HPC workloads & hyperconverged infrastructure applications. The launch was based on [Epyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epyc)'s 7 nm second-generation Rome platform and supported by [Dell EMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell_EMC), [Hewlett Packard Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett_Packard_Enterprise), [Lenovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenovo), [Supermicro](55117694.md), and [Nutanix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutanix). [IBM Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Cloud) was its first public cloud partner.[^cite_note-218] In August 2022, AMD announced its initial lineup of CPUs based on the new [Zen 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_4) architecture.[^cite_note-219]

The [Steam Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_Deck),[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5), [Xbox Series X and Series S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S) all use chips based on the Zen 2 microarchitecture, with proprietary tweaks and different configurations in each system's implementation than AMD sells in its own commercially available APUs.[^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223]

#### ATI prior to AMD acquisition

Main article: [ATI Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Technologies)

Lee Ka Lau,[^cite_note-224] Francis Lau, Benny Lau, and Kwok Yuen Ho[^cite_note-225] founded ATI in 1985 as Array Technology Incorporated, renamed later Array Technologies Incorporated and finally ATI Technologies.[^cite_note-226] Working primarily in the [OEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_equipment_manufacturer) field, ATI produced integrated graphics cards for PC manufacturers such as [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) and [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_International). By 1987, ATI had grown into an independent graphics-card retailer, introducing [EGA Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EGA_Wonder) and [VGA Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VGA_Wonder) card product lines that year.[^cite_note-227] In the early nineties, they released products able to process graphics without the CPU: in May 1991, the Mach8, in 1992 the Mach32, which offered improved memory bandwidth and [GUI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_User_Interface) acceleration. ATI Technologies went public in 1993, with shares listed on [NASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ) and on the [Toronto Stock Exchange](205584.md).



ATI's former Silicon Valley office at 4555 Great America Pkwy, Santa Clara, CA



ATI "Graphics Solution Rev 3" from 1985/1986, supporting [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules_Graphics_Card) graphics. As the [PCB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printed_circuit_board) reveals, the layout dates from 1985, whereas the marking on the central chip *CW16800-A* says "8639"—meaning that chip was manufactured in week 39, 1986. Notice [UM6845E CRT controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_6845). This card uses the [ISA 8-bit interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_Standard_Architecture).



ATI VGA Wonder with 256 KB RAM

In 1994, the Mach64 accelerator debuted, powering the Graphics Xpression and Graphics Pro Turbo, offering hardware support for [YUV-to-RGB colour space conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YUV%E2%80%93RGB_conversion_formulas) in addition to hardware zoom; early techniques of hardware-based [video acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_acceleration).

ATI introduced its first combination of [2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2D_accelerator) and [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_accelerator) accelerator under the name [3D Rage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Rage). This chip was based on the Mach 64, but it featured elemental 3D acceleration. The ATI Rage line powered almost the entire range of ATI graphics products. In particular, the [Rage Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Rage_Pro) was one of the first viable 2D-plus-3D alternatives to [3dfx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3dfx)'s 3D-only [Voodoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voodoo_Graphics) chipset. 3D acceleration in the Rage line advanced from the basic functionality within the initial 3D Rage to a more advanced [DirectX 6.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirectX_6.0) accelerator in 1999 [Rage 128](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Rage_128).

The [All-in-Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-in-Wonder) product line, introduced in 1996, was the first combination of integrated graphics chip with [TV tuner card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_tuner_card) and the first chip that enabled display of computer graphics on a [TV set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_set).[^cite_note-228] The cards featured 3D acceleration powered by ATI's [3D Rage II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Rage_II), [64-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64-bit) 2D performance, [TV](29831.md)-quality video acceleration, analogue [video capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_capture), TV tuner functionality, [flicker-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flicker-free) [TV-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV-out) and [stereo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereo) TV audio reception.

ATI entered the [mobile computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_computing) sector by introducing 3D-graphics acceleration to laptops in 1996. The [Mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_Radeon) product line had to meet requirements different from those of desktop PCs, such as minimized power usage, reduced heat output, [TMDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition-Minimized_Differential_Signaling) output capabilities for [laptop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptop) [screens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_monitor), and maximized integration. In 1997, ATI acquired [Tseng Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tseng_Labs)'s graphics assets, which included 40 engineers.

The [Radeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radeon) line of graphics products was unveiled in 2000. The [initial Radeon GPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radeon_R100_series) offered an all-new design with [DirectX 7.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirectX_7.0) 3D acceleration, video acceleration, and 2D acceleration. Technology developed for a specific Radeon generation could be built in varying levels of features and performance in order to provide products suited for the entire market range, from high-end to budget to mobile versions.

In 2000, ATI acquired [ArtX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArtX), which engineered the [Flipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flipper_\(graphics_chip\)) graphics chip used in the [GameCube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameCube) [video game console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console). They also created a modified version of the chip (codenamed [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_\(graphics_chip\))) for the successor of the GameCube, the [Wii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Wii). [Microsoft](19001.md) contracted ATI to design the graphics core (codenamed [Xenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenos_\(graphics_chip\))) for the [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360). Later in 2005, ATI acquired [Terayon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terayon)'s [cable modem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_modem) [silicon intellectual property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_intellectual_property), strengthening their lead in the consumer [digital television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_television) market.[^cite_note-229] K. Y. Ho remained as [chairman of the board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_board) until he retired in November 2005. [Dave Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Orton) replaced him as the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(corporate_title\)) and [CEO](52234.md) of the organization.

On July 24, 2006, a joint announcement revealed that [AMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Micro_Devices) would [acquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_acquisition) ATI in a deal valued at \$5.6 billion.[^cite_note-230] The acquisition consideration closed on October 25, 2006,[^cite_note-231] and included over \$2 billion financed from a loan and 56 million shares of AMD [stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_capital).[^cite_note-232] ATI's operations became part of the AMD Graphics Product Group (GPG),[^cite_note-233] and ATI's CEO Dave Orton became the [executive vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_vice_president) of Visual and Media Businesses at AMD until his resignation in 2007.[^cite_note-234] The top-level management was reorganized with the [senior vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_vice_president) and [general manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_manager), and the senior vice president and general manager of Consumer Electronics Group, both of whom would report to the CEO of AMD.[^cite_note-235] On August 30, 2010, John Trikola announced that AMD would retire the ATI brand for its graphics chipsets in favour of the AMD name.[^cite_note-236]

#### Radeon within AMD

Main article: [Radeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radeon)

In 2007, the ATI division of AMD released the [TeraScale microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeraScale_\(microarchitecture\)) implementing a [unified shader model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_shader_model). This design replaced the previous fixed-function hardware of previous graphics cards with multipurpose, programmable shaders. Initially released as part of the GPU for the [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360), this technology would go on to be used in Radeon branded HD 2000 parts. Three generations of TeraScale would be designed and used in parts from 2007 to 2015.

#### Combined GPU and CPU divisions

In a 2009 restructuring, AMD merged the CPU and GPU divisions to support the company's APUs, which fused both graphics and general purpose processing.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] In 2011, AMD released the successor to TeraScale, [Graphics Core Next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_Core_Next) (GCN).[^cite_note-239] This new microarchitecture emphasized [GPGPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPGPU) compute capability in addition to graphics processing, with a particular aim of supporting heterogeneous computing on AMD's APUs. GCN's [reduced instruction set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_instruction_set_computer) [ISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instruction_set_architecture) allowed for significantly increased compute capability over TeraScale's [very long instruction word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_long_instruction_word) ISA. Since GCN's introduction with the [HD 7970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radeon_HD_7000_series), five generations of the GCN architecture have been produced from 2011 through at least 2018.[^cite_note-240]

#### Radeon Technologies Group

In September 2015, AMD separated the graphics technology division of the company into an independent internal unit called the Radeon Technologies Group (RTG), headed by [Raja Koduri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Koduri).[^cite_note-241] This gave the graphics division of AMD autonomy in product design and marketing.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243] The RTG then went on to create and release the [Polaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Polaris) and [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_Core_Next#Vega) microarchitectures released in 2016 and 2017, respectively.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-\:7-245] In particular the Vega, or fifth-generation [GCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_Core_Next), microarchitecture includes a number of major revisions to improve performance and compute capabilities.[^cite_note-\:7-245][^cite_note-246]

In November 2017, Raja Koduri left RTG[^cite_note-Hexus_Net-247] and CEO and President Lisa Su took his position. In January 2018, it was reported that two industry veterans joined RTG, namely Mike Rayfield as senior vice president and general manager of RTG, and David Wang as senior vice president of engineering for RTG.[^cite_note-hexus.net-248] In January 2020, AMD announced that its second-generation [RDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDNA_\(microarchitecture\)) graphics architecture was in development, with the aim of competing with the [Nvidia](39120.md) [RTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_RTX) graphics products for performance leadership. In October 2020, AMD announced its new [RX 6000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radeon_RX_6000_series) series[^cite_note-249] series GPUs, their first high-end product based on RDNA2 and capable of handling ray-tracing natively, aiming to challenge Nvidia's RTX 3000 GPUs.

### Semi-custom and game console products

In 2012, AMD's then CEO [Rory Read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Read) began a program to offer semi-custom designs.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] Rather than AMD simply designing and offering a single product, potential customers could work with AMD to design a custom chip based on AMD's intellectual property. Customers pay a non-recurring engineering fee for design and development, and a purchase price for the resulting semi-custom products. In particular, AMD noted its unique position of offering both x86 and graphics intellectual property. These semi-custom designs would have design wins as the APUs in the [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4) and [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One) and the subsequent PlayStation 4 Pro, Xbox One S, Xbox One X, [Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S), and [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5).[^cite_note-256] Financially, these semi-custom products would represent a majority of the company's revenue in 2016.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] In November 2017, AMD and Intel announced that Intel would market a product combining in a single package an [Intel Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Core) CPU, a semi-custom AMD Radeon GPU, and [HBM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBM2) memory.[^cite_note-259]

### Other hardware

#### AMD motherboard chipsets

See also: [Comparison of AMD chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_AMD_chipsets)

Before the launch of [Athlon 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64) processors in 2003, AMD designed [chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipset) for its processors spanning the [K6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K6) and [K7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_K7) processor generations. The chipsets include the AMD-640, AMD-751, and the AMD-761 chipsets. The situation changed in 2003 with the release of Athlon 64 processors, and AMD chose not to further design its own chipsets for its desktop processors while opening the desktop platform to allow other firms to design chipsets. This was the "[Open Platform Management Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Platform_Management_Architecture)" with [ATI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATI_Technologies), [VIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIA_Technologies) and [SiS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Integrated_Systems) developing its own chipset for [Athlon 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64) processors and later [Athlon 64 X2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64_X2) and [Athlon 64 FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlon_64_FX) processors, including the [Quad FX platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Quad_FX_platform) chipset from Nvidia.

The initiative went further with the release of [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) server processors as AMD stopped the design of server chipsets in 2004 after releasing the AMD-8111 chipset, and again opened the server platform for firms to develop chipsets for Opteron processors. As of today, Nvidia and [Broadcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcom) are the sole designing firms of server chipsets for Opteron processors.

As the company completed the acquisition of ATI Technologies in 2006, the firm gained the ATI design team for chipsets which previously designed the [Radeon Xpress 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xpress_200) and the [Radeon Xpress 3200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xpress_3200) chipsets. AMD then renamed the chipsets for AMD processors under AMD branding (for instance, the CrossFire Xpress 3200 chipset was renamed as [AMD 580X CrossFire chipset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_580_chipset_series)). In February 2007, AMD announced the first AMD-branded chipset since 2004 with the release of the AMD [690G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_690_chipset_series) chipset (previously under the development codename *RS690*), targeted at mainstream [IGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Graphics_Processor) computing. It was the industry's first to implement a [HDMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDMI) 1.2 port on motherboards, shipping for more than a million units. While ATI had aimed at releasing an Intel IGP chipset, the plan was scrapped and the inventories of [Radeon Xpress 1250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_690_chipset_series#Radeon_Xpress_1250) (codenamed *RS600*, sold under ATI brand) was sold to two OEMs, Abit and ASRock. Although AMD stated the firm would still produce Intel chipsets, Intel had not granted the license of 1333 MHz FSB to ATI.

On November 15, 2007, AMD announced a new chipset series portfolio, the [AMD 7-Series chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_700_chipset_series), covering from the enthusiast multi-graphics segment to the value IGP segment, to replace the [AMD 480/570/580 chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_580_chipset_series) and [AMD 690 series chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_690_chipset_series), marking AMD's first enthusiast multi-graphics chipset. Discrete graphics chipsets were launched on November 15, 2007, as part of the codenamed *Spider* desktop platform, and IGP chipsets were launched at a later time in spring 2008 as part of the codenamed *Cartwheel* platform.

AMD returned to the server chipsets market with the [AMD 800S series server chipsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_800_chipset_series). It includes support for up to six [SATA 6.0 Gbit/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_ATA#SATA_Revision_3.0_\(SATA_6Gb/s\)) ports, the C6 power state, which is featured in [Fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Fusion) processors and [AHCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Host_Controller_Interface) 1.2 with SATA [FIS-based switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_multiplier#FIS\(Frame_Information_Structure\)%E2%80%93based_switching) support. This is a chipset family supporting [Phenom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Phenom) processors and [Quad FX enthusiast platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Quad_FX_platform) (890FX), [IGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Graphics_Processor) (890GX).

With the advent of AMD's APUs in 2011, traditional northbridge features such as the connection to graphics and the PCI Express controller were incorporated into the APU die. Accordingly, APUs were connected to a single chip chipset, renamed the Fusion Controller Hub (FCH), which primarily provided southbridge functionality.[^cite_note-260]

AMD released new chipsets in 2017 to support the release of its new [Ryzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryzen) products. As the [Zen microarchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_\(microarchitecture\)) already includes much of the northbridge connectivity, the AM4-based chipsets primarily varied in the number of additional PCI Express lanes, USB connections, and SATA connections available.[^cite_note-chipset_toms-261] These AM4 chipsets were designed in conjunction with [ASMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASMedia).[^cite_note-su_interview-262]

#### Embedded products

##### Embedded CPUs

Main articles: [AMD Élan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_%C3%89lan), [Alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy_\(microarchitecture\)), and [Geode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geode_\(processor\))



An AMD Élan SC450 in [Nokia 9110 Communicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_9110_Communicator)

In the early 1990s, AMD began marketing a series of [embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_system) [system-on-a-chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System-on-a-chip) (SoCs) called [AMD Élan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_%C3%89lan), starting with the SC300 and SC310. Both combines a 32-Bit, [Am386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am386)SX, low-voltage 25 MHz or 33 MHz CPU with [memory controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_controller), [PC/AT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC/AT) peripheral controllers, [real-time clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_clock), [PLL clock generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-locked_loop#Clock_generation) and [ISA bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISA_bus) interface. The SC300 integrates in addition two [PC card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_card) slots and a [CGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_Graphics_Adapter)-compatible [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCD) controller. They were followed in 1996 by the SC4xx types, now supporting [VESA Local Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VESA_Local_Bus) and using the [Am486](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am486) with up to 100 MHz [clock speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_speed). An SC450 at 33 MHz, for example, was used in the [Nokia 9110 Communicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_9110_Communicator). In 1999 the SC520 was announced. Using an [Am586](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am586) with 100 MHz or 133 MHz and supporting [SDRAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SDRAM) and [PCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_Component_Interconnect) it was the latest member of the series.[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264]

In February 2002, AMD acquired Alchemy Semiconductor for its [Alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy_\(processor\)) line of [MIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIPS_architecture) processors for the hand-held and [portable media player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_media_player) markets.[^cite_note-265] On June 13, 2006, AMD officially announced that the line was to be transferred to Raza Microelectronics, Inc., a designer of MIPS processors for embedded applications.[^cite_note-Alchemy-266]

In August 2003, AMD also purchased the [Geode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geode_\(processor\)) business which was originally the [Cyrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrix) [MediaGX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaGX) from [National Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Semiconductor) to augment its existing line of embedded x86 processor products.[^cite_note-267] During the second quarter of 2004, it launched new low-power Geode NX processors based on the K7 Thoroughbred architecture with speeds of fanless processors 667 MHz and 1 GHz, and 1.4 GHz processor with fan, of [TDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_Design_Power) 25 W. This technology is used in a variety of embedded systems (Casino slot machines and customer kiosks for instance), several [UMPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Mobile_PC) designs in Asia markets, and the [OLPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Laptop_per_Child) [XO-1 computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OLPC_XO-1), an inexpensive laptop computer intended to be distributed to children in developing countries around the world.[^cite_note-268] The Geode LX processor was announced in 2005 and is said will continue to be available through 2015.

AMD has also introduced [64-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64) processors into its embedded product line, starting with the AMD [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) processor. Leveraging the high throughput enabled through [HyperTransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HyperTransport) and the [Direct Connect Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Connect_Architecture) these server-class processors have been targeted at high-end telecom and storage applications. In 2007, AMD added the AMD Athlon, [AMD Turion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Turion), and Mobile AMD Sempron processors to its embedded product line. Leveraging the same [64-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64) instruction set and [Direct Connect Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Connect_Architecture) as the AMD [Opteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opteron) but at lower power levels, these processors were well suited to a variety of traditional embedded applications. Throughout 2007 and into 2008, AMD continued to add both single-core Mobile AMD Sempron and AMD Athlon processors and [dual-core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-core_\(computing\)) AMD Athlon X2 and AMD Turion processors to its embedded product line and now offers embedded [64-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64) solutions starting with 8 W TDP Mobile AMD Sempron and AMD Athlon processors for fan-less designs up to multi-processor systems leveraging multi-core AMD Opteron processors all supporting longer than standard availability.[^cite_note-269]

The ATI acquisition in 2006 included the [Imageon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imageon) and [Xilleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xilleon) product lines. In late 2008, the entire handheld division was sold off to [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm), who have since produced the [Adreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adreno) series.[^cite_note-270] Also in 2008, the Xilleon division was sold to [Broadcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcom).[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272]

In April 2007, AMD announced the release of the [M690T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_690_chipset_series#M690T) integrated graphics chipset for embedded designs. This enabled AMD to offer complete processor and chipset solutions targeted at embedded applications requiring high-performance 3D and video such as emerging digital signage, kiosk, and Point of Sale applications. The M690T was followed by the [M690E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_690_chipset_series#M690E) specifically for embedded applications which removed the TV output, which required [Macrovision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrovision) licensing for OEMs, and enabled native support for dual [TMDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_Minimized_Differential_Signaling) outputs, enabling dual independent [DVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Visual_Interface) interfaces.[^cite_note-273]

In January 2011, AMD announced the AMD Embedded G-Series [Accelerated Processing Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Accelerated_Processing_Unit).[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275] This was the first APU for embedded applications. These were followed by updates in 2013 and 2016.[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277]

In May 2012, AMD announced the AMD Embedded R-Series [Accelerated Processing Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Accelerated_Processing_Unit).[^cite_note-R-Series-278] This family of products incorporates the Bulldozer CPU architecture, and Discrete-class Radeon HD 7000G Series graphics. This was followed by a system-on-a-chip (SoC) version in 2015 which offered a faster CPU and faster graphics, with support for [DDR4 SDRAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDR4_SDRAM) memory.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280]

Since then, AMD has released regular updates to its embedded GPU lineup in 2009, 2011, 2015, and 2016; reflecting improvements in its GPU technology.[^cite_note-AMD_embedded-282][^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285]

## Fabrication

Main article: [GlobalFoundries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlobalFoundries)

Previously, AMD produced its chips at company-owned [semiconductor foundries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundry_\(electronics\)). AMD pursued a strategy of collaboration with other semiconductor manufacturers [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) and [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola) to co-develop production technologies.[^cite_note-vr1-305][^cite_note-vr2-306] AMD's founder Jerry Sanders termed this the "Virtual Gorilla" strategy to compete with Intel's significantly greater investments in fabrication.[^cite_note-vr3-307] Two former fabrication sites in Sunnyvale are [listed as a Superfund site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Superfund_sites_in_California), added in 1986 and 1990 when [volatile organic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_organic_compound) were discovered in groundwater. [Acid neutralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutralization_\(chemistry\)) system (ANS) tank vaults were discovered with holes when excavated with the contaminated soil.[^cite_note-l155-308][^cite_note-n215-309]

In 2008, AMD spun off its chip foundries into an independent company named GlobalFoundries.[^cite_note-310] This breakup of the company was attributed to the increasing costs of each process node. The [Emirate of Abu Dhabi](8821238.md) purchased the newly created company through its subsidiary [Advanced Technology Investment Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Technology_Investment_Company) (ATIC), purchasing the final stake from AMD in 2009.[^cite_note-311]

With the spin-off of its foundries, AMD became a [fabless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabless_manufacturing) semiconductor manufacturer, designing products to be produced at for-hire foundries. Part of the GlobalFoundries spin-off included an agreement with AMD to produce some number of products at GlobalFoundries.[^cite_note-312] Both prior to the spin-off and after, AMD has pursued production with other foundries including [TSMC](463574.md) and [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Foundry).[^cite_note-313][^cite_note-samsung-314] It has been argued that this would reduce risk for AMD by decreasing dependence on any one foundry which has caused issues in the past.[^cite_note-samsung-314][^cite_note-315]

In 2018, AMD started shifting the production of its CPUs and GPUs to TSMC, following GlobalFoundries' announcement that it was halting development of its [7 nm process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nm_process).[^cite_note-\:8-316] AMD revised its wafer purchase requirement with GlobalFoundries in 2019, allowing AMD to freely choose foundries for 7 nm nodes and below, while maintaining purchase agreements for 12 nm and above through 2021.[^cite_note-\:8-316]

### Litigation with Intel

See also: [Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Corp._v._Advanced_Micro_Devices,_Inc.) and [Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. v. Intel Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Micro_Devices,_Inc._v._Intel_Corp.)



AMD processor with Intel copyright

AMD has a long history of litigation with former (and current) partner and x86 creator [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Corporation).[^cite_note-litigation-336][^cite_note-337][^cite_note-\:2-5]

- In 1986, Intel broke an agreement it had with AMD to allow them to produce Intel's microchips for [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM); AMD filed for [arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration) in 1987 and the arbitrator decided in AMD's favor in 1992. Intel disputed this, and the case ended up in the [Supreme Court of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_California). In 1994, that court upheld the arbitrator's decision and awarded damages for breach of contract.

- In 1990, Intel brought a copyright infringement action alleging illegal use of its 287 microcode. The case ended in 1994 with a jury finding for AMD and its right to use Intel's microcode in its microprocessors through the 486 generation.

- In 1997, Intel filed suit against AMD and [Cyrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrix) Corp. for misuse of the term [MMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMX_\(instruction_set\)). AMD and Intel settled, with AMD acknowledging MMX as a trademark owned by Intel, and with Intel granting AMD rights to market the AMD K6 MMX processor.

- In 2005, following an investigation, the Japan Federal Trade Commission found Intel guilty of a number of violations. On June 27, 2005, AMD won an [antitrust](92025.md) suit against Intel in Japan, and on the same day, AMD filed a broad antitrust complaint against Intel in the U.S. Federal District Court in [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware). The complaint alleges systematic use of secret rebates, special discounts, threats, and other means used by Intel to lock AMD processors out of the global market. Since the start of this action, the court has issued [subpoenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subpoena) to major computer manufacturers including [Acer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_Inc.), [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell), [Lenovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenovo), [HP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) and [Toshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiba).

- In November 2009, Intel agreed to pay AMD \$1.25 billion and renew a five-year patent cross-licensing agreement as part of a deal to settle all outstanding legal disputes between them.

  [^cite_note-amd_suit-338]

- On August 31, 2011, in Austin, Texas, AMD achieved a [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Record) for the "Highest frequency of a computer processor": 8.429 GHz.

  [^cite_note-Record-339]

  &#x20;The company ran an 8-core FX-8150 processor with only one active module (two cores), and cooled with liquid helium.

  [^cite_note-youtube-340]
