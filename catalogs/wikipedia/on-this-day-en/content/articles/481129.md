The **prime minister of Latvia** ([Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *ministru prezidents*) is the most powerful member of the [Government of Latvia](19235330.md), who presides over the Latvian Cabinet of Ministers. The officeholder is nominated by the [president of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia), but must be able to obtain the support of a parliamentary majority in the [Saeima](499765.md).

The tables below display all Latvian prime ministers from both the first period of Latvian independence (1918–1940) and since the country regained its independence (1990–present). From 1990 to 6 July 1993, the head of government was known as the chairman of the Council of Ministers.

A direct translation of the official Latvian term is [minister-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-president). Although the equivalent is used in some European languages, it is not used conventionally in English.

### Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic (1940–1990)

From 1940 to 1990, the [Republic of Latvia](17514.md) was under occupation by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In place of the internationally recognised republican institutions, the Soviet-installed [Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) *de-facto* governed the country and implemented its own government structures.

## Notes

[**<sup>1</sup>**](#ref_11)  During the 1918–1920 Independence War, Latvia was contested by two other governments: the government of [Soviet Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Socialist_Soviet_Republic), led by [Pēteris Stučka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%93teris_Stu%C4%8Dka), and the government of [Andrievs Niedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrievs_Niedra), backed by [Baltic Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Germans). Some sources may list Stučka and Niedra as prime ministers for periods when their governments controlled most of Latvia.

[**<sup>2</sup>**](#ref_22)  [On 15 May 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Latvian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), prime minister Ulmanis dissolved parliament and banned all political parties (including his own *Farmer's Union*), establishing authoritarian rule.

[**<sup>3</sup>**](#ref_33)  Puppet leader appointed by Soviet authorities. Not recognized as such by the Latvian government.
