The **1980s** (pronounced "nineteen-eighties", shortened to "**the '80s**" or "**the Eighties**") was the decade that began on 1 January 1980, and ended on 31 December 1989.

The decade saw a dominance of [conservatism](6675.md) and [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) economics, socioeconomic changes due to advances in technology and a worldwide move away from [planned economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_economy) and towards [laissez-faire capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire#Capitalism) in comparison to the 1970s. As economic deconstruction increased in the [developed world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_world), multiple [multinational corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporations) associated with the [manufacturing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_industry) relocated into [Thailand](30128.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), and [China](5405.md). [Japan](15573.md) and [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) saw large economic growth during this decade. The [AIDS epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_epidemic) became recognized in the 1980s and has since killed an estimated 40.4 million people (as of 2022).[^cite_note-1] The [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) theory began to spread within the scientific and political community in the 1980s.

The United Kingdom and the United States moved closer to [supply-side economic policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-side_economics), beginning a trend towards global instability of international trade that would pick up more steam in the [following decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s) as the fall of the USSR made [right-wing](56522.md) economic policy more powerful.

The [final decade of the Cold War opened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_\(1979%E2%80%931985\)) with the US–Soviet confrontation continuing largely without any interruption. Superpower tensions escalated rapidly as President Reagan scrapped the policy of détente and adopted a new, much more aggressive stance on the Soviet Union. The world came perilously close to nuclear war for the first time since the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis) in 1962, but [the second half of the decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_\(1985%E2%80%931991\)) saw a dramatic easing of superpower tensions and ultimately the total collapse of Soviet communism.

Developing countries across the world faced economic and social difficulties as they suffered from multiple debt crises in the 1980s, requiring many of these countries to apply for financial assistance from the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) and the [World Bank](36752.md). [Ethiopia](187749.md) witnessed [widespread famine in the mid-1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%931985_famine_in_Ethiopia) during the corrupt rule of [Mengistu Haile Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam), resulting in the country having to depend on foreign aid to provide food to its population and worldwide efforts to address and raise money to help Ethiopians, such as the [Live Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Aid) concert in 1985.

Major civil discontent and violence occurred, including the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War), the [Ethiopian Civil War](6572888.md), the [Moro conflict](4602307.md), the [Salvadoran Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Civil_War), the [Ugandan Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Bush_War), the [insurgency in Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Laos), the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War), the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War), the [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War), the [Lord's Resistance Army insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord's_Resistance_Army_insurgency), and the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War). [Islamism](15012.md) became a powerful political force in the 1980s and many [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) organizations, including [Al Qaeda](1921.md), were formed.

By 1986, nationalism was making a comeback in the Eastern Bloc, and the desire for democracy in [socialist states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_state), combined with economic recession, resulted in [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev)'s [glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost) and [perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika), which reduced Communist Party power, legalized dissent and sanctioned limited forms of capitalism such as [joint ventures](538782.md) with companies from [capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalist) countries. After tension for most of the decade, by 1988 relations between the communist and capitalist blocs had improved significantly[^cite_note-2] and the Soviet Union was increasingly unwilling to defend its governments in satellite states.

The [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989) brought the overthrow and attempted overthrow of a number of communist-led governments, such as in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Hungary), the [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989) in China, the [Czechoslovak "Velvet Revolution"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Revolution), [Erich Honecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Honecker)'s East German regime, Poland's Soviet-backed government, and [the violent overthrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) of the [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu) regime in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania). Destruction of the 155-km [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall), at the end of the decade, signaled a seismic geopolitical shift. The [Cold War](325329.md) ended in the early 1990s with the successful [Reunification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Germany) and the [USSR's demise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) after the [August Coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) of 1991.

The 1980s was an era of tremendous [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) around the world, surpassing the 1970s and 1990s, and arguably being the largest in human history. During the 1980s, the world population grew from 4.4 to 5.3 billion people. There were approximately 1.33 billion births and 480 million deaths. Population growth was particularly rapid in a number of African, Middle Eastern, and South Asian countries during this decade, with rates of natural increase close to or exceeding 4% annually. The 1980s saw the advent of the ongoing practice of [sex-selective abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex-selective_abortion) in China and India as [ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound) technology permitted parents to selectively abort baby girls.[^cite_note-3]

The 1980s saw great advances in genetic and digital technology. After years of animal experimentation since 1985, the first genetic modification of 10 adult human beings took place in May 1989, a [gene tagging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_tagging) experiment[^cite_note-4] which led to the first true gene therapy implementation in September 1990. The first "[designer babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designer_babies)", a pair of female twins, were created in a laboratory in late 1989 and born in July 1990 after being sex-selected via the controversial [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology) procedure [preimplantation genetic diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimplantation_genetic_diagnosis).[^cite_note-5] [Gestational surrogacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_surrogacy) was first performed in 1985 with the first birth in 1986, making it possible for a woman to become a biological mother without experiencing pregnancy for the first time in history.[^cite_note-people.com-6]

The global [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) took shape in academia by the second half of the 1980s, as well as many other [computer networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) of both academic and commercial use such as [USENET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USENET), [Fidonet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidonet), and the [bulletin board system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system). By 1989, the Internet and the networks linked to it were a global system with extensive transoceanic satellite links and nodes in most [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries).[^cite_note-7] Based on earlier work, from 1980 onwards [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee) formalized the concept of the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web) by 1989. [Television](29831.md) viewing became commonplace in the [Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World), with the number of TV sets in China and India increasing by 15 and 10 times respectively.[^cite_note-8]

The [Atari Video Computer System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600) console became widespread in the first part of the decade, often simply called "Atari". The 1980 Atari VCS port of *[Space Invaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Invaders)* was its first [killer app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killer_application). The [video game crash of 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_crash_of_1983) ended the system's popularity and decimated the industry until the [Nintendo Entertainment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System) re-established the console market in North America. The hand-held [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy) launched in 1989. *[Super Mario Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros.)* and *[Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris)* were the decade's best selling games. *[Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man)* was the highest grossing arcade game. [Home computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_computers) became commonplace. The 1981 [IBM PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_PC) led to a large market for [IBM PC compatibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_PC_compatible). The 1984 release of the [Macintosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macintosh_128K) popularized the [WIMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WIMP_\(computing\)) style of interaction.

### Wars

Main article: [List of wars 1945–89 § 1980–1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wars_1945%E2%80%9389#1980%E2%80%931989)

The most prominent [armed conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_conflict) of the decade include:

#### International wars



[Invasion of Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Grenada), October 1983

The most notable wars of the decade include:

- The [Cold War](325329.md) (1947–1991)&#x20;

  - [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) (1979–1989) – a war fought between the Soviet Union and the Islamist Mujahideen Resistance in Afghanistan. The Mujahideen found other support from a variety of sources including the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) of the United States (see [Operation Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone)), as well as [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), Pakistan and other Muslim nations through the context of the Cold War and the regional India–Pakistan conflict.

  - [Invasion of Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Grenada) (1983) – a 1983 US led invasion of [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), triggered by a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coup) which ousted a brief [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution) government. The successful invasion led to a change of government but was controversial due to charges of [American imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism), [Cold War](325329.md) politics, the involvement of [Cuba](5042481.md), the unstable state of the Grenadian government, and Grenada's status as a [Commonwealth realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm).

  - [Salvadoran Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Civil_War) (1980–1992) – part of the cold war conflicts, reached its peak in the 1980s, 70,000 Salvadorans died.

  - [Cambodian–Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War) (1978–1991)

- Argentina [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_invasion_of_the_Falkland_Islands) the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md), sparking the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War). It occurred from 2 April to 14 July 1982, between the United Kingdom and [Argentina](18951905.md) as British forces fought to recover the islands. Britain emerged victorious and its stance in international affairs and its long-decaying reputation as a [colonial power](7299.md) received an unexpected boost. The [military junta](216815.md) of Argentina, on the other hand, was left humiliated by the defeat; and its leader [Leopoldo Galtieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_Galtieri) was deposed three days after the end of the war. A military investigation known as the Rattenbach Report even recommended his execution.

- [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md) (early 20th century – present)&#x20;

  - [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War) – the [Government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Israel) ordered the invasion as a response to the assassination attempt against Israel's ambassador to the United Kingdom, [Shlomo Argov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Argov), by the [Abu Nidal Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nidal_Organization) and due to the constant terror attacks on northern Israel made by the terrorist organizations which resided in Lebanon. After attacking the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization), as well as [Syrian](7515849.md), leftist and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [Lebanese](17771.md) forces, Israel occupied southern Lebanon and eventually surrounded the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) in west [Beirut](37428.md) and subjected to heavy bombardment, they negotiated passage from Lebanon.

  - In October 1985 eight Israeli [F-15 Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15_Eagles) carried out [Operation Wooden Leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Wooden_Leg) intending to bomb the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization)'s new headquarters in [Tunis](57659.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), more than 2,000 km from Israel. The attack was later condemned by the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md). The United States is thought to have assisted or known of the attack.

- The [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) took place from 1980 to 1988. [Iraq](7515928.md) was accused of using illegal [chemical weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_weapons) to kill [Iranian](14653.md) forces and against its own dissident [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) populations. Both sides suffered enormous casualties, but the poorly equipped Iranian armies suffered worse for it, being forced to use soldiers as young as 15 in human-wave attacks. Iran finally agreed to an armistice in 1988.

- The United States launched an [aerial bombardment of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_United_States_bombing_of_Libya) in 1986 in retaliation for Libyan support of terrorism and attacks on US personnel in Germany and Turkey.

- The [South African Border War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Border_War) between South Africa and the alliance of [Angola](701.md), [Namibia](21292.md) and [Zambia](34415.md) ended in 1989, ending over thirty years of conflict.

- The United States engaged in significant direct and indirect conflict in the decade via alliances with various groups in a number of Central and South American countries claiming that the US was acting to oppose the spread of [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) and end illicit drug trade.&#x20;

  - The US government supported the government of [Colombia's attempts](1857628.md) to destroy its large illicit [cocaine](7701.md)-trafficking industry and provided support for right-wing military government in the [Salvadoran civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_civil_war) which became controversial after the [El Mozote massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mozote_massacre) on 11 December 1981, in which US trained Salvadoran paramilitaries killed 1000 Salvadoran civilians.

  - The United States, along with members of the [Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Eastern_Caribbean_States), [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Grenada) [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada) in 1983.

  - The [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair) erupted which involved US interventionism supporting the [Contras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras) in [Nicaragua](21362.md), resulting in members of the US government being indicted in 1986.

  - US [military action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Panama) began against [Panama](22997.md) in December 1989 to overthrow its dictator, [Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega) resulting in 3,500 civilian casualties and the restoration of democratic rule.

- [Battle of Cuito Cuanavale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cuito_Cuanavale) took place as part of the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) and [South African Border War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Border_War) from 1987 to 1988. The battle involved the largest fighting in Africa since World War II between military forces from [Angola](701.md), [Cuba](5042481.md) (expeditionary forces) and [Namibia](21292.md) versus military forces from South Africa and the dissident Angolan [UNITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNITA) organization.

- The [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) between [Azerbaijan](746.md) and the [Armenia](10918072.md) started in 1988 and ended in 1994.

- The [United States invasion of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Panama) in December 1989 led to the deposition of [Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega).

#### Civil wars and guerrilla wars

The most notable internal conflicts of the decade include:

- The [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989) occurred in the People's Republic of China in 1989, in which pro-democracy protesters demanded political reform. The protests were crushed by the People's Liberation Army.

- The [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) (First Uprising) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) and [West Bank](33209.md) began in 1987 when Palestinian Arabs mounted large-scale protests against the Israeli military presence in the Gaza Strip and West Bank, largely inhabited by Palestinians. The First Intifada would continue until peace negotiations began between the [Palestinian Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) and the Israeli government in 1993.

- [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) (1975–1990) – Throughout the decade, Lebanon was engulfed in civil war between Islamic and Christian factions.

- The [Kanak and Socialist National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanak_and_Socialist_National_Liberation_Front) began a violent campaign for independence in [New Caledonia](21342.md).

- [Greenpeace](12233.md)'s attempts to monitor [French nuclear testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nuclear_testing) on [Mururoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mururoa) were halted by the [sinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Rainbow_Warrior) of the *[Rainbow Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Warrior_\(1978\))*.

- The [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War) erupts in 1983 between the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) government of [Sudan](27421.md) in the north and non-Muslim rebel secessionists in [Southern Sudan](32350676.md). The conflict continues through the present day [Darfur genocide](55472698.md).

- [1986 Egyptian conscripts riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Egyptian_conscripts_riot): On 25 February 1986 around 25,000 conscripts of the [Central Security Forces (CSF)](4317752.md), an Egyptian paramilitary force, staged violent protests in and around Cairo, due to the rumour that their three-year mandatory service would be prolonged by one additional year without any additional benefits or rank promotion. It was suppressed by the [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Army).

- [Internal conflict in Peru](9624647.md): The communist [Túpac Amaru Revolutionary Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BApac_Amaru_Revolutionary_Movement) starts its fight against the Peruvian state in 1980, that would continue until the end of the 1990s.

- [Haitian](13373.md) [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator) [Jean-Claude Duvalier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Duvalier) was overthrown by a popular [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Duvalier_protest_movement) on 6 February 1986.

- [The Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles) in [Northern Ireland](21265.md) continued.

- [Ethiopian Civil War](6572888.md) (1974–1991)

- [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) (1975–2002)

- [Ugandan Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Bush_War) (1980–1986)

- [Sri Lankan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Civil_War) (1983–2009)

### Terrorist attacks

Main article: [List of terrorist incidents § 1970–present](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_terrorist_incidents#1970%E2%80%93present)



[1983 Beirut barracks bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Beirut_barracks_bombing)

The most notable terrorist attacks of the decade include:

- [Bologna massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_massacre) in Italy on 2 August 1980, three members of the [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascist) group [Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclei_Armati_Rivoluzionari) detonate a time bomb at [Bologna Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Central_Station), killing 85 people.

- [El Mozote massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mozote_massacre) in [El Salvador](9356.md) on 11 December 1981, against civilians, committed by government forces supported by the United States during their anti-guerrilla campaign against Marxist–Leninist rebels.

- The [1983 Beirut barracks bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Beirut_barracks_bombing) – during the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) two [truck bombs](155282.md) struck separate buildings housing United States and French military forces killing 299 American and French servicemen. The organization [Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Organization) claimed responsibility for the bombing.

- The [Rome and Vienna airport attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_and_Vienna_airport_attacks) took place on 27 December 1985, against the Israeli [El Al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Al) airline. The attack was done by militants loyal to [Abu Nidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nidal), backed by the government of [Libya](17633.md).

- [Air India Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182) was destroyed on 23 June 1985, by Sikh-Canadian militants. It was the biggest mass murder involving Canadians in Canada's history.

- On 21 December 1988, [Pan Am Flight 103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_103) was blown up over the village of Lockerbie, Scotland, while en route from London's Heathrow Airport to New York's JFK. The bombing killed all 259 people on board, plus 11 people on the ground. The bombing was and remains the worst terrorist attack on UK soil.

### Coups

Main article: [List of coups d'état and coup attempts § 1980–1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coups_d'%C3%A9tat_and_coup_attempts#1980%E2%80%931989)

The most prominent [coups d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coups_d'%C3%A9tat) of the decade include:

- A [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Surinamese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) is launched in [Suriname](26828.md) on 25 February 1980; the country's politics are dominated by the military until 1991.

- [Nigeria](21383.md) suffered military coups in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Nigerian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Nigerian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).

- [Sitiveni Rabuka](516580.md) staged two [military coups in Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Fijian_coups_d'%C3%A9tat) in 1987, and declared the country a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) the same year.

- The "[Anti-Bureaucratic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Bureaucratic_Revolution)" – a series of interconnected coups d'états – take place in [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) from 1988 to 1989 through mass protests organized and committed by supporters of Serbian politician [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) overthrow the governments of Serbia's autonomous provinces of [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Autonomous_province_of_Kosovo?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Autonomous_province_of_Vojvodina?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the government of [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Montenegro), and finally the main government of [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Serbia) with Milošević becoming [President of Serbia](774382.md).

### Decolonization and independence

- Following the decolonization and independence of the [Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).&#x20;

  - In 1982, Canada gained official independence from the United Kingdom with the [Canada Act 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Act_1982), authorized by the signature by [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II). This Act severed all political dependencies of the United Kingdom in Canada (although the Queen remained the head of state).

  - In 1986, Australia gained full independence from the United Kingdom with the [Australia Act 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Act_1986), which severed the last remaining powers of the British government over the Australian government, including the removal of the privy council as the highest court of appeal. Australia retained the Queen as head of state.

  - In 1986, New Zealand and the United Kingdom fully separated New Zealand's governments from the influence of the Parliament of the United Kingdom, resulting in New Zealand's full independence with the [Constitution Act 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act_1986) which also reorganized the [New Zealand government](8899161.md).

  - Independence was granted to [Vanuatu](32443.md) from the British/French condominium (1980), [Kiribati](16674.md) from joint US-British government (1981) and [Palau](23372.md) from the United States (1986).

  - [Zimbabwe](34399.md) becomes independent from official colonial rule of the United Kingdom in 1980.

  - Independence was given to [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md), [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) (both 1981), and [Saint Kitts and Nevis](27198.md) (1983) in the Caribbean; and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) (1984) in Southeast Asia.

### Politics

#### Americas



US President Reagan and Soviet General Secretary Gorbachev signing the [INF Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate-Range_Nuclear_Forces_Treaty), 1987

- [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) was elected US president in 1980. In international affairs, Reagan pursued a hardline policy towards preventing the spread of communism, initiating a considerable buildup of US military power to challenge the Soviet Union. He further directly challenged the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain) by demanding that the Soviet Union dismantle the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall).

- The [Reagan Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_Administration) accelerated the [war on drugs](1181646.md), publicized through anti-drug campaigns including the [Just Say No](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Say_No) campaign of First Lady [Nancy Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Reagan). Drugs gained attention in the US as a serious problem in the '80s. Cocaine was relatively popular among celebrities and affluent youth, while crack, a cheaper offshoot of the drug, was linked to high crime rates in inner cities during the [American crack epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_crack_epidemic).&#x20;

- The [Professional Air Traffic Controllers Organization (1968)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Air_Traffic_Controllers_Organization_\(1968\)) (PATCO) declared a strike on 3 August 1981, seeking better working conditions, better pay, and a 32-hour workweek. The strike caused considerable disruption of the US air transportation system. Resolution came when [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) fired over 11,000 striking air traffic controllers who had ignored his order to return to work, banning them from federal service for life. After seeking appeals, many of the controllers were re-hired while the FAA attempted to replace much of their air traffic control staffing. The remainder continued to be banned until President Clinton lifted the final aspects in 1993.

- Political unrest in the province of [Quebec](7954867.md), which, due to the many differences between the dominant francophone population and the anglophone minority, and also to francophone rights in the predominantly [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_English)-speaking Canada, came to a head in 1980 when the provincial government called a public [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Quebec_referendum) on partial separation from the rest of Canada. The referendum ended with the "no" side winning majority (59.56% no, 40.44% yes).

- [Military dictatorships](146728.md) give way to democracy in [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Argentina#New_democracy_\(1983%E2%80%93present\)) (1983), [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Uruguay#Military_dictatorship,_1973%E2%80%931985) (1984–85), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_in_Brazil) (1985–1988) and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Chilean_national_plebiscite) (1988–89). This marked the end of the [Operation Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Condor) for 30 years.

#### Europe



The fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989 marked the beginning of German reunification



Former [president of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Finland) from 1956 to 1982. Funeral cortege of [Urho Kekkonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urho_Kekkonen) in Helsinki, 1986

- The [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community)'s [enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_European_Union) continued with the accession of Greece in 1981 and Spain and Portugal in 1986.

- In 1983, [Bettino Craxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettino_Craxi) became the first [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) to hold the office of [Prime Minister of Italy](273741.md); he remained in power until 1987, becoming one of the longest-serving Prime Ministers in the history of Italian Republic. At the end of his presidency the [Mani pulite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_pulite) corruption scandal broke up, causing the collapse of the political system.

- Significant political reforms occurred in a number of communist countries in eastern Europe as the populations of these countries grew increasingly hostile and politically active in opposing communist governments. These reforms included attempts to increase individual liberties and market liberalization, and promises of democratic renewal. The collapse of communism in eastern Europe was generally peaceful, the exception being [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution), whose leader [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu) tried to keep the people isolated from the events happening outside the country. While making a speech in Bucharest in December 1989, he was booed and shouted down by the crowd, and then tried to flee the city with his wife [Elena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Ceau%C8%99escu). Two days later, they were captured, charged with genocide, and [shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_of_Nicolae_and_Elena_Ceau%C8%99escu) on Christmas Day.

- In [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), following the death of communist leader [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito) in May 1980, the trend of political reform of the communist system occurred along with a trend towards [ethnic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_nationalism) and inter-ethnic hostility, especially in Serbia, beginning with the 1986 [Memorandum of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum_of_the_Serbian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts) followed by the agenda of Serbian communist leader [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) who aggressively pushed for increased political influence of Serbs in the late 1980s, condemning non-Serb Yugoslav politicians who challenged his agenda as being enemies of Serbs.

- There was [continuing civil strife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles) in [Northern Ireland](21265.md), including the adoption of hunger strikes by [Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republican_Army) prisoners seeking the reintroduction of political status.

- [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev) became leader of the Soviet Union in 1985, and initiated major reforms to the Soviet Union's government through increasing the rights of expressing political dissent and opening elections to opposition candidates (while maintaining legal dominance of the Communist Party). Gorbachev pursued negotiation with the United States to decrease tensions and eventually end the [Cold War](325329.md).

- During the [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989), most of the communist governments in Eastern Europe collapsed. The [fall of the Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Berlin_Wall) in 1989 would be followed in 1990 by the [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification).

- The United Kingdom was governed by the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) under Prime Minister [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher), the first female leader of a Western country. Under her [Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Margaret_Thatcher), the party introduced widespread economic reforms including the [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) of industries and the de-regulation of [stock markets](52328.md) echoing similar reforms of [US President](24113.md) [Ronald Reagan](25433.md). She was also a staunch opponent of communism, earning her the nickname *The Iron Lady*.

- Poor industrial relations marked the beginning of the decade; the [UK miners' strike (1984–85)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_miners'_strike_\(1984%E2%80%9385\)) was a major [industrial action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_action) affecting the [UK coal industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_the_United_Kingdom). The strike by the [National Union of Mineworkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Mineworkers_\(Great_Britain\)) (NUM) was led by [Arthur Scargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Scargill), although some NUM members considered it to be unconstitutional and did not observe it. The [BBC](19344654.md) has referred to the strike as "the most bitter industrial dispute in British history."

  [^cite_note-9]

  &#x20;At its height, the strike involved 142,000 mineworkers, making it the biggest since the [1926 General Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_United_Kingdom_general_strike).

  [^cite_note-10]

- In November 1982, [Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev), who had led the Soviet Union since 1964, died. He was followed in quick succession by [Yuri Andropov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Andropov), the former KGB chief, and [Konstantin Chernenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Chernenko), both of whom were in poor health during their short tenures in office.

- Presidents of France were [Valéry Giscard d'Estaing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val%C3%A9ry_Giscard_d'Estaing) and [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand). The Chancellors of West Germany were [Helmut Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Schmidt) and [Helmut Kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Kohl).

#### Asia

- The Prime Ministers of Japan were [Masayoshi Ōhira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masayoshi_%C5%8Chira), [Zenkō Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenk%C5%8D_Suzuki), [Yasuhiro Nakasone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuhiro_Nakasone), [Noboru Takeshita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noboru_Takeshita), [Sōsuke Uno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%8Dsuke_Uno) and [Toshiki Kaifu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiki_Kaifu).

- Following the [assassination of Park Chung-hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Park_Chung-hee), South Korean president [Chun Doo Hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chun_Doo_Hwan) came to power at the end of 1979 and ruled as a dictator until his presidential term expired in 1987. He was responsible for the [Gwangju Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Uprising) in May 1980 when police and soldiers battled armed protesters. Relations with North Korea showed little sign of improvement during the 1980s. In 1983, when Chun was in Burma, [a bomb apparently planted by North Korean agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon_bombing) killed a number of South Korean government officials. The [June Democratic Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Democratic_Struggle) in 1987, a nationwide pro-democracy movement in South Korea, leads to democratic reforms, an end to authoritarian rule and [democratic elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_South_Korean_presidential_election). After leaving office, Chun was succeeded by [Roh Tae Woo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roh_Tae_Woo), the first democratic ruler of the country, which saw its international prestige greatly rise with hosting the Olympics in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics). Roh pursued a policy of normalizing relations with China and the Soviet Union, but had to face militant left-wing student groups who demanded [reunification with North Korea](61104.md) and the withdrawal of US troops.

- In the Philippines, after 20 years of dictatorship, Philippine president [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) was ousted from power, fleeing with [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos) and their children to [Hawaii](13270.md) and replaced by [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) through the non-violent [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution) from 22 to 25 February 1986.

- The [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) were held in South Korea, the first time the country hosted them.

Blame for the famine has been attributed to drought, [Ethiopia's civil war](6572888.md), and policies taken by the [Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg) military regime.

## Disasters

### Natural disasters



[1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_eruption_of_Mount_St._Helens)



The [Holy Saviour's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Saviour's_Church,_Gyumri) in [Gyumri](1529357.md) after the [Spitak earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitak_earthquake)

- [Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Helens) [erupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_eruption_of_Mount_St._Helens) in [Washington](13015878.md), US on 18 May 1980, killing 57 people.

- [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS), a global pandemic that has killed over 40 million people, was [identified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_HIV/AIDS) in the 1980s, with the first reported cases in 1981.

- On 17 October 1989, the [Loma Prieta earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loma_Prieta_earthquake) struck the San Francisco Bay Area during game three of the [1989 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_World_Series), gaining worldwide attention. Sixty-five people were killed and thousands injured, with major structural damage on freeways and buildings and broken gas-line fires in San Francisco, California. The cost of the damage totaled US\$13 billion (1989 US\$).

- The [1988–89 North American drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_North_American_drought) decimated the US with many parts of the country affected. This was the worst drought to hit the United States in many years. The drought caused \$60 billion in damage (between \$80 billion and \$120 billion for 2008 US\$). The concurrent [heat waves](293270.md) killed 5,800 to 17,000 people in the United States.

- [Hurricane Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen) (1980), [Hurricane Alicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Alicia) (1983), [Hurricane Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Gilbert) (1988), [Hurricane Joan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Joan%E2%80%93Miriam) (1988), and [Hurricane Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hugo) (1989) were some notably destructive Atlantic hurricanes of the 1980s.

- Other [natural disasters](58921.md) of the 1980s include the 1982–1983 [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_El_Ni%C3%B1o_event) which brought destructive weather to most of the world; the [1985 Mexico City earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Mexico_City_earthquake), which registered 8.0 on the [moment magnitude scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) and devastated Mexico City and other areas throughout central Mexico; the 1985 [Nevado del Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevado_del_Ruiz) [lahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) in [Colombia](5222.md); the 1986 [Lake Nyos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nyos) [limnic eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limnic_eruption) in [Cameroon](5447.md); and the [1988 Armenian earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake), which rocked the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) region of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR).

### Non-natural disasters



The [space shuttle *Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger) disintegrates on January 28, 1986

- In 1980, [Dan-Air Flight 1008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan-Air_Flight_1008), a [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727), crashed on approach to [Tenerife](269223.md) in the [Canary Islands](5717.md), killing 146 people on board.

- In 1980, [Saudia Flight 163](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudia_Flight_163), a [Lockheed L-1011 TriStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-1011_TriStar), caught fire moments after takeoff from the [Saudi Arabian](349303.md) capital of [Riyadh](51554.md). The flight immediately returned to the airport, but evacuation of the plane was delayed and all 301 people aboard died.

- In 1982, [Pan Am Flight 759](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_759), a Boeing 727, was forced down by [microburst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microburst) while on approach to [New Orleans International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans_International_Airport), killing 153 people.

- In 1983, [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007), a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) carrying 269 people between New York City and [Seoul](19159283.md) via [Anchorage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchorage,_Alaska), was shot down by Soviet fighter jets after accidentally straying into Soviet [prohibited airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibited_airspace), killing everyone on board.

- In 1984, the [Bhopal disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal_disaster) resulted from a toxic [methyl isocyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methyl_isocyanate) (MIC) gas leak at the [Union Carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Carbide) plant in [Bhopal, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal,_India), killing 3,000 immediately and ultimately claiming 15,000–20,000 lives.

- In 1985, the [Heysel Stadium disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Stadium_disaster) occurred before the [European Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_European_Cup_final) in [Brussels](3708.md), Belgium, when a [crowd crush](69280791.md) led to 39 deaths and 600 injuries.

- In 1985, [Air India Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182), a Boeing 747 flying from [Montreal](7954681.md) to [Bombay](19189.md) via London and Delhi, is blown up over Irish waters by a bomb planted by [Sikh separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan_movement), killing all 320 passengers and crew on board. This was the deadliest act of aviation terrorism until the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) of 2001.

- In 1985, [Delta Air Lines Flight 191](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines_Flight_191), a [Lockheed L-1011 TriStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-1011_TriStar), crashed on approach to [Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth_International_Airport) in [Texas](29810.md) due to a microburst. 137 people were killed while 27 survived.

- [Japan Air Lines Flight 123](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Air_Lines_Flight_123), a Boeing 747 carrying 524 people, crashed in 1985, while on a flight from Tokyo to [Osaka](52230.md), killing 520 of the people on board, leaving four survivors. This was the deadliest single-aircraft crash to date.

- In 1985, [Arrow Air Flight 1285R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Air_Flight_1285R), a [Douglas DC-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-8), [stalled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stall_\(aerodynamics\)) and crashed seconds after taking off from [Gander, Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gander,_Newfoundland). All 256 people on board, many of them US servicemen returning home from duty overseas, perished.

- In 1986, the NASA [Space Shuttle *Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger) [disintegrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster) 73 seconds after launch, killing all of the crew on board. This was the first disaster involving the destruction of a NASA Space Shuttle. A faulty [O-ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O-ring) was the cause of the accident.

- On 26 April 1986, the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster), a large-scale [nuclear meltdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_meltdown) in the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), Soviet Union, spread a large amount of radioactive material across Europe, killing 47 people, dooming countless others to future radiation-related cancer, and causing the displacement of 300,000 people.

- In 1986, [Aeroméxico Flight 498](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerom%C3%A9xico_Flight_498), a [Douglas DC-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-9), crashed after colliding with a private [Piper Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_Cherokee) over [Cerritos, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerritos,_California), killing 64 on both aircraft and 15 others on the ground. On the same day, the Soviet passenger ship *[Admiral Nakhimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Admiral_Nakhimov)* sank after colliding with the bulk carrier *Pyotr Vasev* in the [Black Sea](3386.md), killing 423 people.

- In 1987, [LOT Flight 5055](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOT_Flight_5055), an [Ilyushin Il-62M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-62M) caught fire due to an [uncontained engine failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncontained_engine_failure) and subsequently crashed, killing all 183 passengers and crew.

- In 1987, [Northwest Airlines Flight 255](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Airlines_Flight_255), a [McDonnell Douglas MD-82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_MD-82), crashed almost immediately after takeoff [Detroit Wayne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Metropolitan_Wayne_County_Airport) in [Michigan](18859.md) due to [pilot error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_error), killing 156 on board.

- In 1987, a fire broke out on [South African Airways Flight 295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways_Flight_295), a Boeing 747, eventually causing the aircraft to crash into the Indian Ocean. All 159 aboard were killed.

- On 20 December 1987, the [Philippine](23440.md) passenger ferry *[Doña Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C3%B1a_Paz)* burned and sank after colliding with the oil tanker [MT *Vector*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MT_Vector) off the island of [Marinduque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinduque). With an estimated death toll of over 4,000, this was and remains the world's deadliest peacetime maritime disaster.

- In 1988, [Iran Air Flight 655](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Air_Flight_655), an [Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300) en route from Tehran to [Dubai](211583.md) via [Bandar Abbas](464348.md), was shot down by the US missile cruiser [USS *Vincennes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Vincennes_\(CG-49\)) over the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), killing all 290 people on the plane. The event is one of the most controversial aviation occurrences of all time, with the true cause disputed between the Americans and the Iranians.

- In 1988, [Pan Am Flight 103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_103), a Boeing 747 en route from Frankfurt to Detroit (via London and New York), was destroyed by a bomb while it was flying over [Lockerbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockerbie), Scotland, killing the 259 passengers and crew members on board and 11 people on the ground. This was the worst terrorist attack to have occurred on British soil.

- On 24 March 1989, the [oil tanker](15923603.md) [Exxon Valdez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exxon_Valdez) ran aground on [Bligh Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bligh_Reef) in [Alaska](624.md)'s [Prince William Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_Sound) [spilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exxon_Valdez_oil_spill) an estimated equivalent of 260,000 to 750,000 barrels of crude oil. Although not among the largest oil spills in history, its remote and sensitive location made it one of the most devastating ecological disasters, with after-effects continuing to be felt present-day.

- In 1989, the [Hillsborough disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_disaster) occurs during a [FA Cup](11237.md) semi-final in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), England, fatally crushing 96 football fans and injuring nearly 1,000 more.

- In 1989, [United Airlines Flight 232](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines_Flight_232), a McDonnell Douglas DC-10 carrying 296 people, suffered an in-flight engine failure and was forced to crash-land at [Sioux City, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_City,_Iowa). 185 survived, while 111 were killed when the plane burst into flames upon touchdown.

### Medicine and biology

The 1980s had many fundamental advances in medicine and biology. The first [surrogate pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrogate_pregnancy) of an unrelated child took place on 13 April 1986, in Michigan.[^cite_note-people.com-6] The first [genetically modified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_engineering) crops, [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotiana) (*Nicotiana*) plants were grown in China in 1988.[^cite_note-31]

[Gene therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy) techniques became established by the end of the 1980s, allowing [gene tagging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_tagging) and [gene therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy) to become a possibility, both of which were first performed in human beings in May 1989 and September 1990, respectively.

*[Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man)* (1980) quickly became one of the most famous video games of all time.

[Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_game) and video games had been growing in popularity since the late 1970s, and by 1982 were a major industry. In the United States, a variety of factors, including a glut of low-quality games and the rise of home computers, caused a [tremendous crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_video_game_crash_of_1983) in late 1983. For the next three years, the video game market practically ceased to exist in the US. But in the second half of the decade, it would be revived by [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo), whose Famicom console and mascot [Mario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario) had been enjoying considerable success in Japan, which did not have an equivalent crash, since 1983. Renamed the [Nintendo Entertainment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System) (NES), it would claim 90% of the American video game market by 1989.

In 1980, Pac-Man was introduced to arcades, and became one of the most popular video games of all time. Also in 1980, [Game & Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_&_Watch) was created; it was not one of the best known game systems, but it facilitated mini-games and was concurrent with the NES. *[Donkey Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_\(arcade_game\))*, released in 1981, was a smash arcade hit and market breakthrough for [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo). *[Super Mario Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros.)*, *[Duck Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck_Hunt)*, *[Dragon Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Quest)*, *[The Legend of Zelda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda)*, and the *[Mega Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Man)* series would become major hits for the console.

Electronics and computers were also at the forefront of the advertising industry, with many commercials like "1984" from Apple achieving acclaim and pop-culture relevance.[^cite_note-33]

[Walkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkman) and [boomboxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boombox), invented during the late 1970s, became very popular as they were introduced to various countries in the early 1980s, and had a profound impact on the music industry and youth culture. Consumer [VCRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videocassette_recorder) and video rental stores became commonplace as [VHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHS) won out over the competing [Betamax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betamax) standard. In addition, in the early 1980s various companies began selling compact, modestly priced [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer) to the public. This, along with the development of [Musical Instrument Digital Interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIDI) (MIDI), made it easier to integrate and synchronize synthesizers and other electronic instruments, like drum machines, for use in musical composition.

[High definition television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_definition_television) (HDTV) of both the analog and digital variety were first developed in the 1980s though their use did not become widespread until the mid-2000s.

In 1981, [Hayes Microcomputer Products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayes_Microcomputer_Products) started selling the Smartmodem. The Smartmodem paved the way for the modern modems that exist today, mainly because it was the first modem to transform what had previously required a two-stage process into a process involving only one stage. The Smartmodem contributed to the rise in popularity of [BBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system) systems in the 1980s and early 1990s, which were the main way to connect to remote computers and perform various social and entertainment activities before the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) and the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web) finally became popular in the mid-1990s.

The 1980s witnessed a rapid expansion in the communications industry. Almost a decade after [Martin Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Cooper_\(inventor\)), then an employee of [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola), made the first mobile phone call in 1973, [Millicom Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millicom), a telecommunications agency, and [E.F. Johnson & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.F._Johnson_Company), introduced the first portable cellular phone commercially available for use on a cellular network, the "Lunch Box" in 1981.[^cite_note-\:0-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-\:1-38] Two years later, Motorola launched the [DynaTAC 8000X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola_DynaTAC) or the "Brick," the first commercially available handheld mobile phone weighing 3 pounds (1.4 kg).[^cite_note-\:1-38] While revolutionary, these early products were bulky and challenging to handle. This led to fierce competition in the market, with companies vying to produce a lighter, more portable phone, setting the stage for the future of mobile technology.

The race for a slimmer version of the portable cell phone was underway, and technology entrepreneur [Jan Stenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Stenbeck) was determined to lead the charge. Stenbeck founded the tech start-up [Technophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technophone) with a singular goal in mind: to create a lightweight, pocket-sized mobile phone. In 1986, under the guidance of [Technophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excel_mobile_phones) [chief executive officer](52234.md), Nils Martensson, the company unveiled the first pocket-sized mobile phone, the Excell PCT105.[^cite_note-\:0-36][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Information technology

- During the decade [Microsoft](19001.md) released the operating systems **[MS-DOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-DOS)** (1981), **[Windows 1.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_1.0)** (1985), and **[Windows 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_2.0)** (1987).

- The **[CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD)** – the most basic CD ("Digital Audio Compact Disc") was released in October 1982 for distribution and listening to [digital audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_audio), and at the time contained up to 74 minutes of music.

- **[TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCP/IP)**: [ARPANET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARPANET) officially changed its main protocol from NCP to [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCP/IP) on 1 January 1983, when the new protocols were activated. The TCP/IP protocol will become the dominant communications protocol from then onwards, and would be used as the foundation on which the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) would be based.

- The **[GNU Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Project)** (1983). The **[Free Software Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Software_Foundation)** (1985).

- **[FidoNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FidoNet)** – In 1984, FidoNet was launched, enabling [BBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system) users to send private messages (e-mails) and public messages (in the forum) between all BBS systems that were connected to the FidoNet network, in addition to sending files to each other. The rise in popularity and availability of the Internet around the world around the mid-1990s eventually contributed to the irrelevance of FidoNet.

- **[World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web)** – In 1989, the British computer scientist [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee) first proposed a project to his employer [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN), based on the concept of [hypertext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertext), to facilitate sharing and updating information among researchers. In mid-November 1989 he would develop the first successful communication between a [Hypertext Transfer Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertext_Transfer_Protocol) (HTTP) client and server via the [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet). In the coming years Berners-Lee developed the system which would later become the foundation of the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web).

### Space exploration



The [Space Shuttle *Columbia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia) seconds after engine ignition, 1981

American interplanetary probes continued in the 1980s, the Voyager duo being the most known. After making a flyby of Jupiter in 1979, they went near Saturn in 1980–1981. *[Voyager 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_2)* reached Uranus in 1986 (just a few days before the *Challenger* disaster), and Neptune in 1989 before the probes exited the Solar System.

No American probes were launched to Mars in the 1980s, and the [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_program) probes, launched there in 1975, completed their operations by 1982. The Soviets launched two Mars probes in 1988, but they failed.

The arrival of [Halley's Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley's_Comet) in 1986 was met by a series of Soviet, Japanese and [European Space Agency](10363.md) (ESA) probes, namely [Halley Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley_Armada).

After a six-year hiatus, American space flights with astronauts resumed with the launch of the [Space Shuttle *Columbia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia) in April 1981. The shuttle program progressed smoothly from there, with three more orbiters entering service in 1983–1985. But that all came to an end with the [tragic loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster) of the [*Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger) ([STS-51-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-51-L)) on 28 January 1986, taking with it seven astronauts, including [Christa McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christa_McAuliffe), who was to have been the first teacher in space. In full view of the world, a faulty O-ring on the right solid rocket booster allowed hot gases to burn through the external fuel tank and cause it to explode, destroying the shuttle in the process. Extensive efforts were made to improve [NASA](18426568.md)'s increasingly careless management practices, and to make the shuttle safer. Flights resumed with the launch of [*Discovery*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Discovery) in September 1988.

The Soviet program with cosmonauts went well during the decade, experiencing only minor setbacks. The [Salyut 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyut_6) space station, launched in 1977, was replaced by [Salyut 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyut_7) in 1982. Then came *[Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir)* in 1986, which ended up operating for more than a decade, and was destined to be the last in the line of Soviet space stations that had begun in 1971. One of the Soviet Union's last "superprojects" was the [*Buran* space shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buran_\(spacecraft\)); it was only used once, in 1988.

### Automobiles

The American auto industry began in the 1980s in a thoroughly grim situation, faced with poor quality control, rising import competition, and a severe economic downturn.[^cite_note-41] [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) and [American Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Motors) (AMC) were near bankruptcy, and Ford was little better off.[^cite_note-42] Only [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) (GM) continued with business as usual. But the auto makers recovered with the economy by 1983, and in 1985 auto sales in the United States hit a new record. However, the Japanese were now a major presence, and would begin manufacturing cars in the US to get around tariffs. In 1986, [Hyundai](42257.md) became the first Korean auto maker to enter the American market. In the same year, the Yugoslavian-built [Yugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_Koral) was brought to the US, but the car was so small and cheap, that it became the subject of jokes. It was sold up to 1991, when economic sanctions against Yugoslavia forced its withdrawal from the American market.

As the decade progressed, cars became smaller and more efficient in design. In 1983, [Ford](30433662.md) design teams began to incorporate aerodynamic styling to decrease drag while in motion. The [Thunderbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Thunderbird) was one of the first cars to receive these design changes. In 1985, Ford released the [Taurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Taurus) with a design that was revolutionary among domestic mass market automobiles.

GM began suffering significant losses in the late 1980s, partially the result of chairman [Roger Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Smith_\(executive\))'s restructuring attempts, and partially because of increasingly dated cars. An example were customers who increasingly purchased European luxury cars rather than [Cadillacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac). In 1985, GM started [Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Corporation) (the first new American make since the Edsel), with the goal of producing high-quality import fighters. Production would not begin until 1990.

Chrysler introduced its new compact, front-wheel drive [K-cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_K_platform) in 1981. Under the leadership of [Lee Iacocca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Iacocca), the company turned a profit again the following year, and by 1983 paid off its government loans. A succession of models using this automobile platform followed. The most significant were the [minivans](20828.md) in 1984. These proved a to be popular and they would dominate the van market for more than a decade. In 1987, Chrysler purchased the Italian makes of [Lamborghini](18271.md) and [Maserati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati). In the same year, Chrysler bought [AMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Motors_Corporation) from [Renault](162292.md) laying to rest the last significant independent US automaker, but acquiring the hugely profitable [Jeep](15658.md) line and continuing the [Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_\(automobile\)) brand until the late 1990s.[^cite_note-43]

The [DMC DeLorean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMC_DeLorean) was the brainchild of [John DeLorean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_DeLorean), a flamboyant former GM executive. Production of the gull-winged sports car began in Northern Ireland in 1981. John DeLorean was arrested in October 1982 in a sting operation where he was attempting to sell cocaine to save his struggling company. He was acquitted of all charges in 1984, but too late for the [DeLorean Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeLorean_Motor_Company), which closed down in 1983. The DeLorean gained renewed fame afterward as the time machine in the [*Back to the Future* film trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_the_Future_\(series\)).



1983 Ford Fiesta XR2. A typical mid-1980s car.

The imposition of [corporate average fuel economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_average_fuel_economy) (CAFE) fuel-mileage standards in 1979 spelled the end of big-block engines, but [performance cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_car) and [convertibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convertible) reemerged in the 1980s. [Turbochargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbocharger) were widely used to boost the performance of small cars, and technology from [fuel injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_injection) began to take over from the widely used application of [carburetors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carburetors) by the late 1980s. Front-wheel drive also became dominant.

The 1980s marked the decline of European brands in North America by the end of the decade. Renault, [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn), and [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot) ceased importation by the end of the decade. [Alfa Romeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo) would continue until 1993. [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Automobiles) also ceased imports to North America in the 1980s.

## Economics

- The early 1980s was marked by a severe global [economic recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1980s_recession) that affected much of the developed world.

- Inflation peaked in the US in April 1980 at 14.76% and subsequently fell to a low of 1.10% in December 1986 but then rebounded to 4.65% at the end of the decade.

  [^cite_note-44]

- Finland's economy grew by almost the fastest pace in the world, which eventually culminated in the [recession of the 1990s Finnish economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1990s_depression_in_Finland). In Finland, the 1980s were called the "Nousukausi", or "economic upswing".

- In the late 1980s, Japan experienced an [economic bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_asset_price_bubble), which would culminate in a stock market crash in 1991 that would begin a [period of economic stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Decades).

- Developing countries reliant on loans from the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) would experience debt crises throughout the 1980s.

- [Laissez faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire_economics) and [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) economics have a resurgence in the developed world, led by the UK and US which emphasised reduced government intervention, lower taxes and deregulation of the stock markets, measures that became associated with an economic revival in the mid- to late-1980s.

- Brazil and Mexico suffers from a [debt crisis in Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_debt_crisis) starting in 1982 under President [João Figueiredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Figueiredo) and [Miguel de la Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_de_la_Madrid). Economic problems worsened between 1979 and 1985 by firing and resignation of most officials of the Brazilian and Mexican government after the [Diretas Já](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diretas_J%C3%A1) movement in 1984, and a failed response of emergency aid in the [Mexico City earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Mexico_City_earthquake) just after the 175th anniversary of independence holiday in 1985. [Tancredo Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tancredo_Neves) (later succeeded by [José Sarney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Sarney) three months later) won a [direct presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Brazilian_presidential_election) in 1985, marking the end of a [21-year military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_military_dictatorship), while Mexico's [Carlos Salinas de Gortari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Salinas_de_Gortari) won a [controversial presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Mexican_general_election) in 1988 amid charges of voter fraud, bribery, corruption and other abuses of power.

- Enactment of the [Canada–United States Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_Free_Trade_Agreement) in 1989 to further establish a strong economic bond between the two prosperous neighbor countries of North America.

- In the Soviet Union, the [eleventh Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Five-Year_Plan_\(Soviet_Union\)) was initiated in 1981 during a [period of economic stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Stagnation) that began in the late 1970s. The Plan was a near failure, as most of the targets were not met. With the ascent of [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev) as [General Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union), the twelfth Five-Year Plan sought to [accelerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uskoreniye) and [restructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika) the Soviet economy through reforms to decentralize production and distribution systems.

- Under the leadership of [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping), China embarked on the [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) in the 1980s, opening the country's economy to the West and allowing capitalist enterprises to operate in a [market socialist system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_with_Chinese_characteristics). The corruption of [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_China) leadership was met by dissent from students and workers in the [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989) which were suppressed by the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md).

- The [Solidarity movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(Polish_trade_union\)) began in Poland in 1980, involving workers demanding political liberalization and democracy in Poland. Attempts by the Communist government to prevent the rise of the Solidarity movement failed and negotiations between the movement and the government took place. Solidarity would be instrumental in encouraging people in other communist states to demand political reform.

- The financial world and the stock market were glamorized in a way they had not been since the 1920s, and figures like [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and [Michael Milken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Milken) were widely seen as symbols of the decade. Widespread fear of Japanese economic strength would grip the United States in the 1980s.

- The "Black Monday" [stock market crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Monday_\(1987\)) on 19 October 1987, decreased the value of the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](47361.md) by more than 22%, causing widespread secondary drops in world markets.

- During the 1980s, for the first time in world history, transpacific trade (with [East Asia](19605700.md), such as China, and Latin America, primarily with Mexico) equaled that of transatlantic trade (with [Western Europe](33800.md) or with neighboring Canada),

  [^cite_note-45]

  &#x20;solidifying American economic power.

  [^cite_note-46]

- [The Savings and Loan crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savings_and_loan_crisis) and [Keating five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keating_Five) scandal.

- The phrase [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang_\(financial_markets\)), used in reference to the sudden deregulation of financial markets, was coined to describe measures, including abolition of fixed commission charges and of the distinction between stockjobbers and stockbrokers on the London Stock Exchange and change from open-outcry to electronic, screen-based trading, effected by Margaret Thatcher in 1986.

### Music

Main article: [1980s in music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_in_music)







[Michael Jackson](14995351.md), [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_\(musician\)), and [Madonna](142056.md) were among the best-selling musical talents of the decade, all considered some of the most globally popular and culturally significant [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) and [R\&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_R\&B) talents of the 1980s, pictured here in 1983, 1986 and 1987 respectively.

In the United States, [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV) was launched and [music videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_video) began to have a larger effect on the record industry. Pop artists/bands such as [Duran Duran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duran_Duran), [Michael Jackson](14995351.md), [George Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Michael), [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston), [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_\(musician\)), [Cyndi Lauper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyndi_Lauper) and [Madonna](142056.md) mastered the format and helped turn this new product into a profitable business. At the beginning of the decade [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music) fell from favor with the rise of the [New Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Romantic),[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] [new pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_pop) and [synthpop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthpop) genres developed by many British and American artists, popular phenomena throughout the decade especially in the early and mid-1980s. Music evolved into fragmented and combined subgenres, including house, goth, and rap metal.[^cite_note-50] Famous music videos include those of [Peter Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gabriel).

The advent of numerous new technologies had a significant impact on 1980s music, and led to a distinct production aesthetic that included [synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer) sounds, [drum machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_machine) and drum reverb.

[Duran Duran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duran_Duran), the biggest band of the 1980s, were leaders in the [Second British Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_British_Invasion), with a level of fame similar to [Beatlemania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatlemania) by 1985. Their debut single was "[Planet Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_Earth_\(Duran_Duran_song\))" (1981). Their breakthrough album was *[Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_\(Duran_Duran_album\))* (1982). The single "[Hungry Like the Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_Like_the_Wolf)" was number 1 in Canada. UK number 1 singles include "[Is There Something I Should Know?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_There_Something_I_Should_Know%3F)" and "[The Reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reflex)", which was the band's most successful single and was also number 1 in the US and on the Eurochart Hot 100. "[A View to a Kill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_View_to_a_Kill_\(song\))", theme song of the James Bond film, was number 1 in the US. "[Notorious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notorious_\(Duran_Duran_song\))" was number 1 in Italy, Spain and Canada. "[The Wild Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wild_Boys_\(song\))" was number 1 in West Germany and South Africa. The band went on to sell over 100 million records and win Brit, Grammy and MTV awards.

[Michael Jackson](14995351.md) was one of the icons of the 1980s and his [leather jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather_jacket), white [glove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glove), and [Moonwalk dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonwalk_dance) were often imitated. Jackson's 1982 album *[Thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_\(Michael_Jackson_album\))* became—and currently remains—the [best-selling album of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums), with sales estimated by various sources as somewhere between 65 and 110 million copies worldwide. His 1987 album *[Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_\(album\))* sold over 45 million copies and became the first album to have five number-one singles chart on the [Billboard Hot 100](423161.md). Jackson had the most number-one singles throughout the decade (9), and spent the most weeks at number one (27 weeks). His 1987 [Bad World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_World_Tour) grossed over \$125 million worldwide, making it the highest grossing world tour by a solo artist during the decade. Jackson earned numerous awards and titles during the 1980s, the most notable of which were a record eight [Grammy Awards](44635.md) and eight [American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Award) in 1984, and the honor of "Artist of the Decade" by US President [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush). As of 2013, estimates of his total sales worldwide had included figures of over 500 million to one billion records sold.[^cite_note-51]

[Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_\(musician\)) was a popular star of the 1980s and the most successful chart act of the decade. His breakthrough album [*1999*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_\(Prince_album\)), released in 1982, produced three top-ten hits and the album itself charted at number nine on the [Billboard 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200). His sixth studio album [*Purple Rain*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Rain_\(album\)) was an international success, boosting Prince to superstardom and selling over 25 million copies worldwide. The album produced the US number-one singles, "[When Doves Cry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Doves_Cry)" and "[Let's Go Crazy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Go_Crazy)" and sold 13 million copies in the US as of 1996. Prince released an album every year for the rest of the decade, all charting within the top ten, with the exception of *[Lovesexy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovesexy)*. He went on to sell over 120 million records worldwide and win seven [Grammy Awards](44635.md).

[Hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock), [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music), and [glam metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_metal) became some of the most dominant music genres of the decade, peaking with the arrival of such bands as [Mötley Crüe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6tley_Cr%C3%BCe), [Guns N' Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guns_N'_Roses), [Metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallica), [Iron Maiden](159172.md), [Bon Jovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Jovi), [Def Leppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Leppard), [Poison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poison_\(band\)), [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_\(band\)), [Megadeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadeth), [Slayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slayer), [Sepultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepultura), [Anthrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthrax_\(American_band\)), and [virtuoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtuoso) guitarists such as [Joe Satriani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Satriani) and [Yngwie Malmsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yngwie_Malmsteen). The 1980s were also known for song parodies becoming more mainstream, a trend led by parodic musician ["Weird Al" Yankovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Weird_Al%22_Yankovic). He was best known for his [Michael Jackson](14995351.md) parodies "[Eat It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eat_It)" and "[Fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_\(song\))" as well as other parodies like "[Another One Rides The Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_One_Rides_The_Bus)" (parody of "[Another One Bites The Dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_One_Bites_The_Dust)" by [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_\(Band\))).

By 1989, the [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop) scene had evolved, gaining recognition and exhibiting a stronger influence on the music industry. This time period is also considered part of the [golden age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_hip-hop) of hip-hop. The [Beastie Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beastie_Boys), [Public Enemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Enemy_\(group\)), [Run-D.M.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run%E2%80%93D.M.C.), [Grandmaster Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_Flash), the [Furious Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_Flash_and_the_Furious_Five), [N.W.A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N.W.A), [LL Cool J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL_Cool_J), [De La Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Soul), [A Tribe Called Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Tribe_Called_Quest), [Ice-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice-T), [DJ Jazzy Jeff & The Fresh Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Jazzy_Jeff_&_The_Fresh_Prince), [Tone Lōc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tone_L%C5%8Dc), [Biz Markie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biz_Markie), [The Sugar Hill Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sugar_Hill_Gang) and others experienced success in this genre.

[Country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music) advanced into a new realm of popularity with youth appeal and record-breaking marks. The [techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno) style of electronic dance music emerged in [Detroit](8687.md), [Michigan](18859.md), during the mid to late 1980s. The [house music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_music) style, another form of electronic dance music, emerged in [Chicago](6886.md), [Illinois](14849.md), in the early 1980s. It was initially popularized in mid-1980s discothèques catering to the African-American, Latino and gay communities, first in Chicago, then in New York City and Detroit. It eventually reached Europe before becoming infused in mainstream pop and dance music worldwide.

Leading [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) bands included [Black Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Flag_\(band\)), [Minor Threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Threat), [Suicidal Tendencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicidal_Tendencies) and [Minutemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minutemen_\(band\)). Punk rock gave birth to many subgenres like [hardcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_punk), which in turn gave birth to a few counterculture movements, most notably the [Straight Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Edge) movement which began in the early 1980s. The 1980s also saw the birth of the [grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge) genre, with the arrival of such bands as [Soundgarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundgarden) and [Nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_\(band\)).

**The film industry**

The 1980s saw the return of studio-driven films, coming from the filmmaker-driven [New Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hollywood) era of the 1970s.[^cite_note-61] The period was when '[high concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_concept)' films gained popularity, where movies were to be easily marketable and understandable, and, therefore, they had short cinematic [plots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plot_\(narrative\)) that could be summarized in one or two sentences. The modern Hollywood [blockbuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockbuster_\(entertainment\)) is the most popular film format from the 1980s. Producer [Don Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Simpson)[^cite_note-62] is usually credited with the creation of the high-concept picture of the modern Hollywood blockbuster. In the mid-1980s, a wave of British directors, including [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott), [Alan Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Parker), [Adrian Lyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Lyne) and [Tony Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Scott) (with the latter directing a number of Don Simpson films) ushered in a new era of blockbusters using the crowd-pleasing skills they had honed in UK television commercials.[^cite_note-63]

A significant development in the home media business is the establishment of [The Criterion Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Criterion_Collection) in 1984, an American company "dedicated to gathering the greatest films from around the world and publishing them in editions that offer the highest technical quality". Through their releases, they were able to introduce what is now a standard to home video: [letterboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letterboxing_\(filming\)) to retain the original aspect ratio, [film commentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_commentary) and supplements/special features.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Television

Main article: [1980s in television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_in_television)

Music video channel [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV) was launched in the United States in 1981 and had a profound impact on the [music industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_industry) and [popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_culture), especially in the 1980s and early 1990s.

The 1980s was a decade of transformation in television. [Cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) became more accessible and therefore, more popular. By the middle of the decade, almost 70% of the US population had cable television and over 85% were paying for cable services such as [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) or [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_\(TV_network\)).[^cite_note-68] People who lived in rural areas where cable TV service was not available could still access cable channels through a large (and expensive) [satellite dish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_dish), which, by the mid-1990s, was phased out in favor of the small rooftop dishes that offer [DirecTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirecTV) and [Dish Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dish_Network) services.

[CNN](62028.md) and [Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bravo_\(American_TV_network\)) began in 1980; [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4), [Rete 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rete_4) and [Italia 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italia_1) in 1982; [RTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTL_\(German_TV_channel\)) and [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_\(French_TV_channel\)) in 1984; [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) in 1986; [M6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_\(TV_channel\)) in 1987; [Turner Network Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Network_Television) in 1988; [CNBC](223441.md) and [ProSieben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProSieben) in 1989.

New [prime-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime-time_television) [soap operas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_opera) included *[Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_\(TV_series\))*, its spin-off *[Knots Landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knots_Landing)*, *[Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty_\(1981_TV_series\))*, *[Falcon Crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Crest)*, *[EastEnders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EastEnders)* and *[Neighbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbours)*. Magic

[Larry Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Bird) (left) and [Magic Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Johnson), the two most popular NBA players of the 1980s.

[^cite_note-72]

- The [1980 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics) in Moscow were [boycotted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics_boycott) by 65 countries led by the United States in protest of the 1979 [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Afghanistan).

- A young United States team famously defeated the heavily favored Soviet team in the [Miracle on Ice](295721.md) game, and went on to win the gold medal for ice hockey, at the [1980 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Winter_Olympics).

- The [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) won the [Stanley Cup](66968.md) for four straight years in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Stanley_Cup_Final), [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Stanley_Cup_Final), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Stanley_Cup_Final), and [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Stanley_Cup_Final). Since their last Cup win in 1983, they were the third NHL team to win four consecutive championships and hold the NHL record for most consecutive playoff series' wins at 19 (stretching from the [1980 Playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) to the [1984 Playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Stanley_Cup_Final)).

- The [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Eskimos) of the [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) won the first three [Grey Cup](230107.md) championships of the decade (having won the last two of the previous decade), adding one more in 1987.

- India won the [1983 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Cricket_World_Cup). Australia won [1987 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Cricket_World_Cup).

- The [1984 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics) were held in [Sarajevo](26786.md), [Yugoslavia](34244.md) (now Sarajevo, [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md)). Yugoslavia became the second communist country to host the [Olympic Games](22576.md), but unlike the Soviet Union in 1980, there were no boycotts of the Games by Western countries.

- The [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) in Los Angeles were boycotted by the Soviet Union and most of the Communist world (China, Romania, and Yugoslavia participated in the games) in retaliation for the boycott of the 1980 Games in Moscow.

- The [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) were held in [Seoul](19159283.md), South Korea. Attempts to include North Korea in the games were unsuccessful and it boycotted along with six other countries, but with 160 nations participating, it had the highest attendance of any Olympics to date.

Popular video games include *[Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man)*, *[Super Mario Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros.)*, *[Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_\(1980_video_game\))*, *[The Legend of Zelda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda)*, *[Final Fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_\(video_game\))*, *[Dragon Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Quest)*, *[Out Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_Run)*, *[Manic Miner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manic_Miner)*, *[Donkey Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_\(arcade_game\))*, *[Frogger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogger)*, and *[Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris)*. *Pac-Man* was the first game to achieve widespread popularity in mainstream culture and the first game character to be popular in his own right.

[Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega) released its 16-bit console, [Mega Drive/Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Drive), in 1988 in Japan and in North America in 1989. In 1989, Nintendo released the [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy), a monochrome handheld console.

The beginning of the decade saw the continuation of the clothing styles of the [late 1970s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_in_fashion) and evolved into [heavy metal fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_fashion) by the end. However, fashion became more extravagant during the 1980s. The 1980s included teased and colourfully dyed hair, ripped jeans, neon clothing and many colours and different designs which at first were not accepted.

Significant hairstyle trends of the 1980s include the [perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_\(hairstyle\)),[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] the [mullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullet_\(haircut\)),[^cite_note-79] the [Jheri curl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jheri_curl), [big hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_hair),[^cite_note-80] the [Seiko-chan cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiko-chan_cut)[^cite_note-81] and the [hi-top fade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi-top_fade).

Significant clothing trends of the 1980s include [shoulder pads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_pads_\(fashion\)#1980s), [jean jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denim), [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather) pants, leather [aviator jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator_jacket), [jumpsuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumpsuit), [Members Only jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_Only_\(fashion_brand\)), [skin-tight acid-washed jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slim-fit_pants#The_1980s), [Izod Lacoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izod_Lacoste) and "preppy" [polo shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo_shirt), [leggings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leggings) and [leg warmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leg_warmer) (popularized in the film *[Flashdance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashdance)*), off-the-shoulder shirts, and cut sweatshirts (popularized in the same film).

[Miniskirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniskirt) returned to mainstream fashion in the mid-1980s after a ten-year absence, mostly made of [denim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denim) material. From that point on, miniskirts and minidresses have remained in mainstream fashion to this day.

Makeup on the 1980s was aggressive, shining and colourful. Women emphasised their lips, eyebrows and cheeks with makeup. They used much [blush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouge_\(cosmetics\)) and [eyeliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_liner).

Additional trends of the 1980s include athletic [headbands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headband),[^cite_note-82] [Ray-Ban Aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray-Ban_Aviator) sunglasses (popularized in the film *[Top Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun)*), [Ray-Ban Wayfarer sunglasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray-Ban_Wayfarer) (popularized in the films *[Risky Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risky_Business)* and *[The Blues Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blues_Brothers_\(film\))* and the TV series *[Miami Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Vice)*)[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] and [Swatch watches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swatch_watch).[^cite_note-85] Girls and women also wore jelly shoes, large crucifix necklaces, and brassieres all inspired by Madonna's "[Like a Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Virgin_\(song\))" music video.

The [New Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Romantic) movement was a British style in fashion and music influenced by futurist [disco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

### Cultural start and end of the decade

Some sources claim the existence of a "long 1980s".[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] Dates given include, for example, mid 1970s to early 1990s,[^cite_note-90] 1976 to 1993[^cite_note-91] or 1994,[^cite_note-92] and 1979 to 1990[^cite_note-93] or 1991[^cite_note-94] or after 2000.[^cite_note-95]

The *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times)* declared the 1977 *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_\(film\))* as the first movie of the eighties.[^cite_note-96] A shift in television happened in the late 1970s as well: of the top shows considered to be "shows of the 1980s", more began 1978–1979 than began 1980–1981.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] 1977 also saw[^cite_note-99] the introduction of [ROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROM) cartridge-based [video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console), with the Atari Video Computer System, the [Fairchild Channel F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Channel_F), and the [Bally Astrocade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally_Astrocade), as well as seeing the introduction of the first [mass-produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production) home computers, with the Apple II, the [TRS-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRS-80), and the [Commodore PET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_PET).

Some consider the 1980s to have ended with the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989,[^cite_note-100] or with the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.[^cite_note-101] Reagan's last day in office 20 January 1989, marked the "end of an era".[^cite_note-102] Music saw a change, with the premier of *[Yo! MTV Raps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo!_MTV_Raps)* on 6 August 1988. On the religious front, 1988 also saw the "unraveling of the decade's conservative dominance" with the release of *[The Last Temptation of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Temptation_of_Christ_\(film\))* and the three [televangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televangelism) scandals of [Jim Bakker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bakker#PTL), [Jimmy Swaggart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Swaggart#1988_prostitution_scandal), and [Oral Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_Roberts#Ministry_and_university).[^cite_note-103] The years 1988–1993 were a cultural bridge between the politically conservative 1980s and the [Internet boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_World_Wide_Web) of the 1990s, which began with the release of [Mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_\(web_browser\)) in 1993.[^cite_note-104]
