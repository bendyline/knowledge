**Richard Donald Marles** (born 13 July 1967) is an Australian politician and lawyer who has served as the 19th [deputy prime minister of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_prime_minister_of_Australia) and the [minister for defence](593152.md) since 2022. He has been the [deputy leader of the Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaders_of_the_Australian_Labor_Party#Leader) since 2019 and the [member of parliament](98066.md#Australia) (MP) for the [Victorian](4689460.md) division of [Corio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Corio) since 2007.

Marles grew up in [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong), Victoria, and is a lawyer by profession. He was assistant secretary of the [Australian Council of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Council_of_Trade_Unions) from 2000 to 2007. He was elected to the [House of Representatives](61565.md) at the [2007 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Australian_federal_election), after defeating incumbent Labor MP [Gavan O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavan_O'Connor) for preselection in the seat of [Corio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Corio). Marles was made a [parliamentary secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_secretary) in 2009 and briefly served as [Minister for Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Trade_\(Australia\)) in 2013, having supported [Kevin Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Rudd)'s return as prime minister. He was appointed to [shadow cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_cabinet_of_Australia) after the ALP's defeat at the [2013 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Australian_federal_election) and became a senior figure in the [Labor Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Right) faction.

Marles was elected deputy leader under [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) after the [2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_federal_election), becoming deputy [opposition leader](579586.md). Following the ALP's victory at the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_federal_election) he was appointed Deputy Prime Minister and Defence Minister.

## Early life

Marles was born on 13 July 1967 in [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong), Victoria.[^cite_note-aph-1] He is the son of Donald Marles,[^cite_note-2] a former headmaster of [Trinity Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Grammar_School_\(Victoria\)), and [Fay Marles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fay_Marles) ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Pearce), Victoria's first [Equal Opportunity Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Equal_Opportunity_and_Human_Rights_Commission) and later [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_\(education\)) of the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne).[^cite_note-bramston-3]

Marles was educated at [Geelong Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong_Grammar_School), where his father had been a house master,[^cite_note-4] and the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), where he was a resident of [Ormond College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormond_College). He joined the Melbourne University Labor Club in his first week at university[^cite_note-bramston-3] and served as president of the [Melbourne University Student Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne_Student_Union) in 1988.[^cite_note-5] He was also the general secretary of the [National Union of Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Students_\(Australia\)) in 1989. He graduated with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) and [Bachelor of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws) with Honours and began his career as a solicitor with Melbourne industrial law firm [Slater and Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slater_and_Gordon_Lawyers). In 1994, he became legal officer for the [Transport Workers Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Workers_Union_of_Australia) (TWU) and was elected TWU national assistant secretary four years later. In 2000, he joined Australia's peak union body, the [Australian Council of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Council_of_Trade_Unions), as assistant secretary, remaining in the position until 2007.[^cite_note-bramston-3]

## Political career



Marles visiting Dadaab Refugee Camp in Kenya, in 2012

### Early career

In March 2006, Marles nominated for Labor preselection against the sitting member for Corio, [Gavan O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavan_O'Connor), as part of a challenge to several sitting members organised by the right-wing [Labor Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Unity) faction of the party. In the local ballot Marles polled 57% of the vote, and his endorsement was then confirmed by the party's public office selection committee.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Marles was elected member for Corio on 24 November 2007 in the election that returned the Labor Party to office under the leadership of Kevin Rudd. From February 2008 to June 2009 he was chair of the House of Representatives Standing Committee on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Affairs.

### Parliamentary secretary and Minister for Trade



Marles visiting [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), United States, in February 2025

In June 2009 Marles was appointed Parliamentary Secretary for Innovation and Industry. He retained his seat in the 2010 election and was sworn in as Parliamentary Secretary for Pacific Island Affairs in the [First Gillard Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Gillard_Ministry) on 14 September 2010.[^cite_note-8] In July 2011, Marles became the first Australian member of parliament to visit [Wallis and Futuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallis_and_Futuna).[^cite_note-tahitipresse-9] Marles arrived in Wallis and Futuna to attend a ceremony with King [Kapiliele Faupala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapiliele_Faupala) in [Mata-Utu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mata-Utu) marking the 50th anniversary of the islands' status as a French [Overseas collectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_collectivity).[^cite_note-tahitipresse-9] Marles had previously visited [New Caledonia](21342.md) in October 2010 and [French Polynesia](10737.md) in March 2011.[^cite_note-tahitipresse-9]

In the [ministerial reshuffle of 2 March 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Gillard_Ministry#December_2011_to_March_2012), Marles was given the additional role of Parliamentary Secretary for Foreign Affairs.[^cite_note-10] On 21 March 2013 he resigned these roles after expressing support for [Kevin Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Rudd) to challenge Julia Gillard for the leadership; a challenge that did not eventuate.[^cite_note-11]

In June 2013, he was appointed the Minister for Trade and a member of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Australia),[^cite_note-dpmc-12] succeeding [Craig Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Emerson), who resigned following the [June 2013 leadership spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_spill,_June_2013) that saw Kevin Rudd defeat Julia Gillard for leadership of the Labor Party.

### Shadow minister

After the ALP's defeat at the [2013 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Australian_federal_election), Marles was appointed Shadow Minister for Immigration and Border Protection under opposition leader [Bill Shorten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Shorten).[^cite_note-aph-1] In February 2016, he began co-hosting the weekly television program *[Pyne & Marles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyne_&_Marles)* on [Sky News Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News_Live) with [Liberal](18453.md) MP [Christopher Pyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Pyne).[^cite_note-13] Marles had his portfolio changed after the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_federal_election), becoming Shadow Minister for Defence.[^cite_note-aph-1] He has been cited as holding pro-U.S. views and as "somewhat of a [hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_hawk)".[^cite_note-bramston-3][^cite_note-AFR_19_Dec-14]

### Deputy Leader of the Opposition

In May 2019, after Labor lost the [2019 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_federal_election), it was reported that Marles would stand for the deputy leadership of the party, and would likely be elected unopposed following [Clare O'Neil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_O'Neil)'s decision not to run.[^cite_note-15] He was formally endorsed as deputy to [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) on 30 May, and selected the portfolio of Defence in the shadow cabinet.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

Following a shadow cabinet reshuffle in January 2021, Marles was placed in charge of a new "super portfolio" relating to recovery from the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Australia),[^cite_note-18] encompassing a "broad brief across national reconstruction, jobs, skills, small business and science".[^cite_note-19]

### Deputy Prime Minister and Defence Minister



Marles and Philippine Defence Secretary [Gilbert Teodoro](21940341.md) witness joint [Australia–Philippine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Philippines_relations) [military exercises](1225714.md) in [San Vicente, Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vicente,_Palawan), Philippines, in August 2025

Two days after the [2022 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_federal_election), Albanese had himself, Marles and three other senior Labor frontbenchers sworn in as an interim five-person government. Although counting was still underway, it was apparent by this time that no other party could realistically form even a minority government. As Albanese flew to Tokyo to take part in the Quad soon after being sworn in, Marles served as Acting Prime Minister until Albanese returned to lead the nation full-time. He continues to return to the role whenever Albanese leaves the country.[^cite_note-20]

On 1 June 2022, Marles was sworn in as [Deputy Prime Minister of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia) and [Minister for Defence](593152.md).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] One of his early decisions was relocating 500 Australian troops to [Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville) over the course of six years from 2025 to strengthen the [Australian Army](2795.md)'s ability to conduct operations missions in the [Pacific](23070.md).[^cite_note-23] This announcement was criticised by the city's mayor [Jenny Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Hill_\(politician\)) who claimed that [Townsville City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville_City_Council) was not consulted about military personnel's housing.[^cite_note-24] On 29 September 2023, Marles announced that the [Australian Defence Force](67538.md) would retire its fleet of [MRH-90 Taipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRH-90) helicopters following a fatal crash during [Exercise Talisman Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Talisman_Sabre) in July 2023 which killed four military personnel.[^cite_note-25]

In September 2023, Albanese and Philippine President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) agreed to have their respective defence ministers (Marles and [Gilbert Teodoro](21940341.md)) meet annually due to "rising security challenges" in the [Indo-Pacific](330456.md).[^cite_note-26]

In early February 2024, Marles and [Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_\(Australia\)) [Penny Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Wong) hosted New Zealand [Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(New_Zealand\)) [Winston Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Peters) and [Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(New_Zealand\)) [Judith Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Collins) at a joint bilateral meeting of foreign and defence ministers in [Melbourne](17306237.md). Marles confirmed that Australia would send officials to brief their New Zealand counterparts about [AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUKUS) Pillar Two, which would focus on advanced military technology including quantum computing and artificial intelligence. New Zealand is not expected to join AUKUS Pillar One due to its [nuclear-free policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_nuclear-free_zone). The two governments also agreed to deepen bilateral security and military cooperation.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

On 6 May 2024, Marles denounced an incident in which a Chinese [J-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-10) fighter jet dropped flares in close proximity to an Australian [MH60R Seahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_MH-60_Jayhawk) helicopter.[^cite_note-29] The helicopter had been operating off [HMAS *Hobart*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Hobart_\(DDG_39\)) in international waters in the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea) while enforcing [United Nations](31769.md)-mandated sanctions against [North Korea](21255.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

On 19 June 2024, Marles, Wong and [Minister for International Development and the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_International_Development_and_the_Pacific) [Pat Conroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Conroy_\(politician\)) attended the 30th Australia-Papua New Guinea Ministerial Forum in [Port Moresby](57387.md).[^cite_note-32] During the visit, Marles acknowledged the [2024 Enga landslide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Enga_landslide) that occurred in late May 2024 and confirmed that the Australian delegation would travel there. The Albanese government also confirmed plans to sign a bilateral security agreement with [Papua New Guinea](22943.md).[^cite_note-33]

On 11 July 2024, Marles announced that Australia would provide Ukraine with \$250 million in military assistance, the largest single military package from Australia to Ukraine since the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022.[^cite_note-34]



Marles with US Secretary of Defense [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) and Japanese Minister of Defense [Gen Nakatani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Nakatani) in May 2025

On 12 September 2024, Marles stripped nine commanding officers who served in the [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md) of their distinguished service medals, implementing the final recommendation of the [Brereton Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brereton_Report) which had found "credible evidence" that Australian soldiers had unlawfully killed 39 people.[^cite_note-35]

On 20 February 2025, Marles and his New Zealand counterpart Collins confirmed that the Australian and [New Zealand militaries](21359.md) were monitoring three Chinese warships that were sailing through [Australia's exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Australia) near Sydney.[^cite_note-36] After the Chinese warships commenced [live-fire exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Chinese_naval_exercises_in_the_Tasman_Sea) in the [Tasman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_Sea), Marles criticised the Chinese government for not giving the Australian government prior notice of the first live fire exercise, describing them as "disconcerting for planes that were in the air." Australian civil aviation and defence authorities had only learnt about the exercise ten minutes after initial contact between the Chinese warships and a [Virgin Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Australia) pilot nearby.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

After US Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) initiated a review of AUKUS, Marles stated that it was "totally understandable" that an incoming government would like to conduct a review.[^cite_note-39] In July 2025, Marles and UK Defense Minister [John Healey](434625.md) signed a fifty-year nuclear submarine treaty.[^cite_note-40]

In 2025, the [Albanese government](70837765.md) was criticized for supplying parts for [F-35 fighter jets](11812.md) to the global supply chain that can be accessed by [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Marles said that Australia was part of the F-35 supply chains "that are organised by [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) in the United States and have multiple supplies in respect of all of those supply chains."[^cite_note-43]

On 24 August 2025, Marles witnessed the 2025 Exercise Amphibious and Land Operations (ALON) [military exercises](1225714.md) between Australian and [Philippine forces](23448.md) held in [Palawan](240395.md), Philippines, which involved over 3,600 personnel—Australia's largest deployment of troops to Southeast Asia since the [2006 crisis in Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_crisis_in_Timor-Leste).[^cite_note-44] Marles said that the exercises intend to "uphold the [rules-based order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules-based_order) in this \[Indo-Pacific] region."[^cite_note-45] He also announced that a new [defence pact](4991894.md) between [Australia and the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Philippines_relations) will be signed in 2026.[^cite_note-46]

## Political positions

Marles is a senior figure in his state's [Labor Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Right) faction.[^cite_note-AFR_19_Dec-14]

### Refugees and asylum seekers

Marles supports the turning back of asylum seekers who arrive in Australia by boat and a Pacific Solution for the resettlement of refugees.[^cite_note-47]

Marles was supportive of the [Australian War Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_War_Memorial) commemorating [Operation Sovereign Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sovereign_Borders) navy personnel who undertook activities to stop asylum seekers coming to Australia by boat. That position was criticised by several [Labor Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Left) MPs as well as the [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Greens).[^cite_note-48]

### National defence



Marles (center) speaks with US Secretary of Defense [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin) (left) and Japanese Minister of Defense [Yasukazu Hamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasukazu_Hamada) (right) in 2022

In 2020, as shadow defence minister, Marles was critical of the Morrison government's handling of the programme to purchase French submarines, which, he said, had "profoundly compromised" Australia's national security. Marles otherwise supported the bipartisan consensus on national defence matters.[^cite_note-49]

### Fossil fuels and energy

On an interview on Sky News on 20 February 2019, Marles stated that it would be "a good thing" if the thermal coal market in Australia collapsed.[^cite_note-50] He later back-tracked on this statement, saying that his "attack on coal was tone-deaf".[^cite_note-51]

Following the 2019 Federal Election, Marles maintained that public funds should not be used to subsidise coal, saying "a Labor government is not going to put a cent into subsidising coal-fired power", and the market should be allowed to make its own decisions, while also saying that if a private company decided to push forward with a mine and gained the necessary approvals that Labor would not stand in its way.[^cite_note-52]

## Personal life

Marles lives in Geelong with his wife Rachel Schutze. He has three children from his current marriage and one from his first marriage to [Lisa Neville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Neville), who was elected to the [Victorian Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Legislative_Assembly) in 2002 and later became a state minister.[^cite_note-53]

Marles is a supporter and member of the [Geelong Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong_Football_Club).[^cite_note-54]
