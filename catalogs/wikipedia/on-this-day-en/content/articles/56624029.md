**Abiy Ahmed Ali**[^cite_note-1] (born 15 August 1976) is an Ethiopian politician and former military officer who is the current [prime minister of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Ethiopia) since 2018. He is also the leader of the [Prosperity Party](62842687.md) since 2019.[^cite_note-Prime_Minister-2][^cite_note-16th-3]

Abiy joined the rebel forces fighting against the regime of [Mengistu Haile Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam) in his teens and after the [collapse of the Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Derg_regime) in 1991, became a Lieutenant Colonel in the [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md) (ENDF). He served as a military radio operator during the [Badme War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War) against [Eritrea](17238590.md) from 1998 to 2000[^cite_note-\:7-4] and soon after converted to [Pentecostal Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism).[^cite_note-5] He later rose through the ranks of government via the [Information Network Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Network_Security_Agency) (INSA), which was established in 2006.[^cite_note-\:43-6] In the 2010 national election, Abiy became an elected member of the [Ethiopian parliament](4376253.md), representing the district of [Agaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaro). He was then elected Prime Minister by the House of Peoples' Representatives in 2018, following the dissolution of the [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF) which had governed [Ethiopia](187749.md) for 28 years.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-chair-8]

Abiy initially embarked on sweeping political reforms, releasing thousands of political prisoners and unbanning opposition groups. He was awarded the [2019 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Nobel_Peace_Prize) "for his efforts to achieve peace and international cooperation, and in particular for his decisive initiative to resolve the [border conflict with neighbouring Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_border_conflict)".[^cite_note-NOBEL-9] As part of his economic reforms, Abiy pursued large-scale privatisation of state-owned enterprises and liberalised several key sectors, including the [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md). In 2019, he disbanded the EPRDF and formed his own party, the [Prosperity Party](62842687.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Following rising ethnic and political tensions in 2020, the [Tigray People's Liberation Front](1276759.md) (TPLF) [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Command_attacks_\(Ethiopia\)) the [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md) (ENDF) Northern Command, starting the 2-year [Tigray War](65760352.md) between the combined forces of the ENDF and the [Eritrean army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Defence_Forces) against forces loyal to the TPLF and the [Oromo Liberation Army](41461749.md) (OLA). The war resulted in severe humanitarian crises, widespread displacement, and thousands of deaths.[^cite_note-12] After the [Pretoria Agreement](72171064.md) ended the Tigray War, Abiy began an effort in 2023 to consolidate all remaining regional militias into the ENDF. [Fano](65998148.md) rebuffed requests to disarm and integrate into federal forces, instead attacking the ENDF and beginning the [War in Amhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Amhara).

Since 2019, Ethiopia has undergone [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding_in_Ethiopia) under Abiy's premiership,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] marked by severe [human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Ethiopia), [media censorship](5967520.md), [internet shutdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_Ethiopia), [civil conflicts](67622911.md), systematic persecution and ethnic violence in the [Tigray](357350.md), [Amhara](1746709.md) and [Oromia](503738.md) regions.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] [Politically motivated purges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression_under_Abiy_Ahmed) also became common and [many journalists and activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_detained_journalists_and_activists_in_Ethiopia_\(2023\)) were arrested by police for alleged breach of "constitutional laws".[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Early life

Abiy Ahmed was born in the small town of [Beshasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beshasha), Ethiopia.[^cite_note-abiyot-20][^cite_note-Prime_Minister-2][^cite_note-DW\.COM-21][^cite_note-Girma_2015-22] His father, Ahmed Ali, was a respected [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_people) elder who donated much of to land to build a mosque during the reign of [Haile Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie). While his mother, Tezeta Wolde, lived in a dilapidated house and sold traditional *[tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej)* wine for a living.[^cite_note-Hurst-23] In a 2018 interview with *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, Abiy stated that his mother "was [Amhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amhara_people) and [Orthodox Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Orthodox_Tewahedo_Church)" and "converted to Islam when she married".[^cite_note-NYT2018-24] However, in a 2021 [Oromia Broadcasting Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromia_Broadcasting_Network) interview redistributed on [YouTube](3524766.md), Abiy asserted that his parents were both Oromo.[^cite_note-\:6-25]

Abiy is the 13th child of his father and the sixth and youngest child of his mother, the fourth of his father's four wives.[^cite_note-abiyot-20] His childhood name was *Abiyot* (English: "Revolution"). The name was sometimes given to children in the aftermath of the [Ethiopian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Revolution) in the mid-1970s.[^cite_note-abiyot-20] As a child, Abiy was sent to [Agaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaro) to attend the local primary school and later continued his studies at local secondary school in Agaro town. According to several personal reports, Abiy seems to have earned a reputation as a troublemaker with a poor record in school. He later dropped out in the seventh grade and then moved to [Addis Ababa](52593.md).[^cite_note-abiyot-20][^cite_note-Hurst-23]

## Military and intelligence career

At the age of 14, in early 1991,[^cite_note-26] he joined the armed struggle against the [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) [regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Republic_of_Ethiopia) of [Mengistu Haile Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam) after the death of his oldest brother. He was a child soldier, affiliated to the [Oromo People's Democratic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_Democratic_Party) (OPDO), which at that time was a tiny organization of only around 200 fighters part of the large coalition army of the EPRDF which had over 100,000 fighters that resulted in the regime's fall later that year.[^cite_note-prelude-27][^cite_note-abiyot-20][^cite_note-\:0-28] As there were only so few OPDO fighters in an army with its core of about 90,000 [Tigrayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayans), Abiy quickly had to learn the [Tigrinya language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language). As a speaker of Tigrinya in a security apparatus dominated by Tigrayans, he could move forward with his military career.[^cite_note-prelude-27]

After the [fall of the Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Derg), he took formal military training from Assefa Brigade in [West Wollega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welega_Province) and was stationed there. Later on in 1993 he became a soldier in the now [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md) and worked mostly in the intelligence and communications departments. In 1995, after the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md), he was deployed as a member of the [United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Assistance_Mission_for_Rwanda) (UNAMIR) in the country's capital, [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali).[^cite_note-29]

In the [Eritrean–Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War) that occurred between 1998 and 2000, he led an intelligence team to discover positions of the [Eritrean Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Defence_Forces).[^cite_note-BBC50013273-30] During the final days of the war in June 2000, he narrowly escaped an Eritrean [artillery](2508.md) attack which had killed most his unit. Abiy later recounted to fellow churchmen that it had been the moment he had a [revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revelation) and decided to convert to [Pentecostal Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism).[^cite_note-\:7-4]

Later on, Abiy was posted back to his home town of Beshasha, where he – as an officer of the Defense Forces – had to address a critical situation of inter-religious clashes between Muslims and Christians with a number of deaths.[^cite_note-prelude-27][^cite_note-31] He brought calm and peace in a situation of communal tensions accompanying the clashes.[^cite_note-prelude-27] In later years, following his election as an MP, he continued these efforts to bring about reconciliation between the religions through the creation of the Religious Forum for Peace.[^cite_note-BBC50013273-30]

### INSA

In 2006, Abiy was one of the co-founders of the [Information Network Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Network_Security_Agency) (INSA), where he worked in different positions.[^cite_note-abiyot-20] The Ethiopian government established INSA with support from the [United States](3434750.md), modeling it after the American's [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA). Initially the agency was tasked with [intercepting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) and [analyzing intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_analysis), particularly from neighboring [Somalia](27358.md) at the time of the US backed [2006 invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)), but the agency gradually expanded its role into domestic surveillance. Through INSA, Abiy Ahmed ascended through the ranks of government and became influential politically.[^cite_note-\:43-6] For two years, he was acting director of INSA due to the director's [leave of absence](507071.md).[^cite_note-abiyot-20] In this capacity, he was board member of several government agencies working on information and communications, like [Ethio telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethio_telecom) and [Ethiopian Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Broadcasting_Corporation). He attained the rank of [Lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel)[^cite_note-cve-32][^cite_note-prelude-27] before deciding in 2010 to leave the military and his post as deputy director of INSA to become a politician.

## Political career

### Member of Parliament

Abiy started his political career as a member of the [Oromo Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_Democratic_Party) (ODP).[^cite_note-33] The ODP has been the ruling party in [Oromia Region](503738.md) since 1991 and also one of four coalition parties of the ruling coalition in Ethiopia, the [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF). He became a member of the central committee of ODP and congress member of the executive committee of the EPRDF in quick succession.[^cite_note-prelude-27] In the 2010 national election, Abiy represented the [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Ethiopia) of [Agaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaro) and became an elected member of the [House of Peoples' Representatives](4501670.md), the lower chamber of the Ethiopian [Federal Parliamentary Assembly](4376253.md). Before and during his time of parliamentary service, there were several religious clashes among Muslims and Christians in [Jimma Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimma_Zone). Some of these confrontations turned violent and resulted in the loss of life and property. Abiy, as an elected member of parliament took a proactive role in working with several religious institutions and elders to bring about reconciliation in the zone. He helped set up a forum entitled "Religious Forum for Peace"*,* an outcome of the need to devise a sustainable resolution mechanism to restore peaceful Muslim-Christian community interaction in the region.[^cite_note-cve-32]

In 2014, during his time in parliament, Abiy became the director-general of a new and in 2011 founded Government Research Institute called [Science and Technology Information Center (STIC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_Technology_Information_Center_\(Ethiopia\)).[^cite_note-abiyot-20][^cite_note-\:3-34] The following year, Abiy became an executive member of ODP. The same year he was elected to the House of Peoples' Representatives for a second term, this time for his home [woreda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woreda) of [Gomma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomma_\(woreda\)).[^cite_note-EritreaHub-35]

### Rise to power

Starting from 2015, Abiy became one of the central figures in the violent fight against illegal [land grabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grabbing) activities in Oromia Region and especially around [Addis Ababa](52593.md). Although the Addis Ababa Master Plan at the heart of the land-grabbing plans was stopped in 2016, the disputes continued for some time resulting in injuries and deaths.[^cite_note-aamp-36] It was this fight against land-grabbing, that finally boosted Abiy Ahmed's political career, brought him into the spotlight and allowed him to climb the political ladder.[^cite_note-prelude-27]

In October 2015, Abiy became the Ethiopian [Minister of Science and Technology (MoST)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science_and_Technology,_Ethiopia), a post which he left after only 12 months. From October 2016 on, Abiy served as Deputy President of [Oromia Region](503738.md) as part of the team of Oromia Region's president [Lemma Megersa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemma_Megersa) while staying a member of the Ethiopian Federal House of Peoples' Representatives.[^cite_note-\:4-37][^cite_note-38] Abiy Ahmed also became the head of the Oromia Urban Development and Planning Office. In this role, Abiy was expected to be the major driving force behind Oromia Economic Revolution, Oromia Land and Investment reform, youth employment as well as resistance to widespread land grabbing in Oromia region.[^cite_note-39] As one of his duties in office, he took care of the one million displaced [Oromo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_people) displaced from the [Somali Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Region) from the 2017 unrest.[^cite_note-40]

As head of the ODP Secretariat from October 2017, Abiy facilitated the formation of a new alliance between the [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_people) and [Amhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amhara_people) groups, which together constitute two-thirds of the Ethiopian population.[^cite_note-41]

In early 2018, many political observers considered Abiy and Lemma Megersa as the most popular politicians within the Oromo community, as well as other Ethiopian communities.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] This came after several years of unrest in Ethiopia. But despite this favourable rating for Abiy Ahmed and Lemma Megersa, young people from the Oromia region called for immediate action without delays to bring fundamental change and freedom to Oromia Region and Ethiopia – otherwise more unrest was to be expected.[^cite_note-aamp-36] According to Abiy himself, people are asking for a different rhetoric, with an open and respectful discussion in the political space to allow political progress and to win people for democracy instead of pushing them.[^cite_note-aamp-36]

Until early 2018, Abiy continued to serve as head of the ODP secretariat and of the Oromia Housing and Urban Development Office and as Deputy President of Oromia Region. He left all these posts after his election as the leader of the [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-\:4-37]

### EPRDF leadership election

Following three years of protest and unrest, on 15 February 2018 the Ethiopian Prime Minister, [Hailemariam Desalegn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailemariam_Desalegn), announced his resignation – which included his resignation from the post of EPRDF chairman. With the EPRDF's large majority in Parliament, its EPRDF chairman was all but assured of becoming the next Prime Minister. The EPRDF chairman, on the other hand, is one of the heads of the four parties that make up the ruling coalition: [Oromo Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_Democratic_Party) (ODP), [Amhara Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amhara_Democratic_Party) (ADP), [Southern Ethiopian People's Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ethiopian_People's_Democratic_Movement) (SEPDM) and [Tigray People's Liberation Front](1276759.md) (TPLF).[^cite_note-45]

Hailemariam's resignation triggered the first ever contested leadership election among EPRDF coalition members to replace him. A lot of political observers made Lemma Megersa (the ODP chairman) and Abiy Ahmed the front-runners to become the Leader of the ruling coalition and eventually [Prime Minister of Ethiopia](1768778.md). Despite being the clear favorite for the general public, Lemma Megersa was not a member of the national parliament, a requirement to become Prime Minister as required by the Ethiopian constitution. Therefore, Lemma Megersa was excluded from the leadership race.[^cite_note-Cameroun-46] On 22 February 2018, Lemma Megersa's party, ODP, called for an emergency executive committee meeting and replaced him as Chairman of ODP with Abiy Ahmed, who was a member of parliament. Some observers saw that as a strategic move by the ODP to retain its leadership role within the coalition and to promote Abiy Ahmed to become prime minister.[^cite_note-EritreaHub-35]

On 1 March 2018, the 180 EPRDF executive committee members started their meeting to elect the leader of the party. Each of the four parties sent in 45 members. The contest for the leadership was among Abiy Ahmed of ODP, [Demeke Mekonnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demeke_Mekonnen), the Deputy Prime Minister and ADP leader, [Shiferaw Shigute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiferaw_Shigute) as Chairman of SEPDM and [Debretsion Gebremichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debretsion_Gebremichael) as the Leader of TPLF. Despite being the overwhelming favorite by the majority of Ethiopians, Abiy Ahmed faced major opposition from TPLF and SEPDM members during the leadership discussions.[^cite_note-47]

On 27 March 2018, a few hours before the beginning of the leadership elections, Demeke Mekonnen, who had been seen as the major opponent to Abiy Ahmed, dropped out of the race. Many observers saw this as an endorsement of Abiy Ahmed. Demeke was then approved as deputy prime minister for another term. Following Demeke's exit, Abiy Ahmed received a presumably unanimous vote from both the ADP and ODP executive members, with 18 additional votes in a secret ballot coming from elsewhere. By midnight, Abiy Ahmed was declared chairman of the ruling coalition in Ethiopia, the EPRDF, and was considered as the Prime Minister Designate of Ethiopia by receiving 108 votes while Shiferaw Shigute received 58 and Debretsion Gebremichael received 2 votes.[^cite_note-chair-8] On 2 April 2018, Abiy Ahmed was elected as Prime Minister of Ethiopia by the House of Representatives and sworn in.[^cite_note-16th-3]

## Prime Minister of Ethiopia

Main article: [Premiership of Abiy Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Abiy_Ahmed)

On 2 April 2018, Abiy was confirmed and sworn in by the Ethiopian parliament as [Prime Minister of Ethiopia](1768778.md). During his acceptance speech, he promised political reform; to promote the unity of Ethiopia and unity among the peoples of Ethiopia; to reach out to the Eritrean government to resolve the ongoing Eritrean–Ethiopian border conflict after the [Eritrean–Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War) and to also reach out to the political opposition inside and outside of Ethiopia. His acceptance speech sparked optimism and received an overwhelmingly positive reaction from the Ethiopian public including the opposition groups inside and outside Ethiopia. Following his speech, his popularity and support across the country reached a historical high and some political observers argued that Abiy was overwhelmingly more popular than the ruling party coalition, the EPRDF.[^cite_note-chair-8]

### Domestic policy

Main article: [Early reforms of Abiy Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_reforms_of_Abiy_Ahmed)

Since taking office in April 2018, Abiy's government has presided over the release of thousands of political prisoners from Ethiopian jails and the rapid opening of the country's political landscape.[^cite_note-WaPO_editorial-48][^cite_note-Chatham_knack-49][^cite_note-end_of_SOE-50] In May 2018 alone the Oromo region pardoned over 7,600 prisoners.[^cite_note-51] On 29 May [Ginbot 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginbot_7) leader [Andargachew Tsege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andargachew_Tsege), facing the death penalty on terrorism charges, was released after being pardoned by President [Mulatu Teshome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulatu_Teshome), along with 575 other detainees.[^cite_note-Andy_BBC-52]

That same day, charges were dropped against Andargachew's colleague [Berhanu Nega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berhanu_Nega) and the Oromo dissident and public intellectual [Jawar Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawar_Mohammed), as well as their respectively affiliated US-based [ESAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESAT) and [OMN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromia_Media_Network) satellite television networks.[^cite_note-WAPO_charges_dropped-53] Shortly thereafter, Abiy took the "unprecedented and previously unimaginable" step of meeting Andargachew, who twenty-four hours previously had been on death row, at his office; a move even critics of the ruling party termed "bold and remarkable".[^cite_note-54] Abiy had previously met former [Oromo Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_Liberation_Front) leaders including founder [Lencho Letta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lencho_Letta), who had committed to peaceful participation in the political process, upon their arrival at [Bole International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bole_International_Airport).[^cite_note-55]

On 30 May 2018, it was announced the ruling party would amend the country's "draconian" anti-terrorism law, widely perceived as a tool of political repression. On 1 June 2018, Abiy announced the government would seek to end the state of emergency two months in advance of the expiration its six-month tenure, citing an improved domestic situation. On 4 June 2018, Parliament approved the necessary legislation, ending the state of emergency.[^cite_note-end_of_SOE-50] In his first briefing to the [House of Peoples' Representatives](4501670.md) in June 2018, Abiy countered criticism of his government's release of convicted "terrorists" which according to the opposition is just a name the [EPRDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPRDF) gives you if you are a part or even meet the "opposition". He argued that policies that sanctioned arbitrary detention and torture themselves constituted extra-constitutional acts of terror aimed at suppressing opposition.[^cite_note-End_of_torture-56] This followed the additional pardon of 304 prisoners (289 of which had been sentenced on terrorism-related charges) on 15 June.[^cite_note-terror_pardons-57]



Abiy speaking at the Green Legacy Initiative, Addis Ababa, 21 June 2024

The pace of [reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_reforms_of_Abiy_Ahmed) has revealed fissures within the ruling coalition, with hardliners in the military and the hitherto dominant [TPLF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPLF) said to be "seething" at the end of the state of emergency and the release of political prisoners.[^cite_note-58]

An editorial on the previously pro-government website Tigrai Online arguing for the maintenance of the state of emergency gave voice to this sentiment, saying that Abiy was "doing too much too fast".[^cite_note-Keep_SOE-59] Another article critical of the release of political prisoners suggested that Ethiopia's criminal justice system had become a revolving door and that Abiy's administration had quite inexplicably been rushing to pardon and release thousands of prisoners, among them many deadly criminals and dangerous arsonists.[^cite_note-60] On 13 June 2018, the TPLF executive committee denounced the decisions to hand over Badme and privatize [SOEs](204768.md) as "fundamentally flawed", saying that the ruling coalition suffered from a fundamental leadership deficit.[^cite_note-Standart_TPLF-61]

### Transparency

In 2018, to expand the [free press in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Ethiopia), Abiy invited exiled media outlets to return.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] One of the media outlets invited to return was [ESAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESAT) (which had called for the genocide of Ethiopian [Tigrayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayans)).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] However, since assuming office in April 2018, Abiy himself had, as of March 2019, only given one press conference,[^cite_note-72] on 25 August 2018 and around five months after he assumed office, where he answered questions from journalists. As of 21 March 2019, he has not given another press conference where he has not refused to answer questions from journalists (rather than reading prepared statements).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-no_to_press_Abiy_1-75]

According to the NGOs [Human Rights Watch](46530.md), [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists) and [Amnesty International](18947898.md), Abiy's government has since mid 2019 been arresting Ethiopian journalists and closing media outlets (except for ESAT-TV).[^cite_note-detained-journalists_1-76][^cite_note-detained-journalists_2-77][^cite_note-detained-journalists_3-78][^cite_note-detained-journalists_4-79][^cite_note-detained-journalists_5-80][^cite_note-detained-journalists_6-81] From the international media outlets, his government has suspended the press license of [Reuters](18998750.md)'s correspondent, and issued a warning letter to the correspondents of both [BBC](19344654.md) and [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle) for what the government described as "violation of the rules of media broadcasting".[^cite_note-journalists_suspended_1-82][^cite_note-Crisis_Group_ref_2-83][^cite_note-Human_Right_Watch_ref_1-84] As of June 2022, 18 journalists were arrested on allegation of "inciting violence" while reporting for independent media outlets or YouTube channels.[^cite_note-85]

### Economic reforms



Abiy has announced that [state-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprises) such as [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) are to be partially or wholly privatised.

In June 2018, the ruling coalition announced its intention to pursue the large-scale [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) of [state-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprises) and the liberalization of several key economic sectors long considered off-limits, marking a landmark shift in the country's state-oriented development model.[^cite_note-econ_lib-86]

State monopolies in the telecommunications, aviation, electricity, and logistics sectors are to be ended and those industries opened up to private sector competition.[^cite_note-eprdf_statement-87] Shares in the state-owned firms in those sectors, including [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md), Africa's largest and most profitable, are to be [offered for purchase](63879.md) to both domestic and foreign investors, although the government will continue to hold a majority share in these firms, thereby retaining control of the [commanding heights of the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_heights_of_the_economy).[^cite_note-privatization_hornaffairs-88] State-owned enterprises in sectors deemed less critical, including railway operators, sugar, industrial parks, hotels and various manufacturing firms, may be fully privatised.[^cite_note-89]

Aside from representing an ideological shift with respect to views on the degree of government control over the economy, the move was seen as a pragmatic measure aimed at improving the country's dwindling [foreign-exchange reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign-exchange_reserves), which by the end of the 2017 fiscal year were equal in value to less than two months worth of imports, as well as easing its growing [sovereign debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_debt) load.[^cite_note-privatization_hornaffairs-88][^cite_note-econ_lib-86]

In June 2018, Abiy announced the government's intention to establish an Ethiopian [stock exchange](28168.md) in tandem with the privatization of state-owned enterprises.[^cite_note-Parlia_session-90] As of 2015, Ethiopia was the largest country in the world, in terms of both population and gross domestic product, without a stock exchange.[^cite_note-91]

#### Djibouti and port agreements

Since taking power Abiy has pursued a policy of expanding landlocked Ethiopia's access to ports in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) region. Shortly before his assumption of office it was announced that the Ethiopian government would take a 19% stake in [Berbera Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera_Port) in the Somaliland region located in northern Somalia as part of a joint venture with [DP World](3272467.md).[^cite_note-AJ_Berbera-99] In May 2018, Ethiopia signed an agreement with the government of Djibouti to take an equity stake in the [Port of Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Djibouti), enabling Ethiopia to have a say in the port's development and the setting of port handling fees.[^cite_note-100]

Two days later a similar agreement was signed with the Sudanese government granting Ethiopia an ownership stake in the [Port Sudan](213572.md). The Ethio-Djibouti agreement grants the Djiboutian government the option of taking stakes in state-owned Ethiopian firms in return, such as the [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) and [Ethio Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethio_Telecom).[^cite_note-Reuters_port_Sudan-101] This in turn was followed shortly thereafter by an announcement that Abiy and Kenyan President [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta) had reached an agreement for the construction of an Ethiopian logistics facility at [Lamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamu) Port as part of the [Lamu Port and Lamu-Southern Sudan-Ethiopia Transport Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamu_Port_and_Lamu-Southern_Sudan-Ethiopia_Transport_Corridor) (LAPSSET) project.[^cite_note-Lamu_logistics-102] The potential normalization of [Ethiopia-Eritrea relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia-Eritrea_relations) likewise opens the possibility for Ethiopia to resume using the Ports of [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) and [Asseb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asseb), which, prior to the Ethio-Eritrean conflict, were its main ports, which would be of particular benefit to the northern region of [Tigray](357350.md).[^cite_note-econ_lib-86] All these developments would reduce Ethiopian reliance on Djibouti's port which, since 1998, has handled almost all of Ethiopia's maritime traffic.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-Reuters_port_Sudan-101]

#### Eritrea

Main articles: [Eritrea–Ethiopia relations](57858711.md) and [2018 Eritrea–Ethiopia summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Eritrea%E2%80%93Ethiopia_summit)

Upon taking office, Abiy stated his willingness to negotiate an end to the Ethio-Eritrean conflict. In June 2018, it was announced that the government had agreed to hand over the disputed border town of [Badme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badme) to Eritrea, thereby complying with the terms of the 2000 [Algiers Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Agreement_\(2000\)) to bring an end to the state of tension between Eritrea and Ethiopia that had persisted despite the end of hostilities during the [Ethiopia-Eritrea War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia-Eritrea_War).[^cite_note-econ_lib-86] Ethiopia had until then rejected the international boundary commission's ruling awarding Badme to Eritrea, resulting in a [frozen conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frozen_conflict) (popularly termed a policy of "no war, but no peace") between the two states.[^cite_note-104]

During the national celebration on 20 June 2018, the president of Eritrea, [Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaias_Afwerki), accepted the peace initiative put forward by Abiy and suggested that he would send a delegation to Addis Ababa. On 26 June 2018, Eritrean Foreign Minister [Osman Saleh Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Saleh_Mohammed) visited Addis Ababa in the first Eritrean high-level delegation to Ethiopia in over two decades.[^cite_note-105]



Abiy with British Foreign Secretary [James Cleverly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cleverly) on 8 December 2022

In [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara), on 8 July 2018, Abiy became the first Ethiopian leader to meet with an Eritrean counterpart in over two decades, in the [2018 Eritrea–Ethiopia summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Eritrea%E2%80%93Ethiopia_summit).[^cite_note-106] The very next day, the two signed a "Joint Declaration of Peace and Friendship" declaring an end to tensions and agreeing, amongst other matters, to re-establish diplomatic relations; reopen direct telecommunication, road, and aviation links; and facilitate Ethiopian use of the ports of [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) and [Asseb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asseb).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-Giddy_excitement-108][^cite_note-109] Abiy was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) in 2019 for his efforts in ending the war.[^cite_note-NOBEL-9]

In practice, the agreement has been described as "largely unimplemented". Critics say not much has changed between the two nations. Among the Eritrean diaspora, many voiced disapproval for the Nobel Peace Prize focusing on the agreement with Eritrea when so little had changed in practice.[^cite_note-110] In July 2020, Eritrea's Ministry of Information said: "Two years after the signing of the Peace Agreement, Ethiopian troops continue to be present in our sovereign territories, Trade and economic ties of both countries have not resumed to the desired extent or scale."[^cite_note-111]

In October 2023, Abiy said that the [secession of Eritrea from Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Eritrean_independence_referendum) in 1993 was a historical mistake that threatens the existence of landlocked Ethiopia, saying that "In 2030 we are projected to have a population of 150 million. 150 million people can't live in a geographic prison."[^cite_note-112] He said Ethiopia has "natural rights" to direct access to the [Red Sea](26191.md) and if denied, "there will be no fairness and justice and if there is no fairness and justice, it's a matter of time, we will fight".[^cite_note-113]

#### Calls to revoke Nobel Peace Prize

In June 2021, representatives from multiple countries called for the award of the [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) to Abiy to be re-considered because of the war crimes committed in Tigray.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] In an opinion piece, Simon Tisdall, one-time foreign editor of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, wrote that Abiy "should hand back his Nobel Peace Prize over his actions in the breakaway region".[^cite_note-116]

A person on a petition organization called [Change.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change.org) launched a campaign to gather 35,000 signatures for revoking his Peace Prize; as of September 2021, nearly 30,000 have been obtained.[^cite_note-117]

#### Egypt

The dispute between Egypt and [Ethiopia](187749.md) over the [Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ethiopian_Renaissance_Dam) has become a national preoccupation in both countries.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] Abiy has warned: "No force can stop Ethiopia from building a dam. If there is need to go to war, we could get millions readied."[^cite_note-120]



Abiy with [European Commission](9974.md) President [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), 23 July 2023

After the murder of activist, singer and political icon [Hachalu Hundessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachalu_Hundessa) ignited violence across [Addis Ababa](52593.md) and other Ethiopian cities, Abiy hinted, without obvious suspects or clear motives for the killing, that Hundessa may have been murdered by Egyptian security agents acting on orders from [Cairo](6293.md) to stir up trouble.[^cite_note-\:5-121] An Egyptian diplomat responded by saying that Egypt "has nothing to do with current tensions in Ethiopia".[^cite_note-122] [Ian Bremmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Bremmer) wrote in a [*Time* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\)) article that Prime Minister Abiy "may just be looking for a [scapegoat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scapegoat) that can unite Ethiopians against a perceived common enemy".[^cite_note-\:5-121]

### Religious harmony

Ethiopia is a country of various religious groups, primarily Christian and Muslim communities. Both inter-religious and intra-religious divisions and conflicts were a major concern, where both the [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Orthodox_Tewahedo_Church) and the Ethiopian Islamic Council experienced religious and administrative divisions and conflicts.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] In 2018, he was given a special "peace and reconciliation" award by the Ethiopian Church for his work in reconciling rival factions within the church.[^cite_note-125]

### Security sector reform



Abiy with US Secretary of State [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) in Addis Ababa\
on 15 March 2023

In June 2018, Abiy, speaking to senior commanders of the [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md) (ENDF) declared his intention to carry out reforms of the military to strengthen its effectiveness and professionalism, with the view of limiting its role in politics. This followed renewed calls both within Ethiopia and from international human rights groups, namely [Amnesty International](18947898.md), to dissolve highly controversial regional militias such as the Liyyu force.[^cite_note-military_reform-126] This move is considered likely to face resistance from [TPLF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPLF) hardliners, who occupy much of the military high command.[^cite_note-rally_PM-127]



Abiy with military commanders\
in February 2019

Notably, he has also called for the eventual reconstitution of the [Ethiopian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Navy), dissolved in 1996 in the aftermath of Eritrea's secession after an extraterritorial sojourn in [Djibouti](17207794.md), saying that "we should build our naval force capacity in the future."[^cite_note-new_navy-128] It was reported that this move would appeal to nationalists still smarting from the country's loss of its coastline 25 years prior. Ethiopia already has a maritime training institute on [Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana) as well as a [national shipping line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Shipping_Lines).

On 7 June 2018, Abiy carried out a wide-ranging reshuffle of top security officials, replacing ENDF Chief of Staff [Samora Yunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samora_Yunis) with Lieutenant General [Se'are Mekonnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Se'are_Mekonnen), [National Intelligence and Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_and_Security_Service_\(Ethiopia\)) (NISS) director [Getachew Assefa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getachew_Assefa) with Lieutenant General [Adem Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adem_Mohammed), National Security Advisor and former army chief [Abadula Gemeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadula_Gemeda), and [Sebhat Nega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebhat_Nega), one of the founders of the [TPLF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPLF) and director-general of the Foreign Relations Strategic Research Institute[^cite_note-retired-129][^cite_note-130] Sebhat's retirements had been previously announced that May.[^cite_note-131]

### Grenade attack

Main article: [2018 Ethiopian rally grenade attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Ethiopian_rally_grenade_attack)

A large peaceful demonstration was organized in Addis Ababa at [Meskel Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskel_Square) on 23 June 2018 to show support for the new prime minister. Just after Abiy had finished addressing the crowd a [grenade was thrown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Ethiopian_rally_grenade_attack) and landed just 17 metres away from where he and other top officials were sitting. Two people were killed and over 165 were injured. Following the attack, 9 police officials were detained, including the deputy police commissioner, Girma Kassa, who was fired immediately. Questions were asked as to how a police car carrying attackers got so close to the prime minister and soon after the car was set alight destroying evidence. After the attack the prime minister addressed the nation on national TV unhurt by the blast and describing it as an "unsuccessful attempt by forces who do not want to see Ethiopia united". On the same day the prime minister made an unannounced visit to the Black Lion general hospital to meet victims of the attack.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

### Cabinet reshuffle

In the parliamentary session held on 16 October 2018, Abiy proposed to reduce the number of ministries from 28 to 20 with half of the [cabinet positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Ethiopia\)) for female ministers, a first in the history of the country.[^cite_note-136] The new cabinet restructure included the first female president, [Sahle-Work Zewde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahle-Work_Zewde); the first female minister of the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Ethiopia\)), [Aisha Mohammed Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisha_Mohammed_Mussa);[^cite_note-137] the first female minister of the new [Ministry of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Peace_\(Ethiopia\)), [Muferiat Kamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muferiat_Kamil) responsible for the Ethiopian Federal Police and the intelligence agencies; the first female press secretary for the Office of the Prime Minister, [Billene Seyoum Woldeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billene_Seyoum_Woldeyes).[^cite_note-138]

### Internet shutdowns



Abiy with Indian Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) during the [15th BRICS summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_BRICS_summit) in Johannesburg, South Africa, 24 August 2023

According to [NGOs](46539.md) like [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) and [NetBlocks](59736785.md), politically motivated Internet shutdowns have intensified in severity and duration under the leadership of Abiy Ahmed despite the country's rapid digitalization and reliance on cellular internet connectivity in recent years.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] In 2020, Internet shutdowns by the Ethiopian government had been described as "frequently deployed".[^cite_note-\:2-141] [Access Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access_Now) said that shutdowns have become a "go-to tool for authorities to muzzle unrest and activism."[^cite_note-\:2-141] His government will cut internet as and when, "it's neither water nor air" have said Abiy.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

### Political party reform

On 21 November 2019, upon approval of [EPRDF ruling coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front), a new party, [Prosperity Party](62842687.md), is formed via merging of three of the four parties that made up the [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF) and other five affiliate parties. The parties include the [Oromo Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_Democratic_Party) (ODP), the [Southern Ethiopian People's Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ethiopian_People's_Democratic_Movement) (SEPDM), the [Amhara Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amhara_Democratic_Party) (ADP), the [Harari National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hareri_National_League) (HNL), the [Ethiopian Somali Peoples Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Somali_People's_Democratic_Party) (ESPDP), the [Afar National Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_National_Democratic_Party) (ANDP), the Gambella Peoples Unity Party (GPUP), and the Benishangul Gumuz Peoples Democratic Party (BGPDP). The programs and bylaws of the newly merged party were first approved by the executive committee of EPRDF. Abiy believes that "Prosperity Party is committed to strengthening and applying a true federal system which recognizes the diversity and contributions of all Ethiopians".[^cite_note-144]

### 2021 elections

In June 2020, Abiy and the [National Election Board of Ethiopia](27380218.md) (NEBE) postponed parliamentary elections because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The postponement was criticised, especially by the opposition,[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] and questions were raised about the delay's constitutional legitimacy.[^cite_note-147] An [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Ethiopian_general_election) was eventually held in 2021. The [African Union](10634933.md) described the election as an improvement compared to [the 2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Ethiopian_general_election) and positive overall, urging the government to continue the commitment to democracy.[^cite_note-148]

## Political positions



Abiy with Rwandan President [Paul Kagame](393593.md) and Israeli President [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) at the [Kigali Genocide Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali_Genocide_Memorial) in Rwanda, 7 April 2024

Abiy has been described as a "liberal populist" by the academic and journalist [Abiye Teklemariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiye_Teklemariam) and the influential Oromo activist [Jawar Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawar_Mohammed). Alemayehu Weldemariam, a U.S.-based Ethiopian lawyer and public intellectual, has called Abiy "an [opportunistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunistic) populist jockeying for power on a [democratizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratizing) platform."[^cite_note-\:1-149] On the other hand, Tom Gardner argues in *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)* that he's not a populist, but more of a [liberal democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democrat). However, Gardner acknowledges that Abiy has "occasionally used language that can be read as [euphemistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemistic) and conspiracy-minded", and might have "exploited the system's vulnerabilities, such as a pliable media and politicized judiciary, for his own ends."[^cite_note-\:1-149]

Abiy's government was accused of [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) and restricting [freedom of the press](105323.md), especially since the start of the Tigray War. Numerous watchdogs and human rights groups accused Abiy's government of "increasingly intimidating" the media as well as harassing opponents to propel unrest.[^cite_note-150] In 2019 [World Press Freedom Index](8264708.md), Ethiopia improved the rank by jumping forty positions from 150 to 110 out of 180 countries.[^cite_note-151] In 2021, 46 journalists were detained, making Ethiopia the worst jailer in Africa. Journalist Gobeze Sisay was arrested by unknown plainclothes officers in his home on 1 May. On 3 May, the [Ethiopian Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Human_Rights_Commission) (EHRC) released statement about Gobez Sisay's whereabout.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] Similarly, the founder of [Terara Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terara_Network) was arrested in Addis Ababa on 10 December 2021 in allegation of "disseminating misinformation", which was transferred to [Sabata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabata_\(town\)) Daliti police station in [Oromia Special Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromia_Special_Zone_Surrounding_Finfinne). He was released on 5 April 2022 with bail of 50,000 Ethiopian birr.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]

## Personal life

Abiy married [Zinash Tayachew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinash_Tayachew), an Amhara woman and [gospel singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_singer) from [Gondar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondar),[^cite_note-abiyot-20][^cite_note-159] while both were serving in the [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md).[^cite_note-\:0-28] They are the parents of three daughters and one adopted son.[^cite_note-\:0-28] Abiy speaks Oromo, Amharic, [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language) and English.[^cite_note-prelude-27] He is a fitness aficionado and frequents physical and gym activities in Addis Ababa.[^cite_note-\:0-28]

### Religion

Although he was raised in a family of religious plurality, Abiy is a [Pentecostal Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism).[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-economist.com-161] He and his family are regular church attendees, and he also occasionally ministers in preaching and teaching the [gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel) at the [Ethiopian Full Gospel Believers' Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Full_Gospel_Believers'_Church).

### Education

While serving in the Ethiopian National Defense Force, Abiy received his first degree, a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in [computer engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering)[^cite_note-religion-162] from the Microlink Information Technology College in Addis Ababa in 2009.[^cite_note-Walta38214-163]

Abiy holds a Master of Arts in [transformational leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformational_leadership)[^cite_note-religion-162] earned from the business school at the [University of Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenwich) in [London](17867.md), in collaboration with the [International Leadership Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Leadership_Institute), Addis Ababa, in 2011. He also holds a [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) from the Leadstar College of Management and Leadership in Addis Ababa in partnership with [Ashland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland_University)[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] in 2013.[^cite_note-166]

Abiy, who had started his Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) work as a regular student,[^cite_note-167] submitted his PhD thesis in 2016,[^cite_note-Abiy_Ahmed_PhDthesis_2016-168] and defended it in 2017[^cite_note-deWaal_AbiyAhmed_PhD-169][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-cve-32] at the [Institute for Peace and Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Peace_and_Security_Studies), [Addis Ababa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Ababa_University). He did his PhD work on the [Agaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaro) constituency with the PhD thesis entitled *"Social Capital and its Role in Traditional Conflict Resolution in Ethiopia: The Case of Inter-Religious Conflict In Jimma Zone State"*, supervised by [Amr Abdallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amr_Abdallah?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-Abiy_Ahmed_PhDthesis_2016-168]). In 2022, [Alex de Waal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Waal) described Abiy's PhD thesis as constituting an anecdote on an event in which an individual friendship between people of opposite sides augmented [social capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_capital) and inspired the resolution of a local violent conflict in [Jimma Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimma_Zone). De Waal saw the thesis as "perhaps enough for an undergraduate paper", but not deep enough to cover social capital, background literature on [armed conflict and resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_conflict_studies), or literature on "identity, nationalism and conflict".[^cite_note-deWaal_AbiyAhmed_PhD-169] In 2023, de Waal and colleagues recommended that Addis Ababa University re-examine Abiy's PhD thesis for plagiarism, based on their claim of the presence of plagiarism on every page of Chapter 2 of the thesis.[^cite_note-deWaal_plagiarism-171]

Abiy published a related short research article on [de-escalation](1285569.md) strategies in the Horn of Africa in a special journal issue dedicated to [countering violent extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countering_violent_extremism).[^cite_note-cve-32]
