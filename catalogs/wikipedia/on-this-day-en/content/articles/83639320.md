**K2 Airways Flight 1732** was a scheduled international cargo flight operated by [K2 Airways](82403505.md) from [Sharjah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_International_Airport) in [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah), United Arab Emirates, to [Jinnah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinnah_International_Airport) in [Karachi](17123.md), Pakistan. On 7 July 2026, the [Boeing 737-400](15103676.md) freighter crashed in the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) following a reported malfunction of navigation systems.[^cite_note-paa-1][^cite_note-mint-2]. After 12 hours of [search and rescue](172599.md) operations, the wreckage was discovered 53 nautical miles from the coast of [Ormara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormara), Pakistan.[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-avherald-4] All five crew members on board are presumed dead as no remains of the crew have been found.[^cite_note-5]

According to the [Pakistan Airports Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Airports_Authority), the flight crew reported a navigational system malfunction at approximately 9\:18 pm local time while roughly 155 nautical miles from Karachi. Controllers provided navigational guidance, but three minutes later at 9\:21 pm, radar showed the aircraft executing a rapid descent and significant heading change.[^cite_note-observer-6] Subsequent radar and radio contact were lost.[^cite_note-paa-1] Preliminary automatic dependent surveillance-broadcast (ADS-B) data obtained by flight tracking service [Flightradar24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightradar24) suggested a possible crash location in the Arabian Sea southwest of Karachi.[^cite_note-flightradar-7]

## Background

### Aircraft

The aircraft, a 27-year-old Boeing 737-4M0(BDSF) cargo aircraft, was the sole aircraft operated by [K2 Airways](82403505.md) and was equipped with two [CFM International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International) [CFM56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM56)-3C1 engines. The aircraft was first delivered to [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot) in 1999 and leased to [Garuda Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Indonesia) in 2004 (as PK-GZO), before being converted into a freighter aircraft in 2011 for [GE Capital Aviation Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Capital_Aviation_Services), which then leased it to [TNT Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Airways) in 2012 and to [ASL Airlines Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASL_Airlines_Belgium) (operating for [FedEx Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx_Express)) in 2017. In 2024, the aircraft was acquired by [AerCap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AerCap) and leased to K2 Airways.[^cite_note-Arora-8][^cite_note-9]

### Flight crew

The flight was operated by a crew of five: [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_in_command) Muhammed Rizwan Idrees, [first officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_officer_\(aviation\)) Faisal Jatoi, [Loadmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loadmaster) Towfiq Khan and engineers Arif Siddiqui and Mohammed Hamid.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Accident

[Map](83639320.md)

On 7 July 2026, K2 Airways Flight 1732 was operating from [Sharjah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_International_Airport) in the United Arab Emirates to [Jinnah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinnah_International_Airport), Karachi, Pakistan. At around 9\:18 p.m. PKT, while flying over the [Arabian Sea](2747.md), the crew reported a navigational system issue to Air Traffic Control.[^cite_note-aviation-safety-12] Air traffic controllers assisted the flight crew until they lost communication and radar contact approximately three minutes later, about 155 nmi (178 mi; 287 km) west of Karachi. The aircraft was carrying five crew members.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The final known [radio communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_communication) from the flight was made with the [Karachi Area Control Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Area_Control_Centre) (ACC) shortly before the aircraft disappeared over the Arabian Sea. According to reports, the crew transmitted the phrase "rolling or floating, 1732" during the communication.[^cite_note-15] The transmission was recorded in flight records and was reported as the last known message from the aircraft before radar and communication contact were lost.[^cite_note-gulfnews-16]

According to [ADS-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADS-B) data from [Flightradar24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightradar24), the aircraft began to descend from its cruising altitude of 35,000 feet at around 9\:18 p.m., reaching 29,500 feet. It then rapidly climbed to 36,675 feet before beginning to descend. The aircraft's last ADS-B broadcast was at 9\:20\:27, at an altitude of 34,350 feet. Satellite data, also from Flightradar24, collected a final data point at 9\:21\:59, which placed the aircraft at an altitude of 1,100 feet with a vertical speed of -22,400 feet per minute.[^cite_note-flightradar-7][^cite_note-17]

## Aftermath

The [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md) ship [PNS *Zulfiquar* (F251)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Zulfiquar_\(F251\)) and the PNSC *Karachi* as well as a [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) [Saab 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_2000) [Erieye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erieye) [AEW\&C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEW\&C) and Pakistan Navy [RAS-72 Sea Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72) undertook a search for the missing aircraft.[^cite_note-Arora-8] Rough seas and the incoming monsoon season are making the search-and-rescue operation more difficult.[^cite_note-18]

According to the [Pakistan Civil Aviation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Civil_Aviation_Authority), some wreckage was recovered 53 nmi (61 mi; 98 km) of the coast of [Ormara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormara) 12 hours after the aircraft was reported missing.[^cite_note-mint-2][^cite_note-gulfnews-16] The search continues for the five crew members and the aircraft's flight recorders.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

On 29 September 2026, after months of search, the [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md) located an aircraft [flight recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_recorder) at a depth of 3200 meters in the Arabian Sea. The operation was one of the first of its kind, and was made by using special sea equipment developed by the [National Engineering and Scientific Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Engineering_and_Scientific_Commission). The informations that are being collected are being sent to aviation authorities.[^cite_note-21]

## Reactions

Prime Minister [Shehbaz Sharif](6502561.md) expressed grief over the incident and conveyed sympathies to the families of the five crew members. He directed relevant authorities, including the [Pakistan Civil Aviation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Civil_Aviation_Authority), [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md) and [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md), to intensify search and rescue operations and use available resources in the effort.[^cite_note-22]

President [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md) expressed concern over the disappearance of the aircraft and expressed hope for the success of the search and rescue operation. In a statement issued by the [Aiwan-e-Sadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiwan-e-Sadr), he conveyed condolences to the families of the crew members and expressed support for them following the incident.[^cite_note-23]

[K2 Airways](82403505.md) said it was cooperating with authorities during the search operation and expressed concern for the safety of the crew members. The airline also offered support to the families of those on board.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The families of the crew members criticised K2 Airways over what they described as a lack of communication following the crash, stating that the airline had not contacted them or provided updates or support. They called on Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif to ensure the continuation of deep-sea search and recovery operations, order an independent and transparent investigation into the crash, and take legal action if negligence was established.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]
