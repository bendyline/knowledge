**Frederik X**[^cite_note-9] (Frederik André Henrik Christian, pronounced [\[ˈfʁeðˀʁek, ˈfʁæðˀʁæk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Danish); born 26 May 1968) is [King of Denmark](20714373.md), reigning since 14 January 2024.

Frederik is the elder son of [Queen Margrethe II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Margrethe_II) and [Prince Henrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Henrik_of_Denmark). He was born during the reign of his maternal grandfather, [King Frederik IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Frederik_IX), and became [Crown Prince of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Prince_of_Denmark) following his mother's accession in 1972. He received his early education privately at home and later attended [Krebs School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krebs_School), [École des Roches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_des_Roches?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Øregård Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98reg%C3%A5rd_Gymnasium). He earned a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree in political science from [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University). After university, he served in diplomatic posts at the [United Nations](31769.md) and in [Paris](22989.md), and he has trained in all three branches of the [Danish Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Armed_Forces).

Frederik met Australian marketing consultant [Mary Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Donaldson) while attending the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics) in [Sydney](27862.md). [They married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Frederik,_Crown_Prince_of_Denmark,_and_Mary_Donaldson) on 14 May 2004 at [Copenhagen Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Cathedral) and have four children: [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian,_Crown_Prince_of_Denmark), [Isabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Isabella_of_Denmark), [Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Vincent_of_Denmark), and [Josephine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Josephine_of_Denmark).

## Early life and education



Prince Frederik as an infant in the arms of his mother, 1968

Frederik was born at 23\:50 on 26 May 1968[^cite_note-10] by emergency [caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section) at [Rigshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigshospitalet), the [Copenhagen University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_University_Hospital) in [Copenhagen](5166.md), to the then [Princess Margrethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrethe_II) (later Queen Margrethe II), oldest daughter of [King Frederik IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Frederik_IX) and heir presumptive to the Danish throne, and [Prince Henrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Henrik_of_Denmark). At the time of his birth, his maternal grandfather was on the throne of Denmark, his maternal uncle-by-marriage [Constantine II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_II_of_Greece) was king of Greece and his matrilineal great-grandfather, [Gustaf VI Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf), was on the throne of Sweden.[^cite_note-Jones_2022_p._387-11][^cite_note-Paxton_2016_p._855-12]

He was baptised on 24 June at [Holmen Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen_Church) in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-kongeligedaab-13] He was named Frederik after his maternal grandfather, King Frederik IX, continuing the Danish royal tradition of the heir apparent being named either Frederik or Christian.[^cite_note-14] He became [Crown Prince of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Prince_of_Denmark) when his mother ascended the throne on 14 January 1972. Frederik's only sibling is [Prince Joachim of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Joachim_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-Skou_2010_p._218-15]

Frederik attended primary school at [Krebs' Skole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krebs_School) between 1974 and 1981, as a private pupil at [Amalienborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg) from 1974 to 1976, and from the third form again at Krebs' Skole.[^cite_note-CV1-16] From 1982 to 1983, he attended the [École des Roches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_des_Roches?action=edit\&redlink=1), a boarding school in [Normandy](21724.md), France.[^cite_note-CV1-16] In 1986, Frederik graduated from [Øregård Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98reg%C3%A5rd_Gymnasium).[^cite_note-CV1-16] In addition to Danish, he is fluent in French (his father's language), English, and German.[^cite_note-GuardianProfile-17]

In the autumn of 1989, Frederik began studying [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Science) at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University).[^cite_note-PF-18] As part of his education, he spent the 1992–1993 academic year at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he studied political science under the name Frederik Henriksen. Whilst at Harvard, he participated in the [Phoenix – SK Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phoenix_%E2%80%93_S_K_Club), and lived in a [rent-controlled](41258426.md) apartment.[^cite_note-19] Frederik is the first Danish royal to complete a university education.[^cite_note-GuardianProfile-17]

## Early career

Frederik took up a position for three months with the Danish UN mission in New York in 1994.[^cite_note-itv-20] In 1995, he obtained his MSc degree in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) from [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University). He completed the course in the prescribed number of years with an exam result above average, thus becoming the first royal to obtain a master's degree. His final paper was an analysis on the foreign policy of the Baltic States, which he had visited several times during his studies.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-PF-18] The prince was posted as First Secretary to the Danish Embassy in Paris from October 1998 to October 1999.[^cite_note-Dansk_pressehistorisk_selskab_1998_p._94-22][^cite_note-Davidsen_Christensen_2001_p._22-23]

## Military service

Frederik has completed extensive military studies and training in all three services, including training as a [frogman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogman) in the naval elite special operations forces [Frømandskorpset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogman_Corps_\(Denmark\)). It was there that he earned the nickname "Pingo", when his [wetsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetsuit) filled with water and he was forced to waddle like a penguin.[^cite_note-24]

In 2001 and 2002, he completed further leadership training at the [Royal Danish Defence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Defence_College). Frederik remained active in the defence services, and in the period 2002–2003 served as a staff officer at Defence Command Denmark, and from 2003 as a senior lecturer with the Institute of Strategy at the Royal Danish Defence College.[^cite_note-Politiken_2001_n253-25][^cite_note-Politiken_2002_n116-26][^cite_note-Forsvaret_y395-27]

On 8 April 2015 he was named [counter admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_admiral) in the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy) and [major general](36301471.md) in the [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Army) and the [Air Force](548819.md).[^cite_note-28] On 14 January 2024 with his accession as king, he was appointed grade four level, the highest military rank, as [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) in the Navy, [general](148537.md) in the Army and general in the Air Force.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] By his appointment, Frederik has the same grade as the Danish Chief of Defence.[^cite_note-31]

## Marriage and children

Further information: [Wedding of Frederik, Crown Prince of Denmark, and Mary Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Frederik,_Crown_Prince_of_Denmark,_and_Mary_Donaldson)



Prince Frederik and Princess Mary on a 2004 [Faroese](17238607.md) commemorative stamp

During a [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Council_of_State) on 8 October 2003, Queen Margrethe gave her consent to the marriage of Crown Prince Frederik to [Mary Elizabeth Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary,_Crown_Princess_of_Denmark), an Australian [marketing consultant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_consultant) whom the prince had met while attending the [Sydney Olympics in 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics). Their wedding took place on 14 May 2004 at [Copenhagen Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Cathedral), Copenhagen.[^cite_note-itv-20]

The couple have four children: [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian,_Crown_Prince_of_Denmark) (born 15 October 2005), [Isabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Isabella_of_Denmark) (born 21 April 2007), and twins [Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Vincent_of_Denmark) and [Josephine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Josephine_of_Denmark) (born 8 January 2011).[^cite_note-itv-20]

## Reign



The proclamation of King Frederik X from the balcony of [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace) on 14 January 2024

Queen Margrethe II announced [her abdication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication_of_Margrethe_II) during her annual live broadcast New Year's Eve address on 31 December 2023. Frederik succeeded her as King of Denmark on 14 January 2024, after Margrethe formally signed an instrument of abdication during a meeting of the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(Denmark\)).[^cite_note-32]

After succeeding to the throne, he was proclaimed king from the balcony of [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace) by Prime Minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) as has been the custom for Danish monarchs since the introduction of the [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark) in 1849. His [motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_mottos_of_Danish_monarchs) is *Forbundne, forpligtet, for Kongeriget Danmark* (English: "United, committed, for the Kingdom of Denmark"), the first motto that does not mention God since [Frederik VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_VII).[^cite_note-33]

On 15 January, the royal family appeared in Parliament for a ceremony marking the transition of head of state.[^cite_note-34] On 21 January, the royal family attended a celebratory church service at [Aarhus Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_Cathedral), led by the [Bishop of Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Aarhus) and Royal Chaplain-in-Ordinary, [Henrik Wigh-Poulsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Wigh-Poulsen).[^cite_note-35]

On 31 January 2024, Frederik visited Poland and was received by its president [Andrzej Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Duda), in his first overseas trip as monarch. Danish monarchs traditionally travel first to another [Scandinavian country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_country), but Frederik had planned the trip prior to Margrethe's abdication.[^cite_note-36]

The King and Queen made their first state visits in May 2024, visiting [Sweden](5058739.md) and [Norway](21241.md).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] In June, they toured [Greenland](12118.md), one of the autonomous territories of the Kingdom of Denmark. Their planned tour of the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) was postponed due to workers' strikes.[^cite_note-39] The tour is now scheduled for 2025.[^cite_note-40]



Frederik arriving to the 2025 opening of parliament

In November 2024 the Royal Court announced that the King had decided to phase out the system of granting companies [royal warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_purveyors_to_the_Court_of_Denmark), which has been extant in Denmark since the 19th century. The motivation was that a system in which companies can claim special recognition from the royal house was not in keeping with the times. All warrants and similar titles are to be phased out by 31 December 2029.[^cite_note-41]

In January 2025, when [U.S president elect Donald Trump](4848272.md) renewed talks about the intended [U.S purchase of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposals_for_the_United_States_to_purchase_Greenland), the King made a speech promoting unity and collaboration within the Kingdom of Denmark. He also issued a royal decree to update the [royal coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_coat_of_arms_of_Denmark) from his mother's 1972 design. The changes included increasing the sizes of the symbols of [Greenland](12118.md) and the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) to make them more prominent.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The new design “strengthens the prominence of the Commonwealth in the royal coat of arms,” according to a statement made by The Royal House on 1 January 2025.[^cite_note-44]

## Personal interests

### Scientific research, climate change and sustainability



Prince Frederik arriving at Nordic Climate Solutions, 2009



Prince Frederik and Princess Mary admire the night skies above [ESO](175184.md)'s [Paranal Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranal_Observatory) in northern Chile, 2013

[^cite_note-57]

Frederik has a special interest in scientific research, climate change, and sustainability.[^cite_note-58] For his commitment to sustainability, he was interviewed by the *[Financial Times](136566.md)* and [CNN International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_International)'s *Future Cities* program.[^cite_note-FT_Magazine_2010-59][^cite_note-60] He participated in expeditions, forums and events on climate.[^cite_note-FT_Magazine_2010-59][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] The prince has represented Denmark as a promoter of sustainable Danish energy.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] The prince was one of the authors of the *Kongelig Polartokt* (Polar Cruise Royal), about the challenges of climate, published in 2009 with a preface written by [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] In 2010, he wrote the [foreword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreword) to the highschool textbook *Naturen og klimaændringerne i Nordøstgrønland* (Nature and climate change in [Northeast Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Greenland_National_Park)).[^cite_note-70] He supports scientific research projects, as a patron, as expeditionary, with regular attendance at events and through his foundation, *Kronprins Frederiks Fond*.[^cite_note-71]

### Sports and health



Prince Frederik after finishing the Vasaloppet 2012

The king encourages Danish participation in sports.[^cite_note-BilledBladet_2010_g518-72] He is a patron and honorary member of various sports organizations,[^cite_note-Simon_Staun_2009_z851-73][^cite_note-Forsvar_2003_g594-74][^cite_note-Fokus_2018_s909-75][^cite_note-Altinget.dk_2024_d925-76] and a former member of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md).[^cite_note-77] He also promotes an active lifestyle in society.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

Frederik is an avid sportsman,[^cite_note-81] running marathons in Copenhagen, New York, and Paris, and completing the 42 kilometers with a respectable time of 3 hours, 22 minutes and 50 seconds in the [Copenhagen Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Marathon).[^cite_note-Hello-4][^cite_note-82] In 2013, he completed the KMD Ironman Copenhagen in the time of 10\:45\:32 and is the first royal person to complete an [Ironman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironman_Triathlon).[^cite_note-83]

Frederik is a keen sailor, being an accomplished [Farr 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farr_40) and [International Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_\(keelboat\)) skipper.[^cite_note-84] At the 2003 [Dragon European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_European_Championship), where 51 boats participated, the Prince and his crew had been leading after four out of six races; they finished in fourth place.[^cite_note-85] At the 2008 [Farr 40 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farr_40_World_Championship) with 33 boats participating, Frederik and his crew also took fourth place.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] Helming the [Swan 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_60) yacht *Emma*, he won its [IRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rating_Certificate) category in the 2010 Fyn Cup in Denmark,[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] and was fourth in the 2011 Danish Dragon Championship with *Nanoq*. 27 boats participated.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

In 2016, on the subject of the [Olympics in Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics), Frederik told the press that he did not regret not chasing his dream to compete in the Olympics after meeting his wife. He had always thought about training and competing, but that would have required him to limit his activities and concentrate on training, instead, he put his energy into other aspects of life.[^cite_note-93] In October 2016, Frederik had to cancel his appearance at the royal reception for the [Danish Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics) and [Paralympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_at_the_2016_Summer_Paralympics) athletes after he fractured his spine while jumping on a trampoline with his eldest son.[^cite_note-94]

Frederik took part in the relay event during the [2019 IAAF World Cross Country Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_IAAF_World_Cross_Country_Championships) in [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus) in March 2019.[^cite_note-95]

Frederik has competed in [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing_\(sport\)); he skied the 90 km (56 mi) Swedish [Vasaloppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasaloppet), the oldest cross-country ski race in the world, in 2012,[^cite_note-Skiferie_2024_s757-96][^cite_note-Nielsen_2012_k369-97] 2013,[^cite_note-Puccio_2013_g452-98][^cite_note-nyheder.tv2.dk_2013_v555-99] 2014,[^cite_note-Newspaq_2014_o334-100] and 2015.[^cite_note-Mikkelsen_Mikkelsen_2015_a150-101][^cite_note-Jyllands-Posten_2015_c017-102] In 2016, he completed the 54 kilometres (34 mi) Norwegian [Birkebeinerrennet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkebeinerrennet) with Norway's [Crown Prince Haakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon,_Crown_Prince_of_Norway).[^cite_note-Carlson_2016_w769-103] Also in 2016, Frederik completed the 160 km (99 mi) [Arctic Circle Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle_Race?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Sisimiut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisimiut), Greenland.[^cite_note-Bleeg_2016_q933-104][^cite_note-Grønbech_2016_g715-105]

#### International Olympic Committee



Prince Frederik as member of the International Olympic Committee, 2020

On 9 October 2009, Crown Prince Frederik was elected a member of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md), replacing former Danish member Kaj Holm, who had reached the age of retirement.[^cite_note-106] The Crown Prince's candidature was met with some skepticism in Denmark, as it would mean that the Crown Prince would be on a semi-political committee along with several people who are suspected or even convicted of criminal acts.[^cite_note-Villeman-107] Another concern was whether or not the Crown Prince's loyalty would be towards his country and government, as the [Danish constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark) prescribes, or with the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md), as is sworn upon election to the committee.[^cite_note-Villeman-107] The Crown Prince was given special observer status in [National Olympic Committee and Sports Confederation of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee_and_Sports_Confederation_of_Denmark), as a way to allow him to work, without having political power.[^cite_note-108]

Frederik announced that his point of focus and reason for joining the International Olympic Committee is to promote an active lifestyle among youth. He was elected for an eight-year term, and made it clear that he would terminate his membership upon ascending the Danish throne.[^cite_note-109]

In 2012, Frederik carried the [Olympic flame](74217.md) through [Notting Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notting_Hill), a neighbourhood in [West London](60445975.md).[^cite_note-110]

In 2016, Frederik faced criticism for voting against Danish [Minister for Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Culture_\(Denmark\)) [Bertel Haarder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertel_Haarder) and a majority in the [Danish Parliament](429743.md)'s wishes on [the subject of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics#Russian_doping_scandal) whether or not Russia should be allowed to compete at the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics) following allegations of state-sponsored [doping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_at_the_Olympic_Games).[^cite_note-111]

On 19 June 2017, the Crown Prince announced that he would continue another term of 8 years.[^cite_note-112] However, in 2021, Frederik was announced that he would step down as an active member of the International Olympic Committee at the committee's annual session prior to the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics), citing a wish to intensify his everyday work as the reason for stepping down in the middle of his term.[^cite_note-113]

#### Royal Run

In celebration of his 50th birthday, on 21 May 2018, Frederik initiated a public running event across five cities in Denmark called [Royal Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Run?action=edit\&redlink=1) with more than 70,000 participants, including himself and his own family.[^cite_note-114] The event was generally deemed as "exceeding expectations" by the public.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

The event has since become annual, and was continued in 2019,[^cite_note-Hansen_2019_j700-117] 2021,[^cite_note-Bach_Hansen_2021_b216-118] 2022,[^cite_note-TV2_Østjylland_2023_s959-119] and 2023.[^cite_note-Sølvsten_2020_o242-120] The 2020 edition was cancelled due to the [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Denmark) situation in Denmark.[^cite_note-Larsen_2021_w004-121] The 2024 edition took place on 20 May in Copenhagen/[Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg), [Kalundborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalundborg), [Fredericia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericia), [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus), and [Brønderslev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8nderslev).[^cite_note-nyheder.tv2.dk_2024_t211-122] The 2025 edition took place on 9 June 2025.

### Expeditions

The Crown Prince participated in an expedition to [Mongolia](19271.md) in 1986.[^cite_note-kongehuset-123] In 2000, he participated in "Expedition Sirius 2000", a four-month and 2,795 km [dog-sled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog-sled) expedition in the northern part of Greenland. The expedition Sirius marked the 50-year anniversary of the [Sirius Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius_Patrol). Prince Frederik was part of the polar expedition as a film photographer, whose job was to ensure an optimal coverage of this event.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]

### Social endeavours

When younger, he was known to have pursued a life of fast cars and fast living, regularly seen frequenting nightclubs alongside celebrities. He played harmonica at a friend's rock concert, and was sighted at [Burning Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_Man) as late as 2014.[^cite_note-126]

## Crown Prince Frederik's Foundation

The purpose of the foundation is to provide financial assistance to students of social policy and sciences, for one year's study at Harvard.[^cite_note-127] It also provides financial support for scientific expeditions, particularly to foreign parts of the world, such as [Greenland](12118.md) and the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md), and sports purposes, including those with a particularly social aspect.[^cite_note-128]

## Titles, styles, honours and arms

### Titles and styles

Until his mother's accession to the throne, Frederik was styled "[His Royal Highness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Royal_Highness) Prince Frederik of Denmark"; at that point, he became known as "His Royal Highness The Crown Prince of Denmark". When Margrethe II created the title count of Monpezat for her male-line descendants on 29 April 2008, Frederik became known as "His Royal Highness The Crown Prince of Denmark, Count of Monpezat".[^cite_note-129] Since his accession, he has been styled "His Majesty The King, Count of Monpezat".[^cite_note-130]

### Honours

The King has received a number of honours.[^cite_note-kongehuset-123] One of the more surprising honours he has received is the special honour from the [Danish Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Red_Cross). Normally, this accolade is only given to former presidents of the Danish Red Cross, but a special exception was made for the king on this occasion.[^cite_note-131]
