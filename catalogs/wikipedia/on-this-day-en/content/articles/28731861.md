The **Chornomorets Stadium** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Стадіон «Чорноморець», [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Stadion «Chornomorets»*) is a football [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium) built in 2011 in [Odesa](55545.md), [Ukraine](31750.md). The stadium has a capacity of 34,164 and is the home of [FC Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md) and [FC Seasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Seasters). The inaugural match, between FC Chornomorets Odesa and [FC Karpaty Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Karpaty_Lviv), was played on 19 November 2011, and ended with a 2–2 draw. The first goal was scored by [Vitaliy Balashov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Balashov) in the 46th minute from a penalty.

The stadium was constructed on the site of the old Soviet multi-use **Central Stadium of the Black Sea Shipping Company** (ChMP), which was demolished in 2009. The venue was considered to be one of the stadiums of [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012) but failed to be nominated as such.[^cite_note-1]

## History

### Name

- 1935—1938: Stanislav Kosior Stadium

- 1938—1945: Taras Shevchenko Park Stadium

- 1945—1958: Central Stadium Kharchovyk

- 1958: Avanhard Stadium

- 1958—2009: Central Stadium of the Black Sea Shipping Company (alternatively Central Stadium ChMP)

- since 2011: Chornomorets Stadium

### Former stadium

The former ChMP stadium was built on the same site in 1935, and it was originally named as **Stanislav Kosior Stadium** after the [First Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_Ukraine) of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Ukraine_\(Soviet_Union\)) of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR), [Stanislav Kosior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Kosior). After Stanislav Kosior was repressed in the 1930s, the name was changed to **[Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko) Park Stadium**.&#x20;

After [World War II](32927.md), the stadium was passed to the ownership of the republican Ministry of Food and received name as the **Central Stadium Kharchovyk**. At the end of the 1950s, it was renamed as **Avanhard Stadium** after the Ukrainian Sport Society of industrial workers. In 1959, the stadium was renamed to the Central Stadium of the [Black Sea Shipping Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Shipping_Company) or alternatively **Central Stadium Chornomorets**.

ChMP could hold 34,362 people. It hosted the [Ukrainian Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Super_Cup) from 2004 until 2007. At the end of 2008, it was closed and, in 2009, it was demolished.[^cite_note-2]

### New stadium

#### Financial crisis

The stadium was a part of a defunct [Imexbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imexbank) assets. On May 26, 2020, the [Deposit Guarantee Fund of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_Guarantee_Fund_of_Ukraine?action=edit\&redlink=1) sold at auction the Chornomorets Stadium to the American company ["Allrise Capital Inc."](https://allrisecapital.com/), for ₴193.8 million (\$7.24 million).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Allrise Capital's main owner is Russian expat in America Vladimir Yevseyev.[^cite_note-664321/_Prosecutor's_office-8]

In August 2020, the Shevchenko District Court of [Kyiv](585629.md) arrested the stadium complex and handed over to the [Agency in search and management of assets](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%BE%20%D0%B7%20%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B7%D1%88%D1%83%D0%BA%D1%83%20%D1%82%D0%B0%20%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%B6%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%83%20%D0%B0%D0%BA%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B2%D1%96%D0%B2) (Ukrainian). The pretrial investigation began based on a statement of public organization "Olimpik" that owns the football club with the same name.[^cite_note-9] About ten days later it was announced that the same court decided to cancel arrest of the stadium.[^cite_note-10]

#### Russo-Ukrainian war



After the attack on 7 August 2026

In February 2023, due to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the Russian text on the stadium's facade was replaced by the Ukrainian version.[^cite_note-11]

On 7 August 2026, the stadium was hit by a Russian drone, partially damaging the roof and stands. Two people were injured.[^cite_note-12] FC Chornomorets' match against [FC Kolos Kovalivka](47279129.md), which was to take place the following day, was postponed.[^cite_note-13]
