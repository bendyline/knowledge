**Mohammed VI**[^cite_note-2] (born **Mohammed ben El Hassan Al Alaoui**;[^cite_note-3] 21 August 1963) is  [King of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Morocco), reigning since 1999.

A member of the [Alawi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawi_dynasty), Mohammed was born during the reign of his father, King [Hassan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_II). At an early age, he began partaking in public engagements on behalf of his father. Upon ascending to the throne following Hassan's death,[^cite_note-4] Mohammed initially introduced several reforms and changed the [family code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudawana) to grant more rights to [women in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Morocco).[^cite_note-BBCProfile-5]

In 2011, [protests in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932012_Moroccan_protests) that were considered part of the wider [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) occurred against alleged government corruption. In response, Mohammed enacted several reforms and introduced [a new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Morocco). These reforms were passed by [public referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Moroccan_constitutional_referendum) on 1 July 2011.[^cite_note-BBC-6] His other reforms have included modernising the [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Morocco) and [military force of Morocco](19299.md), promoting [non-sectarian Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim) and [Berber culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_culture), including designating [Standard Moroccan Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Moroccan_Amazigh) as an official national language alongside [Standard Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Arabic) and [Yennayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yennayer) (the Amazigh New Year) as a national holiday, and curtailing the influence of religious extremism.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-article5-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

In foreign policy, Mohammed continued in the moderate tradition established by his father, who was held to be a moderating influence among Arab nations and in relations between the Arab world and the West.[^cite_note-13] He diversified Morocco's ties with key global players, including the [United States](3434750.md), the [European Union](9317.md), and [China](5405.md), and prioritized relations with African countries and international recognition of [Morocco's claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara) to the territory of [Western Sahara](33220.md). Morocco became the sixth [Arab League](52625.md) country to [normalize ties with Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Morocco_normalization_agreement) under the [Abraham Accords](64883396.md).[^cite_note-14]

Mohammed has vast business holdings across several economic sectors in Morocco. In 2015, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* estimated his net worth at over US\$5.2 billion.[^cite_note-\:2-15] [Leaked diplomatic cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) from [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks) in 2010 led to allegations of [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Morocco) in the royal court, implicating him and his closest advisors.[^cite_note-g1-16]

## Early life and education



Mohammed with his father King [Hassan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_II) in 1968

Mohammed was born on 21 August 1963, as the first child and eldest son of King [Hassan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_II) and his wife, [Lalla Latifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalla_Latifa).[^cite_note-17] His father was keen on giving him a religious and political education from an early age; at the age of four, he started attending the [Quranic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quranic_school) at the [Royal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Rabat).[^cite_note-embassy-18] His educational routine commenced at 6 am with an hour-long [recitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recitation) of the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), followed by formal lessons. He completed his first primary and secondary studies at the [Collège Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_Royal_\(Rabat\)), a specialized college constructed within the fortified walls of the palace. Hassan II, desiring his son to experience competitive pressure, selected twelve classmates recognized for their intellect to accompany Mohammed in his studies. As depicted in *Le Roi prédateur*, a 2012 biography authored by two French journalists, there is an account of Hassan instructing his aides to administer twenty lashes to Mohammed when he appeared to lag in his studies.[^cite_note-\:5-19] Mohammed's education was supervised by Mohammed Aouad, a diplomat and professor who later became a minister in the government, and assisted by Moroccan and European governesses.[^cite_note-\:8-20] Mohammed excelled in languages.[^cite_note-\:5-19]

Mohammed's first public engagement was in 1971, when, at the age of eight, he received United States Vice President [Spiro Agnew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiro_Agnew) at the Royal Palace in Rabat.[^cite_note-\:8-20] At the age of ten, he represented his father at the funeral of French president [Georges Pompidou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Pompidou) in 1974.[^cite_note-21]

Mohammed attained his [Baccalaureate](188874.md) in 1981, before gaining a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in law at the [Mohammed V University at Agdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_V_University_at_Agdal) in 1985.[^cite_note-22] His research paper dealt with "the Arab-African Union and the Strategy of the Kingdom of Morocco in matters of International Relations".[^cite_note-embassy-18] He was furthermore appointed president of the [Pan Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Arab_Games), and was [commissioned](36301328.md) a [Brigadier General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_General) of the [Royal Moroccan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Moroccan_Army) on 26 November 1985. Mohammed served as the Coordinator of the Offices and Services of the Royal Armed Forces until 1994.

In 1987, Mohammed obtained his first *Certificat d'Études Supérieures* (CES) in political sciences, and in July 1988 he obtained a *Diplôme d'Études Approfondies* ([DEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEA_\(former_French_degree\))) in public law.[^cite_note-embassy-18] In November 1988, he trained in [Brussels](3708.md) with [Jacques Delors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Delors), the President of the [European Commission](9974.md).[^cite_note-embassy-18]

According to a biography by Ferran Sales Aige, Mohammed's father received reports from his spies indicating that the young prince was visiting bars regularly. This led to a deepening dissatisfaction from the king towards his son. In a moment of despair, Hassan II was rumoured to have described his son's behaviour as a "chromosome error." Mohammed was sent to study law in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), with his activities closely monitored by the interior minister dispatched by his father.[^cite_note-\:5-19] He obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in law [with distinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honours) on 29 October 1993 from the French [University of Nice Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis) for his thesis on "[EEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEC)-[Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) Relations".[^cite_note-embassy-18] On 12 July 1994, he was promoted to the military rank of [Major General](36301471.md), and that same year he became president of the High Council of Culture and [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief) of the Royal Moroccan Army.

According to the *New York Times*, before ascending to the throne, Mohammed "gained a reputation as a playboy during the years he spent waiting in the wings, showing a fondness for fast cars and nightclubs."[^cite_note-web.archive.org-23] According to a childhood friend, Mohammed harboured fantasies about the world beyond the palace walls and seldom ventured outside. One of his favourite songs was "[Breakfast in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_in_America_\(song\))" by the English rock band [Supertramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supertramp), which celebrates the allure of travel by jumbo jet.[^cite_note-\:5-19] Over time, a noticeable estrangement developed between him and his father. He actively avoided encounters with Hassan II, even during his visits to Morocco. Instead, he frequently frequented Amnesia, an illicit club located underground in [Rabat](57400.md). According to *Le Roi prédateur*, Mohammed's close friend from school, [Fouad Ali El Himma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouad_Ali_El_Himma), facilitated his visits to Amnesia by installing a private lift from his apartment above that descended directly to the club's premises.[^cite_note-\:5-19]

## Reign

### Accession and early years

Mohammed ascended the throne on [the death of his father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Hassan_II_of_Morocco) on 23 July 1999.[^cite_note-24] His enthronement took place on 30 July, on the occasion of which he addressed his people on national television, promising to take on poverty and [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Morocco), while creating jobs and improving [Morocco's human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Morocco) record. [Islamist](15012.md) conservatives opposed his reformist rhetoric, and some of his reforms angered [fundamentalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_fundamentalism).[^cite_note-BBCProfile-5][^cite_note-25] His initial directives also included the dismissal of his father's hardline interior minister, [Driss Basri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driss_Basri), and the appointment of some of his former classmates to key positions in the state bureaucracy.[^cite_note-26]

Mohammed and his sister, [Princess Lalla Meryem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Lalla_Meryem), made a state visit to the United States in June 2000, as guests of the president [Bill Clinton](3356.md).[^cite_note-27] The [Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) designated Morocco as a [major non-NATO ally](3812445.md) in 2004. The two countries later signed a free-trade agreement in 2006, the only one of its kind between the United States and an African country, which was met with some criticism within Morocco due to increasing [trade deficit](4810.md).[^cite_note-\:4-28]

In February 2004, Mohammed enacted a new family code ([Mudawana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudawana)), which granted women more power.[^cite_note-BBCProfile-5] In July, he announced that Morocco would lift visa restrictions for Algerians, with Algerian president [Abdelaziz Bouteflika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelaziz_Bouteflika) reciprocating the measure in 2005.[^cite_note-29] Mohammed also created the [Equity and Reconciliation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_and_Reconciliation_Commission), which was tasked with researching [human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_violation) under Hassan II. This move was welcomed by many as promoting [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) but was also criticized because the commission's reports did not name the perpetrators. According to human rights organizations, human rights violations are still common in Morocco.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In March 2006, the government created the [Royal Advisory Council for Saharan Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Advisory_Council_for_Saharan_Affairs) (CORCAS), an advisory committee which defends Morocco's [claim to Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara), and whose members are appointed by the king. The CORCAS proposed a [plan for Western Sahara's autonomy](8371462.md), provided it remains under Moroccan sovereignty.[^cite_note-33] Mohammed went on to visit Western Sahara in 2006 and 2015.[^cite_note-34]

In 2009, an opinion poll jointly conducted by [TelQuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TelQuel) and *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde)* showed that 91% of Moroccans approved of Mohammed VI's reign. The opinion poll was banned by the authorities.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

### 2011 protests and constitutional reform

Main article: [2011–2012 Moroccan protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932012_Moroccan_protests)

The [2011 Moroccan protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Moroccan_protests), led by the 20 February Movement, were primarily motivated by corruption and general political discontentment, as well as by the hardships of the [global economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession). Then-recent revolutions influenced the demonstrations in [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_revolution) and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_revolution) which overthrew their respective leaders, and demands by protesters included "urgent" political and social reforms, including the relinquishment of some of the King's powers.[^cite_note-37]

In a speech delivered on 9 March 2011, Mohammed said that parliament would receive "new powers that enable it to discharge its representative, legislative, and regulatory mission". In addition, the powers of the judiciary were granted greater independence from the king, who announced that he was empanelling a committee of legal scholars to produce a draft constitution by June 2011.[^cite_note-38] On 1 July, voters approved a set of political reforms proposed by the king in a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Moroccan_constitutional_referendum).

The reforms consisted of the following:[^cite_note-BBC-6]

- [Standard Moroccan Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Moroccan_Amazigh) is designated an official national language, along with standard [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic).

  [^cite_note-article5-10]

- The state preserves and protects the [Hassaniya Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya_Arabic) dialect and all the linguistic components of [Moroccan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_culture) as a heritage of the nation.

  [^cite_note-article5-10]

- The [prime minister](46308899.md) (officially titled "head of government") presides over the Council of Government, which prepares the general policy of the state; previously the king held this position. The prime minister also has the power to dissolve the parliament.

  [^cite_note-Parisien-39]

- The king now must appoint the prime minister from the party that wins the most seats in the parliamentary elections, but it can be any member of the winning party and not necessarily the party's leader. Previously, the king could nominate anybody he wanted for this position regardless of the election results. That was usually the case when no party had a big advantage over the other parties, in terms of the number of seats in the [parliament](4503449.md).

  [^cite_note-BBC-6]

  [^cite_note-40]

  [^cite_note-\:6-41]

- The king is no longer "sacred or holy" but the "integrity of his person" is "inviolable".

  [^cite_note-42]

- High administrative and diplomatic posts (including ambassadors, CEOs of state-owned companies, and provincial and regional governors) are now appointed by the prime minister and the ministerial council which is presided over by the king; previously the latter exclusively held this power.

  [^cite_note-43]

- The parliament has the power to grant amnesty. Previously this was also exclusively held by the king.

  [^cite_note-44]

- The king guarantees the independence of the judiciary system from the legislative and executive branches.

  [^cite_note-Parisien-39]

  [^cite_note-45]

- Women are guaranteed "civic and social" equality with men. Previously, only "political equality" was guaranteed, though the 1996 constitution granted all citizens equality in terms of rights before the law.

  [^cite_note-\:6-41]

- The king retains complete control over the armed forces and the judiciary as well as matters about religion and foreign policy, as well as the authority to appoint and dismiss prime ministers.

  [^cite_note-46]

- In theory, all citizens have freedom of thought, ideas, artistic expression and creation. Previously only free speech and the freedom of circulation and association were guaranteed. However, [criticizing or directly opposing the king](934323.md) is still punishable with prison.

  [^cite_note-\:6-41]

  [^cite_note-telquelcons-47]

### Royal pardon scandal

Main article: [Daniel Galván scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Galv%C3%A1n_scandal)

Protests broke out in Rabat on 2 August 2013 after Mohammed pardoned 48 jailed Spaniards, including Daniel Galván, a pedophile who had been serving a 30-year sentence for raping 11 children aged between 4 and 15.[^cite_note-48] In response to the protests, he revoked Galván's pardon and Morocco issued an international arrest warrant; Galván was arrested in Spain, where he continued his sentence.[^cite_note-49]

Those pardoned included a drug trafficking suspect, who was released before standing trial.[^cite_note-31lakome-50] The detainee, Antonio Garcia, a recidivist drug trafficker, had been arrested in possession of 9 tons of [hashish](14415.md) in [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier) and was sentenced to 10 years.[^cite_note-29lakome-51] He had resisted arrest using a firearm.[^cite_note-31lakome-50] Some media claimed that his release embarrassed Spain.[^cite_note-29lakome-51]

### Later developments

#### Foreign policy



Joint [U.S.-Israeli delegation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Morocco_normalization_agreement) meeting with Mohammed VI during a visit to Rabat, 22 December 2020



[Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) at a dinner with Mohammed and his entourage, 2022

In the latter part of his reign, Mohammed increasingly prioritized African relations in Morocco's foreign policy.[^cite_note-\:7-52] In July 2016, Mohammed addressed a letter to the 27th [African Union](10634933.md) (AU) summit in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali), in which he requested Moroccan admission to the organization. Morocco had previously been a member of the AU's predecessor, the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity), until it withdrew in 1984 in protest at the admission of the [Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](38762388.md). Mohammed justified his country's withdrawal saying that "the admission of a non-sovereign entity, by means of transgression and collusion" had prompted Morocco to "seek to avoid the division of Africa".[^cite_note-53] Morocco was admitted to the African Union in January 2017.

Morocco also developed partnerships with the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) as well as other non-traditional [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power), mainly China and Russia, intending to diversify trade links and foreign investments and limit Morocco's traditional reliance on the [European Union](9317.md) and other Western countries.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-\:7-52][^cite_note-55] The country offered to act as a mediator in the [Libyan crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Crisis_\(2011%E2%80%93present\)) and remained neutral in the [Qatar diplomatic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_diplomatic_crisis).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-\:4-28]

Morocco and Israel restored [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Morocco_relations) on 10 December 2020, as part of the [Israel–Morocco normalization agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Morocco_normalization_agreement) involving the United States, which at the same time recognized [Morocco's sovereignty](4255996.md) over [Western Sahara](33220.md).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] In June 2021, Mohammed congratulated [Naftali Bennett](39754840.md) on his election as Israeli prime minister.[^cite_note-59] On the [International Day of Solidarity with the Palestinian People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Day_of_Solidarity_with_the_Palestinian_People) in November 2021, the king announced that Morocco would continue to push for a restart of [Israeli–Palestinian peace negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_process). He called on both sides "to refrain from actions that obstruct the peace process".[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

[Relations with neighbouring Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Morocco_relations) remained strained, despite calls from Mohammed for a reconciliation.[^cite_note-62] Tensions intensified in the 2020s, primarily as a result of the Israel–Morocco normalization agreement and Western Saharan border clashes. In August 2021, Algeria accused Morocco of supporting the [Movement for the Self-Determination of Kabylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Self-Determination_of_Kabylie), which it blamed for [wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Algeria_wildfires) in northern Algeria,[^cite_note-63] and later severed diplomatic relations with Morocco.[^cite_note-64]

In February 2023, Mohammed and his foreign minister [Nasser Bourita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Bourita) visited [Gabon](12027.md), meeting with its president [Ali Bongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Bongo) and conducting a donation of 2,000 tonnes of fertilizer to the country.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] On 4 December 2023, Mohammed and his entourage made an official visit to [Dubai](211583.md), at the invitation of UAE President Sheikh [Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan](2121945.md), in which the two leaders signed a declaration committing to the development of "deep-rooted" bilateral relations.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

#### Western Sahara policy

From late 2019 onwards, several countries allied with Morocco in the [Western Sahara conflict](4255996.md)—primarily African and Arab countries—established consulates in the Western Saharan cities of [Laayoune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laayoune) and [Dakhla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakhla,_Western_Sahara), with a total of 28 as of 2023.[^cite_note-69]

In November 2020, an [escalation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Saharan_clashes_\(2020%E2%80%93present\)) of the conflict began when Sahrawi protesters blocked a road connecting [Guerguerat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerguerat) to [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) via Mauritania. Morocco responded by intervening militarily to resume the movement of people and goods through Guerguerat, which the Polisario Front said had violated the 1991 ceasefire agreement.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

In August 2022, Mohammed said that the Western Sahara issue formed the basis for Morocco's foreign policy, through which it "measures the sincerity of friendships and the efficiency of partnerships". He called on other countries "to clarify their positions" on the conflict "and reconsider them in a manner that leaves no room for doubt".[^cite_note-72] In 2023, Israel under the [third Netanyahu government](72545811.md) became the second country to recognize Moroccan sovereignty over the territory,[^cite_note-73] followed by [Paraguay](23420.md) the following year.[^cite_note-74]

Following the approval of [United Nations Security Council Resolution 2797](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolutions_2701_to_2800) on 31 October 2025, which endorsed Morocco's autonomy plan, Mohammed issued a royal decree designating its anniversary as [a national holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_holidays_in_Morocco).[^cite_note-75]

#### Domestic policy

After the [Moroccan national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_national_football_team) reached fourth place at the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), Mohammed awarded the members of the team with the [Order of the Throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Throne) during a reception at the Royal Palace of Rabat.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] In March 2023, he was invited by president of the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF) [Patrice Motsepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Motsepe) to receive the CAF's outstanding achievement award.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] During the awards ceremony in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali), [Chakib Benmoussa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakib_Benmoussa), attending on behalf of the king, announced in a letter written by Mohammed that Morocco would join the [Portugal–Spain 2030 FIFA World Cup bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco%E2%80%93Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bid) as a co-host.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] The bid was unanimously approved by the FIFA Council in October.[^cite_note-82]

In May 2023, Mohammed authorized the creation of a national public holiday for [Yennayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yennayer) (Berber New Year).[^cite_note-83]

Following the [September 2023 Al Haouz earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Al_Haouz_earthquake) which killed nearly three thousand people, Mohammed visited hospitals to support victims and donated blood for the needy.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Under his instructions, the royal holding [Al Mada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mada_\(holding\)) donated one billion dirhams for relief operations of quake-hit regions.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] On 29 September 2026 he appointed [Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri](37468300.md) as the first woman prime minister after her victory in the [general election](79785786.md).[^cite_note-89]

## Business and wealth

Main articles: [Al Mada (holding)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mada_\(holding\)), [Attijariwafa Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attijariwafa_Bank), and [ONA Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ONA_Group)



Graphic detailing ownership of the palace-controlled holding,

[^cite_note-cable-90]

&#x20;the *Société Nationale d'investissement* as of June 2013

Mohammed is Morocco's leading businessman and banker.[^cite_note-cable-90] In 2015, he was estimated by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine to be worth US\$5.7 billion[^cite_note-\:2-15] although in 2019 *Business Insider* quoted US\$2.1 billion.[^cite_note-\:1-91] The Moroccan Royal Family, meanwhile, has one of the largest fortunes in the world.[^cite_note-forbes-92] Together, they hold the majority stakes in the [Al Mada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mada_\(holding\)) holding, formerly named the *Société Nationale d'Investissement* (SNI), which was originally state-owned but was merged in 2013 with Omnium Nord Africain ([ONA Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ONA_Group)), to form a single [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company) that was taken off the [Casablanca Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca_Stock_Exchange)—resulting in the scrapping of an equivalent of 50 billion dirhams [marketcap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) (\~US\$6 billion).[^cite_note-telquel1-93] Al Mada has a diverse portfolio consisting of many important businesses in Morocco, operating in various sectors including: [Attijariwafa Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attijariwafa_Bank) ([banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking)), [Managem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managem) ([mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining)), Onapar, [SOMED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOMED) (tourism/[real-estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-estate) and exclusive distributor of [Maserati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati)), Wafa Assurance ([insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance)), [Marjane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjane_\(business\)) ([hypermarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypermarket) chain), [Wana-Inwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inwi) ([telecommunications](33094374.md)), [SONASID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SONASID) ([siderurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siderurgy)), [Lafarge Maroc](41902084.md), [Sopriam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopriam) (exclusive distributor of [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot)-[Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn) in Morocco), [Renault Maroc](162292.md) (exclusive distributor of Renault in Morocco) and [Nareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nareva) (energy).[^cite_note-telquel-94][^cite_note-95] It also owns many food-processing companies and is currently in the process of disengaging from this sector.[^cite_note-telquel-94] Between mid-2012 and 2013, the holding sold *Lessieur*, *Centrale Laitière*, *Bimo* and *Cosumar* to foreign groups for a total amount of \~\$1.37 billion (11.4 billion Dirhams including 9.7 billion in 2013 and 1.7 in 2012).[^cite_note-telquel-94]

SNI and ONA both owned stakes in [Brasseries du Maroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasseries_du_Maroc), the largest [alcoholic beverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) manufacturer and distributor of brands such as [Heineken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken) in the country.[^cite_note-96] In March 2018, the SNI adopted its current name, Al Mada.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Mohammed is also a leading agricultural producer and land owner in Morocco, where agriculture is exempted from taxes.[^cite_note-telquel-94] His personal holding company [SIGER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGER) has shares in the large agricultural group *Les domaines agricoles* (originally called *Les domaines royaux*, now commonly known as *Les domaines*), which was founded by Hassan II.[^cite_note-telquel-94] In 2008, [Telquel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telquel) estimated that *Les domaines* had a revenue of \$157 million (1.5 billion dirhams), with 170,000 tons of [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) exported in that year.[^cite_note-telquel-94] According to the same magazine, the company officially owns 12,000 [hectares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hectares) of agricultural lands.[^cite_note-telquel-94] *Chergui*, a manufacturer of [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product), is the most recognizable brand of the group.[^cite_note-telquel-94] Between 1994 and 2004, the group was managed by Mohammed's brother-in-law Khalid Benharbit, the husband of [Princess Lalla Hasna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Lalla_Hasna).[^cite_note-telquel-94] *Les domaines* also owns the Royal Golf de Marrakech, which originally belonged to [Thami El Glaoui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thami_El_Glaoui).[^cite_note-telquel-94]

His palace's daily operating budget is reported by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* to be \$960,000, which is paid by the Moroccan state as part of a 2.576 billion dirhams/year budget as of 2014,[^cite_note-99] with much of it accounted for by the expense of personnel, clothes and car repairs.[^cite_note-forbes-92]

## Allegations of corruption

Royal involvement in business is a major topic in Morocco, but public discussion of it is sensitive. The US embassy in Rabat reported to Washington in a leaked cable that "corruption is prevalent at all levels of Moroccan society".[^cite_note-g1-16] Corruption allegedly reaches the highest levels in Morocco, where the business interests of Mohammed and some of his advisors influence "every large housing project," according to [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks) documents published in December 2010 and quoted in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*.[^cite_note-100] The documents released by the whistleblower website also quote the case of a businessman working for a US consortium, whose plans in Morocco were paralysed for months after he refused to join forces with a company linked with the royal palace. The documents quoted a company executive linked to the royal family as saying at a meeting that decisions on big investments in the kingdom were taken by only three people: the king, his secretary [Mounir Majidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mounir_Majidi), and the monarch's close friend, adviser and former classmate [Fouad Ali El Himma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouad_Ali_El_Himma). This corruption especially affects the housing sector, the WikiLeaks documents show.[^cite_note-101]

In April 2016, Mohammed's personal secretary Mounir Majidi was named in the [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

## Family and personal life



Private mansion in Paris, property of Mohammed VI

Mohammed has three sisters: [Princess Lalla Meryem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Lalla_Meryem), [Princess Lalla Asma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Lalla_Asma), and [Princess Lalla Hasna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Lalla_Hasna) and one brother, [Prince Moulay Rachid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Moulay_Rachid). The *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* noted "conflicting reports about whether the new monarch had been married on Friday night, within hours of his father's death \[in 1999]... to heed a Moroccan tradition that a King be married before he ascends the throne." A palace official subsequently denied that a marriage had taken place.[^cite_note-web.archive.org-23]

His engagement to [Salma Bennani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salma_Bennani) was announced on 12 October 2001.[^cite_note-104] They married in private in [Rabat](57400.md) on 21 March 2002,[^cite_note-105] and their wedding was celebrated at the [Dar al-Makhzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_al-Makhzen_\(Rabat\)) in Rabat on 12 and 13 July 2002. Bennani became [princess consort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_consort) with the style of [Her Royal Highness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Royal_Highness) on her marriage. They had two children: Crown Prince *Moulay* [Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulay_Hassan,_Crown_Prince_of_Morocco) (born 8 May 2003) and [Princess Lalla Khadija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Lalla_Khadija) (born 28 February 2007).[^cite_note-BBCProfile-5] The couple's divorce was announced on 21 March 2018.[^cite_note-mwn-106][^cite_note-afrik-107]

Mohammed's birthday on 21 August is a [public holiday](5426626.md),[^cite_note-108] although festivities were cancelled upon the death of his aunt in 2014.[^cite_note-109]

In 2020, Mohammed purchased an €80 million mansion in [Paris](22989.md) from the [Saudi royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_royal_family).[^cite_note-110]

### Health

Mohammed's health has been a recurring topic both within and outside Morocco.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-\:3-112]

In 2017, he underwent a successful surgery at the [Quinze-Vingts National Ophthalmology Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinze-Vingts_National_Ophthalmology_Hospital) in Paris to remove a [pterygium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterygium_\(eye\)) in his left eye.[^cite_note-113] In February 2018, he underwent a [radiofrequency ablation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiofrequency_ablation) in Paris to normalize an irregular heart rate and was visited by his siblings and children.[^cite_note-114] In September 2019, he was advised to rest for several days to recover from acute [viral pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_pneumonia), while his son Crown Prince [Moulay Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulay_Hassan) represented him at former French President [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac)'s funeral.[^cite_note-115] In June 2020, he underwent a procedure in Rabat to treat a recurrence of [atrial flutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrial_flutter).[^cite_note-116]

In June 2022, Mohammed tested positive for [COVID-19](63030231.md).[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] His personal doctor said he did not exhibit symptoms and recommended "a period of rest for a few days". *[Jeune Afrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeune_Afrique)* reported that he contracted the disease while on a private visit to France.[^cite_note-VOACovid-119] On 10 July 2022, he made his first public appearance since his COVID-19 diagnosis when he performed [Eid al-Adha](358226.md) rituals and prayers.[^cite_note-VOACovid-119]

Mohammed temporarily used a [cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistive_cane) in late 2024, reportedly due to [sciatica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciatica) which caused muscle contraction in his lower back.[^cite_note-\:3-112][^cite_note-120] In December 2024, he underwent surgery after fracturing his left shoulder in a fall.[^cite_note-121]
