On 18 June 2026, militants attacked [Diori Hamani International Airport](4271962.md) in [Niamey](57061.md), Niger, killing 13 people and injuring four others; 22 attackers were also killed, and 20 suspects were arrested. [Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin](53401088.md) (JNIM) claimed responsibility for the attack later that day.[^cite_note-1]

The attack was the second to take place at the airport in 2026. [The attack in January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2026_Diori_Hamani_International_Airport_attack) resulted in 20 deaths and four injuries.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

### Diori Hamani International Airport

[Diori Hamani International Airport](4271962.md) is an international airport and military airbase in [Niamey](57061.md), Niger's capital.[^cite_note-4] It is the largest airport in the country,[^cite_note-5] having served 363,093 passengers in 2019. The [air traffic control](48563.md) is operated by the [ASECNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASECNA), which bases one of its five air traffic zones for the continent at Niamey.[^cite_note-6]

### Perpetrators

[Al-Qaeda](1921.md) offshoot [Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin](53401088.md) is the main group behind a surge in militant [jihadist](7826589.md) attacks across several West African nations, especially [Burkina Faso](3470.md), [Niger](21373.md) and [Mali](19127.md). It formed in Mali in 2017 as a coalition of five jihadist militant groups. In the first half of 2025, JNIM carried out over 280 attacks in Burkina Faso, double the number for the same period in 2024. The group has claimed to have killed almost 1,000 people across the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) since April, almost all in Burkina Faso. It is one of the deadliest militant groups in Africa.[^cite_note-7]

## Attack

The gunmen took two taxis and a van to a security checkpoint a few hundred metres from the airport, before trying to enter the terminal.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Residents said they had finished morning prayers at about 6 a.m. when explosions and heavy gunfire occurred at the airport. The attackers, some of whom wore [explosive belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_belt), were dispersed in the surrounding neighbourhoods.[^cite_note-10] Civilians picked up [machetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machete) and sticks to defend themselves.[^cite_note-BBC-11] The airport vicinity was placed on lockdown. The attack lasted two hours,[^cite_note-12] with security forces launching a manhunt for any remaining attackers.[^cite_note-BBC-11]

Authorities said 22 attackers were killed and 20 suspects were arrested. A large cache of weapons, including [RPG-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7) grenade launchers, [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) rifles, explosives, [grenades](20353360.md), communications equipment and thousands of rounds of ammunition, was seized.[^cite_note-BBC-11]

The Ministry of Defence said eleven soldiers and two civilians were killed, with four others being wounded.[^cite_note-BBC-11]

## Aftermath

[Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin](53401088.md) claimed responsibility for the attack in a brief statement published via its official media arm, the Az-Zallaqa Foundation.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Hours after the attack, the National Civil Aviation Agency said the airport was operating normally.[^cite_note-15]

## Reactions

Several entities and surrounding nations condemned the attacks. The [Algerian Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Algeria\)) strongly condemned the attack and reaffirmed its full solidarity with the government and people of Niger.[^cite_note-16] [African Union](10634933.md) chairperson [Mahmoud Ali Youssouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Ali_Youssouf) also condemned the attack and wished the injured victims a speedy recovery.[^cite_note-17] Additionally, the [United States Embassy in Niamey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Niamey) strongly condemned the attack and commended Niger's Security and Defense Forces for their response.[^cite_note-18]

Niger's military government claimed the attack was carried out by "armed mercenaries under the funding of [French president](24899.md) [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md)". France, however, denied the claim.[^cite_note-19]

The attack "highlights a dangerous escalation in both the capability and ambition of these groups, marking a departure from localized rural insurgencies to coordinated strikes on vital national infrastructure", according to the [Armed Conflict Location and Event Data](43040226.md) (ACLED).[^cite_note-20] Ibrahim Yahaya Ibrahim, deputy project director of the [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group), said that the attack was part of a larger inclusion of cities in attacks by militant groups in the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) compared to rural areas.[^cite_note-21]
