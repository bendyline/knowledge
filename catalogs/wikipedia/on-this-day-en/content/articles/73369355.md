**Sange** is a [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo#Overview) of [Uvira Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uvira_Territory), located in the [South Kivu](446959.md) province in the eastern [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md). One of the three officially recognized communes in the territory, alongside [Kiliba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiliba) and [Luvungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luvungi), Sange had approximately 32,279 registered voters as of 2018. The commune lies at an altitude of 1,021 meters, about 24 kilometers (15 miles) west of the border with [Burundi](21490998.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:1-4] Located in the central part of the territory, Sange is bordered to the north by the [Luberizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luberizi) *groupement*, to the south and east by the Kabunambo *groupement* (Ruzizi Plain [Chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdoms_and_sectors_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)), and to the west by the [Kigoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigoma,_Bafuliiru_Chiefdom) *groupement* of the Bafuliiru Chiefdom.[^cite_note-\:02-5]

Sange is situated in a predominantly [sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) region, featuring a mix of sandy-[loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loam) and sandy-[clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) soils, making it one of the most fertile lands in Uvira Territory. Geologically, the area falls within the northwest basin of [Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika) and is characterized by a combination of [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) and [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) rock formations. A swift examination of Sange's morphology suggests that black soils of the [Chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem) group, [Solonchak soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solonchak), and [Alkali soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali_soil) characterize the region.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-4]

The region is widely known for its [2010 fuel tank explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_South_Kivu_fuel_tank_explosion), which killed at least 235 people, including about 60 children assembled to watch the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md), were burned to death, and approximately 196 were injured, some of whom suffered severe burns, according to the [United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) (MONUSCO).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Geography

The average monthly air temperature is between 22.5° and 25 °C; the monthly average daily maximum temperatures increase at the end of the dry season (30.5° to 32.5° in September), while the monthly average daily minimum temperatures are lowest during half of the dry season (14.5° to 17°C in July). The monthly relative insolation generally oscillates between 35 and 60% from October to April and between 50 and 80% from May to September, with July being the sunniest month.[^cite_note-\:2-9]

## History

The area now known as Sange was historically part of a largely uninhabited savannah within [Uvira Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uvira_Territory).[^cite_note-\:02-5] While the broader territory was initially settled by the [Fuliiru people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuliru_people), who had migrated from [Lwindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luindi_Chiefdom) (now the [Lwindi Chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luindi_Chiefdom)) to establish themselves along the [Ruzizi Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzizi_Plain) in the northern and northwestern parts of Uvira Territory,[^cite_note-\:73-10][^cite_note-\:110-11][^cite_note-\:13-12] the specific area of Sange remained largely unsettled and populated by wild animals until the early 20th century.[^cite_note-\:02-5] Sange was eventually inhabited through two significant waves of migration, beginning with displaced [Barundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Burundi) [Hutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu) from neighboring [Burundi](21490998.md) who sought and obtained [grazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing) rights in the [Ruzizi Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzizi_Plain) from the *[Mwami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwami)* of the Fuliiru.[^cite_note-\:02-5][^cite_note-\:14224-13] The second wave involved Fuliiru from the mid-plateaus, including areas such as [Lubarika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubarika), who settled in the region over time.[^cite_note-\:02-5]

The administrative organization of the area began to take formal shape with the creation of the [Uvira Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uvira_Territory) on 18 August 1928, which was divided into three customary [chiefdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdoms_and_sectors_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo): [Bafuliiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafuliiru_Chiefdom), Bavira, and Barundi.[^cite_note-\:5-14][^cite_note-\:11022-15] This structure was further codified under Ordinance-Law No. 21/91 of 25 February 1938, which outlined the territorial boundaries, with Sange becoming the *groupement* of the Ruzizi Plain Chiefdom.[^cite_note-\:5-14][^cite_note-16] In 1961, amid political and ethnic tensions, the Barundi Chiefdom was renamed the Ruzizi Plain Chiefdom under pressure from the Bafuliiru, a move that provoked strong opposition from the Barundi population and ultimately led to [armed conflict between the two groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuliru_people#Interethnic_conflict).[^cite_note-\:02-5] Barundi fled to Burundi, creating a power vacuum that led to the appointment of Makike as head of the Ruzizi Plain Chiefdom and Balibwa as chief of the Sange *groupement*.[^cite_note-\:04-17] After a series of negotiations facilitated by their *Mwami* Kinyoni, the Barundi regained control of the Ruzizi Plain Chiefdom, with Kinyoni first appointing Kinenwa as interim chief, who was later replaced by Biyaka.[^cite_note-\:04-17] During the [Mulelist Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwilu_rebellion) of 1964, the entire population fled to Burundi, but they gradually returned in the following years. The [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War) resulted in significant human losses, widespread looting, and the displacement of residents to [Tanzania](30118.md), while the [Second Congo War](877492.md) also aggravated the situation and led to severe destruction and [Mai-Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai-Mai) uprisings in the region.[^cite_note-\:02-5]

By July 2015, unidentified gunmen caused six deaths in a month across [Bwegera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwegera), Sange, Rukobero, Namijembwe, and Rushima.[^cite_note-\:28-18] Victims included a man shot on his way home in Bwegera, a herder in Namijembwe, a middle-aged man in Sange, and the local chief of Rukobero, who was murdered in his own house.[^cite_note-\:28-18] On 31 July 2020, an intoxicated [FARDC](8029.md) soldier fired multiple shots that killed at least 12 people and injuring nine others in Sange.[^cite_note-19]

### Administrative history and divisions

The local administrative history traces back to Chief Kere, a [Vira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vira_people) leader credited with founding the villages of Kibogoye (located in Sange), Kagando (currently within the [Ruzizi Plain Chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uvira_Territory#Chiefdoms,_administrative_posts,_and_communes)), and Kakamba (now situated in the [Luvungi *groupement*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luvungi)).[^cite_note-\:04-17] During this period, Sange lay in the area known as the "Bafuliiru territory" (present-day [Bafuliiru Chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafuliiru_Chiefdom)), under the authority of *[Mwami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwami)* Kalingishi, who granted extensive [grazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing) lands to Burundian pastoralists settled in the Ruzizi Plain.[^cite_note-\:04-17] Over time, however, the Barundi community, led by Ndabagoye, came under considerable European influence, leading to administrative reorganization that transferred authority from the Barundi to Chief Kere.[^cite_note-\:04-17]

Following this transition, a Burundian notable named Ndalishize of the Ndorogwe family governed under Chief Kere's supervision, and upon his death, he was succeeded by his son Ngarukiye, who was later deposed due to misconduct and replaced by Mr. Biyaka, also of Burundian origin.[^cite_note-\:04-17] Biyaka established a secondary customary tribunal from the Ndalishize lineage and maintained cooperative relations with Ngarukiye throughout his tenure, ruling between 1977 and 1978, after which Bigere Nankuma succeeded him until he died in 1988. That same year, Sange was officially detached from the Ruzizi Plain Chiefdom in 1988, when it was elevated to the status of a *Cité*.[^cite_note-\:02-5] Leadership then passed to Mkwana Zakabemba, appointed as the first *Chef de cité* of Sange.[^cite_note-\:04-17]

#### Commune status

On 9 June 2013, the provincial governor submitted a proposal to the [Provincial Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_South_Kivu) requesting the elevation of several agglomerations to the rank of *[ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)* ([city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)) and *[commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)* (municipality).[^cite_note-\:42-20] The Assembly approved the proposal through Decision No. 09/200/PLENIERE/ASPRO/SK of 7 October 2009. Implementation proceeded through Decree No. 012/14 of 18 February 2012 and was consolidated in Decree No. 13/029 of 13 June 2013, which, in its Article 1, elevated Sange, [Kiliba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiliba), and [Luvungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luvungi), together with Bulambika, Fizi, Hombo-Sud, Kavumu, [Kamanyola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamanyola), Lulimba-Misisi, [Lulingu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulingu), [Minembwe](20041711.md), [Minova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minova), Nyangezi, Nyabibwe, and Swima, to the status of commune.[^cite_note-\:42-20] In July 2015, however, the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Matata_government) suspended the enforcement of these decrees.[^cite_note-21] On 27 December 2018, an appendix to the electoral law was passed which listed Sange, Kiliba, and Luvungi as communes of [Uvira Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uvira_Territory).[^cite_note-\:3-22] In 2024, Sange was described as being administratively divided into nine *quartiers* ([quarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarters_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)): Kahungwe, Kajembo, Kibogoye, Kinanira, Kyanyanda, Musenyi, Nyakabere 1, Nyakabere 2, and Rutanga.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## 2010 fuel tank explosion

Main article: [2010 South Kivu fuel tank explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_South_Kivu_fuel_tank_explosion)

On 2 July 2010, Sange experienced a tragedy that resulted in 235 fatalities and 198 injuries due to a [tanker truck](2863776.md) explosion.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The tank truck, carrying 59,000 liters of [gasoline](23639.md) from [Tanzania](30118.md), overturned on a narrow and dangerous road that crossed the center of Sange, 60 miles south of the city of [Bukavu](448570.md).[^cite_note-29] The fire quickly spread from the truck, causing devastation. Some people died while attempting to collect leaking fuel, while most of the deaths occurred among those who were indoors watching the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) match.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Additionally, some individuals were caught in the fire while trying to flee and were reduced to ashes. The fire also spread to approximately 20 houses, which were primarily made of mud and covered with straw or sheet metal, resulting in their destruction.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Demographics

Around 1940, the first [Catholic school](1452366.md) in the region was established in Rutanga. Sange hosts seven primary schools and four secondary schools.[^cite_note-\:042-34] In the 1930s, a health post was founded in Rutanga, which later evolved into the Centre de santé de Sange. Sange has three [health centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_health_center) and one health post located in Nyakabere.[^cite_note-\:042-34]

## Economy

The economy is primarily based on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), which constitutes the main source of livelihood for the majority of residents. The principal subsistence crops include [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana), [peanut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), which are cultivated across the fertile lowlands of the region.[^cite_note-\:042-34] Alongside [crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop) production, [livestock](25160767.md) breeding ranks as the second most important activity, with families raising [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle) and [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat) for household consumption and to earn income.[^cite_note-\:042-34]

Complementing these two sectors is a traditional [blacksmithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacksmith) craft, which historically played a key role in the local economy. Using the *Matare* stone, local artisans forged various agricultural and domestic implements such as [hoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoe_\(tool\)), [spears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spear), and [knives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knife).[^cite_note-\:042-34] When [Belgian colonizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo) introduced [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) and [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) cultivation in the 1950s, Sange became more connected to regional trade circuits. At present, the local economy continues to rely heavily on farming, livestock raising, and small-scale trading.[^cite_note-\:042-34]
