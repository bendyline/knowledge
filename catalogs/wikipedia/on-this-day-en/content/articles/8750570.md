The **president pro tempore of the Senate of the Philippines** ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Pangulong pro tempore ng Senado ng Pilipinas*) is the second highest-ranking official of the [Senate of the Philippines](299521.md). During the absence of the [president of the Senate](8108148.md), the president [pro tempore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_tempore) presides over the Senate.

By tradition, the president pro tempore is elected by a majority vote immediately after the Senate president during the opening of a new Congress, or whenever the position becomes vacant, whether through a motion or the incumbent's resignation. Although the American custom of electing the [most senior member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest-serving_senators_of_the_Philippines#Seniority_in_the_Philippine_Senate) of the majority party as the president pro tempore is not exclusively followed, there have been instances in past Senates when senior members of the majority party have been elected to the position.[^cite_note-1]

The incumbent president pro tempore of the Senate of the Philippines is [Tito Sotto](3873709.md) since June 17, 2026.[^cite_note-\:10-2]

## Powers and duties

According to Rule IV, Section 4 of the Rules of the Senate, the president pro tempore is mandated to discharge the powers and duties of the Senate president in the following cases:[^cite_note-3]

- a. When the President is absent for one or more days;

- b. When the President is temporarily incapacitated; or

- c. In the event of the resignation, removal, death or absolute incapacity of the President.

Section 5 further notes that the president pro tempore shall serve as acting president in cases specified in Section 4(c) until the Senate elects a new president.

Section 20 of Rule X of the Senate Rules also provide that along with the [floor leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor_leaders_of_the_Senate_of_the_Philippines), the president pro tempore is an [*ex officio* member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member) of all [permanent committees](8578506.md), and may also be elected chairperson of any of the committees.

## History

### Pre-martial law period

The Philippine Senate unanimously elected [Rafael Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Palma) as its first president pro tempore upon its establishment in 1916.[^cite_note-\:3-4] He was succeeded by [Espiridion Guanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espiridion_Guanco) in the [5th Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Philippine_Legislature).[^cite_note-5] [Sergio Osmeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a) became the first president pro tempore to serve as [acting Senate president](8108148.md#Acting_Senate_president) when [Manuel Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) went ill in 1930.[^cite_note-6] He is also the longest to serve as president pro tempore in Senate history. In 1932, [José Clarín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Clar%C3%ADn) acted as the Senate president when Quezon went on leave.[^cite_note-7] Upon Clarín's death in 1935, [José Avelino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Avelino) would briefly serve as president pro tempore until the Senate was abolished in favor of a unicameral [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Philippines).

The Senate was then restored by the constitutional amendment in 1940, but senators elected in 1941 were not able to assume office due to the outbreak of [World War II](32927.md). Congress would only reconvene by 1945, and [Elpidio Quirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elpidio_Quirino) was elected as president pro tempore alongside [Manuel Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Roxas) as Senate president.[^cite_note-\:0-8] When the Senate encountered multiple deadlocks in electing a new Senate president at the start of the [2nd Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Congress_of_the_Philippines) in January 1952, president pro tempore [Quintín Paredes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quint%C3%ADn_Paredes) presided over the sessions until March 5, 1952, when he was formally elected president of the Senate after [Felisberto Verano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felisberto_Verano) of the [Nacionalista Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalista_Party) voted for him, a member of the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party).[^cite_note-9]

[Jose Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Roy) served as the last president pro tempore of the Senate when it was abolished for a second time in 1972 upon the declaration of martial law.[^cite_note-10]

### Fifth Republic



Senate President [Koko Pimentel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koko_Pimentel) (right) congratulates [Ralph Recto](7787924.md) (left) after his election as president pro tempore of the Senate on February 27, 2017.

[Teofisto Guingona Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teofisto_Guingona_Jr.) served as president pro tempore of the reestablished Senate from 1987 to 1990, and was succeeded by [Sotero Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotero_Laurel).

In 1993, the Senate elected [Leticia Ramos-Shahani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leticia_Ramos-Shahani) as its first female president pro tempore.[^cite_note-11] Ramos-Shahani was the first female lawmaker in history to serve as a deputy presiding officer in either houses of Congress. When [Marcelo Fernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Fernan) resigned the Senate presidency in June 1999 due to failing health, president pro tempore [Blas Ople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blas_Ople) was designated as acting presiding officer, until he himself was elected president nearly a month later. [Franklin Drilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Drilon) was temporarily designated by Fernan as the Senate's officer-in-charge before Ople's return from a trip to Switzerland and his assumption as the chamber's acting president.[^cite_note-12]

On August 12, 2002, [Manny Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Villar) stepped down as president pro tempore to be elected chairperson of the Senate committees on [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Finance) and [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations). [Juan Flavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Flavier) was subsequently elected to succeed Villar.[^cite_note-\:9-13]

[Juan Ponce Enrile](3322486.md) resigned as Senate president on June 5, 2013.[^cite_note-14] President pro tempore [Jinggoy Estrada](2395334.md) served as acting president until Franklin Drilon was elected president of the Senate on July 22, 2013. Drilon, who had earlier served as president pro tempore under Senate president [Koko Pimentel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koko_Pimentel), was later removed from the position along with other [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party) senators from their committee chairmanships following a motion by [Manny Pacquiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Pacquiao) to declare the post vacant. He was succeeded by [Ralph Recto](7787924.md), a fellow Liberal and former [minority leader](8767721.md), who had previously served as Drilon's president pro tempore during the [16th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Congress_of_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-15]

In June 2022, [Juan Miguel Zubiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Miguel_Zubiri) became the first [majority floor leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_Floor_Leader_of_the_Senate_of_the_Philippines) to concurrently serve as president pro tempore after he was elected to the position to allow the Senate to discharge its administrative functions without interruption while then-Senate president [Tito Sotto](3873709.md) was nearing the end of his term.[^cite_note-\:1-16] [Loren Legarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loren_Legarda), then the most senior senator in the [19th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Congress_of_the_Philippines), was elected on July 25, 2022, as the second female Senate president pro tempore.[^cite_note-\:2-17]

On September 8, 2025, [Panfilo Lacson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panfilo_Lacson) was elected president pro tempore of the Senate,[^cite_note-18] the oldest senator in history to assume the position. Legarda succeeded Lacson in an [election on May 11, 2026](83157100.md), her second time serving in the position.[^cite_note-\:7-19]

On June 3, 2026, [Sherwin Gatchalian](22207546.md) was elected president pro tempore, replacing Loren Legarda. The 11-member minority bloc, together with Senator [Francis Escudero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Escudero), was present during the day's session, constituting a [quorum](237014.md) of 12 out of the 22 senators then under the Senate's jurisdiction, excluding detained senator [Jinggoy Estrada](2395334.md) and [Ronald dela Rosa](50966529.md), who is in hiding following the issuance of an arrest warrant by the [International Criminal Court](14880.md).[^cite_note-\:8-20] Gatchalian, who served as temporary presiding officer prior to his election, cited *[Avelino v. Cuenco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avelino_v._Cuenco)*, a Supreme Court decision that resolved a dispute concerning the Senate presidency.[^cite_note-21]

On June 17, 2026, Gatchalian was formally elected Senate president, and [Tito Sotto](3873709.md) succeeded him as president pro tempore, becoming the second-oldest senator to serve in the position.[^cite_note-\:10-2]

## List of Senate presidents pro tempore



[Rafael Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Palma) was the first president pro tempore of the Senate.



[Sergio Osmeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a) was the longest-serving president pro tempore of the Senate.



[Leticia Ramos-Shahani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leticia_Ramos-Shahani) was the first female president pro tempore of the Senate.



[Tito Sotto](3873709.md) is the current president pro tempore of the Senate.

Since the office was established in 1916, 32 individuals are known to have served as president pro tempore of the Senate.

All senators from 1941 onwards were elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large), with the whole Philippines as one constituency.
