**Deutsche Lufthansa AG** (German: [\[ˌdɔʏtʃə ˈlʊfthanzaː ʔaːˈɡeː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/11/De-Lufthansa.ogg/De-Lufthansa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/11/De-Lufthansa.ogg/De-Lufthansa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), trading as **Lufthansa Group**, is a German aviation holding company. Its principal airline, **Lufthansa German Airlines** (branded as **Lufthansa**) is the [flag carrier](465816.md) of Germany.[^cite_note-8] Lufthansa Group is the [second-largest airline group in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_airlines_in_Europe) by passengers carried, as well as [largest in Europe and fourth largest in the world by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_airlines_in_the_world).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Lufthansa Airlines is also one of the five founding members of [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance), the world's largest [airline alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_alliance), established in 1997.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Lufthansa Group owns several other airlines, including [Austrian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Airlines), [Brussels Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airlines), [Discover Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discover_Airlines), [Eurowings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurowings), [ITA Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITA_Airways), and [Swiss International Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_International_Air_Lines). The group also owns several aviation-related companies, including [Global Load Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Load_Control), [Lufthansa Consulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Consulting), [Lufthansa Flight Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_Training), [Lufthansa Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Systems) and [Lufthansa Technik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Technik).

The modern Deutsche Lufthansa AG was founded as **Aktiengesellschaft für Luftverkehrsbedarf** (**Luftag**) on 6 January 1953 by staff of the former [Deutsche Luft Hansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Luft_Hansa), Germany's national airline founded in 1926. While Deutsche Luft Hansa played a significant role in the development of commercial aviation in Germany, it was liquidated in 1951 due to its association with [the Nazi regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nazi_Germany) during [World War II](32927.md). Luftag adopted the branding of the former flag carrier by acquiring the Luft Hansa name and logo in 1954.

The corporate headquarters of Deutsche Lufthansa AG are in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne),[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Group:_Annual_Report_2020-13] Lufthansa's main operations base, the *Lufthansa Aviation Center*, is located at [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport), the airline's primary hub.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The airline also maintains a secondary hub at [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport), along with its *Flight Operations Centre*.[^cite_note-16]

Lufthansa's first aircraft, a [Convair 340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_240) *(type pictured)*, was delivered in August 1954.

Lufthansa traces its history to 1926[^cite_note-17] when [Deutsche Luft Hansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Luft_Hansa) was formed in [Berlin](3354.md) by the merger of [Deutscher Aero Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Luft-Reederei), [the world's sixth-oldest airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_by_foundation_date), and [Junkers Luftverkehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Luftverkehr).[^cite_note-18] Deutsche Luft Hansa was Germany's [flag carrier](465816.md) until the outbreak of [war](32927.md) when it came under the command of the [Luftwaffe](17885.md). During the war, the airline was focused mainly on [aircraft maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_maintenance) and repair in which [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) was employed on the site of [Berlin Tempelhof Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Tempelhof_Airport). Following the [surrender of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Germany) and the ensuing [Allied occupation of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_occupation_of_Germany), all aircraft in the country were seized and Deutsche Luft Hansa was dissolved. The remaining assets were liquidated on 1 January 1951.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

In an effort to create a new national airline, a company called **Aktiengesellschaft für Luftverkehrsbedarf** (**Luftag**)[^cite_note-Flight_International-21] was founded in the city of [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) in [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) on 6 January 1953, with many of its staff having worked for the pre-war Deutsche Luft Hansa.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]



Lufthansa Lockheed L-1049 Super Constellation operating a transatlantic scheduled service from Hamburg to Montreal and Chicago in 1956

West Germany had not yet been granted full sovereignty over its airspace, so it was not known when the new airline could become operational. Nevertheless, in 1953, Luftag placed orders for four [Convair CV-340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_CV-340) and four [Lockheed L-1049 Super Constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-1049_Super_Constellation) aircraft and set up a maintenance base at [Hamburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Airport).[^cite_note-Flight_International-21][^cite_note-lex-24] On 6 August 1954, Luftag acquired the name and logo of the liquidated Deutsche Lufthansa for [DM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark) 30,000 (equivalent to €41,000 today),[^cite_note-lex-24] thus continuing the tradition of a German flag carrier with that name.

On 1 April 1955, Lufthansa won approval to commence operation of scheduled domestic flights.[^cite_note-lex-24] The airline's initial network linked [Hamburg](13467.md), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), [Frankfurt](10992.md), Cologne, and [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-Flight_International-1955-25] International flights started on 15 May 1955, to [London](17867.md), [Paris](22989.md), and [Madrid](41188263.md),[^cite_note-Flight_International-1955-25][^cite_note-26] followed by Super Constellation flights to [New York City](645042.md) from 1 June of that year,[^cite_note-Flight_International-1955-25] and across the South Atlantic from August 1956.

However, the political realities of the time presented challenges for the airline. The United States, Soviet Union, Britain and France did not allow Lufthansa to fly to any part of the divided [Berlin](3354.md) during the [division of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Germany_\(1945%E2%80%9390\)).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The airline had hoped this would only be a temporary matter and planned to move its headquarters and primary [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) back to the German capital once the political situation changed, plans that ultimately never came to fruition,[^cite_note-Flight_International-21] even after the lifting of these restrictions owing to [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990 and the opening of the new [Berlin-Brandenburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Brandenburg_Airport) in 2020.[^cite_note-29] Instead, Lufthansa turned Frankfurt Airport into its primary hub in 1958.

The airline also embarked on a marketing campaign to encourage travelers to consider visiting West Germany as it rebuilt in the wake of World War II and to use its hub to connect to other locations across Europe. By 1963, the airline, initially limited in its public relations efforts, had become a major purveyor of West Germany's image abroad.[^cite_note-30]

During this time, [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) attempted to establish its own "[Lufthansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Lufthansa_\(East_Germany\))" airline in 1955, but legal challenges from the West German carrier led to its abandonment. East Germany subsequently launched [Interflug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interflug) as its national carrier in 1963.[^cite_note-31]

### 1960s: Introduction of jetliners



A [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) at [Hamburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Airport) in 1984, shortly before the type was retired

Lufthansa embraced the [jet age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_age) in 1958 by ordering four [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) aircraft. This marked a significant leap forward, allowing them to launch jet flights between Frankfurt and New York City in March 1960. To further bolster their jet fleet, [Boeing 720B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_720B) aircraft (a 707 derivative for shorter flights from shorter runways) were later acquired.[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Technik-2025-32]

Lufthansa's network continued to expand throughout the early 1960s. In February 1961, their Far East routes extended beyond [Bangkok](56615.md) to include [Hong Kong](13404.md) and Tokyo.[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Technik-2025-32] Africa saw additions in 1962 with [Lagos](85232.md), Nigeria, and [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa joining the network.



Lufthansa [Boeing 727-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727-100), 1978

Innovation continued with the introduction of the [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) in 1964.[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Technik-2025-32] This enabled Lufthansa to launch a pioneering [Polar route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_route) from Frankfurt to Tokyo via Anchorage in May of that year. Further expansion plans were solidified in February 1965 with the order of twenty-one [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) aircraft, entering service in 1968.



Lufthansa was the launch customer of the [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737). The image shows an original 737-100 at [Hannover Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannover_Airport) in 1968.

Lufthansa's role in Boeing's history is noteworthy. They hold the distinction of being the first customers for the Boeing 737[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Technik-2025-32] and one of only four buyers of the initial 737-100 model (alongside [NASA](18426568.md), [Malaysia-Singapore Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia-Singapore_Airlines), and [Avianca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avianca)). While NASA technically had the first built 737 airframe, it was ultimately delivered last and originally intended for Lufthansa, making them the first foreign launch customer for a Boeing airliner.

### 1970s–1980s: The wide-body era



Lufthansa became the first European airline to introduce the Boeing 747 on regular passenger services in 1970. Seen here is a 747-200 at [Düsseldorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport) in 1985.



Lufthansa operated the high-capacity [Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300)-600 on domestic and European routes until 2009

The [wide-body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-body) era for Lufthansa started with a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) flight on 26 April 1970. It was followed by the introduction of the [DC-10-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10) on 14 January 1974, and the first [Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300) in 1976. In 1979, Lufthansa and [Swissair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swissair) became launch customers for the [Airbus A310](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310) with an order for 25 aircraft.[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Technik-2025-32]

The company's fleet modernisation programme for the 1990s began on 29 June 1985, with an order for fifteen [Airbus A320s](164933.md) and seven [Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300)-600s. Ten [Boeing 737-300s](15103676.md) were ordered a few days later. All were delivered between 1987 and 1992. Lufthansa also bought [Airbus A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321), [Airbus A340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340), and [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) aircraft.[^cite_note-Lufthansa_Technik-2025-32]

In 1987, Lufthansa, together with [Air France](14998012.md), [Iberia](98613.md), and [Scandinavian Airlines](162868.md), founded [Amadeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadeus_IT_Group), an IT company (also known as a [GDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_reservations_system)) that would enable travel agencies to sell the founders and other airlines' products from a single system.[^cite_note-33]

### 1990s–2000s: Further expansion

Following [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) on 3 October 1990, Lufthansa swiftly reintegrated Berlin into its network, marking the city's return as a key destination within 25 days.

The mid-1990s saw a period of strategic restructuring for Lufthansa. This involved the establishment of independent operating companies within Lufthansa Group, specializing in areas like maintenance ([Lufthansa Technik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Technik)), cargo ([Lufthansa Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Cargo)), and information technology ([Lufthansa Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Systems)). Over time, the group further expanded through the addition of [LSG Sky Chefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSG_Sky_Chefs) (catering), [Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_\(airline\)) (leisure travel), and [Lufthansa CityLine](1759685.md) (regional operations).[^cite_note-34]

Lufthansa joined [Air Canada](145623.md), [Scandinavian Airlines](162868.md), [Thai Airways International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Airways_International), and [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) to form [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) on 14 May 1997, the world's first multilateral [airline alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_alliance).[^cite_note-35]

Lufthansa actively addressed its historical legacy during this period. In 1999, the airline participated in a German initiative aimed at resolving wartime misdeeds, acknowledging the use of forced labor by its predecessor, Deutsche Luft Hansa. As part of the initiative, Lufthansa also reportedly paid tens of millions of German marks.[^cite_note-Steffen-2016-36] Additionally, a historical study was commissioned to shed light on this aspect of the company's past. However, Lufthansa was criticised for not publishing the resulting study for more than a decade.[^cite_note-Steffen-2016-36]

The early 2000s witnessed Lufthansa demonstrate remarkable resilience in the face of industry challenges. Despite significant industry losses following the [11 September attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_September_attacks), the airline maintained profitability and strategically avoided workforce reductions.[^cite_note-37] Lufthansa became the launch customer for the [Connexion by Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connexion_by_Boeing) in-flight internet connectivity in 2004.[^cite_note-38]

Lufthansa further solidified its position as a major European airline group through strategic acquisitions. The acquisitions of Swiss International Air Lines in 2005, Brussels Airlines (staged between 2009 and 2017),[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] and Austrian Airlines in 2009[^cite_note-42] expanded the group's reach and network capabilities.



A [Boeing 747-8I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-8I) and [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380)-800 of Lufthansa at Frankfurt Airport. The A380 and 747-8, together with the [Airbus A350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350), formed the backbone for Lufthansa's long-haul routes in the 2010s.

At the end of the 2000s, Lufthansa made a large commitment to very large aircraft, introducing the first of 14 [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) in 2010 and becoming the launch customer for the [Boeing 747-8I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-8I) in 2012, eventually purchasing 19 of the type.[^cite_note-43]

### 2010s: Losses and strikes

In 2011 Lufthansa planned significant growth at [Berlin Brandenburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Brandenburg_Airport) for the originally planned opening in 2012 with many new connections from Berlin.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

After a loss of 298 million euros in the first quarter of 2010 and another 13 million loss in the year 2011 due to the economic recession and restructuring costs, Deutsche Lufthansa AG cut 3,500 administrative positions or around 20 percent of the clerical total of 16,800 in 2012.[^cite_note-46] Lufthansa announced a restructuring program called SCORE to improve its operating profit. As a part of the restructuring plan, the company started to transfer all short-haul flights outside its hubs in Frankfurt, Munich, and Düsseldorf to the company's re-branded [low-cost carrier](428422.md) [Germanwings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanwings).[^cite_note-47]

In September 2013, Lufthansa Group announced its biggest order, for 59 wide-body aircraft valued at more than 14 billion euros at list prices. Earlier in the same year, Lufthansa placed an order for 100 next-generation narrow-body aircraft.[^cite_note-48]

The group has had a long-standing dispute with the Vereinigung Cockpit union, which has advocated for a system in which pilots can retire at the age of 55, and 60% of their pay be retained. Lufthansa pilots were joined by pilots from the group's budget carrier Germanwings to stage a nationwide strike in support of their demands in April 2014 which lasted three days. The pilots staged a six-hour strike at the end of the summer holidays in September 2014, which caused the cancellation of 200 Lufthansa flights and 100 Germanwings flights.[^cite_note-49]

During the course of the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), part of the fleet was branded "Fanhansa".[^cite_note-50]

In November 2014, Lufthansa signed an outsourcing deal worth \$1.25 billion with [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) that will see the US company take over the airline's IT infrastructure services division and staff.[^cite_note-51]

Main article: [Germanwings Flight 9525](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanwings_Flight_9525)

Carsten Spohr, Lufthansa's CEO, called the March 2015 [Germanwings Flight 9525](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanwings_Flight_9525) crash, "the darkest day for Lufthansa in its 60-year history", when co-pilot Andreas Lubitz intentionally flew an aircraft into a mountain, killing himself, 144 passengers and 5 crew members.[^cite_note-52] Nonetheless, damage control by Spohr and his team was poor according to several sources. It was revealed that Lubitz suffered from a severe case of depression and mental disorders and had intentionally crashed Germanwings Flight 9525 into the French Alps, killing all 150 aboard whereas Spohr had misleadingly said the co-pilot "was 100% airworthy without any restrictions, without any conditions".[^cite_note-53]

In June 2015, Lufthansa announced plans to close its small long-haul base at [Düsseldorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport) for economic reasons by October 2015. At the time, the base consisted of two [Airbus A340-300s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340-300) rotating between [Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey) and [Chicago](6886.md). As a result, service to Chicago from Düsseldorf was first made seasonal, suspended for the winter 2015 season, and then canceled altogether.[^cite_note-54] Service to Newark, however, was initially maintained. From the winter 2015 schedule through the end of the winter 2016 schedule, Düsseldorf was served by aircraft which also flew the Munich-Newark route. The Düsseldorf-Newark route ended on 30 November 2018, which was operated with an [Airbus A330-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330-300) aircraft. Their base was officially closed in March 2019.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

On 22 March 2016, Lufthansa ended [Boeing 737-500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-500) operations.[^cite_note-57] The airline's last Boeing 737 (a 737-300) was retired on 29 October 2016, after a flight from [Milan](36511.md) to Frankfurt. Lufthansa operated the 737 in several variants for almost 50 years, the first aircraft having been delivered on 27 December 1967.[^cite_note-58]

On 1 September 2015, Lufthansa implemented a 16 euro surcharge on [Global Distribution System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Distribution_System) bookings. The surcharge is payable unless tickets are purchased from the airline's website, or its airport service centres and ticket counters. Amadeus said the new model would make "comparison and transparency more difficult because travellers will now be forced to go to multiple channels to search for the best fares."[^cite_note-59] For the period between 1–14 September, the airline experienced a 16.1% drop in revenue, indicating to some that the new fee backfired, although the airline maintains that the decrease was due to the pilot strike, and "other seasonal effects".[^cite_note-60]

In October 2017, Lufthansa took over 81 aircraft from the insolvent [Air Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Berlin). The total purchase price for the shares acquired by Lufthansa from the insolvency estate of Air Berlin amounted to around 210 million euros.[^cite_note-AirBerlin1-5]

On 4 December 2017, Lufthansa became the first European airline to receive the [Skytrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skytrax) 5-star certification.[^cite_note-61] As stated by Skytrax, a key factor in the positive rating was the announcement of a new Business Class cabin and seating that was expected to be introduced in 2020.[^cite_note-62] While this makes Lufthansa the 10th airline to be holding this award, in reality the 5th star was given to a product that was supposed to be introduced two years after the evaluation.[^cite_note-63] In celebration, Lufthansa painted an [Airbus A320](164933.md) and a [Boeing 747-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-8) in the "5 Starhansa" livery.[^cite_note-64]

In March 2018, Lufthansa and other airlines like [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways) and [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) accepted a request from Beijing to list Taiwan as part of China.[^cite_note-65]

In March 2019, Lufthansa ordered 20 [Boeing 787-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787-9) and an additional 20 [Airbus A350-900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350-900) for its own and the group's fleet replacement and expansion. Also, the airline announced it would sell six [A380 aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380-800) back to Airbus, beginning in 2022.

Pro-migration activists from Germany have criticised Lufthansa for performing deportation flights on behalf of the German government.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-Asylum-activists-67] In 2019, 4,573 people were deported on their planes, while their subsidiary Eurowings performed 1,312 deportations.[^cite_note-68] This totals more than 25% of deportations in Germany in 2019. At least two deportees perished during transport.[^cite_note-Tod-69][^cite_note-Asylum-activists-67]

### 2020s: COVID-19 pandemic and recovery



15 aircraft of Lufthansa, parked at [Berlin Brandenburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Brandenburg_Airport) on 21 March 2020 due to the cancellation of 95 percent of all flights of the airline on 19 March 2020

On 19 March 2020, Lufthansa cancelled 95 percent of all flights due to a [travel ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_restrictions_related_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic) because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-70]

On 14 May, Lufthansa Group announced that it planned to operate 1,800 weekly flights by the end of June.[^cite_note-71] The company's recovery plans involved high-density cargo to replace paying customers.[^cite_note-cbcluft-72] All Lufthansa Group required all passengers to wear a mask while aboard.[^cite_note-cbcluft-72]

On 25 June, Deutsche Lufthansa AG shareholders accepted a [€](9472.md)9,000,000,000 bailout, consisting of capital measures and the participation of the Economic Stabilisation Fund (WSF) of the [Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy](69466069.md).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] The measures, which passed after initial opposition by principal shareholder [Heinz Hermann Thiele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Hermann_Thiele), gave the government a 20% stake in the airline.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

In January 2021, Lufthansa CEO Spohr announced that the entire currently stored [Airbus A340-600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340-600) fleet will be retired with immediate effect and not return to service anymore.[^cite_note-80] This decision was later overturned, with several A340-600 aircraft returning to service in 2021 after several months in storage. In June 2021, Lufthansa said it wants to repay state aid it received during the pandemic before Germany's federal election in September 2021 if possible.[^cite_note-81] Also in June 2021, Lufthansa said it would change its communications to adopt a more [gender-neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-neutral_language) and inclusive language. It will remove greetings such as "[Ladies and Gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salutation)".[^cite_note-82]

In January 2022, Lufthansa admitted it had operated over 18,000 empty flights to keep airport slots during the pandemic.[^cite_note-83]

In March 2022, Lufthansa originally confirmed that its entire [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) fleet would be retired, having been in storage since early 2020.[^cite_note-84] This decision was reversed in June 2022, with plans to now return up to five aircraft from storage by 2023 to be based at [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport). There is also an option to return all eight remaining A380 aircraft to service by 2024, as six of the formerly 14 have already been sold.[^cite_note-85]

In May 2022, [Skytrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skytrax) demoted Lufthansa from its aforementioned 5-star rating which it held since 2017 as the first European carrier to do so, to an overall 4-star rating.[^cite_note-86]

In 2023, the airline was affected by an IT glitch, leaving thousands of passengers stranded around the world. According to the German air traffic control agency, the airline's flights were redirected from Frankfurt to other airports due to an IT glitch. The issue was reportedly caused after construction work cut through fiber optic cables in the city.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

In May 2023, Lufthansa Group announced an agreement with the Italian Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) to acquire a 41 per cent stake in [ITA Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITA_Airways). Part of the agreement allows Lufthansa to acquire the remaining shares at a later date. In May 2024, Lufthansa rolled out a new safety video in line with the Allegris launch.[^cite_note-89]

In 2025, some of Lufthansa's Boeing 747 would be refitted with its Business Class seats, being split into half, of which the first half included new luxurious seats, while the other half would still have the original first class seats.[^cite_note-90] In September Lufthansa announced plans to cut 4000 of its 10,000 administrative jobs despite 1.4 billion Euro revenue in 2024.[^cite_note-91]

On 23 April 2026, Lufthansa announced it would withdraw 27 aircraft from its CityLine fleet. This decision was made to help the company save money because of high fuel costs and labour issues.[^cite_note-92]

#### Investment history

Lufthansa bought a 19% stake in [JetBlue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue) in December 2007 and entered a code-sharing agreement with the airline. It was the first major investment by a European carrier in an American carrier since the [EU–U.S. Open Skies Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93U.S._Open_Skies_Agreement) came into effect in 2008. Lufthansa sold its stake in JetBlue in March 2015.

On 28 October 2008, Lufthansa exercised its option to purchase a further 60% share in [BMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Midland_International) (in addition to the 20% Lufthansa already owned), this resulted in a dispute with the former owner [Sir Michael Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Michael_Bishop). Both parties reached an agreement at the end of June 2009, and the acquisition took place with effect from 1 July 2009.[^cite_note-108] Lufthansa acquired the remaining 20% from Scandinavian Airlines on 1 November 2009, taking complete control of BMI.[^cite_note-109]

On 25 May 2023, Lufthansa announced it would pay €325 million to acquire 41% of [ITA Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITA_Airways). Lufthansa also has an option to buy the rest of ITA at a later date, with the purchase price depending on ITA's business performance.[^cite_note-110]

In January 2025, Lufthansa took a 10% stake in the Latvian flag carrier [AirBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic).[^cite_note-111]

On 20 November 2025, Lufthansa Group announced its intention to bid in the privatization of [Portugal](23033.md)’s national carrier — [TAP Air Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAP_Air_Portugal), with an initial intention to acquire a minority stake.[^cite_note-112]

### Logistics

Lufthansa Group's Logistics business segment includes [Lufthansa Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Cargo), as well as the airfreight container management specialist Jettainer group, the time\:matters group, which specialises in particularly urgent shipments, the subsidiary Heyworld, which specialises in tailored solutions for the e-commerce sector, and CB Customs Broker, the customs and customs clearance specialist. This business segment also encompasses Lufthansa Group’s 50% stake in the cargo airline [AeroLogic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AeroLogic). Lufthansa Cargo also has equity investments in various handling companies and smaller companies involved in various aspects of the sector’s digitalisation.[^cite_note-117]

### MRO

Lufthansa Group's MRO segment consists of [Lufthansa Technik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Technik), the world’s leading manufacturer-independent provider of maintenance, repair and overhaul services (MRO) for civilian commercial aircraft.[^cite_note-118]

In 2008, Lufthansa Technik restored a [Junkers Ju 52/3m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_52/3m) built in 1936 to [airworthiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airworthiness); this aircraft was in use on the 10-hour Berlin to Rome route, across the [Alps](981.md), in the 1930s. Lufthansa is now restoring a [Lockheed Super Constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Super_Constellation), using parts from three such aircraft bought at auctions. Lufthansa's Super Constellations and L1649 "Starliners" served routes such as [Hamburg](13467.md)–[Madrid](41188263.md)–[Dakar](44251.md)–[Caracas](55289.md)–[Santiago](51572.md). Lufthansa Technik recruits retired employees and volunteers for skilled labour.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

### Additional businesses and group functions

The Additional Businesses and Group Functions segment include Lufthansa Group's service companies, including [Lufthansa Aviation Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Aviation_Training) and [Lufthansa Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Systems), as well as the Group Functions for Lufthansa Group.[^cite_note-121]

Until April 2009, Lufthansa inventory and departure control systems, based on [Unisys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unisys), were managed by [LH Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Systems). Lufthansa reservations systems were outsourced to Amadeus in the early 1990s. Following a decision to outsource all components of the [Passenger Service System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_Service_System), the functions were outsourced to the Altéa platform managed by [Amadeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadeus_IT_Group).

Since 2007 [Lufthansa Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Systems), the IT services provider branch of the group relies on solutions by [Actian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actian), such as [Ingres database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingres_database) and the [OpenROAD platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenROAD), to power its Lido/FlightPlanning solution, which is used by around 300 commercial airlines across the world for flight planning.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

[Lufthansa Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Cargo), the cargo airline subsidiary of Lufthansa, uses Zeenea Data Discovery Platform as their data catalog solution.[^cite_note-124] Zeenea is a French metadata management startup founded in Paris in 2017.[^cite_note-125] On 8 August 2024, [HCLSoftware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HCLSoftware) announced intent to acquire Zeenea for 24 million euros, which is expected to continue operating as an independent unit under [Actian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actian), their data & analytics division.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

### Ownership

Lufthansa was a [state-owned enterprise](204768.md) until 1994.[^cite_note-128] Deutsche Lufthansa AG shares have been publicly traded on all German stock exchanges since 1966. In addition to floor trading, it is also traded electronically using the [Xetra system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xetra_\(trading_system\)). It is a [DAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAX) index share and is listed in the [Frankfurt Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange)'s [Prime Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Standard).[^cite_note-129] At the end of 2019, the shareholders' register showed that German investors held 67.3% of the shares (previous year: 72.1%). The second-largest group, with 10.4%, was shareholders from Luxembourg. Investors from the US accounted for 8.1%, followed by Ireland and the United Kingdom, each with 3.6%. This ensures compliance with the provisions of the German Aviation Compliance Documentation Act (LuftNaSiG). As of the reporting date, 58% of the shares were held by institutional investors (previous year: 53%), and 42% were held by private individuals (previous year: 47%). [Lansdowne Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansdowne_Partners) International Ltd. and BlackRock, Inc. were the largest shareholders in Lufthansa Group at year-end, with 4.9% and 3.1% respectively. All the transactions requiring disclosure and published during the financial year 2019, as well as the quarterly updates on the shareholder structure, are available online. During the 2020 COVID crisis [Heinz Hermann Thiele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Hermann_Thiele) increased his stake to more than 12%; he died a few months later. The [free float](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_float) for Lufthansa shares was 67% in 2020, as per the definition of the [Deutsche Börse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_B%C3%B6rse).

### Headquarters



Lufthansa's former headquarters in Deutz, Cologne

Lufthansa's corporate headquarters are in Cologne. In 1971, Lawrence Fellows of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* described the then-new headquarters building that Lufthansa occupied in Cologne as "gleaming".[^cite_note-140] In 1986, [left-wing terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Cells_\(German_group\)) bombed the building.[^cite_note-141] No one was injured.[^cite_note-142] In 2006, builders laid the first stone of the new Lufthansa headquarters in [Deutz, Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutz,_Cologne). By the end of 2007, Lufthansa planned to move 800 employees, including the company's finance department, to the new building.[^cite_note-143] However, in early 2013, Lufthansa revealed plans to relocate its head office from Cologne to Frankfurt by 2017.[^cite_note-144]

Several Lufthansa departments are not at the headquarters; instead, they are in the Lufthansa Aviation Center at Frankfurt Airport. These departments include Corporate Communications and Investor Relations.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] The Aviation Center with a transparent facade and several indoor gardens was designed by [Christoph Ingenhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Ingenhoven).[^cite_note-147]

### Employment relations

Relations between Lufthansa and their pilots had been very tense in circa 2015, with many strikes occurring, causing many flights to be cancelled, as well as major losses to the company.[^cite_note-148] A major dispute between Lufthansa and the pilot's union has been settled after nearly five years and overall 14 strikes in December 2017.[^cite_note-149] Without taking into account the €9 billion bailout from the German government, Lufthansa cut 31,000 jobs in the COVID-19 years.[^cite_note-150] During the 2022 collective bargaining, Verdi said that Lufthansa's wage offer meant real wage losses for employees and called on around 20,000 ground workers in Germany to go on warning strikes.[^cite_note-151]

### Branding



A Lufthansa [Airbus A320](164933.md)-200 in the old livery used since 1988



A Lufthansa [Airbus A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo_family) in the livery adapted since 2018

The Lufthansa logo, an encircled stylised [crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crane_\(bird\)) in flight, was first created in 1918 by Otto Firle. It was part of the livery of the first German airline, [Deutsche Luft-Reederei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Luft-Reederei) (abbreviated DLR), which began air service on 5 February 1919. In 1926, [Deutsche Luft Hansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Luft_Hansa) adopted this symbol, and in 1954, Lufthansa expressed continuity by adopting it and later in 1963 – a variant thereof as redesigned by [Robert Lisovskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lisovskyi).

The original creator of the name Lufthansa is believed to be F.A. Fischer von Puturzyn. In 1925, he published a book entitled "Luft-Hansa" which examined the options open to aviation policymakers at the time. Luft Hansa was the name given to the new airline, which resulted from the merger of [Junkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers)' airline ([Luftverkehr AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Luftverkehr)) and *Deutscher Aero Lloyd*.[^cite_note-152]

After World War II, the company kept blue and yellow as its main colours and the crane logo. Since the beginning of the 1960s, [Helvetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetica) was used for the company name in the livery. The 1970s retro livery featured the top half of the fuselage painted in all-white on top and the lower fuselage (bottom half, including the engines) was gray/silver aluminium, below a blue cheatline window band and a black painted nose. The crane logo was painted blue on the engines, on the bottom half of the fuselage just below the cockpit windows, and a yellow circle inside a blue band on the tail.

German designer [Otl Aicher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otl_Aicher) created a comprehensive corporate design for the airline in 1967. The crane logo was now always displayed in a circle which, on the livery, was yellow on an otherwise blue tailfin. Helvetica was used as the main typeface for both the livery and publications. The blue band and general paint scheme of the aircraft were retained from the previous livery.

Aicher's concept was retained in the 1988 design. The window band was removed, and the fuselage was painted in grey.

In 2018, Lufthansa changed their livery. The encircled crane was retained, and the background changed from yellow to dark blue. The vertical stabilizer and the rear fuselage were painted in dark blue, and the tail cone remained white. The main fuselage was painted in all white, and the brand name "Lufthansa" was painted above the windows, also in dark blue.

The company slogan is 'Say yes to the world.'[^cite_note-153]

## Fleet

Main article: [Lufthansa fleet](40837167.md)

### Airbus A380

Lufthansa had initially ordered a total of fifteen [Airbus A380-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380-800), of which ten were delivered by June 2012. In September 2011, two more A380s were ordered; this order was confirmed on 14 March 2013. However, in September 2013 it was announced that the Lufthansa Supervisory Board had approved the purchase of only twelve of the first fifteen A380s. Thus, a total of fourteen A380s have been added to the fleet.

Lufthansa used initially its A380s from and to Frankfurt am Main (nine aircraft) and since March 2018 to and from Munich as well (five aircraft). From 6 to 12 December 2011, Lufthansa already used an A380 once a day on the route from Munich to New York-JFK. This happened mainly against the backdrop of Christmas shopping in New York City.

On 13 March 2019, Lufthansa announced that it will be removing six A380 aircraft from the fleet and replacing them with Boeing 787-9 and Airbus A350-900 aircraft.[^cite_note-177] Those six aircraft were sold back to Airbus for €315 million, and all will have exited the fleet by November 2023. It was later disclosed that the sale price was reduced to €302 million because five of the six A380-800s sustained storm damage, which was not covered by insurance while stored.[^cite_note-178]

On 8 March 2020, Lufthansa announced that it would be grounding all of its A380 aircraft due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

Lufthansa announced on 27 June 2022 that the remaining fleet of eight A380s will be reactivated and brought back into service for the 2023 summer season.[^cite_note-181] The stronger than anticipated customer demand and quicker recovery of international travel from the pandemic is cited as one of two reasons.[^cite_note-182] The other reason is the persistent delay of Boeing 777-9 delivery, which Lufthansa would not receive until 2025 or later. Lufthansa is still assessing how many and which A380 will be reactivated and which routes the A380 will serve again.[^cite_note-183]



Lufthansa A380 *Deutschland* departing New York (2023)

On 2 December 2022, Lufthansa reactivated the first of two A380s to be entered into revenue service beginning in the summer of 2023. The first A380 to be reactivated was a nine-year-old D-AIMK, which left [Teruel Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teruel_Airport) for Frankfurt Airport after three years of storage. Since the A380 was inactive for a long time, the landing gear was not retracted during the flight, out of fear that it might not deploy again. The A380 flew at slower speeds and lower altitudes, lasting three hours. After the preparatory evaluation and minor repairs in Frankfurt, the A380 departed for Lufthansa Technik in [Manila](184334.md), [Philippines](23440.md) for the extensive maintenance and replacement work.

Following their reactivation, Lufthansa announced the A380 was to begin revenue flights from [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) to [Boston Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Logan) on 1 June 2023 and [New York-JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York-JFK) on 4 July 2023, with routes to [Los Angeles](18131.md) and [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvarnabhumi_Airport) to follow in October.[^cite_note-184]

The airline reactivated its eighth and last remaining A380 in September 2024.[^cite_note-185]

### Aircraft naming conventions

In September 1960, a Lufthansa Boeing 707 (D-ABOC), which would serve the Frankfurt-New York intercontinental route, was christened *Berlin* after the divided city of Berlin by then-mayor [Willy Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Brandt). Following *Berlin*, other Lufthansa 707 planes were named "[Hamburg](13467.md)", "[Frankfurt](10992.md)", "[München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchen)", and "[Bonn](3295.md)". With these names, the company established a tradition of [naming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_naming_and_launching) the planes in its fleet after German cities and towns or federal states, with a rule of thumb that the aeroplane make, size, or route would correspond roughly to the relative size or importance of the city or town it was named after.

This tradition continued, with two notable exceptions, until 2010: The first was an [Airbus A340-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340-300) registered D-AIFC, named "[Gander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gander,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador)/[Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Regional_Municipality)", after two Canadian cities along the standard flight path from Europe to North America. It became the first Lufthansa aeroplane named after a non-German city. The name commemorates the hospitality of the communities of Gander and Halifax, which served as improvised safe havens for the passengers and crew of the multitude of international aircraft unable to return to their originating airports during [Operation Yellow Ribbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Yellow_Ribbon) after the [September 11, 2001 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks). The other aircraft not named after a German city was an [Airbus A321-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321-100) registered as D-AIRA, which was designated *Finkenwerder* in honor of the Airbus facility in the district of [Hamburg-Finkenwerder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg-Finkenwerder),[^cite_note-lhtaufnamen2-186] where about 40% of Airbus narrowbody models are manufactured.

In February 2010, Lufthansa announced that its first two [Airbus A380s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) would be named *Frankfurt am Main* (D-AIMA) and *München* (D-AIMB) after Lufthansa's two hub airports. Subsequent A380 aircraft were named after other Lufthansa Group hub airports *[Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich)*, *Wien* ([Vienna](55866.md)) and *Brüssel* ([Brussels](3708.md)) and the major German cities of *[Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf)* and *[Berlin](3354.md)*. The remaining A380s were named after [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) hub cities *Tokyo*, *Beijing*, *Johannesburg*, *New York*, *San Francisco* and *Delhi*. However, D-AIMN *San Francisco* was renamed *Deutschland* (Germany) in 2014.[^cite_note-lhtaufnamen2-186]

As of 2014, there were several short- and long-haul aircraft in Lufthansa's fleet that do not bear any names. They either never received one or their former one has been given to a newer aircraft, which was the case for several [Boeing 747-400s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400). For example, the former *Bayern* ([Bavaria](3764.md)), a Boeing 747-400 still in active service, lost that name to a new [ Boeing 747-8I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-8).[^cite_note-lhtaufnamen2-186]

## Services

### Frequent-flyer programme

Main article: [Miles & More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_&_More)

Lufthansa's [frequent-flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequent-flyer_program) programme is called *Miles & More*, and is shared among several European airlines, including all of Lufthansa's subsidiary airlines (excluding the [SunExpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunExpress) joint ventures), [Croatia Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_Airlines), [LOT Polish Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOT_Polish_Airlines), and [Luxair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxair) (previously part-owned by Lufthansa).[^cite_note-187] Miles & More members may earn miles on Lufthansa flights and [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) partner flights, as well as through Miles & More credit cards, and purchases made through the Lufthansa shops. Status within Miles & More is determined by points earned through flights within Star Alliance (Points) and Miles & More partner airlines (Qualifying Points, HON Circle Points) during one calendar year. Membership levels are: Miles & More member (no minimal threshold), Frequent Traveller (Star Alliance Silver, 650 Points, 325 Qualifying Points), Senator (Star Alliance Gold, 2000 Points, 1000 Qualifying Points), and HON Circle Member (Star Alliance Gold, 6000 HON Circle Points). All Miles & More status levels higher than Miles & More members offer lounge access and executive bonus miles, with the higher levels offering more exclusive benefits.[^cite_note-188]

### Cabins

#### First Class



[First Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_\(aviation\)) of Lufthansa's Boeing 747-8Is in a 1-2-1 layout

First Class is offered on Airbus A340-600s, the front of the upper deck on Airbus A380s, and the nose of the main deck on Boeing 747-8s. Each seat converts to a 2 metres (6 ft 7 in) bed, and includes laptop power outlets, as well as entertainment facilities. Meals are available on demand. Lufthansa offers dedicated First Class check-in counters at most airports, and offers dedicated First Class lounges in Frankfurt and [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport), as well as a dedicated first-class terminal in Frankfurt. Arriving passengers have the option of using Lufthansa's First Class arrival facilities, as well as the new Welcome Lounge. Lufthansa introduced a new First Class product aboard the Airbus A380 and planned to gradually introduce it on all of its long-haul aircraft.[^cite_note-189] However, with the new program SCORE, introduced to boost profits by 1.5 billion euros over the following years, Lufthansa halted route expansion and extensively decreased its First Class offerings on most routes.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191] In October 2022, a new suite style First Class product was unveiled, and will be introduced on new A350 deliveries in 2023. In 2017 the airline announced that its first few [Boeing 777-9s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/777-9) would not include First Class seats, however, First Class could be installed on later deliveries.[^cite_note-192] As of June 2021, the only remaining First Class seats Lufthansa offered were on its [Boeing 747-8Is](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-8I), with 10 [Airbus A350-900s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A350-900) with First Class seats to be delivered starting in July 2023.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194]

#### Business Class



Business Class in a 2-2 layout on the upper deck of a Boeing 747-8I

Business Class is offered on all long-haul aircraft. Seats convert to 2 metres (6 ft 7 in) lie-flat beds and include laptop power outlets and entertainment facilities.[^cite_note-195] Lufthansa offers dedicated Business Class check-in counters at all airports, as well as dedicated Business Class lounges at most airports, or contract lounges at other airports, as well as the Lufthansa Welcome Lounge upon arrival in Frankfurt. As of 2014, Business Class on all widebody aircraft features lie-flat seats.[^cite_note-196] Lufthansa has released plans for a new business class set to be released in 2023 on the Boeing 787-9 and Airbus A350, and will retrofit the rest of the fleet in the coming years.[^cite_note-197]

#### Premium Economy



Economy Class aboard a Lufthansa [Boeing 787-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787-9)

Introduced in 2014,[^cite_note-198] Lufthansa's long-haul Premium Economy was rolled out on all long-haul aircraft, starting with some Boeing 747-8Is. Similar in design to [Air Canada](145623.md)'s Premium Economy or [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways)' World Traveller Plus cabins, Premium Economy features 38-inch (970 mm) pitch along with up to 3 inches (76 mm) more width than economy class, depending on the aircraft. The seats also feature a 11-or-12-inch (280 or 300 mm) personal seat-back entertainment screen and a larger armrest separating the seats. Along with the planned introduction of the Boeing 777-9X, the airline plans to add a new Premium Economy cabin with a "shell" design. These seats are also to be installed on SWISS' Boeing 777-300ERs and Airbus A340-300s from the first and second quarter of 2021, respectively.[^cite_note-199]

### Bus service

A bus service from [Nuremberg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Airport) to [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) was reinstated in 2021 to replace short-haul flights between the two cities. Lufthansa operated a check-in point in [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) and a bus service from Nuremberg to [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) in the late 1990s.[^cite_note-200]

## Accidents and incidents

### Fatal

- On 11 January 1959, [Lufthansa Flight 502](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_502), a Lufthansa [Lockheed Super Constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Super_Constellation) ([registered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) D-ALAK) crashed onto a beach shortly off [Galeão Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gale%C3%A3o_Airport) in Rio de Janeiro following a scheduled passenger flight from [Hamburg](13467.md), Germany. Of the 29 passengers and 10 crew members on board, only the co-pilot and two flight attendants survived. The investigation into the accident resulted in blaming the pilots for having executed a too low approach, which may have been caused by fatigue.

  [^cite_note-201]

- On 4 December 1961, a Lufthansa [Boeing 720](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_720) (registered D-ABOK) crashed of unknown causes near [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) during a training flight from Frankfurt to Cologne, killing the three occupants. It was the first crash involving an aircraft of that type.

  [^cite_note-202]

- On 15 July 1964, another [Boeing 720](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_720) (registered D-ABOP) crashed during a training flight, with the three people, including [Werner Baake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Baake), on board losing their lives (in what was only the second crash for this aircraft type). The accident occurred near [Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansbach) after the pilots had lost control of the aircraft when executing an [aileron roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileron_roll).

- On 28 January 1966 at 17\:50 local time, [Lufthansa Flight 005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_005) from Frankfurt to [Bremen](9420388.md), which was operated using a [Convair CV-440 Metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_CV-440_Metropolitan) (registered D-ACAT), crashed 0.5 kilometres (0.31 mi) short of [Bremen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Airport), killing all 42 passengers and four crew members on board. The pilots had tried to execute a [go-around](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-around) when approaching the airport, during which the aircraft [stalled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stall_\(flight\)) and went out of control, possibly due to pilot error.

  [^cite_note-203]

  D-ABYB, the aircraft that was destroyed in the Flight 540 accident. It is seen here during a promotional event at [Nuremberg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Airport) in 1970.

  &#x20;On 20 November 1974 at 07\:54 local time, [Lufthansa Flight 540](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_540), a [Boeing 747-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-100) (registered D-ABYB), crashed shortly after take-off at [Jomo Kenyatta International Airport](142547.md) in what was the first air accident involving a Boeing 747. A total of 55 out of the 140 passengers and four out of the 17 crew lost their lives, making it the worst accident in the history of the airline.

  [^cite_note-204]

- On 26 July 1979 at 21\:32 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC), a cargo-configured [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) (registered D-ABUY) operating [Lufthansa Flight 527](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_527) from [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) to [Dakar](44251.md) and onwards to Germany [crashed into a mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Cargo_Flight_527) 25 kilometres (16 mi) from [Galeão Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gale%C3%A3o_Airport) during initial climb, killing the three crew members on board. A flawed communication between the pilots and the air traffic controller had resulted in the aircraft flying on the wrong path.

  [^cite_note-205]

- In January 1984, a woman was found dead in a suitcase which was lying on an [LAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX) baggage carousel for a while. The suitcase had arrived on a Lufthansa flight. The woman was later discovered to have been an Iranian citizen who had recently married another Iranian with [Green card](300237.md) status. She had been denied a US visa in West Germany and therefore decided to enter the US like this.

  [^cite_note-206]

- On 14 September 1993, [Lufthansa Flight 2904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_2904), an [Airbus A320-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320-200) (registered D-AIPN) flying from [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) to [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Chopin_Airport) with 64 passengers and four crew members on board, overran the runway upon landing at [Warsaw-Okecie Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Chopin_Airport), and crashed into an earth embankment, resulting in the death of the [co-pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-pilot) and one passenger.

  [^cite_note-207]

  [^cite_note-208]

- On 28 May 1999, [German border police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesgrenzschutz) suffocated [Aamir Ageeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aamir_Ageeb) to death, whom they were escorting aboard Lufthansa Flight 588 from Frankfurt to [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_International_Airport). During takeoff, the officers restrained and pinned down [Ageeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aamir_Ageeb), a Sudanese man deported from Germany after being rejected for [asylum](1621432.md).

  [^cite_note-Tod-69]

The incident led to the [German interior ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_the_Interior_and_Community) suspending its policy of forcible air deportation, and contributed to protests over Lufthansa's role in transporting deported asylum seekers.

### Non-fatal

- On 20 December 1973 at 00\:33 local time, a Lufthansa [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) (registered D-ABOT) with 98 passengers and 11 crew members on board collided with a [middle marker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marker_beacon) shack upon approaching [Palam Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palam_Airport) in [Delhi](37756.md) following a scheduled passenger flight from [Bangkok](56615.md) (as part of a multi-leg flight back to [Germany](11867.md)). There were no injuries, but the aircraft was damaged beyond repair. Visibility was poor at the time of the accident.

  [^cite_note-210]

- On 11 June 2018, one of the airline's [Airbus A340-300s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340-300), registered as D-AIFA, was being towed to its departure gate at [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) when the towing vehicle caught [fire](11145.md). Despite the quick action of the airport fire brigade, the aircraft suffered substantial fire and smoke damage to the nose and flight deck. Six people were treated for smoke inhalation.

  [^cite_note-211]

### Hijackings and criminal events

- In 1972, there were four reported hijackings involving Lufthansa aircraft:&#x20;

  - On 22 February, [Flight 649](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_649), a [Boeing 747-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-200) (registered D-ABYD) with 172 passengers and 15 crew members on board was hijacked en route from [New Delhi](51585.md) to [Athens](1216.md) (as part of a multi-leg flight from [Tokyo](30057.md) to Frankfurt) by five [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) terrorists who then pressed for a \$5 million ransom from the [German government](1327803.md). The aircraft landed at [Aden International Airport](9421129.md), and the hostages were released on the following day once the demands of the perpetrators were accepted.

    [^cite_note-212]

    [^cite_note-213]

  - On 10 July, a similar [hijacking](2075.md) attempt occurred on board a Lufthansa [Boeing 737-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-100) during a flight from Cologne to [Munich](19058.md).

    [^cite_note-214]

  - 11 October a [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) was hijacked on a flight from [Lisbon](18091.md) to Frankfurt. Upon landing at [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport), the perpetrator tried to flee but was captured by police forces.

    [^cite_note-215]

  - On 29 October, two men hijacked [Flight 615](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_615) with 11 other passengers and seven crew members on board during a flight from [Beirut](37428.md) to [Ankara](802.md) (and onwards to Germany), in order to liberate the three surviving members of the [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_\(group\)) group responsible for the [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre). Whilst the hijacked [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) (registered D-ABIG) was forced to circle over [Zagreb Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Airport) in danger of eventual [fuel starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_starvation), the West German authorities decided to comply with the demands. The prisoners were handed over and the aircraft was allowed to be flown to [Tripoli](31232.md), where the hostages were released.

    [^cite_note-216]

    [^cite_note-217]

    [^cite_note-218]

- On 17 December 1973, in the wake of the events surrounding [Pan Am Flight 110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_110), a parked Lufthansa [Boeing 737-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-100) (registered D-ABEY) was hijacked at [Leonardo da Vinci-Fiumicino Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci-Fiumicino_Airport) in Rome. Ten Italian hostages that had been taken by [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) terrorists at the airport were forced into the aircraft by five perpetrators, and the German crew (two pilots and two flight attendants) that was on board preparing the departure to [Munich](19058.md) had to fly the aircraft instead first to [Athens](1216.md) and then to several other airports until the ordeal ended at [Kuwait International Airport](464414.md) the next day, where the hijackers surrendered.

  [^cite_note-219]

  [^cite_note-220]

- On 28 June 1977, a Lufthansa [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) was [hijacked](2075.md) during a flight from Frankfurt to [Istanbul](3391396.md) and forced to divert to [Munich](19058.md).

  [^cite_note-221]

- The [hijacking of the Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_181) occurred on 13 October 1977, at a time when West Germany had come under intense terroristic pressure known as [German Autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Autumn). The [Boeing 737-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-200) (registered D-ABCE) was hijacked en route [Flight 181](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_181) from [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca) to Frankfurt by four terrorists of the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), who thus wanted to force the German government to release several [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_Faction) terrorists. The crew had to divert the aircraft with 87 other passengers first to Rome, and then onwards to [Larnaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larnaca), Bahrain, [Dubai](211583.md), [Aden](38050.md) (where the captain was killed when he returned to the aircraft after negotiations with the local authorities), and finally to [Mogadishu](60154.md) in an ordeal that took several days. At [Mogadishu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu_Airport), the German [GSG 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSG_9) special forces stormed the aircraft in the early hours of 18 October local time, killing three terrorists and freeing all hostages.

  [^cite_note-222]

  [^cite_note-223]

- On 11 December 1978, Lufthansa was the victim of a major heist (robbery) at [John F. Kennedy International Airport](97218.md). The [Lufthansa heist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_heist) led to Lufthansa losing about US\$5 million.

- On 12 September 1979, a hijacking attempt occurred on board a Lufthansa [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) on a flight from Frankfurt to Cologne, but the perpetrator quickly surrendered.

  [^cite_note-224]

- Three hijackings occurred in due course in early 1985:&#x20;

  - On 27 February, a [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) was hijacked en route a Lufthansa flight from Frankfurt to [Damascus](8914.md). Two perpetrators forced the pilots to divert the aircraft (with 35 other passengers on board) to [Vienna International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_International_Airport), where they surrendered.

    [^cite_note-225]

  - On 27 March, another [727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) was hijacked, this time on a flight from Munich to [Athens](1216.md). A man demanded the pilots to divert to [Libya](17633.md). During a fuel stop at [Istanbul](3391396.md), the aircraft was stormed and the perpetrator arrested.

    [^cite_note-226]

  - Only two days later, a mentally ill person on board a Lufthansa [Boeing 737-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-200) on a flight from [Hamburg](13467.md) to London demanded to be taken to Hawaii instead.

    [^cite_note-227]

- On 11 February 1993, [Lufthansa Flight 592](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_592) from Frankfurt to [Addis Ababa](52593.md) via [Cairo](6293.md) with 94 passengers and 10 crew members was hijacked during the first leg by 20-year-old Nebiu Zewolde Demeke, who forced the pilots to divert the [Airbus A310](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310) (registered D-AIDM) to the United States, with the intent of securing the [right of asylum](1621432.md) there. Demeke, who had been on the flight to be [deported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deported) back to his native [Ethiopia](187749.md), surrendered to authorities upon arrival at [John F. Kennedy International Airport](97218.md) in New York City. No passengers or crew members were harmed during the 12-hour ordeal.

  [^cite_note-228]

## Customer issues

### Barring of visibly Jewish passengers

In May 2022, during a New York to Frankfurt flight, the captain alerted Lufthansa security that some passengers had failed to follow crew instructions requiring the [wearing of masks](63631542.md) and barring gatherings. The company then barred over a hundred visibly Jewish passengers from the flight from boarding a connecting flight to Budapest.[^cite_note-sherman-229][^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] The majority were rebooked on other flights the same day.[^cite_note-sherman-229] In August 2022, as a result of the incident, Lufthansa adopted the IHRA [Working Definition of Antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Definition_of_Antisemitism) and appointed a senior manager with responsibility for preventing antisemitism and other discrimination.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] In October 2024, Lufthansa paid a US Department of Transportation penalty of \$4M in relation to the episode, less the \$2M it had already paid to passengers in a legal settlement. Lufthansa said it made the payment to avoid litigation but denied discrimination, blaming the incident on "an unfortunate series of inaccurate communications".[^cite_note-sherman-229]
