**Jalalabad Airport** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): د جلال اباد هوايي ډګر; [IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **JAA**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **OAJL**), also known as **Nangarhar Airport**,[^cite_note-Yousuf_Zarifi-6][^cite_note-Civilian_Flights_Resume_at_Nangarhar_Airport-1] and referred to as **Jalalabad Airfield** (**JAF**) by the U.S. military during the [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md),[^cite_note-7] is located next to the [Kabul–Jalalabad Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul%E2%80%93Jalalabad_Road), about 3 miles (4.8 km) southeast of [Jalalabad](16274.md), which is the capital of [Nangarhar Province](457263.md) in [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-skybrary.aero-8] It is a [domestic airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_airport) under the country's [Ministry of Transport and Civil Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_and_Civil_Aviation_\(Afghanistan\)) (MoTCA),[^cite_note-Civilian_Flights_Resume_at_Nangarhar_Airport-1] and serves the population of not only Nangarhar but also neighboring [Kunar](2159600.md), [Nuristan](457267.md) and [Laghman](457257.md) provinces. It is also used by the [Ministry of Defense](1289906.md) for military purposes. Security in and around the airport is provided by the [Afghan National Security Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Security_Forces).

Situated at an elevation of 1,840 feet (561 m) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), Jalalabad Airport has one [asphalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphalt_concrete) [runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) measuring around 7,260 by 140 feet (2,213 m × 43 m). It also has at least 18 helipads, a number of hangars and buildings used for military purposes.[^cite_note-Google_Earth-3] The other closest major public [Afghan airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Afghanistan) to Jalalabad are the [Kabul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport) in neighboring [Kabul Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Province) to the west and [Khost Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost_Airport) in [Khost Province](457249.md) to the south.

Jalalabad Airport was used in the past by the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md) and civilian contractors. They operated out of [Forward Operating Base Fenty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Operating_Base_Fenty). Members of the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (ISAF) and later [Resolute Support Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission) (RSM) also used the airport in the past.[^cite_note-skybrary.aero-8][^cite_note-9]

## History

Work on the airport began in the 1950s when Afghanistan was ruled by King [Zahir Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Zahir_Shah).[^cite_note-10] It was modernized during the 1960s with United States assistance.[^cite_note-Capsule_Facts_for_the_Armed_Forces:_Afghanistan-2] During the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) turned the civilian airport into a military air base, used by an Afghan [Mil Mi-24](384591.md) squadron in [Jalalabad](16274.md).[^cite_note-11] In 1989, the [Afghan Interim Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen) attempted to take the airport in what became the Battle of Jalalabad Airport, later escalating into the [Battle of Jalalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jalalabad_\(1989\)).[^cite_note-12] They were unsuccessful and pushed out by the [Afghan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan). It was recently expanded by [NATO](21133.md) forces during their decades-long [war](19666611.md) with the [Taliban](30635.md).

### New Jalalabad Airport

According to MoTCA, a new airport will be built in the [Kuz Kunar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuz_Kunar_District) of [Nangarhar Province](457263.md).[^cite_note-13]

## Airlines and destinations

Further information: [List of airlines of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_of_Afghanistan)

Jalalabad Airport handles only domestic flights. According to MoTCA, "there will be three to four flights each week."[^cite_note-Civilian_Flights_Resume_at_Nangarhar_Airport-1] Between August 2021 and July 2022, there were no scheduled airlines operating at Jalalabad Airport except those operated by the [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) and United Nations agencies.[^cite_note-Yousuf_Zarifi-6][^cite_note-14]

## Accidents and incidents



A U.S. Air Force [C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130) at Jalalabad Airport in 2011

### 2010 militant attack

On 30 June 2010, a [car bomb](155282.md) was set off and insurgents stormed the airport. According to [Zabiullah Mujahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabiullah_Mujahid), a [Taliban](30635.md) spokesman, said the Taliban were responsible and killed 32 Afghan and non-Afghan security forces. According to [NATO](21133.md), there were eight Taliban deaths and one Afghan and one coalition member injured.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

### 2015 aircraft crash

14 people, including 6 United States Airmen and 5 passengers and 3 local nationals, were killed when a [Lockheed Martin C-130J Super Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_C-130J_Super_Hercules) aircraft [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Jalalabad_USAF_C-130J_crash) shortly after take-off from Jalalabad Airport on 2 October 2015.[^cite_note-17]
