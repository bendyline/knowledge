The **Washington Capitals** (colloquially known as the **Caps**) are a professional [ice hockey](14790.md) team based in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) The Capitals compete in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) as a member of the [Metropolitan Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Division) in the [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_\(NHL\)). The team is owned by [Ted Leonsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Leonsis), through [Monumental Sports & Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_Sports_&_Entertainment), and initially played its home games at the [Capital Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Centre_\(Landover,_Maryland\)) in [Landover, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landover,_Maryland), before moving to [Capital One Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Arena) in Washington, D.C., in 1997.

The franchise was founded in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_NHL_season) as an expansion team, alongside the [Kansas City Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Scouts), and struggled throughout its first eight years of existence. Beginning in 1982, with a core of players such as [Mike Gartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gartner), [Rod Langway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Langway), [Larry Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Murphy_\(ice_hockey\)), and [Scott Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Stevens), they became a regular playoff contender for the next 14 seasons. After purchasing the team in 1999, Leonsis revitalized the franchise by drafting star players such as [Alexander Ovechkin](755749.md), [Nicklas Bäckström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicklas_B%C3%A4ckstr%C3%B6m), [John Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carlson_\(ice_hockey\)), [Braden Holtby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braden_Holtby), [Evgeny Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Kuznetsov) and [Tom Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wilson_\(ice_hockey\)). In the [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_NHL_season), the Capitals won the franchise's first [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy) for being the team with the most points at the end of the regular season. They won it a second time in [2015–16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NHL_season), and for a third time the following season in [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NHL_season). In addition to 14 division titles and three Presidents' Trophies, the Capitals have reached the [Stanley Cup Final](3314237.md) in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Stanley_Cup_Final) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_Final), winning the latter in five games against the [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md).

The Capitals have retired the use of four numbers in honor of four players. In addition, the team holds an association with a number of individuals inducted into the [Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame). The Capitals are affiliated with two [minor league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league) teams: the [Hershey Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey_Bears) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) and the [South Carolina Stingrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Stingrays) of the [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL).[^cite_note-3]

## History

### Early years (1974–1982)

The NHL awarded an expansion franchise to the city of Washington on June 8, 1972, and the Capitals joined the NHL as an [expansion team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team) for the [1974–75 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_NHL_season) along with the [Kansas City Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Scouts). The Capitals were owned by [Abe Pollin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abe_Pollin) (also owner of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s [Washington Bullets/Wizards](72858.md)). Pollin had built the [Capital Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Centre_\(Landover,_Maryland\)) in suburban [Landover, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landover,_Maryland), to house both the Bullets (who formerly played in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore)) and the Capitals.[^cite_note-4] His first act as owner was to hire [Hall of Famer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame) [Milt Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milt_Schmidt) as general manager.[^cite_note-5] The team name was decided via a contest in which 12,000 entries were given to Pollin. Other names included the Comets, Cyclones, Streaks, and Domes.[^cite_note-6] [Jim Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Anderson_\(ice_hockey\)) was hired as head coach prior to the inaugural season.[^cite_note-Caps_1st-7]

With a combined 30 teams between the NHL and the [World Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association) (WHA), the available talent was stretched thin. The Capitals had few players with significant NHL experience and were at a disadvantage against the long-standing teams that were stocked with veteran players.[^cite_note-8]

Two hockey players in full pads and helmets on the ice, both in motion, with two others further behind them.

Capitals defenseman [Darren Veitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Veitch) chasing [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) defenseman [Ray Bourque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Bourque) in 1980

The Capitals' inaugural season in [1974–75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Washington_Capitals_season) set NHL records in futility. They finished last in the league with a 8–67–5 record; their 21 points were half that of their expansion brethren, the Scouts. The eight wins are the fewest for an NHL team playing at least 70 games, and the .131 winning percentage is still the worst in NHL history. They also set records for most road losses (39 out of 40), most consecutive road losses (37), and most consecutive losses (17). Head coach Jim Anderson said, "I'd rather find out my wife was cheating on me than keep losing like this. At least I could tell my wife to cut it out."[^cite_note-9] Anderson was fired 54 games into the season and replaced by head scout [Red Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sullivan). Sullivan, after a 14-game losing streak, resigned citing stomach issues. Schmidt had to take over the coaching reins for the remainder of the season.[^cite_note-Caps_1st-7]

In [1975–76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Washington_Capitals_season), Washington went 25 straight games without a win and allowed 394 goals en route to another horrendous record: 11–59–10 (32 points).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] In the middle of the season, Schmidt resigned and was replaced as general manager by [Max McNab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_McNab) and as head coach by [Tom McVie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_McVie).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] After the season, along with the Scouts, both teams would travel to [Tokyo](30057.md) and [Sapporo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapporo), Japan for four exhibition games for the NHL's first games in an Asian country.[^cite_note-14] For the rest of the 1970s and early 1980s, the Capitals alternated between dreadful seasons and finishing only a few points out of the [Stanley Cup playoffs](10490910.md); in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_Washington_Capitals_season), for instance, they were in playoff contention until the last day of the season.[^cite_note-15] In 1978, the Capitals made a trade to acquire [Dennis Maruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Maruk) who would score 50 goals during the 1980–81 season and 60 goals in the [1981–82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Washington_Capitals_season) season.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Thanks to the subpar seasons, the Capitals were able to draft players [Mike Gartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gartner) and [Bobby Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Carpenter_\(ice_hockey\)).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] However, McNab and coach [Gary Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Green_\(ice_hockey\)) were fired in November 1981.[^cite_note-21] [Roger Crozier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Crozier) took over as GM for the remainder of the season, but he too was fired in August 1982.[^cite_note-22] One of Crozier's decisions included the hiring of [Bryan Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Murray_\(ice_hockey\)).[^cite_note-23]

By the summer of 1982, serious talk began of the heavily money-losing team leaving Washington before the next season. Fans started a "Save the Caps" ticket-buying campaign to drum up support for the team.[^cite_note-24] Just before the August deadline by which Pollin said he would be forced to relocate (reported to take the form of a merger with the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils)), the [Prince George's County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George's_County,_Maryland) council boosted the effort by voting to give the Caps a near-complete break from a heavy tax on admission to professional sporting events. Days later, Pollin announced new investing partners had signed on and confirmed he would be able to keep the team in Washington.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-bog-26]

### Postseason inconsistencies (1982–1996)



[Mike Gartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Gartner) helped lift the Capitals to becoming a playoff contender in the 1980s.

One of the new part-owners was [Dick Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Patrick), on whose advice Pollin hired [David Poile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Poile) as general manager.[^cite_note-bog-26][^cite_note-27] As his first move, Poile pulled off one of the largest trades in franchise history on September 9, 1982, when he dealt captain [Ryan Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Walter) and [Rick Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Green_\(ice_hockey\)) to the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) in exchange for [Rod Langway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Langway) (named captain only a few weeks later), [Brian Engblom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Engblom), [Doug Jarvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Jarvis) and [Craig Laughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Laughlin).[^cite_note-28] Another significant move was the drafting of defenseman [Scott Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Stevens) during the [1982 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_NHL_entry_draft) (the pick was made by Crozier, prior to Poile's hiring).[^cite_note-Stevens-29] The result of both acquisitions helped the team get a third-place finish in the [Patrick Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Division), which resulted in their first playoff appearance. Although they were eliminated by the three-time defending (and eventual) Stanley Cup champion Islanders three games to one, the Caps' dramatic turnaround ended any talk of the club leaving Washington.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

In the [1983–84 offseason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals traded a first round draft pick for [Dave Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Christian).[^cite_note-32] They also traded Dennis Maruk to the [Minnesota North Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_North_Stars) for a second round draft pick.[^cite_note-33] The regular season had the Capitals gain over 100 points for the first time in their history. The Capitals also made a trade with the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) sending Engblom and [Ken Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Houston) for [Larry Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Murphy_\(ice_hockey\)).[^cite_note-34] They also made it to the second round of the playoffs, after defeating the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) in a three-game sweep. However, they were defeated by the Islanders in the second round.[^cite_note-Heartbreak-35] The Capitals matched their point total the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Washington_Capitals_season), but had a two-game series lead in the first round disappear against the Islanders as Washington lost the next three games to lose the series.[^cite_note-Heartbreak-35] In [1985–86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Washington_Capitals_season), the team had their best record yet, winning 50 games and earning 107 points.[^cite_note-Heartbreak-35] After defeating the Islanders in a three-game sweep, the Capitals were defeated by the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) in six games.[^cite_note-36]



First Lady [Nancy Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Reagan) shooting a puck against Washington goaltender [Pete Peeters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Peeters) while attending a Capitals–Flyers game in 1988

The [1986–87 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Washington_Capitals_season) saw the team trade Bobby Carpenter and a second round draft pick for [Bob Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Crawford_\(ice_hockey\)), [Kelly Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Miller_\(ice_hockey,_born_1963\)), and [Mike Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Ridley).[^cite_note-37] However, the season ended with a loss to the Islanders in the division semifinals. The Capitals had a 3–1 series lead, but the Islanders came back to force game seven which would be known as the [Easter Epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Epic). In the fourth overtime, at 1\:56 am on Easter Sunday 1987, [Pat LaFontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_LaFontaine) scored, giving the Islanders a 2–1 victory, ending the Capitals season.[^cite_note-38]

At the [1987 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_NHL_entry_draft), the Capitals traded [Gaetan Duchesne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetan_Duchesne), [Alan Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Haworth_\(ice_hockey\)), and first round pick (who would become [Joe Sakic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Sakic)) for [Dale Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hunter) and [Clint Malarchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Malarchuk).[^cite_note-39] The Capitals were unable to move past the second round for another season in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), losing the division finals against the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils).[^cite_note-40] For the [1989 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) push, Gartner and Murphy were traded to the [Minnesota North Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_North_Stars) in exchange for [Dino Ciccarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Ciccarelli) and defenseman [Bob Rouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Rouse).[^cite_note-41] However, they were eliminated in the first round by the Philadelphia Flyers.[^cite_note-42]

In the [1989–90 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Washington_Capitals_season), Bryan Murray was fired and replaced by his brother, the assistant coach, [Terry Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Murray).[^cite_note-43] As they approached the deadline, the Capitals acquired goaltender [Mike Liut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Liut) who assisted in their playoff push.[^cite_note-44] The Capitals finally made the conference finals in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), but went down in a four-game sweep at the hands of the first-place Boston Bruins.[^cite_note-45] The next two seasons were undone by the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) who defeated Washington both seasons in the playoffs.[^cite_note-Pens_victories1-46] It included a series where the Capitals had a 3–1 series lead in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) only to lose the next three games and the series.[^cite_note-Pens_Caps-47] Their final season with Langway did not end well for himself nor the Capitals. He was limited to 21 games in the [1992–93 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Washington_Capitals_season) due to a torn cartilage injury.[^cite_note-48] In the playoffs, they lost in six games to the Islanders in the division semifinals. In game six of the series, Dale Hunter hit [Pierre Turgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Turgeon) after Turgeon scored the 6–1 goal for the Islanders. Hunter was given a 21-game suspension the following season as a result.[^cite_note-49]

At the [1993 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_NHL_entry_draft), [Craig Berube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Berube) was acquired from the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) in return for a fifth round draft pick.[^cite_note-50] In [1993–94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals struggled throughout the first half of the season which culminated in Terry Murray's dismissal as head coach and [Jim Schoenfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Schoenfeld) announced as his replacement.[^cite_note-51] Approaching the trade deadline, the Capitals acquired [Joé Juneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A9_Juneau).[^cite_note-52] Although the Capitals defeated the Penguins in the conference quarterfinals,[^cite_note-53] they were defeated in five games by the Rangers in the conference semifinals.[^cite_note-54] At the [1994 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_NHL_entry_draft), Mike Ridley was traded along with a first round pick for [Rob Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Pearson) and the tenth overall pick,[^cite_note-55] which was used to select [Nolan Baumgartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Baumgartner).[^cite_note-56] However, their [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitals%E2%80%93Penguins_rivalry), the Penguins, would defeat them in the first round of both the [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) and [1996 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). Both series had the Capitals leading the series, including a 3–1 series lead in 1995—the 1996 series ended with a game six fourth overtime goal by Pittsburgh forward [Petr Nedvěd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Nedv%C4%9Bd).[^cite_note-Pens_Caps-47] In the [1996–97 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_Washington_Capitals_season), David Poile completed a blockbuster trade with the Bruins to acquire forwards [Adam Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Oates) and [Rick Tocchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Tocchet) along with goaltender [Bill Ranford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Ranford) for [Jason Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Allison), [Jim Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carey_\(ice_hockey\)), [Anson Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anson_Carter), and two draft picks.[^cite_note-57] However, the team was unable to make the [1997 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), and as a result, Poile was fired along with Schoenfeld.[^cite_note-58]

### Stanley Cup Final appearance and rebuild (1997–2005)



The Capitals moved into the MCI Center (presently [Capital One Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Arena)) in 1997.

In [1997–98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals hired general manager [George McPhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McPhee) as well as [Ron Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Wilson_\(ice_hockey,_born_1955\)) as the next head coach.[^cite_note-59] Midway through the season, they moved to [MCI Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Arena), now Capital One Arena, their current home arena.[^cite_note-60] [Peter Bondra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bondra) led the team with 52 goals in the regular season.[^cite_note-61] In the [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Caps got past the Boston Bruins,[^cite_note-62] [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators),[^cite_note-63] and [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) en route to the team's first [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Stanley_Cup_Final) appearance.[^cite_note-64] However, the team was outmatched by the defending champions, the [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings), who won in a four-game sweep.[^cite_note-65] During the game two of the series, [Esa Tikkanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esa_Tikkanen) forced Detroit goaltender [Chris Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Osgood) out of position but slid the puck past the open net, which led to the Red Wings' comeback in the game.[^cite_note-66] That same season, Oates, [Phil Housley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Housley), and Hunter all scored their 1,000th career point, the only time in NHL history that one team had three players reach that same milestone in a single season.[^cite_note-67]

After their 1998 Stanley Cup run, the Capitals finished the [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Washington_Capitals_season) with a record of 31–45–6 and failed to qualify for the playoffs.[^cite_note-68] They also traded out Ranford,[^cite_note-69] Berube, Juneau, and captain Dale Hunter.[^cite_note-70] During the season, the team was sold to a group headed by [America Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_Online) (AOL) executive [Ted Leonsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Leonsis).[^cite_note-71]

The Capitals went on to win back-to-back [Southeast Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Division_\(NHL\)) titles in 2000 and 2001, yet both years lost in the first round of the playoffs to the Penguins.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-Pens_Caps-47] The [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_Washington_Capitals_season) also saw the Capitals acquire [Trevor Linden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Linden) and [Dainius Zubrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainius_Zubrus) from the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md).[^cite_note-73] However, after the playoffs, [Adam Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Oates) demanded a trade, feeling he was no longer an important part of the team with Linden playing.[^cite_note-74] Management refused to trade Oates and stripped him of his team captaincy.[^cite_note-75]

In the summer of 2001, the Capitals landed five-time [Art Ross Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Ross_Trophy) winner [Jaromír Jágr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarom%C3%ADr_J%C3%A1gr) through a trade with the Penguins.[^cite_note-Jagr-76] Jagr was then signed to the largest contract ever in NHL history—\$77 million over seven years at an average salary of \$11 million per year (over \$134,000 per game) with an option for an eighth year. During the [2001–02 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Washington_Capitals_season), Linden was dealt to the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks).[^cite_note-77] Oates finally received his trade request, going to the Philadelphia Flyers in exchange for a prospect and three draft picks.[^cite_note-78] The Capitals failed to defend their division title and missed the playoffs in 2002 despite a winning record.[^cite_note-79] Jagr's point total also dropped from 121 the season prior to 79. Ron Wilson was fired as head coach.[^cite_note-Jagr-76]

Before the [2002–03 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Washington_Capitals_season), the Caps made more roster changes, including the signing of [Robert Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lang_\(ice_hockey\)) as a free agent, a linemate of Jagr's from Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-Jagr-76] The Capitals also hired [Grand Rapids Griffins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Rapids_Griffins) coach [Bruce Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Cassidy) as head coach.[^cite_note-80] Washington returned to the playoffs in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), but lost in six games to the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning) after starting off with a two-game lead.[^cite_note-81]



[Kolzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_K%C3%B6lzig) was among Washington's best players between the late 1990s and early 2000s.

In the [2003–04 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_Washington_Capitals_season), the Caps unloaded much of their high-priced talent. It started with Jagr moving to the New York Rangers for [Anson Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anson_Carter). As a stipulation of the trade, agreement Washington agreed to pay \$20 million of Jagr's contract.[^cite_note-Jagr-76] This was quickly followed by Peter Bondra departing for the Ottawa Senators.[^cite_note-82] Not long after, Robert Lang was sent to the Detroit Red Wings,[^cite_note-83] as well as [Sergei Gonchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Gonchar) to the Boston Bruins.[^cite_note-84] The Capitals fired Cassidy and replaced him with [Glen Hanlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Hanlon);[^cite_note-85] Washington ended the year 23–46–10–3.[^cite_note-86]

In the [2004 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_NHL_entry_draft), the Capitals won the draft lottery, moving ahead of the Pittsburgh Penguins, who held the NHL's worst record, and selected [Alexander Ovechkin](755749.md) first overall.[^cite_note-87] During the [NHL labor dispute of 2004–05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout), which cost the NHL its entire season, Ovechkin stayed in Russia, playing for [Dynamo Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC_Dynamo_Moscow).[^cite_note-88] The Capitals' 2005 offseason consisted of making D.C.-area native Halpern the team's captain.[^cite_note-89]

### Ovechkin–Backstrom era (2005–present)

#### Returning to the playoffs (2005–2015)



The Capitals bench in 2005

The Capitals finished the [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Washington_Capitals_season) with a 29–41–12 campaign.[^cite_note-90] In his rookie season, Ovechkin led all 2005–06 rookies in goals with 52 and points with 106. Ovechkin became the first player since [Teemu Selänne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teemu_Sel%C3%A4nne) to score at least 50 goals in his rookie season.[^cite_note-91] His efforts won him the [Calder Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy).[^cite_note-92] Despite missing the playoffs, the Capitals had an 11-point improvement over the prior season.[^cite_note-93]



[Alexander Semin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Semin) and [Alexander Ovechkin](755749.md) celebrate following a goal in January 2007.

In the 2006 offseason, Halpern left the Capitals to join the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars); [Chris Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Clark_\(ice_hockey\)) subsequently became the Capitals' new captain.[^cite_note-94] [Richard Zedník](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Zedn%C3%ADk) returned to the Capitals in [2006–07](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Washington_Capitals_season),[^cite_note-95] but was later dealt at the trade deadline to the New York Islanders after an injury-plagued season.[^cite_note-96] Despite the transactions, the Capitals finished with the same point total (70) in 2006–07 as they did the year before, although they won one fewer game.[^cite_note-97] Ovechkin was the Capitals' lone representative in the season's [All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_National_Hockey_League_All-Star_Game).[^cite_note-98] Washington's campaign also saw the breakout of [Alexander Semin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Semin), who notched 38 goals in only his second NHL season.[^cite_note-99]

In the [2007–08 offseason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals signed Swedish forward [Nicklas Bäckström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicklas_B%C3%A4ckstr%C3%B6m), the fourth overall pick in the [2006 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_NHL_entry_draft), to a three-year entry-level contract.[^cite_note-100] They also signed 19-year-old goaltender [Semyon Varlamov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Varlamov) to a three-year entry-level contract.[^cite_note-101] After starting the season 6–14–1, the Capitals fired head coach Glen Hanlon and replaced him with [Hershey Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey_Bears) head coach [Bruce Boudreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Boudreau).[^cite_note-102] On January 10, 2008, the Capitals signed Ovechkin to an NHL-record \$124 million contract extension at 13 years, the second-longest term of any contract in the NHL after New York Islanders goaltender [Rick DiPietro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_DiPietro)'s 15-year contract.[^cite_note-103] Aided by key acquisitions at the trade deadline ([Matt Cooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Cooke), [Sergei Fedorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Fedorov), and [Cristobal Huet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristobal_Huet)),[^cite_note-104] Ovechkin's NHL-leading 65 goals,[^cite_note-64_and_65-105] and [Mike Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Green_\(ice_hockey,_born_1985\))'s league defensemen-leading 18 goals,[^cite_note-Mike_Green-106] the Capitals won the Southeast Division title for the first time since the [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_Washington_Capitals_season), edging out the [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) for the division title on the final game of the season. Washington's remarkable end-of-season run included winning 11 of the final 12 regular season games. The Capitals became the first team in NHL history to make the playoffs after being ranked 14th or lower in their conference standings at the season's midpoint.[^cite_note-107] For the postseason, the Capitals played against the Philadelphia Flyers in the first round and managed to force a game seven after being down three games to one in the series. However, they ultimately lost to the Flyers 3–2 in overtime.[^cite_note-108] The accolades for the team continued to grow after the end of the season. Ovechkin won the [Art Ross Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Ross_Trophy), the [Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_%22Rocket%22_Richard_Trophy), the [Hart Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Memorial_Trophy), and the [Lester B. Pearson Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lindsay_Award), becoming the first player in NHL history to win all four awards in the same season.[^cite_note-109] Nicklas Bäckström was a finalist for the Calder Trophy, but ended up second to the Chicago Blackhawks' [Patrick Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Kane).[^cite_note-110] In addition to player awards, head coach Bruce Boudreau won the [Jack Adams Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Adams_Award) for the NHL's coach of the year.[^cite_note-Boudreau-111] However, at free agency, the leading Capitals goaltender in games played, wins, save percentage, and shutouts, [Olaf Kölzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_K%C3%B6lzig) left the team to join the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning).[^cite_note-112]



The Capitals celebrate after defeating the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) in the first round of the [2009 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).

The [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Washington_Capitals_season) was highlighted by the play of Mike Green and Ovechkin. Green led all NHL defensemen in goals and points, also setting the record for the longest consecutive goal-scoring streak by a defenseman with eight games.[^cite_note-Mike_Green-106] Ovechkin won his second Hart Trophy, his second Lester B. Pearson Award, and his second Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophy.[^cite_note-113] The Capitals finished the regular season with a record of 50–24–8 and a team-record 108 points, and they won their second consecutive Southeast Division title.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] They then overcame a 3–1 series deficit against the New York Rangers in the first round of the [2009 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-116] The Capitals were then defeated by the eventual Stanley Cup champions, the Pittsburgh Penguins, in the conference semifinals in seven games.[^cite_note-117]



Ovechkin and [Sidney Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Crosby) taking the ceremonial puck drop before the [2011 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NHL_Winter_Classic)

The Capitals finished the [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Washington_Capitals_season) first in the NHL with 121 points, thereby winning the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy). Ovechkin led the team in points with 109.[^cite_note-118] Backstrom finished with 101 points, fourth-most in the NHL.[^cite_note-Backstrom_ext-119] Once again, Mike Green led all defensemen in points, finishing with 76.[^cite_note-Mike_Green-106] Defenseman [John Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carlson_\(ice_hockey\)) made his NHL debut this season as well.[^cite_note-120] Despite having a top-ranked regular season, the Capitals were defeated by the eighth-seeded Montreal Canadiens in the first round of the playoffs, giving up a 3–1 series lead in the process.[^cite_note-121] Backstrom was given a ten-year contract extension after the season.[^cite_note-Backstrom_ext-119]

The [2010–11 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Washington_Capitals_season) saw the Capitals repeat as the Southeast Division champions and as the top team in the Eastern Conference with 107 points.[^cite_note-122] The season was highlighted by their participation in the [2011 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NHL_Winter_Classic), where they defeated the Pittsburgh Penguins 3–1 at [Heinz Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrisure_Stadium).[^cite_note-123] However, in the [playoffs](26981832.md), after defeating the New York Rangers in five games in the conference quarterfinals,[^cite_note-124] they were swept by the Tampa Bay Lightning in the conference semifinals.[^cite_note-125]

The Capitals started the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Washington_Capitals_season) with a record of 7–0, but they only won five of their next 15 games. As a result, general manager George McPhee fired head coach Boudreau, hiring Capitals legend Dale Hunter as his replacement.[^cite_note-Boudreau-111] By the end of the 2011–12 season, the team's top two goaltenders, [Michal Neuvirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michal_Neuvirth) and [Tomáš Vokoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Vokoun), were injured and the Capitals were required to lean on their goaltending prospect [Braden Holtby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braden_Holtby) to help the team into the [2012 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-126] The Capitals finished with the seventh overall seed in East, drawing the defending champion Boston Bruins in the first round. The Capitals defeated the defending Stanley Cup champion and second-seeded Boston Bruins in seven games on an overtime goal by [Joel Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Ward_\(ice_hockey\)). Every game in the series was decided by a one-goal margin; previously, no single series in the Stanley Cup playoffs had ever gone as far as six or seven games while neither team ever held more than a one-goal lead.[^cite_note-127] The Capitals then advanced to the second round to face the top-seeded New York Rangers. The series went seven games, ending with a 2–1 Rangers victory at [Madison Square Garden](75129.md).[^cite_note-128] Following the season's end, head coach Dale Hunter announced he would step down. [Adam Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Oates) was later named head coach of the team.[^cite_note-129]



[Joel Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Ward_\(ice_hockey\)) scored the overtime series-winning goal in game seven against a heavily favored defending Stanley Cup champion [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) in the opening round of the [2012 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).

The [lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_NHL_lockout)-shortened [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Washington_Capitals_season) saw the Capitals off to a rocky start, as they managed just two wins in their first ten games.[^cite_note-130] The team rebounded to win the Southeast Division, earning the third seed in the Eastern Conference for the [2013 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-131] However, in the playoffs, the Capitals' fell to the Rangers in seven games for the second consecutive season.[^cite_note-132]

In the [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals struggled to stay in a playoff spot and ultimately missed the playoffs by just three points in the standings, resulting in them missing the playoffs for the first time since [2006–07](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Washington_Capitals_season).[^cite_note-133] [Evgeny Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Kuznetsov) made his NHL debut during the season.[^cite_note-134] On April 26, 2014, 15 days after the regular season ended, the Capitals announced they would not renew general manager George McPhee's contract and that they had fired head coach Adam Oates.[^cite_note-135]



The Capitals hosted the [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks) for the [2015 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_Winter_Classic) at [Nationals Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationals_Park).

On May 26, 2014, the Capitals announced the promotion of [Brian MacLellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_MacLellan) from director of player personnel to general manager and the hiring of [Barry Trotz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Trotz) as the new head coach.[^cite_note-136] In Ovechkin's 691st NHL game on November 4, 2014, he became the Capitals' all-time points leader during a game against the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames), surpassing [Peter Bondra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bondra).[^cite_note-137] On January 1, 2015, the Washington Capitals defeated the Chicago Blackhawks 3–2 in the [2015 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_Winter_Classic) at [Nationals Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationals_Park) in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-138] In the [2015 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Capitals defeated the Islanders in seven games in the first round.[^cite_note-139] In the second round, the Capitals held a 3–1 series lead over the New York Rangers. However, for the fifth time in their history, they gave up the series lead, as the Rangers defeated the Capitals in game seven.[^cite_note-140]

#### Back-to-back Presidents' Trophies and first Stanley Cup championship (2015–2018)

During the offseason of the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Washington_Capitals_season), Mike Green left in free agency and [Justin Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Williams) was acquired in free agency.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] The Capitals also traded [Pheonix Copley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheonix_Copley) and [Troy Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Brouwer) for [T. J. Oshie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._Oshie) with the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues).[^cite_note-143] the Capitals finished in first place in the league with a record of 56–18–8 and 120 points.[^cite_note-144] After defeating the Philadelphia Flyers in the first round,[^cite_note-145] they faced their [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitals%E2%80%93Penguins_rivalry), the Pittsburgh Penguins, for the first time since 2009. The Penguins would win the series in six games.[^cite_note-146]



[Evgeny Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Kuznetsov) skating forward with the puck against the Penguins during the second round of the [2017 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_playoffs)

Ovechkin reached the 1,000-point milestone on January 11, 2017, with a goal against the Pittsburgh Penguins. Ovechkin was the 84th NHL player to reach the 1,000-point milestone, the fourth Russian-born player and the 37th player to reach the milestone while playing for one team throughout their NHL career.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] The Capitals won their second consecutive Presidents' Trophy, becoming just the seventh team in NHL history and the first time since the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) in 2010–11 and 2011–12 to win back-to-back Presidents' Trophies.[^cite_note-149] Ovechkin finished the 2016–17 season with 33 goals, leading the Capitals in goals for the twelfth straight season.[^cite_note-150] Nearing the trade deadline, the Capitals traded with the Blues to acquire former Washington goaltender Pheonix Copley and defenseman [Kevin Shattenkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Shattenkirk).[^cite_note-151] In the [2017 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Capitals defeated the eighth-seeded [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) in six games in the first round to set up a second consecutive showdown with the Pittsburgh Penguins in the second round.[^cite_note-152] After falling behind 3–1 in the series, they battled back to force a game seven at home, where they were eliminated 2–0, and lost their series 4–3.[^cite_note-153]

After the 2017 playoffs, the Capitals were unable to retain a number of players, losing [Kevin Shattenkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Shattenkirk) and [Justin Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Williams) to free agency, and defenseman [Nate Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Schmidt) to the [2017 NHL expansion draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NHL_expansion_draft).[^cite_note-154] In addition, after signing Kuznetsov to long-term contract, the Capitals shipped [Marcus Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Johansson_\(ice_hockey,_born_1990\)) to the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) to assist with the salary cap restrictions.[^cite_note-155]



Ovechkin with the [Stanley Cup](66968.md) at the [National Mall](167796.md), following the [2018 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_Final)

Despite a slow 5–6–1 start to the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Washington_Capitals_season),[^cite_note-156] which extended out to 10–9–1, the Capitals went on a 10–2–2 run in December,[^cite_note-157] and were able to clinch the Metropolitan Division for a third consecutive season on April 1.[^cite_note-158] In the [2018 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Capitals battled back from a 2–0 series deficit against the seventh-seeded [Columbus Blue Jackets](73128.md) in the first round of the playoffs, winning four straight and beating the Blue Jackets in six games.[^cite_note-159] They faced the Penguins again in the second round, and this time, they defeated the Penguins with an overtime goal by Evgeny Kuznetsov in game six. It marked the first time in 20 seasons that the Capitals made the conference finals and the first time in 24 seasons that they had defeated the Penguins in playoff series.[^cite_note-160] The Capitals advanced to the [2018 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_Final) on May 23, after beating the top-seeded [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning) in seven games.[^cite_note-161] They then faced the expansion [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md) and overcame them in five games, including a 4–3 win in the decisive game five. Not only was it the Capitals' first Stanley Cup victory, but it was also the first championship for a Washington, D.C. team in one of the four major North American sports leagues since the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Redskins) defeated the [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) 26 years beforehand in [Super Bowl XXVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVI).[^cite_note-162] Shortly after the Stanley Cup Final, Barry Trotz resigned as the head coach of the Capitals, after negotiations for a contract extension fell through.[^cite_note-163] Assistant coach [Todd Reirden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Reirden) was named as Trotz's replacement on June 29.[^cite_note-164]

#### Playoff struggles (2018–present)

During the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals clinched their fourth straight Metropolitan Division title.[^cite_note-165] In the [2019 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Capitals efforts to repeat as champions ultimately fell short, as they were eliminated in the first round by the Carolina Hurricanes in seven games.[^cite_note-166]

In the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Washington_Capitals_season), prior to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) pausing the season, Ovechkin led the league in goals with 48 and scored his 700th goal on February 22, 2020.[^cite_note-167] The Capitals clinched the division title at the time of the pause,[^cite_note-168] but lost to the New York Islanders in the first round of the [2020 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-169] Following the loss, Reirden was fired and replaced by [Peter Laviolette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Laviolette).[^cite_note-170] The Capitals also lost Braden Holtby to free agency,[^cite_note-171] but gained veterans [Henrik Lundqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist) and [Zdeno Chára](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zdeno_Ch%C3%A1ra).[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] Lundqvist would not play a game with the Capitals due to a heart condition.[^cite_note-174]

During the regular season of the [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Washington_Capitals_season), the team made a trade with the Detroit Red Wings to acquire [Anthony Mantha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Mantha), trading out [Richard Pánik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_P%C3%A1nik), [Jakub Vrána](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Vr%C3%A1na), and two draft picks.[^cite_note-175] However, in the [2021 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), they lost to the Boston Bruins in five games of the first round.[^cite_note-176] In the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Washington_Capitals_season), the Capitals reacquired Marcus Johansson, trading [Daniel Sprong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Sprong) and two draft picks.[^cite_note-177] In the playoffs, they matched against the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy)-winning [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) in the first round, but fell to them in six games.[^cite_note-178] The [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Washington_Capitals_season) saw the Capitals play against the Carolina Hurricanes in the [2023 NHL Stadium Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NHL_Stadium_Series) at [Carter-Finley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter-Finley_Stadium) in [Raleigh, North Carolina](57845.md). The Hurricanes defeated the Capitals 4–1.[^cite_note-179] Washington would also miss the playoffs for the first time since 2014.[^cite_note-180] Following the season, the team and Laviolette agreed to mutually part ways.[^cite_note-181] The Capitals hired 41-year-old [Spencer Carbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Carbery) to be their new head coach on May 30, 2023.[^cite_note-182]



Panoramic view of [Capital One Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Arena) before the Toronto Maple Leafs @ Washington Capitals matchup on [Black Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Friday_\(shopping\)) 2025

In December 2023, [Monumental Sports & Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_Sports_&_Entertainment) founder and team owner [Ted Leonsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Leonsis) engaged in a non-binding partnership with Virginia governor [Glenn Youngkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Youngkin) to move the Capitals and [Washington Wizards](72858.md) to a planned arena in [Potomac Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_Yard) in [Alexandria, Virginia](18940583.md), by 2028.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184] The structure would be part of an arts and entertainment district at the site, which would include a practice facility, restaurants, an esports venue, concert hall, and a new headquarters for Monumental.[^cite_note-185] In March 2024, after \$1.5 billion in proposed state funding for the stadium was voted down in the [Virginia General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_General_Assembly), Washington mayor [Muriel Bowser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Bowser) announced she had signed a deal with Leonsis to keep the Capitals and Wizards in the District "at least until 2050."[^cite_note-186] The Capitals clinched the [2024 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) by beating the Philadelphia Flyers on April 16, finishing with a 40–31–11 record and the second wild card spot.[^cite_note-187] They were swept by the Presidents' Trophy-winning New York Rangers in the first round.[^cite_note-188] On July 8, 2024, the Capitals announced that [Chris Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Patrick_\(ice_hockey\)) would be promoted to general manager, with MacLellan retaining his position as president of hockey operations.[^cite_note-189]

During the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Washington_Capitals_season), Ovechkin broke [Wayne Gretzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky)'s regular season goal record of 894, with his 895th career goal in a 4–1 loss to the New York Islanders on April 6, 2025.[^cite_note-190] The Capitals also clinched their sixth Metropolitan Division title and the first seed in the Eastern Conference.[^cite_note-191] In the playoffs, they were triumphant over the Montreal Canadiens before being defeated by the Hurricanes in the second round.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] Following the season, Nicklas Bäckström left the team to rejoin [Brynäs IF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn%C3%A4s_IF) of the [Swedish Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Hockey_League).[^cite_note-194]

## Team information

### Logos and jerseys



Original logo used by the Capitals (1974–1995)

The Capitals took to the ice in red, white, and blue jerseys featuring contrast-colored shoulders and stars on the chest and sleeves based on the [flag of the United States](11447.md). The original logo crest underwent a few modifications throughout the jersey's history.[^cite_note-logo_history-1]

Prior to the start of the [1995–96 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_NHL_season), the team abandoned its traditional red, white, and blue color scheme in favor of a blue, black, and copper palette with an American bald eagle with five stars as its logo. The alternate logo depicted the [Capitol](31979.md) building with crossed hockey sticks behind.[^cite_note-195] For the [1997–98 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_NHL_season), the team unveiled a black alternate jersey, devoid of blue with copper stripes on the ends of sleeves and at the waist. The crest on the white and blue jerseys were the bald eagle logo, while the crest on the black jersey was the Capitol logo. Initially, the team name was placed along the bottom black stripe, but was removed on the white jersey in 1997, while it remained on the blue jersey until its retirement. Prior to the [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_NHL_season), the team retired its blue road jersey in favor of the black alternate jersey, but still kept the white jersey for home games.[^cite_note-logo_history-1]





Second and third black, blue, and copper logos used by the Capitals. The logo on the left was used from 1995 to 2002, while the logo on the right was used from 2002 to 2007.

The Capitals unveiled new uniforms on June 22, 2007, which coincided with the [2007 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NHL_entry_draft) and the new league-wide adaptation of the [Reebok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_uniform#Reebok_Edge_\(2007%E2%80%932017\))-designed uniform system for [2007–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_NHL_season). The change marked a return to the red, white, and blue color scheme originally used from 1974 to 1995.[^cite_note-196] The new primary logo is reminiscent of the original Capitals' logo, complete with a hockey stick formed by the letter "t"; it also includes a new feature not present in the original logo in the form of three stars representing [DC](108956.md), [Maryland](18858.md), and [Virginia](32432.md).[^cite_note-197] The stars are a reference to the [flag of Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_District_of_Columbia), which is in turn based on the shield of [George Washington's family coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_George_Washington). The new alternate logo used an eagle in the shape of a "W" with the silhouette of the Washington Monument and the United States Capitol building in the negative space within and below.[^cite_note-logo_history-1]

For the [2011 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NHL_Winter_Classic), the Capitals wore a white jersey honoring the franchise's past with the original logo. The jersey resembled the one the franchise wore from 1974 to 1995. Instead of wearing the combination of blue pants and white helmets the team used when it played at the [Capital Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Centre_\(Landover,_Maryland\)), the Capitals chose red pants and helmets for the New Year's Day game. The Capitals wore the same jersey, minus the NHL Winter Classic patch, on February 1, 2011, to honor Hockey Hall of Fame winger [Dino Ciccarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Ciccarelli).[^cite_note-198]



The current alternate logo used by the Capitals, introduced in 2007. Given the nickname "Weagle" by fans

The Capitals announced on September 16, 2011, that they would wear a third jersey modeled after the Winter Classic jersey for 16 road games during the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NHL_season).[^cite_note-logo_history-1] For the [2015 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_Winter_Classic), which took place on New Year's Day at Nationals Park in Washington, D.C., the Capitals wore a newly designed jersey intended to pay homage to hockey's outdoor roots. The primary color of the jersey was a vintage deep red. The addition of stripes on the shoulders, waist and legs brought in elements of Washington's professional hockey jerseys from the 1930s, predating the Capitals franchise's formation in the 1970s. A large "W" on the front of the jersey, topped with the common three stars, offset in blue to contrast the white "Capitals" wordmark.[^cite_note-199]

Starting with the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NHL_season), the Capitals wore their throwback red third jerseys, replacing the white Winter Classic thirds.[^cite_note-logo_history-1]

Prior to the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NHL_season), the NHL announced a new partnership with [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_uniform#Adidas_ADIZERO_\(2017%E2%80%932024\)), and the Capitals unveiled new uniforms with minor changes. There were no third jerseys during that season, but the return of the program in the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_NHL_season) saw the return of the Capitals' red throwback uniforms as their alternates.[^cite_note-logo_history-1]

For the [2018 Stadium Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NHL_Stadium_Series), the Capitals used newly designed navy uniforms, honoring the fact that the game was held at the [U.S. Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Academy). The chest logo was based on the regular stylized "Capitals" logo, but shortened to "Caps", the nickname commonly used for the team. There were also features honoring various aspects of D.C., as well as the presence of a slightly altered W logo from the 2015 Winter Classic on the pants.[^cite_note-200]

In 2021, as part of Adidas' "Reverse Retro" uniform series, the Capital unveiled a recolored version of their blue "screaming eagle" uniform used from 1995 to 2000. The base color is red with dark blue accents.[^cite_note-logo_history-1] The Capitals also replaced their throwback red alternates with a dark blue third jersey; this design featured three red stars and a uniquely-designed white "W" with the [Washington Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Monument) triangle in the middle. The uniform also features white/red/white stripes on the waist and sleeves and a white shoulder yoke.[^cite_note-201]

In 2022, a second "Reverse Retro" uniform was released, this time featuring a black version of the "screaming eagle" uniform with blue and copper accents.[^cite_note-202] This uniform was restored as the team's alternate ahead of the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NHL_season), updated to the [Fanatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_uniform#Fanatics_\(2024%E2%80%93beyond\)) template.[^cite_note-203]

For the [2023 Stadium Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NHL_Stadium_Series), the Capitals unveiled a white uniform centered around the alternate "Weagle" logo, with its navy wings extending towards the sleeves with white numbers.[^cite_note-204]

Prior to the 2025–26 season, the Capitals redesigned the "screaming eagle" alternate, now recolored to a red base with dark blue and silver accents. The white shoulder yoke and dark blue and white stripes were a visual nod to the original red uniforms.[^cite_note-205]

### Practice facility



The Capitals practice at the [MedStar Capitals Iceplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MedStar_Capitals_Iceplex) in [Ballston, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballston,_Virginia).

Since 2006, the Capitals have practiced in the [Ballston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballston,_Virginia) neighborhood of [Arlington County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia). The practice facility is known as the [MedStar Capitals Iceplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MedStar_Capitals_Iceplex).[^cite_note-206]

### Mascot

Since 1995, the Capitals' mascot has been Slapshot, a bald eagle that wears the number 00.[^cite_note-207] He was named by a fifth grader who won the naming contest.[^cite_note-208]

### Honored members

#### Hall of Fame honorees

The Washington Capitals hold an affiliation with a number of inductees to the [Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame), including nine inductees from the players category and one from the builders category, five of whom played at least five seasons with the club (Gartner, Oates, Stevens, Langway, and Murphy).

In addition to players, members of the local [sports media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_media) have been honored by the Hockey Hall of Fame. In 2007, Dave Fay, a sports journalist for *[The Washington Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Times)*, was a recipient of the [Elmer Ferguson Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Ferguson_Memorial_Award).[^cite_note-211] In 2010, [play-by-play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play-by-play) radio broadcaster [Ron Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Weber) was awarded the [Foster Hewitt Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_Hewitt_Memorial_Award) from the Hall of Fame for his contributions to hockey broadcasting.[^cite_note-212]

## Broadcasters

Main article: [List of Washington Capitals broadcasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Washington_Capitals_broadcasters)

[Monumental Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_Sports_Network) (MSN) has carried Capitals games locally since its founding as Home Team Sports (HTS) in 1984.[^cite_note-bs-226] The channel was later rebranded into Comcast SportsNet Mid-Atlantic (CSN) from 2001 through 2017, and then NBC Sports Washington from 2017 to 2023.[^cite_note-227] MSN's commentators are [Joe Beninati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Beninati), [Craig Laughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Laughlin), "Inside-the-Glass" reporter [Alan May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_May), and rinkside reporter Al Koken.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]

The Capitals' flagship radio station is [WJFK-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WJFK-FM) (106.7 FM); commentators are [John Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walton_\(sports_broadcaster\)) and [Ken Sabourin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Sabourin). The team's radio network consists of stations in Washington, [Virginia](32432.md), [Maryland](18858.md), [West Virginia](32905.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), and [North Carolina](21650.md).[^cite_note-231]

Mike Vogel has been covering the team online for the Washington Capitals on its website since the 1995–96 season, writing daily game stories and analysis. Vogel, who also participates in podcasts and in-stadium video presentations as well as guesting on various Washington DC radio and television programs, has been described as "the most interesting man in Caps media".[^cite_note-232]
