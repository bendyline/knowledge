# Village head



The village head of [Kabanjahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabanjahe) in the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) in the 1930s

A **village head**, **village headman** or **village chief** is the leader of a village, hamlet, or other type of settlement too small to have their own [mayor](101146.md).[^cite_note-1]

Unlike the position of mayor, denoting a leader of a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City), [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) or other large settlements with its own incorporated government, the title of village head may be purely ceremonial or even entirely unofficial, simply denoting an individual which commands sizeable political and social influence in a given area. This is especially the case in the modern era, as increased urbanization and advances in communication mean that most villages are subject to some form of local government already, and thus the need for local chiefs has decreased. However, in some countries, village heads continue to exist as an official government position analogous to that of a mayor, or at the very least as a consultative or representative role that mediates between small communities and higher levels of government.

## Usage

### Brunei

See also: [Villages of Brunei § Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villages_of_Brunei#Administration)

In [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), village head is called *ketua kampung* or *ketua kampong* in the [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language). It is an administrative post which leads the community of a [village administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villages_of_Brunei), the third and lowest [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Brunei) of the country.

### China

In China, village head ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 村长; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 村長; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *cūn zhǎng*) is a local government or tribal post. The village headman is the person appointed to administer an area that is often a single [village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village).

#### Duties and functions

The headman has several official duties in the village, and is sometimes seen as a [mediator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediation) in disputes and a general "fixer" of village or individuals problems.

Examples of headmanship have been observed among the [Zuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuni_people),[^cite_note-2] [!Kung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/!Kung_people), and [Mehinacu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehinacu),[^cite_note-3] among others. Nearby tribal leaders recognized or appointed by the [Chinese](5405.md) were known as *tusi* (*tu-szu*; [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [土司](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%9C%9F%E5%8F%B8); [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *tǔsī*; [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade%E2%80%93Giles): *t'u<sup>3</sup>-szu<sup>1</sup>*), although they could command larger areas than a single village.

### Indonesia

The village head in [Indonesia](14579.md) is called *Kepala Desa*.

### Malaysia

See also: [Category\:Villages in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Villages_in_Malaysia)

Generally in [Malaysia](3607937.md), the village head is called *Ketua Kampung*, except for the [proto Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_asli) village where the position is called *Batin*. Ketua Kampung was appointed and assisted by *Majlis Pengurusan Komuniti Kampung* (Village Community Management Board). In [Sarawak](28258.md), the head of a traditional [long house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_house) is called *Tuai Rumah*.

### Philippines

Main article: [Barangay captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay_captain)

Each [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) (village) in the Philippines is led by a [barangay captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay_captain).

## Historical usage

### China

Main article: [Dibao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibao)

In the [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) and early [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) era, *dibao* were officially appointed village officials, usually selected from the local landowning class and responsible for land use and boundaries in their jurisdiction.

### Japan

In [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) Japan, the village head was called *[nanushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanushi)* (名主) and was in charge of tax collection, general village administration, management of public natural resources (such as mountain, field, river and ocean) of the village, as well as negotiating with the territorial lord as the representative of the villagers.[^cite_note-4]
