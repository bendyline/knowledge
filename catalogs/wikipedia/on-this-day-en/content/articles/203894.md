**Obock** (also **Obok**, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): أبخ, *Hayyú*) is a small port town in [Djibouti](17207794.md). It is located on the Northern shore of the [Gulf of Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tadjoura), where it opens out into the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md). The town is home to [an airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obock_Airport) and has ferries to [Djibouti City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_City). The French form Obock derives from Arabic "Oboh", which is a deformation of Oboki, a name given to a local [wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi).

## History

Main articles: [Ifat Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifat_Sultanate), [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate), and [Obock Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obock_Territory)

The fishing village was originally built on the [plateau](538005.md) of Dala-h Húgub near the Dar'i [Wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi), with some houses constructed of mud and stone and Daboyta. Most of the inhabitants made their living through [animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal) husbandry, fishing, [commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce) and used a well for drinking [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water). During the Middle Ages, Obock was ruled by the [Ifat Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifat_Sultanate) and then the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate). The Sultans of Raheita emerged from the Adal Sultanate. Although nominally part of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) since 1554, between 1821 and 1841, [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt), Pasha of Egypt, came to control [Yemen](350939.md) and modern-day [Eritrea](17238590.md), and claims on Ethiopia as far as [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar).[^cite_note-awdalpress.com-2] In 1884, the commander of the patrol sloop L’Inferent, a French ship, confirmed the Egyptian presence in the area around Obock. The commander of the patrol sloop Le Vaudreuil also reported that the Egyptians were occupying the interior between Obock and [Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadjoura).[^cite_note-Clifford-3] In actuality, however, Egypt had little authority over the interior and their period of rule on the coast was brief, lasting only a few years before the Egyptian garrison was withdrawn from the area in 1862,

### French Somaliland



Panorama of Obock in 1882 with first French factory on the left

During the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa), growing French interest in the area took place against a backdrop of British activity in [Egypt](8087628.md) and the opening of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1869. Between 1883 and 1887, France signed various treaties with the then ruling [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people) and [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) [Sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan), which allowed it to expand the protectorate to include the [Gulf of Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tadjoura).[^cite_note-Uwechue-4] Obock was originally significant as the site of the first [French](5843419.md) colony in the region, established by treaty with the local [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) rulers on March 11, 1862.[^cite_note-Scott's_monthly_stamp_journal-5] The French were interested in having a [coaling station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaling_station) for [steamships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship), which would become especially important upon the opening of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1869. (Up to that time French ships had to buy coal at the British [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) of [Aden](38050.md) across the gulf, an unwise dependency in case of war.)&#x20;



The French traders settlement and the coal depot in the mid 1880s.

The site was not the subject of any occupation, just visited by the ships of the [naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval) divisions assigned to the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), until the installation of trader Pierre Arnoux in 1881, followed by [Paul Soleillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Soleillet). Obock became a true colony in 1884 with the arrival in August of [Léonce Lagarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9once_Lagarde), who established an administration and extended French possession in the [Gulf of Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tadjoura), forming the Territory of Obock and outbuildings, with Obock as its capital. By 1885, Obock had 800 inhabitants and a [school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School). However, the anchorage was more exposed than the site of [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_\(city\)) on the south side of the [Gulf of Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tadjoura), and the colonial administration moved there in 1894. The population of Obock subsequently declined.[^cite_note-6]



Panorama of Obock in 1920.

&#x20;However, until the French occupation of Tadjoura in 1927, Obock remained the only place on the northern coast of the Gulf of Tadjoura with a colonial administrative office. It became the capital of the Dankali District in 1914, then an administrative position from 1927 which was subsumed into the "Circle of Adaels" in 1929. During [World War II](32927.md), [Italy](14532.md)'s declaration of war on [France](5843419.md) and [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) came on 10 June 1940, with them undertaking some offensive actions beginning on 18 June.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompsonAdloff196816-7] From [Harar Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrar_Governorate), troops under General Guglielmo Nasi were sent to attack French Somaliland. There were some skirmishes. When the [Vichy French government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) on 10 July learned that the armistice was not yet put into effect in [French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Somaliland), President [Philippe Pétain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_P%C3%A9tain) sent General Gaëtan Germain as his personal representative to correct the situation. Negotiations at [Dewele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewele), [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) on the local implementation of the armistice were only finally completed on 8 August.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERovighi1995109-8] By that time, the British offensive against the Italians had tightened the [blockade of French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_French_Somaliland). [Famine](51329.md) set in and malnutrition-related diseases took many lives, 70% of them women and children. Many townsfolk left for the [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterland). The locals named the blockade the carmii, a word for a type of sorghum usually reserved for [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), which used as human food at the height of the famine. Obock became the capital of a circle responsible for resupplying the colony during the Allied blockade from 1941 to 1943. Only a few Arab dhows (boutres) managed to run the blockade to [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_city) and Obock and only two French [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) from [Madagascar](18964.md) managed to run it. The [Japanese declaration of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_declaration_of_war_on_the_United_States_and_the_British_Empire) on the Allies (7 December 1941) gave the colony some respite, since the British were forced to withdraw all but two ships from the [blockade](293054.md) for use in the [Far East](11672.md).&#x20;

The [Commander-in-Chief, East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Command), [William Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Platt), codenamed the negotiations for the [surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_\(military\)) of [French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Somaliland) "Pentagon", because there were five sides: himself, the Vichy governor, the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French), the British minister at Addis Ababa and the [United States](3434750.md). [Christian Raimond Dupont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Raimond_Dupont) surrendered and Colonel Raynal's troops crossed back into French Somaliland on 26 December 1942, completing its liberation. The official handover took place at 10\:00 p.m. on 28 December.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEImbert-Vier2008172-9] The first governor appointed under the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French) was [André Bayardelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Bayardelle?action=edit\&redlink=1). A local battalion from French Somaliland participated in the [Liberation of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris) in 1944. In 1963, Obock's circle was created by division of that of Tadjourah region.

### Djibouti

A [third independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Afars_and_Issas_independence_referendum) was held in the [French Territory of the Afars and the Issas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Territory_of_the_Afars_and_the_Issas) on 8 May 1977. The previous referendums were held in [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_French_Somaliland_overseas_territory_referendum) and [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_French_Somaliland_independence_referendum),[^cite_note-Kseoah-10] which rejected [independence](62450.md). This referendum backed independence from [France](5843419.md).[^cite_note-11] A landslide 98.8% of the electorate supported disengagement from France, officially marking [Djibouti's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Djibouti\)). Obock is expected to be the site of the [Chinese naval base in Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_naval_base_in_Djibouti).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

Obock has become a key stop in the route for illegal migration from Ethiopia to Saudi Arabia, being the location from where smugglers take migrants on boats to Yemen.[^cite_note-14]

## Demographics

As of the 2024 census, the population of Obock was at 20,152.[^cite_note-pop-1] The town inhabitants belong to various mainly [Afro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic_languages)-speaking ethnic groups. The [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) , [Gadabursi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadabuursi) and [Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issa_\(clan\)) [Somali](1571696.md), are predominant.

## Postage stamps

During its time as a French colony, Obock issued its own [postage stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps); for more detail see [Postage stamps and postal history of Obock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps_and_postal_history_of_Obock).

## Overview

Obock is connected to other environs by [RN-14 National Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_14_\(Djibouti\)). A ferry ride from [Djibouti City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_City) to Obock takes three hours; the distance is 237 km (147 mi).

## Climate and geography

Obock has a dry climate. It is classified as hot and [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *[*BSh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate)*). Obock is 13 m above sea level on the plateau of Gazelles ("Dala-h Húgub in Afar") in desert terrain. The sky is always clear and bright throughout the year.[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-15]
