The [Republic of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Nicaragua) elects on the national level a [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state)—the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(government_title\))—and a [unicameral](204304.md) [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature). The [president of Nicaragua](302862.md) and their vice-president are elected on one ballot for a five-year term by the people.

The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Nicaragua) (*Asamblea Nacional*) has 92 members: 90 deputies elected for a five-year term by [proportional representation](23298.md) (20 nationally and 70 regionally), the outgoing president, and the runner-up in the last presidential election. Should the president be reelected (not originally planned for in the Nicaraguan constitution), the outgoing vice president takes the seat reserved for him instead. In July 2026, current President [Daniel Ortega](8778.md) announced the suspension of future elections in Nicaragua.&#x20;

## Past elections

### Presidential elections 1984–2011

#### 1984

The 1984 election took place on November 4. Of the 1,551,597 citizens registered in July, 1,170,142 voted (75.41%). The null votes were 6% of the total. The national averages of valid votes for president were:

- [Daniel Ortega](8778.md), [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 66.97%

- [Clemente Guido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemente_Guido?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Democratic Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Conservative_Party) (PCD) – 14.04%

- [Virgilio Godoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgilio_Godoy), [Independent Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Liberal_Party_\(Nicaragua\)) (PLI) – 9.60%

- [Mauricio Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Diaz_\(politician\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Popular Social Christian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Social_Christian_Party) (PPSC) – 5.56%

- [Allan Zambrana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Zambrana?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nicaraguan Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Communist_Party) (PCdeN) – 1.45%

- [Domingo Sánchez Sancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domingo_S%C3%A1nchez_Sancho?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nicaraguan Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Socialist_Party) (PSN) – 1.31%

- [Isidro Téllez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidro_T%C3%A9llez?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Marxist-Leninist Popular Action Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist-Leninist_Popular_Action_Movement) (MAP-ML) – 1.03%

The pro-Sandinista magazine, *Envio* claimed that this election was considered to have the "most freedom of choice" in the nation's history and was approved by international advocates of free elections.<http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2578>

#### 1990

The historical election of 1990 took place on February 25. The total registered voters were 1,752,088 and the abstentions 241,250 or 13.7%. The [United Nicaraguan Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nicaraguan_Opposition) coalition of those who opposed the ruling [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) was victorious, winning 55% of the vote. [Violeta Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_Chamorro) became president. The national averages of valid votes for president were:

- [Violeta Barrios de Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_Barrios_de_Chamorro), [National Opposition Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Opposition_Union_\(Nicaragua,_1990\)) (UNO) – 777,552 votes – 54.73%

- [Daniel Ortega](8778.md), [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 579,886 votes – 40.82%

- Other – 63,106 – 4.45%<http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/621>

### Parliamentary election results 1984–2016

#### 1984

The 1984 parliamentary election was held together with the presidential election on November 4. The percentages for National Assembly representatives were very similar to those the parties had received for their presidential candidate. The electoral quotient needed to win one of the 90 National Assembly seats was obtained by dividing the number of valid votes in each region by the number of representatives that had been assigned to each region, proportional to its population.

Each party's "left over" votes—those insufficient to earn it a seat in a given region—were then added together and re-tallied nationally. The seats earned in this second count went to the next candidate on the party's slate in the regions where it had come closest to winning on the first round. In addition, any party getting at least 1% of the presidential vote (which all six losing parties did) was allowed a seat for its defeated presidential candidate. The final composition of the National Assembly was thus:

- [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 61 seats

- [Democratic Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Conservative_Party) (PCD) – 14 seats

- [Independent Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Liberal_Party_\(Nicaragua\)) (PLI) – 9 seats

- [Popular Social Christian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Social_Christian_Party) (PPSC) – 6 seats

- [Nicaraguan Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Communist_Party) (PCdeN) – 2 seats

- [Nicaraguan Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Socialist_Party) (PSN) – 2 seats

- [Marxist-Leninist Popular Action Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist-Leninist_Popular_Action_Movement) (MAP-ML) – 2 seats

Source: <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2578>

### Municipal election results 1990–2017

#### 1990

The 1990 municipal election was held together with the presidential and the parliamentary elections on February 25. Municipal Councils were elected in 131 municipalities nationwide. The final results for the elections were:

- [National Opposition Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Opposition_Union_\(Nicaragua,_1990\)) (UNO) – 98 municipalities controlled

- [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 31 municipalities controlled

- [Central American Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Unionist_Party) (PUCA) – 2 municipalities controlled

Sources: <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2587> <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2613>

#### 1996

A great expectation in the 1996 municipal elections was the participation for the first (and last) time of what the Electoral Law terms "popular subscription associations". According to the Electoral Law, to be formed, an association needed, among other things, to present to the [Supreme Electoral Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Council_\(Nicaragua\)) a "written request signed by a minimum of 5% of the citizens on the electoral rolls corresponding to the respective electoral area". A total of 53 associations participated in the municipal elections. One of them (the [Civic Association of Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Association_of_Potos%C3%AD)) won the mayor's post.

Despite winning only one municipality, an important number of association candidates finished in second or third place. In the nation's capital, [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), two independent candidates; [Pedro Solórzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Sol%C3%B3rzano?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Viva Managua Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viva_Managua_Movement) association and [Herty Lewites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herty_Lewites) of the Sol (sun) association competed against the AL and FSLN official candidates. ALN's [Roberto Cedeño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Cede%C3%B1o?action=edit\&redlink=1) got the 28% of the votes followed closely by Solórzano with 26%, [Carlos Guadamúz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Guadam%C3%BAz?action=edit\&redlink=1) from the FSLN with 25.7% and [Herty Lewites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herty_Lewites) who became [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua)'s mayor four years later came in fourth place with 12.3%.

The 1996 municipal election took place together with the presidential election on October 20. Municipal Councils were elected in 145 municipalities nationwide. The final results for the elections were:

- [Liberal Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Liberal_Alliance) (AL) – 92 municipalities controlled

- [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 51 municipalities controlled

- [Sandinista Renovation Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_Renovation_Movement) (MRS) – 1 municipality controlled

- [Civic Association of Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Association_of_Potos%C3%AD) (ACP) – 1 municipality controlled

Source: <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/1990>

#### 2000

In the 2000 municipal election 1,532,816 voters elected Municipal Councils in 151 municipalities nationwide. It was the first time that the presidential and municipal elections were held separately. The final results for the elections were:

- [Constitutionalist Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalist_Liberal_Party) (PLC) – 94 municipalities controlled

- [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 52 municipalities controlled

- [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Nicaragua) (PC) – 5 municipalities controlled

The FSLN won for the first time in ten years the municipality of [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), Nicaragua's capital city with its candidate [Herty Lewites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herty_Lewites) that pulled 44% of the votes.

Source: <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2672>

#### 2004

In the 2004 municipal election 1,664,243 voters elected Municipal Councils in 152 municipalities nationwide, with nearly a 56% abstention. The final results for the elections were:

- [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN-Convergence) – 87 municipalities controlled

- [Constitutionalist Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalist_Liberal_Party) (PLC) – 57 municipalities controlled

- [Alliance for the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_the_Republic_\(Nicaragua\)) (APRE) – 4 municipalities controlled

- [Yapti Tasba Masraka Nanih Aslatakanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yapti_Tasba_Masraka_Nanih_Aslatakanka) (YATAMA) – 3 municipalities controlled

- [Nicaraguan Resistance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Resistance_Party) (PRN) – 1 municipality controlled

Note: Elections took place for the first time in the newly created municipality of [San José de Bocay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9_de_Bocay) in the [Jinotega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinotega_\(department\)) department.

The 2004 municipal elections represented a huge Sandinista victory. The FSLN-Convergence won 14 of the 17 departmental capitals, 87 of the 152 municipalities—including 5 of the 6 that make up [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua)'s greater metropolitan area—and 25 of [Nicaragua](21362.md)'s 42 largest cities. In total it will govern a little over 4 million inhabitants, nearly 71% of the national population.

The Sandinista victory was attributed to the success of the FSLN-Convergence alliance. Of the 87 mayors elected on the FSLN ticket, 17 come from these allies: 5 are independents, 3 are from the Resistance, 3 belong to the [Sandinista Renovation Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_Renovation_Movement) (MRS), 2 are Conservatives, 2 are Liberals, 1 is from the [Christian Unity Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Unity_Movement) (MUC) and 1 is a Social Christian. Of the deputy mayors who ran with an FSLN mayoral candidate, 28 are Liberals, 16 are independent, 14 are from the MUC, 9 are Conservatives, 9 are from the MRS, 3 are from the Resistance and 1 is a Social Christian. These allied candidates allowed the FSLN to win 12 municipal governments for the first time.

Source: <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2672>

### Autonomous elections on the Caribbean Coast results 1990–2014

#### 1990

The first autonomous elections on the Caribbean Coast took place in 1990 together with the presidential, parliamentary and municipal election on February 25. The voters elected the 45 Regional Council members in what was officially called the [North Atlantic Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Autonomous_Region) (RAAN) and the 45 in the [South Atlantic Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Autonomous_Region) (RAAS). The abstention was 21%, only 7% higher than the national average:

- [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN) – 39 seats (RAAN: 21, RAAS: 18)

- [Yapti Tasba Masraka Nanih Aslatakanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yapti_Tasba_Masraka_Nanih_Aslatakanka) (YATAMA) – 26 seats (RAAN: 22, RAAS: 4)

- [National Opposition Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Opposition_Union_\(Nicaragua,_1990\)) (UNO) – 25 seats (RAAN: 2, RAAS: 23)

Note: National Assembly representatives also have a seat.

Sources: <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/2587> <http://www.envio.org.ni/articulo/3222>

The voters elected 45 members to each Regional Council in the newly renamed [North Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region) (RACCN) and [South Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region) (RACCS):
