The [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md), officially known as the XXV Winter Olympic Games, were an international winter [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) held in [Milan](36511.md) and [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md), Italy, from 6 to 22 February. A total of 2,900 athletes representing 92 [National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) (NOCs) participated,[^cite_note-1] including first-time entrants [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics), [Guinea-Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics), and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The games featured 116 events in eight [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_sports) across 16 disciplines, including [ski mountaineering](424477.md), which made its Olympic debut.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

Athletes representing 29 NOCs received at least one medal, with 19 winning at least one gold medal.[^cite_note-7] Athletes from [Norway](79531438.md) won the most medals overall, with 41, surpassing the previous record of 39 medals set by Norway at the [2018 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics). Norwegian athletes also won the most gold medals with 18, breaking the previous record of 16 golds set by the Norwegians at the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-8]

[Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) won the first medal and first gold medal in their Winter Olympic history. It is also the first [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_nations_at_the_Winter_Olympics), [Latin American](18524.md) and [South American](26769.md) National Olympic Committee to win a medal at the Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] [Georgia](79463437.md) also won the first medal in their Winter Olympic history.[^cite_note-12]

Among individual participants, Norwegian [cross-country skier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skier) [Johannes Høsflot Klæbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_H%C3%B8sflot_Kl%C3%A6bo) won the most gold and overall medals, with six medals (all gold).[^cite_note-13] In doing so, he set the record for the most gold medals won at a single Winter Olympics and most career gold medals won by a Winter Olympic athlete (11 gold medals total).[^cite_note-14] Among Winter Olympians, Klaebo's 13 overall medals trails only biathlete [Ole Einar Bjørndalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Einar_Bj%C3%B8rndalen) and cross-country skier [Marit Bjørgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marit_Bj%C3%B8rgen), the latter of whom holds the record with 15 medals.[^cite_note-15]

## Medals

See also: [Olympic medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal)

The official medals of the Games were unveiled in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), designed as two halves that symbolise the culmination of an athlete's journey and of all those who have walked beside them along the way. The medals were created by the [Istituto Poligrafico e Zecca dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Poligrafico_e_Zecca_dello_Stato) (IPZS).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] They have an essential design that places emotion and teamwork at its core. They feature the traditional Olympic five-ring symbol on one side with an inscription on the reverse that details the event and commemorates the two host cities.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Medal table

Further information: [List of 2026 Winter Olympics medal winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2026_Winter_Olympics_medal_winners)

The medal table is based on information provided by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC) and is consistent with IOC conventional sorting in its published medal tables. The table uses the [Olympic medal table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal_table) sorting method. By default, the table is ordered by the number of gold medals the athletes from a nation have won, where a nation is an entity represented by a NOC. The number of silver medals is taken into consideration next and then the number of bronze medals. If teams are still tied, equal ranking is given and they are listed alphabetically by their [IOC country code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_IOC_country_codes).[^cite_note-20]
