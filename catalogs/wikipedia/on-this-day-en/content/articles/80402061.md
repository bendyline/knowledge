On 7 July 2025, the [Houthis](17240332.md) attacked **MV *Eternity C,*** a Liberia-[flagged](6162348.md) [bulk carrier](1324895.md), in the southern [Red Sea](26191.md) on route from [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera), Somalia, where it has been delivering aid. The vessel was assaulted with [sea drones](7808557.md) and [rocket-propelled grenades](26286.md) and was seriously damaged. The vessel was abandoned and sank shortly after the attacks. Among at least 25 Filipino, Indian, Greek and Russian personnel on board, four Filipino crew members have been either confirmed or presumed killed in the attack; while the rest survived, with eleven of them captured by the Houthis and released in December.

The Houthis stated they attacked *Eternity C* because the vessel's operator continues to make port visits to Israel with other ships,[^cite_note-1] and that they took an unspecified number of crew to a "safe location", although the United States stated the Houthis had taken the crew hostage.[^cite_note-2] The vessel is [registered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_registration) in [Monrovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monrovia), and owned and managed by Cosmo Ship Management of [Athens](1216.md), Greece.[^cite_note-3]

During the same period, the merchant ship *Magic Seas* was hijacked and sunk by the Houthis after an attack.

## Background

### Red Sea crisis

Main articles: [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md) and [Houthi attacks on commercial vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_attacks_on_commercial_vessels)

Since November 2023, the [Houthis](17240332.md), who [captured the capital of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sanaa_\(2014\)) in 2014 during the [country's civil war](46215853.md), have conducted attacks against merchant and naval vessels they claim are linked to Israel, mainly in the [Red Sea](26191.md) and [Gulf of Aden](58716.md).[^cite_note-\:0-4] The Houthis state the attacks are carried out in solidarity with the [Palestinians](23267.md) in the [Gaza war](74998519.md). The attacks sparked [airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US%E2%80%93UK_airstrikes_on_Yemen) led by the United States, Israel, and the United Kingdom against targets in [Houthi-controlled territory in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_Yemen).[^cite_note-\:2-5]

The attacks forced companies to halt shipping in the Red Sea, which previously accounted for 12% of global trade.[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-7]

### MV *Eternity C*

MV *Eternity C* was a [bulk carrier](1324895.md) which sailed under the flag of Liberia. The ship had completed a humanitarian delivery for the [World Food Programme](33582.md) to [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera), Somalia, where it has arrived in late June and left around noon on 6 July, and was heading to [Jeddah](277718.md), Saudi Arabia, to refuel.[^cite_note-l197-8][^cite_note-n238-9][^cite_note-q073-10]

### Prior attack

A similar incident occurred prior to that involving *Eternity C*.[^cite_note-2bbc-11] On 6 July, the Houthis launched [missile](19594.md) and [drone attack](38212231.md) at cargo ship *Magic Seas*[^cite_note-2bbc-11][^cite_note-rMS-12] in the Red Sea, southwest of the Houthi-controlled port city of [Hodeidah](769308.md) in Yemen.[^cite_note-ajMS-13] The said bulk carrier was carrying iron and fertiliser from [China](5405.md) to [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-rMS-12] The strikes reportedly caused damages on the vessel's cargo, and as reported by the [United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations](9629829.md) (UKMTO), set the ship ablaze,[^cite_note-rMS-12] causing it to sink.[^cite_note-2bbc-11][^cite_note-aj709-14] All 22 personnel aboard—the crew of 17 Filipinos, a Romanian and a Vietnamese; and three Sri Lankan security guards[^cite_note-nwM-15]—abandoned the ship[^cite_note-ajMS-13] prior to its sinking,[^cite_note-g709-16] were rescued by a passing [Emirati](69328.md) merchant vessel,[^cite_note-rMS-12] and were later brought to [Djibouti](17207794.md).[^cite_note-rMS-12]

The Yemen's [Iran](14653.md)-backed group, on 7 July, claimed responsibility for the assault;[^cite_note-rMS-12] and released a video footage the following day, showing armed men boarding the vessel, and the vessel's explosion and sinking.[^cite_note-2bbc-11][^cite_note-nwM-15] Significantly, it was their first known attack in the area within few months.[^cite_note-ajMS-13]

Both vessels, [Liberian](17791.md)-flagged and [Greek](12108.md)-operated,[^cite_note-2bbc-11] had docked in [Israel](9282173.md) in the past.[^cite_note-g709-16]

## Events

### Attack and immediate aftermath

The *Eternity C*, while navigating the Red Sea off Hodeidah in western Yemen[^cite_note-g709-16][^cite_note-xn1204-17] and *en route* to Jeddah, [Saudi Arabia](349303.md),[^cite_note-gct1203-18] was attacked twice by Houthi militants[^cite_note-g709-16] with [gunfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunfire), [remote-controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telerobotics) [sea drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_drone) loaded with [explosives](10192.md),[^cite_note-gct1203-18] and [rocket-propelled grenades](26286.md) from [skiffs](1913729.md).[^cite_note-g709-16][^cite_note-cnn710-19] The first occurred in the afternoon of 7 July 2025; while the second in the night of 8 July forced the crew to jump into the water.[^cite_note-g709-16] The bulk carrier was badly damaged[^cite_note-g709-16] and, as UKMTO and the [Agence France-Presse](471981.md) (AFP) reported, eventually sank in the morning of 9 July.[^cite_note-g709-16][^cite_note-cnn710-19]

There were 25 people reportedly aboard the vessel—a 22-member crew (21 Filipinos and a Russian)[^cite_note-cnn710-19] and a three-member [private maritime security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_military_company) team (two Indians and a Greek).[^cite_note-k710-20][^cite_note-aj709-14][^cite_note-\:11-21]

[Operation Aspides](76128469.md), the [European Union](9317.md)'s maritime security mission that coordinated rescue operations,[^cite_note-cnn710-19] later presumed the deaths of at least four in the attack and its aftermath.[^cite_note-AP713-22][^cite_note-\:11-21] On 4 December, the [Department of Migrant Workers](69632130.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) confirmed that three Filipino crew members died, whose remains were later transported back to their country;[^cite_note-23] while another reportedly remained missing after jumping out of the ship.[^cite_note-24]

### Search and rescue operations

On 9 July, a [search and rescue](172599.md) mission began overnight.[^cite_note-\:11-21] Operation Aspides later reported that ten mariners had been rescued until 10 July, including eight Filipino crew members and two security personnel[^cite_note-cnn710-19]—its Greek leader[^cite_note-k710-20] and an Indian.[^cite_note-25] They were later brought by a ship to [Jizan](1907222.md), Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-R715-26]

Also on 9 July, the Houthis issued a statement claiming responsibility on the attack, saying that the ship allegedly headed toward [Eilat](66162.md), Israel. They also released a video showing a cargo vessel sinking after the attack.[^cite_note-cnn710-19] They claimed having taken some mariners to an undisclosed location,[^cite_note-cnn710-19][^cite_note-AP713-22] but offered no evidence.[^cite_note-AP713-22] Meanwhile, [United States](3434750.md) authorities in Yemen accused the rebels of kidnapping the crew members,[^cite_note-AP713-22] and demanded their immediate release.[^cite_note-cnn710-19][^cite_note-27]

On 13 July, two private security firms running the search for the missing individuals announced that the vessel's owner called off the operations.[^cite_note-AP713-22][^cite_note-R715-26] At that time, 11 others remained unaccounted for;[^cite_note-AP713-22] as Cosmoship, the vessel's manager based in [Greece](12108.md), considered the remaining 15 (then included five deaths as claimed by maritime security sources) as missing.[^cite_note-R715-26]

### Detention and release of remaining mariners

On 17 July, [Russia](25391.md)'s state-run [RIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIA_Novosti) news agency reported that a Russian crew member had been picked up by the [Yemeni Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Navy).[^cite_note-28] Later identified as an electrician,[^cite_note-AFP729-29] he had been hospitalized in [Sana'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana'a) after being badly injured,[^cite_note-T1204-30] losing his leg onboard the ship.[^cite_note-AFP729-29]

On 28 July, the Houthis released a [propaganda video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_through_media), which was posted later in [X](9988187.md),[^cite_note-31] allegedly showing ten crew members being held.[^cite_note-jp729-32] Among them are nine Filipinos and a Russian.[^cite_note-AFP729-29] The video claimed that the rebels rescued the crew, pulling them from the sea,[^cite_note-ti728-33] as reportedly detailed by the latter.[^cite_note-jp729-32] According to the rebels, eleven crew members were rescued, including the two injured who were treated, and a body was recovered from the ship.[^cite_note-ti728-33] The Philippine [Department of Foreign Affairs](4718612.md) (DFA) later said that a Filipino and a foreign personnel were injured, and these Filipino sailors were moved to rebel-controlled Sana'a.[^cite_note-34]

The DFA and the [Omani Foreign Ministry](36308453.md) had been mediating in the release of seafarers, which was discussed twice—in a bilateral meeting in July, and in a phone call in November.[^cite_note-mb1202-35] On 3 December, a day after the announcement by DFA,[^cite_note-mb1202-35] all 11 captive seafarers—nine Filipinos, one Indian,[^cite_note-36] and one Russian[^cite_note-T1204-30]—were freed in Sana'a[^cite_note-xn1204-17] and were transported to [Muscat](57053.md), [Oman](22316.md), on an [Omani Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force_of_Oman) aircraft.[^cite_note-37] AFP reported that their release, done in exchange for the repatriation of 35 Yemenis stranded abroad, was part of an agreement between the governments [of Yemen](78626.md) and [of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Oman) which was the result of "pressure" from the former.[^cite_note-38]

## Reactions

The [United States Department of State](31975.md) condemned the attacks on both the *Magic Seas* and *Eternity C* that "demonstrate the ongoing threat that \[the Houthis] pose to freedom of navigation and to regional \[...] security."[^cite_note-2bbc-11]

[Arsenio Dominguez](75445890.md), Secretary-General of the [International Maritime Organization](14986.md), in an address to the council during a convention in [London](17867.md), likewise condemned the renewed assaults on both merchant ships and called for intensified diplomatic efforts.[^cite_note-39]
