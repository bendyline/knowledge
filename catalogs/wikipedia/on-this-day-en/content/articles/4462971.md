The **Parliament of Romania** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Parlamentul României*) is the national [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) legislature of [Romania](25445.md), consisting of the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Romania\)) (*Camera Deputaților*) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) (*Senat*). It meets at the [Palace of the Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Parliament) in [Bucharest](36877.md), the capital.

Prior to the modification of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Romania) in 2003, the two houses had identical attributes. A text of a law had to be approved by both houses. If the text differed, a special commission ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *comisie de mediere*) was formed by deputies and senators, that "negotiated" between the two houses the form of the future law. The report of this commission had to be approved in a joint session of the Parliament.

After the 2003 [referendum](51582.md), a law still has to be approved by both houses, but each house has designated matters it gets to deliberate before the other, in capacity of "deciding chamber" ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *cameră decizională*). If that first chamber adopts a law proposal (relating to its competences), it is passed on to the other one, which can approve or reject. If it makes amendments, the bill is sent back to the deciding chamber, the decision of which is final.

In 2009, a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Romanian_parliamentary_reform_referendum) was held to consult the population about turning the parliament into a unicameral body and reducing the number of representatives to 300. Although the referendum passed, the results are not binding, a referendum explicitly mentioning the modification of the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Romania) being required to achieve this.[^cite_note-7]

## History

The parliamentary history of [Romania](25445.md) starts in May 1831 in [Wallachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachia), where a constitutional document was adopted, more specifically [Regulamentul Organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulamentul_Organic) ("The Organic Statute" or "The Organic Regulation"); less than a year later, in January 1832, this same statute was implemented in [Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia) as well. The organic regulation laid the foundations for the parliamentary institution in the [Romanian Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Principalities_of_Moldavia_and_Wallachia).

The Paris Convention of 19 August 1858 and, especially, *Statutul Dezvoltător* ("The Expanding Statute") of that convention (which introduced a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) parliament, by founding *Corpul Ponderator*, later renamed *Senat*), adopted on the initiative of prince (*Domnitor*) [Alexandru Ioan Cuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza), by means of a [plebiscite](51582.md) (i.e. referendum) in 1864, perfected and enlarged the principle of national representation. Under the political regime established by the Paris Convention, the legislative power faced an obvious process of modernization, and the legislative power as National Representation, which operated in accordance with the organization and operation mode of parliaments in [Western Europe](33800.md) at that time.[^cite_note-8]

The historical process of formation of the Parliament of Romania in the modern age strongly boosted the affirmation of national [sovereignty](28791.md), subsequently leading to the [Union of the two Romanian Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Principalities_of_Moldavia_and_Wallachia) (i.e. Wallachia and Moldavia) in 1859. Under the dome of the Romanian Parliament, on 9 May 1877, the Declaration of Independence of Romania was read, and, in 1920, the documents of union with [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania) and [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia) under the [Treaty of Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon) (4 June 1920) were read, the formal beginning of [Greater Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Romania), the enlarged [interwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period) [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) (which also included the entire [historical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_regions_of_Romania) of [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina) as per the [Treaty of Saint-German-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_\(1919\)) from 1919).

In February 1938, amid the rather chaotic European political atmosphere which eventually led to [World War II](32927.md), King [Carol II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania), who always tended to favour his own personal rule over [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system), imposed a rule of [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) monarchy. Under the royal dictatorship, the parliament became merely a decorative body, deprived of its main attributes.

Carol II abdicated in September 1940, and the succeeding [National Legionary State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Legionary_State) suspended the parliament. The National Legionary State as such lasted less than five months, but it was succeeded by [Ion Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Antonescu)'s military dictatorship, and the parliament still remained suspended. After 23 August 1944, under the pressure of [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and other communist forces, the parliament was re-organized as a [single legislative body](204304.md), the [Assembly of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Romania), changed under the 1948 constitution, into the [Great National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_National_Assembly_\(Romania\)), a merely formal body, totally subordinate to the power of the [Romanian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Communist_Party) (PCR).

The [Romanian Revolution of December 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_revolution) opened the road for [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) to restore authentic [pluralistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluralism_\(political_philosophy\)) [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), respecting [human rights](13831.md), and observing the [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) and the rulers' responsibility before representative bodies. Thanks to the documents issued by the [provisional revolutionary power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Council_of_National_Unity), Romania returned to a bicameral parliamentary system, represented politically as such both by the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate. All these stipulations can be found in the country's new [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Romania), approved by [referendum](51582.md) in 1991.

During more than a decade of [post-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-communism) transition, the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Romania\)) and [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) debated and adopted numerous laws and regulations aimed at reforming the entire society on a democratic basis, guaranteeing respect of fundamental human rights, promoting reform and [privatization](24661.md), consolidating [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) economic institutions and those of a state ruled by law, which led to Romania's integration into such notable international institutions as the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](21133.md) (NATO) in 2004 and the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) three years later in 2007.

The [1866](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1866_Constitution_of_Romania), [1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Constitution_of_Romania), and [1938 Constitution of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Constitution_of_Romania) state that the assembly presidents are elected at the beginning of each session. This tradition was kept in the early years of the People's Republic. In modern times, both presidents of the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Romania) and of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Senate_of_Romania) are elected for the entire duration of that house's term. Under special circumstances the presidents of the houses can be revoked.

## Functioning

Prior to the modifications of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Romania) in 2003, the two houses had identical attributes. A text of a law had to be approved by both houses. If the text differed, a special commission (*comisie de mediere*) was formed by deputies and senators, that "negotiated" between the two houses the form of the future law. The report of this commission had to be approved in a joint session of the Parliament. This [French procedure](1009776.md) proved to be extremely long and inefficient with respect to the expectations of the Romanians towards democracy.

After the 2003 [referendum](51582.md), a law still has to be approved by both houses, but in some matters one is "superior" to the other, being called "decision chamber" (*"cameră decizională"*). This eliminates the process of "negotiation" between the two houses, and keeps the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) as the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) and the [Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Romania\)) as the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house).
