There have been eight [heads of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) of the [Central African Republic](5478.md) and the [Central African Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Empire) since independence from [France](5843419.md) on 13 August 1960. This list includes not only those persons who were sworn into office as President of the Central African Republic but also those who served as *de facto* heads of state.

[Jean-Bédel Bokassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-B%C3%A9del_Bokassa) served as a *de facto* head of state (and also reigned as [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Central_Africa) from 1976 to 1979), while [David Dacko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dacko) (who served as *de facto* head of state from 1979 to 1981), [André Kolingba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Kolingba), [Ange-Félix Patassé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ange-F%C3%A9lix_Patass%C3%A9), and [François Bozizé](213625.md) were elected into office at some point during their tenure. To date, Kolingba is the only former head of state of the Central African Republic to voluntarily step down from the office through a democratic process, following the [1993 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Central_African_general_election).

The current president of the Central African Republic is [Faustin-Archange Touadéra](15370659.md), since 30 March 2016.[^cite_note-2] The president is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Armed_Forces).

## Succession

Before the adoption of the [2023 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Central_African_Republic), the [president of the National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_National_Assembly_of_the_Central_African_Republic) was the constitutional successor of the president in the event of a vacancy.[^cite_note-3]

## Term

Before the adoption of the [2023 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Central_African_Republic),[^cite_note-4] there was a two-term limit for the president in the [Constitution of the Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Central_African_Republic). The term limit was not met by any president.[^cite_note-5] The constitution of 2023 removed term-limits and extended the presidential term from five years to seven years.

## Footnotes

- [**<sup>A</sup>**](#ref_Note1A)  Dacko became the official President of the Central African Republic after defeating [Abel Goumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Goumba) in an internal power struggle. Dacko had support from the [French government](3398091.md).

- [**<sup>B</sup>**](#ref_Note1B)  Bokassa seized power by staging a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Sylvestre_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) from 31 December 1965 until 1 January 1966. Bokassa forced Dacko to officially resign from the presidency at 03\:20 [WAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa_Time) (02\:20 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)) on 1 January.

  [^cite_note-Titleyp28-9]

- [**<sup>C</sup>**](#ref_Note1C)  Bokassa staged a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coup) against the Dacko government on 31 December 1965 – 1 January 1966. After becoming president, Bokassa took control of [MESAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MESAN) and imposed one-party rule under MESAN.

- [**<sup>D</sup>**](#ref_Note1D)  Bokassa, then-[president for life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_for_life) of the Central African Republic, instituted a new constitution at the session of the MESAN congress and declared the republic a monarchy, the [Central African Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Empire) (CAE). Bokassa became the emperor of the CAE as "Bokassa I".

  [^cite_note-Kalckpxxxiv-10]

- [**<sup>E</sup>**](#ref_Note1E)  By 1979, French support for Bokassa had all but eroded after the government's brutal suppression of rioting in [Bangui](56617.md) and [massacre of schoolchildren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Ngaragba_Prison_massacre) who had protested against wearing the expensive, government-required [school uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_uniform). Dacko, who was Bokassa's personal adviser at the time, managed to leave for Paris where the French convinced him to cooperate in a coup to remove Bokassa from power and restore him to the presidency. The French successfully executed [Operation Caban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Caban) on 20–21 September 1979 and installed Dacko as president.

  [^cite_note-21]

  [^cite_note-22]

- [**<sup>F</sup>**](#ref_Note1F)  General Kolingba (who was also the armed forces [chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff)) overthrew Dacko from the presidency in a bloodless coup.

  [^cite_note-Kalckp113-16]

- [**<sup>G</sup>**](#ref_Note1G)  On 21 September 1985, Kolingba dissolved the Military Committee for National Recovery,

  [^cite_note-23]

  &#x20;and created the positions of head of state and president.

  [^cite_note-24]

- [**<sup>H</sup>**](#ref_Note1H)  A constitution was adopted by a referendum on 21 November 1986 and Kolingba was elected to a six-year term in office.

  [^cite_note-Kalckp199-11]

  [^cite_note-Kalckp113-16]

- [**<sup>I</sup>**](#ref_Note1I)  The country held a multiparty presidential election on 22 August and 19 September 1993. Patassé was the candidate from the [Movement for the Liberation of the Central African People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Central_African_People) party and ran on the [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_platform) that he would pay the previously withheld salaries to soldiers and civil servants.

  [^cite_note-25]

  &#x20;Patassé defeated Dacko, Kolingba, Bozizé and [Abel Goumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Goumba) to win the election.

  [^cite_note-26]

- [**<sup>J</sup>**](#ref_Note1J)  Bozizé's second coup attempt was successful; he seized power in [Bangui](56617.md) on 15 March 2003.

  [^cite_note-27]

- [**<sup>K</sup>**](#ref_Note1K)  Djotodia ousted Bozizé in the [2012–13 conflict](38024441.md#Toppling_Boziz%C3%A9_\(2012%E2%80%932013\)); he seized power in [Bangui](56617.md) on 24 March 2013.

  [^cite_note-28]
