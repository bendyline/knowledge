**Kawasaki**[^cite_note-1], officially **Kawasaki City**[^cite_note-2], is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Japan) in [Kanagawa Prefecture](61757.md), Japan, one of the main cities of the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md) and [Keihin Industrial Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihin_Industrial_Area). It is the second most populated city in [Kanagawa Prefecture](61757.md) after [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama), and the [eighth most populated city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Japan) in Japan (including the Tokyo Metropolitan Area).[^cite_note-Archived_copy-3]

As of October 1, 2017, the city has an estimated population of 1,503,690, with 716,470 [households](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household),[^cite_note-Archived_copy-3] and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 10,000 persons per km<sup>2</sup>. Kawasaki is the only city in Japan with more than one million inhabitants that is not a prefectural capital. The total area is 142.70 km<sup>2</sup> (55.10 sq mi).

## History

### Prehistoric and ancient era

Archaeological evidence from the Japanese [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) and [Jōmon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) can only be found in the northwest [Tama Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_Hills). The course of the Tama and the coast of the Bay of Tokyo have also changed in historical times, so that large parts of the urban area are geologically young.

### Classical era

#### Nara period to the Sengoku period

With the introduction of the [Ritsuryō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritsury%C5%8D) legal system, the area came to the [Musashi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musashi_Province) in the 7th century. In the [Nara period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_period), the center of the Tachibana district was probably in the area of today's Takatsu district. Since the [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period), the domain of the Inage clan has expanded here. Around the [Heiken-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiken-ji) Buddhist temple (better known as Kawasaki-Daishi), founded in 1128, a monzen-machi, a busy district for the supply of pilgrims, soon emerged. Between the [Kamakura period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_period) and [Sengoku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period), smaller feudal lords ruled the area until it finally came under the control of the [Later Hōjō clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_H%C5%8Dj%C5%8D_clan).

### Early modern

In 1611, Koizumi Jidayū had Nikaryō Yōsui built, a canal system on the right bank of the Tama for irrigating the fields, which in some cases still runs through the densely built-up city. On the long-distance [Kaidō roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaid%C5%8D) [Tōkaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_\(road\)) and Nakaharakaidō built by [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period)-[Bakufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakufu), stations were built in the area of what would later become Kawasaki, which increased its importance. The Kawasaki station (Kawasaki-juku, near today's Kawasaki station) on the Tōkaidō was not officially recognized until 1623 as the last of the 53 Tōkaidō stations. The Bakufu let the bridges over the Tama collapse and there were ferry connections to nearby Edo in several places in today's Kawasaki, which laid the foundation for the development of the city.

### Modern

The rapid [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) of the area, which continues to this day, began in the [Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era) and [Taishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taish%C5%8D) eras. In 1872, [Kawasaki Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_Station) was established on the [Tōkaidō Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_Main_Line) which was Japan's first railway line.[^cite_note-4] In 1889, the city (machi) Kawasaki in the district (gun) Tachibana was created according to the Japanese municipal system introduced the year before. In 1912 the border between [Kanagawa](61757.md) and [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Prefecture) prefectures was established as the [Tama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_River). On July 1, 1924, the independent city (shi-) of Kawasaki with 48,394 inhabitants was formed through a merger with the city of Daishi (formerly Daishigawara) and the village of Miyuki.[^cite_note-shi_profile-5]

People from the Korean peninsula were made to work in the industrial sector in the city,[^cite_note-総務省｜一般戦災死没者の追悼｜川崎市における戦災の状況（神奈川県）-6] working on railways construction, or rebuilding roads damaged by U.S. bombings towards the end of WWII.[^cite_note-7] People from Okinawa were also coming to the city, and in 1924, the oldest Okinawans Association in Japan was founded in Kawasaki.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

#### World War II

As part of [World War II](32927.md), the city was bombed three times by the [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) (USAAF) between April 1945 and July 1945. The most serious attack was an area bombing with [Napalm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napalm) bombs on April 15, 1945. The attacks destroyed around 35% of the urban area and claimed 1,520 dead and 8,759 injured. The attacks burned down 9.3 km<sup>2</sup> of the city (see [Bombing of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Tokyo)). Kawasaki became a target of the first mainland bombing by the US military in 1942, followed by multiple bombings, partly due to the city's heavy and chemical industrial complex supplying the war efforts in Asia and the Pacific.[^cite_note-総務省｜一般戦災死没者の追悼｜川崎市における戦災の状況（神奈川県）-6]

### Contemporary period

#### Shōwa era (1945–1989)

On April 15, 1945, large parts of the area around the train station and the industrial area at the port were destroyed by air raids. Since the 1950s, residential areas for commuters have been created in the northeastern part of the city, which are connected directly to the centers of Tokyo by new railway lines. On April 1, 1972, Kawasaki became a decree-designated city ([seirei shitei toshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_designated_by_government_ordinance_of_Japan)) with 5 districts. In 1973[^cite_note-shi_profile-5] the population exceeded the million mark. In 1982 the new districts of Miyamae and Asao were created by splitting off from the districts of Takatsu and Tama. In the course of [deindustrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialization), industrial areas have recently been increasingly converted into residential areas (mostly [Multi-family residential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-family_residential)), so that a further increase in population density can be expected.

### Location

Kawasaki is located on the right bank of the [Tama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_River), which flows into the Tokyo Bay here. The city lies like a narrow band between [Tokyo](30057.md) in the northeast and [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) in the southwest. The city connects the two major cities and is part of the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md), the largest and most densely populated urban area in the world.

The eastern area along the coast of [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay) is a densely populated industrial zone, part of the [Keihin Industrial Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihin_region). In contrast, the western districts in the [Tama Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_Hills) consist largely of residential areas for commuters in the [Tokyo](30057.md) / [Yokohama region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama).

### Adjacent cities and towns

In the northeast, Kawasaki borders the [Special wards of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo) (starting at Tokyo Bay) [Ōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cta,_Tokyo) and [Setagaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setagaya), in the northwest the cities (-shi) belonging to [Tokyo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Prefecture) (-shi) [Komae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komae,_Tokyo), [Chofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%8Dfu,_Tokyo), [Machida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machida,_Tokyo), [Inagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagi), [Tama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama,_Tokyo) enclose the place. The opposite southwest side is entirely occupied by the districts of [Tsurumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsurumi-ku,_Yokohama), [Kōhoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dhoku-ku,_Yokohama), [Tsuzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuzuki-ku,_Yokohama) and [Aoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoba-ku,_Yokohama) in the city of Yokohama. With the completion of the [Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay_Aqua-Line), the city of [Kisarazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisarazu), located on the opposite side of the Tokyo Bay in [Chiba Prefecture](179796.md), also became a neighbor in December 1997.

### Bodies of water

Two rivers cross the urban area. The Tama unites with the tributaries Misawa, Yamashita, Gotanda, Nikaryō main river and Hirase; Katahira, Asao, Shimpukuji, Arima, E, Shibu and Yagami flow into the Tsurumi.

The land on the coast of the city is crossed by a network of [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canals) (Tama Canal, Suehiro Canal, Chidori Canal, Yakō Canal, Daishi Canal, Mizue Canal, Shiohama Canal, Iriesaki Canal, Asano Canal, Ikegami Canal, Minami-Watarida Canal, Tanabe Canal, Shiraishi Canal and the Sakai Canal). In addition, the historic Nikaryō Yōsui canal still exists in the hinterland.

Kawasaki is governed by Mayor Norihiko Fukuda, an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_\(politician\)) elected on 27 October 2013.[^cite_note-12] The city assembly has 63 elected members. Mayor Fukuda was re-elected to a second term in office on 22 October 2017 with support from [LDP](164231.md) and Kōmeitō against former municipal MP Akiko Yoshizawa and [JCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Communist_Party)-supported former primary school teacher Hirokazu Ichiko.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The 60-member city parliament of Kawasaki was re-elected in the unified elections in April 2023. The LDP won 17 seats and is the largest party in the assembly.[^cite_note-15]

Kawasaki was in June 2008 the second Japanese "government-designated city" (seirei shitei toshi) after [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima), which allowed foreigners to participate in municipal referendums.[^cite_note-16]

In the 105-member prefectural parliament of Kanagawa, the seven districts of Kawasaki serve as constituencies, electing 18 deputies in total.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

For the [House of Representatives (Japan)](217778.md), Kawasaki comprises the constituencies [Kanagawa 9th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa_9th_district) (Tama and Asao wards), [10th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa_10th_district) (Kawasaki and Saiwai wards), [18th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa_18th_district) (Nakahara and Takatsu wards) and [19th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa_19th_district) (Miyamae ward, with Tsuzuki ward, [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama)), following changes since the last general election in 2021.[^cite_note-19] In the 2021 election, the three seats went unchanged to Liberal Democrats [Kazunori Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazunori_Tanaka) and [Daishirō Yamagiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daishiro_Yamagiwa), and ex-Democrat [Hirofumi Ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirofumi_Ryu) of the Constitutional Democratic Party.[^cite_note-20]

## Economy



Keihin industrial Zone (Kawasaki)

Kawasaki, particularly its eastern portion, has several factories and development bases of heavy industry (e.g., [JFE Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFE_Group), [ENEOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Oil_Corporation)) and high technology (e.g., [Fujitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujitsu), [NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEC_Corporation), [Toshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiba), [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell) Japan and [Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Corporation)[^cite_note-24]). Many of these factories are built on reclaimed land near [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay), taking advantage of access to the sea as well as regional rail and highway networks and the nearby [Haneda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport).

The areas around [Kawasaki Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_Station), [Musashi-Kosugi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musashi-Kosugi_Station), [Mizonokuchi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizonokuchi_Station) and [Shin-Yurigaoka Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin-Yurigaoka_Station) are major commercial districts, housing numerous corporate offices and shopping centers.

[Fujitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujitsu)'s Main Branch is located in [Nakahara-ku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakahara-ku,_Kawasaki).[^cite_note-25] It was formerly Fujitsu's headquarters.[^cite_note-26]

### Highways



Developed national road 466 to Tokyo and Yokohama in Miyamae-ku



[Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay_Aqua-Line) is an expressway across Tokyo Bay.

Expressway

- [Tōmei Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dmei_Expressway) is a north-south expressway running from Tokyo to [Nagoya](61720.md) and in central area. Tōmei-Kawasaki Interchange is served from Kawasaki.

- [Daisan Keihin Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisan_Keihin_Road) is a north-south expressway running from Tokyo to [Hodogaya-ku, Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodogaya-ku,_Yokohama) and in central area. Keihin-Kawasaki Interchange is served from Kawasaki.

- Shuto Expressway Route K1 (Yokohane Route) is a north-south expressway running from [Shuto Expressway Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Route) to Shuto Expressway Route K3 (Kariba Route) and in southern area. Daishi Interchange, Hama-Kawasaki Interchange, and Asada Interchange are served from Kawasaki.

- [Bayshore Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayshore_Route) is a north-south expressway running from [Kanazawa-ku, Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanazawa-ku,_Yokohama) to [Ichikawa, Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichikawa,_Chiba) and in southern area. Ukishima Interchange and Higashi-Ōgishima Interchange are served from Kawasaki.

- Shuto Expressway Route K6 (Kawasaki Route) is an expressway in southern area. Daishi Interchange, Tonomachi Interchange, and Ukishima Interchange are served from Kawasaki.

- [Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay_Aqua-Line) is an expressway across Tokyo Bay from [Kawasaki-ku, Kawasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki-ku,_Kawasaki) to [Kisarazu, Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisarazu,_Chiba). Ukishima Interchange is served from Kawasaki.

National Route

- [National Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_1) and [15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_15) are north-south highways running in southern area. Due to elongated territory from east to west, these highways run short length in Kawasaki.

- [Japan National Route 246](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_246) is a north-south highways running in central area. It also runs short length in Kawasaki.

- [Japan National Route 132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_132) is short highway running in southern area. It bounds National Route 15 and port of kawasaki.

- [Japan National Route 357](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_357) is an industrial highway in southern area. It runs only in Higashi-Ōgishima Island in Kawasaki.

- [Japan National Route 409](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_409) is a highway running from [Takatsu-ku, Kawasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takatsu-ku,_Kawasaki) to [Narita, Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita,_Chiba). It bounds central area and downtown area in Kawasaki.
