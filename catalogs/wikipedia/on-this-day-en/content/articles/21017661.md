# Baloch nationalism



Flag used by most [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) nationalists and separatists

**Baloch nationalism** ([Balochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_language): بلۏچی راجدۏستی, romanized: *Baloči ràjdòsti*) is an ideology that asserts that the [Baloch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people), an [Iranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranic) [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) native to [Iran](14653.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md), should form a distinct [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation).

## Baloch ethnicity and nationalism

The [Baloch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) are an [Iranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranic) ethnic group native to southeastern [Iran](14653.md), southwestern [Pakistan](23235.md) and southern [Afghanistan](737.md).

The Baloch nationalist movement's demands have ranged from greater cultural, economic and political rights, to political autonomy, to outright [secession](144732.md) and the creation of an independent state of Balochistan. The movement is [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism_in_Balochistan) and was originally inspired by [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) liberation movements.[^cite_note-1]

The movement claims to receive considerable support from the [Baloch diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_diaspora) in [Oman](22316.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (UAE), [Sweden](5058739.md), [Norway](21241.md), the [United States](3434750.md), [Canada](5042916.md), and elsewhere. Pakistan has repeatedly made claims that the Baloch nationalists have received funding from [India](14533.md),[^cite_note-2] although these have been denied by India.[^cite_note-3] Similarly, Afghanistan has acknowledged providing covert support to the Baloch nationalist militants. In the 1960s and 1970s, the [Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Afghanistan_\(1973%E2%80%931978\)) provided sanctuary to Baloch militants. The Republic of Afghanistan had established training camps in [Kandahar](17260.md) to train Baloch militants and also to provide arms and ammunition.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The [Ba'athist Iraqi](26215470.md) government supported Baloch groups in Iran but halted such actions once the [Shah](13877.md) of [Imperial Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran) stopped supporting [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan) groups in [Iraq](35423251.md).[^cite_note-6]

## History of modern Baloch nationalism



Leaders of the Baloch nationalist movement in the 1970s from left to right: [Gul Khan Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gul_Khan_Nasir), [Ataullah Mengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ataullah_Mengal), and [Ghaus Bakhsh Bizenjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaus_Bakhsh_Bizenjo).

Baloch nationalism in its modern form began in the form of the *[Anjuman-i Ittihad-i Balochan o Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjuman-i_Ittihad-i_Balochan_o_Balochistan)* (AIB) based in [Mastung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastung,_Pakistan) in 1929, led by [Yousaf Aziz Magsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousaf_Aziz_Magsi), [Abdul Aziz Kurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Aziz_Kurd), and others.[^cite_note-Dawn-7] In November 1929, Yousaf Aziz Magsi published an article stating the aims of the group, namely:

1. Unification and independence of [Balochistan](8431743.md);

2. A democratic, socialist system guided by Islamic universalism;

3. Abolition of the [*sardari*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar)-[*jirga*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga) system;

4. Free, compulsory education for the Baloch, and equality for Baloch women;

5. Promotion of [Baloch culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_Culture).

   [^cite_note-breseeg-8]

   [^cite_note-Dawn-7]

Simultaneously with the formation of the *Anjuman*, Baloch intellectuals in [Karachi](17123.md) formed a nationalist organisation, called the Baloch League.[^cite_note-breseeg-8]

&#x20;In February 1937, the Anjuman reorganised and became the [Kalat State National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalat_State_National_Party) (KSNP), carrying on the *Anjuman*'s political agenda of an independent united state of Balochistan. They demanded the independence of the [Khanate of Kalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kalat), which was later incorporated into Pakistan in 1955.[^cite_note-breseeg-8] The party was dominated by more secular-minded, anti-imperialist and populist elements, such as [Ghaus Bakhsh Bizenjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaus_Bakhsh_Bizenjo), [Mir Gul Khan Naseer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Gul_Khan_Naseer) and Abdul Aziz Kurd. When parliamentary elections were held in the state of Kalat, the party was the largest winner with a considerable majority.[^cite_note-breseeg-8]



Flag of [Balochistan](8431743.md), which was introduced and used by [Balochistan Liberation Army](6010135.md) (BLA) as the national flag of Balochistan. Currently it is one of the popular symbols of the separatist movement in the region.

In 2017, the World Baloch Organisation (WBO) placed advertisements on taxis in [London](17867.md) saying #*FreeBalochistan* along with slogans such as "stop [enforced disappearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enforced_disappearances_in_Pakistan#Balochistan)" and "save the Baloch people". These were initially allowed but later denied permission by [Transport for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_London) (TfL). The World Baloch Organisation (WBO) claimed that this was a result of pressure from the [Pakistani government](1897833.md) after the [British High Commissioner in Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commission_of_the_United_Kingdom,_Islamabad) was summoned to appear before the [Pakistani Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(Pakistan\)).[^cite_note-9]

## Surveys

A survey in 2009 by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) found that 58% of respondents in Balochistan chose "Pakistani" as their primary mode of identification, 32% chose their ethnicity and 10% chose both equally.[^cite_note-PEW-10]

In 2012, [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup,_Inc.) conducted a survey for the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s [Department for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_International_Development) (DFID) that revealed that only 37% of Baloch were in favour of [independence](62450.md). Amongst [Balochistan's](4687.md) [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) population support for independence was even lower at 12%. 67% of Balochistan's population favoured greater [provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_province) [autonomy](191145.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]
