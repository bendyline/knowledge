The **RS-28 Sarmat** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): РС-28 Сармат,[^cite_note-RS28-3] named after the [Sarmatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatians);[^cite_note-4] [NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **SS-29**[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026222-5] or **SS-X-30**[^cite_note-crsreports_SS-X-30-6]), often colloquially referred to as **Satan II** by media outlets, is a three-stage Russian [silo-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_launch_facility), [liquid-fueled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-propellant_rocket), [HGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_glide_vehicle)-capable and [FOBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_Orbital_Bombardment_System)-capable [super-heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_ICBM) [intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBM) produced by the [Makeyev Rocket Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makeyev_Rocket_Design_Bureau).[^cite_note-RS28-3][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] It is intended to replace the Soviet [R-36M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-36_\(missile\)#R-36M) ICBM in Russia's [arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal).[^cite_note-redstaroboima-10] It was one of six new Russian strategic weapons unveiled by Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) on 1 March 2018.[^cite_note-11]

Development began in 2011 with an expected entry into service of 2018-2020. The program experienced numerous delays and test failures. The first test flight occurred on 20 April 2022. A catastrophic test failure in September 2024 destroyed the [Plesetsk Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesetsk_Cosmodrome) test site and subsequent test launches were performed at the [Yasny/Dombarovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dombarovsky_\(air_base\)) operational base. As of 2026, the missile has not yet entered service.

## Development

The [Makeyev Rocket Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makeyev_Rocket_Design_Bureau) was contracted to develop the RS-28 in June 2011.[^cite_note-12]

The program experienced administrative, technical and manufacturing delays, and numerous test failures. The missile was originally expected to enter service in 2018[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026232-13][^cite_note-slipping-14]-2020; by February 2014, the target was 2020.[^cite_note-15] Delivery of the prototype from [KrasMash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoyarsk_Machine-Building_Plant) was expected in June 2015. The program was "accelerated" due to the [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md), but in June 2015 [TASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TASS) reported that the prototype was only 60% complete and delivery was rescheduled for September or October; the cause was identified as late delivery of subcomponents by contractors.[^cite_note-slipping-14] In September, delivery of the prototype was expected by the end of 2015 with launch tests commencing in 2016.[^cite_note-16] The PDU-99 first-stage engine, designed by [NPO Energomash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPO_Energomash) and manufactured by [Proton-PM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton-PM), was tested in August 2016; reportedly the test firings were delayed by development problems.[^cite_note-17]

[Silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_launch_facility) launch tests were originally expected in 2015.[^cite_note-18] In March 2017, TASS reported the launch tests at [Plesetsk Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesetsk_Cosmodrome) were delayed until later in the year because the test stands were not ready; this was the third launch test delay.[^cite_note-delayedtests-19] The first successful launch test occurred in December 2017, with the missile flying a few dozen kilometers within the test range.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]



[NASA](18426568.md) [FIRMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Information_for_Resource_Management_System) imagery of the 20 and 21 September 2024 fire at Plesetsk with first detection at 2024-09-20 23\:52\:00 ([UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC))

The test flight record has been described as "abysmal".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026232-13] The first successful flight test occurred at Plesetsk on 20 April 2022.[^cite_note-iiss_wright_2022-06-16-22] According to the [United States](3434750.md), a February 2023 test failed[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026232-13][^cite_note-23] On 20 September 2024,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaireMarcuz20263-24] a test failed and caused a 60 metres (200 ft) diameter crater. The missile may have exploded in the silo, possibly caused by the rocket crashing into the silo after a rocket motor failure, or the liquid fuel being ignited during defuelling if the test was cancelled.[^cite_note-iiss_wright_2024-09-26-25][^cite_note-26] The damage left Plesetsk without a suitable silo for RS-28 testing.[^cite_note-iiss_wright_2024-09-26-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaireMarcuz20263-24] Testing likely moved to the [Yasny/Dombarovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dombarovsky_\(air_base\)) base operated by [Strategic Rocket Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Rocket_Forces)' (SRF) 13th Division. A silo previously used for [Dnepr rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnepr_\(rocket\)) test launches was reactivated and upgraded after the September 2024 failure for the RS-28.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaireMarcuz20263–5-27] On 28 November 2025, a RS-28 test launch from the upgraded silo failed shortly after rocket motor ignition and the missile crashed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaireMarcuz20263-24] Yasny/Dombarovsky performed a successful test launch on 12 May 2026.[^cite_note-iiss_gwadera_2026-05-19-28]

[Dallas Analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Analytics) obtained internal Russian documents concerning a delayed shipment of missile parts from [ZlatMash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatoust_Machine-Building_Plant) to [Uzhur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhur) in 2022. The delays were caused by the [Russian Ministry of Defence](7875884.md) (MoD) not having finalized supplier [contracts](19280537.md), the SRF being unable to provide adaquate transport, and incomplete documentation. Time saving measures to compensate for the delays led to violations of [process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process), which may have been a contributing factor to the [2024 arrest of Vladimir Verteletsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purges_in_the_Russian_Ministry_of_Defense_in_2024), head of the MOD department responsible for the contracts. Price negotiations with suppliers were ongoing as late as mid-2025.[^cite_note-29]

On 16 August 2022, a state contract was signed for the manufacture and supply of the Sarmat strategic missile system.

According to the [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](100091.md), the political pressure from the delays caused Russian officials to release confusing and contradictory reports of progress and repeated claims that the missile was close to entering service.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026232-13] Examples include the announcement in September 2023 by [Yury Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Borisov), Director General of [Roscosmos](406573.md), that the missile had entered service, followed by the announcement by [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), the [President of Russia](239646.md), that the missile was not in mass production and had yet to be delivered to the military.[^cite_note-30]

## Deployment

By 2016, the RS-28 was part of an effort to replace Soviet ICBMs by 2022.[^cite_note-31] By 2026, the RS-28 was expected to replace the [RS-20V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-36_\(missile\)), [UR-100N UTTKh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UR-100N), and [Avangard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avangard_\(hypersonic_glide_vehicle\))-equipped UR-100Ns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026230–232-32]

In December 2019, a [National Defense Management Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Management_Center) exhibition reported that 20 regiments were expected to be rearmed with the RS-28 from 2020 to 2027.[^cite_note-ndmc-33]

The RS-20V formally began retiring in 2021. The 302nd Missile Regiment at Uzhur was the first to begin conversion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026230-34] By March 2026, the regiment was disarmed and its infrastructure was being upgraded to handle the RS-28.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle2026233-35]

According to Russian state media sources, RS-28's launch sites were to be equipped with the "*Mozyr*" active protection system, which is claimed to negate a potential adversary's [first strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-emptive_nuclear_strike) advantage by discharging a cloud of metal arrows or balls kinetically that allegedly would destroy incoming bombs, cruise missiles and ICBM warheads at altitudes of up to 6 km (3.7 mi); however development of the Mozyr system ceased in 1991.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

## Design

The Sarmat is a three-stage, liquid-fueled missile with a range of 18,000 kilometres (11,000 mi) and a launch weight of 208.1 tonnes (204.8 long tons; 229.4 short tons). The missile is 35.3 metres (116 ft) long and 3 metres (9.8 ft) in diameter. Designated a "heavy" ICBM, the Sarmat can load a wide variety of warhead options. According to Russian media, Sarmat is capable of carrying 10 tonnes of payload,[^cite_note-MissileThreat2017-41] of ten 750 [kiloton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_equivalent#Kiloton_and_megaton),[^cite_note-42] 15 or 16 lighter [MIRV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIRV) [warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead),[^cite_note-43] or [Avangard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avangard_\(hypersonic_glide_vehicle\)) [hypersonic glide vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_glide_vehicle) (HGVs),[^cite_note-iiss_gwadera_2026-05-19-28][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristensenKordaJohnsKnight-Boyle20262303-44] or a combination of warheads and several [countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetration_aid) against [anti-ballistic missile](1791.md) systems.[^cite_note-45] According to MoD, the missile is a response to the U.S. [Prompt Global Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_Global_Strike) system.[^cite_note-invuln-46][^cite_note-47]

According to Russia, the RS-28 has a range of over 35,000 km (22,000 mi) using a [sub-orbital](455295.md) trajectory.[^cite_note-iiss_gwadera_2026-05-19-28]

According to Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), Sarmat has a short [boost phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost_phase), which shortens the interval when it can be tracked by [satellites](27683.md) with [infrared sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermographic_camera), such as the U.S. [Space-Based Infrared System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-Based_Infrared_System), allegedly making it more difficult to [intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost-phase_intercept).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-auto-49][^cite_note-50] Sarmat provides Russia with a [Fractional Orbital Bombardment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_Orbital_Bombardment_System) (FOBS) capability that can fly a trajectory over the [South Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole) to targets in the United States, which has the advantage of being able to avoid missile defense systems in the northern United States.[^cite_note-auto-49]
