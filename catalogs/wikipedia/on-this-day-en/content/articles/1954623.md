**Étienne, Count Davignon** (French pronunciation: [\[etjɛn daviɲɔ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); 4 October 1932 – 18 May 2026) was a Belgian diplomat and civil servant who served as [European Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner) from 1977 to 1985.

In March 2026, a Belgian court ruled that Davignon could stand trial for his alleged role in events leading to the assassination of Congolese independence leader [Patrice Lumumba](49240.md) in January 1961. He was accused of involvement in the unlawful detention and degrading treatment of Lumumba. Davignon was serving as a junior diplomatic intern in [Kinshasa](45773.md) at the time. Davignon consistently denied the allegations.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## Career

After receiving a Doctorate of Law from the [Catholic University of Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Leuven_\(1834%E2%80%931968\)), Davignon joined the Belgian Foreign Ministry, in 1959, and within two years had become an [attaché](1653163.md) under [Paul-Henri Spaak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul-Henri_Spaak), then [Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Belgium\)).[^cite_note-csr-3] He remained in Belgian government until 1965. In 1970, he chaired the committee of experts which produced the [Davignon report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davignon_report) on foreign policy for Europe.[^cite_note-foe-4]

From 1974 to 1977, Davignon was the first head of the [International Energy Agency](285292.md),[^cite_note-csr-3] before becoming a member of the European Commission, of which he was vice-president from 1981 until 1985. From 1989 to 2001, he was chairman of the Belgian bank [Société Générale de Belgique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale_de_Belgique), which is now part of the French supplier [Engie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engie) and was not an arm of the French bank [Société Générale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale), but a Belgian institution. As of 2010, he was Vice Chairman of Suez subsidiary Suez-Tractebel.[^cite_note-5]

As chairman of Société Générale de Belgique, he was a member of the [European Round Table of Industrialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Round_Table_of_Industrialists).[^cite_note-csr-3] At the time of his death in 2026 he was the co-chairman of the EU-Japan Business Dialogue Round Table, chairman of the Paul-Henri Spaak Foundation, president of the Egmont – Royal Institute for International Relations, chairman of CSR Europe, chairman of the European Academy of Business in Society and was chairman of the annual [Bilderberg conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilderberg_Group) from 1998 to 2001.[^cite_note-6] He was a member of the Steering Committee of the [Bilderberg Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilderberg_Group).[^cite_note-7]

Davignon was the chairman of the board of directors of [Brussels Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airlines),[^cite_note-8] which he co-founded after the bankruptcy of [Sabena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabena). He was also a member of the board of numerous Belgian companies, and was the chairman of the board of directors and of the General Assembly of the [ICHEC Brussels Management School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICHEC_Brussels_Management_School).[^cite_note-9]

On 26 January 2004, [Albert II of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_II_of_Belgium) gave him the honorary title of *Minister of State*, which entitled him to a seat on the [Crown Council of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Council_of_Belgium).

In 2018, [Philippe of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_of_Belgium) conferred the title of Count Davignon upon him.

Davignon was a crucial member of the Strategic Advisory Panel of The European Business Awards. He was a member of the [Cercle Gaulois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercle_Gaulois) and a member of the advisory board of the [Itinera Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itinera_Institute) [think tank](37101.md). He was also president of the [Brussels](3708.md)-based think tank Friends of Europe.[^cite_note-foe-4]

## Alleged involvement in the dismissal and assassination of Patrice Lumumba

Davignon's role as a junior diplomatic intern in [Kinshasa](45773.md) at the time of [Patrice Lumumba](49240.md)'s dismissal as prime minister in September 1960 and his assassination in January 1961 has been a subject of scrutiny.[^cite_note-\:1-10] A Belgian parliamentary inquiry in 2001 concluded that although there was no proof of direct involvement, the Belgian state and certain officials bore a "moral responsibility" for the circumstances leading to Lumumba's death.[^cite_note-\:0-11] The inquiry said that Davignon was "tasked with convincing then-Congolese President [Joseph Kasa Vubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kasa-Vubu) to dismiss Lumumba \[as prime minister] and providing him with the necessary legal arguments."[^cite_note-\:2-12]

Davignon wrote a [telex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telex) in September 1960 stating that it was a "primordial problem to remove Lumumba and achieve unity of the Congolese leaders against him". In a 2010 interview, Davignon denied that this meant a call for Lumumba to be killed.[^cite_note-13]

In 2011, Lumumba's family filed a complaint in Belgium accusing several individuals, including Davignon, of involvement in the assassination. Following a lengthy investigation, the Belgian federal prosecutor's office announced in June 2025 that it was requesting Davignon, the sole surviving defendant, be tried on charges of [war crimes](48626.md), specifically for his alleged role in the [unlawful](627536.md) [detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detention_\(confinement\)) and degrading treatment of Lumumba. Prosecutors added that a charge of intent to kill should be dropped.[^cite_note-\:0-11] Prosecutors alleged that Davignon had knowledge of the plan to arrest Patrice Lumumba in September 1960.[^cite_note-\:1-10] On 20 January, judges at the Brussels Criminal Court’s Council Chamber heard Davignon's legal defense on why the case should not go to trial.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:2-12] On 17 March, it was decided that Davignon would stand trial.[^cite_note-15]

In 2026, Davignon died before he could stand trial.[^cite_note-16]

## Personal life and death



Davignon in 2024

Davignon was the long-term partner of [Antoinette Spaak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoinette_Spaak), daughter of [Paul-Henri Spaak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul-Henri_Spaak), whom he had met while working as his [chef de cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chef_de_cabinet). The two began dating c. 1968,[^cite_note-17] and remained in a relationship until Spaak's death in 2020.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

His grandfather, [Julien Davignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Davignon), also served in the government of Belgium, being Minister for Foreign Affairs in 1914, at the outbreak of World War I.[^cite_note-20]

Davignon died in Brussels on 18 May 2026, at the age of 93.[^cite_note-21]
