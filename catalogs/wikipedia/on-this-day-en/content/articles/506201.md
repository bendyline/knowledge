The **Major Oak** is a large dead English oak (*[Quercus robur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)*) near [Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwinstowe), in the midst of [Sherwood Forest](28739.md), Nottinghamshire, England. Thought to be between 800 and 1,200 years old, the tree was estimated to weigh 23 long tons and had a [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(botany\)) of 92 feet (28 m) at its peak. According to local folklore, it was the shelter of [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood) and his [Merry Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Men).

In June 2026, the [Royal Society for the Protection of Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_for_the_Protection_of_Birds) (RSPB), which manages the forest, announced that the tree had died after failing to produce leaves that year. This was attributed to prolonged stress from a series of hot, dry summers, [soil compaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compaction_\(geology\)), changes to the [water table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_table) due to nearby [coal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_the_United_Kingdom), and counter-productive measures taken to preserve it.

## Description

In 1999 the Major Oak was estimated to weigh 23 long tons, had a [girth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_girth_measurement) of 33 feet (10 m) and a [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(botany\)) of 92 feet (28 m) at its peak, and was thought to be between 800 and 1,200 years old.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-NYT-3][^cite_note-4]

## Name

The tree was originally known as the Cockpen Tree, after the [cockfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockfighting) that once took place beneath it.[^cite_note-5] It was later named the Major Oak after Major [Hayman Rooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayman_Rooke), a [British Army](4887.md) soldier and antiquarian who described the tree in his 1790 book *Description and Sketches of Some Remarkable Oaks*, writing: "I think no one can behold this majestic ruin without pronouncing it to be of very remote antiquity; and might venture to say, that it cannot be much less than a thousand years old."[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:1-7]

## History



*The Major Oak* (1844), by [Henry Dawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dawson_\(artist\)), Nottingham Museums

It is unclear whether the Major Oak is a single tree or several saplings that became [fused together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inosculation),[^cite_note-Sherwood-old-8] which may account for its distinctive shape. The tree is commonly considered the UK's second-largest oak, surpassed only by the Majesty Oak in Fredville Park in the village of [Nonington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonington), near Dover, Kent.[^cite_note-9]

The tree has long been linked in local folklore with [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood) and his [Merry Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Men), who were said to have sheltered beneath it.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Major Hayman Rooke first formally identified the tree in 1790.[^cite_note-\:1-7] Support chains were first fitted in 1908, and its massive branches were partially supported by an elaborate system of scaffolding beginning in the 1970s.[^cite_note-12] In 1974, fences were installed around the tree to protect its root system, as the volume of visitors was compacting the surrounding soil.[^cite_note-13]

Around 1912, [Emmeline Pankhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmeline_Pankhurst) was among approximately 30 [suffragettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette) reported to have climbed inside the tree.[^cite_note-14]

The "Robin Hood Oak" on the campus of the [State University of New York College of Environmental Science and Forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_College_of_Environmental_Science_and_Forestry) is a direct descendant of the Major Oak, having been grown from one of the Major Oak's acorns collected in 1926.[^cite_note-15]

The formation sign of the [46th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_Infantry_Division_\(United_Kingdom\)) of the British Army during World War II was the Major Oak.[^cite_note-16] Among the units of the division were battalions of the [Sherwood Foresters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_Foresters) regiment.[^cite_note-regiments-17]

In 1982 the tree suffered significant damage from a fire that was extinguished by the [fire brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_Fire_and_Rescue_Service). [Nottinghamshire Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_Police) reported that the blaze had been caused by arsonists.[^cite_note-18]

In a 2002 public survey, the Major Oak was voted "Britain's favourite tree".[^cite_note-19] In 2003, a [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) of 260 [saplings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saplings) grown from [acorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn) of the Major Oak was established in [Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset), intended to support an internet-based study of the tree's history, photographic record, variation in size and leafing among the saplings, comparison of their [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA), and to eventually serve as a public amenity.[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-20] In 2013, Major Oak saplings were given to [pantomime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantomime) actors [Barney Harwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barney_Harwood), [Su Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Pollard), and [David Hasselhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hasselhoff).[^cite_note-patel-21]

In 2014 it was voted [England's Tree of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_the_Year_\(United_Kingdom\)) in a public poll organised by the [Woodland Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_Trust), receiving 18% of the votes.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-\:0-2] The tree was also featured on the 2005 television programme *[Seven Natural Wonders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Natural_Wonders)* as one of the wonders of the [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands).[^cite_note-23]

As of June 2026, the tree receives approximately 350,000 visitors annually.[^cite_note-barkham-24]

## Death

From 2022 onwards, the tree suffered from summer [heat waves](293270.md) and produced progressively fewer leaves.[^cite_note-25] Rumours of the tree's decline were initially denied by the [RSPB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_for_the_Protection_of_Birds), which manages the forest.[^cite_note-26] However, its death was officially confirmed by the RSPB in June 2026, after prolonged stress from a succession of hot, dry summers caused it to fail to produce any leaves that year.[^cite_note-barkham-24][^cite_note-27] The RSPB attributed part of the blame to the scaffolding, which had artificially forced the tree to pump water towards its propped-up branches – at the expense of its trunk – rather than allowing it to shed branches naturally and begin "growing down", as would be expected for a tree of its age. Additional contributing factors included [soil compaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compaction_\(geology\)) resulting from two centuries of tourist and vehicular traffic, and alterations to the [water table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_table) caused by nearby [coal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-barkham-24]

The Major Oak is situated within a [Site of Special Scientific Interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_of_Special_Scientific_Interest) and will remain standing rather than being felled.[^cite_note-patel-21][^cite_note-28]
