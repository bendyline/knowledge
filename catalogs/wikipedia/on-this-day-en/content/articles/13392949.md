**PlayStation**[^cite_note-1] is a [video gaming](5363.md) [brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand) owned and produced by [Sony Interactive Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Interactive_Entertainment) (SIE), a subsidiary of Japanese conglomerate [Sony](26989.md). The brand is recognized primarily for its series of [home video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_video_game_console), though it also includes [handheld devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handheld_game_console), online services, and other media products.

The brainchild of Sony executive [Ken Kutaragi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Kutaragi), the brand began with the [first PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\)) home console released in Japan in 1994 and worldwide the following year, which became the first console of any type to ship over 100 million units, which made PlayStation a globally recognized brand.[^cite_note-100Mrecord2-2] Since then there have been numerous newer consoles—the most recent being the [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5) released in 2020—while there have also been a series of handheld consoles and a number of other electronics such as a [media center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_theater_PC) and a [smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone). The main series of controllers utilized by the PlayStation series is the [DualShock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualShock), a line of [vibration-feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haptic_technology) [gamepads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamepad).[^cite_note-3] SIE also operate numerous online services like [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network), the [PlayStation Store](9783860.md), and the subscription-based [PlayStation Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Plus), which may also offer non-gaming entertainment services; the PlayStation Network has over 103 million active users monthly as of December 2019.[^cite_note-sie200107-4]

&#x20;The series also has a strong line-up of first-party games due to [PlayStation Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Studios), a group of many studios owned by Sony Interactive Entertainment that exclusively developed them for PlayStation consoles. In addition, the series features various budget re-releases of games by Sony with different names for each region; these include the [PlayStation Hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Hits), [Platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essentials_\(PlayStation\)), [Essentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essentials_\(PlayStation\)), and [The Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_\(PlayStation\)) selection of games. It is also known for the four iconic PlayStation face buttons (Triangle, Cross, X, Square) and has been known for its numerous marketing campaigns, the latest of which being the "Play Has No Limits" commercials.

### Origins

Further information: [Timeline of early 3D computer graphics hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_early_3D_computer_graphics_hardware)

PlayStation was the brainchild of [Ken Kutaragi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Kutaragi), a [Sony](26989.md) executive who managed one of the company's hardware engineering divisions and was later dubbed "The Father of the PlayStation".[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]



One of two known remaining prototypes of Sony's original "PlayStation", a [Super NES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_NES) with a [built-in CD-ROM drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_NES_CD-ROM)

[^cite_note-7]

Until 1991, Sony had little direct involvement with the video game industry. The company supplied components for other consoles, such as the sound chip for the [Super Famicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System) from [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo), and operated a video game studio, [Sony Imagesoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Imagesoft).[^cite_note-8] As part of a joint project between Nintendo and Sony that began as early as 1988, the two companies worked to create a [CD-ROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-ROM) version of the Super Famicom,[^cite_note-9] though Nintendo denied the existence of the Sony deal as late as March 1991.[^cite_note-cgw199106-10] At the [Consumer Electronics Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Electronics_Show) in June 1991, Sony revealed a Super Famicom with a built-in CD-ROM drive that incorporated [Green Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Book_\(CD_standard\)) technology or CD-i, called "Play Station" (also known as [SNES-CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNES-CD)). However, a day after the announcement at CES, Nintendo announced that it would be breaking its partnership with Sony, opting to go with [Philips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips) instead but using the same technology.[^cite_note-History-11] The deal was broken by Nintendo after they were unable to agree on how revenue would be split between the two companies.[^cite_note-History-11] The breaking of the partnership infuriated Sony President [Norio Ohga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norio_Ohga), who responded by appointing Kutaragi with the responsibility of developing the PlayStation project to rival Nintendo.[^cite_note-History-11]

At that time, negotiations were still ongoing between Nintendo and Sony, with Nintendo offering Sony a "non-gaming role" regarding their new partnership with Philips. This proposal was swiftly rejected by Kutaragi who was facing increasing criticism over his work with regard to entering the video game industry from within Sony. Negotiations officially ended in May 1992 and in order to decide the fate of the PlayStation project, a meeting was held in June 1992, consisting of Sony President Ohga, PlayStation Head Kutaragi and several senior members of Sony's board. At the meeting, Kutaragi unveiled a proprietary CD-ROM-based system he had been working on which involved playing video games with 3D graphics to the board. Eventually, Sony President Ohga decided to retain the project after being reminded by Kutaragi of the humiliation he suffered from Nintendo. Nevertheless, due to strong opposition from a majority present at the meeting as well as widespread internal opposition to the project by the older generation of Sony executives, Kutaragi and his team had to be shifted from Sony's headquarters to [Sony Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Music), a completely separate financial entity owned by Sony, so as to retain the project and maintain relationships with Philips for the MMCD development project (which helped lead to the creation of the [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD)).[^cite_note-History-11]





Original PlayStation logo introduced in 1994 (left); this version had been primarily marketed until the introduction of a monochrome variant in 2009 (right)

According to SCE's producer Ryoji Akagawa and chairman [Shigeo Maruyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Maruyama), there was uncertainty over whether the console should primarily focus on [2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2D_computer_graphics) [sprite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprite_\(computer_graphics\)) graphics or [3D polygon graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics). Eventually, after witnessing the success of [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega)'s *[Virtua Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtua_Fighter_\(video_game\))* in Japanese arcades, Sony realized "the direction of the PlayStation became instantly clear" and 3D polygon graphics became the console's primary focus.[^cite_note-12]

The PlayStation logo was designed by Manabu Sakamoto. He wanted the logo to capture the 3D support of the console, but instead of just adding apparent depth to the letters "P" and "S", he created an [optical illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusion) that suggested the letters in depth of space. Sakamoto also stuck with four bright principal colors, red, yellow, green, and blue, only having to tune the green color for better harmony across the logo. Sakamoto also designed the black-and-white logo based on the same design, reserved for times where colors could not be used.[^cite_note-gi_first_25-13]

### Formation of Sony Computer Entertainment



Sony Computer Entertainment logo (1993–2016)

At [Sony Music Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Music_Entertainment), Kutaragi worked closely with [Shigeo Maruyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Maruyama), the CEO of Sony Music, and with Akira Sato to form Sony Computer Entertainment Inc. (SCEI) on November 16, 1993.[^cite_note-scei-history-14] A building block of SCEI was its initial partnership with Sony Music which helped SCEI attract creative talent to the company as well as assist SCEI in manufacturing, marketing and producing discs, something that Sony Music had been doing with Music Discs. The final two key members of SCEI were Terry Tokunaka, the president of SCEI from Sony's headquarters, and [Olaf Olafsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Olafsson). Olafsson was CEO and president of New York-based Sony Interactive Entertainment[^cite_note-15] which was the parent company for the 1994-founded Sony Computer Entertainment of America (SCEA).

The PlayStation project, SCEI's first official project, was finally given the green light by Sony executives in 1993 after a few years of development. Also in 1993, [Phil Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Harrison), who later became President of [SCE Worldwide Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIE_Worldwide_Studios), was recruited into SCEI to attract developers and publishers to produce games for their new PlayStation platform.[^cite_note-History-11]

In March 1994, *[Computer Gaming World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Gaming_World)* reported a rumor that the "**Sony PS-X**" would be released in Japan "before the end of this year and will retail for less than \$400".[^cite_note-cgw199403-16] After a demonstration of Sony's distribution plan as well as tech demos of its new console to game publishers and developers in a hotel in [Tokyo](30057.md) in 1994, numerous developers began to approach PlayStation. Two of whom later became major partners were [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts) in the West and [Namco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namco) in Japan. One of the factors that attracted developers to the platform was the use of a 3D-capable, CD-ROM-based console that was much cheaper and easier to manufacture compared to Nintendo's rival console, which used cartridge systems. The project eventually hit Japanese stores in December 1994 and gained massive sales due to its lower price point than its competitor, the [Sega Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Saturn). The popularity of the console spread after its release worldwide in North America and Europe.[^cite_note-History-11]

## Home consoles

### PlayStation

Main article: [PlayStation (console)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\))



Original PlayStation with a [controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_controller)

The original PlayStation, released in Japan on December 3, [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_in_video_gaming), was the first of the PlayStation series of console and hand-held game devices. It has included successor consoles and upgrades including the [Net Yaroze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_Yaroze) (a special black PlayStation with tools and instructions to program PlayStation games and applications), "PS one" (a smaller version of the original) and the [PocketStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PocketStation) (a handheld which enhances PlayStation games and also acts as a memory card). It was part of the [fifth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_generation_of_video_game_consoles) competing against the [Sega Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Saturn) and the [Nintendo 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_64). By December 2003, the PlayStation and PS one had shipped a combined total of 102.49 million units,[^cite_note-ps1shipments-17] eventually becoming the first video game console to sell 120 million units.[^cite_note-100Mrecord-18]

#### PS One

Main article: [PlayStation models § PS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_models#PS_One)

Released on July 7, [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_in_video_gaming),[^cite_note-19] concurrently with its successor the [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2), the PS One (stylized as PS one) was a considerably smaller, redesigned version of the original PlayStation video game console.[^cite_note-PSOne-20] The PS one went on to outsell all other consoles, including its successor, throughout the remainder of the year.[^cite_note-PSOne-20] It featured two main changes from its predecessor, the first being a cosmetic change to the console and the second being the home menu's [graphical user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_user_interface) (GUI); a variation of the GUI previously used only on PAL consoles up to that point.

### PlayStation 2

Main article: [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2)



Original PlayStation 2 with a [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD) disc drive and a [DualShock 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualShock#DualShock_2) controller

Released in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_in_video_gaming), 15 months after the [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast) and a year before its other competitors, the [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_\(console\)) and the [GameCube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameCube), the PlayStation 2 is part of the [sixth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_video_game_consoles_\(sixth_generation\)), and is [backwards-compatible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backwards-compatible) with most original PlayStation games. Like its predecessor, it has received a slimmer redesign. It is the [most successful console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_million-selling_game_consoles) in the world,[^cite_note-21] having sold over 155 million units as of December 28, 2012.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-22] On November 29, 2005, the PS2 became the fastest game console to reach 100 million units shipped, accomplishing the feat within 5 years and 9 months from its launch. This achievement occurred faster than its predecessor, the PlayStation, which took "9 years and 6 months since launch" to reach the same figure.[^cite_note-100Mrecord-18] PlayStation 2 shipments in Japan ended on December 28, 2012.[^cite_note-famitsu-23] *The Guardian* reported on January 4, 2013, that PS2 production had ended worldwide, but studies showed that many people all around the world still own one even if it is no longer in use. PlayStation 2 has been ranked as the best-selling console of all time as of 2015.[^cite_note-Guardian_end-24]

#### Slimline model

Main article: [PlayStation 2 models § Slimline case designs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2_models#Slimline_case_designs)

Released in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_in_video_gaming), four years after the launch of the original PlayStation 2, the PlayStation 2 Slimline was the first major redesign of the PlayStation 2. Compared to its predecessor, the Slimline was smaller, thinner, quieter and also included a built-in [Ethernet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethernet) port (in some markets it also has an integrated [modem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modem)). In [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_in_video_gaming), Sony began shipping a revision of the Slimline which was lighter than the original Slimline together with a lighter [AC adapter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_adapter).[^cite_note-ign-lighterPS2-25] In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_in_video_gaming), Sony released yet another revision of the Slimline which had an overhauled internal design incorporating the power supply into the console itself like the original PlayStation 2 resulting in a further reduced total weight of the console.[^cite_note-26]

### PlayStation 3

Main article: [PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3)



Original PlayStation 3 console with a [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray) disc drive and a [DualShock 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualShock_3) controller

Released on November 11, 2006, in Japan, the PlayStation 3 (PS3) is a [seventh generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_video_game_consoles_\(seventh_generation\)) game console from Sony. It competes with the [Microsoft Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360) and the [Nintendo Wii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii). The PS3 is the first console in the series to introduce the use of [motion-sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_detection) technology through its [Sixaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixaxis) wireless controller. The console also incorporates a [Blu-ray Disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc) player and features [high-definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_video) resolution. The PS3 was originally offered with either a 20 GB or 60 GB hard drive, but over the years its capacity increased in increments available up to 500 GB. The PlayStation 3 has sold over 80 million consoles worldwide as of November 2013.[^cite_note-WorldPS3Sales-27]

#### Slim model

Like its predecessors, the PlayStation 3 was re-released in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_in_video_gaming) as a "slim" model. The redesigned model is 33% smaller, 36% lighter, and consumes 34% to 45% less power than previous models.[^cite_note-PS3SlimSpecs-28][^cite_note-29] In addition, it features a redesigned cooling system and a smaller [Cell processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_processor) which was moved to a [45nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45nm) manufacturing process.[^cite_note-ArsTechnicaInterview-30] It sold in excess of a million units within its first 3 weeks on sale.[^cite_note-31] The redesign added support for [CEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-Definition_Multimedia_Interface#CEC) (branded as BRAVIA Sync, VIERA Link, and others), allowing console control via a TV remote. The PS3 slim also runs quieter and is cooler than previous models due to its 45 nm Cell. The PS3 Slim no longer has the "main power" switch (similar to [PlayStation 2 slim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slim_PlayStation_2)), like the previous PS3 models, which was located at the back of the console.[^cite_note-PS3SlimSpecs-28] It was officially released on September 1, 2009, in North America and Europe and on September 3, 2009, in Japan, Australia and New Zealand.[^cite_note-PS3SlimSpecs-28][^cite_note-PS3SlimPressReleaseAU-32][^cite_note-33]

#### Super Slim model

In 2012, Sony revealed a new "Super Slim" PlayStation 3. The new console, with a completely redesigned case that has a sliding door covering the disc drive (which has been moved to the top of the console), is 4.3 pounds, almost three pounds lighter than the previous "slim" model. The console comes with either 12 GB flash memory or a 250 GB, 500 GB hard drive. Several bundles which include a Super Slim PS3 and a selection of games are available.

### PlayStation 4

Main article: [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4)



Original PlayStation 4 with a Blu-ray disc drive and a [DualShock 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualShock#DualShock_4) controller

The [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4) (PS4) was announced by [Sony Computer Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment) at a press conference on February 20, 2013. In the meeting, Sony revealed some hardware specifications of the new console.[^cite_note-PS4_announcement-34][^cite_note-PS4_announced_-_TechRadar-35] It introduced the [x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86) architecture to the PlayStation series. According to lead system architect Mark Cerny, development on the PlayStation 4 began as early as 2008.[^cite_note-PS4_announced_-_IGN-36] PlayStation Europe CEO Jim Ryan emphasized in 2011 that Sony wanted to avoid launching the next-generation console behind the competition.[^cite_note-37]

Among the new applications and services, Sony introduced the PlayStation App, allowing PS4 owners to turn smartphones and tablets into a [second screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_screen) to enhance gameplay.[^cite_note-SCE_introduces_PS4-38] The company also planned to debut [PlayStation Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Now) game streaming service, powered by technology from [Gaikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaikai).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] By incorporating a share button on the new controller and making it possible to view in-game content being streamed live from friends, Sony planned to place more focus on social gameplay as well.[^cite_note-SCE_introduces_PS4-38] The PlayStation 4 was first released in North America on November 15, 2013. As part of the [eighth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_video_game_consoles_\(eighth_generation\)), it competes with Microsoft's [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One) and Nintendo's [Wii U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii_U) and [Switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch).

#### Slim model

**PlayStation 4 Slim** (officially marketed simply as **PlayStation 4** or **PS4**) was unveiled on September 7, 2016. It is a revision of the original PS4 hardware with a streamlined form factor. The new casing is 40% smaller and carries a rounded body with a matte finish on the top of the console rather than a two-tone finish. The two USB ports on the front have a larger gap between them, and the optical audio port was also removed.<sup>[\[168\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-168)</sup> It ships with a minor update to the DualShock 4 controller, with the light bar visible through the top of the touchpad and a dark matte grey coloured exterior instead of a partially shiny black. The PS4 Slim was released on September 15, 2016, with a 500 GB model at the same price point as the original PS4 model.<sup>[\[169\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-ars-ps4slim-169)</sup> Its model number is CUH-2000.<sup>[\[170\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-ps4pro_model_no-170)</sup>

#### Pro model

**PlayStation 4 Pro** or **PS4 Pro** for short (originally announced under the codename **Neo**)<sup>[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-PS4Neo-35)</sup> was unveiled on September 7, 2016. Its model number is CUH-7000.<sup>[\[170\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-ps4pro_model_no-170)</sup> It is an updated version of the PlayStation 4 with improved hardware, including an upgraded GPU with 4.2 teraflops of processing power, and a higher CPU clock. It is designed primarily to enable selected games to be playable at [4K resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4K_resolution), and improved quality for [PlayStation VR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_VR). All games are backwards and forward compatible between PS4 and PS4 Pro, but games with optimizations will have improved graphics performance on PS4 Pro. Although capable of streaming 4K video from online sources, PS4 Pro does not support [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray).<sup>[\[171\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-verge-no4kbluray-171) [\[172\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-verge-ps4pro-172) [\[173\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-engadget-pro-173)</sup> Additionally the PS4 Pro is the only PS4 model which can remote play at 1080p. The other models are limited to 720p.<sup>[\[174\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4#cite_note-sony_ps4_website-174)</sup>

### PlayStation 5

Main article: [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5)



Original PlayStation 5 with a [4K Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray) disc drive and a [DualSense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualSense) controller

The PlayStation 5 (PS5)[^cite_note-2019-10-08_GameSpot-41] was released worldwide on November 12, 2020, and, alongside the [Xbox Series X and Series S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S), released the same month, is part of the [ninth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_generation_of_video_game_consoles). The first news of the PS5 came from [Mark Cerny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Cerny) in an interview with *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\))* in April 2019.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_april2019-42] Sony intended for the PlayStation 5 to be its next-generation console and planned to ship it worldwide by the end of 2020.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_oct2019-43] In early 2019, Sony's financial report for the quarter ending March 31, 2019, affirmed that new next-generation hardware was in development but would ship no earlier than April 2020.[^cite_note-44]

The current specifications were released in October 2019.[^cite_note-sieannounce-45] The console uses an 8-core, [16-thread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_multithreading) CPU based on AMD's [Zen 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_2) microarchitecture, manufactured on the [7 nanometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nanometer) process node. The graphics processor is a custom variant of AMD's Navi family using the [RDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDNA_\(microarchitecture\)) microarchitecture, which includes support for [hardware acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_acceleration) of [ray-tracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_tracing_\(graphics\)) rendering, enabling real-time ray-traced graphics.[^cite_note-sieannounce-45] The new console ships with a custom [SSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_drive) storage, as Cerny emphasized the need for fast loading times and higher bandwidth to make games more immersive, as well as to support the required content streaming from disc for [8K resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8K_resolution).[^cite_note-wired_ps5_april2019-42] In a second interview with Wired in October 2019, further details of the new hardware were revealed: the console's integrated [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray) drive would support 100 GB Blu-ray discs[^cite_note-wired_ps5_oct2019-43] and [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray);[^cite_note-46] while a game installation from a disc is mandatory as to take advantage of the SSD, the user will have some fine-grain control of how much they want to have installed, such as only installing multiplayer components of a game.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_oct2019-43] Sony is developing an improved suspended gameplay state for the PlayStation 5 to consume less energy than the PlayStation 4.[^cite_note-47]

The system's new controller, the [DualSense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualSense) has adaptive triggers that can change the resistance to the player as necessary, such as changing the resistance during the action of pulling an arrow back in a bow in-game.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_oct2019-43] The controller also has strong [haptic feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haptic_technology) through [voice coil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_coil) actuators, which together with an improved controller speaker is intended to give better in-game feedback.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_oct2019-43] [USB-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB-C) connectivity, together with a higher-rated battery are other improvements to the new controller.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_oct2019-43]

The PlayStation 5 features a completely revamped [user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_interface).[^cite_note-2019-10-08_GameSpot-41] The PlayStation 5 is backwards-compatible with most PlayStation 4 and PlayStation [VR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality) games, with Cerny stating that the transition to the new console is meant to be a soft one.[^cite_note-wired_ps5_april2019-42][^cite_note-sieannounce-45] In a later interview, Jim Ryan talked of the PlayStation 5 being able to play "99%" of PlayStation 4 games, an estimate derived from a sample size of "thousands".[^cite_note-48]

#### Slim model

A revised slimmer design for the PS5, similar to past "slim" rereleases, was released in October 2023. Besides the slimmer design, the slim model features 1 TB of internal storage and replaces one of the USB-A ports with USB-C. The base version also removes the internal optical disc drive, replaced by an external drive.[^cite_note-49]

#### Pro model

The PlayStation 5 Pro was released in November 2024. It features three main upgrades over the base PS5 model: a GPU that was about 45% faster than the base PS5 GPU, the inclusion of an [image upscaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_upscaling) technology called PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR), and an increase of internal storage to 2 TB.[^cite_note-verge_pro_features-50][^cite_note-51] Games are able to take advantage of the improved graphical performance if programmed for it, with about 50 games ready to use this feature at launch.[^cite_note-verge_pro_features-50]

PocketStation

The PocketStation is a Memory Card [peripheral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral) by Sony Computer Entertainment for the original [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\)). Categorized by Sony as a combination of a Memory Card and a miniature [personal digital assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_digital_assistant).[^cite_note-Pocketstation-90] Released exclusively in Japan on January 23, 1999,[^cite_note-91] it featured a monochrome [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_crystal_display), a speaker, a [real-time clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_clock) and infrared communication capability. It could also be used as a standard PlayStation memory card by connecting it to a PlayStation memory card slot.[^cite_note-Pocketstation-90] As a handheld device, it allowed users to play minigames downloaded from compatible PlayStation discs on the go. Over 80 PlayStation games featured integration with the peripheral, allowing players to download exclusive minigames, train characters, or unlock special items for titles like Final Fantasy VIII, Street Fighter Alpha 3, and Ridge Racer Type 4. It was extremely popular in Japan and Sony originally had plans to release it in the United States but the plan was ultimately scrapped due to various manufacturing and supply-and-demand problems.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

### PlayStation Portable

Main article: [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable)

The [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable) (PSP) was Sony's first handheld console to compete with Nintendo's DS console. The original model (PSP-1000) was released in December [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_in_video_gaming) and March [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_in_video_gaming),[^cite_note-GSUSlaunch-94] The console is the first to utilize a new [proprietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_hardware) optical storage medium known as [Universal Media Disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Media_Disc) (UMD), which can store both games and movies.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] It contains 32 [MB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabyte) of internal [flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_memory) storage, expandable via [Memory Stick PRO Duo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_Stick_PRO_Duo) cards.[^cite_note-gizmodo_specs-97] It has a similar control layout to the PS3 with its PlayStation logo button and its Triangle ('Triangle'), Circle ('Circle/O'), Cross ('Cross/X') and Square ('Square') buttons in their white-colored forms.

#### PSP-2000 and PSP-3000 models

The PSP-2000 (also known as the Slim & Lite in PAL territories) was the first major hardware revision of the PlayStation Portable, released in September [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_in_video_gaming). The 2000 series was 33% lighter and 19% slimmer than the original PlayStation Portable.[^cite_note-SonyE3-98][^cite_note-99] The capacity of the battery was also reduced by ⅓ but the run time remained the same as the previous model due to lower power consumption. Older model batteries will still work and they extend the amount of playing time.[^cite_note-100] The PSP Slim & Lite has a new gloss finish. Its serial port was also modified to accommodate a new video-out feature (while rendering older PSP remote controls incompatible). On a PSP-2000, PSP games will only output to external monitors or TVs in progressive scan mode, so that televisions incapable of supporting progressive scan will not display PSP games; non-game video will output in either progressive or interlaced mode. [USB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB) charging was also made possible.[^cite_note-101] Buttons are also reportedly more responsive on the PSP-2000.[^cite_note-102] In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_in_video_gaming), Sony released a second hardware revision called the PSP-3000 which included several features that were not present in the PSP-2000, such as a built-in microphone and upgraded screen, as well as the ability to output PSP games in interlaced mode.

#### PSP Go model

Released in October [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_in_video_gaming), the PSP Go diverged from the original design of the previous models in multiple facets. Unlike previous PSP models, the PSP Go does not feature a UMD drive but instead has 16 [GB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabyte) of internal [flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_memory) to store games, videos and other media.[^cite_note-Announcement-103] This can be extended by up to 32 GB with the use of a [Memory Stick Micro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_Stick_Micro) (M2) flash card. Also unlike previous PSP models, the PSP Go's rechargeable battery is not removable or replaceable by the user. The unit is 43% lighter and 56% smaller than the original PSP-1000,[^cite_note-kotaku_announce-104] and 16% lighter and 35% smaller than the PSP-3000.[^cite_note-TSA-105] It has a 3.8" 480 × 272 [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-crystal_display)[^cite_note-PlayStation-106] (compared to the larger 4.3" 480 × 272 pixel LCD on previous PSP models).[^cite_note-107] The screen slides up to reveal the main controls. The overall shape and sliding mechanism are similar to that of Sony's [mylo COM-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mylo_\(Sony\)) internet device.[^cite_note-Giz-108] The PSP Go was produced and sold concurrently with its predecessor the PSP-3000 although it did not replace it.[^cite_note-kotaku_announce-104] All games on the PSP Go must be purchased and downloaded from the [PlayStation Store](9783860.md) as the handheld is not compatible with the original PSP's physical media, the Universal Media Disc. The handheld also features connectivity with the [PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3)'s controllers the [Sixaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixaxis) and [DualShock 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualShock_3) via [Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluetooth) connection.[^cite_note-TSA-105]

#### PSP Street

### PlayStation Vita

Main article: [PlayStation Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vita)

Released in Japan on December 17, 2011, and North America on February 22, 2012,[^cite_note-111] the PlayStation Vita[^cite_note-112] was previously codenamed *Next Generation Portable* (*NGP*). It was officially unveiled by Sony on January 27, 2011, at the PlayStation Meeting 2011.[^cite_note-113] The original model of the handheld, the PCH-1000 series features a 5-inch OLED touchscreen,[^cite_note-114] two analog sticks, a rear touchpad, Sixaxis motion sensing and a 4 core ARM Cortex-A9 MPCore processor.

The new PCH-2000 series system is a lighter redesign of the device that was announced at the SCEJA Press Conference in September 2013 prior to the Tokyo Game Show. This model is 20% thinner and 15% lighter compared to the original model, has an additional hour of battery life, an LCD instead of OLED, includes a micro USB Type B port, and 1 GB of internal storage memory. It was released in Japan on October 10, 2013, in six colors: white, black, pink, yellow, blue, and olive green, and in North America on May 6, 2014.[^cite_note-115]

The Vita was discontinued in March 2019. SIE president Jim Ryan said that while the Vita was a great device, they have moved away from portable consoles, "clearly it's a business that we're no longer in now".[^cite_note-gi_first_25-13]

#### PlayStation TV

Main article: [PlayStation TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_TV)

PlayStation TV, known in Asia as PlayStation Vita TV or PS Vita TV, is a [microconsole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microconsole) and a non-portable variant of the PlayStation Vita handheld. It was announced on September 9, 2013, at a Sony Computer Entertainment Japan presentation. Instead of featuring a display screen, the console connects to a television via HDMI. Users can play using a Sixaxis or a DualShock 3 controller, although due to the difference in features between the controller and the handheld, certain games are not compatible with PS TV, such as those that are dependent on the system's touchscreen, rear touchpad, microphone or camera. The device is said to be compatible with over 100 Vita games, as well as various digital PlayStation Portable, PlayStation and PC Engine titles. The system supports Remote Play compatibility with the PlayStation 4, allowing players to stream games from the PS4 to a separate TV connected to PS TV, and also allows users to stream content from video services such as Hulu and Niconico, as well as access the PlayStation Store. The system was released in Japan on November 14, 2013, in North America on October 14, 2014, and in Europe and Australasia on November 14, 2014.[^cite_note-116]

Released in 1994, the PlayStation control pad was the first controller made for the original [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\)). It featured a basic design of a [D-pad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-pad), 4 main select buttons (Green Triangle ('Green Triangle'), Red Circle ('Red Circle/Red O')), Blue Cross ('Blue Cross/Blue X') and Pink Square ('Pink Square'), and start and select buttons on the face. 'Shoulder buttons' are also featured on the top \[L1, L2, R1, R2] (named by the side \[L=Left, R=Right] and 1 and 2 \[top and bottom]). In 1996, Sony released the PlayStation Analog Joystick for use with flight simulation games.[^cite_note-117] The original digital controller was then replaced by the Dual Analog in 1997, which added two analog sticks based on the same potentiometer technology as the Analog Joystick.[^cite_note-118] This controller was then also succeeded by the DualShock controller.

### DualShock, Sixaxis and DualSense

Released in 1998, the DualShock controller for the PlayStation succeeded its predecessor, the Dual Analog, and became the longest-running series of controllers for the PlayStation brand. In addition to the inputs of the original, digital controller (Triangle, Circle, Cross, Square, L1, L2, R1, R2, Start, Select and a [D-pad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-pad)), the DualShock featured two [analog sticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_stick) similarly to the previous [Dual Analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Analog) controller, which can also be depressed to activate the L3 and R3 buttons.[^cite_note-Dualshock_IGN-119]

The DualShock series consists of four controllers: the DualShock which was the fourth controller released for the PlayStation; the DualShock 2, the only standard controller released for the PlayStation 2, and the DualShock 3, the second and current controller released for the PlayStation 3, and the DualShock 4, which went through a massive redesign and is the default input of the PlayStation 4, and upon release was compatible with the PS3 originally only via USB and eventually with a firmware update, Bluetooth connectivity was enabled. The Sixaxis was the first official controller for the PlayStation 3, and is based on the same design as the DualShock series (but lacking the vibration motors of the DualShock series of controllers).

Like the Dual Analog, the DualShock and DualShock 2 feature an "Analog" button between the analog sticks that toggles the analog sticks on and off (for use with games which support only the digital input of the original controller). On the PlayStation 3 [Sixaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixaxis) and DualShock 3 controllers, the analog sticks are always enabled. Beginning with the Sixaxis, a 'PlayStation button' (which featured the incorporated PS logo and is similar in function to the [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360) "Guide" button) was included on controllers. The PlayStation button replaces the "Analog" button of the DualShock and DualShock 2 controllers. Pressing the PS button on the PS3 brings up the [XMB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XrossMediaBar), while holding it down brings up system options, known as "Quick Menu" on the PS4 (such as quit the game, change controller settings, turn off the system, and turn off the controller).[^cite_note-120]

### PlayStation Move

Main article: [PlayStation Move](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Move)



PlayStation Move navigation controller



PlayStation Move controller

PlayStation Move is a motion-sensing [game controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_controller) platform for the PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4, and PlayStation VR video game consoles by [Sony Computer Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment) (SCE). Based on the handheld motion controller wand, PlayStation Move uses the [PlayStation Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Eye) (PS3) and PlayStation Camera (PS4, PSVR) webcams to track the wand's position and the inertial sensors in the wand to detect its motion. First revealed on June 2, 2009, PlayStation Move was launched in Q3/Q4 2010. Hardware available at launch included the main PlayStation Move motion controller and an optional PlayStation Move sub-controller.[^cite_note-GDC_2010_PR-121] Although PlayStation Move is implemented on the existing PlayStation 3 console, Sony stated that it is treating Move's debut as its own major "platform launch", planning an aggressive marketing campaign to support it. In addition to selling the controllers individually,[^cite_note-bundling-122] Sony also planned to provide several different bundle options for PlayStation Move hardware; including a starter kit with a PS Eye, a Move motion controller, and a demo/sampler disc, priced under [US\$](18717338.md)100;[^cite_note-\$100_starter_kit-123] a full console pack with a PS3 console, [DualShock 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DualShock_3) gamepad, PS Eye, and Move motion controller; and bundles of a Move motion controller with select games. The PlayStation Move was compatible with the PS4, although in a limited number of games, and then repurposed as motion controllers for the PlayStation VR.[^cite_note-bundling-122]

### PSX (2003)

Main article: [PSX (DVR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSX_\(DVR\))

Released solely in Japan in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_in_video_gaming), the [Sony](26989.md) PSX was a fully integrated [DVR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video_recorder) and PlayStation 2 [video game console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console). It was the first Sony product to utilize the [XrossMediaBar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XrossMediaBar) (XMB)[^cite_note-PSX-124] and can be linked with a [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable) to transfer videos and music via [USB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB).[^cite_note-125] It also features software for [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_video_editing), [photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_editing) and [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_engineering) editing.[^cite_note-PSX-124] PSX supports online game compatibility using an internal broadband adapter. Games that utilize the [PS2 HDD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS2_HDD) (for example, [Final Fantasy XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XI)) are supported as well.[^cite_note-126] It was the first product released by Sony under the PlayStation brand that did not include a controller with the device itself.[^cite_note-127]

### Television sets

### Re-releases

Main articles: [Greatest Hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_\(PlayStation\)), [Platinum Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essentials_\(PlayStation\)), [The Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_\(PlayStation\)), [PS one Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_PS_one_Classics), [Classics HD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_remasters_for_PlayStation_consoles), and [PlayStation Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Mobile)

Greatest Hits (North America), Platinum Range (PAL territories) and The Best (Japan and Asia) are [video games](5363.md) for the Sony PlayStation, PlayStation 2, PlayStation 3, and PlayStation Portable consoles that have been officially re-released at a lower price by Sony. Each region has its own qualifications to enter the re-release program. Initially, during the PlayStation era, a game had to sell at least 150,000 copies (later 250,000)[^cite_note-GH_2003-135] and be on the market for at least a year[^cite_note-136] to enter the Greatest Hits range. During the [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2) era, the requirements increased with the minimum number of copies sold increasing to 400,000 and the game had to be on the market for at least 9 months.[^cite_note-GH_2003-135] For the [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable), games had to be on the market for at least 9 months with 250,000 copies or more sold.[^cite_note-137] Currently, a PlayStation 3 game must be on the market for 10 months and sell at least 500,000 copies to meet the Greatest Hits criteria.[^cite_note-138] PS one Classics were games that were released originally on the PlayStation and have been re-released on the PlayStation Store for the PlayStation 3 and PlayStation Portable. Classics HD are compilations of PlayStation 2 games that have been remastered for the PlayStation 3 on a single disc with additional features such as upscaled graphics, [PlayStation Move](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Move) support, [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoscopy) support and [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network) trophies. [PlayStation Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Mobile) (formerly PlayStation Suite) is a cross-platform, cross-device software framework aimed at providing PlayStation content, currently original PlayStation games, across several devices including [PlayStation Certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Certified) [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) devices as well as the [PlayStation Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vita).

### PlayStation Indies

Sony has generally supported [indie game development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_game_development) since incorporating the digital distribution storefront in the PlayStation 3, though initially required developers to complete multiple steps to get an indie game certified on the platform. Sony improved and simplified the process in transitioning to the PlayStation 4.[^cite_note-139]

As Sony prepared to transition from the PlayStation 4 to PlayStation 5, they introduced a new PlayStation Indies program led by [Shuhei Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuhei_Yoshida) in July 2020. The program's goals are to spotlight new and upcoming indie titles for the PlayStation 4 and 5, focusing on those that are more innovative and novel, akin to past titles such as *[PaRappa the Rapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PaRappa_the_Rapper)*, *[Katamari Damacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy)*, *[LittleBigPlanet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LittleBigPlanet)*, and *[Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_\(2012_video_game\))*. Sony also anticipates bringing more indie titles to the PlayStation Now series as part of this program.[^cite_note-140]

## Online services

### PlayStation 2 online service

Main article: [PlayStation 2 online functionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2_online_functionality)

Online gaming on PlayStation consoles first started in July 2001 with the release of PlayStation 2's unnamed online service in Japan. Later in August 2002 it was released in North America, followed by the European release in June 2003. This service was shut down on March 31, 2016.

### PlayStation Network

Main article: [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network)

Released in 2006, the PlayStation Network is an online service[^cite_note-141] focusing on online [multiplayer gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer_gaming) and digital media delivery. The service is provided and run by [Sony Computer Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment) for use with the [PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3), and was later implemented on the [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable), [PlayStation Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vita), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4) and [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5) [video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console).[^cite_note-gamespot150306-142] The service has over 103 million active users monthly (as of December 2019).[^cite_note-sie200107-4] The Sony Entertainment Network provides other features for users like [PlayStation Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Home), [PlayStation Store](9783860.md), and [Trophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3_Trophies).

### PlayStation Store

Main article: [PlayStation Store](9783860.md)

The PlayStation Store is an online virtual market available to users of the PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5 and [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable) [game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console) via the [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network). The store uses both physical currency and [PlayStation Network Cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network_Card). The PlayStation Store's gaming content is updated every Tuesday and offers a range of [downloadable content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downloadable_content) both for purchase and available free of charge. Available content includes full games, add-on content, [playable demos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_demo), [themes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_\(computing\)) and game and movie [trailers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_\(film\)). The service is accessible through an icon on the [XMB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XrossMediaBar) on the PS3 and PSP. The PS3 store can also be accessed on the PSP via a [Remote Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Play) connection to the PS3. The PSP store is also available via the PC application, [Media Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_Go). As of September 24, 2009, there have been more than 600 million downloads from the PlayStation Store worldwide.[^cite_note-143]

### Life with PlayStation

Main article: [Life with PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network#Life_with_PlayStation)

Life with PlayStation was a [Folding@home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding@home) application available for PlayStation 3 which connected to [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University)'s Folding\@home [distributed computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing) network and allowed the user to donate their console's spare processing cycles to the project.[^cite_note-144] Folding\@home is supported by Stanford University and volunteers make a contribution to society by [donating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donation) computing power to this project. Research made by the project may eventually contribute to the creation of vital cures. The Folding\@home client was developed by Sony Computer Entertainment in collaboration with Stanford University.[^cite_note-145] Life with PlayStation also consisted of a 3D virtual view of the Earth and contained current weather and news information of various cities and countries from around the world, as well as a [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage) channel which offered information about historical sites, and the United Village channel which is a project designed to share information about communities and cultures worldwide.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] As of PlayStation 3 system software update version 4.30 on October 24, 2012, the Life With PlayStation project has ended.

### PlayStation Plus

Main article: [PlayStation Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network#PlayStation_Plus)

PlayStation Plus, a subscription-based service on the [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network), complements the standard PSN services.[^cite_note-148] It enables an auto-download feature for game patches and system software updates. Subscribers gain early or exclusive access to some [betas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betaware), game demos, premium downloadable content (such as full game trials of retail games like *[Infamous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infamous_\(video_game\))*, and *[LittleBigPlanet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LittleBigPlanet)*) and other [PlayStation Store](9783860.md) items, as well as a free subscription to [Qore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qore_\(PlayStation_Network\)). Other downloadable items include PlayStation Store discounts and free PlayStation Network games, PS one Classics, PlayStation Minis, themes and avatars.[^cite_note-149] It offers a 14-day free trial.

### PlayStation Blog

PlayStation Blog (stylized as PlayStation.Blog) is an online PlayStation-focused gaming [blog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blog), part of the [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network). It was launched on June 11, 2007[^cite_note-150] and has featured in numerous interviews with third-party companies such as [Square Enix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Enix).[^cite_note-151] It features posts from high-ranking [Sony Interactive Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Interactive_Entertainment) executives.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] A sub-site of the blog called PlayStation Blog Share was launched on March 17, 2010, and allowed readers of the blog as well as users of the PlayStation Blog to submit ideas to the PlayStation team about anything PlayStation-related and vote on the ideas of other submissions.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] [Sony Computer Entertainment Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment_Europe) launched a European sub-outlet, PlayStation Blog Europe, on May 28, 2009, to replace the "semi-official" site Three Speech that shut down on April 17.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] This branch was merged into the main outlet on June 1, 2020.[^cite_note-158]

### PlayStation App

Main article: [PlayStation App](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_App)

The PlayStation App is an application that was released on January 11, 2011, in several European countries for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_\(Apple\)) (version 4 and above) and for [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) (version 1.6 and above),[^cite_note-PSNAppAnnouncement-159] and has been installed more than 3.6 million times as of March 2, 2014.[^cite_note-160] It allows users to view their trophies, see which of their PSN friends are online and read up to date information about PlayStation.[^cite_note-PSNAppAnnouncement-159] It does not feature any gaming functionality.[^cite_note-PSNAppAnnouncement-159]

### PlayStation Mobile

Main article: [PlayStation Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Mobile)

The PlayStation Mobile (formerly *PlayStation Suite*) was a software framework that was used to provide downloadable PlayStation content to devices running [Android 2.3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) and above as well as the [PlayStation Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vita). As such, it was a cross-platform and cross-device framework, which Sony dubbed "hardware-neutral". It was set to release before the end of calendar year 2011, and released on October 3, 2012. In addition, Android devices that had the ability to playback PlayStation Suite content smoothly was certified with the [PlayStation Certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Certified) certification.[^cite_note-KotakuJan27-161] The PlayStation Mobile program did not gain much traction,[^cite_note-162] and after depreciating Android support[^cite_note-163] was discontinued after 2015.[^cite_note-164]

### PlayStation Now

Main article: [PlayStation Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Now)

PlayStation Now (PS Now) was a Gaikai-based video game streaming service used to provide PlayStation gaming content to [PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3) (PS3), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4) (PS4), [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5) (PS5), [PlayStation Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vita), [PlayStation TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_TV) and [BRAVIA televisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bravia_\(brand\)).[^cite_note-165] The service allowed users to pay for access to a selection of original PlayStation 3 titles on either a per-game basis or via a subscription. PlayStation Now was announced on January 7, 2014, at the 2014 [Consumer Electronic Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Electronic_Show). At CES, Sony presented demos of The Last of Us, God of War: Ascension, Puppeteer and Beyond: Two Souls, playable through PS Now on Bravia TVs and PlayStation Vitas. PlayStation Now was launched in Open Beta in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Canada](5042916.md) on PS4 on July 31, 2014, on PS3 on September 18, 2014, on PS Vita and PS TV on October 14, 2014, with support for select 2014 Bravia TVs coming later in the year.[^cite_note-Wikipedia-166] It was merged into [PlayStation Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Plus) in May and June 2022, and is no longer available as a standalone subscription.[^cite_note-167]

### Online social networking services

#### PlayStation Home

Main article: [PlayStation Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Home)

PlayStation Home was a community-based social gaming networking service for the PlayStation 3 on the [PlayStation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Network) (PSN). It was available directly from the PlayStation 3 [XrossMediaBar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XrossMediaBar#PlayStation_3_XMB). Membership is free and only requires a PSN account. Home has been in development since early 2005 and started an open public [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_version#Beta) test on December 11, 2008.[^cite_note-Home_Launch-168] Home allows users to create a custom [avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_\(computing\)), which can be made to suit the user's preference.[^cite_note-169] Users can decorate their avatar's personal apartment ("HomeSpace") with default, bought, or won items. They can travel throughout the Home world (except across regions), which is constantly updated by Sony and partners. Each part of the world is known as a space. Public spaces can just be for display, fun, or for meeting people. Home features many mini-games which can be single player or multiplayer. Users can shop for new items to express themselves more through their avatars or HomeSpace.[^cite_note-170] Home features video screens in many places for advertising, but the main video content is shown at the theatre for entertainment. Home plays host to a variety of special events which range from prize-giving events to entertaining events. Users can also use Home to connect with friends and customize content.[^cite_note-Home_Launch-168] *[Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Home#Xi)*, a once notable feature of Home, is the world's first console based [Alternate Reality Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternate_Reality_Game) that took place in secret areas in Home and was created by [nDreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDreams).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

#### Room for PlayStation Portable

Main article: [Room for PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_for_PlayStation_Portable)

"Room" (officially spelled as *R∞M* with capital letters and the [infinity symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_symbol) in place of the "oo") was being beta tested in Japan from October 2009 to April 2010. Development of Room was halted on April 15, 2010, due to negative feedback from the community.[^cite_note-173] Announced at [TGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Game_Show) 2009, it was supposed to be a similar service to the PlayStation Home and was being developed for the PSP.[^cite_note-174] Launching directly from the PlayStation Network section of the XMB was also to be enabled. Just like in Home, PSP owners would have been able to invite other PSP owners into their rooms to "enjoy real time communication."[^cite_note-175] A closed beta test had begun in Q4 2009 in Japan.[^cite_note-176]

### Linux operating systems

#### Linux for PlayStation 2

Main article: [Linux for PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_for_PlayStation_2)

In 2002, Sony released the first useful and fully functioning [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) for a video game console, after the [Net Yaroze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_Yaroze) experiment for the original PlayStation. The kit, which included an internal hard disk drive and the necessary software tools, turned the [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2) into a full-fledged [computer system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_system) running [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux). Users can utilize a network adapter to connect the PlayStation 2 to the internet, a monitor cable adaptor to connect the PlayStation 2 to computer monitors as well as a USB Keyboard and Mouse which can be used to control Linux on the PlayStation 2.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

#### Linux for PlayStation 3

Main article: [OtherOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OtherOS)

The PlayStation 3 (excluding PlayStation 3 Slim) also supports running Linux OS on firmware versions before 3.21 without the need to buy additional hardware. [Yellow Dog Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Dog_Linux) provides an official distribution that can be downloaded, and other distributions such as [Fedora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedora_\(operating_system\)), [Gentoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentoo_Linux) and [Ubuntu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuntu_\(operating_system\)) have been successfully installed and operated on the console.[^cite_note-ArsTechnicaInterview-30] The use of Linux on the PlayStation 3 allowed users to access 6 of the 7 [Synergistic Processing Elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergistic_Processing_Unit); Sony implemented a [hypervisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypervisor) restricting access to the [RSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSX_'Reality_Synthesizer'). The feature to install a second operating system on a PlayStation 3 was removed in a firmware update released in 2010.[^cite_note-185]

## Magazines

Historically, the PlayStation brand was supported by a global network of officially licensed print publications that operated in close cooperation with Sony. These magazines served as primary information hubs for the gaming community, featuring industry news, upcoming game previews, exclusive developer interviews, and comprehensive reviews, along with gameplay strategies and cheat codes. They also frequently included promotional demo discs for upcoming PlayStation games. Over time, changing media consumption habits led to the cessation of these print titles. Readers increasingly migrated to specialized [video game news websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_game_websites) for immediate coverage, while Sony simultaneously shifted its marketing and community engagement to digital platforms like the [PlayStation Blog](#PlayStation_Blog) and [State of Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Play_\(video_program\)) broadcasts.[^cite_note-186] By the early 2020s, all official PlayStation print publications had ceased operations.[^cite_note-187]

The brand's history features numerous defunct publications. In the United Kingdom, the original *[Official UK PlayStation Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Official_Magazine_%E2%80%93_UK#Official_UK_PlayStation_Magazine)* covered the first-generation console from 1995 to 2004, while the *[Official UK PlayStation 2 Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_UK_PlayStation_2_Magazine)* focused on its successor from 2000 until its 100th and final issue in July 2008. *[PlayStation Official Magazine – UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Official_Magazine_%E2%80%93_UK)* and *[PlayStation Official Magazine – Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Official_Magazine_%E2%80%93_Australia)* both concluded publication in 2021 after publisher [Future plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_plc) chose not to renew the official licensing agreement with Sony.[^cite_note-188] Following the closure of these official publications, Future plc launched *[Play Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_\(UK_magazine\))* (stylized as *PLAY*) as an independent, unofficial successor to continue coverage of PlayStation consoles, which was distributed in both the United Kingdom and Australia (as *PLAY Australia*) until the magazine itself ceased publication in late 2024.[^cite_note-189] Sony also expanded coverage to the Irish market with the *[Official PlayStation Magazine – Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_PlayStation_Magazine_%E2%80%93_Ireland)* in 2000, which ran until its closure in 2001.

Regional variations were established across other global markets, featuring dedicated European editions such as France's *[PlayStation Le Magazine Officiel](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation,%20le%20magazine%20officiel)* and *PlayStation 2 Le Magazine Officiel*, Germany's *Das Offizielle PlayStation Magazin* and *Das Offizielle PlayStation 2 Magazin*, Italy's *[PlayStation Magazine Ufficiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Magazine_Ufficiale)* and *PlayStation 2 Magazine Ufficiale*, Spain's *PlayStation Revista Oficial - España* and *PlayStation 2 Revista Oficial - España*, Portugal's *Revista Oficial Playstation – Edição Portuguesa* and *PlayStation 2 Revista Oficial - Portugal*, the Netherlands and Belgium's *Officieel PlayStation Magazine – Benelux*, the Netherlands *[Officieel PlayStation Magazine](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officieel%20PlayStation%20Magazine)* and *PlayStation 2 Officieel Nederlandstalig Magazine*, Czechia's *Oficiální Český PlayStation magazín* and *PlayStation 2 Oficiální Magazín - CZ*, Poland's *Oficjalny Polski PlayStation Magazyn* and *PlayStation 2 Oficjalny Polski Magazyn*, Hungary's *Hivatalos Magyar PlayStation Magazin*, Bulgaria's *PlayStation Ofitsialnoto Bŭlgarsko Izdanie*, Greece's *Official PlayStation Magazine – GR* and *PlayStation 2 Official Magazine - GR*, Denmark's *Officielt Dansk PlayStation Magasin* and *PlayStation 2 Officielt Dansk Magasin*, Norway's *Offisielt Norsk PlayStation Magasin* and *PlayStation 2 Offisielt Norsk Magasin*, Sweden's *[Svenska PlayStationmagasinet](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svenska%20Playstationmagasinet)*, Finland's *[Virallinen PlayStation-lehti](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virallinen%20PlayStation-lehti)*, Turkey's *PlayStation Official Magazine - TR* and *Resmi PlayStation 2 Dergisi*, and Russia's *Official PlayStation Magazine Rossiya* and *PlayStation 2 Official Magazine Rossiya*.

Beyond Europe, localized official editions were established in major console markets, Brazil's *[Playstation Revista Oficial - Brasil](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playstation%20Revista%20Oficial%20-%20Brasil)* concluded its 300-issue run in January 2023. Japan's prominent, long-running, and officially licensed publication *[Dengeki PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dengeki_PlayStation)* ceased regular print operations in March 2020, after 25 years of publication and 686 issues.[^cite_note-190]

The *[Official U.S. PlayStation Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_U.S._PlayStation_Magazine)* operated for 112 issues between 1997 and 2007. Following its discontinuation, Sony officially licensed the independent magazine *[PSM: 100% Independent PlayStation Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation:_The_Official_Magazine#PSM:_100%_Independent_PlayStation_Magazine)*, which rebranded as *[PlayStation: The Official Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation:_The_Official_Magazine)* (US) in late 2007 and published its final issue in December 2012.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]

*[PlayStation Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Underground)* was a non-traditional magazine that [Sony Computer Entertainment America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment) produced and published between Spring 1997 to Spring 2001 in the United States. Subscribers received two PlayStation CDs, along with a booklet and colorful packaging every quarter.[^cite_note-193] The CDs contained interviews, cheats, programmers moves, game demos and one-of-a-kind Memory Card saves. Several issues showed how a game was created from basic design to final product. Since the CDs could only be run on a PlayStation, it proved a useful marketing tool which spawned a line of *PlayStation Underground JamPacks* Demo CDs and which contained highlights from recent issues of *PlayStation Underground*, along with seemingly as many game demos that could be packed on a single CD. Unlike *PlayStation Underground* these were available in most stores for \$4.95, were published twice a year in Summer and Winter and usually spotlighted newly released or coming soon games. By 2001, Sony had decided to phase out *Underground* to focus on the *JamPacks* with the release of the PlayStation 2. *PlayStation Underground* CDs are mainly in the hands of collectors these days.[^cite_note-194]

### Notable advertising campaigns

#### It Only Does Everything

Main article: [Kevin Butler (character)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Butler_\(character\))

The most notable of recent PlayStation commercials is the series of "It Only Does Everything" commercials featuring a fictional character called Kevin Butler who is a vice president at PlayStation. These commercials usually advertise the PlayStation 3 and its games through a series of comedic answers to "Dear PlayStation" queries.[^cite_note-First_Commercial-205] These commercials garnered popularity among gamers, though its debut commercial received criticism from the Nigerian government due to a reference to the common [419 scams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advance-fee_fraud) originating in Nigeria. Sony issued an apology and a new version of the advert with the offending line changed was produced.[^cite_note-214]

A spin-off of the campaign has been created for the PlayStation Portable which features similar campaign commercials called the "Step Your Game Up" campaign featuring a fictional teenage character named [Marcus Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Rivers) acting similarly to Kevin Butler but answering the "Dear PlayStation" queries about the PSP.[^cite_note-Step_Your_Game_Up-202]

#### Netherlands Ceramic White PSP Commercials

In July 2006, an advertising campaign in the [Netherlands](21148.md) was released in which a [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people) model dressed entirely in white and a [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) model dressed entirely in black was used to compare Sony's new Ceramic White PSP and the original Piano Black PSP. This series of ads depicted both models fighting with each other[^cite_note-215] and drew criticism from the media for being racist, though Sony maintains that the ad did not feature any racist message.[^cite_note-216]

#### All I want for Xmas is a PSP

In November 2006, a marketing company employed by Sony's American division created a website entitled "*All I want for Xmas is a PSP*", designed to promote the [PSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable) [virally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_Marketing). The site contained a blog which was purportedly written by "Charlie", a teenage boy attempting to get his friend Jeremy's parents to buy him a PSP, and providing a "music video" of either Charlie or Jeremy "rapping" about the PSP. Visitors to the website quickly recognized that the domain name was registered to a marketing company, exposing the campaign on sites such as [YouTube](3524766.md) and [digg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digg). Sony was forced to admit that the site was in fact a marketing campaign and in an interview with next-gen.biz, Sony admitted that the idea was "*poorly executed*".[^cite_note-217]

### Sponsorship

Between 1999 and 2007 and 2009 to 2010, PlayStation was the main kit sponsor of French [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [AJ Auxerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AJ_Auxerre) and [Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais), respectively.

PlayStation had been an official sponsor of [WWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE) pay-per-view events in the past, including [In Your House: Buried Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Your_House_11:_Buried_Alive), [WrestleMania 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_13), [Badd Blood: In Your House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badd_Blood:_In_Your_House) and [InVasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InVasion), while the PlayStation 2 had sponsored 3 consecutive [Royal Rumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Rumble) events (2003–2005), [Armageddon 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armageddon_\(2004\)), and [No Mercy 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Mercy_\(2005\)).

Between 2016 and 2022, PlayStation was the sponsor of the [Fiesta Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_Bowl).[^cite_note-218]

## Reception

In 2005, Australian newspaper *[The Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age)* wrote an article about the PlayStation brand. Among the numerous interviews conducted with various people in the industry was an interview with Jeffrey Brand, associate professor in communication and media at [Bond University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_University) who said, "PlayStation re-ignited our imagination with video games". Game designer [Yoshiki Okamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshiki_Okamoto) called the brand "revolutionary — PlayStation has changed gaming, distribution, sales, image and more", while Evan Wells of [Naughty Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naughty_Dog) said "PlayStation is responsible for making playing games cool."[^cite_note-219]

In 2009, [ViTrue, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ViTrue,_Inc.) listed the PlayStation brand as number 13 on their "The Vitrue 100: Top Social Brands of 2009". The ranking was based on various aspects mainly dealing with popular social media sites in aspects such as Social Networking, Video Sharing, Photo Sharing and Blogs.[^cite_note-220]

In 2010, *[Gizmodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gizmodo)* stated that the PlayStation brand was one of the last Sony products to completely stand apart from its competitors, stating that "If you ask the average person on the street what their favorite Sony product is, more often than not you'll hear PlayStation".[^cite_note-221] As of April 2012, the PlayStation brand is the "most followed" brand on social networking site, [Facebook](7529378.md), with over 22 million fans and followers in total which is more than any other brand in the entertainment industry. A study by Greenlight's Entertainment Retail has also shown that the PlayStation brand is the most interactive making 634 posts and tweets on social networking sites Facebook and [Twitter](9988187.md).[^cite_note-222]

In July 2014, Sony boasted in a company release video that the PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4 and PlayStation Vita sold a combined total of 100 million units.[^cite_note-223] It was announced at [Tokyo Game Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Game_Show) on September 1, 2014, that PlayStation home game consoles claim 78% market share of all home consoles in Japan.[^cite_note-224]

As of 2015, PlayStation is the best-selling home console brand worldwide.[^cite_note-225]
