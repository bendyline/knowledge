# Death and funeral of Alexei Navalny

This article is about the death of Russian opposition activist [Alexei Navalny](29174356.md). For his attempted killing in 2020, see [Poisoning of Alexei Navalny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisoning_of_Alexei_Navalny).

On 16 February 2024, at 14\:19 [Moscow time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_time) (11\:19 GMT), the Russian [Federal Penitentiary Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Penitentiary_Service) (FSIN) of the [Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](482044.md) announced that [Russian opposition](38212364.md) activist and political prisoner [Alexei Navalny](29174356.md) died while serving a 19-year prison sentence in [FKU IK-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FKU_IK-3), a [corrective colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrective_labor_colony) in the village of [Kharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharp) in the [Russian Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_\(Russia\)).[^cite_note-fsin-3][^cite_note-bbc-4][^cite_note-5] Navalny's spokeswoman, [Kira Yarmysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kira_Yarmysh), confirmed his death the next day and demanded his body be returned to his family as soon as possible.[^cite_note-6] The official cause of death per the Russian government was an episode of [high blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_crisis) brought about by a chronic [heart arrythmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrythmia),[^cite_note-theinsru-1] but this diagnosis has been disputed by Navalny's widow [Yulia Navalnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Navalnaya).[^cite_note-\:9-7] Navalny was 47 years old when he died.[^cite_note-bbc-4]

Navalny's death spurred numerous protests and gatherings in different countries, including Russia, where hundreds of mourners were detained.[^cite_note-reutersprotests-8][^cite_note-AJdetained-9] Western officials and Russian opposition activists held Russian authorities, including President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), responsible for his death.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:4-11] US intelligence agencies later concluded that Putin likely did not order Navalny's death.[^cite_note-wsj-20240427-12] Navalny's funeral was held in [Moscow](19004.md) on 1 March 2024, at the Church of the Icon of the Mother of God Soothe My Sorrows in the [Maryino District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryino_District). He was buried in the Borisovskoye сemetery in the [Brateyevo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brateyevo_District).[^cite_note-apfuneral-13][^cite_note-14]

In February 2026, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Sweden, and the Netherlands released a joint statement saying that Navalny was killed by [epibatidine](2103074.md), a deadly toxin found in [poison dart frogs](812186.md), citing tests on samples from his body that "conclusively" confirmed its presence.[^cite_note-15] According to [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News), the toxin was likely made in a laboratory.[^cite_note-auto1-16][^cite_note-auto-17]

## Background

### Assassination attempt

Main article: [Poisoning of Alexei Navalny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisoning_of_Alexei_Navalny)

Alexei Navalny was considered one of the most prominent critics of Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), having denounced [corruption](27352290.md) under [his regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_under_Vladimir_Putin) and unsuccessfully trying to run for president against him in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Russian_presidential_election). In 2017, Navalny suffered eye injuries after being assaulted with a [green disinfectant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliant_green_\(dye\)) by an unknown assailant. In 2020, Navalny [was poisoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisoning_of_Alexei_Navalny) with a [Novichok](1766982.md) nerve agent and was evacuated to Germany for medical treatment.[^cite_note-bbc-4][^cite_note-\:5-18]

In 2021, before returning to Russia, Navalny took part in the filming of the [Oscar-winning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Academy_Awards) documentary *[Navalny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navalny_\(film\))*. In the film's final sequence, he urged Russians not to give up if he was killed, saying that "this means that we are unusually strong at this moment, since they decided to kill me".[^cite_note-19]

### Imprisonment

In January 2021, US President [Joe Biden](145422.md) warned Putin that Navalny's arrest and possible death would bring "devastating" consequences to Russia.[^cite_note-20] He was [imprisoned](29174356.md#Imprisonment) based on charges of fraud, extremism and violating probation.[^cite_note-21] The [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) ruled on 16 February 2021 that the Russian government should release Navalny immediately, with the court saying that the resolution was made in "regard to the nature and extent of risk to the applicant's life". In December 2020, a series of laws were also passed that gave the [Russian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Constitution) precedence over rulings made by international bodies as well as international treaties.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] A few days later, a Moscow court rejected Navalny's appeal and upheld his prison sentence, however it reduced his sentence by six weeks after deciding to count his time under house arrest as part of his time served. Another court convicted Navalny on slander charges against a [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_in_World_War_II) veteran, fining him 850,000 rubles (\$11,500).[^cite_note-26]



Protest in support of Navalny in Moscow, 21 April 2021

During his imprisonment, Navalny struggled with health issues and did not consistently receive medical care.[^cite_note-27]

> A 2.5×3 meter concrete doghouse. Most of the time it is unbearable there because it is cold and damp. Water on the floor. I have a beach version - very hot and almost no air. The window is tiny, because of the thickness of the walls air does not go - even the cobwebs do not move. There is no ventilation. At night you lie there and feel like a fish on the shore. The iron bunk is fastened to the wall. The handle that lowers it is on the outside. At 5 a.m., they take away your mattress and pillow and raise the bunk. At 9 p.m. they lower the bunk again and give you back the mattress. Iron table, iron bench, sink, hole in the floor. There are two cameras under the ceiling.

— Alexei Navalny on the conditions of detention in solitary confinement

[^cite_note-28]

In December 2023 he was transferred from a penal colony east of Moscow to the [Polar Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FKU_IK-3,_Kharp) colony in [Kharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharp), [Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](482044.md), in Russia's [Far North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_\(Russia\)). The Polar Wolf colony has more harsh conditions, including reported torture of inmates, and according to *Novaya Gazeta* is reserved for "especially dangerous repeat offenders".[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] On 15 February, the day before his death, he appeared via [video link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_link) at a court hearing, during which he made jokes and seemed to be in good health. At the time of his death, Navalny was serving a 19-year sentence,[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-bbc-4] and was in solitary confinement for the 27th time, having spent a cumulative 300 days in solitary confinement over the course of his sentence.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Death

On 16 February, the [Federal Penitentiary Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Penitentiary_Service) department for the [Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](482044.md) published a statement that Navalny lost consciousness after a walk. He was attended by medical workers from the penal colony and an emergency medical team was called.[^cite_note-fsin-3][^cite_note-bbc-4] Referring to a statement by representatives of the [Labytnangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labytnangi) City Hospital, several Russian state-owned news agencies reported that an ambulance arrived at the scene in less than seven minutes and performed resuscitation measures for more than half an hour.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-meduza_q-35] Navalny was not revived,[^cite_note-fsin-3] and he was officially reported dead at 14\:17 [Yekaterinburg Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg_Time)[^cite_note-36] with reports of his death first appearing in the media at 16\:19 Yekaterinburg Time (14\:19 MSK).[^cite_note-37] His death was confirmed the next day by his spokeswoman Kira Yarmysh, citing an official notice delivered to his mother, Lyudmila. Yarmysh also called for his remains to be returned to his family.[^cite_note-38]

An inmate of the Polar Wolf colony noted highly unusual activity at the prison on the evening of 15 February, which seemed to indicate a surprise prison inspection.[^cite_note-Meduza_inmate_Navalnys_prison-39] He stated that the prisoners were locked in their barracks and a search was conducted on the morning of 16 February. The prisoner stated that they were informed about Navalny's death at 10\:00, well before the press release on Navalny's death. The prisoner also noted that first aid had only appeared after Navalny was already known to be dead. The prisoner's interpretation of the events was that Navalny had likely died on the evening of 15 February, and that it had been a surprise to prison authorities.[^cite_note-Meduza_inmate_Navalnys_prison-39][^cite_note-40] According to human rights group [Gulagu.net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulagu.net), an official report states that several cameras in the penal colony were inactive on 16 February. Gulagu.net interpreted this as the result of [FSB](385990.md) officers arriving at the prison on 14 February and disabling audio and video monitoring equipment. Gulagu.net stated that there were bruises on Navalny's body, which the medical examiner carrying out an autopsy on the body was told to attribute as having occurred post-mortem.[^cite_note-41]

### Investigation into death and release of remains

The [Investigative Committee of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) announced "a set of investigative and operative measures" into Navalny's death.[^cite_note-yulia-42] The Polar Wolf penal colony claimed that it had sent Navalny's body to the morgue in [Salekhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salekhard), but it was not found there.[^cite_note-43] The Investigative Committee of Russia informed the family that the body would be given to them after the cause of death was determined through an investigation; it had previously told them the investigation was complete.[^cite_note-44] The authorities legally can hold his body for up to 30 days.[^cite_note-45] Yarmysh alleged that the Russian authorities were trying to cover up the truth of his death.[^cite_note-46]

According to *Novaya Gazeta*, the body was first taken to [Labytnangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labytnangi), then to a clinical morgue in Salekhard on the evening of the 16th. His body had bruises consistent with chest compressions, indicating that it was likely attempts were made to resuscitate Navalny.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The independent Russian news outlet [Mediazona](52026388.md) reported that live cam footage recorded a FSIN convoy travelling from Labytnangi to Salekhard on the night of the 16th, and suggested that this convoy was carrying Navalny's body.[^cite_note-49] On the morning of 19 February, Navalny's mother, Lyudmila Navalnaya, and his lawyers attempted to access the morgue where his body is alleged to be located, and were not given access.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The Investigative Committee then informed Navalny's team that his remains were sent for a "chemical examination" and would not be returned to the family for another fourteen days.[^cite_note-53] Navalny's wife, Yulia, expressed her belief that authorities were withholding his body while waiting for traces of Novichok to disappear.[^cite_note-54]

On 22 February, Lyudmila Navalnaya released a video message stating that investigators had allowed her to see the body of her son and that she had signed a death certificate that stated that her son had died of natural causes.[^cite_note-55] By law, Navalny's body should have been turned over to his family, but according to Navalnaya, the investigators refused to do so, instead insisting that Navalny be buried in secret[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] or inside the prison grounds if Navalnaya did not agree within several hours.[^cite_note-58] Navalny's remains were finally returned to his mother on 24 February.[^cite_note-59]

On 21 March, a court in Labytnangi rejected a lawsuit filed by Lyudmila Navalnaya accusing authorities of providing inadequate medical care to her son in prison, with Navalny associate [Ivan Zhdanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Zhdanov) saying that the court ruled that only Alexei Navalny could file the lawsuit, and Yulia Navalnaya saying that the court rejected the lawsuit due to concerns over the release of information regarding his death.[^cite_note-60]

#### Potential cause

Alexander Polupan, a doctor who treated Navalny's earlier poisoning, questioned the rapid timing of medical care, noted that a detached blood clot (a possible cause of death claimed by Russian state media) cannot be verified without an autopsy,[^cite_note-61] and said Navalny had no underlying conditions that would put him at risk of a [thromboembolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thromboembolism).[^cite_note-62] His mother was told that he had died from "sudden death syndrome" (an umbrella term for different cardiac syndromes that cause cardiac arrest); his lawyer was told that the cause of death was still unclear.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

According to official documents obtained by *[The Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Insider_\(website\))*, an early version of a report prepared by [Investigative Committee of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) mentioned [abdominal pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_pain), [vomiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vomiting), and [convulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convulsions) as Navalny's symptoms, which were confirmed by medical experts as clearly indicative of poisoning. However, in the final version of the same document all mention of Navalny's abdominal pain, vomiting, and convulsions was removed, while an inventory of objects taken from the scene of Navalny's death still listed "samples of vomit", which according to the document were submitted for examination, despite the fact that neither the fact of the examination nor Navalny's vomiting had ever been officially reported.[^cite_note-65]

In February 2026, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Sweden, and the Netherlands released a joint statement saying that Navalny was killed by [epibatidine](2103074.md), a deadly toxin found in [poison dart frogs](812186.md), citing tests on samples from his body that "conclusively" confirmed its presence.[^cite_note-66] According to [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News), the toxin was likely made in a laboratory.[^cite_note-auto1-16][^cite_note-auto-17]

### Official cause

On 26 July 2024, the [Investigative Committee of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) concluded that Navalny's death "does not have a criminal nature" and was the result of a "combined disease", which included a number of diagnoses: [cholecystitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholecystitis), [pancreatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreatitis), [intervertebral hernia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernia) and others. The judgement states that the death "has an [arrhythmogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia) character" and the trigger factor was "a critical [increase in blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_crisis)".[^cite_note-theinsru-1]

[Yulia Navalnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Navalnaya) reacted to the official conclusion of death:

> We know very well that when Alexei felt ill, he was taken not to the medical centre, but back to the solitary confinement cell. That he died there, alone. That he was taken to the infirmary unconscious. That in the last minutes before his death he complained of a sharp pain in his stomach.

According to Navalnaya, her husband did not have any heart disease while alive.[^cite_note-\:9-7]

### Culpability

On 27 April, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that US intelligence agencies had concluded that Putin likely did not order Navalny's death. The assessment, which US intelligence says does not absolve Putin of culpability, was shared by the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md), the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Director_of_National_Intelligence), and the [Department of State](31975.md). The *Journal* noted that some European agencies were skeptical of the US assessment, finding it "doubtful that harm could have come to Navalny without the president's prior awareness" in [Putin's Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_under_Vladimir_Putin).[^cite_note-wsj-20240427-12][^cite_note-politico-20240427-67]

## Funeral



Hearse with Navalny's body on March 1, 2024

On 27 February, Kira Yarmysh said that they were trying to find a place to hold a public memorial for Navalny, adding that most funeral locations they had contacted had refused to allow a ceremony on their premises. On the same day, Vasily Dubkov, one of Navalny's lawyers who accompanied Lyudmila Navalnaya in retrieving her son's remains in Yamalo-Nenets, was reportedly arrested in Moscow on charges of "violating public order".[^cite_note-68] On 28 February, Yarmysh announced that Navalny would be buried in the afternoon of 1 March at the [Borisovskoye Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borisovskoye_Cemetery), following a service at the Church of the Icon of the Mother of God Soothe My Sorrows in Moscow's [Maryino District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryino_District), where Navalny previously resided.[^cite_note-69] [Ivan Zhdanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Zhdanov), a director of the Anti-Corruption Foundation, said that the funeral had initially been scheduled on 29 February but was moved after no venue agreed to hold it on that day, which coincided with Putin's annual [Presidential Address to the Federal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Address_to_the_Federal_Assembly). He added that Navalny's relatives were being pressured by authorities to conduct a "quiet family funeral."[^cite_note-apfuneral-13]

People attending Alexei Navalny's funeral chant his name, March 1, 2024



Alexei Navalny's grave, March 3, 2024

Navalny's funeral proceeded on 1 March at the Mother of God Soothe My Sorrows church, and was attended by his parents, US ambassador to Russia [Lynne M. Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynne_M._Tracy), the ambassadors of Germany and France,[^cite_note-bbcfuneral-70] several other Western diplomats, disqualified opposition presidential candidates [Boris Nadezhdin](71749458.md) and [Yekaterina Duntsova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterina_Duntsova), and former mayor of [Yekaterinburg](66524.md) [Yevgeny Roizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Roizman). The ceremony, which was streamed live on his YouTube channel, was conducted under a heavy police presence and crowd control barriers were erected around the church. Ivan Zhdanov accused the morgue where Navalny's remains were held of delaying his release, while Yulia Navalnaya accused Putin and Moscow mayor [Sergei Sobyanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Sobyanin) of trying to block a public funeral. Yarmysh called on Navalny's supporters overseas to lay flowers in his memory at his funeral.[^cite_note-apfuneral-13]

"[My Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Way)" by [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra) and the [theme music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_2:_Judgment_Day_\(score\)) from *[Terminator 2: Judgment Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_2:_Judgment_Day)* were played as his body was lowered into the ground.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

Navalny's associates said that queues for the funeral reached more than a kilometer[^cite_note-bbcfuneral-70] with thousands in attendance, with some chanting anti-war and anti-Putin slogans, before police closed off the Borisovskoye cemetery at 22\:00, six hours after Navalny was laid to rest. The Russian human rights group [OVD-Info](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OVD-Info) said that 128 people were arrested in 19 cities across the country for participating in memorial events on his funeral,[^cite_note-74] most of whom were trying to lay flowers at monuments to victims of Soviet repression.[^cite_note-apfuneral-13] Police continued to be deployed around the cemetery on 2 March, when Lyudmila Navalnaya and Yulia Navalnaya's mother Alla Abrosimova visited to lay flowers at Navalny's grave.[^cite_note-75]

A memorial service to mark 40 days since Navalny's death was held on 26 March and was officiated by Dmitry Safronov, a priest who had signed a public petition to return Navalny's remains to his family. He was demoted into a psalm-reader on 23 April by the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) and was banned from giving blessings and wearing a cassock for three years and was ordered transferred from his parish.[^cite_note-76]

Navalny's widow [Yulia Navalnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Navalnaya) said all those responsible for the death of her husband "will be held accountable".[^cite_note-yulia-42] In a video recorded 19 February 2024, Yulia announced that she would continue the work her husband had started, and stated "By killing Alexei, Putin killed half of me — half of my heart and half of my soul. But I still have the other half left, and it tells me that I have no right to give up...Fight, and don't give up. I am not afraid, and you should not be afraid either."[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Navalnaya called for the EU to recognize the results of the [2024 Russian presidential election](56748201.md) as illegitimate, stating "A president who assassinated his main political opponent cannot be legitimate by definition."[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

Navalny's mother, Lyudmila, said she did not "want to hear any words of sympathy", adding that he was "alive, healthy and cheerful" four days prior to his death.[^cite_note-moscowtimesreactions-81] [Maria Pevchikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Pevchikh), the head of the board of the [Anti-Corruption Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Corruption_Foundation) founded by Navalny, said that he would "live on forever in millions of hearts," and asserted that he was murdered.[^cite_note-82] She later alleged that such an event was part of a plot by Putin to thwart the release of Navalny and two American citizens in an exchange with [Federal Security Service](385990.md)'s operative [Vadim Krasikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Krasikov), who assassinated former Chechen commander [Zelimkhan Khangoshvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelimkhan_Khangoshvili) in Germany in 2019.[^cite_note-83] Two Western officials who spoke to *[Politico](9172097.md)* confirmed that the United States and Germany were in preliminary discussions about creating an exchange, but said that no offer was sent to Russia while declining to comment whether Krasikov was part of the discussion.[^cite_note-84]

Opposition politician [Boris Nadezhdin](71749458.md), who unsuccessfully attempted to file his candidacy for the [2024 Russian presidential election](56748201.md), called Navalny "one of the most talented and bravest people in Russia I ever knew."[^cite_note-85] [Mikhail Khodorkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khodorkovsky), a former oligarch in exile in London, urged Russians to vote for Navalny as a [write-in candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write-in_candidate) in the election as a mark of protest. He also called for Western nations to declare Putin's government, the presidential election, and its result illegitimate.[^cite_note-tributesng-86][^cite_note-87] [Leonid Volkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Volkov_\(politician\)), a [Russian opposition](38212364.md) politician living in Lithuania, stated: "If this is true, then not 'Navalny died,' but 'Putin killed Navalny' and only that."[^cite_note-moscowtimesreactions-81] Opposition politician [Dmitry Gudkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Gudkov) said: "Even if Alexei died from 'natural' causes, they were caused by his poisoning and further prison torture. The blood is on Putin's hands."[^cite_note-moscowtimesreactions-81]

[Dmitry Muratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Muratov), a [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) laureate and editor-in-chief of the Latvia-based *[Novaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Gazeta)* newspaper, expressed his condolences to Navalny's family. Muratov called the death [murder](20171.md), adding that Navalny "was tortured and tormented for three years. As Navalny's doctor told me: the body cannot endure such things. Murder was added to Alexei Navalny's sentence."[^cite_note-lemonde-navalny-88] Human rights campaigner [Oleg Orlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Orlov), co-chairman of the Nobel Peace Prize-winning human rights group [Memorial](570357.md), said Navalny's death in prison was a "crime of the regime".[^cite_note-89] Writer and journalist [Mikhail Zygar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Zygar) said that Navalny "was our future for so long. Now we don't have that future anymore." Veteran human rights activist [Lev Ponomaryov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Ponomaryov) declared, "There are still many of us. We need to act together." Fiction writer [Boris Akunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Akunin) said, "There is nothing more the dictator \[Putin] can do to Navalny. Navalny is dead and has become immortal."[^cite_note-90] [Ilya Yashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Yashin), a friend of Navalny's and a fellow political prisoner, stated that Navalny "died a hero".[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

One of Navalny's lawyers, Leonid Solovyov, told *Novaya Gazeta* that Navalny was "normal" when a lawyer saw him on 14 January.[^cite_note-lemonde-navalny-88]

Russians began bringing flowers to monuments to victims of [political repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression_in_the_Soviet_Union) in cities across the country.[^cite_note-93] Russian human rights group [OVD-Info](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OVD-Info) initially reported that by 17 February, more than 400 people had been detained by authorities in over 36 cities for taking part in the gatherings; the number was later corrected to 366 people.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-ap1-95] Among those arrested was a priest who intended to hold a mass for Navalny and subsequently suffered a stroke while in police custody.[^cite_note-ap1-95][^cite_note-96] In some cities, flowers were removed and the police took photos of people laying flowers in memory of Navalny.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-meduzaprotests-98] People laid flowers at Moscow's [Solovetsky Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solovetsky_Stone) and the [Wall of Grief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_of_Grief).[^cite_note-99] The Moscow Prosecutor's Office warned Russians against mass protests.[^cite_note-navalny-meduza-100] In Saint Petersburg alone, 154 people were sentenced to 14 days' imprisonment for violating anti-protest laws.[^cite_note-101] At least six of them were reportedly issued military draft notices upon their release.[^cite_note-102] Russian digital map company [2GIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2GIS) reportedly blocked reviews of memorial sites after people used the service to write about memorials for Navalny.[^cite_note-103]

*[Sobesednik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobesednik_\(newspaper\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, the only newspaper within Russia which published a detailed report on Navalny's death, stated that "Russia is a happy country \[because] Navalnys are sometimes born in it", and described him as "the symbol of a peaceful country".[^cite_note-104] Shortly after hitting Moscow newsstands, virtually all copies of the issue with a print circulation of 154,810 were confiscated without any legal justification.[^cite_note-105]

The political party [Civic Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Initiative_\(Russia\)) released a statement calling Navalny's death "a political murder".[^cite_note-106] It also announced plans to organize a march in honor of Navalny and [Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov) in Moscow on 2 March.[^cite_note-107]

According to OVD-Info, over 46,000 people sent appeals to the Investigative Committee of Russia demanding the release of Navalny's remains to his family after the refusal of the Investigative Committee to do so.[^cite_note-108]

The [Russian Volunteer Corps](71652852.md), a Russian anti-Kremlin and far-right armed unit fighting in Ukraine against Russian government forces said that Navalny's death came while they were revising their plans to free him, which had been in place since December and which would have involved taking him to Ukraine.[^cite_note-109]

#### Russian government

Presidential spokesperson [Dmitry Peskov](9214683.md) told reporters that Putin had been informed of Navalny's death,[^cite_note-110] although Putin did not publicly comment on it during a meeting in [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) on the day it was announced.[^cite_note-111] The Directorate of the [Investigative Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) for the [Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](482044.md) organized a procedural investigation into the death.[^cite_note-112] The [Federal Penitentiary Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Penitentiary_Service) also began inspections "in accordance with all applicable rules".[^cite_note-113] Peskov denied accusations from Yulia Navalnaya that Putin had been responsible for her husband's death, calling them "absolutely unfounded",[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] and rejected EU foreign policy chief [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell)'s call for an international investigation into Navalny's death, stating that the Kremlin "does not accept such demands at all".[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] In an interview on Russian state television, [Sergey Naryshkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Naryshkin), the head of the Russian [Foreign Intelligence Service](614404.md) (SVR), also said that Navalny died of natural causes.[^cite_note-119]

The Russian independent media outlet [Agentstvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proekt) reported that within thirty minutes of Navalny's death being announced, the ruling [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) party issued a message to its deputies in the [State Duma](1010187.md) to "keep strictly in line with the Federal Prison Service's version \[or] better to refrain from commenting at all".[^cite_note-120] Russian state-controlled media provided minimal coverage of Navalny's death.[^cite_note-121]

In response to international condemnation over Navalny's death, foreign ministry spokesperson [Maria Zakharova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Zakharova) criticized Western countries for having "its conclusions ready".[^cite_note-122] Peskov himself described foreign leaders' reactions as "absolutely rabid".[^cite_note-United_Nations-Navalny-123] Putin's close associate [Vyacheslav Volodin](32671558.md), Speaker of the State Duma, along with [Vasily Piskaryov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Piskaryov), chairman of the Duma's commission on foreign interference, blamed Navalny's death on "Washington", "[Brussels](9317.md)" and various critics of the Kremlin in "[unfriendly countries](70389552.md)".[^cite_note-navalny-meduza-100][^cite_note-\:3-124] Several other politicians and public figures, including [Tina Kandelaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Kandelaki), [Anton Krasovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Krasovsky), and [Sergei Markov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Markov), blamed Navalny's death on the US or the West.[^cite_note-125] [A Just Russia – For Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Just_Russia_%E2%80%93_For_Truth) leader [Sergey Mironov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Mironov) stated that Navalny's death was beneficial for "Russia's enemies".[^cite_note-\:4-11] [Margarita Simonyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Simonyan), editor-in-chief of the Russian state-owned network [RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT_\(TV_network\)), stated on [Telegram](41501305.md) that "everyone has long forgotten him \[Navalny], that there was no point in killing him" since it was beneficial to "exactly the opposite forces".[^cite_note-\:3-124]

[Vladislav Davankov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladislav_Davankov), deputy chair in the Duma and presidential candidate for the [New People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People_\(political_party\)) party, stated that Navalny's death was a "tragedy".[^cite_note-126]

Shortly following his victory in the [2024 Russian presidential election](56748201.md) on 17 March, Putin made his first direct mention of Navalny in a news conference,[^cite_note-127] saying that he had considered exchanging him for prisoners held in the West on the condition that Navalny would remain abroad.[^cite_note-128] On 18 March however, Dmitry Peskov said that no negotiations had taken place and that the idea was forwarded to Putin by a person that he did not identify.[^cite_note-129]

Direct or indirect accusations against the Russian authorities in connection with Navalny's death have been made by many leaders of [Western countries](21208200.md) and representatives of major international organizations.[^cite_note-130] Leaders of prominent countries in the "[Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South)", along with most [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states) in Central Asia and the Caucasus, did not issue official reactions to the news; nor did president [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) of Turkey, which is the only [NATO member state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_NATO) not designated on Russia's "[unfriendly countries list](70389552.md)".[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-\:2-132] On 4 March, 43 countries called on the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) to conduct an independent investigation into Navalny’s death.[^cite_note-133]

On 18 February, US ambassador to Russia [Lynne Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynne_Tracy) and UK ambassador [Nigel Casey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Casey) publicly laid flowers in honor of Navalny at the Solovetsky Stone in Moscow.[^cite_note-134]

#### Public

[Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono) of [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2) speaking and leading a chant of Navalny's name at his band's concert in [Las Vegas](47737.md) on 17 February

Rallies were held in honor of Navalny in over 25 countries.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-meduzaprotests-98][^cite_note-199] In Turkey, police detained protesters and broke up rallies.[^cite_note-\:2-132] At least eight other instances of rallies for Navalny being broken up were reported in Belarus, Cuba, France, Greece, Italy, Thailand, Uzbekistan, and Vietnam.[^cite_note-200] In [Berlin](3354.md), a march held by [Pussy Riot](34684163.md) members [Nadya Tolokonnikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadya_Tolokonnikova) and [Lucy Shtein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Shtein), as well as opposition politician [Lyubov Sobol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyubov_Sobol) and former Russian state media journalist [Marina Ovsyannikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Ovsyannikova), was prevented by police from reaching the [Brandenburg Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Gate) after they had held a protest at the Russian embassy.[^cite_note-ap1-95]

Mourners laid flowers in Navalny's honor in [Tashkent](57533.md), Uzbekistan, and [Almaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaty), Kazakhstan, where many Russians have [fled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_emigration_following_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) since 2022 to avoid [mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_mobilization) in the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-201]

In Finland, an exhibit dedicated in memory of Navalny and other Russian dissidents was opened at the [Helsinki Central Library Oodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Central_Library_Oodi) on 17 February.[^cite_note-202] A group of Russian residents in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) also organized a petition for a park adjacent to the Russian Embassy to be renamed in honor of Navalny.[^cite_note-ap1-95]

American commentator [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson), who faced criticism for hosting "[The Vladimir Putin Interview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vladimir_Putin_Interview)" several days before Navalny's death, told *[The Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Mail)* and *[The New York Times](30680.md)*: "It's horrifying what happened to Navalny. The whole thing is barbaric and awful. No decent person would defend it."[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204]

On 17 February, Irish singer [Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono) of [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2) led crowds in a chant of Navalny's name at [his band's concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2\:UV_Achtung_Baby_Live_at_Sphere) in [Las Vegas](47737.md), stating "Tonight, the people who believe in freedom must say his name. Not just remember it, but say it."[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206]

On the day of Navalny's funeral on 1 March, a protest and memorial service was held by Russian exiles in Georgia at the Russian embassy in [Tbilisi](42298.md), while in Italy, members of the [Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_\(Italy\)) protested at the Russian embassy in [Rome](25458.md).[^cite_note-apfuneral-13]

Hours following the announcement of Navalny's death, a group of hackers opposed to the Russian government launched a cyberattack on the online shopping service JSC Kaluzhskoe, which provides commissary needs for the Federal Penitentiary Service, defacing the website with messages supporting Navalny and lowering the price of goods sold to relatives of prisoners by inserting unauthorized discounts. The group also said it had gained access to a database containing records and contact details of inmates and their relatives.[^cite_note-207]

#### Sanctions

Ahead of a meeting of EU foreign ministers, German foreign minister [Annalena Baerbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annalena_Baerbock) proposed additional sanctions against Russia as a direct response to Navalny's death. His widow, Yulia Navalnaya, addressed the meeting in Brussels and called for more sanctions directly targeting Putin's inner circle. [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell), the EU foreign policy commissioner, pledged action to hold "Vladimir Putin and his regime" accountable.[^cite_note-208] Borrell also stated that the EU's human rights sanctions list may be renamed after Navalny in a symbolic move of support.[^cite_note-209]

On 21 February, the United Kingdom imposed sanctions on the head of the Polar Wolf colony, Colonel Vadim Kalinin, and five of his deputies.[^cite_note-210] On 23 February, the United States announced sanctions on three Russian officials, including the deputy director of the [Federal Penitentiary Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Penitentiary_Service) who was promoted three days after Navalny's death.[^cite_note-211] On 3 March, Canada announced sanctions on six senior Russian officials working in the prosecutorial, judicial and correctional services for their involvement in Navalny's imprisonment and death.[^cite_note-212] On 22 March, the EU imposed sanctions on 33 Russian officials in the justice and penal systems in connection with Navalny's death, including Kalinin and the management of the [IK-6 corrective colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrective_colony_No._6,_Vladimir_Oblast) in [Vladimir Oblast](463919.md), where Navalny was held before his transfer to the Polar Wolf colony.[^cite_note-213]
