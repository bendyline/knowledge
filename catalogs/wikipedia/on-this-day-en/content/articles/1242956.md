**Gross national income** (**GNI**), formerly known as **gross national product** (**GNP**), is the total amount of [factor incomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_income) earned by the residents of a country. It is equal to [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP), plus factor incomes received from non-residents by residents, minus factor income paid by residents to non-residents.[^cite_note-2]<sup>: 44 </sup>

In contrast to GDP, GNI is not a concept of value added, but a concept of income. GNI is the basis of calculation of the largest part of contributions to the [Budget of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_of_the_European_Union#GNI-based_own_resources).[^cite_note-3] In February 2017, Ireland's GDP became so distorted from the [base erosion and profit shifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_erosion_and_profit_shifting) ("BEPS") tax planning tools of U.S. multinationals, that the [Central Bank of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Ireland) replaced Irish GDP with a new metric, Irish [Modified GNI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_gross_national_income) (or "GNI\*"). In 2017, Irish GDP was 127% of Irish GNI and 162% of Irish Modified GNI.[^cite_note-ber-4]

GNI contrast with [net national income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_national_income) : NNI = GNI - [Depreciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depreciation_\(economics\))[^cite_note-5]

The [Atlas method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_method) can be applied to correct for fluctuating [exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate).[^cite_note-a083-6]

## History and name change

The modern concept of GNP, along with GDP, was first developed by [Simon Kuznets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Kuznets) for a 1934 [U.S. Congress](31756.md) report. Countries like the US and the UK originally preferred GNP as a measure of economic activity while other like Norway preferred GDP.[^cite_note-10] Over time economists settled on using GDP, the US switched to using GDP in 1991.

GNP was defined in the [United Nations](31769.md)' 1953 System of National Accounts (SNA) as:

"the market value of product before deduction of provisions of consumption of fixed capital, attributable to factors of production supplied by normal residents of the given country"

Despite framing GNP as a concept of production, the attribution of the value was defined by the income earned by the owner of the factor of production.

In the 1993 revision to the SNA, the GNP definition was reframed from the point of view of the residents receiving income rather than the point of view of the factor of production. To reflect this, GNP was renamed GNI; the "national" part was kept as it is embedded in economic usage, even though the same concept of residence is used to define both GDP and GNI.[^cite_note-11]

GNP continues to be used in the [National income and product accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Income_and_Product_Accounts) to refer to GNI calculated for expenditure data.

## Type of income included in GNI

GNI includes the [salaries and wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compensation_of_employees) of [cross-border commuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_worker) and seasonal workers working overseas but does not include [remittance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) sent by workers to their families overseas. This explains why [France](5843419.md) GNI is higher than its GDP as a lot of French residents work in [Luxembourg](17515.md), [Monaco](19261.md), or [Switzerland](26748.md). In comparison, [India](14533.md) GNI is lower than its GDP despite being the larger receiver of remittances.

GNI also includes the propriety income: rent, interest, and "profit". The "profit" included both distributed income of corporations (dividends) and reinvested earnings on [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment), those are [profit retained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retained_earnings) by the corporation. Like in the [IMF balance of payments manual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF_Balance_of_Payments_Manual) they are treated as if they were distributed to foreign direct investors in proportion to their ownership of the [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(finance\)) of the enterprise and then reinvested by them using additions in equity.

The GNI of [EU](9317.md) countries also included subsidies received from the EU and excluded [tariffs](55551.md) as the EU receives those.

GNI contrasts with Gross national disposable income which includes all current transfer income like international cooperation and remittance.

## Gross national product

**Gross national product** (**GNP**) is the market value of all the goods and services produced in one year by labor and property supplied by the citizens of a country. Unlike [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP), which defines production based on the geographical location of production, GNP indicates allocated production based on location of ownership. In fact it calculates income by the location of ownership and residence, and so its name is also the less ambiguous *gross national income*.

GNP is an economic statistic that is equal to GDP plus any income earned by residents from overseas investments minus income earned within the domestic economy by [overseas](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/overseas) residents.

GNP does not distinguish between qualitative improvements in the state of the technical arts (e.g., increasing computer processing speeds), and quantitative increases in goods (e.g., number of computers produced), and considers both to be forms of "[economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth)".[^cite_note-13]

When a country's capital or labour resources are employed outside its borders, or when a foreign firm is operating in its territory, GDP and GNP can produce different measures of total output. In 2009 for instance, the [United States](3434750.md) estimated its GDP at \$14.119 [trillion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trillion_\(short_scale\)), and its GNP at \$14.265 trillion.[^cite_note-14]

The term *gross national income* (GNI) has gradually replaced the *Gross national product* (GNP) in international statistics.[^cite_note-nominal-15][^cite_note-ppp-12] While being conceptually identical, the precise calculation method has evolved at the same time as the name change.[^cite_note-Eurostat-16]

### Use of GNP

The [United States](3434750.md) used GNP as its primary measure of total economic activity until 1991, when it began to use GDP.[^cite_note-17] In making the switch, the [Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis) (BEA) noted both that GDP provided an easier comparison of other measures of economic activity in the United States and that "virtually all other countries have already adopted GDP as their primary measure of production".[^cite_note-announcement-18] Many economists have questioned how meaningful GNP or GDP is as a measure of a nation's economic well-being, as it does not count most [unpaid work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unpaid_work) and counts much economic activity that is unproductive or actually destructive.[^cite_note-19]

### GNI vs GDP

While GDP measures the market value of all final goods and services produced in a given country, GNI measures income generated by the country's citizens, regardless of the geographic location of the income. In many states, those two figures are close, as the difference between income received by the country versus payments made to the rest of the world is not significant. According to the [World Bank](36752.md), the GNI of the US in 2016 was 1.5% higher than GDP.[^cite_note-20]

In developing countries, on the other hand, the difference might be significant due to a large amount of foreign aid and capital inflow. In 2016, the GNI[^cite_note-21] of [Armenia](10918072.md) was 4.45% higher than GDP.[^cite_note-22] Based on the [OECD](33853117.md) reports, in 2015 alone, Armenia has received a total of US\$409 million development assistance. Over the past 25 years, [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID) has provided more than one billion USD to improve the living of the people in Armenia. GNI equals GDP plus wages, salaries, and property income of the country's residents earned abroad that also constitutes the higher GNI figure. According to the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) report on migration from Armenia in 2015–17, every year around 15–20 thousand people leave Armenia permanently,[^cite_note-23] and roughly 47% of those are working migrants that leave the country to earn income and sustain the families left in Armenia. In 2016 Armenian residents received in a total of around \$150 million remittances.[^cite_note-24] Armenia's GNI, measured in US dollars, amounted to US\$13.5 billion in 2021, according to the National Statistical Office. This is an 8.23% increase over the prior year. GNI in USD terms in Armenia has historically ranged from a record high of US\$13.8 billion in 2019 to a record low of US\$1.06 billion in 1992. Regarding interest rates on GNI expressed in USD, Armenia is ranked 119th out of the 155 monitored nations.[^cite_note-25]
