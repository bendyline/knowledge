The **World Athletics Ultimate Championship** is a biennial [track and field](194904.md) invitational event held by [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics). Announced in June 2024, it is a season-ending meet that will be held in even-numbered years between [World Athletics Championships](218926.md). The meet will have a streamlined format oriented towards television audiences, and limited fields for individual events consisting of defending Olympic, world, and [Diamond League](21778161.md) champions, and entrants determined by the [World Athletics Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Rankings).&#x20;

The [inaugural World Athletics Ultimate Championship](81879269.md) was held 11–13 September 2026 in [Budapest](36787.md), Hungary.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

## History

In June 2024, World Athletics announced that it would hold a new biennial event known as the World Athletics Ultimate Championship, with the inaugural edition to be held in [Budapest](36787.md), Hungary in September 2026 following the [Diamond League](21778161.md) final; the event is intended to serve as a season-closing meet featuring limited fields of top athletes, and will take place in even-numbered years when the [World Athletics Championships](218926.md) are not being held. It is being designed to appeal to younger demographics and television audiences, with a focus on fast-paced sessions lasting less than three hours each, and events determined to be popular among viewers. The inaugural Ultimate Championship will have a total purse of US\$10 million, with gold medalists to be awarded US\$150,000 each.[^cite_note-\:1-1][^cite_note-\:0-2]

President [Sebastian Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Coe) stated that the Ultimate Championship was part of an effort by World Athletics to "remain interesting" and "create new formats that keep our sport at the top of people's consciousness."[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Swedish-American pole vaulter [Armand Duplantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Duplantis) and Jamaican sprinter [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) were named as ambassadors for the inaugural edition, billed as "Ultimate Star" and "Ultimate Legend" respectively.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## Format

The Ultimate Championship features limited fields in each discipline, with a quota of sixteen athletes for individual track events, eight athletes for field events, and eight teams for relays. For individual events, invites are extended to champions from the most recent [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_Summer_Olympics), world championships, and Diamond League final, with the remainder of the field determined by the [World Athletics Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Rankings) during the one-year ranking period ending prior to the Diamond League final. There is no limit on how many entrants may come from a single country.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

For relay events, the [World Athletics Relays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Relays) are used as direct qualifiers for the Ultimate Championship, with the top six finishers advancing, and the final two teams chosen based on performance over the past year. Unlike individual events, a single country may only be represented by one team.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The disciplines for the inaugural event were announced in November 2024; to ensure a faster pace, the Ultimate Championship features only 28 events, leaving out long-distance events such as the [10,000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_metres) and the [steeplechase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steeplechase_\(athletics\)), and focusing on field events that were determined to be the most popular among viewers at previous World Athletics Championships.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-\:1-1]

Field events (except vertical jumps) use a more compact format than standard competitions: two rounds for all eight athletes, then a third round for the top-6 athletes, and a final, fourth round for the top-4, with the order changing from highest to lowest mark after each round.[^cite_note-12]
