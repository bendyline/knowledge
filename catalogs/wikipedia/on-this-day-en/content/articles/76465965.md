The **2026 NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament** was an event involving 68 teams playing in a [single-elimination tournament](939680.md) to determine the [NCAA Division I](669931.md) men's [college basketball](768856.md) national champion for the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_season). The 87th edition of the tournament began on March 17 and concluded with the [championship game](79949085.md) on April 6, at [Lucas Oil Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Oil_Stadium) in [Indianapolis](57707.md). The Michigan Wolverines won their second title, and the first under Dusty May. It was Michigan’s first national championship since [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_championship_game), ending a streak of four straight title game defeats (two of which were vacated by the NCAA) for the Wolverines.

[Atlantic Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Atlantic_Sun_men's_basketball_tournament) champion [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Queens_Royals_men's_basketball_team) and [Western Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_WAC_men's_basketball_tournament) (WAC) champion [California Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_California_Baptist_Lancers_men's_basketball_team) made their tournament debuts. Queens qualified in its first year of eligibility, becoming only the fifth school since 1972 to achieve the feat.&#x20;

[Ohio Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ohio_Valley_Conference_men's_basketball_tournament) champion [Tennessee State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Tennessee_State_Tigers_basketball_team) made its third-ever appearance, its first since [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament), while [Big Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Big_Sky_Conference_men's_basketball_tournament) champion [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Idaho_Vandals_men's_basketball_team) made its first appearance since [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). [Coastal Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CAA_men's_basketball_tournament) champion [Hofstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Hofstra_Pride_men's_basketball_team) made its first appearance since [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-1] [Mid-American Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_MAC_men's_basketball_tournament) (MAC) champion [Akron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Akron_Zips_men's_basketball_team) and [Southland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Southland_Conference_men's_basketball_tournament) champion [McNeese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_McNeese_Cowboys_basketball_team) each made their third consecutive tournament appearance.

For the first time since [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament) there was a [Philadelphia Big 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Big_5) school in the tournament, with both [Villanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Villanova_Wildcats_men's_basketball_team) and [Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Penn_Quakers_men's_basketball_team) qualifying. Teams from 33 U.S. states and the District of Columbia participated.

For the second consecutive season, all top four seeds advanced to the second round. This was the first time since the tournament expanded to 64 teams in 1985 that this had happened in consecutive seasons.[^cite_note-2] The biggest upset in the first round was No. 5 seed [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Wisconsin_Badgers_men's_basketball_team) losing to No. 12 seed [High Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025-26_High_Point_Panthers_men's_basketball_team).[^cite_note-3] [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Nebraska_Cornhuskers_men's_basketball_team), High Point, [Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Howard_Bison_men's_basketball_team), and [Prairie View A\&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Prairie_View_A\&M_Panthers_basketball_team) each earned their first ever tournament wins, although Howard and Prairie View A\&M accomplished this in the First Four. Nebraska had previously been the only [power conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_conferences#Outside_of_football) team to have never won an NCAA tournament game.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The average margin of victory in the first round was 17.4 points, the highest since the tournament expanded in 1985. This, coupled with higher seeds' tendency to win during the 2025 tournament, has led to a belief that mid-major programs are unable to compete with major conference programs in the [NIL](68930532.md) and transfer portal era.[^cite_note-6] However, No. 1 overall seed [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Duke_Blue_Devils_men's_basketball_team) did get a challenge from No. 16 seed [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Siena_Saints_men's_basketball_team), trailing by 13 points before recovering to win the game by six points.[^cite_note-7] Additionally, for the 18th straight year, a double digit seed made the Sweet 16, although for the third consecutive year no 12-seed or lower team made the Sweet 16, and for the fourth consecutive year only one double digit seed made the Sweet 16.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] For the second straight season, no mid-major teams made the Sweet 16.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

The Final Four comprised No. 1 seed Michigan, No. 1 seed Arizona, No. 2 seed Connecticut, and No. 3 seed Illinois. While Illinois was the lowest numbered seed to make the Final Four in two years, the Final Four was noted for not having a real Cinderella story.[^cite_note-13]

This was the final tournament played under the 68-team format, as the NCAA voted to expand the tournament to 76 teams for the [2027 edition](81569697.md).[^cite_note-14]

## Tournament procedure

Further information: [NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament § Format](507399.md#Format)

A total of 68 teams entered the 2026 tournament. Thirty-one automatic bids were awarded to each program that won its conference's tournament. The remaining 37 teams received at-large bids, with selections extended by the [NCAA selection committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_basketball_tournament_selection_process) on Selection Sunday (March 15). The selection committee also [seeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeding_\(sports\)) the entire field from 1 to 68.

Eight teams (the four-lowest seeded automatic qualifiers and the four lowest-seeded at large-teams) played in the First Four. The winners of these games advanced to the main tournament bracket.

## Media coverage

### Television

Main article: [NCAA March Madness (TV program)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_March_Madness_\(TV_program\))

[CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports) and [TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Sports_\(United_States\)) had US television rights to the tournament.[^cite_note-wp=2032extend-24][^cite_note-25] As part of a cycle that began in 2016, [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(American_TV_channel\)) televised the 2026 Final Four and the National Championship Game.

In November 2025, [TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Basketball_on_TNT_Sports) and [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) announced an agreement to allow ESPN's [Dick Vitale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Vitale) to call a First Four game on truTV alongside TNT Sports' [Charles Barkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Barkley). In exchange, Vitale and Barkley called a December 13, 2025 regular season game on ESPN between Indiana and Kentucky.[^cite_note-26]

*[CBS Mornings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Mornings)* co-host and *[The NFL Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_NFL_Today)* analyst [Nate Burleson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Burleson) replaced [Ernie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Johnson_Jr.) for the first two weeks of the tournament, as Johnson announced his semi-retirement from hosting March Madness to focus on his increasing *[Inside the NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_the_NBA)* duties for [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_ESPN) and [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_ABC) under a sub-licensing agreement from TNT Sports. Johnson continued to host the Final Four and National Championship Game.[^cite_note-27]
