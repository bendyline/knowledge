The **Hōlei Sea Arch** was a 90-foot (27 m)-high [natural arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_arch) of [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) on the southern coast of the [Big Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_Hawaii) of [Hawaiʻi](13270.md), within [Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawai%CA%BBi_Volcanoes_National_Park). Carved by the Pacific Ocean from an ancient [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) flow roughly 550 years ago, it stood at the end of the [Chain of Craters Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_of_Craters_Road) and was one of the park's best-known coastal landmarks. The [National Park Service](161535.md) (NPS) closed the original overlook in 2020 because of cliff instability, and in 2022 a large surf event broke away part of the arch's base. The arch collapsed into the ocean in late September 2026, around the time of [Hurricane Nolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Nolo).

## Geology

The arch was cut into the cliff of a lava flow that the NPS dates to about 550 years ago.[^cite_note-HNN-collapse-1] The park attributes its shape to [differential erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_erosion), in which waves wear away lava layers of differing hardness at different rates.[^cite_note-HNN-collapse-1] It was composed of [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt).[^cite_note-2]

Sea arches occur along a wide stretch of Hawaiʻi's southern coastline. They are remnants of once-continuous sea cliffs, formed where lava met the sea and added land to the island, and where waves later broke apart the rock and carved sea caves.[^cite_note-KHON-2022-3] The NPS described such arches as temporary features that eventually crumble, though others may form as the cliffs shift.[^cite_note-NPS-thingstodo-4]

## Visiting

The arch was reached by car along the Chain of Craters Road, which runs from the area of [Halemaʻumaʻu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halema%CA%BBuma%CA%BBu), the main [Kīlauea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%ABlauea) [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera), down to the coast.[^cite_note-5] The road ends where [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) from Kīlauea's [Puʻu ʻŌʻō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu%CA%BBu_%CA%BB%C5%8C%CA%BB%C5%8D) eruption, which began in 1983, repeatedly covered it; the arch stood near that end point.[^cite_note-6] From the end of the road, visitors walked a short distance to an observation area, a trip the NPS estimated at 20 to 30 minutes. No fee applied beyond park admission.[^cite_note-NPS-thingstodo-4]

## Instability and closures

The NPS said the coastline on either side of the arch had shown signs of instability for years. Earthquakes during the 2018 [Kīlauea eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_lower_Puna_eruption) and summit collapse damaged the nearby cliffs.[^cite_note-SA-collapse-7]

On January 15, 2020, the park closed the original overlook "until further notice" after new cracks and instability appeared on the coastal cliffs, and roped off the trail to it.[^cite_note-8] In March 2020 the park opened a new viewing area about 1,000 feet (300 m) past the gate at the end of the road, set back from the cliff edge.[^cite_note-9]

On July 17 and 18, 2022, swells of 20 to 25 feet (6 to 8 m) driven by the remnants of Hurricane Darby struck the island's southern coast. A large piece of rock fell from the base of one of the arch's legs, and the park warned that the arch could fall at any time without warning.[^cite_note-10] The park noted that walking on the arch was forbidden and that the surrounding cliffs were too steep and dangerous for hiking.[^cite_note-KHON-2022-3]

## Collapse

The arch was still standing on September 23, 2026.[^cite_note-SA-collapse-7] Hurricane Nolo passed Hawaiʻi Island that weekend, and most of the park was closed from Thursday, September 24, until early Sunday, September 27.[^cite_note-SA-collapse-7] Reports that the arch was gone began circulating on social media after the park reopened, and the park confirmed the collapse on October 1.[^cite_note-HPR-collapse-11] The NPS said the exact time of the collapse is unknown, but that the arch had fallen by the night of September 27.[^cite_note-SA-collapse-7][^cite_note-12] The park did not say whether high surf or other storm conditions from Nolo caused the collapse.[^cite_note-HPR-collapse-11]
