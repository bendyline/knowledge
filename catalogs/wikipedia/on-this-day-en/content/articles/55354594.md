**Shaheen Shah Afridi**[^cite_note-4] (born 6 April 2000) is a Pakistani international [cricketer](25675557.md) who represents the [Pakistan national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_cricket_team). He is the captain of the national [ODI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) team and previously served as captained of [T20I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty20_International) team. A left-arm fast [bowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_\(cricket\)), he made his international debut in 2018 and was named the [ICC Men's Cricketer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Awards) in 2021, becoming the first Pakistani cricketer to win the [Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Garfield_Sobers_Trophy).[^cite_note-5] He led the [Lahore Qalandars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Qalandars) to [Pakistan Super League](38199013.md) titles in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistan_Super_League), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistan_Super_League), and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pakistan_Super_League), becoming the first captain to win three [PSL](38199013.md) championships.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Early and personal life

Afridi was born on 6 April 2000 in [Landi Kotal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landi_Kotal), [Khyber Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Agency) of the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas) (present-day [Khyber District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_District), [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md)) into a family belonging to the [Zakhakhel Afridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afridi) tribe of the [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns).[^cite_note-BR-9] He grew up in his hometown of Landi Kotal. He is the youngest of seven brothers; his eldest brother, 15 years his senior, is [Riaz Afridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riaz_Afridi) who played a single [Test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) for Pakistan in 2004.[^cite_note-Afridi8for-10] His cousin [Yasir Afridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasir_Afridi) is a professional [footballer](10669.md) who has represented the [Pakistan national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_football_team).

In March 2021, Afridi became engaged with Ansha Afridi who is daughter of former Pakistan cricketer [Shahid Afridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Afridi).[^cite_note-11] On 3 February 2023, they married in a private [nikah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_in_Islam) ceremony.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] On 24 August 2024, their first child was born, a son named Aliyaar Afridi.[^cite_note-14]

In July 2022, Afridi was made an honorary [Deputy Superintendent of the Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_superintendent_of_police) (DSP) rank in the [KP Police](21591049.md) as a goodwill ambassador.[^cite_note-15]

In January 2023, it was announced that Afridi would design [Lahore Qalandars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Qalandars)' new kits for the [2023 PSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistan_Super_League).[^cite_note-16]

## Youth career

Shaheen began his cricket career from the Tatara Ground in [Landi Kotal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landi_Kotal), which is named after the nearby Tatara Hills.[^cite_note-17] Riaz Afridi introduced Shaheen to hard-ball cricket at the [FATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas_cricket_team) Under-16 trials in 2015, with Shaheen having played only [tennis-ball cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis-ball_cricket) until then.[^cite_note-PakPassion-18] Success at this level led to Shaheen being selected for the Under-16 tour of Australia in November 2015, where he played his part with four wickets in the 2–1 victories in the One-Day and Twenty20 series.[^cite_note-PakPassion-18]

In December 2016, Afridi was included in the [Pakistan Under-19 cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Under-19_cricket_team) selected for the [2016 Under-19 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Under-19_Asia_Cup) held in Sri Lanka.[^cite_note-BR-9] He took 3 wickets for 27 runs in Pakistan's nine-wickets victory over Singapore in their opening match of the U-19 Asia Cup.[^cite_note-19]

## Domestic and franchise career

In early September 2017, Afridi signed a two-year contract with [Dhaka Dynamites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Dynamites), a major [Bangladesh Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Premier_League) franchise.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Dawn-21][^cite_note-22] Later he made his [first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) debut for [Khan Research Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Research_Laboratories_cricket_team) in the [2017–18 Quaid-e-Azam Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Quaid-e-Azam_Trophy) on 26 September 2017.[^cite_note-FC-23] In the second innings of the match, he took 8 wickets for 39 runs,[^cite_note-ICC2-24] the best figures by a Pakistani bowler on first-class debut.[^cite_note-Afridi8for-10][^cite_note-ICC-25]

In December 2017, Afridi was named in Pakistan's squad for the [2018 Under-19 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Under-19_Cricket_World_Cup).[^cite_note-PakistanU19-26] He was the leading wicket-taker for Pakistan in the tournament, with 12 wickets.[^cite_note-U19runs-27] Following Pakistan's matches in the tournament, the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) (ICC) named Afridi as the rising star of the squad.[^cite_note-rising-28]

Afridi made his [Twenty20](770690.md) debut for [Lahore Qalandars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Qalandars) in the [2018 Pakistan Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistan_Super_League) (PSL) on 23 February 2018.[^cite_note-T20-29] The following month in the PSL, during Lahore's match with the [Multan Sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_Sultans), Afridi took five wickets for four runs. Lahore won the fixture by 6 wickets and Afridi was named the player of the match.[^cite_note-Five-30][^cite_note-FiveMOTM-31]

In April 2018, Afridi was named in [Baluchistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_cricket_team)'s squad for the [2018 Pakistan Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistan_Cup).[^cite_note-squads1-32][^cite_note-squads2-33] He made his [List A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_A_cricket) debut for Baluchistan on 25 April 2018.[^cite_note-LA-34]

In July 2019, Afridi was selected to play for the Rotterdam Rhinos in the [inaugural edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Euro_T20_Slam) of the [Euro T20 Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_T20_Slam) cricket tournament.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] However, the following month the tournament was cancelled.[^cite_note-37]

In December 2019, it was announced that Afridi will play for [Hampshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_County_Cricket_Club) in the [2020 T20 Blast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_t20_Blast) in England.[^cite_note-38] In September 2020, Hampshire confirmed his participation in the 2020 T20 Blast, and announced that he will be available after fulfilling his [national duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_England_in_2020).[^cite_note-39]

On 20 September 2020, in the final round of group matches in the T20 Blast, Afridi took a hat-trick and four wickets in four balls, finishing with match figures of 6/19 from his four overs and recording the best ever bowling figures at the [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(cricket_ground\)) in T20 cricket.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

On 2 October 2020, in the [2020–21 National T20 Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_National_T20_Cup), Afridi took his second [five-wicket haul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-wicket_haul) in three T20 games, with figures of 5/20 from his four overs.[^cite_note-42] Three days later, Afridi took another five-wicket haul, with 5/21 in the match against [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_cricket_team).[^cite_note-43] In October 2021, Afridi signed with [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex_County_Cricket_Club) to play in domestic matches in England until July 2022.[^cite_note-44] However, he returned to Pakistan in mid-May to prepare for the national team's [home series against the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2022).[^cite_note-45]

In December 2021, he was named as the captain of Lahore Qalandars.[^cite_note-46] Afridi later stated that [Imran Khan](413184.md) had suggested for him to become captain, which led to the change in captaincy.[^cite_note-47] Under his captaincy, Qalandars won the [2022 PSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistan_Super_League), which made him the youngest captain to win a T20 league.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] He also ended the tournament as the leading wicket-taker.[^cite_note-50]

In December 2024, he was directly signed by the defending [Bangladesh Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Premier_League) Champion, [Fortune Barishal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Barishal), making his debut for them on the 30th of December.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In June 2025, Afridi nominated for the [Big Bash League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bash_League) (BBL) international player draft for the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Big_Bash_League_season). He was selected first overall by the [Brisbane Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Heat).[^cite_note-54]

## International career



Afridi (behind the stumps) and [Prince William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William,_Duke_of_Cambridge) playing tapeball cricket in [Lahore](125315.md), in 2019

In March 2018, he was named in Pakistan's [Twenty20 International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty20_International) (T20I) squad for their series [against the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2017%E2%80%9318).[^cite_note-PakSquad-55][^cite_note-PakSquadICC-56] He made his T20I debut for Pakistan against the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_national_cricket_team) on 3 April 2018.[^cite_note-T20I-57] In September 2018, he was named in Pakistan's [One Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) (ODI) squad for the [2018 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Asia_Cup).[^cite_note-AC18-58][^cite_note-59] He made his ODI debut for Pakistan against [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_national_cricket_team) on 21 September 2018.[^cite_note-60]

His rapid rise to international cricket was noted, as despite having played only three first-class matches, he was already being discussed as a strong candidate for Test selection. His early career trajectory was also compared to that of [Wasim Akram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasim_Akram), observing that while both debuted with limited domestic experience, Afridi was being introduced to the highest level far more gradually and systematically, with his pace, height, left-arm angle, and control as defining attributes.[^cite_note-SamiuddinAfridi2018-61] In November 2018, he was named in Pakistan's [Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) squad for their series [against New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_cricket_team_against_Pakistan_in_the_UAE_in_2018%E2%80%9319).[^cite_note-PakTest-62] He made his Test debut for Pakistan against [New Zealand](478115.md) on 3 December 2018.[^cite_note-63]

In April 2019, he was named in [Pakistan's squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cricket_World_Cup_squads#Pakistan) for the [2019 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cricket_World_Cup).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] On 5 July 2019, in the match against [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_cricket_team), Shaheen became the youngest bowler to take a [five-wicket haul in a World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cricket_World_Cup_five-wicket_hauls) match, finishing with figures of 6/35.[^cite_note-66] These were also the best bowling figures by a bowler for Pakistan in a World Cup match.[^cite_note-67] Following the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup), the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) (ICC) named Afridi as the rising star of the squad.[^cite_note-68]

In December 2019, during the second Test match [against Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2019%E2%80%9320), Afridi took his first [five-wicket haul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-wicket_haul) in Test cricket.[^cite_note-69] In June 2020, he was named in a 29-man squad for Pakistan's [tour to England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_England_in_2020) during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] In July, he was shortlisted in Pakistan's 20-man squad for the Test matches against England.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] In September 2021, he was named in Pakistan's squad for the [2021 ICC Men's T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ICC_Men's_T20_World_Cup).[^cite_note-74]

In August 2021, during the second Test of the [tour of West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_the_West_Indies_in_2021), Afridi was declared [Player of the Match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_of_the_Match_awards_\(cricket\)) as he took 10 for 94, the best match figures by a Pakistan fast bowler since [Mohammad Asif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Asif_\(cricketer\)) in 2006, and only the fourth instance of a Pakistan quick taking ten wickets in a Test in the 21st century. Observers highlighted Afridi's sustained pace, reverse swing, and ability to strike with both the new and old ball, noting that his efforts helped Pakistan level the series and extend their record of not losing a multi-match bilateral Test series to the West Indies since 2000.[^cite_note-Bandarupalli2021-75]

In December 2021, Afridi entered the top five of the ICC Test bowling rankings for the first time in his career. It was noted that Afridi's ascent reflected both his wicket-taking consistency and his impact as Pakistan's premier strike bowler, making him the youngest fast bowler in the world at that time to occupy a top-five position in the Test rankings.[^cite_note-AfridiRankings2021-76] In January 2022, Afridi was named the Cricketer of the Year by the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council). He took 78 wickets in 36 international matches in 2021.[^cite_note-77]

In October 2023, he took a 5 wicket haul against Australia in the 2023 Cricket World Cup match.[^cite_note-78] He finished the [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup) with the sixth most wickets, taking 18 wickets in 9 matches. On 31 October, he became the fastest Pakistani bowler to get 100 wickets in ODIs, as well as the fastest pacer to do so.[^cite_note-sportstar.thehindu.com-79] He achieved the feat in 51 matches, overtaking [Saqlain Mushtaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqlain_Mushtaq), who took 53 matches to complete 100 wickets.[^cite_note-sportstar.thehindu.com-79] However, analysts noted a dip in Afridi's trademark new-ball effectiveness in ODIs. Analysts argued that Afridi's opening bursts, once defined by prodigious swing and early wickets, had begun to wear thin, with batters increasingly prepared for his inswinging fuller length. It suggested that Afridi might have been over-striving for the perfect, wicket-taking delivery, reducing his variation and predictability in the powerplay.[^cite_note-AfridiNewHit2023-80]

In November 2023, Afridi rose to the top of the ICC ODI bowling rankings for the first time in his career. Afridi climbed seven places after a strong start to the 2023 ODI World Cup, where a series of impactful new-ball and middle-overs spells significantly boosted his ranking. His ascent made him the first Pakistan fast bowler since the 1990s to reach No. 1 in ODIs.[^cite_note-AfridiODI1-81] On 15 November 2023, Afridi was appointed as T20I captain after [Babar Azam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babar_Azam) resigned from captaincy in all three formats of the game.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

In May 2024, he was named in Pakistan's squad for the [2024 ICC Men's T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_ICC_Men's_T20_World_Cup) tournament.[^cite_note-84] In December 2024, he completed 100 T20Is wickets against South Africa, becoming only the third Pakistani to reach the mark,[^cite_note-85] Additionally, he was the first Pakistani to reach 100 wickets in all three formats of international cricket, and the youngest bowler to do so.[^cite_note-86] Afridi also became the youngest ever to achieve 100 wickets in all-format of cricket history.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

In November 2024, Afridi returned to the top of the ICC ODI bowling rankings, reclaiming the No. 1 position. The update followed a series of strong white-ball performances that lifted him back above his competitors in the global standings.[^cite_note-AfridiRankings2024-89]

During the [2025 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup), Afridi took 10 wickets in seven matches, finishing as one of Pakistan's leading bowlers in the competition.[^cite_note-90] In a Super Four match against [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_cricket_team), he recorded figures of 3 for 17 and added useful 29 runs, helping Pakistan recover from a top-order collapse.[^cite_note-91] His contributions with both ball and bat were noted as adding depth to the team's overall performance.[^cite_note-92]

In October 2025, during the first Test of the [home series against South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2025%E2%80%9326), which Pakistan won by 93 runs, Afridi was decisive with the old ball, exploiting reverse swing to claim four wickets in the final innings, a haul greater than all Pakistani pace bowlers had managed collectively across the previous four home Tests.[^cite_note-93] On 20 October 2025, Afridi was appointed as ODI captain of Pakistan team.[^cite_note-94] In the third and last T20I of the home series against South Africa, which Pakistan won, Afridi was noted for his new ball spell, taking 3/26, including two wickets in the first over, reducing South Africa to 22 in the powerplay, their third-lowest in T20I history.[^cite_note-95] As captain, Afridi led Pakistan to a landmark victory in the ODI series, becoming the first Pakistan skipper to win an ODI series at home against the Proteas. He chose to field first in the decider, and his strategic decisions and leadership under pressure were pivotal in the triumph.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]
