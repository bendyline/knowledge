# Lebanon–Syria border



Map of Lebanon

The **[Lebanon](17771.md)–[Syria](7515849.md) border** is 394 km (245 miles) in length and runs from the Mediterranean coast in the north to the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Israel](9282173.md) in the south.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts at confluence of the [Nahr al-Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahr_al-Kabir) river with the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), and then follows this river eastwards some distance inland. The Lebanese border forms a [salient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salient_\(geography\)) to include the villages of Karha and Knaisse Akkar in the north-east of [Akkar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkar_District), just west of the Syrian [Lake Homs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Homs), before turning to the south-east via a series of irregular lines, cutting across the [Orontes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontes_River) (at [34°27′18″N 36°29′24″E  /  34.455°N 36.490°E  / 34.455; 36.490](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Lebanon%E2%80%93Syria_border\&params=34.455_N_36.490_E_)) and the [trans-Beqaa](2162385.md) road between [Qaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaa) and [Al-Qusayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qusayr,_Syria) (at [34°25′18″N 36°32′36″E  /  34.4217°N 36.5433°E  / 34.4217; 36.5433](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Lebanon%E2%80%93Syria_border\&params=34.4217_N_36.5433_E_)), reaching the [Anti-Lebanon Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Lebanon_Mountains) at about [34°13′N 36°36′E  /  34.22°N 36.60°E  / 34.22; 36.60](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Lebanon%E2%80%93Syria_border\&params=34.22_N_36.60_E_). The border then turns towards the south-west, generally following the Anti-Lebanon Mountains via a series of irregular lines, until reaching [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon).

The precise location of the Lebanese–Israeli–Syrian [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) is unclear due to Israel's [occupation of the Golan Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Golan_Heights) stemming from the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md).[^cite_note-2] The *de jure* tripoint lies on the [Hasbani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbani_River), a tributary of the [river Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Jordan), at [33°14′32″N 35°37′28″E  /  33.2422°N 35.6244°E  / 33.2422; 35.6244](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Lebanon%E2%80%93Syria_border\&params=33.2422_N_35.6244_E_), just north-east of the Israeli town of [Ma'ayan Baruch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'ayan_Baruch). The *de facto* tripoints lie on the tripoint(s) with the United Nations [UNDOF Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNDOF_Zone). The situation is further complicated by the dispute over the [Shebaa farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebaa_farms) area on the Golan-Lebanon border.[^cite_note-3]

## History



[French Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon), with [Greater Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon) in green

At the beginning of the 20th century what are now Lebanon and Syria belonged to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), with the [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) semi-autonomous as the [Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon_Mutasarrifate). During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) an [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt), supported by Britain, succeeded in removing the Ottomans from most of the Middle East. However, as a result of the secret 1916 Anglo-French [Sykes-Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes-Picot_Agreement) Britain and France split the Ottoman domains between them, with Syria and Lebanon falling under a French [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon).[^cite_note-4]

In 1920 France split its mandate into several political entities, one of which was an expanded Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate referred to as '[Greater Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon)', set up chiefly so as to provide a national polity for the generally pro-French [Maronite Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Christians).[^cite_note-Brunet-Jailly2015-5] The process leading to the precise delineation of the frontier is unclear, though it appears to have been based on a map stemming from the 1860s French expedition to the region which was championed by Lebanese nationalist Bulus Nujaym.[^cite_note-Buheiry-6][^cite_note-Zamir1988-7] Many Syrian nationalists opposed the new entity, seeing Lebanon as an integral part of the [region of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\)).[^cite_note-8] The remaining Syrian states were eventually merged into one Syrian polity in 1930s, minus Lebanon (and also [Hatay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay), which became part of the new [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey)). Both Lebanon and Syria gained full independence in the period 1943–1946.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Relations between the two new states were often tense, and a precise border has never been fixed officially with any precision, in spite of Lebanese demands to this effect and some preliminary conducted in the 1950–1960s.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Many Lebanese feared that its larger neighbour harboured designs to annex the country. In 1975 long-standing sectarian tensions within Lebanon erupted into [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), prompting Syria to [occupy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_occupation_of_Lebanon) the country the following year; Syrian troops were to remain in Lebanon until 2005.[^cite_note-\:0-13][^cite_note-14] Since 2011, the border region has been seriously affected by the [spillover from the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Civil_War_spillover_in_Lebanon).

On 17 March, 2026, the official Syria news agency [SANA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arab_News_Agency) reported that [Syrian forces](27479.md) discovered a two smuggling tunnel leading to the Lebanese side of the border, an area controlled by [Hezbollah](13919.md). The two tunnel that were discovered within 24 hour, were located near the Syrian village of [Hosh al-Sayyid Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawsh_al-Sayyid_Ali), and later sealed.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]
