**Bamban**, officially the **Municipality of Bamban** ([Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language): *Balen ning Bamban*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Bayan ng Bamban*), is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_the_Philippines) in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Province) of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac), [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 81,012 people.[^cite_note-5]

## Etymology

The municipality derives its name from the *bamban* plants *([Donax canniformis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donax_canniformis))* that once dominated large areas of land along the river near the foothills. It was initially called *cabambanan* or *mabamban*, but was later called simply as *bamban*.[^cite_note-6]

### Precolonial history

The site of Bamban was originally a vast track of wild land extending eastward; and on the west side, composed of forested foothills and mountains lush with tall trees extending deep into the Zambales ranges. The thick forest and mountains were then inhabited by the Aetas (locally known as Baluga), and the [Zambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambal), both of whom subsisted only on fishing and native or wild animals which abounded in the place. Occasionally, traders from [Pampanga](255602.md) and the Tagalog provinces braved the wilderness to go northward to [Capas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capas) and [Tarlac](2144455.md).

Before the advent of the [Spanish era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1565%E2%80%931898\)), small settlers came, attracted by the fertile land sustained by the river. The settlers started small clearings in the thick growth of bamban plants which covered vast portions of the land bordering the river a small distance from the foothills. Because of these plants, the place was eventually named Bamban.&#x20;

### Spanish colonial era

During the Spanish colonial era, about 1700, Augustinian Recollects came and established the Mission de Pueblos de Bamban. Originally a part of Pampanga, in 1837, a new boundary line was established, thereby permanently making Bamban part of the province of Tarlac.

Official recognition as a town was sanctioned by the gobernadorcillos in Tarlac town. Don Martín Sibal was subsequently appointed head and commissioned with the rank of "Capitán". Among the first settlers were the Sibal, Lugtu, Dayrit, Macale, Vergara, Manipon, Gonzales, and de la Cruz clans.



Children on roof of a school in Bamban, that was buried by lahars during the 1991 eruption of Mt. Pinatubo.

During the revolution of 1896, Bamban was one of the first towns to take up arms against the Spaniards. Northwest of the town situated among the hills are remnants of strongholds built by Bambanenses who joined Gen. [Servillano Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servillano_Aquino) in the uprising in Tarlac. Up to this time, a portion of the foothills in the area is called "Batiawan" or look-out point.

### American colonial era

When the Americans came in 1900, the pueblo gradually grew into a town. The Bamban Sugar Central was established. Don Pablo Lagman was the first to be appointed presidente and Don Laureano Campo as vice-presidente under the American military government. Other prominent families then took over the helm of government. During the Commonwealth era, the Sibals, the Lumboys, the Santoses, the Punsalangs, to mention a few, enjoyed political power until the outbreak of World War II. The whole *población* was devastated and burned during the liberation. Rebuilding began despite the ongoing fighting in the mountains. Growth was fast because of proximity of Clark Air Base. However, prominent families left the town for the city to establish big business and names for themselves.

### The Bamban barricade during the People Power revolution

Main article: [People Power revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_revolution)

Bamban played an important role during the People Power revolution because of its proximity to the Capital and to Philippine Military bases further north. In a tactically significant intervention during the revolution, 20,000 people blocked the Bamban Bridge on Feb. 25 to prevent loyalist forces from the Army's 5th Infantry Division in Tarlac from getting through to Manila to reinforce the Marcos forces surrounding Camps Aguinaldo and Crame, in an event which came to be known as "the Bamban barricade."[^cite_note-Lacorte2022-7]

### 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo

The [1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo) and the subsequent flow of [lahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) to the lowlands of Bamban forced residents to move out or to stay at the Dapdap Resettlement Area, which is on higher grounds.

### Contemporary history

In 2022, local businesswoman [Alice Guo](76882460.md) was elected mayor, which saw significant developments in town, alongside a then suspected fast-rising [Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Offshore_Gaming_Operator) (POGO) hub.[^cite_note-8] However, in 2024, Guo was subjected to a [Senate](299521.md) inquiry. She was suspended over suspected links to POGO related criminal activities and questions over her [Filipino citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law) and was eventually dismissed from office by the [Ombudsman](1866220.md).[^cite_note-9] A court ruling in June 2025, concluded she is a Chinese citizen, thus voiding her entire tenure as mayor.[^cite_note-10]

As Guo’s vice mayor, Leonardo Anunciacion, and seven members of the [Sangguniang Bayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Bayan) were also suspended for three months on the same charges, Guo was replaced by Councilor Erano Timbang, who was the only member of the Sangguniang Bayan who voted to oppose the establishment of the POGO in Bamban.[^cite_note-11] An interim vice mayor and other municipal councilors were also installed.[^cite_note-OIC-12]

## Geography

Bamban is the southernmost town of Tarlac. It shares a common boundary with [Mabalacat, Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabalacat,_Pampanga), and in the west, with [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales). The boundary is defined by the [Parua River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parua_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is a source of irrigation water, food, and sand and gravel for infrastructure projects. The mountainous region situated in the western part comprises almost two-thirds of the total land area, which used to be within the US Military Reservations. Nestled in this part are the BLISS projects and to the north, the MAR settlement project.

Bamban is 99 kilometers (62 mi) from Manila and is 25 kilometers (16 mi) from the provincial capital, [Tarlac City](2144455.md).
