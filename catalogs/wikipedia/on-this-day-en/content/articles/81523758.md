On the evening of 4 November 2025, a major fire broke out at the Tuzla [retirement home](1563666.md) ([Bosnian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_language): *Dom penzionera Tuzla*) in the city of [Tuzla](524638.md), Bosnia and Herzegovina, killing at least 17 people and injuring more than 30 others.[^cite_note-Klix-1][^cite_note-auto-2][^cite_note-auto1-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-death18-5] The incident has been described as one of the deadliest peacetime tragedies in the country.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Fire and response

At around 20\:45 [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time) (19\:45 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC)), a fire began on the seventh floor of the nine-story Tuzla retirement home.[^cite_note-CNN-8] That floor was occupied by residents with limited mobility, which complicated evacuation efforts.[^cite_note-\:0-9] The fire then engulfed the upper levels of the building.[^cite_note-10] Some of the residents required significant assistance to evacuate.[^cite_note-CNN-8]

Firefighters and emergency crews from the local [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) and surrounding [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) evacuated residents and staff. Some of the injured included police officers, firefighters, medical staff, and employees of the retirement home.[^cite_note-11]

## Casualties

On 5 November, official authorities announced that the death toll stood at 11.[^cite_note-auto-2][^cite_note-12] Over 30 injuries were reported, with some in critical condition and undergoing treatment for smoke inhalation.[^cite_note-13] On 6 November, the death toll rose to 12.[^cite_note-14] The next day, an additional person died, totaling to 13 victims.[^cite_note-Klix-1] Two women died on 14 November bringing the death toll to 15, and another two women died on 18 November bringing the death toll to 17.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The victims were identified to be from Tuzla and several surrounding towns and municipalities. Their initials were announced on November 6, and full names on November 10.[^cite_note-17]

## Aftermath

In the aftermath of the fire, the director of the retirement home resigned.[^cite_note-18] The local government initiated an investigation into the cause of the fire, and discussed reforming care-home safety in Bosnia and Herzegovina. National and regional leaders offered condolences and neighbouring authorities pledged support. A day of mourning on 6 November was pronounced in the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District),[^cite_note-19] and [Montenegro](20760.md),[^cite_note-20] while [mayor of Tuzla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Tuzla) [Zijad Lugavić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zijad_Lugavi%C4%87) announced four days of mourning in the city.[^cite_note-21]

Protests demanding responsibility for the incident broke out in Tuzla on 5 and 6 November,[^cite_note-22] and resumed on 8 November.[^cite_note-23]

On November 17, the City of Tuzla announced the cancellation of the “Winter in Tuzla” programme, including the New Year’s Eve celebration, citing respect for the victims of the fire.[^cite_note-24]

## Investigation

Authorities said a power cable from a radio used by one of the residents was squeezed between that person's bed and the wall, which led to a short circuit and the fire.[^cite_note-25] The fact that the fire began on a floor with immobile inhabitants raised questions about building safety standards and evacuation protocols.[^cite_note-26] Videos of mistreatment resurfaced, as well as ignored complaints about unsanitary conditions, tiny meals and a 30% rent increase.[^cite_note-\:0-9]
