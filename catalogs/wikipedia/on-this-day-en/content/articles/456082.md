The **director of national intelligence** (**DNI**) is a [cabinet-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States#Current_Cabinet_and_Cabinet-rank_officials) [United States government](195149.md) intelligence and security official. The position is required by the [Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Reform_and_Terrorism_Prevention_Act_of_2004) to serve as executive head of the [United States Intelligence Community](501244.md) (IC) and to direct and oversee the [National Intelligence Program](501244.md#Programs) (NIP). All 18 IC agencies, including the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA), the [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency) (DIA) and the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA), report directly to the DNI. Other federal agencies with intelligence capabilities also report to the DNI, including the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI).

The DNI also serves as the principal advisor to the president of the United States, the [National Security Council](44026.md), and the [Homeland Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Council) on all intelligence matters. The DNI, supported by the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Director_of_National_Intelligence) (ODNI), produces the [President's Daily Brief](584611.md), a highly [classified](3539962.md) document including intelligence from all IC agencies, shared each morning with the president of the United States.[^cite_note-1] The DNI, who is appointed by the president of the United States and is subject to confirmation by the [United States Senate](24909346.md), serves [at the pleasure of the president](2015257.md).

President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) strengthened the role of the DNI on July 30, 2008, with [Executive Order 13470](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13470),[^cite_note-2] which, among other things, solidified the DNI's legal authority to direct intelligence gathering and analysis, and to set policy for intelligence sharing with foreign agencies and for the hiring and firing of senior intelligence officials.[^cite_note-3] The DNI was given further responsibility for the entire IC's [whistleblowing](33821.md) and source protection by President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) via [Presidential Policy Directive 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_19) on October 10, 2012.

The position was elevated to a [cabinet-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cabinet) role during the [first presidency of Donald Trump](52231341.md) and retained this status in subsequent administrations. Currently, the DNI attends all cabinet meetings and liaises with the executive office of the president and other Cabinet secretaries in the execution of their duties. President Donald Trump nominated [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md) to the position in 2025, and she was subsequently confirmed by the US Senate on February 12, 2025.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

On May 22, 2026, Gabbard announced her resignation from the role of DNI, effective June 30, citing her need to care for her husband, who had been diagnosed with bone cancer.[^cite_note-6] On June 11, President Trump nominated [Jay Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Clayton_\(attorney\)), a New York state attorney and former Chair of the [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission), to replace Gabbard as DNI.[^cite_note-7] He was confirmed by the Senate on July 28 and sworn into office on August 3.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## History

### Founding

Before the DNI was formally established, the head of the United States Intelligence Community was the [director of central intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Central_Intelligence) (DCI), who concurrently served as the director of the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA).

The [9/11 Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_Commission) recommended establishing the DNI position in its *[9/11 Commission Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_Commission_Report)*, not released until July 22, 2004, as it had identified major intelligence failures that called into question how well the intelligence community was able to protect U.S. interests against foreign terrorist attacks.

Senators [Dianne Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Feinstein), [Jay Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Rockefeller) and [Bob Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Graham) introduced S. 2645 on June 19, 2002, to create the position of Director of National Intelligence. Other similar legislation soon followed. After considerable debate on the scope of the DNI's powers and authorities, the [United States Congress](31756.md) passed the [Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Reform_and_Terrorism_Prevention_Act) of 2004 by votes of 336–75 in the [House of Representatives](19468510.md), and 89–2 in the [Senate](24909346.md). President George Bush signed the bill into law on December 17, 2004. Among other things, the law established the DNI position as the designated leader of the [United States Intelligence Community](501244.md) and prohibited the DNI from serving as the CIA director or the head of any other intelligence community element at the same time. In addition, the law required the CIA director to report their agency's activities to the DNI.

Critics say compromises during the bill's crafting led to the establishment of a DNI whose powers are too weak to adequately lead, manage and improve the performance of the intelligence community.[^cite_note-10] In particular, the law left the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) in charge of the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA), the [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office) (NRO), and the [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geospatial-Intelligence_Agency) (NGA).

Under [50 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_50_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 3026](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3026), "under ordinary circumstances, it is desirable" that either the director or the principal deputy director of national intelligence be an active-duty commissioned officer in the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_United_States) or have training or experience in military intelligence activities and requirements. Only one of the two positions can be held by a military officer at any given time. The statute does not specify what rank the commissioned officer will hold during their tenure in either position.

### Appointments

The first director of national intelligence was former [U.S. ambassador to Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_ambassadors_to_Iraq) [John Negroponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Negroponte) who was appointed on February 17, 2005, by President George W. Bush, subject to confirmation by the U.S. Senate. It was reported that President Bush's first choice for DNI was former [director of central intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Central_Intelligence) [Robert M. Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Gates), who was serving as president of [Texas A\&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_University), but who declined the offer.[^cite_note-11] Negroponte was confirmed by a Senate vote of 98–2 on April 21, 2005, and he was sworn in by President Bush the same day.

On February 13, 2007, [Mike McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_McConnell_\(U.S._Naval_officer\)) became the second director of national intelligence, after Negroponte was appointed [Deputy Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Secretary_of_State). [Donald M. Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Kerr) was confirmed by the U.S. Senate to be Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence on October 4, 2007, and sworn in on October 9, 2007. Kerr, from Virginia, was previously the director of the [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office) and the deputy director for science and technology at the [CIA](5183633.md) before that. Earlier in his career, he was an assistant director at the [FBI](11127.md), in charge of their Laboratory Division from 1997 to 2001.

On January 29, 2009, retired [Navy](20518076.md) admiral [Dennis C. Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_C._Blair) became the third DNI on after being nominated by newly inaugurated President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama).[^cite_note-12] President Obama dismissed Blair whose resignation became effective May 28, 2010.[^cite_note-13]

On July 20, 2010, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) nominated retired [Air Force](32090.md) lieutenant general [James Clapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clapper) as the fourth DNI. Clapper was confirmed by the U.S. Senate on August 5, and replaced acting director [David C. Gompert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_C._Gompert).

The fifth DNI, [Dan Coats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Coats), the sixth DNI, [John Ratcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ratcliffe_\(American_politician\)), and acting DNIs [Joseph Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Maguire), [Richard Grenell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Grenell) and [Lora Shiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lora_Shiao), all served between March 16, 2017, and January 21, 2021, during the first administration of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md).

The seventh DNI is [Avril Haines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avril_Haines), who took office on January 21, 2021. The first woman to hold the office, she was nominated by President-elect [Joe Biden](145422.md) on November 23, 2020[^cite_note-nom1-14] and confirmed by the Senate on January 20, 2021.[^cite_note-nom2-15] She resigned January 20, 2025 at the conclusion of Joe Biden's term in office.

On November 13, 2024, President-elect [Donald Trump](4848272.md) announced his intention to nominate [Army Reserve](1187028.md) lieutenant colonel [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md) as the director of national intelligence upon returning to the presidency in January 2025. Gabbard was confirmed by the U.S. Senate on February 12, 2025, replacing acting director [Lora Shiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lora_Shiao). With this appointment, she became the first female military combat veteran to serve as DNI, the first [Pacific Islander American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_Americans) and first [Hindu American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_United_States) in this position as well, to hold a [Cabinet-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) position.[^cite_note-IT-DNI-1-16][^cite_note-apnews.com2-17]

### Website issues

[Declan McCullagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declan_McCullagh) at News.com wrote on August 24, 2007, that the DNI site was configured to prevent search engines from indexing pages at DNI.gov. This effectively made the DNI website invisible to all search engines and in turn, any search queries.[^cite_note-18] Ross Feinstein, Spokesman for the DNI, said this configuration was removed as of September 3, 2007. "We're not even sure how \[the [robots.txt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robots.txt) file] got there." On September 7, McCullagh reported that the DNI appeared to be allowing search engine crawling again.[^cite_note-19]

### Reform initiatives

In September 2007, the Office of the DNI released "*Intelligence Community 100 Day & 500 Day Plans for Integration & Collaboration*". These plans include a series of initiatives designed to build the foundation for increased cooperation and reform of the U.S. Intelligence Community.[^cite_note-20]

## Line of succession

The line of succession for the director of national intelligence is as follows:[^cite_note-21]

1. Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence

2. Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Intelligence Integration

3. Director of the [National Counterterrorism Center](3231506.md)

4. Director of the [National Counterintelligence and Security Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Counterintelligence_and_Security_Center)

5. [Inspector General of the Intelligence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_General_of_the_Intelligence_Community)

#### Associate Director of National Intelligence and Chief Information Officer

The **Associate Director of National Intelligence and Chief Information Officer** (**Intelligence Community CIO**, **ADNI/CIO** or **IC CIO**) is charged with directing and managing activities relating to [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) for the [Intelligence Community](501244.md) (IC) and the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Director_of_National_Intelligence) ([ODNI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ODNI)). The IC CIO reports directly to the DNI. As of January 24, 2022, Dr. Adele Merritt has assumed duties as the IC Chief Information Officer.[^cite_note-Merritt_IC_CIO-43] The Office of the IC CIO was established by [Intelligence Community Directive](456082.md) (ICD) 500,[^cite_note-ICD_500-44] "Director of National Intelligence Chief Information Officer," effective August 7, 2008. ICD 500 superseded [Director of Central Intelligence Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Central_Intelligence_Directive) ([DCID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DCID)) 1/6, "The Intelligence Community Chief Information Officer."[^cite_note-DCID1-6-45]

The IC CIO has four primary areas of responsibility:

- Manage activities relating to the [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) infrastructure and [enterprise architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_architecture) of the Intelligence Community;

- Exercise procurement approval authority over all information technology items related to the enterprise architecture of all Intelligence Community components;

- Direct and manage all information technology-related procurement for the Intelligence Community; and

- Ensure all expenditures for information technology and research and development activities are consistent with the Intelligence Community enterprise architecture and the strategy of the Director for such architecture.
