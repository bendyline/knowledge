**Oslo**[^cite_note-13] is the [capital](181337.md) and [most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_and_cities_in_Norway) city of [Norway](21241.md). It constitutes both a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Norway) and a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Norway). The municipality of Oslo had a population of 724,290 in 2025, while the city's greater [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) had a population of 1,110,887 in 2025,[^cite_note-SSB-2023-14] and the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) had an estimated population of 1,546,706 in 2021.[^cite_note-regjeringen2010-15]

During the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age), the area was part of [Viken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viken_\(region\)). Oslo was founded as a city at the end of the Viking Age in 1040 under the name **Ánslo**, and established as a [*kaupstad*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kjobstad) or trading place in 1048 by [Harald Hardrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Hardrada). The city was elevated to a [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Oslo) in 1070 and a capital under [Haakon V of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_V_of_Norway) around the year 1300. [Personal unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) with [Denmark](76972.md) from [1397 to 1523](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union) and again from [1536 to 1814](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway) reduced its influence. After being destroyed by a fire in 1624, during the reign of [King Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV_of_Denmark), a new city was built closer to [Akershus Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus_Fortress) and named **Christiania** in honour of the king. It became a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Norway) (*[formannskapsdistrikt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formannskapsdistrikt)*) on 1 January 1838. The city functioned as the capital of Norway during the 1814–1905 [union between Sweden and Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway). From 1877, the city's name was spelled **Kristiania** in government usage, a spelling that was adopted by the municipal authorities in 1897, although 'Christiania' was also used. In 1925, the city, after incorporating the village retaining its former name, was [renamed](#1900–present) 'Oslo'. In 1948, Oslo merged with [Aker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker,_Norway), a municipality which surrounded the capital and which was 27 times larger, thus creating the modern, much larger Oslo municipality.

Oslo is the [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Norway) and [governmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Norway) centre of Norway. The city is also a hub of Norwegian trade, banking, industry and shipping. It is an important centre for maritime industries and [maritime trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_trade) in Europe. The city is home to many companies within the maritime sector, some of which are among the world's largest shipping companies, shipbrokers and maritime insurance brokers. Oslo is a pilot city of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and the [European Commission](9974.md) intercultural cities programme.

Oslo is considered a [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) and was ranked as a "Beta World City" by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) in 2008.[^cite_note-16] It was ranked number one in terms of quality of life among European large cities in the European Cities of the Future 2012 report by *fDi* magazine.[^cite_note-fdiintelligence.com-17] A survey conducted by [ECA International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECA_International) in 2011 placed Oslo as the second most expensive city in the world for living expenses after [Tokyo](30057.md).[^cite_note-18] In 2013, Oslo tied with the [Australian](4689264.md) city of [Melbourne](17306237.md) as the fourth most expensive city in the world, according to the [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md)'s Worldwide Cost of Living study.[^cite_note-19]

Oslo's population was increasing at record rates during the early 2000s, making it the fastest growing major [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in Europe at the time.[^cite_note-20] This growth originates primarily from international [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Norway) and related high birth rates, but also from intra-national migration. By 2010 the immigrant population in the city was growing somewhat faster than the [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians) population,[^cite_note-21] and in the city proper this had become more than 25% of the total population if the children of immigrant parents were included.[^cite_note-Immigration-22]

## Urban region

See also: [List of boroughs of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_boroughs_of_Oslo)

The municipality of Oslo has a population of 724,290 as of 1 January 2025.[^cite_note-auto-23] The urban area extends far beyond the boundaries of the municipality into the surrounding [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Norway) of [Akershus](171179.md) (municipalities of [Asker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asker), [Bærum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A6rum), [Lillestrøm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillestr%C3%B8m_Municipality), [Enebakk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enebakk), [Rælingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A6lingen), [Lørenskog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B8renskog), [Nittedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nittedal), [Gjerdrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gjerdrum), [Nordre Follo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordre_Follo)); being, to a great degree suburbs of Oslo making up approximately 500,000 of the population of the [greater Oslo region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Oslo_Region). The total population of this [agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agglomerations) was 1,546,706 in 2023.[^cite_note-SSB-2023-14] The city centre is situated at the end of the [Oslofjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslofjord), from which point the city sprawls out in three distinct "corridors"—inland north-eastwards, and southwards along both sides of the fjord—which gives the [urbanized area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) a shape reminiscent of an upside-down reclining "Y" (on maps, satellite pictures, or from high above the city).

To the north and east, wide forested hills (*Marka*) rise above the city giving the location the shape of a giant [amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheatre). The urban [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Norway) (*bykommune*) of Oslo and county (*[fylke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Norway)*) of Oslo are two parts of the same entity, making Oslo the only city in Norway where two administrative levels are integrated. Of Oslo's total area, 130 km<sup>2</sup> (50 sq mi) is built-up and 9.6 km<sup>2</sup> (3.7 sq mi) is agricultural. The open areas within the built-up zone amount to 22 km<sup>2</sup> (8.5 sq mi).[^cite_note-24]

The city of Oslo was established as a municipality on 3 January 1838 (see [formannskapsdistrikt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formannskapsdistrikt)). It was separated from the county of [Akershus](171179.md) to become a county of its own in 1842. The rural municipality of [Aker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker,_Norway) was merged with Oslo on 1 January 1948 (and simultaneously transferred from Akershus county to Oslo county). Furthermore, Oslo shares several important functions with Akershus county.

## Name and seal

After being destroyed by a fire in 1624, during the reign of [King Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV_of_Denmark), a new city was built closer to [Akershus Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus_Fortress) and named Christiania in the king's honour. The old site east of the Aker river was not abandoned, however, and the village of Oslo remained as a suburb outside the city gates. The suburb called Oslo was eventually included in the city proper. In 1925 the name of the suburb was [transferred](#1900–present) to the whole city, while the suburb was renamed "[Gamlebyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Oslo)", literally "the Old town", to avoid confusion.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-Osloby-29][^cite_note-SNL_Historie-30] The Old Town is an area within the administrative district [Gamle Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamle_Oslo). The previous names are reflected in street names like Oslo gate (Oslo street)[^cite_note-31] and Oslo hospital.[^cite_note-32]

### Toponymy

For full article, see [History of Oslo's name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Oslo's_name)

The origin of the name *Oslo* has been the subject of much debate. It is almost certainly derived from [Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse) and was—in all probability—originally the name of a large farm at [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika), while the meaning of that name is disputed. Modern linguists generally interpret the original *Óslo*, *Áslo* or *Ánslo* as either "meadow at the foot of a hill" or "meadow consecrated to the [Gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%86sir)", with both considered equally likely.[^cite_note-33]

Erroneously, it was once assumed that *Oslo* meant "the mouth of the Lo river", a supposed previous name for the [river Alna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alnaelva). Not only has no evidence been found of a river "Lo" predating the work where [Peder Claussøn Friis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peder_Clauss%C3%B8n_Friis) first proposed this etymology, but the very name is ungrammatical in Norwegian: the correct form would have been *Loaros* (cf. [Nidaros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidaros)).[^cite_note-34] The name *Lo* is now believed to be a [back-formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-formation) arrived at by Friis in support of his \[idea about] etymology for *Oslo*.[^cite_note-35]

### Seal

Main article: [Seal of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_Oslo)

Oslo is one of very few cities in Norway, besides [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen) and [Tønsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8nsberg), that does not have a formal [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms), but which uses a city seal instead.[^cite_note-36] The seal of Oslo shows the city's [patron saint](68055.md), [St. Hallvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallvard_Vebj%C3%B8rnsson), with his attributes, the [millstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millstone) and [arrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow), with a naked woman at his feet. He is seated on a throne with lion decorations, which at the time was also commonly used by the [Norwegian kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Norwegian_monarchs).[^cite_note-Heraldry-37]

### Other names

Oslo has various nicknames and names in other languages. The city is sometimes known as "The Tiger City" ([Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language): *Tigerstaden*), probably inspired by an 1870 poem by [Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rnstjerne_Bj%C3%B8rnson) which referenced then-Christiania in central Oslo. The nickname is mostly used by Norwegians from out of town, and rarely by people from the Oslo region.[^cite_note-38]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Oslo).

### Viking Age

During the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age), the area that includes modern Oslo was located in [Viken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viken,_Norway), the northernmost province of [Denmark](76972.md). Control over the area shifted between Danish and Norwegian kings in the Middle Ages, and Denmark continued to claim the area until 1241.

According to the Norse [sagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saga), Oslo was founded around 1049 by [Harald Hardrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Hardrada).[^cite_note-39] Recent archaeological research, however, uncovered Christian burials which could be dated to prior to AD 1000, evidence of a preceding urban settlement.[^cite_note-40] This called for the celebration of Oslo's millennium in 2000 rather than 2049.

### 1000–1600

Main article: [Old Town, Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Oslo)

Under the reign of [Olaf III of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_III_of_Norway) (1067–1093), Oslo became a cultural centre for [Eastern Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Norway). [Hallvard Vebjørnsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallvard_Vebj%C3%B8rnsson) became the city's [patron saint](68055.md) and is depicted on the city's seal.

In 1174, [Hovedøya Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoved%C3%B8ya_Abbey) was built. The churches and abbeys became major owners of large tracts of land, which proved important for the city's economic development, especially before the Black Death.

At the end of the 12th century, [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) traders from [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) moved into the city and gained major influence in the city.

On 25 July 1197, [Sverre of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverre_of_Norway) and his soldiers attacked Oslo from [Hovedøya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoved%C3%B8ya).[^cite_note-AttackFromHovedøya-41]

During the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Oslo reached new heights during the reign of [Haakon V of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_V_of_Norway) (1299–1319). He was the first king to reside permanently in the city, and it has been regarded as the capital city of Norway since his reign. He also started the construction of the [Akershus Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus_Fortress) and the Oslo [Kongsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongsg%C3%A5rd).

The Black Death came to Norway in 1349 and, like other cities in Europe, the city suffered greatly. The churches' earnings from their land dropped so much that the Hanseatic traders dominated the city's foreign trade in the 15th century.

In 1380, Norway was the weaker part in a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) with Denmark, and Oslo's role was reduced to that of provincial administrative centre, with the monarchs residing in [Copenhagen](5166.md).

### 17th century

Over the years, fires destroyed significant parts of the city multiple times, as many of the city's buildings were built entirely of wood. After the fourteenth calamity, in 1624, which lasted for three days, [Christian IV of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV_of_Denmark) decided that the old city should not be rebuilt again. His men built a network of roads on the other side of the bay in Akershagen near [Akershus Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus_Castle). He demanded that all citizens move their shops and workplaces to the newly built city of "Christiania", named in his honour.[^cite_note-42] The part of the city built starting in 1624 is now often called *Kvadraturen&#x20;*&#x20;because of its orthogonal layout in regular, square blocks.[^cite_note-43] [Anatomigården](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomig%C3%A5rden) is a historic timber framing house located on the north side of Christiania Torv; it was built in 1640.

The transformation of Christiania went slowly for the first hundred years. However, outside of the city at [Vaterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaterland,_Norway), [Grønland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%B8nland,_Oslo), and the [old town of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Oslo), a new, unmanaged part of the city arose populated by citizens of lower class status.

The last [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) outbreak in Oslo occurred in 1654.[^cite_note-44]

### 18th century

In the 18th century, after the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War), the city's economy boomed with shipbuilding and trade. The strong economy transformed Christiania into a trading port.

### 19th century

The Royal Frederick University (now the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo)) was founded in 1811; the fact that it was founded this late reportedly had an adverse effect on the development of the nation.[^cite_note-45]

Royal Palace

Royal Palace, Oslo (2015)

In 1814 the former provincial town of Christiania became the capital of the independent Kingdom of Norway, when the union with Denmark was dissolved and replaced by a [personal union with Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway). Several state institutions were established and the city's role as a capital initiated a period of rapidly increasing population. The government of this new state needed buildings for its expanding administration and institutions. Several important buildings and landmarks were erected in the 19th century, including the [Royal Palace](1489319.md) (1825–1848), the [Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Stock_Exchange) (1826–1828), the [Bank of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Norway) (1828), the [Storting](172897.md) (1861–1866), the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_\(Oslo\)) (1899), and several [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) buildings. Among the world-famous artists who lived here during this period were [Henrik Ibsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen) and [Knut Hamsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knut_Hamsun) (the latter was awarded the Nobel Prize for literature).

Large areas of the surrounding [Aker municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker,_Norway) were incorporated in 1839, 1859, and 1878. The 1859 expansion included [Grünerløkka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCnerl%C3%B8kka), [Grønland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%B8nland,_Oslo), and [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Oslo). At that time the area called *Oslo* (now *Gamlebyen* or Old Town) was a village or suburb outside the city borders east of Aker river.[^cite_note-46] The population increased from approximately 10.000 in 1814 to 230.000 in 1900. In 1850, Christiania overtook [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen) and became the most populous city in the country. Christiania expanded its industry from 1840, most importantly around [Akerselva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akerselva). There was a spectacular building boom during the last decades of the 19th century, with many new apartment buildings and renewal of the city centre, but the boom collapsed in 1899.

In 1877 the city was renamed *Kristiania*.

### 20th century



Oslo, 1950s

The city and municipality used the name *Kristiania* until 1 January 1925 when the original name of *Oslo* [was restored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_renaming).[^cite_note-47] This was because Norway became fully independent in 1905, and Norwegians argued that a name memorializing a Danish king ([Christian IV of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV_of_Denmark)) was inappropriate as the name of the capital of their country.[^cite_note-48]

The municipality developed new areas such as [Ullevål garden city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ullev%C3%A5l_Hageby) (1918–1926) and [Torshov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torshov) (1917–1925). [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_City_Hall) was constructed in the former slum area of [Vika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vika,_Oslo) from 1931 to 1950. In 1948, Oslo merged with [Aker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker,_Norway), a municipality which surrounded the capital and was 27 times larger, thus creating the modern, vastly enlarged Oslo municipality. At the time, Aker was a mostly affluent, green suburban community, and the merger was unpopular in Aker.[^cite_note-49] Other suburbs, such as [Lambertseter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambertseter), began to be developed in the 1950s. [Aker Brygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker_Brygge) was constructed on the site of the former [shipyard](213654.md), [Akers Mekaniske Verksted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker_ASA), from 1982 to 1998.

Norway was [invaded by Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Weser%C3%BCbung) on 9 April 1940. Efforts to stop the invasion, most notably the [sinking of the *Blücher*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dr%C3%B8bak_Sound), delayed the occupation of Oslo for several hours which allowed [King Haakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_VII_of_Norway) to escape the city. Oslo remained occupied throughout the war until Germany capitulated in 1945. During this time, the occupying troops were harried by saboteurs in acts of resistance. On 31 December 1944, allied bombers missed their intended target and hit a [tram](30733.md), resulting in 79 civilian deaths.[^cite_note-50]

### 21st century



Oslo, 2022

During the [2011 Norway attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Norway_attacks), Oslo was hit by a bomb blast that ripped through the [Government Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regjeringskvartalet), damaging several buildings including the building that houses the [Office of the Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Prime_Minister_\(Norway\)). Eight people died in the bomb attack.

On 25 June 2022, two people were killed and 21 others injured in [a mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Oslo_shooting). An Iranian-born Norwegian citizen was subsequently charged with "aggravated terrorism".[^cite_note-BBC_News-2022-51][^cite_note-52]

The city has continued to expand. For a few years, new large scale housing areas and infrastructure projects are being built and planned across the city, notably in [Hasle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasle,_Oslo),[^cite_note-53] [Helsfyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsfyr),[^cite_note-54] [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika),[^cite_note-55] [Nydalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nydalen)[^cite_note-56] and [Sinsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinsen), this is increasing the density in and around [Ring 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_2_\(Oslo\)) and [Ring 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_3_\(Oslo\)). The [Fornebu line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fornebu_Line) on the metro is under construction as of 2024, and further subway lines crossing the inner city are being planned.[^cite_note-57]

## Geography

See also: [Oslo Graben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Graben)



Satellite image of Oslo, July 2018



A map of the urban areas of Oslo in 2005. The grey area in the middle indicates Oslo's city centre.

Oslo occupies an arc of land at the northernmost end of the [Oslofjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslofjord). The [fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord), which is nearly bisected by the [Nesodden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesodden) peninsula opposite Oslo, lies to the south; in all other directions Oslo is surrounded by green hills and mountains. There are 40 islands within the city limits, the largest being [Malmøya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B8ya) (0.56 km<sup>2</sup> or 0.22 sq mi), and scores more around the Oslofjord. Oslo has 343 lakes, the largest being [Maridalsvannet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maridalsvannet) (3.91 km<sup>2</sup> or 1.51 sq mi). This is also a main source of drinking water for large parts of Oslo.

Although [Eastern Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Norway) has a number of rivers, none of these flow into the ocean at Oslo. Instead Oslo has two smaller rivers: [Akerselva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akerselva) (draining Maridalsvannet, which flows into the fjord in [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika)), and [Alna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alna_River). The waterfalls in Akerselva gave power to some of the first modern industry of Norway in the 1840s. Later in the century, the river became the symbol of the stable and consistent economic and social divide of the city into [an East End and a West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_and_West_End_of_Oslo); the labourers' neighbourhoods lie on both sides of the river, and the divide in reality follows Uelands street a bit further west. River Alna flows through Groruddalen, Oslo's major suburb and industrial area. The highest point is Kirkeberget, at 629 m (2,064 ft). Although the city's population is small compared to most European capitals, it occupies an unusually large land area, of which two-thirds are [protected areas](100406.md) of forests, hills and lakes. Its boundaries encompass many [parks and open areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_open_spaces_in_Oslo), giving it an airy and green appearance.&#x20;



### Climate

Oslo has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfb*)[^cite_note-en.climate-data.org-58] or, if the original [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate) winter threshold −3 °C (27 °F) is used, an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (*Cfb*) bordering on a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) in the 1991–2020 base period. Oslo has some of the warmest summers of Norway and fairly cold winters.[^cite_note-59] Oslo receives a fair amount of precipitation during the year.[^cite_note-en.climate-data.org-58] The driest seasons are winter and spring, and the wettest are summer and autumn. Because of the city's northern latitude, daylight varies greatly, from more than 18 hours in midsummer, when it never gets completely dark at night (no darker than [nautical twilight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical_twilight)), to around 6 hours in midwinter.[^cite_note-60]

The warmest month on record is July 1901 with mean 22.7 °C (72.9 °F), and the all-time high 35 °C (95 °F) was also recorded in July 1901.[^cite_note-61] The warmest month in more recent years is July 2018 with mean 22.2 °C (72.0 °F) and average daily high 29 °C (84.2 °F). The record summer of 2018 also recorded the warmest May and May all-time high with 31.1 °C (88.0 °F) on 30th, and 2018 was even the sunniest year on record with 2133 hours of sunshine.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] On [27 July 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_European_heat_wave), the temperature in Oslo rose to 34.6 °C (94.3 °F), the hottest recorded since 1937, when weather recordings started at Blindern. In January, on average three out of four days are below freezing (0 °C \[32 °F]) and one out of four days is colder than −10 °C (14 °F) (1961–1990).[^cite_note-met.no-65] The coldest temperature recorded is −29.6 °C (−21.3 °F), on 21 January 1841, while the coldest recorded at Blindern is −26 °C (−14.8 °F) in January 1941. The coldest temperature more recently was on 6 January 2024, where the temperature reached -23.1 °C (-17 °F) at Oslo-Blindern, which is the coldest measured temperature since January 1987. The coldest month on record is January 1941 and also January 1947 with mean −12.9 °C (8.8 °F) and average daily low −16.7 °C (1.9 °F). The average date for the last overnight freeze (low below 0 °C, 32.0 °F) in spring is 23 April[^cite_note-66] and average date for first freeze in autumn is 17 October[^cite_note-67] giving a frost-free season of 176 days (1981–2010 average for Blindern). Oslo sits right on the border between [hardiness zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zones) 7a and 7b.

Oslo Gardermoen airport is located 35 km northeast of Oslo and has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Köppen climate classification: *Dfb*) bordering on a [subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate) (Köppen climate classification: *Dfc*).[^cite_note-68]

## Parks and recreation areas

Main article: [Parks and open spaces in Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_open_spaces_in_Oslo)



[Frogner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogner_Park)'s [Vigeland installation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigeland_installation) in Oslo



[Huk at Bygdøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huk,_Norway) in Oslo

Oslo has many parks and green areas within the city core, as well as outside it.

- [Frogner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogner_Park) is a large park located a few minutes' walk away from the city centre. This is the biggest and best-known park in Norway, with a large collection of sculptures by [Gustav Vigeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Vigeland).

- [Bygdøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bygd%C3%B8y) is a large green area, commonly called the Museum Peninsula of Oslo. The area is surrounded by the sea and is one of the most expensive districts in Norway.

  [^cite_note-71]

- [Ekebergparken Sculpture Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekebergparken_Sculpture_Park) is a sculpture park and a national heritage park with a panoramic view of the city at [Ekeberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekeberg) in the southeast of the city.

- [St. Hanshaugen Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Hanshaugen_Park) is an old public park on a high hill in central Oslo. "St. Hanshaugen" is also the name of the surrounding neighbourhood as well as the larger administrative district (borough) that includes major parts of central Oslo.

  [^cite_note-72]

- [Tøyen Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8yen_Park) stretches out behind the old [Munch Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munch_Museum), and is a vast, grassy expanse. In the north, there is a lookout point known as Ola Narr. The Tøyen area also includes the [Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Botanical_Garden_\(Oslo\)) and Museum belonging to the University of Oslo.

  [^cite_note-73]

Oslo (with neighbouring Sandvika-Asker) is built in a horseshoe shape on the shores of the Oslofjord and limited in most directions by hills and forests. As a result, any point within the city is relatively close to the forest. There are two major forests bordering the city: *[Østmarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98stmarka)* (literally "Eastern Forest", on the eastern perimeter of the city), and the very large *[Nordmarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordmarka)* (literally "Northern Forest", stretching from the northern perimeter of the city deep into the hinterland).

- [Sognsvann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sognsvann) is a lake in Oslomarka, located at the land border, just north of Oslo. Sognsvann was drinking water for Oslo from 1876 to 1967.

The lake's altitude above sea level is 183 metres. The water is in a popular hiking area. Near the water itself, it is great for barbecues, swimming, beach volleyball and other activities.

The municipality operates eight public swimming pools.[^cite_note-74] [Tøyenbadet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8yenbadet) is the largest indoor swimming facility in Oslo and one of the few pools in Norway offering a 50-metre main pool. Another in that size is the outdoor pool [Frognerbadet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frognerbadet).

## Cityscape

[Bryggetorget](https://no.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryggetorget%20\(Oslo\))

Oslo's cityscape is being redeveloped as a modern city with various access-points, an extensive metro-system with a new financial district and a cultural city. In 2008, an exhibition was held in London presenting the award-winning [Oslo Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Opera_House), the urban regeneration scheme of Oslo's seafront, [Munch/Stenersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munch/Stenersen) and the new Deichman Library. ### Architecture

See also: [Architecture of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Norway)



[Fjordbyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjordbyen) is a large construction project in the seaside of central Oslo, stretching from [Bygdøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bygd%C3%B8y) in the west to [Ormøya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orm%C3%B8ya) in the east. Some areas include: [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika), [Aker brygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker_brygge), [Tjuvholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjuvholmen), and the [central station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Central_Station) area.



Detail of modern architecture at [Aker Brygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aker_Brygge)

Oslo's architecture is very diverse. The architect [Carl Frederik Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Frederik_Stanley) (1769–1805), who was educated in [Copenhagen](5166.md), spent some years in Norway around the turn of the 19th century. He undertook some minor commissions for wealthy patrons in and around Oslo, but his major achievement was the renovation of the [Oslo Katedralskole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Katedralskole), completed in 1800.[^cite_note-76] He added a classical portico to the front of an older structure, and a semicircular auditorium that was sequestered by Parliament in 1814 as a temporary place to assemble, now preserved at [Norsk Folkemuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_Folkemuseum) as a national monument.

When Christiania was made capital of Norway in 1814, there were practically no buildings suitable for the many new government institutions. An ambitious building program was initiated, but realised very slowly because of economic constraints. The first major undertaking was the [Royal Palace](1489319.md), designed by [Hans Linstow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Linstow) and built between 1824 and 1848. Linstow also planned [Karl Johans gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Johans_gate), the avenue connecting the Palace and the city, with a monumental square halfway to be surrounded by buildings for [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_University), the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stortinget) (Storting) and other institutions. Only the university buildings were realised according to this plan. [Christian Heinrich Grosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Heinrich_Grosch), one of the first architects educated completely within Norway, designed the original building for the [Oslo Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Stock_Exchange) (1826–1828), the local branch of the [Bank of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Norway) (1828), [Christiania Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiania_Theatre) (1836–1837), and the first campus for the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) (1841–1856). For the university buildings, he sought the assistance of the German architect [Karl Friedrich Schinkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Friedrich_Schinkel). German architectural influence persisted in Norway, and many wooden buildings followed the principles of [Neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism). In Oslo, the German architect [Alexis de Chateauneuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_de_Chateauneuf) designed [Trefoldighetskirken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trefoldighetskirken_\(Oslo\)), the first neo-gothic church, completed by von Hanno in 1858.

A number of landmark buildings, particularly in Oslo, were built in the [Functionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functionalism_\(architecture\)) style (better known in the US and Britain as Modernist), the first being [Skansen restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skansen_restaurant?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1925–1927) by [Lars Backer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Backer), demolished in 1970. Backer also designed the restaurant at Ekeberg, which opened in 1929. [Kunstnernes Hus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstnernes_Hus) art gallery by [Gudolf Blakstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudolf_Blakstad) and [Herman Munthe-Kaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Munthe-Kaas) (1930) still shows the influence of the preceding classicist trend of the 1920s. The redevelopment of [Oslo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardermoen) (by the Aviaplan consortium) at Gardermoen, which opened in 1998, was Norway's largest construction project to date.

Oslo is the capital of Norway, and as such is the seat of Norway's national government. Most government offices, including that of the [Prime Minister](220458.md), are gathered at *Regjeringskvartalet*, a cluster of buildings close to the national Parliament, the [Storting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Norway).

Constituting both a municipality and a county of Norway, the city of Oslo is represented in the Storting by twenty members of parliament. The [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Norway) is the most represented party in Oslo with five members, the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Labour_Party) has four, the [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Norway) and the [Socialist Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Left_Party_\(Norway\)) have three each; the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Norway\)), the [Red Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Party_\(Norway\)) have two each and the [Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Party_\(Norway\)) has one.[^cite_note-m595-78]

The combined municipality and county of Oslo has had a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) of government since 1986. The supreme authority of the city is the City Council (*Bystyret*), which has 59 seats. Representatives are popularly elected every four years. The city council has five [standing committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_committees), each having its own areas of responsibility.

### Recent elections

The largest parties in the City Council after the 2015-elections were the Labour Party and the Conservatives, with 20 and 19 representatives respectively. After 2015, the [city government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) was a coalition of the Labour Party, the Green Party and the Socialist Left. With support from the Red Party, the coalition maintained a workable majority in the city council. Following the local elections of 2019, the [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics) coalition remained in government until the 2023 local elections.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Greater Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Greater_Oslo)



Office buildings and apartments in [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika), part of the redesign of former dock and industrial land in Oslo known as the [Barcode Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcode_Project)



Børsen, the building of [Oslo Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Stock_Exchange)

Oslo has a varied and strong economy and was ranked number one among European large cities in economic potential in the [fDi Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDi_Magazine) report European Cities of the Future 2012.[^cite_note-fdiintelligence.com-17] It was ranked 2nd in the category of business friendliness, behind Amsterdam.

Oslo is an important centre of maritime knowledge in Europe and is home to approximately 1980 companies and 8,500 employees within the maritime sector. Some of them are the world's largest shipping companies, [shipbrokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbroker), and [insurance brokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_broker).[^cite_note-80] [Det Norske Veritas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Det_Norske_Veritas), headquartered at [Høvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8vik) outside Oslo, is one of the three major maritime [classification societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_society) in the world, with 16.5% of the world fleet to class in its register.[^cite_note-81] The city's port is the largest general cargo port in the country and its leading passenger gateway. Close to 6,000 ships dock at the [Port of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Oslo) annually with a total of 6 million tonnes of cargo and over five million passengers.

The [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) of Oslo totaled €64 billion (€96,000 per capita) in 2016, which amounted to 20% of the national GDP.[^cite_note-82] This compares with [NOK](64149.md)253 billion (€23 billion) in 1995 (adjusting for 2016 inflation). The metropolitan area, bar [Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss,_Norway) and [Drammen](178144.md), contributed 25% of the national GDP in 2003 and was also responsible for more than one quarter of [tax revenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_revenue). In comparison, total tax revenues from the oil and gas industry on the [Norwegian Continental Shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_continental_shelf) amounted to about 16%.[^cite_note-83]

Oslo is one of the [most expensive cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_cities_for_expatriate_employees) in the world.[^cite_note-priciest-84] As of 2006, it is ranked tenth according to the Worldwide [Cost of Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-of-living_index) Survey provided by [Mercer Human Resource Consulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_Human_Resource_Consulting)[^cite_note-85] and first according to the [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) (EIU).[^cite_note-priciest-84] The reason for this discrepancy is that the EIU omits certain factors from its final index calculation, most notably housing. In the 2015 update[^cite_note-86] of the EIU's Worldwide Cost of Living survey, Oslo now ranks as the third most expensive city in the world.[^cite_note-87] Although Oslo does have the most expensive [housing market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_economics) in Norway, it is comparably cheaper than other cities on the list in that regard. Meanwhile, prices on [goods and services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_and_services) remain some of the highest of any city. Oslo hosts 2654 of the largest companies in Norway. Within the ranking of Europe's largest cities ordered by their number of companies Oslo is in fifth position. A whole group of oil and gas companies is situated in Oslo.

According to a report compiled by [Swiss bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_Switzerland) [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS) in the month of August 2006,[^cite_note-88] Oslo and London were the world's most expensive cities.

## Environment and decarbonization

Oslo is a [compact city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_city). It is easy to move around by public transportation and rentable city bikes accessible to all in many places in the city centre.[^cite_note-89] In 2003, Oslo received The European Sustainable City Award and in 2007 Reader's Digest ranked Oslo as number two on a list of the world's greenest, most livable cities.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] In 2019 Oslo was selected as the [European Green Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Capital_Award).

The City of Oslo has set the goal of becoming a low carbon city, and reducing [greenhouse gas emissions](21350772.md) 95% from 1990 levels by 2030.[^cite_note-92] To reach this goal, in 2017, under the leadership of the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Norway\)), Oslo became the first city in the world to implement a [climate budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_budget?action=edit\&redlink=1). This tool makes each individual department and agency of the local government responsible for counting their emissions and cut them according the budget. The progress has to be reported together with the economical budget reports, three times a year.[^cite_note-93] The [C40 Cities Climate Leadership Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C40_Cities_Climate_Leadership_Group) later adopted having a climate budget by 2030 as one of their criteria for membership.[^cite_note-94]

By October 2022, Oslo had an extensive network of bicycle lanes and tram lines, most of its ferry boats had been electrified, and the city was "on course to become the first capital city in the world with an all-electric public transport system", including [electric buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bus).[^cite_note-95] The climate action plan for the Port of Oslo includes implementing a low-carbon contracting process, and installing [shore power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorepower) for vessels which are docked.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

## Culture



The [Henriette Wegner Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henriette_Wegner_Pavilion), an 1824 tea pavilion and art gallery in [Frogner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogner_Park)

Oslo has a large and varied number of [cultural attractions](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Oslo), which include several buildings containing artwork from [Edvard Munch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Munch) and various other international artists but also several [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_people) artists. Several world-famous writers have either lived or been born in Oslo. Examples are [Knut Hamsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knut_Hamsun) and [Henrik Ibsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen). The government has recently invested large amounts of money in cultural installations, facilities, buildings and festivals in the City of Oslo. [Bygdøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bygd%C3%B8y), outside the city centre, is the centre for history and the Norwegian Vikings' history. The area contains many parks and seasites and many museums. Examples are the [Fram Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fram_Museum), [Vikingskiphuset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Ship_Museum_\(Oslo\)) and the [Kon-Tiki Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kon-Tiki_Museum). Oslo hosts the annual Oslo Freedom Forum, a conference described by *The Economist* as "on its way to becoming a human-rights equivalent of the Davos economic forum."[^cite_note-104] Oslo is also known for giving out the [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) every year.

### Museums, galleries



MUNCH Museum, 2022

Oslo houses several major museums and galleries. The [Munch Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munch_Museum) contains *[The Scream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scream)* and other works by [Edvard Munch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Munch), who donated all his work to the city after his death.[^cite_note-108] The city council is planning a new Munch Museum which is most likely to be built in [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika), in the southeast of the city.[^cite_note-autogenerated5-109] The museum will be named [Munch/Stenersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munch/Stenersen).[^cite_note-autogenerated5-109] 50 different museums are located around the city.[^cite_note-110]

[Folkemuseet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_Folkemuseum) is located on the [Bygdøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bygd%C3%B8y) peninsula and is dedicated to [Folk art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_art), Folk Dress, [Sami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_people) culture and the [viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) culture. The outdoor museum contains 155 authentic old buildings from all parts of Norway, including a [Stave Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stave_Church).[^cite_note-111]

The [Vigeland Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigeland_Museum) located in the large [Frogner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogner_Park), is free to access and contains over 212 sculptures by Gustav Vigeland including an obelisk and the Wheel of Life.[^cite_note-112] Another popular sculpture is Sinnataggen, a baby boy stamping his foot in fury. This statue is very well known as an icon in the city.[^cite_note-113] There is also a newer landscaped sculpture park, [Ekebergparken Sculpture Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekebergparken_Sculpture_Park), with works by Norwegian and international artists such as [Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD).[^cite_note-114]



Street in the "Old Town" section of [Norsk Folkemuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_Folkemuseum)

The [Viking Ship Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Ship_Museum_\(Oslo\)) features three [Viking ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_ship) found at Oseberg, Gokstad and Tune and several other unique items from the Viking Age.[^cite_note-115] The museum is currently closed for renovation, but will open again in 2026.[^cite_note-thenorwayguide.com-2022-116][^cite_note-117] The new museum will be called Museum of the Viking Age, and has plans to feature more viking items than at the old location.[^cite_note-thenorwayguide.com-2022-116][^cite_note-118]

The [Oslo City Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_City_Museum) holds a permanent exhibition about the people in Oslo and the history of the city.[^cite_note-119]

The [Kon-Tiki Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kon-Tiki_Museum) houses [Thor Heyerdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_Heyerdahl)'s Kon-tiki and Ra II.[^cite_note-120]

The [Fram Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fram_Museum) features items from arctic and antarctic expeditions, including the wooden ship [Fram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fram_\(ship\)) used by [Fritjof Nansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridtjof_Nansen) and [Roald Amundsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Amundsen) during their expeditions.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

[The National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Art,_Architecture_and_Design) holds and preserves, exhibits and promotes public knowledge about Norway's most extensive collection of art.[^cite_note-autogenerated6-123] The museum shows permanent exhibitions of works from its own collections but also temporary exhibitions that incorporate work loaned from elsewhere.[^cite_note-autogenerated6-123] The National Museums exhibition avenues are the National Gallery, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the National Museum, the Museum of Decorative Arts and the National Museum of Architecture.[^cite_note-autogenerated6-123] A new National Museum in Oslo will open in 2020 located at [Vestbanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Vestbanestasjon) behind the [Nobel Peace Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Center).[^cite_note-124]

The Nobel Peace Center is an independent organisation opened on 11 June 2005 by the [King Harald V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Harald_V) as part of the celebrations to mark Norway's centenary as an independent country.[^cite_note-autogenerated4-125] The building houses a permanent exhibition, expanding every year when a new [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) winner is announced, containing information of every winner in history. The building is mainly used as a communication centre.[^cite_note-autogenerated4-125]

### Music and events



[Nobel Peace Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Center)

Many festivals are held in Oslo, such as Oslo Jazz festival, a six-day [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) festival which has been held annually in August for the past 25 years.[^cite_note-OJF-126] Oslo's biggest [rock](25423.md) festival is Øyafestivalen or simply "Øya". It draws about 60,000 people to the [Tøyen Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8yen_Park) east in Oslo and lasts for four days.[^cite_note-Øya-127] The Oslo International [Church Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Music) Festival[^cite_note-128] has been held annually since 2000. The Oslo [World Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Music) Festival showcases people who are stars in their own country but strangers in Norway. The Oslo [Chamber Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_Music) Festival is held in August every year and world-class chamber musicians and soloists gather in Oslo to perform at this festival. The Norwegian Wood Rock Festival is held every year in June in Oslo.

The [Nobel Peace Prize Ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_prize) is headed by [the Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Institute); the award ceremony is held annually in [The City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_r%C3%A5dhus) on 10 December.[^cite_note-NobelPrize-129] Even though [Sami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_people) land is far away from the capital, the Norwegian Museum of Cultural History marks the Sami National Day with a series of activities and entertainment.

The World Cup Biathlon in [Holmenkollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmenkollen) is held every year and here male and female competitors compete against each other in Sprint, Pursuit and Mass Start disciplines.[^cite_note-Biathlon-130]

Other examples of annual events in Oslo are Desucon, a convention focusing on Japanese culture[^cite_note-Desucon-131] and [Færderseilasen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A6rderseilasen), the world's largest overnight regatta with more than 1100 boats taking part every year.[^cite_note-Færderseilasen-132]

[Rikard Nordraak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikard_Nordraak), composer of the [national anthem of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem_of_Norway), was born in Oslo in 1842.

Norway's principal orchestra is the [Oslo Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Philharmonic), based at the [Oslo Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Concert_Hall) since 1977. Although it was founded in 1919, the Oslo Philharmonic can trace its roots to the founding of the *Christiania Musikerforening* (Christiania Musicians Society) by [Edvard Grieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Grieg) and [Johan Svendsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Svendsen) in 1879.[^cite_note-Oslo-Filharmonien-133]

Oslo has hosted the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) twice, in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1996) and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2010).

### Performing arts



[Oslo Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Opera_House)



The National Theatre is the largest theatre in Norway.

[^cite_note-autogenerated3-134]

Oslo houses over 20 theatres, such as the Norwegian Theatre and the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_\(Oslo\)) located at Karl Johan Street. The National Theatre is the largest theatre in Norway and is situated between the royal palace and the parliament building, [Stortinget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stortinget).[^cite_note-autogenerated3-134] The names of [Ludvig Holberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludvig_Holberg), [Henrik Ibsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen) and [Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rnstjerne_Bj%C3%B8rnson) are engraved on the façade of the building over the main entrance. This theatre represents the actors and play-writers of the country. On the other hand, songwriters, singers and dancers are represented in the form of a newly opened [Oslo Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Opera_House), situated in [Bjørvika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rvika). The Opera was opened in 2008 and is a national landmark, designed by the Norwegian architectural firm, [Snøhetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sn%C3%B8hetta_\(company\)). There are two houses, together containing over 2000 seats. The building cost 500 million euro to build and took five years to build and is known for being the first [Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_House) in the world to let people walk on the roof of the building. The foyer and the roof are also used for concerts as well as the three stages.[^cite_note-135]

### Literature and drama

Most great Norwegian authors have lived in Oslo for some period in their life. For instance, [Nobel Prize](23385442.md)-winning author [Sigrid Undset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigrid_Undset) grew up in Oslo, and described her life there in the autobiographical novel *Elleve år* (1934; translated as *The Longest Years*; New York 1971).

The playwright [Henrik Ibsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen) is probably the most famous Norwegian author. Ibsen wrote plays such as *[Hedda Gabler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedda_Gabler)*, *[Peer Gynt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_Gynt)*, *[A Doll's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Doll's_House)* and *[The Lady from the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lady_from_the_Sea)*. The [Ibsen Quotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibsen_quotes,_Oslo) project completed in 2008 is a work of art consisting of 69 Ibsen quotations in stainless steel lettering which have been set into the granite sidewalks of the city's central streets.[^cite_note-okland-136]

In recent years, novelists like [Lars Saabye Christensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Saabye_Christensen), [Tove Nilsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tove_Nilsen), [Suresh Chandra Shukla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suresh_Chandra_Shukla?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Jo Nesbø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Nesb%C3%B8) and [Roy Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Jacobsen) have described the city and its people in their novels. Early 20th-century literature from Oslo include poets [Rudolf Nilsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Nilsen) and [André Bjerke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Bjerke).

The newspapers *[Aftenposten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftenposten), *[Dagbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagbladet)*, [Verdens Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdens_Gang), [Dagens Næringsliv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagens_N%C3%A6ringsliv)*, *[Finansavisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finansavisen)*, *[Dagsavisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagsavisen)*, *[Morgenbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgenbladet)*, *[Vårt Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A5rt_Land_\(Norwegian_newspaper\)), [Nationen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationen) and [Klassekampen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klassekampen)* are published in Oslo. ### Sports



[Bislett Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislett_Stadium) during a friendly between [Lyn Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Oslo) and [Liverpool F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.)

Oslo is home to the [Holmenkollen National Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmenkollen_National_Arena) and [Holmenkollbakken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmenkollbakken), the country's main [biathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon) and [Nordic skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_skiing) venues. It hosts annual world cup tournaments, including the [Holmenkollen Ski Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmenkollen_Ski_Festival). Oslo hosted the [Biathlon World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon_World_Championships) in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon_World_Championships_1986), [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon_World_Championships_1990), [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon_World_Championships_2000), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon_World_Championships_2002) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon_World_Championships_2016). [FIS Nordic World Ski Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships) have been hosted in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships_1930), [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships_1966), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships_1982) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships_2011), as well as the [1952 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Winter_Olympics).

Oslo is the home of several football clubs in the [Norwegian league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_football_league_system). [Vålerenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A5lerenga_Fotball), [Lyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Lyn) and [Skeid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeid_Fotball) have won both the [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Premier_League) and the [cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Football_Cup), [KFUM-Kameratene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFUM-Kameratene_Oslo) entered the [top division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliteserien) in 2024. while Mercantile SFK and [Frigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigg_Oslo_FK) have won the cup. *Lyn og Frig* a combined team has faced touring sides such as [Aston Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Villa) in 1926.

[Ullevål Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ullev%C3%A5l_Stadion) is the home arena for the [Norway national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_national_football_team) and the [Football Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Football_Cup). The stadium has previously hosted the finals of the [UEFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Championship) in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_European_Competition_for_Women's_Football) and [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_1997), and the [2002 UEFA European Under-19 Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_UEFA_European_Under-19_Football_Championship).[^cite_note-historikk-137] [Røa IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B8a_IL) is Oslo's only team in the women's league, [Toppserien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toppserien). Each year, the international youth football tournament [Norway Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_Cup) is held on [Ekebergsletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekebergsletta) and other places in the city.

Due to the cold climate and proximity to major forests bordering the city, skiing is a popular recreational activity in Oslo. The [Tryvann Ski Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryvann_Ski_Resort) is the most used ski resort in Norway.[^cite_note-obl1-138] The most successful [ice hockey](14790.md) team in Norway, [Vålerenga Ishockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A5lerenga_Ishockey), is based in Oslo. [Manglerud Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manglerud_Star) is another Oslo-team who play in the [top league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GET-ligaen).

[Bislett Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislett_Stadium) is the city's main track and field venue, and hosts the annual [Bislett Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislett_Games), part of [Diamond League](21778161.md). [Bjerke Travbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjerke_Travbane) is the main venue for [harness racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harness_racing) in the country. [Oslo Spektrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Spektrum) is used for large [ice hockey](14790.md) and [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball) matches. [Nordstrand HE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordstrand_IF) and [Oppsal IF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppsal_IF) plays in the women's [GRUNDIGligaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRUNDIGligaen_\(women\)) in handball, while [Bækkelaget HE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A6kkelagets_SK) plays in the [men's league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRUNDIGligaen). [Jordal Amfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordal_Amfi), the home of the ice hockey team [Vålerenga Ishockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A5lerenga_Ishockey), and the [national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_men's_national_ice_hockey_team). The [1999 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_IIHF_World_Championship) in ice hockey were held in Oslo, as have three [Bandy World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy_World_Championship), in [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Bandy_World_Championship), [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Bandy_World_Championship) and [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Bandy_World_Championship). The [UCI Road World Championships](7171411.md) in [bicycle road racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_road_racing) were hosted [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_UCI_Road_World_Championships).

## Crime



Norway Supreme Court

Oslo is commonly regarded as one of the safest capitals in Europe.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] The [Oslo Police District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Police_District) received 72,102 reported crimes in 2020, and crime is generally on the decrease in the city.[^cite_note-143]\[^cite_note-[www.faktisk.no-2022-144](http://www.faktisk.no-2022-144/)] The category of reported crime that's decreasing the quickest in Oslo is property theft.\[^cite_note-[www.faktisk.no-2022-144](http://www.faktisk.no-2022-144/)] 11.6% of all crimes in Norway are reported to be within Oslo's six central boroughs, as of 2020.[^cite_note-145]

### Police



Grønland police station

[Oslo Police District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Police_District) is Norway's largest police district with over 2,300 employees. Over 1,700 of those are police officers, nearly 140 police lawyers and 500 civilian employees. Oslo Police District has five police stations located around the city at Grønland, Sentrum, Stovner, Majorstuen and Manglerud. The [National Criminal Investigation Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Criminal_Investigation_Service_\(Norway\)) is located in Oslo, which is a Norwegian special police division under the [NMJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Ministry_of_Justice_and_the_Police). [PST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politiets_sikkerhetstjeneste) is also located in the Oslo District. PST is a security agency which was established in 1936 and is one of the non-secret agencies in Norway.

## Transport

[Oslo Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_S), 2021

### Public transport

Oslo has Norway's most extensive public transport system, managed by [Ruter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruter).[^cite_note-149] This includes the five-line [Oslo Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Metro),[^cite_note-150] the world's most extensive metro per resident; the six-line [Oslo Tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Oslo);[^cite_note-151] and the eight-line [Oslo Commuter Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Commuter_Rail).[^cite_note-152] The tramway operates within the areas close to the city centre, while the metro, which runs underground through the city centre, operates to suburbs further away; this includes two lines that operate to Bærum, and the [Ring Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_Line_\(Oslo\)) which loops to areas north of the centre.[^cite_note-153] Oslo is also covered by a bus network consisting of 52 city lines, as well as regional buses to the neighboring county of Akershus.[^cite_note-154]

[Oslo Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Central_Station) acts as the central hub,[^cite_note-romplan-155] and offers rail services to most major cities in southern Norway as well as [Stockholm](26741.md) and [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg) in Sweden.[^cite_note-156] The [Airport Express Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flytoget) operates along the high-speed [Gardermoen Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardermoen_Line). The [Drammen Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen_Line) runs under the city centre in the [Oslo Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Tunnel).[^cite_note-157] Some of the city islands and the neighbouring municipality of [Nesodden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesodden) are connected by ferry.[^cite_note-158] Daily [cruiseferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiseferry) services operate to [Copenhagen](5166.md) and [Frederikshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederikshavn) in Denmark, and to [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel) in Germany.[^cite_note-159]

### Roads and automobiles



[European route E6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E6) at Teisen in Oslo

Many of the motorways pass through the downtown and other parts of the city in tunnels. The construction of the roads is partially supported through a [toll ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road).[^cite_note-164] The major motorways through Oslo are [European Route E6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E6) and [E18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E18). There are three [ring roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road) in Oslo; the innermost 2 being city streets and the outermost, [Ring 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_3_\(Oslo\)), being an [expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-access_road).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

Oslo has made an effort since the late 2000s in restricting private car use, as well promoting the use of [electric vehicles](279350.md) above fossil-fueled vehicles. In 2018, Oslo banned all non-resident cars from its downtown areas.[^cite_note-Jones-2018-167][^cite_note-168] Oslo has been called the electric vehicle capital of the world, as 41% of all registered cars in the municipality are fully electric.[^cite_note-Crosse-2018-169] [^cite_note-170] In September 2021, the number of electric vehicles entering Oslo's toll ring was higher than the number of fossil-fueled vehicles.[^cite_note-171] The high amount of electric vehicles in Oslo can be attributed to cheaper tolls, no vehicle import tax, no [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT), free parking, and access to [bus lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_lane) throughout the city.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-Crosse-2018-169]

The population of Oslo was by 2010 increasing at a record rate of nearly 2% annually (17% over the last 10 years), making it the fastest-growing Scandinavian capital.[^cite_note-177] In 2015, according to [Statistics Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Norway) annual report, there were 647,676 permanent residents in the Oslo municipality, of which 628,719 resided in the city proper. There were also 1,019,451 in the city's urban area[^cite_note-Stats2015_b-3][^cite_note-Stats2015_a-173][^cite_note-OneMil-178] and an estimated 1,710,000 in the Greater Oslo Region, within 100 km (62 mi) of the city centre.[^cite_note-regjeringen2010-15]

According to the most recent census 432,000 Oslo residents (70.4% of the population) were ethnically Norwegian, an increase of 6% since 2002 (409,000).[^cite_note-179] Oslo has the largest population of immigrants and Norwegians born to immigrant parents in Norway, both in relative and absolute figures. Of Oslo's 624,000 inhabitants, 189,400 were immigrants or born to immigrant parents, representing 30.4 percent of the capital's population. All suburbs in Oslo were above the national average of 14.1 percent. The suburbs with the highest proportions of people of immigrant origin were Søndre Nordstrand, Stovner and Alna, where they formed around 50 percent of the population.[^cite_note-180]

[Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Norwegians) make up the single largest [ethnic minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minority), followed by [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Somalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people), and [Swedes](29380512.md). Other large immigrant groups are people from [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Norwegians), [Turkey](11125639.md), [Morocco](19291.md), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Norwegian) & [Kurdistan region](679693.md) and [Iran](14653.md) & [Kordestan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kordestan_province).[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

In 2013, 40% of Oslo's primary school pupils were registered as having a [first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) other than Norwegian.[^cite_note-185] The western part of the city is predominantly ethnic Norwegian, with several schools having less than 5% pupils with an immigrant background. The eastern part of Oslo is more mixed, with some schools up to 97% of immigrant background. In the borough of [Groruddalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grorud_Valley) in 2008 for instance, the ethnic Norwegian population decreased by 1,500, while the immigrant population increased by 1,600.[^cite_note-186]

As of 2022, immigrants of non-Western origin and their children enumerated 164,824, and made up an estimated 24% of Oslo's population.[^cite_note-\:0-187]

Immigrants of Western origin and their children enumerated 71,858, and made up an estimated 10% of the city's population. Immigrants made up a total of 35% of Oslo's population in 2022.[^cite_note-\:0-187][^cite_note-188]

## International relations

Oslo is a pilot city of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and the [European Commission](9974.md)'s *Intercultural cities* programme, along with a number of other European cities.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]

### Twin towns – sister cities

See also: [List of twin towns and sister cities in Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_twin_towns_and_sister_cities_in_Norway)

Oslo was formerly twinned with [Minneapolis](6097240.md), [Tel Aviv](31453.md) and [Vilnius](32597.md), but has since abolished the concept of twin cities.

### Cooperation agreements

As of 2012, Oslo had cooperation agreements with:[^cite_note-int_coop-212]

-  [Artvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artvin), Turkey

-  [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg_Municipality), Sweden

-  [Mbombela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbombela_Local_Municipality), South Africa

-  [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), Russia

-  [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md), Germany

-  [Shanghai](27643.md), China

-  [Vilnius](32597.md), Lithuania

-  [Warsaw](32908.md), Poland

### Christmas trees as gifts

Oslo has a tradition of sending a [Christmas tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree) every year to the cities of [Washington, D.C.](108956.md); [New York City](645042.md); [London](17867.md); [Edinburgh](9602.md); [Rotterdam](26049.md); [Antwerp](32149462.md) and [Reykjavík](25798.md).[^cite_note-213] Since 1947, Oslo has sent a 65-to-80 ft-high (20-to-24 m), 50 to 100-year-old [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_Spruce), as an expression of gratitude toward Britain for its support of Norway during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215]
