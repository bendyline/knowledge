**Fairfax County**, officially the **County of Fairfax**, is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)) in the state of [Virginia](32432.md), United States. With a population of 1,150,309 as of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-QF-3] it is the most populous county in Virginia, the most populous jurisdiction in the [Washington metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area), and the most populous location in the [Washington–Baltimore combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington%E2%80%93Baltimore_combined_statistical_area). The [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) is [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia); however, because it is an [independent city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)#Virginia) under Virginia law, the city of Fairfax is not part of the county.[^cite_note-GR6-4]

The county is part of the [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia) region and forms part of the suburban ring of [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), the nation's capital. The county is predominantly [suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) with some [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and [rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_area) pockets. It borders [Montgomery County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Maryland) to its north, [Falls Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church,_Virginia), [Alexandria](18940583.md), [Arlington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), and [Prince George's County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George's_County,_Maryland) to its east, [Charles County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_County,_Maryland) to its southeast, [Prince William County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County,_Virginia) to its southwest, and [Loudoun County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudoun_County,_Virginia) to its northwest.&#x20;

The county is home to the offices of the [director of national intelligence](456082.md) in [McLean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLean,_Virginia) and the headquarters of four [U.S. intelligence agencies](501244.md): the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) at the [George Bush Center for Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bush_Center_for_Intelligence) in [Langley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley,_Virginia), the [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geospatial-Intelligence_Agency) at [Fort Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir), the [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office) in [Chantilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantilly,_Virginia), and the [National Counterterrorism Center](3231506.md) in McLean.

In academia, the county is home to the flagship campus of [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University) in Fairfax, [CIA University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_University) in Chantilly, [Sherman Kent School for Intelligence Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Kent_School_for_Intelligence_Analysis) in [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia), and several [Northern Virginia Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia_Community_College) campuses. In the private sector, as of 2025, eleven of the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies are headquartered in the county as of 2023.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

In 2020, Fairfax County's median household income of \$127,866 was the [fifth-highest in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-income_counties_in_the_United_States).

Prior to [European settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), present-day Fairfax County was initially inhabited by the [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages)-speaking [Doeg tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doeg_people).

### 17th century

In 1608, [Captain John Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smith_\(explorer\)) documented the Doeg tribe's villages, which included *Namassingakent* and *Nemaroughquand* on the south bank of the [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River) in present-day Fairfax County.[^cite_note-swanton-7] By 1670, Virginian colonists from the [Northern Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Neck) region drove the Doeg Tribe out of present-day Fairfax County and into [Maryland](18858.md).

### 18th century

Fairfax County was formed in 1742 from the northern part of [Prince William County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County,_Virginia), and was named after [Thomas Fairfax, 6th Lord Fairfax of Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fairfax,_6th_Lord_Fairfax_of_Cameron), proprietor of the [Northern Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Neck_Proprietary).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The county's logo, seal and flag were adopted from the arms of [Lord Fairfax of Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Fairfax_of_Cameron).

The county's initial settlements were along the Potomac River. [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) settled in Fairfax County and built his home, [Mount Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon), facing the Potomac. [Gunston Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunston_Hall), the home of [George Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason), is nearby. [Fort Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir) is partly on the estate of Belvoir Manor, built along the Potomac by William Fairfax in 1741.

[Thomas Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fairfax,_6th_Lord_Fairfax_of_Cameron), the only member of the British nobility ever to reside in the colonies, lived at [Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belvoir,_Virginia) before moving to the [Shenandoah Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenandoah_Valley). The Belvoir mansion and several of its outbuildings were destroyed by fire immediately after the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) in 1783, and [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) noted the plantation complex deteriorated into ruins.

In 1757, the northwestern two-thirds of Fairfax County became [Loudoun County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudoun_County,_Virginia). In 1789, part of Fairfax County was ceded to the [federal government](195149.md) to form [Alexandria County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_County,_Virginia), then part of the [District of Columbia](108956.md).

### 19th century

Alexandria County was [returned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia_retrocession) to Virginia in 1846, reduced in size by the secession of the [independent city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)#Virginia) of [Alexandria](18940583.md) in 1870, and renamed [Arlington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia) in 1920.

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) the [Battle of Chantilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chantilly), also known as Ox Hill, part of the [Second Battle of Bull Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Bull_Run), was fought within Fairfax County. Other areas of Civil War conflict in the county included [Minor's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor's_Hill), [Munson's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munson's_Hill), and [Upton's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upton's_Hill).

### 20th century

The Fairfax County town of [Falls Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church,_Virginia) became an independent city in 1948.[^cite_note-10] The Fairfax County town of Fairfax was named an independent city in 1961.[^cite_note-City_History-11]

The [federal government](195149.md)'s growth during and after [World War II](32927.md) spurred rapid growth in the county and transformed it from a rural to suburban region. Large businesses began settling in the county, and the opening of [Tysons Corner Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons_Corner_Center) spurred the rise of [Tysons, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia). The technology boom and a steady government-driven economy created rapid growth and an increasingly large and diverse population. The economy has also made Fairfax County one of the nation's wealthiest counties.[^cite_note-12]

A general aviation airport along [U.S. Route 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_50) west of [Seven Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Corners,_Virginia), [Falls Church Airpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church_Airpark), operated in the county from 1948 to 1960. The facility's 2,650-foot unpaved runway was used extensively by private pilots and civil defense officials. Residential development, multiple accidents, and the demand for retail space led to its closure in 1960.[^cite_note-Paul-13][^cite_note-Rollo-14][^cite_note-15]

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the county has an area of 406 square miles (1,050 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 391 square miles (1,010 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 15 square miles (39 km<sup>2</sup>) (3.8%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-16] There are about 44,400,000 trees that create a canopy covering 55.4% of the total land area.[^cite_note-17]

Fairfax County is bounded on the north and southeast by the [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River). Across the river to the northeast is [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), across the river to the north is [Montgomery County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Maryland), and across the river to the southeast are [Prince George's County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George's_County,_Maryland) and [Charles County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_County,_Maryland). The county is partially bounded on the north and east by [Arlington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia) and the independent cities of [Alexandria](18940583.md) and [Falls Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church,_Virginia). It is bounded on the west by [Loudoun County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudoun_County,_Virginia), and on the south by [Prince William County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County,_Virginia).

Most of the county lies in the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(United_States\)) region, with rolling hills and deep stream valleys, such as [Difficult Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difficult_Run) and its tributaries. West of [Route 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Route_28), the hills give way to a flat, gentle valley that stretches west to the [Bull Run Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Run_Mountains) in Loudoun County. Elevations in the county range from near sea level along the tidal sections of the Potomac River in the southeast portion of the county to more than 500 feet (150 m) in the [Tysons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia) area.

In 2023, the plant [hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) shifted from 7a to 7b. The Fairfax County News Letter predicts that Fairfax will shift to zone 8 in the next few decades as temperatures rise.[^cite_note-18]

## Geology

The Piedmont hills in the central county are made up of ancient [metamorphic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rocks) such as [schist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schist), the roots of several ancestral ranges of the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains). The western valley is floored with more recent [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) and [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone). This geology is similar to adjacent bands of rocks in [Maryland](18858.md) and further south in [Virginia](32432.md) along the eastern front of the Appalachian.

An area of 11 square miles (30 km<sup>2</sup>) of the county is known to be underlain with natural [asbestos](21492663.md).[^cite_note-asbestos-19] Much of the asbestos is known to emanate from fibrous [tremolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremolite) or [actinolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actinolite). The threat was discovered in 1987, prompting the county to establish laws to monitor air quality at construction sites, control soil taken from affected areas, and require freshly developed sites to lay 6 inches (150 mm) of clean, stable material over the ground.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

During the construction of [Centreville High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centreville_High_School_\(Fairfax_County,_Virginia\)), for instance, a large amount of asbestos-laden soil was removed and then trucked to [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna,_Virginia) for the construction of the [I-66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_66)/Nutley Street interchange. Fill dirt then had to be trucked in to make the site level. [Marine clays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_clay) can be found in widespread areas of the county east of [Interstate 95](4290790.md), mostly in the Franconia and Mount Vernon districts. These clays contribute to soil instability, leading to significant construction challenges for builders.[^cite_note-marine_clays-22]

As of the 2020 census, the county had a population of 1,150,309. The median age was 38.3 years. 23.3% of residents were under the age of 18 and 13.8% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 97.1 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 94.6 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-108][^cite_note-Census2020PL-109]

The racial makeup of the county was 49.5% White, 9.6% [Black or African American](2154.md), 0.6% [American Indian and Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 20.4% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 8.6% from some other race, and 11.3% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race comprised 17.3% of the population.[^cite_note-Census2020PL-109]

98.5% of residents lived in urban areas, while 1.5% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-110]

There were 411,055 households in the county, of which 35.3% had children under the age of 18 living with them and 23.3% had a female householder with no spouse or partner present. About 22.6% of all households were made up of individuals and 7.8% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-108]

There were 427,149 housing units, of which 3.8% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 65.7% were owner-occupied and 34.3% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 0.7% and the rental vacancy rate was 5.4%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-108]

The median income for a household in the county was \$145,164, and the median income for a family was \$174,085. 7.5% of the population were [military veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veteran#United_States), and 65.5% had a [bachelors degree](188874.md) or [higher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_degree). In the county 5.6% of the population was below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 5.6% of those under age 18 and 5.7% of those age 65 or over, with 7.2% of the population without [health insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-\:1-111]

## Education

### Primary education

Further information: [Fairfax County Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Schools)



[George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University)'s performing arts center in [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia)

The county is served by the [Fairfax County Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Schools) system, to which the county government allocates 52.2% of its budget.[^cite_note-fy_2007-112] Including state and federal government contributions, along with citizen and corporate contributions, this brings the 2023 budget for the school system to \$3.5 billion. The school system has estimated that, based on the 2023 fiscal year budget, the county would spend \$18,772 on each student.[^cite_note-113]

The Fairfax County Public School system contains the [Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_High_School_for_Science_and_Technology), a Virginia Governor's School. TJHSST consistently ranks at or near the top of all U.S. high schools due to the extraordinary number of National Merit semifinalists and finalists, its students' high average [SAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT) scores, and the number of students who annually perform nationally recognized research in the sciences and engineering. A Governor's School, TJHSST draws students from Fairfax County along with other [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia) counties, cities, and towns.

[Catholic](606848.md) elementary and middle schools in the county fall under the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Arlington) and include [Paul VI Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_VI_Catholic_High_School), Fairfax County's diocese-run Catholic high school, and [Oakcrest School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakcrest_School_\(McLean,_Virginia\)), an all-girls Catholic school in Fairfax County, which is not run by the diocese.

### Colleges and universities

Further information: [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University), [University of Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fairfax), and [VCU School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VCU_School_of_Medicine)

[George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University) is just outside the city of [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia), near the geographic center of Fairfax County. [Northern Virginia Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia_Community_College) (NVCC) serves Fairfax County with campuses in [Annandale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annandale,_Virginia) and [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Virginia) and a center in [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia) that is a satellite branch of the Loudoun campus. The NVCC [Alexandria](18940583.md) campus borders Fairfax County. The [Central Intelligence Agency University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_University) and its [Sherman Kent School for Intelligence Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Kent_School_for_Intelligence_Analysis) are both located in Fairfax County, specifically in [Chantilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantilly,_Virginia) and [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia) respectively. George Mason University faculty have twice won the [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Economics). George Mason University economics professors [James M. Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_M._Buchanan) and [Vernon L. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon_L._Smith) won it in 1986 and 2002, respectively.[^cite_note-114]

The [University of Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fairfax), a for-profit [proprietary college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_college) and alleged [diploma mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploma_mill) was once headquartered in [Vienna, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna,_Virginia).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] [Virginia Commonwealth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Commonwealth_University)'s [School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VCU_School_of_Medicine) recently constructed a medical campus wing at [Inova Fairfax Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inova_Fairfax_Hospital) in order to allow third- and fourth-year medical students to study at other state-of-the-art facilities in [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia).[^cite_note-vcu_inova-117]

## Economy



In 2007, a [U.S. Department of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Labor) study described Fairfax County as the second economic pillar of the [Washington metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area) economy after [Washington, D.C.](108956.md); in November 2007, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine described it as "one of the great economic success stories of our time."

[^cite_note-118]

Fairfax County's economy revolves around professional services and technology. Many residents work for the government or contractors of the federal government. The government is the largest employer, with [Fort Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir) in southern Fairfax the county's single largest source of federal employment. Fairfax County has a gross county product of approximately \$95 billion.[^cite_note-119]

Major employers in the county include [Airbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Americas), [Volkswagen Group of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group_of_America), [Hilton Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Hotels_Corporation),[^cite_note-hilton_hotels-120] [DXC Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DXC_Technology), [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman), [Science Applications International Corporation (SAIC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Applications_International_Corporation), [Leidos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidos), [Booz Allen Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booz_Allen_Hamilton), [Gannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gannett), [Capital One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One), [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics), [ICF International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICF_International), [Freddie Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddie_Mac), [Sallie Mae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLM_Corporation), [ManTech International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ManTech_International), [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars,_Incorporated), [NII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NII_Holdings), and [NVR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NVR_\(homebuilding_company\)). The county is home to eleven [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) company headquarters,[^cite_note-\:3-5] 11 [Hispanic 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_500) companies,[^cite_note-hispanic_biz-121] and five companies on the *Black Enterprise* 500 list.

The county's economy is supported by the [Fairfax County Economic Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Economic_Development_Authority), which provides services and information to promote Fairfax County as a leading business and technology center. The FCEDA is the nation's largest non-state economic development authority. Fairfax County is also home to the Northern Virginia Technology Council, a trade association for local technology companies.[^cite_note-reuters_bill_gates-122][^cite_note-nvtc-123] Fairfax County has a higher concentration of high-tech workers than [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley).[^cite_note-tidwell-124]

### Tysons

Main article: [Tysons, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia)



[Tysons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia), a leading business center and one of the nation's largest business districts.

[Tysons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia), located in the county, is Virginia's largest office market and the nation's largest suburban business district, with 26,600,000 square feet (2,470,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of office space.[^cite_note-tysons-125][^cite_note-tysons_business-126] It is the country's 12th-largest business district and is expected to grow substantially in the coming decades. It contains a quarter of the county's total office space inventory, which was 105,200,000 square feet (9,770,000 m<sup>2</sup>) as of 2006, representing roughly the same size as the [Lower Manhattan](455646.md) region of New York City.[^cite_note-CoStar-127]

In October 2011, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* described the area as "the place where the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) was invented, but today it looks increasingly like the center of the global [military-industrial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military-industrial_complex)",[^cite_note-128] because it is home to the nation's first [ISPs](100245.md), many of which are now defunct, and attracts numerous [defense contractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_contractor) that have relocated from other states to or near Tysons Corner.

Tysons draws over 100,000 workers from around the [Washington metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area), and draws 55,000 shoppers daily to its two [super-regional malls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-regional_mall), [Tysons Corner Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons_Corner_Center) and [Tysons Galleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons_Galleria), compared to 62,500 shoppers daily in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md).

After years of delays attributed to stalling and controversy, the \$5.2 billion expansion of the [Washington Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Metro) [Silver Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)) in Virginia from Washington, D.C., to [Dulles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Dulles_International_Airport) was funded by the [Federal Transit Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Transit_Administration) in December 2008.[^cite_note-129] The Silver Line added four stations in Tysons, including [a station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons_station) between Tysons Corner Center and Tysons Galleria.

Along with the expansion of Washington Metro, Fairfax County government has a plan to "urbanize" the Tysons area. The plan calls for a private-public partnership and a grid-like street system to make Tysons a more urban environment, tripling available housing to allow more workers to live near their workplaces. The goal is to have 95% of Tysons Corner within 1⁄2-mile (800 m) of a metro station.[^cite_note-130]

## Arts and culture

Further information: [Capital One Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Hall), [EagleBank Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EagleBank_Arena), [Wolf Trap National Park for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Trap_National_Park_for_the_Performing_Arts), and [Workhouse Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workhouse_Arts_Center)



[Wolf Trap National Park for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Trap_National_Park_for_the_Performing_Arts)

Annual festivals include the "Celebrate Fairfax!" festival held in June at the Fairfax County Government Center in [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia), the [Tephra Fine Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tephra_Fine_Arts_Festival)[^cite_note-132] held in May at Reston Town Center in [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia), and the [International Children's Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Children's_Festival_at_Wolf_Trap) held in September at the [Wolf Trap National Park for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Trap_National_Park_for_the_Performing_Arts), a performing arts center in [Wolf Trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Trap,_Virginia).

Fairfax County supports a summer concert series held in multiple venues throughout the county on various nights. The concert series are called Arts in the Parks, Braddock Nights, Franconia District Nights, Mt. Vernon Nights, Nottoway Nights, Spotlight by Starlight, Sounds of Summer and Starlight Cinema.[^cite_note-2009_summer-133]

[Capital One Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Hall), part of the [Capital One Headquarters Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Tower_\(Virginia\)) in [Tysons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia), is a major performing arts center that opened in 2021 and seats 1,600 in its main theater.

[EagleBank Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EagleBank_Arena), originally known as the Patriot Center, is located on the Fairfax campus of [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University) just outside Fairfax, hosts concerts and shows. The nearby Center for the Arts at George Mason is a major year-round arts venue, and the [Workhouse Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workhouse_Arts_Center) in [Lorton, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorton,_Virginia) includes studios for artists, event facilities for performing groups, and gallery exhibitions in addition to hosting the annual Clifton Film Celebration.[^cite_note-134] Smaller local art venues include the Alden Theater at the McLean Community Center, ArtSpace Herndon, Center Stage at the Reston Community Center, Greater Reston Arts Center, James Lee Community Center Theater, and Vienna Arts Society.

Fairfax County uses the urban county executive form of government, which county voters approved in a 1966 referendum.[^cite_note-WaPo10-13-1966-135][^cite_note-Burchard1967-136] Under the urban county executive plan, the county is governed by a 10-member [Fairfax County Board of Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Board_of_Supervisors) with the day-to-day running of the county tasked to the appointed Fairfax County executive. Nine of the board members are elected from the single-member districts of Braddock, Dranesville, Franconia, Hunter Mill, Mason, Mount Vernon, Providence, Springfield, and Sully, while the chairman is elected at-large.[^cite_note-\:2-137]

In addition to the board of supervisors, there are three constitutional officers, the [commonwealth's attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth's_attorney), clerk of the circuit court, and [sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Sheriff's_Office). The [Fairfax County School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Schools) has 13 members, which are directly elected by Fairfax County voters. Fairfax County also has a Consumer Protection Commission, which advises the board of supervisors and the Department of Cable and Consumer Services on consumer affairs, investigates illegal, fraudulent, deceptive, or dangerous consumer practices, and refers suspected violations of Virginia law to the commonwealth attorney or county attorney for investigation.[^cite_note-\:0-138]

[Fairfax County Government Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Government_Center) is west of the City of Fairfax in an [unincorporated area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area).[^cite_note-139] Fairfax County contains an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) unincorporated area in the City of Fairfax's central business district, where many county facilities (including the courthouses and jail) are.[^cite_note-Fairfaxcitymap-140][^cite_note-Courtadd-141]

Fairfax County was once considered a [Republican](32070.md) bastion, but [Democrats](5043544.md) now control the board of supervisors, school board (officially nonpartisan), offices of the county sheriff, and the commonwealth's attorney. Democrats also hold all the Fairfax seats in the [Virginia House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_House_of_Delegates) and every seat in the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Senate).

Fairfax County encompasses parts of three [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_districts), the [8th District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia's_8th_congressional_district), the [10th District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia's_10th_congressional_district), and the [11th District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia's_11th_congressional_district). Democrats represent all three districts, with [Suhas Subramanyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhas_Subramanyam) representing the 10th, [Don Beyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Beyer) representing the 8th, and [James Walkinshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Walkinshaw) representing the 11th.

In the [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election), Fairfax County solidly backed [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) for re-election as president, who came just short of matching his 2008 performance in the county, winning it 59.6% to [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney)'s 39.1%. Former [Virginia Governor](253093.md) [Tim Kaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Kaine), running for the [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) in 2012, carried Fairfax County with 61% of the vote as part of his statewide victory. U.S. Representatives Connolly, Moran, and Wolf were also re-elected.

Republican governor [Bob McDonnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_McDonnell) won Fairfax County with 51% of the vote in 2009, but the Republican resurgence in Fairfax was short-lived. In the [2013 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Virginia_elections), Democratic gubernatorial candidate [Terry McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_McAuliffe) won Fairfax County with 58% of the vote, defeating incumbent state attorney general and former Republican state senator from Fairfax [Ken Cuccinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Cuccinelli). McAuliffe's running mates, [Ralph Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Northam) and [Mark Herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Herring), also carried Fairfax County in their respective bids for [lieutenant governor](1461923.md) and [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Virginia). These Democratic victories mirrored the Democratic ticket's sweep of the state's three executive offices for the first time since 1989.

In the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), Fairfax continued its trend towards Democratic candidates. Representatives Beyer and Connolly were re-elected, with Connolly running unopposed. Fairfax County supported [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) for president with 64.4% of the vote to [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s 28.6%, exemplifying a heavy swing toward Democrats across Northern Virginia.

In the [2020 general election](48410011.md), Democratic presidential nominee [Joe Biden](145422.md) won the county with 69.89% of the vote, the largest percentage for a Democrat in the county since [1916](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_United_States_presidential_election). As of 2025, Springfield supervisor [Pat Herrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Herrity) is the only elected [Republican](32070.md) official in Fairfax County.

## Transportation

### Roads

Several major highways run through Fairfax County, including the [Capital Beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Beltway) (Interstate 495), [Interstate 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_66), [Interstate 95](4290790.md), and [Interstate 395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_395_\(Virginia%E2%80%93District_of_Columbia\)). The [American Legion Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Legion_Memorial_Bridge_\(Potomac_River\)) connects Fairfax to Montgomery County, Maryland. The [George Washington Memorial Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Memorial_Parkway), [Dulles Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_Toll_Road), and [Fairfax County Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Parkway) are also major arteries. Other notable roads include [Braddock Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braddock_Road_\(Northern_Virginia\)), [Old Keene Mill Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Route_644_\(Fairfax_County\)), [Little River Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Route_236), State Routes [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Route_7_\(Virginia\)), [28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Route_28_\(Virginia\)), and [123](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Route_123_\(Virginia\)), and U.S. Routes [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_1_in_Virginia), [29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_29_in_Virginia), and [50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_50_in_Virginia).

The county is part of the [Washington metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area), the nation's third-most congested area as of 2008.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145]

Northern Virginia, including Fairfax County, is the third-worst congested traffic area in the nation, in terms of percentage of congested roadways and time spent in traffic. Of the lane miles in the region, 44 percent are rated "F", or worst, for congestion. Northern Virginia residents spend an average of 46 hours a year stuck in traffic.

### Air

Further information: [Dulles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport)



[Dulles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport), partially located in Fairfax County

[Dulles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport) lies partly within Fairfax County and provides most air service to the county. Fairfax is also served by two other airports in the Washington area, [Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Washington_National_Airport) and [Baltimore-Washington International Thurgood Marshall Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore-Washington_International_Thurgood_Marshall_Airport). [Manassas Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manassas_Regional_Airport), in neighboring [Prince William County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County), is also used for regional cargo and private jet service.

From 1945 to 1961, the eastern part of Fairfax County hosted [Falls Church Airpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church_Airpark), an airfield primarily used for [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) and [civil defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_defense) purposes until encroaching residential development forced its closure.[^cite_note-At-146] The area the airport occupied is now mainly used as a shopping center, with the western end of the complex occupied by the Thomas Jefferson branch of the [Fairfax County Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Library) system. Parts of several apartment complexes are also on some of the airport's former grounds.[^cite_note-Paul-13][^cite_note-Rollo-14]

### Public transportation



[Vienna station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_station_\(Washington_Metro\)), the western terminus of the [Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)) of the [Washington Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Metro) rapid rail system

Fairfax County has multiple public transportation services, including the [Washington Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Metro)'s [Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)), [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)), [Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)) and [Yellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)) lines. The Silver line, which runs through the [Tysons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia), [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia), and [Herndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon,_Virginia) areas of the county, opened in 2014, later extended in 2022, as the first new Washington Metro line since the [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Washington_Metro\)) opened in 1991.[^cite_note-sivlerline-147]

The [Virginia Railway Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Railway_Express) (VRE) provides commuter rail service to [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Washington_Metro\)) in Washington, D.C., with stations in Fairfax County. The VRE's Fairfax County stations are [Lorton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorton_station_\(VRE\)) and [Franconia-Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia-Springfield_station) on the [Fredericksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericksburg,_Virginia) line, and [Burke Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke_Centre_station), [Rolling Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Road_station), and [Backlick Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backlick_Road_station) on the [Manassas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manassas,_Virginia) line.[^cite_note-vre-148]

Fairfax County contracts its bus service, the [Fairfax Connector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_Connector), to [Transdev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdev). The City of Fairfax's [CUE Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUE_Bus) also serves parts of the county, namely [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University) and [Vienna/Fairfax-GMU Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_station_\(Washington_Metro\)) on the Orange line. The county is also served by [WMATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMATA)'s Metrobus service.

## Parks and recreation

Further information: [Fairfax County Park Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Park_Authority)

The county has many protected areas, a total of over 390 county parks on more than 23,000 acres (93 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-fairfaxparks-149] The [Fairfax County Park Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Park_Authority) maintains parks and recreation centers through the county. There are also two national protected areas that are inside the county at least in part, including [Elizabeth Hartwell Mason Neck National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Hartwell_Mason_Neck_National_Wildlife_Refuge), [George Washington Memorial Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Memorial_Parkway), and [Wolf Trap National Park for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Trap_National_Park_for_the_Performing_Arts). [Mason Neck State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_Neck_State_Park) is also in [Lorton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorton,_Virginia).

Fairfax County is a member of the [Northern Virginia Regional Park Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia_Regional_Park_Authority).

[Reston Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston_Zoo) is in [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia).[^cite_note-reston_zoo-150] The [National Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Park_\(United_States\)) is nearby in Washington, D.C.

### Trails

Further information: [Mount Vernon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon_Trail), [Gerry Connolly Cross County Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Connolly_Cross_County_Trail), and [Washington & Old Dominion Railroad Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_&_Old_Dominion_Railroad_Trail)

The county maintains many miles of bike trails running through parks, adjacent to roads and through towns such as Vienna and Herndon. The [Washington & Old Dominion Railroad Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_&_Old_Dominion_Railroad_Trail) runs through Fairfax County, offering one of the region's best, and safest, routes for recreational walking and biking. In addition, nine miles (14 km) of the [Mount Vernon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon_Trail) runs through Fairfax County along the Potomac River.

Compared to other regions of the Washington area, Fairfax County has a dearth of designated [bike lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_lanes) for cyclists wishing to commute in the region. On May 16, 2008, [Bike-to-Work Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike-to-Work_Day), the Fairfax County Department of Transportation released the first countywide bicycle route map.[^cite_note-bike_map-151]

The [Gerry Connolly Cross County Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Connolly_Cross_County_Trail) runs from Great Falls National Park in the county's northern end to Occoquan Regional Park in the southern end. Consisting of mostly dirt paths and short asphalt sections, the trail is used mostly by recreational mountain bikers, hikers, and horse riders.

Three incorporated towns, [Clifton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton,_Virginia), [Herndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon,_Virginia), and [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna,_Virginia), are in Fairfax County.[^cite_note-152]

The [independent cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)#Virginia) of [Falls Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church,_Virginia) and [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia) were formed out of areas formerly under Fairfax County's jurisdiction but are politically separate. Nevertheless, the Postal Service has long considered several portions of Fairfax County to be unincorporated Falls Church and Fairfax City. Several portions of the county also have [Alexandria](18940583.md) mailing addresses; many locals refer to these neighborhoods collectively as "South Alexandria", "Lower Alexandria", or "Alexandria, Fairfax County".[^cite_note-153] "South Alexandria" communities include Hollin Hills, Franconia, Groveton, Hybla Valley, Huntington, Belle Haven, Mount Vernon, Fort Hunt, Engleside, Burgundy Village, Waynewood, Wilton Woods, Rose Hill, Virginia Hills, Hayfield, and Kingstowne.

It has been proposed[^cite_note-154] to convert the entire county into a single independent city, primarily to gain more control over taxes and roads. The most recent such proposal was made on June 30, 2009.

Other communities in Fairfax County are unincorporated areas. Virginia law dictates that no unincorporated area of a county may be incorporated as a separate town or city following the adoption of the urban county executive form of government.[^cite_note-155] Fairfax County adopted the urban county executive form of government in 1966.[^cite_note-WaPo10-13-1966-135][^cite_note-Burchard1967-136]

As of the [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census,_2000), Fairfax County's 13 largest communities are all unincorporated [CDPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census-designated_place), the largest of which are [Centreville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centreville,_Virginia), [Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reston,_Virginia), and [McLean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLean,_Virginia), each with a population over 45,000.
