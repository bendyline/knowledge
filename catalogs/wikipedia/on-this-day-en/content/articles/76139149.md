The [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) has conducted numerous concerted aerial bombardment campaigns on the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) during the [Gaza war](74998519.md). Since the *de jure* enactment of a [ceasefire in October 2025](81215079.md#Components), strikes have continued but have become less frequent.

The bombing, combined with other simultaneous Israeli military actions in Gaza, killed at least 72,000 Palestinians (mostly civilians), which is between 3% and 4% of the total population of the territory, and damaged or destroyed Palestinian [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_schools_during_the_Israeli_invasion_of_Gaza), [hospitals](75314000.md), mosques, churches, and other [civilian infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_airstrikes_on_municipal_services_in_the_Gaza_Strip) including [refugee camps](75393092.md).[^cite_note-cbc20231230-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-snapshot-5]

Israel said the bombing was targeted, and by October 2024, Israel said it bombed 40,000 targets[^cite_note-reuters-40000-6] in the Gaza Strip. According to Israeli military intelligence members, if a specific target was given to the air force to bomb, that target was usually a family home; they said the method of generating targets was that a list of persons considered likely to be militants was generated by software, and their family homes were chosen as targets.[^cite_note-lavender-7]

By one estimate, as of April 2024 the bomb tonnage dropped on Gaza was more than 70,000 tonnes,[^cite_note-euromed-70000-8] surpassing the combined bomb tonnage [dropped on Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden), [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II), and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blitz) in World War II.[^cite_note-9] Satellite imagery showed at least 69% of all buildings were damaged or destroyed,[^cite_note-theguardian20240130-10][^cite_note-aljazeera-wasteland-11] which surpasses the scale of [destruction in Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Cologne_in_World_War_II) and Dresden and approaches that of Hamburg during World War II.[^cite_note-cbc20231230-3][^cite_note-ft20231206-12] An analysis in *[The Lancet](463899.md)* concluded that, as of January 2025, official figures significantly under-report mortality; the analysis estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury up to 30 June 2024, and when extended to October 2024 likely exceeding 70,000.[^cite_note-lancet-study-13][^cite_note-ochaopt.org-14] The recorded death toll, per the [Gaza Health Ministry](52186562.md) and as of August 2026, is over 73,000, not including those missing and presumed dead.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-deaths-1] Researchers have estimated the percentage of civilians among the fatalities at not less than 80%.[^cite_note-death-toll-the-nation-16][^cite_note-death-toll-aoav-17]

The aerial bombing and artillery shelling campaign has been accompanied or followed by destruction of infrastructure by other means. Areas including [Rafah](584373.md) have been razed to the ground in a planned way, by means of controlled demolitions and bulldozing.

Israel has faced accusations of [war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_war_crimes_in_the_Gaza_war) and [genocide](75668427.md) due to the large number of civilian casualties and the large percentage of civilian infrastructure destroyed.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Israel ordered civilians [evacuate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_evacuations), and threatened that civilians who did not evacuate would be considered "an accomplice in a terrorist organisation", thus implying legitimacy for their bombing.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-23] Israel stated that its struck targets were used by [Hamas](13913.md), but an [Airwars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airwars) analysis did not find evidence of militant presence in most Israeli airstrikes in October 2023.[^cite_note-24] The United Nations reported that 86% of the Gaza Strip was under Israeli evacuation orders.[^cite_note-25] Satellite data analysis indicated that 80% of the buildings in northern Gaza had been damaged or destroyed.[^cite_note-Maslin-Shah-2023-26][^cite_note-theguardian20240130-10][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-30]

## Background

Israel's bombing campaign of the Gaza Strip began in response to the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md).[^cite_note-31] In prior conflicts — such as the [2014 Gaza War](43243199.md) — Israel damaged or destroyed tens of thousands of buildings.[^cite_note-ynetnews.com-32] The costs of rebuilding in prior conflicts have estimated to range in the billions of dollars.[^cite_note-haaretz.com-33]

Israel alleges that hospitals, clinics, mosques and schools are used for fighting, and also claims that ambulances transport combat equipment and militants throughout the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-Zitun-34] Israel published videos from alleged interrogations of captured militants telling about the military activities in hospitals and ambulances,[^cite_note-Zitun-34] and other IDF videos show alleged use for weapons storage,[^cite_note-35] and as access points to [warfare tunnels](43413500.md).[^cite_note-36] The IDF says that such military use is a breach of international law.[^cite_note-37]

### Airstrikes before 7 October in 2023

In May 2023, airstrikes on the family homes of [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) members in the Gaza Strip killed 13 people, three alleged militants, and 10 civilian family members.[^cite_note-CNN_king_pin-38] Reports on the total number of dead ranged from 12 to 15.[^cite_note-tg_9may-39][^cite_note-NPR_15-40] The dead included four women and four children.[^cite_note-41] The airstrikes were in response to fire from Islamic Jihad, that was in turn in response to the death in Israeli custody of a member of their political wing, [Khader Adnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khader_Adnan).[^cite_note-tg_9may-39] Israel claimed two of the men, Jihad Ghannam and Tareq Izzeldeen were responsible for attacks in the [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-ABC_May_13-43]

There were three days of Israeli bombing of the Gaza Strip in late September 2023.[^cite_note-44] Dozens of people were wounded on the first day.[^cite_note-45] Earlier that week Israeli forces shot protestors at the border, as they threw explosives at Israeli posts.[^cite_note-46]

### Munitions

Further information: [United States support for Israel in the Gaza war](65284610.md)



[USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) airmen prepare for departure in a [C-17 Globemaster III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17_Globemaster_III) carrying ammunition to Israel, 15 October 2023, at [Ramstein Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramstein_Air_Base), Germany.

The Israeli bombing campaign has used mostly American-made bombs. From October 2023 until July 2024, the US has transferred more than 27,000 bombs to Israel. Although there was a pause of one shipment of [MK-84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_84_bomb) 2,000 pound bombs, no significant change in the supply of the bombs happened since October.[^cite_note-reuters_29jun-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Investigative reports by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* and [CNN](62028.md) have shown that the MK-84 bombs have been responsible to some of the deadliest attacks against Gaza civilians.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Unlike the detailed information released for the shipment of weapons to [Ukraine](42085878.md), the US government has revealed few details about the munitions sent to Israel. The bombs are provided from US's own stockpiles and the manufacture of new bombs was ordered from [Boeing](18933266.md) and [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics).[^cite_note-reuters_29jun-47]

## Attacks during the Gaza war

### Medical facilities



[Al-Shifa Hospital](21218880.md) in Gaza City, destroyed by Israeli bombing, 21 December 2024

Further information: [Attacks on health facilities during the Gaza war](75314000.md)

Health officials and medical organizations in the Gaza Strip accused Israel of [deliberately bombing ambulances and health facilities](75314000.md) in the besieged enclave. And said that these attacks are considered a violation of [international laws](8195726.md) that include such attacks in the list of [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-auto1-52] The attacks on healthcare facilities contributed to a severe [humanitarian crisis in Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Gaza_humanitarian_crisis).[^cite_note-53] The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) accused Hamas of military operations inside hospitals, including alleged attacks on soldiers, weapon storage, fighters taking shelter, providing support for underground tunnels, [human shielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_shields_in_the_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict), and holding hostages.[^cite_note-54] Many of these claims, however, have been debunked under scrutiny from journalists.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

By April 2024, 30 out of the 36 hospitals in the Gaza Strip had been bombed, according to [Save the Children](436885.md) and [UNICEF](19698110.md).[^cite_note-58] Israeli airstrikes on medical facilities include the bombing the [Turkish-Palestinian Friendship Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish-Palestinian_Friendship_Hospital),[^cite_note-59] the [Nasser Medical Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Medical_Complex),[^cite_note-60] a [medical convoy at al-Shifa hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shifa_ambulance_airstrike),[^cite_note-61] and the areas around the [Al Shifa](21218880.md) and [Al Quds hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds_Hospital).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

### Refugee camps

Main article: [Attacks on refugee camps in the Gaza war](75393092.md)



Ruins of [Al-Shati refugee camp](16296150.md), Gaza City, 3 July 2024

In the [Gaza war](74998519.md), as part of the [bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Gaza) and [invasion of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_invasion_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) has conducted numerous airstrikes in densely populated [Palestinian refugee camps](400347.md) in both the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) and [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-65]

On 23 October 2023, airstrikes killed 436 people in the [Al-Shati camp](16296150.md) and southern [Khan Younis](101330.md) in one night.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] By 28 October 2023, the IDF bombed residential buildings in the [Jabalia refugee camp](18169472.md) without any prior warning, killing an estimated 50 people per hour.[^cite_note-68] On 31 October 2023, an airstrike on the [Jabalia refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_31,_2023_Jabalia_airstrike) was described as a "massive massacre".[^cite_note-69] On 13 November, an Israeli airstrike on the Jabalia refugee camp killed 30 people, with Gaza's civil defence team unable to rescue injured people from the rubble due to a lack of equipment.[^cite_note-Responders_cannot_reach-70] By 6 March 2024, aerial footage showed that the [Al-Shati refugee camp](16296150.md), which had been one of the world's most densely populated areas before the war, was in complete ruins.[^cite_note-71]

### Schools

Main article: [Attacks on schools during the Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_schools_during_the_Gaza_war)



A destroyed [UNRWA](32270.md) school in the [Zaytun](42179889.md) neighborhood of Gaza City in February 2024.

According to [UNOCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOCHA), 87% of schools in Gaza were hit or damaged between October 2023 and 2024.[^cite_note-72] Similarly, *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* reported that 85% of the schools in Gaza had been bombed by October 2024.[^cite_note-73] By March 2024, the [United Nations](31769.md) had recorded 212 "direct hits" on schools in Gaza by Israeli bombardment, with at least 53 schools totally destroyed.[^cite_note-74]

An airstrike at a [UNRWA school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2023_UNRWA_school_airstrike) killed at least six people.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] On 18 October 2023, the Ahmed Abdel Aziz School in Khan Yunis was hit.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] On 3 November 2023, the IDF bombed the Osama Ben Zaid school.[^cite_note-79] On 4 November 2023, Israel bombed the [Al-Fakhoora school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fakhoora_school_airstrikes), killing at least 15 people.[^cite_note-80] On 5 November 2023, Israel bombed and destroyed [Al-Azhar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Azhar_University_%E2%80%93_Gaza).[^cite_note-81]

On 17 November 2023, dozens were reported killed after an [airstrike on al-Falah School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Falah_School_airstrike) in the Zeitoun neighborhood, south of Gaza City.[^cite_note-82] A strike on the [Al-Fakhoora school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fakhoora_school_airstrikes) reportedly killed at least 50.[^cite_note-Hamas_Health_Ministry_Says-83] Deaf, blind, and intellectually handicapped individuals were at particular risk of death by airstrikes.[^cite_note-84] On 13 December 2023, a UNRWA school in [Beit Hanoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Hanoun) was destroyed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-85] On 10 August 2024, more than 100 Palestinians died in rocket [attacks on Al-Tabaeen school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tabaeen_school_attack).[^cite_note-86]

### Housing and infrastructure

See also: [Israeli airstrikes on municipal services in the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_airstrikes_on_municipal_services_in_the_Gaza_Strip)



Destroyed houses in Gaza, 29 January 2025

On 16 October 2023, Israeli airstrikes destroyed a UNRWA humanitarian aid supply depot.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] The same day, airstrikes destroyed the headquarters of the [Palestinian Civil Defence](49984885.md), an agency responsible for [emergency response services](146330.md), including [firefighting](855608.md) and [search and rescue](172599.md).[^cite_note-89] Journalists reported Israel was targeting [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel) and personal generators.[^cite_note-90] On 15 November 2023, Gaza's last remaining [flour mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour_mill) was hit by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-91]



[Khan Yunis](101330.md) on 11 June 2024

On 12 November 2023, Israel used [earthquake bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_bomb) on an apartment complex in [Khan Yunis](101330.md), killing at least 13 people.[^cite_note-92] Twenty-six people were killed in an airstrike of a residential building in southern Gaza on 18 November.[^cite_note-93] By 28 November 2023, a [United Nations](31769.md) (UN)-led consortium estimated 60% of all housing in Gaza had been destroyed.[^cite_note-94] Numerous casualties were reported in an airstrike on a residential building near [Nasser Medical Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Medical_Complex) in [Khan Younis](101330.md), with hospital staff reporting having to bury 40 bodies on the hospital grounds.[^cite_note-95] On 4 February 2024, two residential towers in [Rafah](584373.md) were bombed, part of a series of strikes killing 127 people.[^cite_note-96] 104 people were killed between 21 and 23 February 2024 in residential building airstrikes conducted without prior warning.[^cite_note-97] In March 2024, a man in Gaza City described the situation there, stating, "Destruction on a massive scale, beyond any description. Our homes were destroyed. Nothing remained of our property".[^cite_note-98]

In March 2024, a joint report by the EU, World Bank, and UN estimated 57% of water infrastructure was damaged or destroyed.[^cite_note-99] Sewage overflowed as a result of the infrastructural damage.[^cite_note-100] In May 2024, the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) and [Ministry of Communications and Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Telecom_and_Information_Technology_of_the_State_of_Palestine) stated that 75% of Gaza's [telecoms towers](2044496.md) had been rendered inoperable by Israeli attacks.[^cite_note-101] In June 2024, UNOSAT said that 57% of Gaza's agricultural land had been degraded by conflict.[^cite_note-102]

### Places of worship

Main article: [Attacks on religious sites during the Israeli invasion of Gaza](77727480.md)



Mosque destroyed by an Israeli bombing in [Khan Younis](101330.md)

On 19 October 2023, an [Israeli airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Porphyrius_airstrike) hit the [Church of Saint Porphyrius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Porphyrius), where 500 people were sheltering.[^cite_note-103] On 8 November 2023, Israel bombed and destroyed the Khalid bin al-Walid Mosque.[^cite_note-104] By 13 November 2023, at least 60 mosques had been destroyed by Israeli bombs.[^cite_note-105] In December 2023, an Israeli bombing destroyed the [Great Mosque of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Gaza).[^cite_note-106] At least seven people were killed in an Israeli airstrike on a [Rafah](584373.md) mosque full of displaced people on 23 February 2024.[^cite_note-107] Five people were killed in a mosque in northern Gaza that was bombed without warning.[^cite_note-108] The al-Riad mosque in [Khan Younis](101330.md) was heavily damaged by an Israeli bombing on 9 March 2024.[^cite_note-109]

By 10 March 2024, more than 1,000 mosques had been destroyed by Israeli attacks.[^cite_note-110] In May 2024, an Israeli bombing on a mosque in Gaza City reportedly killed at least 10 people.[^cite_note-111]

### Safe zones

On 17 October 2023, Israel conducted intensive airstrikes in southern Gaza, in areas it told residents to seek refuge.[^cite_note-Jobain-refuge-112] Israel "pounded" areas in south Gaza it had declared as "safe zones", raising fears amongst residents that nowhere was safe.[^cite_note-Palestinians_trapped_in_Gaza-113] On 20 October 2023, Israeli continued to bombard south Gaza, and IDF spokesman Nir Dinar said, "There are no safe zones".[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] Following Israel's evacuation orders for Palestinians to flee northern Gaza, the IDF intensified its attacks on southern Gaza.[^cite_note-116]

Analyses by [CNN](62028.md), *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, and [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News) all found that Israel had bombed areas it had previously told civilians to evacuate to. The Sky News investigation also concluded that Israel's evacuation orders had been "chaotic and contradictory",[^cite_note-117] *NYT* found that Israel had dropped 2,000-pound bombs in those areas,[^cite_note-118] while CNN stated it had verified at least three locations Israel bombed after telling civilians it was safe to go there.[^cite_note-119]

On 5 January 2024, evacuees fleeing Israeli attacks in central Gaza stated the situation there was "hell on Earth".[^cite_note-120] One survivor of an Israeli airstrike wrote, "Even though that air strike did not kill us, it destroyed something inside us."[^cite_note-121] On 12 January, the UN Secretary-General for Human Rights stated that at least 319 internally displaced persons were killed and 1,135 injured by Israeli airstrikes while sheltering in UN shelters.[^cite_note-122] After an Israeli bomb killed two sheltering in a tent in [Deir el-Balah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el-Balah) on 23 February 2024, a surviving family member stated, "It's just a tent. They are displaced and evacuated from the north here to seek refuge. They were sleeping. Why were they attacked? Even in tents, we are not safe."[^cite_note-123] After a bombing on tents in [Rafah](584373.md) killed 11 people, [Director-General of the WHO](63815422.md) [Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus](39712205.md) stated it was "outrageous and unspeakable".[^cite_note-124]

## Casualties

See also: [Casualties of the Gaza war](75130785.md) and [Israeli war crimes in the Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_war_crimes_in_the_Gaza_war)

More than 73,000 Palestinians have been reported killed by the Gaza Health Ministry.[^cite_note-deaths-1][^cite_note-ochaopt.org-14] According to a January 2025 analysis in *[The Lancet](463899.md)*, it is likely that the death toll due to traumatic injury that time exceeded 70,000. Of this total, it was estimated that 59.1% were women, children and the elderly,[^cite_note-lancet-study-13] and researchers have estimated a civilian death toll of at least 80%.[^cite_note-death-toll-the-nation-16][^cite_note-death-toll-aoav-17]

### Missing persons



Rescue teams searching for survivors under rubble, October 2023

On 11 February 2026, roughly 8,000 people were reported missing beneath rubble.[^cite_note-125] On 27 October 2023, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) stated more than 1,000 unidentified people were buried under rubble.[^cite_note-126] On 3 December 2023, the [Palestinian Civil Defence](49984885.md) stated the situation "beyond dire" as the organization was unable to rescue many people buried under rubble.[^cite_note-127]

Individuals were rescued by aid workers after reportedly surviving several days buried underneath rubble.[^cite_note-128] Emergency responders stated that part of what made rescue so difficult is that Israeli bombs tend to "flatten entire buildings".[^cite_note-129] On 24 February 2024, Dr. [Paul B. Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_B._Spiegel) stated that total death counts were undercounts due to the large number of people under rubble, stating, "We projected the number of deaths that may be missing, and it was probably up to about ten to fifteen per cent more."[^cite_note-130] On 26 February 2024, Israeli warplanes bombed and destroyed an emergency rescue machinery in [Beit Lahia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Lahia).[^cite_note-131]

According to *The New York Times*, "The buried make up a shadow death toll in Gaza, a leaden asterisk to the health ministry's official tally of more than 31,000 dead".[^cite_note-132] In May 2024, a U.S. doctor described the deterioration of rescue operations in Gaza, stating, "We hear bombs and before my thought used to be 'what patients are we going to meet tomorrow?' And now we hear bombs and no one comes."[^cite_note-133] The United Nations stated that more than 10,000 people were estimated to be buried under the rubble.[^cite_note-134] Dr. Marwan al-Hams stated the number was so high because of the lack of heavy equipment or fuel to dig through concrete and steel to rescue them.[^cite_note-135]

In late-June 2024 it was reported by [Save the Children](436885.md) that up to 21,000 children are estimated to be missing due to the bombing and the ongoing war. While the organization acknowledged that it was difficult to collect and verify information in Gaza, it was believed that about 17,000 children were unaccompanied and separated, about 4,000 likely missing in the rubble and an unknown number in mass graves.[^cite_note-136] In July 2024, emergency crews stated there were many people trapped under debris in the [Tel al-Hawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_al-Hawa) neighborhood following heavy attacks by Israel.[^cite_note-137]

### Comparison with Israeli bombing of the Gaza Strip in 2012, 2014 and 2021

Further information: [2012 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Gaza_War), [2014 Gaza War](43243199.md), [List of Israeli strikes and Palestinian casualties in the 2014 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Israeli_strikes_and_Palestinian_casualties_in_the_2014_Gaza_War), and [2021 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Gaza_War)



Ruins of [Beit Hanoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Hanoun) in 2014, following Israeli bombing

An investigation by British NGO [Action on Armed Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_on_Armed_Violence) in November 2023 compared the airstrikes beginning in October 2023 with previous Israeli campaigns in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Gaza_War), [2014](43243199.md) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_crisis), and determined that the 2023 death toll was several times greater, stating: "Each airstrike causing civilian casualties in 2023's Operation Swords of Iron produced just over 10 fatalities on average, compared to 1.3, 2.5 and 1.7 per fatal bombing in those previous bouts of bombing."[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) reported that in both the prior conflicts and since October 2023 Israeli airstrikes caused extensive civilian deaths and injuries, at times eliminating whole families, despite no observable military targets nearby. High-rise residential and commercial towers in Gaza were also struck and destroyed.[^cite_note-141]

## AI-assisted targeting

Main article: [AI-assisted targeting in the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted_targeting_in_the_Gaza_Strip)



Israeli military drone [IAI Heron](1141476.md), used to monitor, target and bomb buildings or individuals

[^cite_note-142]

During the bombing campaign, Israel used [artificial intelligences](1164.md) to determine what targets the Air Force would bomb.[^cite_note-france24-20231212-143] A system known as Habsora, "the Gospel", would automatically provide a targeting recommendation to a human analyst,[^cite_note-NPR20231214-144] who would decide whether to pass it along to soldiers in the field.[^cite_note-NPR20231214-144] The recommendations can be anything from individual fighters, rocket launchers, Hamas command posts,[^cite_note-NPR20231214-144] to private homes of suspected Hamas or Islamic Jihad members.[^cite_note-145] This would automate most of the target selection process.[^cite_note-france24-20231212-143]

[NPR](29697232.md) cited Anthony King, professor of defense and security studies at the [University of Exeter](33719893.md), as saying this may be the first time AI-generated targets are being rolled out on a large scale to try and influence a military operation.[^cite_note-NPR20231214-144]

## Reactions

The *[Financial Times](136566.md)* described northern Gaza as a "bombed-out wasteland", and Palestinians feared northern Gaza was becoming uninhabitable.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186] Israel's bombing was described as "unlike any other in the 21st century".[^cite_note-187]

On 6 January 2024, the United Nations [Under-Secretary-General for Humanitarian Affairs](1607007.md) [Martin Griffiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Griffiths) stated that Gaza had "simply become uninhabitable".[^cite_note-188] James Elder, the [UNICEF](19698110.md) spokesman, stated, "I have never seen such devastation. Just chaos and ruin, with rubble and debris scattered in every single direction."[^cite_note-189] [Tor Wennesland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Wennesland), the UN special coordinator for Middle East Peace, stated, "Israel's use of explosive weapons in densely populated areas has destroyed entire neighborhoods and damaged hospitals and other civilian infrastructure, schools, mosques, and United Nations premises."[^cite_note-190] In November 2024, [Jan Egeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Egeland), the head of the [Norwegian Refugee Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Refugee_Council), stated during an interview with [Christiane Amanpour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiane_Amanpour): "Gaza is destroyed, there is no other way to describe it".[^cite_note-191]

The EU's top diplomat [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell) stated Israel's objective appeared to be making Gaza "temporarily or permanently impossible to live in".[^cite_note-192] [Mary Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Robinson), the former-[president](24676.md) of the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) and leader of [The Elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elders_\(organization\)), called on the United States to cease providing bombs to Israel, stating, "Netanyahu is on the wrong side of history, completely".[^cite_note-193]

On 10 July 2024, the [Biden administration](65732174.md) resumed shipments of the [500-pound bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_82_bomb) to Israel, which had been halted since May that year over concerns about the high number of [civilian casualties in Gaza](75130785.md).[^cite_note-194]

In response to the indiscriminate bombing, U.S. President [Joe Biden](145422.md) noted that support for Israel was declining. [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) remarked, however, "You carpet bombed Germany, you dropped the atom bomb, a lot of civilians died." To which Joe Biden replied, "Yeah, that's why all these institutions were set up after World War Two to see to it that it didn't happen again".[^cite_note-195] In the [United States Congress](31756.md), lawmakers [Tim Walberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Walberg) and [Lindsey Graham](363279.md) supported the bombing and compared the situation to [Hiroshima and Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197]

In May 2025, Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) stated that the destruction of homes in Gaza would lead to the [forced emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_evacuations) of Palestinians.[^cite_note-198]

### Analysis



Palestinians in the ruins of [Beit Lahia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Lahia), February 2025

By October 2024, Israel said it bombed 40,000 locations[^cite_note-reuters-40000-6] in the Gaza Strip (which is 360 km<sup>2</sup>). By one estimate, the bomb tonnage dropped on Gaza is more than 70,000 tonnes,[^cite_note-euromed-70000-8] which academic [Robert Pape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pape) noted surpassed the combined bomb tonnage dropped on Dresden, Hamburg, and London in World War II. He also stated that "Gaza will also go down as a place name denoting one of history's heaviest conventional bombing campaigns."[^cite_note-ft20231206-12] Satellite imagery showed at least 69% of all buildings were damaged or destroyed,[^cite_note-theguardian20240130-10][^cite_note-aljazeera-wasteland-11] which surpasses the scale of destruction in Cologne and Dresden and approaches that of Hamburg during World War II.[^cite_note-cbc20231230-3][^cite_note-ft20231206-12]



Israeli bombing of [Gaza City](55387.md)

Scholars termed the destruction of Gaza a [domicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domicide), leading the [UN special rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_special_rapporteur) on the right to housing to argue that [international law](8195726.md) should be amended to consider domicide a war crime.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] Israel's airstrikes were described as a [carpet bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet_bombing) and "[indiscriminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscriminate_attack)".[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] An US intelligence report found half of the bombs dropped on Gaza had been [unguided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unguided_bomb).[^cite_note-203] Experts stated the bombing campaign against Gaza had been among the deadliest and most destructive in modern history, with Corey Scher of the [CUNY Graduate Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY_Graduate_Center) saying that "Gaza is now a different color from space."[^cite_note-apnews20231221-204] Several months later, Scher, who was involved with mapping the destruction in Gaza, stated, "The rate of damage being registered is unlike anything we have studied before. It is much faster and more extensive than anything we have mapped".[^cite_note-205]

*[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* termed Israel's bombing the "most devastating urban warfare in the modern record".[^cite_note-Maslin-Shah-2023-26] According to analysis by [Humanity & Inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanity_&_Inclusion), approximately 45,000 bombs were dropped on the Gaza Strip in the conflict's first three months, but with a 9% to 14% failure rate, several thousand [unexploded bombs](406804.md) lay amongst the ruins.[^cite_note-206] The [United Nations Mine Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mine_Action_Service) estimated that there was more rubble in Gaza (25 miles long) than in all of Ukraine (600 miles long), with the rubble in Gaza likely "heavily contaminated" by unexploded ordnance.[^cite_note-207] In June 2024, scholars referred to the bombing of Gaza as an example of [urbicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbicide), or the "deliberate, widespread destruction of the urban environment".[^cite_note-208]

## War crimes

Main article: [Israeli war crimes in the Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_war_crimes_in_the_Gaza_war)



Palestinian refugee carries his injured grandchildren from the Israeli bombing of Nuseirat Camp, 28 October 2023

A group of [UN special rapporteurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_special_rapporteur) asserted that Israel's airstrikes are indiscriminate, stating that the airstrikes are "absolutely prohibited under international law" and amount to the war crime of [collective punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_punishment).[^cite_note-UN_experts-209] Israeli [military spokesman](5257972.md) [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_aluf) [Daniel Hagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hagari) said that "while balancing accuracy with the scope of damage, right now we're focused on what causes maximum damage".[^cite_note-210] A *[+972 Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/+972_Magazine)* investigation found the IDF had expanded authorization for bombing non-military targets.[^cite_note-211] Research conducted by Dr. Yagil Levy at the [Open University of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University_of_Israel) confirmed the *+972* report, which stated Israel was "deliberately targeting residential blocks to cause mass civilian casualties".[^cite_note-212]



Israeli army [F-15](495724.md) fighter-bomber loaded with American bombs [JDAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munition), 27 September 2024

[^cite_note-213]

During two airstrikes on 10 and 22 October 2023, the IDF used [Joint Direct Attack Munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munitions) (JDAM) in attacks described by [Amnesty International](18947898.md) as "either direct attacks on civilians" or "indiscriminate attacks".[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215] [Marc Garlasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Garlasco), a war crimes investigator, stated a JDAM bomb "turns earth to liquid".[^cite_note-apnews20231221-204] On 12 January 2024, the spokesperson for the [Office of the U.N. High Commissioner for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_U.N._High_Commissioner_for_Human_Rights) stated Israel's attacks were failing to account for distinction, proportionality and precautions, thus leaving Israeli exposed to liability for war crimes.[^cite_note-216] IDF has argued that it uses delayed fuzing, so that the bomb explodes underground and minimizes the blast and fragmentation; however, experts argue that delayed fuzing creates a new problem of leveling residential buildings, as was seen in the [31 October 2023 Jabalia refugee camp airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_October_2023_Jabalia_refugee_camp_airstrike).[^cite_note-217] This delayed fuzing "pancakes" buildings and endangers civilians in buildings nearby.[^cite_note-218]

In February 2024, the IDF bombed and destroyed the [Belgian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_government)'s Gaza development office.[^cite_note-219] In response, Belgium recalled the Israeli ambassador and condemned the "destruction of civilian infrastructure" as a violation of international law.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-222] Scott Lucas, a professor at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham), stated Israel's bombing campaign was in breach of the law of proportionality.[^cite_note-223] In June 2024, the UN Human Rights Office published a report stating Israel's use of heavy bombardment raised "serious concerns under the laws of war".[^cite_note-224] The head of an independent [U.N. Commission of Inquiry](69971286.md) stated Israel's use of heavy weapons in dense areas "constitutes an intentional and direct attack on the civilian population".[^cite_note-225]

Attorney Dylan Saba argues that Israel's dropping of 2,000 lb bombs (each with a lethal fragmentation radius of 1,200 feet) in densely populated civilian areas is as indiscriminate as using [chemical weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_weapons).[^cite_note-nplusone_12jan-226] This is because such bombs kill everyone within their lethal radius, both militant and civilian, without [distinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinction_\(law\)).[^cite_note-nplusone_12jan-226]

## Aftermath



[Forced displacement](427408.md) of Palestinians in the Gaza Strip devastated by Israeli bombardments, 29 January 2025

The bombardment left behind a large amount of debris, including [unexploded ordnance](406804.md). An official from [United Nations Mine Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mine_Action_Service) (UNMAS), has said it could take up to 14 years to remove the debris, including the rubble of destroyed buildings.[^cite_note-un-news-2024-04-227][^cite_note-reuters-2024-04-26-228] As of June 2024, the war left an estimated 39 million tons of debris in a widely urbanized, densely populated area, according to the [UN Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Environment_Programme).[^cite_note-229] This number had grown to 42 million tons by August 2024.[^cite_note-Gaza_Reduced_to_42_Million_Tonnes-230] In July 2024, [UNOCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOCHA) warned of the "significant risk" of explosive remnants of war and unexploded ordnance on civilians in Gaza, citing multiple casualties caused by unexploded ordnance.[^cite_note-231] In addition to fears about unexploded ordnance, health officials were concerned that Israel's bombing of buildings exposed civilians to highly carcinogenic airborne particles, including asbestos.[^cite_note-232] A UN estimate found that clearing the 40m tonnes of rubble in Gaza could take as long as 15 years and cost between \$500 and \$600 million.[^cite_note-233] The [Norwegian Refugee Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Refugee_Council) further warned that when the war ends, half of all families in Gaza will be homeless due to the destruction of their homes.[^cite_note-234]

According to the United Nations, the Israel military's destruction of infrastructure set back Gaza's human development by nearly 70 years.[^cite_note-235]

### Rebuilding

The *[Financial Times](136566.md)* estimated it would cost billions to rebuild Gaza.[^cite_note-236] [Mohammed Mustafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Mustafa_\(politician\)), the chief economist of the [Palestine Investment Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Investment_Fund), estimated rebuilding Gaza's homes alone would cost around US\$15 billion.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-239] The [World Bank](36752.md) and the [United Nations](31769.md) estimated in April 2024 that the war had caused \$18.5 billion worth of damage to Gaza's infrastructure thus far.[^cite_note-240] In May 2024, the [United Nations Development Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Program) stated it would take at least until 2040 to rebuild the homes destroyed in Gaza.[^cite_note-241] [Mark Jarzombek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Jarzombek), a professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), stated, "The cost of rebuilding will be prohibitive. Construction sites on this scale have to be empty of people, creating another wave of displacements. No matter what one does, for generations Gaza will be struggling with this".[^cite_note-Gaza_Reduced_to_42_Million_Tonnes-230]
