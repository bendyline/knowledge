**Azad Jammu and Kashmir** ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): آزاد جموں و کشمیر, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Urdu): *[Āzād Jammū̃ o Kaśmīr](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a2/LL-Q1617_%28urd%29-%D9%86%D8%B9%D9%85_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%84-%D8%A2%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%AF_%D8%AC%D9%85%D9%88%DA%BA_%D9%88_%DA%A9%D8%B4%D9%85%DB%8C%D8%B1.wav/LL-Q1617_%28urd%29-%D9%86%D8%B9%D9%85_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%84-%D8%A2%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%AF_%D8%AC%D9%85%D9%88%DA%BA_%D9%88_%DA%A9%D8%B4%D9%85%DB%8C%D8%B1.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Free Jammu and Kashmir'),[^cite_note-Bose-8] abbreviated as **AJK** and colloquially referred to as simply **Azad Kashmir** ([/ˌɑːzæd kæʃˈmɪər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AH-zad kash-MEER*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-9] is a region administered by [Pakistan](23235.md) as a nominally self-governing entity[^cite_note-self-governing-10] and constituting the western portion of the larger [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) region, which has been the subject of a [dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir#Kashmir_dispute) between [India](14533.md) and Pakistan [since 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947%E2%80%931948).[^cite_note-tertiary-kashmir-1] On its eastern side, Azad Kashmir is separated from the [Indian-administered territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian-administered_Kashmir) of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) by the [Line of Control (LoC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control), which serves as the *[de facto](43594.md)* border between the Indian and Pakistani-controlled parts of Kashmir. Azad Kashmir borders with the Pakistani-administered [Gilgit–Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit%E2%80%93Baltistan) to the north; it shares borders with the Pakistani provinces of [Punjab](24751.md) and [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) to the south and west, respectively. Geographically, it covers a total area of 13,297 km<sup>2</sup> (5,134 sq mi) and has a total population of over 4.33 million as per the [2023 national census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census).[^cite_note-\:0-11]&#x20;

The territory has a parliamentary form of government modelled after the [British](31717.md) [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system), with the city of [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) serving as its [capital](181337.md). The [President of AJK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Azad_Kashmir) is the constitutional [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), while the [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Azad_Kashmir), supported by a [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers), is the chief executive. The unicameral [Azad Kashmir Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Legislative_Assembly) elects both the Prime Minister and President. The territory has its own [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir) and a [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir), while the [Government of Pakistan](1897833.md)'s [Ministry of Kashmir Affairs and Gilgit-Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Kashmir_Affairs_and_Gilgit-Baltistan) serves as a link between itself and Azad Jammu and Kashmir's government, although the autonomous territory is not represented in the [Parliament of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan).&#x20;

Northern Azad Kashmir lies in a region that experiences strong vibrations of the earth as a result of the [Indian plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Australian_Plate) underthrusting the [Eurasian plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_plate).[^cite_note-12] A major [earthquake in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake) killed at least 100,000 people and left another three million people displaced, causing widespread devastation to the region's infrastructure and economy. Since then, with help from the Government of Pakistan and [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_response_to_the_2005_Kashmir_earthquake), reconstruction of infrastructure is underway. Azad Kashmir's economy largely depends on agriculture, services, tourism, and remittances sent by members of the [British Mirpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Mirpuris) community. Nearly 87% of Azad Kashmiri households own farm property,[^cite_note-13] and the region has the highest rate of school enrolment in Pakistan and a literacy rate of approximately 74%.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-tribune.com.pk-15]&#x20;

## Name

*Azad Kashmir* (Free Kashmir) was the title of a pamphlet issued by the [Muslim Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Muslim_Conference) party at its 13th general session held in 1945 at [Poonch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poonch_\(town\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBehera,_Demystifying_Kashmir200720-16] It is believed to have been a response to the [National Conference](512306.md)'s *Naya Kashmir* (New Kashmir) programme.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKapoor,_Politics_of_Protests_in_Jammu_and_Kashmir2014Chapter_6,_p. 273-17] Sources state that it was no more than a compilation of various resolutions passed by the party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGanai,_Dogra_Raj_and_the_Struggle_for_Freedom_in_Kashmir1999Chapter_6,_p. 341-18] But its intent seems to have been to declare that the Muslims of Jammu and Kashmir were committed to the [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League)'s struggle for a separate homeland (Pakistan),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBehera,_Demystifying_Kashmir200720-16] and that the Muslim Conference was the sole representative organisation of the Muslims of Kashmir.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKapoor,_Politics_of_Protests_in_Jammu_and_Kashmir2014Chapter_6,_p. 273-17] However, the following year, the party passed an "Azad Kashmir resolution" demanding that the maharaja institute a constituent assembly elected on an extended franchise.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaraf,_Kashmiris_Fight_for_Freedom,_Volume_12015663-19] According to scholar [Chitralekha Zutshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitralekha_Zutshi), the organisation's declared goal was to achieve responsible government under the aegis of the maharaja without association with either India or Pakistan.[^cite_note-20] The following year, the party workers assembled at the house of [Sardar Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Ibrahim) on 19 July 1947, reversed the decision, demanding that the Maharaja accede to Pakistan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaraf,_Kashmiris_Fight_for_Freedom,_Volume_220159-21][^cite_note-22]&#x20;

Soon afterward, [Sardar Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Ibrahim_Khan) escaped to Pakistan and led the [Poonch rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Poonch_rebellion) from there, with the assistance of Pakistan's prime minister [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan) and other officials. Liaquat Ali Khan appointed a committee headed by [Mian Iftikharuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mian_Iftikharuddin) to draft a "declaration of freedom".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaraf,_Kashmiris_Fight_for_Freedom,_Volume_22015148–149-23] On 4 October, an Azad Kashmir provisional government was declared in Lahore with Ghulam Nabi Gilkar as president under the assumed name "Mr. Anwar" and Sardar Ibrahim as the prime minister. Gilkar travelled to Srinagar and was arrested by the maharaja's government. Pakistani officials subsequently appointed Sardar Ibrahim as the president of the provisional government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaraf,_Kashmiris_Fight_for_Freedom,_Volume_22015547544–545-24][^cite_note-26]&#x20;

## History

Main articles: [History of Azad Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Azad_Kashmir) and [1947 Poonch rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Poonch_rebellion)



A 1946 map of the [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)); present-day Azad Kashmir constitutes areas of the three western-most districts

At the time of the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, the British abandoned their [suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty) over the [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states), which were left with the options of joining India or Pakistan or remaining independent. [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh), the [maharaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja) of Jammu and Kashmir, wanted his state to remain independent.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-britannica-28] Muslims in the western districts of the Jammu province (current day Azad Kashmir) and in the Frontier Districts province (current day [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md)) had wanted to join Pakistan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnedden2013p._14:_"Similarly,_Muslims_in_Western_Jammu_Province,_particularly_in_Poonch,_many_of_whom_had_martial_capabilities,_and_Muslims_in_the_Frontier_Districts_Province_strongly_wanted_J\&K_to_join_Pakistan."-29]&#x20;

In Spring 1947, an uprising against the maharaja broke out in [Poonch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poonch_district,_Pakistan), an area bordering the [Rawalpindi division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_division) of [West Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Punjab). The maharaja's administration is said to have started levying punitive taxes on the peasantry which provoked a local revolt and the administration resorted to brutal suppression. The area's population, swelled by recently demobilised soldiers following [World War II](32927.md), rebelled against the maharaja's forces and gained control of almost the entire district. Following this victory, the pro-Pakistan chieftains of the western districts of [Muzaffarabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarabad_district), [Poonch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Poonch_District) and [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur_district) proclaimed a provisional Azad Jammu and Kashmir government in Rawalpindi on 3 October 1947.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBose200332–33-30][^cite_note-32] Ghulam Nabi Gilkar, under the assumed name "Mr. Anwar," issued a proclamation in the name of the provisional government in [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md). However, this government quickly fizzled out with the arrest of Anwar in Srinagar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnedden2013p._59-33] On 24 October, a second provisional government of Azad Kashmir was established at [Palandri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palandri) under the leadership of [Sardar Ibrahim Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Ibrahim_Khan).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnedden2013p._61-34]&#x20;

On 21 October, several thousand [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) tribesmen from [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province_\(1901%E2%80%931955\)) poured into Jammu and Kashmir to help with the rebellion against the maharaja's rule. They were led by experienced military leaders and were equipped with modern arms. The maharaja's crumbling forces were unable to withstand the onslaught. The tribesmen captured the towns of Muzaffarabad and [Baramulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baramulla), the latter of which is 32 kilometres (20 mi) northwest of the state capital [Srinagar](294148.md). On 24 October, the Maharaja requested military assistance from India, which responded that it was unable to help him unless he acceded to India. Accordingly, on 26 October 1947, Maharaja Hari Singh signed an [Instrument of Accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Accession_\(Jammu_and_Kashmir\)), handing over control of defence, external affairs, and communications to the Government of India in return for military aid.[^cite_note-35] Indian troops were immediately airlifted into Srinagar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBose200335–36-36] Pakistan intervened subsequently.[^cite_note-britannica-28] Fighting ensued between the Indian and Pakistani armies, with the two areas of control more or less stabilised around what is now known as the "[Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control)".[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

India later approached the United Nations, asking it to resolve the dispute, and resolutions were passed in favour of the holding of a plebiscite with regard to Kashmir's future. However, no such plebiscite has ever been held on either side, since there was a precondition that required the withdrawal of the Pakistani army along with the non-state elements and the subsequent partial withdrawal of the Indian army[^cite_note-UN_Resolution_47-38] from the parts of Kashmir under their respective control – a withdrawal that never took place.[^cite_note-39] In 1949, a formal cease-fire line separating the Indian- and Pakistani-controlled parts of Kashmir came into effect.&#x20;

Following the [1949 cease-fire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Agreement) with India, the government of Pakistan divided the northern and western parts of Kashmir that it controlled at the time of the cease-fire into the following two separately controlled political entities:&#x20;

- Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK) – the narrow, southern part, 400 km (250 mi) long, with a width varying from 15 to 65 km (10 to 40 mi).

- [Gilgit–Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit%E2%80%93Baltistan) formerly called the [Federally Administered Northern Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Northern_Areas) (FANA) – the much larger political entity to the north of AJK with an area of 72,496 km

  2

  &#x20;(27,991 sq mi).

In 1955, the [Poonch uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Poonch_uprising) broke out. It was largely concentrated in areas of [Rawalakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalakot) as well as the rest of [Poonch Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poonch_Division). It ended in 1956.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnedden2013pp._120,_121,_122-40]&#x20;

In 1970, [Yahya Khan's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan) military administration promulgated a 'rudimentary' constitution, 'The Azad Jammu and Kashmir Government Act, 1970' which provided AJK a presidential system, an elected legislative assembly and 'considerable' autonomy. Snedden refers to it as having "delivered the most autonomy ever enjoyed by this region – or by any region in J\&K." The central government only controlling foreign affairs, defence and currency, while the MKA was sidelined.[^cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press-2018-41]<sup>: 121 </sup>&#x20;

[Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto), then Prime-Minister of Pakistan, with some local support imposed the '[Azad Jammu and Kashmir Interim Constitution Act, 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Constitution_of_Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(1974\))’ (Interim till the Kashmir dispute was resolved with India). It allowed AJK a directly elected [AJK Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Kashmir_Legislative_Assembly), and a smaller indirectly elected [Azad Jammu and Kashmir Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Council) in Islamabad. This arrangement reduced the power of the MKA, however Snedden referred to it as a "diminution."[^cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press-2018-41]<sup>: 121–122 </sup>&#x20;

Danish Khan in *[The Friday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Friday_Times)* characterises this development as providing "an avenue for citizens to draw attention from political elites towards immediate socio-economic and developmental concerns such as access to basic infrastructure and public goods," further stating "while public sector investments in infrastructure and social sectors have shown relative improvements over the years, the overarching narrative in the political sphere, spanning across party lines, remains heavily focused on the Jammu & Kashmir conflict rather than indigenous socio-economic development."[^cite_note-42]&#x20;

The Constitution provides Kashmir its own [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Azad_Kashmir), [Prime-Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Azad_Kashmir), [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir), [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir), Auditor General and Chief Election Commissioner as well.[^cite_note-43] The 13th Amendment to the AJK Constitution, passed in [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) empowered the AJK government, increased the powers of the elected assembly, granted Azad Kashmir greater financial and administrative powers and sought to make the federal territory more autonomous. The word "Act" was also deleted from the Constitution.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;

At one time under Pakistani control, Kashmir's [Shaksgam tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Karakoram_Tract), a small region along the northeastern border of Gilgit–Baltistan, was provisionally ceded by Pakistan to the People's Republic of China in 1963 which helped in resolving China-Pakistan boundary dispute and now the forms part of China's [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) Uygur Autonomous Region.&#x20;

In 1972, the then current border between the Indian and Pakistani controlled parts of Kashmir was designated as the "[Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control)". This line has remained unchanged[^cite_note-45] since the 1972 [Simla Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simla_Agreement), which bound the two countries "to settle their differences by peaceful means through bilateral negotiations". Some political experts claim that, in view of that pact, the only solution to the issue is mutual negotiation between the two countries without involving a third party such as the United Nation.[^cite_note-46] The 1974 Interim Constitution Act was passed by the 48-member Azad Jammu and Kashmir unicameral assembly.[^cite_note-47]&#x20;

In April 1997, the [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif) government refused to grant constitutional status to Azad Jammu and Kashmir stating that "'The grant of constitutional rights to these people will amount to unilateral annexation of these areas."[^cite_note-48]&#x20;

## Geography



Landscape of Azad Kashmir

The northern part of Azad Jammu and Kashmir encompasses the lower area of the [Himalayas](37966.md), including Jamgarh Peak (4,734 m or 15,531 ft). However, [Sarwali Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarwali_Peak) (6326 m) in [Neelum Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_Valley) is the highest peak in the state.[^cite_note-brit-2]&#x20;

The region receives rainfall in both the winter and the summer. [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) and Pattan are among the wettest areas of Pakistan. Throughout most of the region, the average rainfall exceeds 1400 mm, with the highest average rainfall occurring near Muzaffarabad (around 1800 mm). During the summer season, monsoon floods of the rivers [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River) and Leepa are common due to extreme rains and snow melting.&#x20;

### Climate



Map of the Köppen-Geiger climate classification

The southern parts of Azad Kashmir, including the Bhimber, Mirpur, and Kotli districts, have extremely hot weather in the summer and moderate cold weather in the winter. They receive rain mostly in [monsoon](57630.md) weather.&#x20;



Paddy field in [Leepa valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leepa_Valley)

In the central and northern parts of the state, the weather remains moderately hot in the summer and cold and chilly in the winter. Snowfall also occurs there in December and January.&#x20;

The region receives rainfall in both the winter and the summer. [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) and Pattan are among the wettest areas of the state, but they don't receive snow. Throughout most of the region, the average rainfall exceeds 1400 mm, with the highest average rainfall occurring near Muzaffarabad (around 1800 mm). During summer, monsoon floods of the [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River) and Leepa rivers are common, due to high rainfall and melting snow.&#x20;

Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK) is nominally a self-governing state,[^cite_note-49] but ever since the 1949 ceasefire between Indian and Pakistani forces, Pakistan has exercised control over the state without incorporating it into Pakistan.[^cite_note-brit-2][^cite_note-ajk144-50] Azad Kashmir has its own elected [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(government_title\)), [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister), [legislative assembly](222426.md), [high court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_court), and [official flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Azad_Kashmir).[^cite_note-ajk.gov.pk-51]&#x20;

Azad Kashmir's budget and tax affairs, are dealt with by the [Azad Jammu and Kashmir Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Council) rather than by Pakistan's Central Board of Revenue. The Azad Jammu and Kashmir Council is a supreme body consisting of 14 members, 8 from the government of Azad Jammu and Kashmir and 6 from the government of Pakistan. Its chairman/chief executive is the prime minister of Pakistan. Other members of the council are the president and the prime minister of Azad Kashmir (or an individual nominated by her/him) and 6 members of the AJK Legislative Assembly.[^cite_note-ajk.gov.pk-51][^cite_note-ajk144-50] Azad Kashmir Day is celebrated in Azad Jammu and Kashmir on 24 October, which is the day that the Azad Jammu and Kashmir government was created in 1947. Pakistan has celebrated [Kashmir Solidarity Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Solidarity_Day) on 5 February of each year since 1990 as a day of protest against [India](14533.md)'s sovereignty over its State of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)).[^cite_note-52] That day is a [national holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Pakistan) in Pakistan.[^cite_note-53] Pakistan observes the Kashmir [Accession Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_Day_\(Jammu_and_Kashmir\)) as Black Day on 27 October of each year since 1947 as a day of protest against the accession of [Jammu and Kashmir State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_State) to India and its military presence in the Indian-controlled parts of Jammu and Kashmir.&#x20;

[Brad Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Adams), the Asia director at the U.S.-based [NGO](46539.md) [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) said in 2006: "Although 'azad' means 'free,' the residents of Azad Kashmir are anything but; the Pakistani authorities govern the Azad Kashmir government with tight controls on basic freedoms."[^cite_note-Adams-54] Scholar [Christopher Snedden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Snedden) has observed that despite tight controls, the people of Azad Kashmir have generally accepted whatever Pakistan has done to them, which in any case has varied little from how most Pakistanis have been treated (by Pakistan). According to [Christopher Snedden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Snedden), one of the reasons for this was that the people of Azad Kashmir had always wanted to be part of Pakistan.[^cite_note-Snedden_2013-55]&#x20;

Consequently, having little to fear from a pro-Pakistan population devoid of options,[^cite_note-Snedden_2013-55] Pakistan imposed its will through the Federal [Ministry of Kashmir Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Kashmir_Affairs_\(Pakistan\)) and failed to empower the people of Azad Kashmir, allowing genuine self-government for only a short period in the 1970s. According to the interim constitution that was drawn up in the 1970s, the only political parties that are allowed to exist are those that pay allegiance to Pakistan: "No person or political party in Azad Jammu and Kashmir shall be permitted... activities prejudicial or detrimental to the State's accession to Pakistan."[^cite_note-Snedden_2013-55] The pro-independence [Jammu and Kashmir Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Liberation_Front) has never been allowed to contest elections in Azad Kashmir.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBose2003100-56] While the interim constitution does not give them a choice, the people of Azad Kashmir have not considered any option other than joining Pakistan.[^cite_note-Snedden_2013-55] Except in a legal sense, Azad Kashmir has been fully integrated into Pakistan.[^cite_note-Snedden_2013-55]&#x20;

Azad Kashmir is home to a vibrant civil society. One of the organisations active in the territory and inside Pakistan is [YFK-International Kashmir Lobby Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YFK-International_Kashmir_Lobby_Group), an NGO that seeks better India-Pakistan relations through conflict resolution in Kashmir.&#x20;

### Development project

According to the project report by the [Asian Development Bank](2512.md), the bank has set out development goals for Azad Kashmir in the areas of health, education, nutrition, and social development. The whole project is estimated to cost US\$76 million.[^cite_note-57] [Germany](11867.md), between 2006 and 2014, has also donated \$38 million towards the AJK Health Infrastructure Programme.[^cite_note-58]&#x20;

## Demographics

### Population

The population of Azad Kashmir, according to the preliminary results of [2023 Pakistani census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), is 4.33 million.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-61] The website of the AJK government reports the literacy rate to be 74%, with the enrolment rate in primary school being 98% and 90% for boys and girls respectively.[^cite_note-62]&#x20;

The population of Azad Kashmir is almost entirely Muslim. The people of this region culturally differ from the [Kashmiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_people) living in the [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley) of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and are closer to the culture of [Pir Panjal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_Panjal_Region_\(Jammu_Division\)). [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur,_Azad_Kashmir), [Kotli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotli), and [Bhimber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimber) are all old towns of the Jammu region.[^cite_note-HRW_report-63]&#x20;

### Religion

Azad Jammu and Kashmir has an almost entirely [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) population. According to data maintained by Christian community organisations, there are around 4,500 Christian residents in the region. Bhimber is home to most of them, followed by Mirpur and Muzaffarabad. A few dozen families also live in Kotli, Poonch, and Bagh. However, the Christian community has been struggling to get residential status and property rights in AJK.&#x20;

There is no official data on the total number of [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) adherents in AJK. Only six Baháʼí families are known to be living in Muzaffarabad with others living in rural areas.&#x20;

The followers of the [Ahmadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadi) faith are estimated to be somewhere between 20,000 and 25,000, and most of them live in Kotli, Mirpur, Bhimber, and Muzaffarabad.[^cite_note-64]&#x20;

### Ethnic groups

See also: [Azad Kashmiri diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Kashmiri_diaspora)

[Christopher Snedden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Snedden) writes that most of the native residents of Azad Kashmir are not of [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris) ethnicity; rather, they could be called "[Jammuites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_region)" due to their historical and cultural links with that region, which is coterminous with neighbouring [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region) and [Hazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_region).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Because their region was formerly a part of the [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) of Jammu and Kashmir and is named after it, many Azad Kashmiris have adopted the "Kashmiri" identity, whereas in an ethnolinguistic context, the term "Kashmiri" would ordinarily refer to natives of the [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley) region.[^cite_note-Snedden2015-73] The population of Azad Kashmir has strong historical, cultural and linguistic affinities with the neighbouring populations of upper [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) and [Potohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau) region of Pakistan,[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] whereas the [Sudhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudhans) have the oral tradition of Pashtuns.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnedden2013p._43:_"Sudhans_from_Poonch_considered_themselves_to_be_''Sudho_Zai''_Pathans_(Pukhtoons),_which_explained_why_the_\[\[Pashtuns|Pashtun\]\]_tribesmen_from_NWFP_province_lost_no_time_coming_to_help_Jammu_and_Kashmir's_Muslims_in_1947.."-77]&#x20;

### Languages

Languages by approximate number of speakers

[^cite_note-SYB_2020-85]

1. [Pahari-Pothwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari-Pothwari) (68.0%)

2. [Gojri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujari_language) (19.0%)

3. [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_language) (5.00%)

4. Others (8.00%)

The official language of Azad Kashmir is [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahman1996226-86][^cite_note-87] while [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_in_Pakistan) is used in higher [domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_of_language_use). The majority of the population, however, are native speakers of other languages. The foremost among these is [Pahari–Pothwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari%E2%80%93Pothwari) (a dialect of [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_dialects_and_languages)) with its various dialects. There are also sizeable communities speaking [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_language) (mostly in the north), [Gujari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujari_language) (throughout the territory), and [Dogri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogri_language) (in the south), as well as pockets of speakers of [Kundal Shahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundal_Shahi_language), [Shina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shina_language) and [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto). With the exception of Pashto and English, those languages belong to the [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages) language family.&#x20;

The dialects of the [Pahari-Pothwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari-Pothwari) (a [Punjabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_dialects_and_languages) dialect in of itself) language complex cover most of the territory of Azad Kashmir. Those are also spoken across the Line of Control in the neighbouring areas of Indian Jammu and Kashmir and are closely related both to [Majhi Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) to the south, other [Lahnda Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahnda) dialects and [Hindko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hindko) to the northwest. The language variety in the southern districts of Azad Kashmir is known by a variety of names – including *Mirpuri*, *Pothwari* and *Pahari* – and is closely related to the Pothwari dialect proper spoken to the east in the [Pothohar region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_region) of Punjab. The dialects of the central districts of Azad Kashmir are occasionally referred to in the literature as *Chibhali* or *Punchi*, but the speakers themselves usually call them *Pahari*, an ambiguous name that is also used for [several unrelated languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari_language_\(name\)) of the lower Himalayas. Going north, the speech forms gradually change into Hindko. Today, in the [Muzaffarabad District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarabad_District) the preferred local name for the language is *Hindko*, although it is still apparently more closely related to the core dialects of Pahari.[^cite_note-88] Further north in the [Neelam Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelam_Valley) the dialect, locally also known as *Parmi*, can more unambiguously be subsumed under Hindko.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAkhtarRehman200768-89]&#x20;

Another major language of Azad Kashmir is [Gujari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujari_language). It is spoken by several hundred thousand[^cite_note-90] people among the traditionally nomadic [Gujars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujars), many of whom are nowadays settled. Not all ethnic Gujars speak Gujari, the proportion of those who have shifted to other languages is probably higher in southern Azad Kashmir.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHallbergO'Leary199296,_98,_100-91] Gujari is most closely related to the [Rajasthani languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthani_languages) (particularly [Mewati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewati_language)), although it also shares features with Punjabi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHallbergO'Leary199293–94-92] It is dispersed over large areas in northern Pakistan and India. Within Pakistan, the Gujari dialects of Azad Kashmir are more similar, in terms of shared basic vocabulary and mutual intelligibility, to the Gujar varieties of the neighbouring [Hazara region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_region_\(Pakistan\)) than to the dialects spoken further to the northwest in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and north in [Gilgit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit_District).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHallbergO'Leary1992111–12,_126-93]&#x20;

There are scattered communities of [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_language) speakers,[^cite_note-94] notably in the [Neelam Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelam_Valley), where they form the second-largest language group after speakers of Hindko.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAkhtarRehman200770-95] There have been calls for the teaching of Kashmiri (particularly in order to counter India's claim of promoting the culture of Kashmir), but the limited attempts at introducing the language at the secondary school level have not been successful, and it is Urdu, rather than Kashmiri, that Kashmiri Muslims have seen as their identity symbol.[^cite_note-96] There is an ongoing process of gradual [shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_shift) to larger local languages,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERahman1996226-86] but at least in the Neelam Valley there still exist communities for whom Kashmiri is the sole mother tongue.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAkhtarRehman200770,_75-97]&#x20;

There are speakers of [Dogri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogri_language) in the southernmost [district of Bhimber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimber_District), where they are estimated to represent almost a third of the district's population.[^cite_note-SYB_2020-85] In the northernmost [district of Neelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_District#Languages), there are small communities of speakers of several other languages. [Shina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shina_language), which like Kashmiri belongs to the broad [Dardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardic_languages) group, is present in two distinct varieties spoken altogether in three villages. [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto), of the [Iranian subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_languages) and the majority language in the neighbouring province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, is spoken in two villages, both situated on the Line of Control. The endangered [Kundal Shahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundal_Shahi_language) is native to the eponymous village and it is the only language not found outside Azad Kashmir.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAkhtarRehman2007-98]&#x20;

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Azad Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Azad_Kashmir)



[Neelum Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_District) is a tourist destination in Azad Kashmir.

As of 2021, GDP of Azad Jammu and Kashmir was estimated to be 10 billion pounds, giving per capita an income of £5604.[^cite_note-99] Historically the economy of Azad Kashmir has been agricultural which meant that land was the main source or mean of production. This means that all food for immediate and long-term consumption was produced from the land. The produce included various crops, fruits, vegetables, etc. The land was also the source of other livelihood necessities such as wood, fuel, grazing for animals which then turned into dairy products. Because of this land was also the main source of revenue for the governments whose primary purpose for centuries was to accumulate revenue.[^cite_note-100]&#x20;

Agriculture is a major part of Azad Kashmir's economy. Low-lying areas that have high populations grow crops like [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) (maize), and [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and also raise cattle. In the elevated areas that are less populated and more spread out, forestry, corn, and livestock are the main sources of income. There are mineral and marble resources in Azad Kashmir close to [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur,_Azad_Kashmir) and [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md). There are also graphite deposits at Mohriwali. There are also reservoirs of low-grade coal, [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), and [zircon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zircon). Local household industries produce carved wooden objects, textiles, and dhurrie carpets.[^cite_note-brit-2] There is also an arts and crafts industry that produces such cultural goods as namdas, shawls, pashmina, pherans, [Papier-mâché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papier-m%C3%A2ch%C3%A9), basketry copper, rugs, wood carving, silk and woolen clothing, patto, carpets, namda gubba, and silverware. Agricultural goods produced in the region include mushrooms, honey, walnuts, apples, cherries, medicinal herbs and plants, resin, deodar, kail, chir, fir, maple, and ash timber.[^cite_note-brit-2][^cite_note-ajk144-50][^cite_note-101]&#x20;



Munda Gali, [Leepa Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leepa_Valley)

The migration to the UK was accelerated and by the completion of [Mangla Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangla_Dam) in 1967 the process of '[chain migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_migration)' became in full flow. Today, remittances from [British Mirpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Mirpuri) community make a critical role in AJK's economy. In the mid-1950s various economic and social development processes were launched in Azad Kashmir. In the 1960s, with the construction of the Mangla Dam in [Mirpur District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur_District), the Azad Jammu and Kashmir Government began to receive royalties from the Pakistani government for the electricity that the dam provided to Pakistan. During the mid-2000s, a multibillion-dollar reconstruction began in the aftermath of the [2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake).[^cite_note-dawn.com-102]&#x20;

In addition to agriculture, textiles, and arts and crafts, remittances have played a major role in the economy of Azad Kashmir. One analyst estimated that the figure for Azad Kashmir was 25.1% in 2001. With regard to annual household income, people living in the higher areas are more dependent on remittances than are those living in the lower areas.[^cite_note-103] In the latter part of 2006, billions of dollars for development were mooted by [international aid agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_aid_agencies) for the reconstruction and rehabilitation of earthquake-hit zones in Azad Kashmir, though much of that amount was subsequently lost in bureaucratic channels, leading to considerable delays in help getting to the neediest. Hundreds of people continued to live in tents long after the earthquake.[^cite_note-dawn.com-102] A land-use plan for the city of [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) was prepared by the [Japan International Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_International_Cooperation_Agency).&#x20;

Tourist destinations in the area include the following:&#x20;

- **[Muzaffarabad](1119495.md)**, the capital city of Azad Kashmir, is located on the banks of the Jhelum and Neelum rivers. It is 138 km (86 mi) from [Rawalpindi](343524.md) and [Islamabad](51153.md). Well-known tourist spots near Muzaffarabad are the [Red Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarabad_Fort), [Pir Chinassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirchinassi), Patika, [Subri Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subri_Lake) and [Awan Patti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awan_Patti).

- The **[Neelam Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelam_Valley)** is situated to the north and northeast of Muzaffarabad, The gateway to the valley. The main tourist attractions in the valley are [Athmuqam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athmuqam), [Kutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutton), [Keran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keran,_Azad_Kashmir), [Changan](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Changan,_Azad_Kashmir\&action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sharda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharda,_Azad_Kashmir), [Kel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kel,_Kashmir), [Arang Kel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arang_Kel) and [Taobat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taobat).

- **[Sudhanoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudhanoti)** is one of the ten districts of Azad Kashmir in Pakistan. Sudhanoti is located 90 km (56 mi) away from Islamabad, the Capital of Pakistan. It is connected with Rawalpindi and Islamabad through Azad Pattan road.

- **[Rawalakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalakot)** city is the headquarters of Poonch District and is located 122 km (76 mi) from Islamabad. Tourist attractions in Poonch District are [Banjosa Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjosa_Lake), [Devi Gali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Gali), [Tatta Pani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatta_Pani,_Azad_Kashmir), and [Toli Pir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toli_Pir).

- **[Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagh,_Azad_Kashmir)** city, the headquarters of Bagh District, is 205 km (127 mi) from Islamabad and 100 km (62 mi) from Muzaffarabad. The principal tourist attractions in Bagh District are Bagh Fort, [Dhirkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhirkot), [Sudhan Gali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudhan_Gali), [Ganga Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Lake_\(Pakistan\)), [Ganga Choti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Choti), [Kotla Waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotla_Waterfall,_Bagh), [Neela Butt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neela_Butt), [Danna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danna,_Bagh), [Panjal Mastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjal_Mastan) National Park, and [Las Danna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Danna).

- The **[Leepa Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leepa_Valley)** is located 105 km (65 mi) southeast of Muzaffarabad. It is the most charming and scenic place for tourists in Azad Kashmir.

- **[New Mirpur City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mirpur_City)** is the headquarters of Mirpur District. The main tourist attractions near New Mirpur City are the [Mangla Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangla_Dam) and [Ramkot Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramkot_Fort).

## Sports

[Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Pakistan), [cricket](14603845.md), and [volleyball](32558.md) are very popular in Azad Kashmir. Many tournaments are also held throughout the year and in the holy month of [Ramazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_\(calendar_month\)), night-time flood-lit tournaments are also organised.&#x20;

### Cricket

Azad Kashmir has its own [T20](770690.md) tournament called the [Kashmir Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Premier_League_\(Pakistan\)), which started in 2021. [New Mirpur City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mirpur_City) has a cricket stadium ([Quaid-e-Azam Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaid-e-Azam_Stadium)) which has been taken over by the [Pakistan Cricket Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Cricket_Board) for renovation to bring it up to the international standards. There is also a cricket stadium in [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) with a capacity of 8,000 people. This stadium has hosted 8 matches of the Inter-District Under 19 Tournament 2013.&#x20;

### Football

There are several registered football clubs in Azad Kashmir. Mirpur based [Pilot FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pilot_FC\&action=edit\&redlink=1) competed at the [2005 National Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_National_Challenge_Cup), the top tier cup competition of Pakistan.[^cite_note-112] Rawalakot based Eleven Star has competed in the second-tier [PFF League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFF_League).[^cite_note-113] Other notable football clubs include Youth Football Club, Kashmir National FC, and Azad Super FC.&#x20;

## Tourism

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Tourism in Azad Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Azad_Kashmir).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tourism_in_Azad_Kashmir\&action=edit)]

The northern part of Azad Jammu and Kashmir encompasses the lower part of the [Himalayas](37966.md), including Jamgarh Peak (15,531 feet, or 4,734 meters). However, [Sarwali peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarwali_Peak) in the [Neelum Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_Valley) is the highest peak in the state.[^cite_note-114] Fertile, green, mountainous valleys are characteristic of Azad Kashmir's geography and it is considered one of the most beautiful regions on the subcontinent.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Azad_Kashmir_brit-115]&#x20;

The southern parts of Azad Kashmir including Bhimber, Mirpur and Kotli districts has extremely hot weather in summers and moderate cold weather in winters. It receives rains mostly in [monsoon](57630.md) weather.&#x20;

In the central and northern parts of state weather remains moderate hot in summers and very cold and chilly in winter. Snow fall also occurs there in December and January.&#x20;

This region receives rainfall in both winters and summers. [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) and Pattan are among the wettest areas of the state. Throughout most of the region, the average rainfall exceeds 1400 mm, with the highest average rainfall occurring near Muzaffarabad (around 1800 mm). During summer, monsoon floods of the [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River) and Leepa river are common, due to high rainfall and melting snow.&#x20;
