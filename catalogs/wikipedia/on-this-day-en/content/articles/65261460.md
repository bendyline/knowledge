**Isar Aerospace** is a German [aerospace company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer) based in [Ottobrunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottobrunn) near [Munich](19058.md). It was founded in 2018[^cite_note-2], named after [the river that flows through Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isar).[^cite_note-3]

The company developed [Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_\(rocket\)), a two-stage, liquid-fueled [rocket](26301.md) designed to launch up to 1,000 kilograms to [low Earth orbit](47568.md).[^cite_note-4] Isar Aerospace intends to manufacture 80% of the rocket themselves, using primarily technology from the tech firms in the area surrounding Munich.[^cite_note-5]

On 5 September 2026, the company's second launch of Spectrum reached orbit and successfully deployed its payloads, making it the first successful orbital flight from [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe), making [Andøya Spaceport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And%C3%B8ya_Spaceport) the second active spaceport in Europe after [Plesetsk Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesetsk_Cosmodrome) and third European spaceport in history after [Kapustin Yar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapustin_Yar) launch facility and [Plesetsk Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesetsk_Cosmodrome).[^cite_note-ReutersSeptember5-6]

## Funding

In 2018 Isar Aerospace received first angel investment from early [SpaceX](832774.md) leader [Bulent Altan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulent_Altan) and others.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] In April 2020 Isar Aerospace closed US\$17M in Series A funding led by Earlybird and [Airbus](26220236.md) Ventures to build its space platform.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] In December 2020, the company got an additional €75 million (\$91 million) in funding led by Lakestar and with participation of HV Capital and Ann-Kristin and [Paul Achleitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Achleitner) as well as existing investors.[^cite_note-11]

In July 2021, [Porsche SE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_SE) acquired a small stake in Isar Aerospace.[^cite_note-12] The financial terms of the deal are not disclosed, but it is known that in the new round of financing, the startup raised about \$75 million. Porsche explained of its investment that Isar Aerospace had great potential to become a leading [European](9239.md) manufacturer of launch vehicles.[^cite_note-tadviser.ru-13][^cite_note-14]

In March 2023, the startup raised a \$165 million Series C from 7-Industries Holding, Bayern Kapital, [Lombard Odier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Lombard_Odier_&_Co) and existing investors.[^cite_note-15] An additional \$70 million was raised in 2024 from the [NATO Innovation Fund](21133.md) and others as an extension of an earlier Series C round. The total funding has exceeded 400 million euros.[^cite_note-16]

## Partnerships

In April 2021, Isar Aerospace, signed a contract with [Andøya Space](30864935.md) to lease a launch pad. The 20-year deal allows the company to launch their Spectrum launch vehicle from [Andøya Spaceport](30864935.md).[^cite_note-17]

In January 2022 the company won [European Commission](9974.md)'s [European Innovation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Innovation_Council) (EIC) Horizon Prize "Low-Cost Space Launch", which included a grant of 10 million Euros.[^cite_note-18] In July 2022, Isar Aerospace announced that the French space agency, the [CNES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNES), selected the startup to be the first private company to launch from the [Guiana Space Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiana_Space_Centre), in [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana). The company will use the launchpad formerly used by [Diamant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamant).[^cite_note-19]

In November 2022, Isar Aerospace and [Exotrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotrail) signed a multiple launch services agreement. The launch vehicle Spectrum will launch Exotrail's spacevan vehicle on several firm launches to low Earth Orbit (LEO) and [geostationary transfer orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostationary_transfer_orbit) (GTO) from Isar Aerospace's launch sites.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In March 2026, Isar Aerospace would sign a launch service agreement with [Astroscale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astroscale), which will see the launch of the [ELSA-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astroscale#End-of-Life_Services_by_Astroscale_\(ELSA\)) in a In-Orbit Demonstration mission.[^cite_note-23]

In May 2026, Isar Aerospace established a cooperation with the German maritime defence company [TKMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TKMS) as part of their (successful)[^cite_note-24] bid for the [Canadian Patrol Submarine Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Patrol_Submarine). Isar Aerospace would help establish sovereign Canadian access to space.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Later in May 2026, Isar Aerospace has signed a letter of intent with [Maritime Launch Services](53488184.md) to conduct rocket launches from their [Nova Scotia](21184.md) spaceport.[^cite_note-27] On 7 July 2026, Isar Aerospace signed a 10-year agreement with Maritime Launch Services including a launch pad and other facilities needed for launching Spectrum from Spaceport Nova Scotia.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

On 1 September 2026, the company signed its second launch service agreement with Astroscale, which will see the launch of their active debris removal mission [ADRAS-J2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astroscale#Active_Debris_Removal_by_Astroscale_\(ADRAS\)) onboard a Spectrum launch vehicle, with the launch targeted to happen between 2027 and 2028 from Andøya Spaceport.[^cite_note-30] On 15 September 2026, after the first successful flight of Spectrum, Isar Aerospace signed a multiple launch service agreement with SEOPS for additional five dedicated launches between 2028 and 2030.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

## Test launches

### Going Full Spectrum

On 30 March 2025, the inaugural launch of Isar Aerospace's [Spectrum rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_\(rocket\)) took place under the mission name "Going Full Spectrum". Launching from [Andøya Spaceport](30864935.md), the liftoff was successful; however, the rocket experienced an anomaly 30 seconds into flight and was terminated, causing it to fall back to the earth and explode upon impact.[^cite_note-NSF-032425-35][^cite_note-NSF-20250330-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] An investigation found that the rocket's vent valve had opened unexpectedly, leading to a loss of [attitude control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attitude_control) at the start of the roll maneuver.[^cite_note-39]

### Onward and Upward

The second test flight of Spectrum, dubbed “Onward and Upward,” was first slated to launch 25 March 2026 from Andøya Spaceport, but was interrupted by a fishing boat that was stuck within the safety zone.[^cite_note-40] The launch was postponed to no earlier than 9 April 2026,[^cite_note-Mäkinen2026-41] and was then delayed due to a leak in a [composite overwrapped pressure vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_overwrapped_pressure_vessel).[^cite_note-Space_Wall2026-42] The launch was again pushed back on 15 June 2026, due to detected 'off-nominal behaviour in the vehicle's fluid systems'.[^cite_note-43] From maritime safety notices sent out by the [Norwegian Coastal Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Coastal_Administration), it was noted that Isar Aerospace gained a launch window from 4 September to 10 September 2026, with a launch attempt made on 4 September 2026,[^cite_note-44] although it would once again be pushed back due to high winds speeds, up to 24 knots, grounding the Spectrum rocket.[^cite_note-45] The mission was intended as a qualification flight and was the first flight of Spectrum to carry payloads, including several [CubeSats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat) and an experiment selected under the [European Space Agency’s](10363.md) Boost! program.[^cite_note-46]

Isar Aerospace would successfully launch this second test flight of Spectrum into orbit on 5 September 2026,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-WallSpaceCom2026-48] making it the first successful orbital flight from [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe),[^cite_note-WallSpaceCom2026-48] and the first to be done so by a commercial company launching from Europe.[^cite_note-IsarOU2026-49] The mission carried five [CubeSats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSats) from universities and companies, together with one experiment: [TriSat-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriSat) ([University of Maribor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maribor) and SkyLabs); FramSat-1 ([NTNU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology) SpaceTeam); Sat1 ([TU Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Wien) Space Team); CyBEEsat ([TU Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Berlin)); Platform 6 ([EnduroSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EnduroSat)) and Let It Go (experiment, Dcubed).[^cite_note-50]
