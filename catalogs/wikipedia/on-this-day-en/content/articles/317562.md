**Rostov-on-Don**,[^cite_note-12] or simply **Rostov**, is a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) city and the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of [Rostov Oblast](478744.md) and the [Southern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_District) of Russia. It lies in the southeastern part of the [East European Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_European_Plain) on the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)), 32 kilometers (20 mi) from the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), directly north of the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus). The southwestern suburbs of the city lie above the Don [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta). Rostov-on-Don has a population of over one million people and is an important cultural, educational, economic and logistical centre of [Southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia).

## History

See also: [Timeline of Rostov-on-Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Rostov-on-Don)

### Early history

From ancient times, the area around the mouth of the Don River has held cultural and commercial importance. Ancient indigenous inhabitants included the [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) and [Sarmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatians) tribes. It was the site of [Tanais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanais), [an ancient Greek colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies_in_antiquity), [Fort Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazaria_\(Genoese_colonies\)) under the [Genoese](47332321.md), and [Fort Azak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov#Fortress_of_Azov) in the time of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).

In 1749, a custom house was established on the [Temernik River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temernik), a tributary of the Don, by edict of [the Empress Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Russia), the daughter of [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great),[^cite_note-gr-2] in order to control trade with [Turkey](11125639.md). It was co-located with a [fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_of_Saint_Dimitry_of_Rostov) named for [Dimitry of Rostov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitry_of_Rostov), a [metropolitan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of the old northern town of [Rostov the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov,_Yaroslavl_Oblast). [Azov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov), a town closer to the Sea of Azov on the Don, gradually lost its commercial importance in the region to the new fortress, but it remains an important historical center.[^cite_note-britannica-13]

In 1756, the "Russian commercial and trading company of Constantinople" was founded at the "merchants' settlement" (Kupecheskaya Sloboda) on the high bank of the Don. Towards the end of the eighteenth century, with the [incorporation of previously Ottoman Black Sea territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1768%E2%80%9374\)) into the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Russia_\(1721%E2%80%9396\)), the settlement lost much of its militarily strategic importance as a frontier post.[^cite_note-britannica-13]

In 1796, the settlement was chartered and in 1797, it became the seat of [Rostovsky Uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostovsky_Uyezd?action=edit\&redlink=1) within [Novorossiysk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Governorate).[^cite_note-gr-2] In 1806, it was officially renamed Rostov-on-Don.[^cite_note-gr-2] During the 19th century, due to its river connections with Russia's interior, Rostov developed into a major trade centre and communications hub. A [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) connection with [Kharkiv](2788558.md) was completed in 1870, with further links following in 1871 to [Voronezh](467218.md) and in 1875 to [Vladikavkaz](229915.md).

Concurrent with improvements in communications, heavy industry developed. [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) from the [Donets Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Basin) and [iron ore](145352.md) from [Krivoy Rog](624764.md) supported the establishment of an [iron foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_foundry) in 1846. In 1859, the production of [pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_pump) and [steam boilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiler_\(power_generation\)) began. Industrial growth was accompanied by a rapid increase in population, with 119,500 residents registered in Rostov by the end of the nineteenth century along with approximately 140 industrial businesses. The harbour was one of the largest trade hubs in southern Russia, especially for the export of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber), and [iron ore](145352.md).

In 1779, Rostov-on-Don became associated with a settlement of [Armenian](10918072.md) refugees from [Crimea](163045.md) at [Nakhichevan-on-Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhichevan-on-Don). The two settlements were separated by a field of wheat. In 1928, the two towns were merged. The former town border lies beneath the Teatralnaya Square of central Rostov-on-Don. By 1928, following the incorporation of the hitherto neighbouring city of Nakhichevan-on-Don, Rostov had become the third-largest city in Russia.

In the early 20th century, epidemics of [cholera](7591.md) during the summer months were not uncommon.

### 20th century

During [World War I](4764461.md), Rostov-on-Don was briefly [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk#Territorial_cessions_in_eastern_Europe) by the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1918.

During the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), the [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) and the [Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) contested Rostov-on-Don, then the most heavily industrialized city of South Russia. By 1928, the regional government had moved from the old [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) capital of [Novocherkassk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novocherkassk) to Rostov-on-Don.

In the Soviet years, the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) demolished two of Rostov-on-Don's principal landmarks: St. Alexander Nevsky Cathedral (1908) and St. George Cathedral (1783–1807).

During [World War II](32927.md), [Nazi German](21212.md) forces occupied Rostov-on-Don, at first from 19/20 November to 2 December 1941,[^cite_note-14] after attacks by the German [First Panzer Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Panzer_Army) in the [Battle of Rostov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rostov_\(1941\)), and then for seven months from 24 July 1942 to 14 February 1943. The town was of strategic importance as a railway junction and a river port accessing the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus), a region rich in oil and minerals. It took ten years to restore the city from the damage during World War II.

In 1942, up to 30,000 [Russian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Jews) were massacred by the German military in Rostov-on-Don at a site called [Zmievskaya Balka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zmievskaya_Balka).

### 21st century

On 19 March 2016, [Flydubai Flight 981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flydubai_Flight_981), a [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) operating from Dubai to Rostov-on-Don in Russia, crashed during a [go-around](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-around) in inclement weather at [Rostov-on-Don Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov-on-Don_Airport), killing all 62 people (55 passengers and seven crew) on board.

Rostov-on-Don hosted several matches of the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).

Rostov-on-Don is the location of the Russian [Southern Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Military_District), which includes the [58th Combined Arms Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/58th_Combined_Arms_Army). As such, it was a key logistical hub during the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and the [2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Ukrainian_counteroffensive).[^cite_note-15]

On 23 June 2023, amid the war, the [Wagner Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group), a private military company fighting on behalf of the Russian Federation, declared [a rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group_rebellion) against the [Russian Ministry of Defence](7875884.md) and took control of Rostov-on-Don.[^cite_note-16] On 24 June, after reaching a negotiated settlement with the Russian government and military, the Wagner Group withdrew from the city.[^cite_note-17]

## Geography

### Climate

[Albert Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Parry_\(academic\)), born in 1901 in Rostov-on-Don, wrote of the summers of his childhood:

There were sultry days of brassy sun, but also cool evenings on the balconies facing the Don River, with the soft glow of charcoal in the [samovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samovar), with the ripe cherries crushed by your spoon against the bottom and sides of your glass of scalding tea.

[^cite_note-21]

Rostov-on-Don lies in a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfa*).[^cite_note-22] The winter is moderately cold, with an average January temperature of −3.0 °C (26.6 °F). The lowest recorded temperature of −31.9 °C (−25.4 °F) occurred in January 1940.

Summers are warm and humid; July temperatures average +23.4 °C (74.1 °F). The city's highest recorded temperature of +40.2 °C (104.4 °F) was reported on 7 July 2020. The mean annual precipitation is 643 millimeters (25.3 in), the average wind speed is 2.7 m/s, and the average air humidity is 72%.[^cite_note-23]

## Symbols



[Coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of Rostov-on-Don

In December 1996, Rostov-on-Don adopted a coat of arms, a flag and a mayoral decoration as the symbols of the town. The first coat of arms of Rostov-on-Don was designed in 1811 and approved by the Tsar. In 1904, some changes were made. One lasting oil painting of the coat-of-arms is kept in the regional local history museum but its accuracy and authenticity are uncertain. In June 1996, the Rostov-on-Don [City Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Duma) adopted a variant of the coat-of-arms in which a tower represents the St. Dimitry Rostovsky Fortress. The ancient Russian arms reference the role Rostov played in the defense of Russia's borders. The coat-of-arms adorns the mayor's decoration but all other cases of its use are first considered for approval by the City Duma.

### Flag



Flag of Rostov-on-Don

The flag of Rostov-on-Don was approved by the Duma on September 20, 1864. At the end of the 19th and beginning of the 20th centuries the home guard regiments, which defended the Southern borders of Russia, were raised under this flag. The "Flag of Rostov" is kept in the town's municipal building under glass. Its length is 1,370 millimeters (54 in) and width, 850 millimeters (33 in). The flag is taken out of the building only on [Victory Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_\(May_9\)) and Rostov-on-Don Day by a guard of honour.

In 1870, an oval-shaped mayoral decoration wrought from precious or semi-precious white metal was introduced. On the front is written "Rostov-on-Don" at the top, the Rostov-on-Don coat-of-arms is in the center and the inscription, "Mayor of the City" is written at the bottom. On its reverse side, the day of its adoption, April 9, 1996, is recorded. The decoration is worn over the suit on a large chain. The mayor returns the decoration to the Duma on his or her retirement from office.

The Emblem of the [Don Host Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cossacks) was introduced in July 5 (18), 1878. The flag of the All Great Don Army was introduced in May 1918 on the "Circle of the Don Saving".

## Economy

### Overview



View of Rostov-on-Don, May 2007

Rostov's favourable geographical position at trading crossroads promotes economic development. The Don River is a major shipping lane connecting southwestern Russia with the north. Rostov-on-Don is a trading port for Russian, Italian, Greek and Turkish merchants selling, for example, wool, wheat and oil. It is also an important river port for passengers.&#x20;

### Volga–Don Canal

Further information: [Volga–Don Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal) and [Port of Rostov-on-Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rostov-on-Don)

With the construction of the [Volga-Don Shipping Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal) in 1952, Rostov-on-Don has become known as a "port of five seas" (reachable from the [Black Sea](3386.md), the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md), the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea), and the [Baltic Sea](3335.md)).

### Modern industry

In modern times, Rostov-on-Don has experienced economic growth. Numerous start-up companies have established headquarters in the city, the median income in 2013 was increasing, and the city is being transformed into a modern, industrial and technology-rich hub. For instance, Rostov-on-Don is a center for helicopter and farm machinery manufacturing.[^cite_note-27] The "Tebodin" engineering company opened its fourth office in Rostov-on-Don in June 2010.[^cite_note-28]

### Transportation



[North Caucasus Railway Administration Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus_Railway_Administration_Building)



[Platov International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platov_International_Airport)

Public transport in Rostov-on-Don includes buses, trolleybuses, trams, and *[marshrutkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshrutka)* (routed minibus, usually a 17-passenger Mercedes Sprinter). The [Rostov Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov_Metro) was planned in the early 1990s and later in the 2000s and 2010s. At the end of 2021, the Government of the Rostov Region and the Sinara company signed an agreement on the creation of a high-speed tramway in Rostov-on-Don on a concession basis. This happened at the international forum Transport Week 2021 in Moscow. By signing this agreement, the regional government put an end to the idea of developing the metro in the city in favour of the tram.

[Platov International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platov_International_Airport) (IATA code ROV) caters for domestic travel, as well as flights to and from the former C.I.S., Europe, Africa and Asia. It was opened in late 2017 as part of preparations for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md),[^cite_note-29] replacing [Rostov-on-Don Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov-on-Don_Airport). The Bataysk military aerodrome (which is located 5.0 miles (8.0 km) northwest of the city center) may be developed into a new airport hub for Southern Russia.

The international river port[^cite_note-30] specializes in the packaging and freighting of minerals and timber. Shipping information is published online.[^cite_note-31]

The main railway stations in Rostov-on-Don are "[Rostov-Glavny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov-Glavny)" and "Rostov-Prigorodny". The "St. Petersburg-Rostov-Caucasus" railway crosses the territory of Rostov-on-Don.[^cite_note-32] The [North Caucasus Railway Administration Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus_Railway_Administration_Building) is in Rostov-on-Don.

Several highways of federal and regional significance cross Rostov. The M-4 "Don" route passes Rostov to the east and [crosses the Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksay_Bridge) in the Aksay city area. The "Rostov-Novoshakhtinsk" starts from the Northern housing block area of the city running north to connect with the M-4 "Don" route between Shakhty and Novoshakhtinsk.

### Communications

In 1929, the first automatic telephone exchange in Russia with a capacity of 6,000 numbers commenced in Rostov-on-Don.[^cite_note-33] Since 2004, standard telephone numbers in Rostov-on-Don have been seven digits in length. Since 2009, city numbers have begun with "2".[^cite_note-34] The city dialing code is "863".

### Financial services

The first commercial bank in the South of Russia, [Rostovsoсbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostovsocbank), was opened in Rostov-on-Don. The bank existed from 1989 to 1998,[^cite_note-35] and before the withdrawal of the banking license it made a full return of deposits to all depositors.[^cite_note-36] The largest bank in the Rostov region is [Center-Invest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center-Invest?action=edit\&redlink=1). In total, there are about 50 banks and their branches,[^cite_note-37] 17% local banks, 80% representative offices of federal banks, and 4% representative offices of foreign banks.

### 2018 FIFA World Cup



Uruguay–Saudi Arabia match on 20 June 2018

In 2018, Rostov-on-Don was one of the Russian cities to host the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md). [Rostov Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov_Arena) with a capacity of 45,000 spectators was built on the left bank of the Don River, left of the exit from the city via the Voroshilovsky Bridge. The stadium hosted 5 games of the FIFA World Cup.

- June 17, 21\:00, Brazil — Switzerland, Group E

- June 20, 18\:00, Uruguay — Saudi Arabia, Group A

- June 23, 18\:00, South Korea — Mexico, Group F

- June 26, 21\:00, Iceland — Croatia, Group D

- July 2, 21\:00, Belgium — Japan, Round of 16

During the FIFA World Cup, Teatralnaya Square served as a venue for the FIFA Fan Fest. The specially arranged area had a capacity of 25,000 people. Fans were able to watch all World Cup games on a big screen. The venue was serviced by food outlets and had several entertainment areas. In preparation for the FIFA World Cup, the city implemented a large-scale development program. Apart from the new stadium, the city built a camping area for fans arriving for the World Cup, the Southern and Western Bypasses, and new hotels. Reconstruction works were carried out at the bridge crossing over the Don River (expanding the traffic way to 6 lanes), a number of healthcare facilities, and the embankment area. A new airport, Yuzhny, was built.
