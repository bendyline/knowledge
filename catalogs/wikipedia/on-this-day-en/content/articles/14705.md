The **Italian Armed Forces** ([Italian](14708.md): *Forze armate italiane*, pronounced [\[ˈfɔrtse arˈmaːte itaˈljaːne\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian)) encompass the [Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Army), the [Italian Navy](1126939.md) and the [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force). A fourth [branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the armed forces, known as the [Carabinieri](45173.md), take on the role as the nation's [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) and are also involved in missions and operations abroad as a combat force. Despite not being a branch of the armed forces, the [Guardia di Finanza](44013914.md) is organized along military lines.[^cite_note-6]

These five forces comprise a total of 340,885 men and women with the official status of active military personnel, of which 167,057 are in the Army, Navy and Air Force.[^cite_note-Budget2014-1][^cite_note-IT-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The [president of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy) heads the armed forces as the president of the [High Council of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_of_Defence_\(Italy\)) established by article 87 of the [Constitution of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy). According to article 78, the [Parliament](1417859.md) has the authority to declare a state of war and vest the powers to lead the war in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_government).

The [military history of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Italy) chronicles a vast time period, lasting from the military conflicts fought by the [ancient peoples of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_peoples_of_Italy), most notably the conquest of the Mediterranean world by the [ancient Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans), through the expansion of the Italian [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_city-states) and [maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics) during the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_period) and the involvement of the [historical Italian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historical_states_of_Italy) in the [Italian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars) and the [wars of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_succession), to the [Napoleonic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_period), the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification), the campaigns of the [colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Empire), the two [world wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_wars), and into the modern day, with world [peacekeeping](84617.md) operations under the aegis of [NATO](21133.md), the [EU](9317.md) or the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN). The [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula) has been a centre of military conflict throughout [European history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Europe) due to its geostrategic position: because of this, [Italy](14532.md) has a long military tradition.

The [Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento) movement emerged to unite Italy in the 19th century. The [Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Piedmont-Sardinia) took the lead in a series of wars to liberate Italy from foreign control. Following three [Wars of Italian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_Italian_Independence) against the Habsburg Austrians in the north, the [Expedition of the Thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_the_Thousand) against the Bourbons of the Two Sicilies in the south, and the [Capture of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rome), the unification of the country was completed in 1871 when Rome was declared capital of Italy.

The [presidential standard of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_standard_of_Italy) ([Italian](14708.md): *Stendardo presidenziale italiano*) is the distinctive standard of the presence of the [President of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy). The standard recalls the colors of the [flag of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy), with particular reference to the standard of the historic [Italian Republic of 1802–1805](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Republic_\(Napoleonic\)); the square shape and the [savoy blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_blue) border, whose use was maintained even in the Republican era, symbolize the four Italian Armed Forces, which are commanded by the President of Italy.[^cite_note-quirinale-14] Blue in [heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldry) also metaphorically symbolizes command.[^cite_note-heraldry-15]

## The four branches of Italian Armed Forces

### Italian Army

Main article: [Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Army)



[Lagunari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagunari) reconnaissance member in a security patrol



[8th Alpini Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Alpini_Regiment) snipers in winter ghillie suits

The [Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Army) ([Italian](14708.md): *Esercito Italiano*; abbreviated as **EI**) is the [land force](1376.md) branch of the Italian Armed Forces. The army's history dates back to the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification) in the 1850s and 1860s. The army fought in colonial engagements in China and [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War). It fought in [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) against the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire) during [World War I](4764461.md), Abyssinia before World War II and in World War II in Albania, Balkans, North Africa, the Soviet Union, and Italy itself. During the [Cold War](325329.md), the army prepared itself to defend against a [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) invasion from the east.&#x20;

Since the end of the Cold War, the army has seen extensive peacekeeping service and combat in [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md). Its best-known [combat vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_vehicle) are the [Dardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardo_IFV) [infantry fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle), the [Centauro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centauro_\(Tank_destroyer\)) [tank destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_destroyer) and the [Ariete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariete) [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank) and among its aircraft the [Mangusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusta_A129_Mangusta) [attack helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter), recently deployed in UN missions. The headquarters of the Army General Staff are located in [Rome](25458.md) opposite the [Quirinal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirinal_Palace), where the [president of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy) resides. The army is an all-volunteer force of [active-duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_duty) personnel.

The Italian Army originated as the [Royal Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Italian_Army) (*[Regio Esercito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regio_Esercito)*), which dates from the [proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy) following the seizure of the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States) and the unification of Italy (*[Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento)*). In 1861, under the leadership of [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi), [Victor Emmanuel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_II_of_Italy) of the [House of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy) was invited to take the throne and of the newly created kingdom.

The Italian Army has participated in operations to aid populations hit by natural disasters. It has, moreover, supplied a remarkable contribution to the forces of police for the control of the territory of the [province of Bolzano/Bozen](169611.md) (1967), in [Sardinia](29376.md) ("Forza Paris" 1992), in [Sicily](27619.md) ("Vespri Siciliani" 1992) and in [Calabria](44772.md) (1994). Currently, it protects sensitive objectives and places throughout the national territory ("Operazione Domino") since the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in the United States.&#x20;

The army is also engaged in Missions abroad under the aegis of the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN), the [NATO](21133.md), and of Multinational forces, such as [Beirut](37428.md) in [Lebanon](17771.md) (1982), [Namibia](21292.md) (1989), [Albania](738.md) (1991), [Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan) (1991), [Somalia](27358.md) (1992), [Mozambique](19301.md) (1993), [Bosnia](3463.md) (1995), [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor) and [Kosovo](17391.md) (both in 1999), the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) (2001), [Darfur](605837.md) (2003), [Afghanistan](737.md) (2002), [Iraq](7515928.md) (2003) and Lebanon again (2006).

### Italian Navy

Main article: [Italian Navy](1126939.md)



Naval ensign of Italy

The [Italian Navy](1126939.md) ([Italian](14708.md): *Marina Militare*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Military Navy'; abbreviated as **MM**) is one of the four branches of Italian Armed Forces and was formed in 1946 from what remained of the *[Regia Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regia_Marina)* (Royal Navy) after World War II. As of August 2014, the Italian Navy had a strength of 30,923 active personnel, with approximately 184 vessels in service, including minor auxiliary vessels. It is considered a multiregional and a [blue-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Coffey-21]

The navy of Italy was created in 1861, following the [proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy). The new navy's baptism of fire came during the [Third Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Italian_War_of_Independence) against the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire). During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), it spent its major efforts in the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md), fighting the [Austro-Hungarian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Navy). In the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), it engaged the [Royal Navy](26061.md) in a two-and-a-half-year struggle for the control of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). After the war, the new *Marina Militare*, being a member of the North Atlantic Treaty Organisation (NATO), has taken part in many coalition peacekeeping operations. It is a [blue-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy). The *[Guardia Costiera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardia_Costiera)* (Coast Guard) is a component of the navy.

Today's Italian Navy is a modern navy with ships of every type. The fleet is in continuous evolution, and as of today oceangoing fleet units include: 2 light [aircraft carriers](2219.md), 4 [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship), 3 [destroyers](8779.md), 11 [frigates](11464.md) and 8 [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine). Patrol and littoral warfare units include: 10 [offshore patrol vessels](536773.md), 10 [mine countermeasure vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_countermeasure_vessel), 4 coastal [patrol boats](536773.md), and a varied fleet of [auxiliary ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_ship) are also in service.[^cite_note-22] The flagship of the fleet is the [carrier *Cavour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_aircraft_carrier_Cavour).

The [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(flag\)) of the Italian Navy is the [flag of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy) bearing the coat of arms of the Italian Navy. The shield's quarters refer to the four Medieval Italian [Maritime Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Republics):

- 1st quarter: on red, a golden winged lion (the [lion of St. Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_of_St._Mark)) wielding a sword ([Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice))

- 2nd quarter: on white field, red cross, the [Saint George's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George's_Cross) ([Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa))

- 3rd quarter: on blue field, white [Maltese cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_cross) ([Republic of Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Amalfi))

- 4th quarter: on red field, white [Pisan cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisan_cross) ([Republic of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa))

The coat of arms is surmounted by a golden crown, which distinguishes military vessels from those of the merchant navy.

### Italian Air Force

Main article: [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force)



Three Italian [F-35s](11812.md) wait in formation as the rest of their team receives fuel from a MAINEiac KC-135 Stratotanker above the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle), May 29, 2023

The [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force) ([Italian](14708.md): *Aeronautica Militare*; **AM**, lit. 'military aeronautics') is the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) of the [Italian Republic](14532.md). Since its formation, the service has held a prominent role in modern [Italian military history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Italy). The acrobatic display team is the *[Frecce Tricolori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frecce_Tricolori)*. Italy was among the earliest adopters of military aviation. Its air arm dates back to 1884, when the [Italian Royal Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Royal_Army) (*Regio Esercito*) was authorised to acquire its own air component. The Air Service (*[Corpo Aeronautico Militare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpo_Aeronautico_Militare)*) operated balloons based near Rome.

In 1911, reconnaissance and bombing sorties during the [Italo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War) by the *Servizio Aeronautico* represented the first use of heavier-than-air aircraft in armed conflict. On 28 March 1923, the Italian Air Force was founded as an independent service by [King Vittorio Emanuele III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_III_of_Italy) of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy).

During the 1930s, it was involved in its first military operations in Ethiopia in 1935, and later in the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) between 1936 and 1939. Eventually, Italy entered [World War II](32927.md) alongside Germany. After the armistice of 8 September 1943, Italy was divided into two sides, and the same fate befell the *Regia Aeronautica*. The Air Force was split into the *[Italian Co-Belligerent Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Co-Belligerent_Air_Force)* in the south aligned with the Allies, and the pro-Axis *[Aeronautica Nazionale Repubblicana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautica_Nazionale_Repubblicana)* in the north until the end of the war. When Italy was made a republic by referendum, the air force was given its current name *Aeronautica Militare*. The [cockade of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockade_of_Italy) is one of the symbols of the Italian Air Force, and is widely used on all Italian state [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), not only military.[^cite_note-23]

Armed conflicts in [Somalia](27358.md), [Mozambique](19301.md) and the nearby [Balkans](4829.md) led to the Italian Air Force becoming a participant in multinational air forces, [such as that of NATO](322473.md) over the former [Yugoslavia](34244.md), just a few minutes flying time east of the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula). The commanders of the Italian Air Force soon saw the need to improve the Italian air defences.

### Carabinieri

Main article: [Carabinieri](45173.md)



[Carabinieri](45173.md) officers armed with a [Beretta PM-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beretta_M12) [submachine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submachine_gun) in 2010



[Carabinieri](45173.md) of the Nucleo Artificieri Antisabotaggio in 2018

The Carabinieri ([/ˌkærəbɪnˈjɛəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), also [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˌkɑːr-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English),[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Italian: [\[karabiˈnjɛːri\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian); formally *Arma dei Carabinieri*, "Arm of Carabineers"; previously *Corpo dei Carabinieri Reali*, "Royal Carabineers Corps")[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] are the national [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) of [Italy](14532.md) who primarily carry out domestic and foreign policing duties. It is one of Italy's main [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) agencies, alongside the [Polizia di Stato](1313023.md) and the [Guardia di Finanza](44013914.md). As with the Guardia di Finanza but in contrast to the Polizia di Stato, the Carabinieri are a military force. As the fourth [branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the Italian Armed Forces, they come under the authority of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Italy\)); for activities related to inland public order and security, they functionally depend on the [Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Italy\)). In practice, there is a significant overlap between the jurisdiction of the Polizia di Stato and Carabinieri, and both of them are contactable through [112](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/112_\(emergency_telephone_number\)), the European Union's Single Emergency number.[^cite_note-30] Unlike the Polizia di Stato, the Carabinieri have responsibility for [policing the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_\(military_police\)), and a number of members regularly participate in military missions abroad.

The corps was instituted in 1814 by King [Victor Emmanuel I of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_I_of_Savoy) with the aim of providing the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_\(1720%E2%80%931861\)), the forerunner of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), with a police corps. It is therefore older than Italy itself. During the process of [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification), the Carabinieri were appointed as the "First Force" of the new national military organization. Although the Carabinieri assisted in the suppression of opposition during the rule of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), they were also responsible for his downfall and many units were disbanded during [World War II](32927.md) by [Nazi Germany](21212.md), which resulted in large numbers of Carabinieri joining the [Italian resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement).

In 2000, they were separated from the Army to become a separate branch of the Italian Armed Forces. Carabinieri have policing powers that can be exercised at any time and in any part of the country, and they are always permitted to carry their assigned weapon as personal equipment ([Beretta 92FS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beretta_92) pistols). The Carabinieri are often referred to as "La Benemerita" (The Reputable or The Meritorious) as they are a trusted and prestigious law enforcement institution in Italy. The first official account of the use of this term to refer to the Carabinieri dates back to 24 June 1864.[^cite_note-31]

The new force was divided into divisions on the scale of one division for each [province of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Italy). The divisions were further divided into companies and subdivided into lieutenancies, which commanded and coordinated the local [police stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_station) and were distributed throughout the national territory in direct contact with the public. They carry out peacekeeping mission abroad, such as [Kosovo](17391.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), and [Iraq](7515928.md). At the [Sea Islands Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Island,_Georgia) of the [G8](59755.md) in 2004, the Carabinieri were given the mandate to establish a [Center of Excellence for Stability Police Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_Excellence_for_Stability_Police_Units) ([CoESPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoESPU)) to spearhead the development of training and doctrinal standards for civilian police units attached to international peacekeeping missions.[^cite_note-32]

## Tomb of the Unknown Soldier

Main article: [Tomb of the Unknown Soldier (Italy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier_\(Italy\))



The [President of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy) [Sergio Mattarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Mattarella), between the [Corazzieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazzieri) and the [guard of honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_of_honour), pays tribute to the [Italian Unknown Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier_\(Italy\)) at [Altare della Patria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altare_della_Patria) in Rome during the [National Unity and Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_and_Armed_Forces_Day) on 4 November 2016



The *[Frecce Tricolori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frecce_Tricolori)*, with the smoke trail representing the [national colours of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_colours_of_Italy), above the [Altare della Patria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altare_della_Patria) in Rome during the celebrations of the *[Festa della Repubblica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festa_della_Repubblica)* on 2 June 2022

The [Tomb of the Italian Unknown Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier_\(Italy\)) ([Italian](14708.md): *Tomba del Milite Ignoto*) is a [war memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_memorial) located in [Rome](25458.md) under the statue of the [goddess Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_\(mythology\)) at the [Altare della Patria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_II_Monument#Altar_of_the_Fatherland). It is a *[sacellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacellum)* dedicated to the Italian soldiers killed and missing during [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wars_involving_Italy).

It is the scene of official ceremonies that take place annually on the occasion of the [Italian Liberation Day](39216075.md) (April 25), the [Italian Republic Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festa_della_Repubblica) (June 2) and the [National Unity and Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_and_Armed_Forces_Day) (November 4), during which the [President of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Italian_Republic) and the [highest offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_order_of_precedence) of the State pay homage to the shrine of the Unknown Soldier with the deposition of a [laurel wreath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_wreath) in memory of the fallen and missing [Italians](385155.md) in the wars.

The reason for his strong [symbolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol) lies in the [metaphorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphor) transition from the figure of the soldier to that of the [people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People) and finally to that of the [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation): this transition between increasingly broader and generic concepts is due to the indistinct traits of the non-identification of the soldier.[^cite_note-33]

His tomb is a shrine that represents all the fallen and missing in the war.[^cite_note-34] The side of the tomb of the Unknown Soldier that gives outward at the Altare della Patria. It is always guarded by a guard of honour and two flames that burn perpetually in braziers.[^cite_note-civitavecchia-35]

The allegorical meaning of the perpetually burning flames is linked to their symbolism, which is centuries old, since it has its origins in [classical antiquity](252905.md), especially in the [cult of the dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral). A fire that burns eternally symbolizes the memory, in this case of the sacrifice of the Unknown Soldier moved by patriotic love, and his everlasting memory of the Italians, even in those who are far from their country: not by chance on the two perennial braziers next to the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier a plaque is placed whose text reads "Italians Abroad to the Motherland" in memory of donations made by [Italian emigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_diaspora) between the end of the 19th century and the beginning of the 20th century for the construction of the Vittoriano.[^cite_note-fiamma-36]

## International stance



Italian soldiers of the [Mechanized Brigade "Sassari"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanized_Brigade_%22Sassari%22) in Afghanistan in 2012

Italy has joined in many [UN](31769.md), [NATO](21133.md) and [EU](9317.md) operations as well as with assistance to [Russia](25391.md) and the other [CIS](36870.md) nations, [Middle East](19323.md) peace process, [peacekeeping](84617.md), and combating the [illegal drug trade](218449.md), [human trafficking](19333662.md), piracy and terrorism.

Italy did take part in the 1982 [Multinational Force in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Force_in_Lebanon) along with US, French and British troops. Italy also participated in the 1990–91 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), with the deployment of eight Panavia Tornado IDS bomber jets; Italian Army troops were subsequently deployed to assist [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) refugees in northern [Iraq](7515928.md) following the conflict.

As part of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom), Italy contributed to the international operation in [Afghanistan](737.md). Italian forces have contributed to [ISAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force), the NATO force in [Afghanistan](737.md), and to the [Provincial reconstruction team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_reconstruction_team). Italy has sent 3,800 troops, including one infantry company from the [2nd Alpini Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Alpini_Regiment) tasked to protect the ISAF HQ, one engineer company, one NBC platoon, one logistic unit, as well as liaison and staff elements integrated into the operation [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_hierarchy). Italian forces also command a multinational engineer task force and have deployed a platoon of Carabinieri [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police).

The Italian Army did not take part in combat operations of the 2003 [Iraq War](5043324.md), dispatching troops only when major combat operations were declared over by the [U.S. President](24113.md) [George W. Bush](3414021.md). Subsequently, Italian troops arrived in the late summer of 2003, and began patrolling [Nasiriyah](33572510.md) and the surrounding area. Italian participation in the military operations in Iraq was concluded by the end of 2006, with full withdrawal of Italian military personnel except for a small group of about 30 soldiers engaged in providing security for the Italian embassy in [Baghdad](4492.md). Italy played a major role in the 2004–2011 [NATO Training Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Training_Mission_%E2%80%93_Iraq) to assist in the development of Iraqi security forces training structures and institutions.
