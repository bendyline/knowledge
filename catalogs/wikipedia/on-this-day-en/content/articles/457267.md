**Nuristan** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto);[^cite_note-3] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari):[^cite_note-4] نورستان, lit. 'Land of [Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C5%ABr_\(Islam\))'), also spelled as **Nurestan**, **Nooristan** or **Nuriston** ([Katë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat%C3%AB_language):[^cite_note-5] نورستان), and historically known as **Kafiristan** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto),[^cite_note-6] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari):[^cite_note-7] کافرستان lit. 'Land of [Infidels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafir)') until 1896, is one of the 34 [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md), located in the eastern part of the country. It is divided into seven [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Afghanistan) and is Afghanistan's least populous province, with a population of around 167,000.[^cite_note-nsia-2] [Parun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parun) serves as the provincial capital. Nuristan is bordered on the south by [Laghman](457257.md) and [Kunar](2159600.md) provinces, on the north by [Badakhshan province](1282198.md), on the west by [Panjshir province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjshir_Province), and on the east by [Pakistan](23235.md).

The origin of the local [Nuristani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristanis) has been disputed, ranging from being the indigenous inhabitants forced to flee to this region after refusing to surrender to invaders, to being linked to various ancient groups of people and the [Turk Shahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turk_Shahi) kings.[^cite_note-Ludwig-8][^cite_note-Genesis-9] Some Nuristanis claim being descendants of the Greek occupying forces of Alexander the Great. It was formerly called [Kafiristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafiristan) ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): كافرستان) ("Land of the Infidels") until the inhabitants were [forcibly converted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_conversion#Islam) from an [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) religion[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsary2014-10][^cite_note-nuristan.info-11] with elements from Indo-Iranian ([Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Vedic_religion)- or [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism)-like) religion infused with local variations,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan2014205-12][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarringtonKendrickSchlagintweit2006111-13][^cite_note-pre_Islamic_religion-14] to [Islam](6037917.md) in 1895, and thence the region has become known as Nuristan ("Land of Light").[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlimberg2004-15] The region was located in an area surrounded by Buddhist states which later declined.[^cite_note-academia-16]

The primary occupations are agriculture, animal husbandry, and [day labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_labor). Located on the southern slopes of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountains in the northeastern part of the country, Nuristan spans the basins of the [Alingar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alingar_River), [Pech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_River), [Landai Sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landai_Sin_River), and [Kunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River) rivers. Most of Nuristan is covered by mountainous [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) and it has a rich biodiversity with a domestically unique [monsoon](57630.md) climate by air coming from the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-17] As of 2020, the entirety of Nuristan is now a protected [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

### Early history

There have been varying theories about the origins of Nuristanis. [George Scott Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Scott_Robertson) considered them to be part of the old Indian population of Eastern Afghanistan and stated they fled to the mountains after the Muslim invasion in the 10th century. He added they probably found other races there whom they killed off and enslaved or amalgamated with them.[^cite_note-Ludwig-8] Oral traditions of some of the Nuristanis place their original abode to be at the confluence of [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) and [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River) a millennium ago. These traditions state they were driven off from [Kandahar](17260.md) to Kabul to [Kapisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Kapisa) to [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_District) with the Muslim invasion. They identify themselves as late arrivals in Nuristan, being driven by [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) who after establishing his empire forced the unsubmissive population to flee.[^cite_note-nuristan.info-11]

The surrounding area fell to [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 330 BC. Later it was conquered by [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya). The [Mauryas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) introduced [Buddhism](3267529.md) to the region, and were attempting to expand their empire to Central Asia until they faced local Greco-Bactrian forces. [Seleucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucus_I_Nicator) is said to have reached a [peace treaty](404505.md) with Chandragupta by giving control of the territory south of the Hindu Kush to the Mauryas upon intermarriage and 500 elephants.[^cite_note-ask-20]

Before their conversion to Islam, the Nuristanis practiced an [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) religion[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsary2014-10][^cite_note-nuristan.info-11] with elements from [Indo-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Iranian_religion) ([Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Vedic_religion)- or Hindu-like) religion infused with locally developed accretions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan2014205-12][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarringtonKendrickSchlagintweit2006111-13][^cite_note-nuristan.info-11][^cite_note-pre_Islamic_religion-14] The high god of the pre-Islamic Nuristani religion was the god *[Imra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imra)*, derived from the Hindu god [Yama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yama_\(Hinduism\)), and was also called *[Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_\(demon\))*.[^cite_note-21] Another god was Indr, derived from [Indra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra). He was seen as the brother of the god Gisht and father of Pano and the goddess Dishani.[^cite_note-22] There were also many other minor gods worshiped in the region.[^cite_note-23]

The area extending from modern Nuristan to [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) was part of a wider cultural area called "Kafiristan" in contemporary Islamic sources, containing a host of "Kafir" cultures and Indo-European languages that became Islamized over a long period. In the scientific literature, the term "Peristan" has been applied to this region. Earlier, it was surrounded by Buddhist areas. The Islamization of the nearby [Badakhshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badakhshan) began in the 8th century and Peristan was surrounded by Muslim states in the 16th century with the Islamization of [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan). The Buddhist states temporarily brought literacy and state rule into the region. The decline of Buddhism resulted in it becoming heavily isolated.[^cite_note-academia-16]

Nuristanis were called "[kafirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafir)" due to their enduring paganism while other regions around them became Muslim. However, the influence from district names in Kafiristan of Katwar or Kator and the ethnic name [Kati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kata_people) has also been suggested.[^cite_note-24] The name Kator was used by Lagaturman, last king of the Turk Shahi. Apparently due to its usage by the last Turk-Shahi ruler, it was adopted as a title by the ruler of the north-west region of the Indian subcontinent, comprising Chitral and Kafiristan. The title "Shah Kator" was assumed by [Chitral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral_\(princely_state\))'s ruler Mohtaram Shah who assumed it upon being impressed by the majesty of the erstwhile pagan rulers of Chitral.[^cite_note-Genesis-9] The theory of Kators being related to Turki Shahis is based on the information of *[Jami- ut-Tawarikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jami'_al-tawarikh)* and *[Tarikh-i-Binakiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarikh-i_guzida)*.[^cite_note-25] The region was also named after its ruling elite. The royal usage may be the origin behind the name of Kator.[^cite_note-26]

The [region was invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_conquest_of_Kafiristan) by forces of Afghan Amir [Abdur Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Khan) in 1896 and most of the people were converted either by force or did so to avoid the *[jizya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizya)*:[^cite_note-Nile-27] The region was renamed Nuristan, meaning *Land of the enlightened*, a reflection of the "enlightening" of the [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism) Nuristani by the "light-giving" of Islam.

In the 19th century, the [Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Afghanistan) incorporated Nuristan into its territory via military conquest; this occurred around the same time as the beginning of [European influence in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_influence_in_Afghanistan). During this period, one of the most well known Afghan generals from this period, Abdul Wakil Khan, was born in Nuristan. He fought against the insurgent forces of [Habibullāh Kalakāni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habibull%C4%81h_Kalak%C4%81ni) and was buried on the same plateau where Afghan king [Amanullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanullah_Khan) is buried.

### Recent history

Further information: [War in Afghanistan (2001–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\))



A U.S. soldier moving along a path overlooking the mountainside village of Aranas while on patrol in 2006



Members of the [Afghan National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Army) (ANA) during a U.S.-led patrol in Wadawu valley during Operation Silver Creek in August 2009

Since the creation of Pakistan in 1947, Afghan politicians (particularly [Mohammed Daoud Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Daoud_Khan)) have been focused on invading [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) and the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas) of Pakistan. This has led to terrorism on both sides of the border as the scene of some of the heaviest [guerrilla](12720.md) fighting during the 1980s [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War). The province was influenced by [Mawlawi Afzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawlawi_Afzal)'s [Islamic Revolutionary State of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Revolutionary_State_of_Afghanistan), which was supported by Pakistan nationalists and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). It dissolved under the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)) (Taliban rule) in the late 1990s.[^cite_note-28]

Nuristan is one of the poorest and most remote provinces of Afghanistan. Prior to the takeover of the Taliban in 2021, few [NGOs](46539.md) operated in Nuristan because of the [insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) and a lack of safe roads. Some road construction projects were launched linking Nangarej to [Mandol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandol_District) and [Chapa Dara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapa_Dara_District) to Titan Dara.[^cite_note-29] The Afghan government also worked on a direct road route to [Laghman province](457257.md), in order to reduce dependence on the road through restive [Kunar province](2159600.md) to the rest of Afghanistan. Other road projects were started aimed at improving the primitive road from [Kamdesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamdesh,_Nuristan) to [Barg-i Matal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barg-i_Matal_District), and from [Nangalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangalam) in Kunar province to the provincial center at Parun.

Since Nuristan is a highly ethnically homogeneous province, there are few incidents of inter-ethnic violence. However, there are instances of disputes among inhabitants, some of which continue for decades. Nuristan has suffered from its inaccessibility and lack of infrastructure. The government presence is under-developed, even compared to neighboring provinces. Nuristan's formal educational sector is weak, with few professional teachers. Due to its proximity to Pakistan, many of the inhabitants are actively involved in trade and commerce across the border.

A map from the [Afghan Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Afghanistan\)) produced in 2009 showed the western region of Nuristan to be under "enemy control". There have been numerous conflicts between militants and U.S.-led Afghan security forces. In April 2008 members of the [3rd Special Forces Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Special_Forces_Group_\(United_States\)) led Afghan soldiers from the [Commando Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandos_\(Afghanistan\)) into the Shok valley in [an unsuccessful attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shok_Valley) to capture warlord [Gulbuddin Hekmatyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbuddin_Hekmatyar). In July 2008, approximately 200 [Taliban guerrillas attacked a NATO position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wanat) just south of Nuristan, near the village of Wanat in the [Waygal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waygal_District), killing 9 U.S. soldiers.[^cite_note-jazeera14-30]

In the following year, in early October, more than 350 insurgents backed by members of the [Hezb-e Islami Gulbuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezb-e_Islami_Gulbuddin) and other militia groups fought U.S.-led Afghan security forces in the [Battle of Kamdesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kamdesh) at Camp Keating in Nuristan. The base was nearly overrun; more than 100 Taliban fighters, eight U.S. soldiers, and seven members of the Afghan security forces were killed during the fighting.[^cite_note-longwarjournal.org-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-washpostkamdesh-33][^cite_note-bbc-nuristan-battle-4oct2009-34] Four days after the battle, in early October 2009, U.S. forces withdrew from their four main bases in Nuristan, as part of a plan by General [Stanley McChrystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_McChrystal) to pull troops out of small outposts and relocate them closer to major towns.[^cite_note-35] The U.S. has pulled out from some areas in the past, but never from all four main bases.[^cite_note-36] A month after the U.S. pullout the Taliban was governing openly in Nuristan.[^cite_note-longwarjournal.org-31] According to *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*, Nuristan is "a place so tough that NATO abandoned it in 2012 after failing to subdue it."[^cite_note-37]

In 2021, the [Taliban](30635.md) gained control of the province during the [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive).

### Population

As of 2021, the total population of the province is about 166,676.[^cite_note-nsia-2]

### Ethnicity, languages and religion

Nuristan is one of the most ethnically homogeneous provinces in Afghanistan, with [Nuristanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristanis) comprising the overwhelming majority of the population, consistently estimated at over 90 percent across multiple sources.[^cite_note-two-48][^cite_note-three-49][^cite_note-five-50][^cite_note-six-51] The Nuristanis are not a single ethnic group but rather a collective of distinct sub-groups, the principal ones include the [Kata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kata_people), [Kalasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristani_Kalasha_language) (or Waigali), [Ashkuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkun_language) (or Wamayi), [Kom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kom_people_\(Afghanistan\)) and [Prasun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prasun_language) tribes, of which the Kata is the largest.[^cite_note-zero-52][^cite_note-three-49][^cite_note-five-50][^cite_note-seven-53] Small communities of [Gujars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjar), a semi-nomadic pastoralist group, as well as [Tajiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks) and [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) are also present, though each constitutes less than one percent of the permanent population.[^cite_note-four-54][^cite_note-six-51][^cite_note-eight-55]

The classification of the [Pashayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashayi_people) is a matter of some inconsistency. Several sources treat Pashayi as one of the constituent sub-groups collectively termed "Nuristani",[^cite_note-three-49][^cite_note-five-50] while others identify them as a linguistically and ethnically distinct population.[^cite_note-seven-53][^cite_note-eight-55] This ambiguity directly affects reported ethnic and linguistic figures, since when Pashayi is counted as a Nuristani sub-group, Nuristanis account for 99 percent of the population, but when treated separately, [Pashayi speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashayi_languages) represent approximately 15 percent of the province's linguistic composition, making Pashayi the second most widely spoken language after the proper [Nuristani languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristani_languages) (spoken by roughly 78 percent of the population and in 84 percent of villages).[^cite_note-two-48][^cite_note-three-49][^cite_note-five-50]

In terms of religion, Nuristan's population is almost entirely [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-four-54][^cite_note-six-51] Historically known as *[Kafiristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafiristan)* ("land of the infidels") due to the pre-Islamic animist and Hindu-influenced practices of its inhabitants, the region was forcibly converted to Islam by Emir [Abdur Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Khan) beginning in 1895.[^cite_note-one-56][^cite_note-four-54] Despite the relative recency of this conversion, the Nuristani society has developed a notably devout Islamic identity,[^cite_note-one-56] though some sources mention residual animist traditions in remote communities.[^cite_note-four-54]

### Education

Further information: [Education in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Afghanistan)

In 2002 the first gender assessment of women's conditions in Nuristan was completed.[^cite_note-65] The overall literacy rate (6+ years of age) fell from 17.7% in 2005 to 17% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-66] The overall net enrolment rate (6–13 years of age) increased from 8.7% in 2005 to 45% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-66]

### Health

Further information: [Health in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan)

The percentage of households with clean drinking water increased from 2% in 2005 to 12% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-66] The percentage of births attended by a skilled birth attendant increased from 1% in 2005 to 22% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-66]
