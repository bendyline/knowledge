**Paktika** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto)[^cite_note-4], [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari)[^cite_note-5]: پکتیکا) is one of the 34 [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md), located in the eastern part of the country. Forming part of the larger [Loya Paktia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loya_Paktia) region, Paktika has a population of about 789,000 residents,[^cite_note-nsia-3] who are mostly ethnic [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) but smaller communities of [Tajiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks) and others may also be found in the province. The town of [Sharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharana,_Afghanistan) serves as the provincial capital, while the most populous city is [Urgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urgun).

In 2021, the [Taliban](30635.md) gained control of the province during the [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive).

Paktika is the southernmost part of a historical region known as Greater Paktia (Pashto: لویه پکتیا, Loya Paktia), that was once a unified province including [Paktia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paktia), [Khost](457249.md) and parts of [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni_Province) and [Logar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province). The tribes that reside in this area were mentioned by the Greek historian [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus), who called them the "Pactyans" as early as the 1st millennium BCE.

### 20th century

The modern province of Paktika has its origins in the administrative reorganisation of 30 April 1964, when Afghanistan was divided into 29 provinces, replacing the previous system of fewer but larger units. At that time, the government announced plans to create a new *province of Urgun and Katawaz*, to be carved out of the former *Governorate of Urgun* ([Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): حکومت ارگون, romanized: *Ḥukūmat-i Urgūn*) within the [High Governorate of the South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Province,_Afghanistan). The new province was not immediately constituted, however, as the government stated it would be established only after "some constructional and developmental projects" were completed. In the meantime, the territory remained subordinate to [Paktia](457274.md).[^cite_note-kabultimes-6] The proposal was still being discussed in 1966, when a geographer at the [U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Intelligence and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Intelligence_and_Research) noted that "it has been proposed that a new province of Katawaz and Urugan be formed from part of Paktia Province", likely tied to an ongoing development plan for the region.[^cite_note-7] The 1971 edition of the *[Times Atlas of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Atlas_of_the_World)* does show a province of Katawaz-Urgun, and in 1973, a map of the minor civil divisions of Afghanistan published by the [University of Nebraska at Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska_at_Omaha) shows a draft map of the *new province of Paktika*,[^cite_note-8] though later sources revert to showing Paktia in its original extent.

The province was never formally constituted. By the mid-1970s, the territory had instead been organised into the *Large District of Urgun* ([Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): لوی ولسوالۍ ارگون, romanized: *Lōy Wōleswālī-ye Urgūn*), subordinate to [Paktia](457274.md), and the *Large District of Katawaz* ([Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): لوی ولسوالۍ کتواز, romanized: *Lōy Wōleswālī-ye Katawāz*), subordinate to [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni_Province).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] This arrangement was confirmed still in place in 1978 by the official yearbook of the [Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Afghanistan_\(1973%E2%80%931978\)).[^cite_note-11] In the meantime, the provincial capital of the remote and underdeveloped region had been relocated from [Urgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urgun) to [Sharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharana), owing to the latter's more favourable position along the main highway connecting [Kabul](16826.md), Ghazni and [Kandahar](17260.md). It was only in 1979 that the modern province of Paktika was formally established by the [pro-Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan), merging the Large District of Urgun with the Large District of Katawaz into a single new province.[^cite_note-12]

Paktika was the site of many battles during the Soviet occupation of the country and the lawless years that followed. For example, the [Siege of Urgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Urgun) took place between 1983 and 1984.

### Recent history

As one of the most remote provinces in Afghanistan and an area that saw much devastation in previous years, Paktika suffers from a severe lack of critical infrastructure. Reconstruction in the province after the fall of the [Taliban](30635.md) has been slow compared to that in nearby provinces such as [Khost](400068.md) and [Zabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabul). This is primarily due to the remoteness of the region and repeated attacks on aid workers and [NATO](21133.md) forces.

In June 2004, members of the [Utah](31716.md) and [Iowa](26810748.md) [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_of_the_United_States) helped [Army Reserve](1187028.md) [Civil Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Affairs) Soldiers from [Oregon](26811621.md) establish a [Provincial Reconstruction Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Reconstruction_Team) base in [Sharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharana,_Afghanistan), the provincial capital, to lead the development effort. The first full contingent of eight Civil Affairs Soldiers from the U.S. Army Reserve's 450th Civil Affairs Battalion (Airborne), based in [Riverdale Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale_Park,_Maryland), [Maryland](18858.md), arrived in September 2004.

The [Shkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shkin,_Paktika) firebase was composed of special operations forces. In an article from *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))*, the U.S. base was described as:

"The U.S. firebase looks like a Wild West cavalry fort, ringed with coils of razor wire. A U.S. flag ripples above the 3-ft.-thick mud walls, and in the watchtower a guard scans the expanse of forested ridges, rising to 9,000 ft., that mark the border. When there's trouble, it usually comes from that direction."

[^cite_note-time.com-13]

While the province hasn't witnessed the outright fighting in the last few years that has affected provinces like [Helmand](457235.md), there is a constant low level of tribal violence, accompanied by criminal and Taliban activity. The last serious fighting in the province took place in 2004, amid reports that then-Governor [Muhammad Ali Jalali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jalali) was collaborating with Taliban forces, and that the Taliban had effectively annexed eastern portions of the province. Jalali and many of his allied officials, were replaced and U.S. Special Forces were dispatched to fight the Taliban while the Pakistani forces fought with the Taliban's allies in neighbouring [South Waziristan](4175082.md).

On 1 November 2004, a civil affairs convoy was ambushed near [Surobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surobi,_Paktika?action=edit\&redlink=1), between the Shkin firebase and Orgun-E. U.S. Army Spc. James Kearney, a turret gunner, died of a head shot from a sniper, which initiated the ambush. Two vehicles were destroyed in the engagement and three other Soldiers were wounded.[^cite_note-14] The Provincial Reconstruction Team base was named Camp Kearney on 21 November 2004 to honor the sacrifice of Spc. James Kearney.[^cite_note-15]



A member of the US military inside a military base in 2012

On Jun 18, 2008 in the [Ziruk District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziruk_District) Governor's compound, two members of the Provincial Reconstruction Team, HMN Mark Retmier and CM1 Ross Toles, were killed due to rocket attacks. The mess hall on Forward Operating Base Sharana was named after CM1 Toles and the hospital was named after HMN Retmier.

Kearney Base became the nucleus of what is now Forward Operating Base Sharana.

On July 4, 2009, combat outpost Zerok in East Paktika Province was attacked. The Haqqani network insurgents attacked the COP using mortars, accurate heavy machine gun fire, RPG fire, recoils rifles, and a 5000 lb Jingle Truck VBIED that destroyed the outpost's radio communication. The accurate enemy indirect fire from their mortars set the US mortar pit on fire, and killed two Able Company, 3rd Battalion 509th parachute Infantry regiment mortarmen, PFC Casillas and PFC Fairbairn. They were both returning fire on the 120 mm mortar. After the VBIED went off, multiple insurgents began maneuvering towards the outpost, some getting within 100 meters of the cop. Because the enemy was advancing so close to the outpost, the request for CAS was called in, but because of the DUSTWUN (large scale search) for PFC Bergdahl, (who went AWOL after abandoning his post only 100 km away from COP Zerok), air support was delayed. After intense fighting the US Paratroopers suppressed and killed most of the enemy, eventually gunships arrived and JDAMs were dropped on enemy targets.

In 2010 the 101st Rakkasan air assault took over COP Zerok. *60 minutes* produced a TV special documenting the unit's takeover of the COP, entitled *COP Zerok*.

In late July 2011, foreign troops and Afghan special forces killed more than 50 insurgents during an operation in eastern Paktika to clear a training camp the [Haqqani network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haqqani_network) used for foreign fighters, NATO said. Disenfranchised insurgents told security forces where the camp was located, the coalition said.[^cite_note-16]

In November 2011, an estimated 60 to 70 Taliban insurgents were killed in an abortive attack on a joint Afghan-ISAF base in the Margha area of Barmal. No international troops were killed or injured in the incident. It is believed the insurgents crossed over from neighboring FATA and Balochistan of Pakistan. In a separate incident the governor of Sar Hawza district died in the same month after his vehicle struck a roadside bomb.[^cite_note-17] Civilians have also been killed by the foreign forces.[^cite_note-18]



Afghan Local Police and ANSF moving up a mountain pass

In the spring of 2012 the 172nd Infantry Brigade opened the first Afghan National Army/ US Joint Artillery Fire Base in the Orgun District.

In early 2013 10th Mountain Division, 2–14th Infantry, Golden Dragons, took over FOB Zerok.

Eight civilians including a pregnant woman and a baby died when [Polish soldiers shelled the village of Nangar Khel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangar_Khel_incident), where a wedding celebration was taking place. Seven Polish soldiers have been charged with war crimes for allegedly opening fire in revenge.

[U.S. Army PFC Bowe Bergdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowe_Bergdahl) turned himself in to the Taliban on July 4, 2009, somewhere between OP Mest, near the town of Yahya Khel, and FOB Sharana. He was freed in a prisoner trade on May 31, 2014.[^cite_note-19]

Paktika was one of the provinces most affected by the [2022 Afghanistan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2022_Afghanistan_earthquake) on 22 June 2022. In [Gayan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayan_District), approximately 1,800 homes, or 70 percent of the district's homes, were destroyed, and 238 people were killed, with 393 others injured.[^cite_note-Reliefweb-20][^cite_note-Marsi-21] In [Barmal District](14912368.md), at least 500 people died, with a thousand others injured.[^cite_note-Reliefweb-20] Many houses constructed primarily of mud and wood were razed to the ground.[^cite_note-Padshah1-22] Heavy rain and the earthquake contributed to landslides that destroyed entire [hamlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)).[^cite_note-TNA1-23] The local clinic in Gayan, which had the capacity of only five patients, was also heavily damaged. Of the about 500 patients admitted to the clinic due to the earthquake, about 200 died.[^cite_note-Hamedani-24] Three days later, an [aftershock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftershock) killed five persons and wounded 11 more in Gayan District.[^cite_note-cbc1-25][^cite_note-TIE1-26][^cite_note-FP1-27]

## Geography

Further information: [Geography of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Afghanistan)

Paktika sits adjacent to the [Durand Line](203570.md) border between Pakistan and Afghanistan. It is bordered by the [Khost](457249.md) and [Paktia](457274.md) provinces to the north. The western border is shared with the provinces of [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni_Province) and [Zabul](362239.md). The [South Waziristan](4175082.md) and [North Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Waziristan) agencies are to the east of Paktika, while [Zhob District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhob_District) of the [Balochistan](4687.md) province of Pakistan borders it the southeast. The Shinkay Hills run through the center of Paktika; Toba Kakar Range runs along the border with Pakistan. The Southern districts are intermittently irrigated and cultivated, the center and north are used primarily for rangeland. There are natural forests in Ziruk, Nika, Gayan, and Bermal districts <https://nps.edu/web/ccs/paktika>

Paktika, like many other areas of Afghanistan, has been severely deforested. This has been a cause of devastating floods in recent years. The province is mainly hilly and interspersed with seasonal river valleys. In the north, the terrain gains elevation and becomes more rugged. In the west, the Rowd-e Lurah River originates in the mountainous [Omna District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omna_District) and flows southwest to the Ghazni Province, forming a shallow river valley that dominates the topography in the [Khairkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khairkot_District), [Jani Khel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janikhel_District_\(Paktika\)), and [Dila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dila_District,_Afghanistan) Districts. The terrain in Omna becomes more hilly further east in proximity to Pakistan. The sparsely populated southern districts are also hilly, with descending elevation towards the south and west.

The [Gomal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomal_River), which has a varied flow depending on season, runs from its origin in the mountains of the [Sar Hawza District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sar_Hawza_District) and flows south, before turning southeast to the Pakistani border, forming the broad river valley that defines the topography of the [Gomal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomal_District), before flowing east through Pakistan and eventually running to the powerful [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River).

## Economy

Further information: [Transportation in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Afghanistan)

As of May 2014, Paktika had regularly scheduled passenger flights to [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport) from [Sharana Airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharana_Airstrip). The province's development is considered backwards compared to the rest of the country but work on some development projects have recently began.[^cite_note-30]

## Demographics

Further information: [Demography of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demography_of_Afghanistan)

### Population

As of 2021, the total population of the province is about 789,000.[^cite_note-nsia-3] The overwhelming majority of Paktika's population (around 99%) live in rural districts. The capital city, Sharana, has around 54,400 inhabitants. The majority of Pakikta's Districts have between 25,000 and 55,000 inhabitants. Only two districts, Nika and Turwo have less than 20,000 inhabitants, with a little more than 15,000 apiece. Two of the least mountainous districts, Urgun and neighboring Barmal have nearly 90,000 inhabitants each. There are around 115,000 households, with eight members apiece, in the Province.

### Ethnicity, languages and religion

Paktika is a multi-ethnic tribal society. According to the [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School), the ethnic groups of the province are as follows: [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people), [Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_people), [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Arabs_in_Afghanistan), [Pashai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashai_people), and other various minority groups.[^cite_note-nps-31] Other sources mention that ethnic Pashtuns make up around 96% of Paktika's population.[^cite_note-mrrd-32] Around 15,000 people (0.17%) are ethnic [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks); and about 5,000 people speak some other languages.[^cite_note-mrrd-32] There is also a small Tajik community in Urgun.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]
