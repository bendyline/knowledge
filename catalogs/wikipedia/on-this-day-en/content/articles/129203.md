A **recall election** (also called a **recall referendum**, **recall petition** or **representative recall**) is a procedure by which voters can remove an elected official from office through a [referendum](51582.md) before that official's [term of office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_of_office) has ended. Recalls appear in the [constitution](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Athenian%20Constitution) in ancient [Athenian democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_democracy).[^cite_note-1] Even where they are legally available, recall elections are only commonly held in a small number of countries including [Peru](170691.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), and [Japan](15573.md).[^cite_note-2] They are considered by groups such as [ACE Electoral Knowledge Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACE_Electoral_Knowledge_Network) as the most rarely used form of [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy).[^cite_note-3]

## Process

The processes for recall elections vary greatly by country and can be originated in different ways. Initiating a recall can be done in two ways:

- Indirect (also known as a "Mixed"

  [^cite_note-\:0-4]

  &#x20;or "Top-down"

  [^cite_note-\:1-5]

  &#x20;recall): A recall may only be triggered by an official authority such as a government, parliament, or president.

- Direct (also known as a "Full"

  [^cite_note-\:0-4]

  &#x20;or "Bottom-up"

  [^cite_note-\:1-5]

  &#x20;recall): A recall may be triggered by the public directly as a [popular initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative).

## Financial costs

The scheduling of a recall outside of normal elections causes additional expense. For example, the [2021 California gubernatorial recall election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_California_gubernatorial_recall_election) cost taxpayers \$300 million[^cite_note-6] for an election that the governor won 61.9% to 38.1%.[^cite_note-7]

## Voter turnout

Many recall elections take place in [off-years](3114588.md), resulting in much lower [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) than regularly scheduled elections. Participation [quorums](237014.md) can reduce voter turnout due to [no-show paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participation_criterion) and [strategic abstention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_voting).[^cite_note-r277-8] [Media coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_coverage) can increase voter turnout.[^cite_note-h444-9]

## By country

### Argentina

The recall referendum arrived in Latin America shortly after its introduction at the US subnational level, in 1923 and 1933, to [Cordoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_Province,_Argentina) and [Entre Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entre_R%C3%ADos_Province) provinces, respectively, both in Argentina. There, recall exists at the provincial level in [Chaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaco_Province) (introduced in 1957), [Chubut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province) (1994), Córdoba (1923, 1987), [Corrientes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrientes_Province) (1960), [La Rioja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rioja_Province,_Argentina) (1986), [Rio Negro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Negro_Province) (1988), [Santiago del Estero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_del_Estero_Province) and [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego_Province,_Argentina) (1991); other provinces include it for their municipalities, namely, Entre Ríos (1933), [Neuquén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuqu%C3%A9n) (1957), [Misiones](233567.md) (1958), [San Juan](1590259.md) (1986), [San Luis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis,_Argentina) (1987). It is also included in [Buenos Aires City](255919.md) (1996).[^cite_note-10]

### Canada

An attempt at introducing recall legislation for Canada's federal [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) was brought in October 1999 by [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_of_Canada) opposition member [Ted White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_White_\(politician\)) through a private members bill entitled *Bill C 269, the Recall Act (An Act to establish the right of electors to recall members of Parliament.)*.[^cite_note-11] However, the legislation stalled and did not progress past [first reading](4062253.md).[^cite_note-12]

As such, no nation-wide recall statute exists, but two provinces, Alberta and [British Columbia](3392.md),[^cite_note-cbc_recall-13][^cite_note-elections_bc-14] have recall laws on the books.

#### Alberta

The province of [Alberta](717.md) enacted recall legislation for members of the Legislative Assembly in 1936 during the [Social Credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Social_Credit_Party) government of [William Aberhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Aberhart).[^cite_note-cbc_recall-13] The legislation was repealed after a petition was introduced for the recall of Aberhart himself.[^cite_note-cbc_recall-13][^cite_note-15]

In 2020, the Government of Alberta announced it will introduce a bill allowing recall elections for members of the Legislative Assembly, municipal governments, and school boards.[^cite_note-16] This bill, Bill 52, was passed and received royal assent on June 17, 2021, and came into effect on April 7, 2022.[^cite_note-17]

#### New Brunswick

Recall was available in New Brunswick in the early 20th century. A successful recall election took place in Saint John in late 1918.[^cite_note-18]

#### British Columbia

British Columbia's *Recall and Initiative Act*, enacted in 1995, provides a process for recalling members of the [Legislative Assembly of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_British_Columbia).[^cite_note-elections_bc-14] Voters in a provincial [riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_\(Canada\)) can petition to have their member of the Legislative Assembly removed from office once said MLA has been in office for at least 18 months. If over 40 percent of registered voters in the riding sign the petition and the petition is validated by [Elections BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_BC), the chief electoral officer informs the [speaker of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_of_British_Columbia) and the member in question that the member has been recalled and their seat vacated. A [by-election](155434.md) is called by the [lieutenant governor of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_of_British_Columbia) as soon as possible to fill the vacant seat. The recalled MLA is permitted to run in the by-election for their former seat. Twenty-six recall petitions have been launched as of 2020; of the six completed petitions returned to Elections BC, five were rejected for having too few valid signatures.[^cite_note-elections_bc-14] The sixth, on the recall of MLA [Paul Reitsma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Reitsma), was halted after Reitsma resigned in 1998 during the secondary verification stage.[^cite_note-cbc_recall-13]

### Colombia

In Colombia, the recall referendum was included by the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991) in 1991. The constitutional replacement was launched as an answer to the movement known as [*la séptima papeleta*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991#Student_movement_and_the_s%C3%A9ptima_papeleta) (the seventh ballot), which requested a constitutional reform to end violence, narcoterrorism, corruption and increasing citizenship apathy. The definition of recall referendum in relation to programmatic vote was approved. It obliges candidates running for office to register a government plan which is later on considered to activate the recall. Since the time the mechanism was regulated by Law 134 in 1994, until 2015, 161 attempts led 41 referendums and none of them succeeded since the threshold of participation was not reached. In 2015, a new law (303/2015) reduced the number of signatures required to activate a recall referendum (from 40 per cent to 30 per cent of the total of votes obtained by the elected authority) and the threshold (dropping from the 50 per cent to the 40 per cent of valid votes on the day of the elections of the challenged authority). The change in the regulation, also quickening the registration of promoters, led to a considerable increase in the number of attempts.[^cite_note-19]

### Cuba

According to [its constitution](1114749.md), Cuba is a [socialist republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_state) in which all members of representative bodies of state power are subject to recall by the masses.[^cite_note-roman103-20] As a principle of [soviet democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_democracy), similar recall provisions have been included in the constitutions of many other [communist countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state), including [that of the Soviet Union](#Soviet_Union). Although allowable according to law, in practice the right of recall was never used.

### Ecuador

Article 105 of the [2008 Constitution of Ecuador](18864296.md) provides for recall of all elected officials:&#x20;

> People in enjoyment of political rights may revoke the mandate of popularly elected authorities. The request for revocation of the mandate may be submitted once the first and before the last year of the period for which the authority in question was elected. During the management period of an authority, only one process of revocation of the mandate may be carried out. The revocation request must be supported by a number not less than ten percent of people registered in the corresponding electoral registry. In the case of the President of the Republic, the support of a number not less than fifteen percent of those registered in the electoral registry will be required.

— Article 105 of the Constitution of Ecuador (translated from Spanish)

#### State parliaments

The recall of members of the [state parliaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Parliament_\(Germany\)) of Germany exist in five [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany); [Bavaria](3764.md), [Berlin](3354.md), [Brandenburg](3765.md), [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\)), and [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md). All of these states only allow for the recall of the entire legislature by triggering a new election, the recall of individual members is not allowed.[^cite_note-22]

Article 18, Section 3 of the [Constitution of Bavaria](https://www.bayern.landtag.de/fileadmin/Internet_Dokumente/Sonstiges_P/BV_Verfassung_Englisch_formatiert_14-12-16.pdf) provides, that the entire [Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Bavaria) can be dismissed by referendum on petition of 1 million citizens, with elections of a new Landtag to be held up to six weeks after the recall referendum.

### India

See also: [Right to Recall laws in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Recall_laws_in_India)

### Kiribati

Section 59 of the [Constitution of Kiribati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Kiribati) provides for a majority of electors in a district (who were electors of the district at the time of the original election) to sign a petition requesting a recall election. The recall election must not occur less than 6 months after the original election or a failed recall attempt. If the recall election is successful another election to fill the seat must be held within 3 months.

### Latvia

Article 14 of the [Constitution of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Latvia) enables the recall of the entire [Saiema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiema), though not of specific representatives:

Article 14: Not less than one tenth of electors has the right to initiate a national referendum regarding recalling of the Saeima.

If the majority of voters and at least two thirds of the number of the voters who participated in the last elections of the Saeima vote in the national referendum regarding recalling of the Saeima, then the Saeima shall be deemed recalled.

The right to initiate a national referendum regarding recalling of the Saeima may not be exercised one year after the convening of the Saeima and one year before the end of the term of office of the Saeima, during the last six months of the term of office of the President, as well as earlier than six months after the previous national referendum regarding recalling of the Saeima.

The electors may not recall any individual member of the Saeima.

### Mexico

See also: [2022 Mexican presidential recall referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mexican_presidential_recall_referendum)

In [Mexico](3966054.md), the [State of Yucatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n) was the first to introduce the recall in 1938. The mechanism, which had never been used, was declared unconstitutional 72 years later by the [Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation](2476388.md). A similar mechanism introduced in [Chihuahua](23962301.md) in 1997 was also declared unconstitutional and consequently eliminated from law. Despite these precedents, the recall was later included in the states of [Oaxaca](156896.md) (1998), [Morelos](195827.md) (2011), [Guerrero](195816.md) (2013), [Zacatecas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacatecas) and [Aguascalientes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguascalientes).[^cite_note-23]

### New Zealand

Early policies of the [New Zealand Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party) included support for "the recall".[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Peru

Recall regulations were introduced in Peru by the [Democratic Constituent Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Constituent_Congress) (*Congreso Constituyente Democrático*) which drafted a new constitution after [Alberto Fujimori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Fujimori)'s [*autogolpe*](1221872.md) in 1992. Between 1997 and 2013, more than 5000 recall referendums were activated against democratically elected authorities from 747 Peruvian municipalities (45.5% of all municipalities). This makes Peru the world's most intensive user of this mechanism.[^cite_note-26]

### Philippines

Article 10 of the [1987 constitution of the Philippines](19554336.md) allows for the recall of local officials. The Local Government Code, as amended, enabled the application of the provisions of the constitution. Elected officials from provincial governors to the [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) (village) councilors are potentially subject to recall. At least 15% of the electorate in a specific place must have their signatures verified in a petition for the recall to take place.[^cite_note-27]

The president, vice president, members of Congress, and the elected officials of the [Bangsamoro](37260549.md) cannot be removed via recall.

Above the barangay level, there had been eight recall elections, of which at least 2 led to a successful recall of an incumbent; the most recent recall election was the [2015 Puerto Princesa mayoral recall election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Puerto_Princesa_mayoral_recall_election).

### Switzerland

While recalls are not provided for at the federal level in Switzerland, six [cantons](180752.md) allow them:[^cite_note-NZZ-28][^cite_note-29]

- [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern): Recall of the executive and legislative has been possible since 1846. 30,000 signatures (4% of all adult citizens) are required to trigger a recall referendum. There has been one unsuccessful attempt to recall the executive in 1852 (the 'Schatzgelder' affair).

- [Schaffhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schaffhausen): Recall of the executive and legislative has been possible since 1876. 1,000 signatures (2% of all adult citizens) are required to trigger a recall referendum. There has been one unsuccessful attempt to recall the executive in 2000, triggered by the lawyer and cantonal MP Gerold Meier.

- [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn): Recall of the executive and legislative has been possible since 1869. 6,000 signatures (3% of all adult citizens) are required to trigger a recall referendum. There has been one unsuccessful attempt to recall the executive and legislative in 1995 (related to a banking scandal). Three further attempts (in 1887, 1961 and 1973) failed to collect the necessary number of signatures.

- [Ticino](84147.md): Recall of the executive has been possible since 1892. 15,000 signatures (7% of all adult citizens) are required to trigger a recall referendum. There has been one unsuccessful recall attempt in 1942. In addition, recall of municipal executives has been possible since 2011. Signatures of 30% of all adult citizens are required to trigger a recall referendum.

- [Thurgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgau): Recall of the executive and legislative has been possible since 1869. 20,000 signatures (13% of all adult citizens) are required to trigger a recall referendum. There have been no recall attempts.

- [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri): Recall of the executive and legislative has been possible since 1888. Since 1979, 600 signatures (3% of all adult citizens) have been required to trigger a recall referendum. In addition, recall of municipal executives and legislatives has been possible since 2011. Signatures of 10% of registered voters are required to trigger a recall referendum. There have been no recall attempts either at the cantonal or municipal levels.

The possibility of recall referendums (together with the popular election of executives, the [popular initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative) and the legislative referendum) was introduced into several cantonal constitutions after the 1860s in the course of a broad movement for democratic reform. The instrument has never been of any practical importance—the few attempts at recall so far have failed, usually because the required number of signatures was not collected—and it was abolished in the course of constitutional revisions in [Aargau](2467.md) (1980), [Baselland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baselland) (1984) and [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lucerne) (2007). The only successful recall so far happened in the Canton of Aargau in the year 1862. However, the possibility of recalling municipal executives was newly introduced in Ticino in 2011, with 59% of voters in favor, as a reaction to the perceived problem of squabbling and dysfunctional municipal governments.[^cite_note-NZZ-28]

### Soviet Union

Article 142 of The [1936 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Constitution_of_the_Soviet_Union) of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) gave the right in theory to recall deputies in legislatures at all levels of government:

> **Article 142.** It is the duty of every deputy to report to his electors on his work and on the work of the [Soviet of Working People's Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_\(council\)), and he is liable to be recalled at any time in the manner established by law upon decision of a majority of the electors.

— [1936 Constitution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Constitution_of_the_Soviet_Union)

As a principle of [soviet democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_democracy), similar recall provisions have been widely copied in the constitutions of other [communist countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state), including those [of Cuba](#Cuba), [China](5405.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), [North Korea](21255.md), and countries in the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc).[^cite_note-30] Although allowable according to law, in practice the right of recall was never used in any soviet system of government, except for an attempt in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People's_Republic) in 1989.[^cite_note-venice-31]

### Taiwan

In [Taiwan](25734.md), according to the [Additional Articles of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_Articles_of_the_Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_China), the recall of the president or the vice president shall be initiated upon the proposal of one-fourth of all members of the [Legislative Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Yuan), and also passed by two-thirds of all the members. The final recall must be passed in a recall election by more than one-half of the valid ballots in a vote in which more than one-half of the electorate in the [free area of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_area_of_the_Republic_of_China) takes part.[^cite_note-32]

Other elected officials can be recalled in elections by more than 1/4 of the total electors in the original electoral district, and where the number of votes consenting to the recall is more than that of dissenting.

On 6 June 2020, [Mayor of Kaohsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Kaohsiung) [Han Kuo-yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Kuo-yu) became [the first mayor to be recalled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Kaohsiung_mayoral_recall_vote). 939,090 votes out of 969,259 agreed the recall.[^cite_note-33]

After [Mayor of Keelung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Keelung) [George Hsieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hsieh) defeated a recall vote in October 2024, the [Kuomintang](16903.md) proposed amendments to the Public Officials Election and Recall Act.[^cite_note-34] In December 2024, the [Legislative Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Yuan) approved changes requiring that initiators and signatories of recall petitions provide photocopies of their [national identification cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_identification_card_\(Taiwan\)).[^cite_note-35]

In 2025, due to the [2024 Taiwanese constitutional controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Taiwanese_constitutional_controversy), civic groups launched the "[Great Recall Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recall_Wave)" to recall more than 30 [Kuomintang](16903.md) legislators and [mayor of Hsinchu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Hsinchu).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] The recall vote for 24 Kuomintang legislators and mayor of Hsinchu City were held on July 26, 2025.[^cite_note-38] The recall vote for the two legislators in [Nantou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantou_County) were held on August 23, 2025.[^cite_note-39] None of the recall attempts proved successful, and the KMT remained the biggest party in the Legislative Yuan.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

### United Kingdom

Main article: [Recall of MPs Act 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recall_of_MPs_Act_2015)

The **Recall of MPs Act 2015 (c. 25)** is an [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament) of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](13964.md) which mandates a recall petition to be held if a [member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament) is found to have committed certain wrongdoings, including any that result in a custodial prison sentence. The petitions cannot be triggered by popular initiative. The subsequent recall petition is successful if signed by at least 10% of the electorate in the MP's constituency. Successful recall leads to a [by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Parliamentary_by-elections) to fill the seat.

The act received [royal assent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Assent#United_Kingdom) on 26 March 2015 after being introduced on 11 September 2014.[^cite_note-stages-43][^cite_note-legislation-44] [The UK's first recall petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_North_Antrim_recall_petition) occurred in 2018, with the [first successful one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Peterborough_by-election) occurring the next year in [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_\(constituency\)).

There are no recall procedures for other elected officials in the United Kingdom.

### United States

#### History



Submitting petitions for the recall of [Seattle, Washington](11388236.md), mayor [Hiram Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Gill) in December 1910; Gill was removed by a recall election the following February, but voters returned him to the office in 1914

Recall first appeared in Colonial America in the laws of the General Court of the [Massachusetts Bay Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) in 1631.[^cite_note-45] This version of the recall involved one elected body removing another official. During the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation) stipulated that state legislatures might recall delegates from the [Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress).[^cite_note-46] According to New York Delegate John Lansing, the power was never exercised by any state. The [Virginia Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Plan), issued at the outset of the [Philadelphia Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Convention) of 1787, proposed to pair recall with rotation in office and to apply these dual principles to the lower house of the national legislature. The recall was rejected by the Constitutional Convention. However, the anti-Federalists used the lack of recall provision as a weapon in the ratification debates.

Only two governors have ever been recalled. In 1921, Governor [Lynn Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Frazier) of [North Dakota](21651.md) was [recalled during a dispute about state-owned industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_North_Dakota_gubernatorial_recall_election). In 2003, Governor [Gray Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_Davis) of [California](5407.md) was [recalled over the state budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_California_gubernatorial_recall_election). Additionally, in 1988, a recall was approved against Governor [Evan Mecham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Mecham) of [Arizona](21883824.md),[^cite_note-47] but he was impeached and convicted before it got on the ballot.[^cite_note-48]

In [Alaska](624.md), [Georgia](48830.md), [Kansas](16716.md), [Minnesota](19590.md), [Montana](19978.md), [Rhode Island](25410.md), and [Washington](13015878.md), specific grounds are required for a recall. Some form of malfeasance or misconduct while in office must be identified by the petitioners. The target may choose to dispute the validity of the grounds in court, and a court then judges whether the allegations in the petition rise to a level where a recall is necessary. Anna Louise Strong, member of the Seattle School board, was recalled from her position in 1918, apparently on grounds that she was supporting extreme labour positions.[^cite_note-49] In the November 2010 general election, [Illinois](14849.md) passed a [referendum](51582.md) to amend the state constitution to allow a recall of the state's governor, in light of former Governor [Rod Blagojevich's corruption scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Blagojevich_corruption_charges). In the other eleven states that permit statewide recall, no grounds are required and recall petitions may be circulated for any reason. However, the target is permitted to submit responses to the stated reasons for recall.

The minimum number of signatures to qualify a recall, and the time limit to do so, vary among the states. In addition, the handling of recalls, once they qualify, differs. In some states a recall triggers a simultaneous special election, where the vote on the recall, as well as the vote on the replacement if the recall succeeds, are on the same ballot. In the 2003 California recall election, over 100 candidates appeared on the replacement portion of the ballot. In other states, a separate special election is held after the target is recalled, or a replacement is appointed by the Governor or some other state authority.

The largest amount of recalls in the United States were held in 2021, as 529 officials faced recalls, but it had the lowest amount of successful recalls as only 25 were removed.[^cite_note-50]

#### 2011 recalls

In 2011, there were at least 150 recall elections in the United States. Of these, 75 officials were recalled, and nine officials resigned under threat of recall. Recalls were held in 17 states in 73 different jurisdictions. [Michigan](18859.md) had the most recalls (at least 30). The year set a record for number of state legislator recall elections (11 elections) beating the previous one-year high (three elections).

Three jurisdictions adopted the recall in 2011.[^cite_note-blogspot1-51]

Of recall elections, 52 were for [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council), 30 were for [mayor](101146.md), 17 were for [school board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_board), 11 were for [state legislators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_\(United_States\)), and one was for a [prosecuting attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecuting_attorney) ([York County, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_County,_Nebraska)). The largest municipality to hold a recall was [Miami-Dade County, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County,_Florida), for mayor.[^cite_note-blogspot1-51]

The busiest day was November 8 ([Election Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Day_\(United_States\))) with 26 recalls. In 34 jurisdictions, recalls were held over multiple days.[^cite_note-blogspot1-51]

### Venezuela

Article 72 of the [Constitution of Venezuela](1211684.md) enables the recall of any elected representative, including the president. This provision was used in the [2004 Venezuelan recall referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Venezuelan_recall_referendum), which attempted to remove President [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez):

Article 72: All \[...] offices filled by popular vote are subject to revocation.

Once one-half of the term of office to which an official has been elected has elapsed, a number of voters representing at least 20% of the registered voters in the affected constituency may petition for the calling of a referendum to revoke that official's mandate.

When a number of voters equal to or greater than the number of those who elected the official vote in favour of the recall, provided that a number of voters equal to or greater than 25% of the total number of registered voters vote in the recall referendum, the official's mandate shall be deemed revoked and immediate action shall be taken to fill the permanent vacancy as provided for by this Constitution and by law.

The 2004 recall referendum failed with 59.25% voting against removing Chavez from office.[^cite_note-108]
