# 200 metres breaststroke

See also: [History of swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_swimming)



Graphic data for World Record Progression in Men and Women Swimming 50m-100m-200m Long and Short Course Butterfly-Backstroke-Breaststroke-Freestyle

The **200 metres [breaststroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaststroke)** is an event in [competitive swimming](22425760.md). World records are ratified by [World Aquatics](317029.md) (formerly FINA). The current long course world record holders are Japan's [Shin Ohashi](83995504.md) and Russia's [Evgeniia Chikunova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeniia_Chikunova), while the current short course world record holders are the Netherlands' [Caspar Corbeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_Corbeau) and the US' [Kate Douglass](67964882.md).

The men's event was first contested at the [Olympic Games](22576.md) in 1908, while the women's event was introduced at the 1924 games; the current Olympic champions are France's [Léon Marchand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Marchand), and Douglass. The men's and women's events have been contested at the [World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Aquatics_Championships) since the inaugural championships in 1973; the current world champions (long course) are China's [Qin Haiyang](61358220.md), and Douglass. The men's and women's events have been contested at the [World Aquatics Swimming Championships (25m)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Aquatics_Swimming_Championships_\(25m\)) since the inaugural championships in 1993; the current world champions (short course) are Spain's [Carles Coll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carles_Coll?action=edit\&redlink=1), and Douglass.

#### Notes

1. **[^](#ref_Butterfly)**&#x52;ecord was set using a butterfly technique and was nullified in 1953.

2. **[^](#ref_Short_Course)**&#x52;ecord was set in a 25 m pool and was annulled after World Aquatics required 50 m pools to be used.

3. **[^](#ref_John_Davies)**&#x41;fter the records set in a 25 m pool were nullified, the world record was reverted to [Herbert Klein's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Klein_\(swimmer\)) mark from 1950, which [John Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Davies_\(swimmer\)) equaled in 1952. However, these records were also later annulled because they employed a butterfly technique.

4. **[^](#ref_Underwater)**[Masaru Furukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaru_Furukawa) and [Mamoru Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamoru_Tanaka?action=edit\&redlink=1) set their world records by swimming most of the race while underwater. Their records were annulled in 1956.
