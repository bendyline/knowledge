**Liuzhou** ([/ljuːˈdʒoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-3] [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [柳州](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9F%B3%E5%B7%9E), Standard Mandarin: [\[ljòʊ.ʈʂóʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Mandarin), Liuzhou Yue dialect: [\[liəu<sup>53</sup> ʦəu<sup>44</sup>\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet)[^cite_note-4]) is a [prefecture-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city) in north-central [Guangxi](47836950.md) Zhuang Autonomous Region, [People's Republic of China](5405.md). The prefecture's population was 4,146,000 as of the 2024 census,[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-1] including 2,519,051 in the built-up area made of five urban districts. Its total area is 18,594 km<sup>2</sup> (7,179 sq mi) and 3,559.4 km<sup>2</sup> (1,374.3 sq mi) for the built-up area.

## Geography



Map including Liuzhou (labeled as LIU-CHOU (LIUCHOW) 柳州) ([AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Map_Service), 1954)

Liuzhou is located on the banks of the winding [Liu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_River), approximately 255 km (158 mi) from [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), the regional capital. By road, it is about 167 km (104 mi) to [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin), 167 km (104 mi) to [Hechi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechi), 237 km (147 mi) to [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), 373 km (232 mi) to [Fangchenggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangchenggang), 448 km (278 mi) to [Beihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beihai).

Swimming in the river is a tradition of the city. The river is normally green, but sometimes in summer, floods from the mountain areas upstream bring sediment which colors the water yellow. In early 2012, a cadmium spill upstream caused serious pollution worries.[^cite_note-danwei-5]

The river can be deep. Normally, the depth is 60–70 metres (200–230 ft) but can as deep as 90 metres (300 ft) before it floods over the wall. In 2000 a bus, with 78 passengers, fell over the side of a bridge. There were no survivors.[^cite_note-6]

### Climate

Liuzhou has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa/Cwa*), with mild winters and long hot summers, and very humid conditions year-round. The monthly 24-hour average temperature ranges from 10.5 °C (50.9 °F) in January to 29 °C (84 °F) in August, while extremes have ranged from −3.8 to 42 °C (25 to 108 °F). Rain is both the heaviest and most frequent from May to August, when nearly two-thirds of the annual rainfall occurs.

The [Liujiang men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liujiang_man) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 柳江人) are among the earliest modern [humans](682482.md) found in [East Asia](19605700.md). Their remains were discovered in the [Tongtianyang Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongtianyang_Cave?action=edit\&redlink=1) (通天岩) in [Liujiang County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liujiang_County), Guangxi.[^cite_note-liujiang_hominid_site-11] Liujiang man is a Late Pleistocene *Homo sapiens sapiens*.

Liuzhou has a history of more than 2,100 years. The city was founded in 111 B.C. when it was known as **Tanzhong** ([潭中](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%BD%AD%E4%B8%AD); 'Center of Deep Pool').

In 742 A.D. it became known as **Longcheng** ([龙城](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%BE%99%E5%9F%8E); 'Dragon City'), after the [Long River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_River_\(Guangxi\)), before finally changing to Liuzhou ([柳州](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9F%B3%E5%B7%9E); 'Willow [Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_\(administrative_division\))') after the [Liu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_River) in 1736.

The most famous historic figure is [Liu Zongyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Zongyuan) (773–819), who was a poet and politician in the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) and who died in Liuzhou. He is commemorated by a park in the city.

Liuzhou was the site of [Liuchow Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liuchow_Airfield), used by Nationalist Chinese and [American Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) in [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-Hump-12] (At that time the airfield was closer to the centre, where the zoo is now.) It was captured by the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) army on 7 November 1944 during the [Battle of Guilin–Liuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guilin%E2%80%93Liuzhou) and recaptured by Nationalist Chinese forces on 30 June 1945 prior to the [Second Guangxi Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Guangxi_Campaign).[^cite_note-Hump-12]

## Economy



Liuzhou city center in 1948

Liuzhou is the second largest city in Guangxi and is the region's industrial center. According to statistics issued by the Liuzhou government in 2015, the city's GDP was 231.1 billion yuan.[^cite_note-hktdc-13]

Among important companies based in Liuzhou are:

- [LiuGong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LiuGong) - a multinational construction machinery manufacturer

- [SAIC-GM-Wuling Automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAIC-GM-Wuling_Automobile) - a joint venture between [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), [SAIC Motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAIC_Motor) and Liuzhou [Wuling Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuling_Motors)

## Tourism



Liuzhou city seen from Ma'an Mountain in 2022

As with much of Guangxi, the landscape around Liuzhou is a mix of rolling hills, mountain peaks, caves and [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) scenery. It is an ideal base for exploring the minority villages in the area.

- **Rongshui:** [Rongshui Miao Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongshui_Miao_Autonomous_County) is located in the north of Liuzhou prefecture, 118 km (73 mi) away from Liuzhou and 168 km (104 mi) from Guilin. The territory is inhabited by Miao, Yao, Dong, Zhuang and Han ethnic groups.

- **Dayaoshan** scenic area is in Jinxiu Yao Autonomous County, 154 km (96 mi) from the city of Liuzhou. It has a scenic area of over 500 km

  2

  &#x20;(190 sq mi).

- **Sanjiang:** [Sanjiang Dong Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjiang_Dong_Autonomous_County) lies to the north of Liuzhou near the Hunan border. It is a Dong minority area and is surrounded by picturesque ethnic minority villages.

- LiuZhou Industrial Museum (柳州工业博物馆) was set up on the original site of the former Cotton Textile Factory No.3, and opened in 2012.

In recent years (post-[COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), 2022/3) some tourists engage in [culinary tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culinary_tourism) since Liuzhou is the birthplace of Guangxi's most famous dish, [Luosifen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luosifen).

## Quotes

Liuzhou appears in the Chinese saying 生在苏州, 活在杭州, 吃在广州, 死在柳州.

*Born in [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou,_Jiangsu), live in [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou), eat in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), die in Liuzhou*

because, in the past, the city was known for its coffins, made from firwood, camphor wood, and sandalwood,[^cite_note-ThingsChinese-14] which are said to preserve the body after death. [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou)'s ["Cantonese" cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine) is famous worldwide, and [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou) is known for its prosperity and the beauty of its location. [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou,_Jiangsu) is reputed to have the most beautiful people in China, so the line is sometimes given as "*Marry in Suzhou*...".

Today many tourists buy miniature coffins, about 3 to 30 cm (1.2 to 11.8 in) long, as souvenirs or good luck charms. The coffins are usually inscribed 升官发财 (*shēng guān fā cái*) which means 'get promotion and get rich". The second and fourth characters are [homophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophone) of 棺材 (*guāncái*) meaning 'coffin'.

Some miniature coffins are used as caskets to hold the ashes of ancestors.[^cite_note-ThingsChinese-14]
