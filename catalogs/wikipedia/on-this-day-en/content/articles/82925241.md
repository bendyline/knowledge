The **2026 UCI Road World Championships** was the 99th edition of the [UCI Road World Championships](7171411.md), the annual world championships for [road bicycle racing](1755460.md). It was held from 20 to 27 September 2026 in [Montreal](7954681.md), [Quebec](7954867.md), [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-uci-announce-1][^cite_note-montreal2026-2]

The championships marked the return of the UCI Road World Championships to Montreal 52 years after the city hosted the [1974 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_UCI_Road_World_Championships), which was the first time the event was held outside [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-montreal2026-2] It was the third time Canada hosted the event, after Montreal in 1974 and [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario) in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_UCI_Road_World_Championships).[^cite_note-cyclingnews-routes-3]

A total of thirteen events were held, consisting of six road races, six [individual time trials](348799.md) and a [team time trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_time_trial) mixed relay.[^cite_note-uci-200days-4] Approximately 1,000 athletes from around 75 countries were expected to compete.[^cite_note-montreal2026-2]

## Background

The event was awarded to [Montreal](7954681.md) at the [UCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Cycliste_Internationale) Congress held during the [2022 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in [Wollongong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollongong), [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-montreal2026-2] The event was organized by the promoter of the [Grand Prix Cycliste de Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Cycliste_de_Qu%C3%A9bec) et [de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Cycliste_de_Montr%C3%A9al), with support from [Cycling Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_Canada).[^cite_note-montreal2026-2]

The Canadian federal government announced a CA\$13-million investment to support the hosting of the championships, with CA\$9.8 million from Sport Canada through the Hosting Program and CA\$3.2 million from Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions.[^cite_note-gpcqm-5]

The event was expected to be the largest sporting event in Montreal since the [1976 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-montreal2026-2] Admission to all events was free of charge.[^cite_note-faq-6]

## Course

The routes were unveiled during the [2025 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali), [Rwanda](25645.md).[^cite_note-cyclingnews-routes-3]

### Road races

The road races featured a final circuit of 13.4 kilometres (8.3 mi) around [Mount Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Royal) in downtown Montreal, with 269 metres (883 ft) of elevation gain per lap.[^cite_note-uci-200days-4] The circuit included the Voie Camillien-Houde climb (2.3 kilometres (1.4 mi) at 6.2% average gradient), the Chemin de la Polytechnique with sections exceeding 11% gradient, and a false flat rising along Avenue du Parc, which served as the finish line for all thirteen events.[^cite_note-uci-200days-4] The circuit was similar to that used by the [Grand Prix Cycliste de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Cycliste_de_Montr%C3%A9al), as well as previous cycling events in Montréal such as the [1974 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_UCI_Road_World_Championships) and [1976 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_individual_road_race).

The elite men's road race covered 273.4 kilometres (169.9 mi) with 3,803 metres (12,477 ft) of total elevation gain,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Technical_Guide''2026101-7] while the elite women's road race spanned 180.1 kilometres (111.9 mi) with 2,570 metres (8,430 ft) of elevation gain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Technical_Guide''202691-8] Both elite road races started in [Brossard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brossard), in the [Montérégie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont%C3%A9r%C3%A9gie) region, before passing through several municipalities and crossing the [Samuel De Champlain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel-De_Champlain_Bridge) to reach the Mount Royal circuit in downtown Montréal.[^cite_note-uci-200days-4][^cite_note-cyclingnews-routes-3]

### Time trials



Amateur bikers ride the time trial course in Old Montreal on 19 September.

For the first time since [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_UCI_Road_World_Championships), the time trial courses for elite men and women was identical in length – a 39.2-kilometre (24.4-mile) route through Montreal, racing along the [Saint Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_River), crossing the [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River), passing through the [Circuit Gilles Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Gilles_Villeneuve), and returning via the [Concorde Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pont_de_la_Concorde_\(Montreal\)).[^cite_note-cyclingnews-routes2-9]

### Virtual route

Online indoor exercise platform and host of the 2026 UCI Cycling Esports World Championships, [MyWhoosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyWhoosh), had a virtual route replicating the Mont Royal final circuit of the road races.[^cite_note-uci-200days-4]

## Schedule

All events started and finished in Montreal, with the exception of the elite road races, which started at [Quartier DIX30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartier_DIX30) in [Brossard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brossard). [Avenue du Parc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Avenue_\(Montreal\)) served as the finish line for all events.[^cite_note-uci-200days-4]
