A **ski resort** is a [resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort) developed for [skiing](28478.md), [snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding), and other [winter sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_sports). In Europe, most ski resorts are towns or villages in or adjacent to a [ski area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_area), a mountainous area with [pistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piste) (marked ski trails) and a [ski lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_lift) system. In North America, it is more common for ski areas to exist well away from towns, so ski resorts usually are [destination resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destination_resort), often purpose-built and self-contained, where skiing is the main activity.

## Ski resort



Map of world ski resorts

Ski resorts are located in both hemispheres, on all continents except Antarctica. They typically are located on mountains, as they require a large slope. They also need to receive sufficient snow (at least in combination with artificial [snowmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmaking), unless the resort uses [dry ski slopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_ski_slope)).

High concentrations of ski resorts are located in the [Alps](981.md), [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Peninsula), western and eastern [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ski_areas_and_resorts_in_North_America), and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ski_areas_and_resorts_in_Japan). There are also ski resorts in the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ski_areas_and_resorts_in_South_America), scattered across central [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ski_areas_and_resorts_in_Asia), and in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skiing_in_Australia) and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skiing_in_New_Zealand).

Extreme locations of non-indoor (at least one ski lift outside) ski resorts include:

- The most northern ski resort near [Tromsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troms%C3%B8), Norway

- The most southern ski resort near [Ushuaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushuaia), Argentina

- The ski resort closest to the equator from the north, near [Lijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijiang), China.

- The ski resort closest to the equator from the south, near [Mahlasela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahlasela_Pass), Lesotho.

The ski industry has identified advancing generations of ski resorts:[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

First generation

Developed around a well-established summer resort or village (e.g. [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), [St. Moritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Moritz), [Kitzbühel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitzb%C3%BChel), [Chamonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamonix), [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md), [Megève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg%C3%A8ve), [Val Gardena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Gardena)).

Second generation

Created from a non-tourist village or pasture (e.g. [St Anton am Arlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Anton_am_Arlberg), [Lech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_\(Vorarlberg\)), [Courchevel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courchevel), [L'Alpe d'Huez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Alpe_d'Huez), [Aspen/Snowmass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen/Snowmass), [Breckenridge Ski Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breckenridge_Ski_Resort)).

Third generation or integrated

Designed from scratch on virgin territory to be a purpose-built ski resort, all the amenities and services nearby (e.g. [Sestriere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestriere), [Flaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaine), [La Plagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Plagne), [Isola 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isola_2000)).

Fourth generation or village resorts

Created from a virgin territory or around an existing village, but more concerned with traditional uses (e.g. [Valmeinier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmeinier), [Valmorel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmorel), [Shahdag Mountain Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahdag_Mountain_Resort)).

The term **ski station** is also used, particularly in Europe, for a skiing facility which is not located in or near a town or village. A ski resort which is also open for summer activities is often referred to as a [mountain resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_resort).

Ski areas have marked paths for skiing known as runs, trails or [pistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piste). Ski areas typically have one or more [chairlifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairlift) for moving skiers rapidly to the top of hills, and to interconnect the various trails. [Rope tows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope_tow) can also be used on short slopes (usually beginner hills or bunny slopes). Larger ski areas may use [gondola lifts](298681.md) or [aerial tramways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_tramway) for transportation across longer distances within the ski area. Resorts post their [trail map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_map) illustrating the location of lifts, trails, services and the ski area boundary, and during the [ski season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_season) issue a daily snow conditions report listing open trails, operating lifts and weather status.

Ski areas usually have at least a basic [first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid) facility, and some kind of [ski patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_patrol) service to ensure that injured skiers are rescued. The ski patrol is usually responsible for rule enforcement, marking hazards, closing individual runs or areas as conditions require, and removing (dismissing) dangerous participants from the area.

The typical ski area base includes a ticket office, [ski lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_lodge), [ski school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_school), equipment rental/repair shop, restaurant/bar, shopping, shuttle bus stop and parking.[^cite_note-3]

Some ski resorts offer [lodging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodging) options on the slopes themselves, with ski-in and ski-out access allowing guests to ski right up to the door. Ski resorts often have other activities, such as [snowmobiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmobile), [sledding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sledding), [horse drawn sleighs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_drawn_sleigh), [dog-sledding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_sled), [ice skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating), indoor or outdoor [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_pool) and [hot tubbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_tub), game rooms, and local forms of entertainment, such as clubs, cinema, theaters and [cabarets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabarets).&#x20;

**Après-ski** (French for *after skiing*) is a term for entertainment, [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife) or social events that occur specifically at ski resorts.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] These add to the enjoyment of resort-goers and provide something to do besides [skiing](28478.md) and [snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding). The culture originated in the Alps, where it is most popular and where skiers often stop at bars on their last run of the day while still wearing all their ski gear.[^cite_note-6] Though the word "ski" is a derivation of the [Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse) *skíð* via [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language), the choice of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) is likely attributed to the early popularity of such activities in the [French Alps](961317.md), with which it was then linked.[^cite_note-7]

## Environmental effects

See also: [Environmental impact of tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_tourism) and [Vegetation and slope stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetation_and_slope_stability)

As rising temperatures, receding glaciers and declining snowfall affect the environment, resort development and operations also have an [environmental impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact) on land, lakes, streams, and wildlife.[^cite_note-\:0-8] Amenities and infrastructure such as concrete buildings, ski lifts, access roads, parking lots, and [railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways) have contributed to the [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) of mountainous zones.

### Primary (direct) impact of resort development

In recent years, the use of [snow cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_cannons) by many ski resorts has increased to compensate for reduced levels of snowfall.[^cite_note-9] In order to sustain good quality snow coverage, snowmaking requires large amounts of water and sometimes the creation of [artificial lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_lake). Snow cannons also introduce a noise element.

### Secondary (indirect) impact of resort development

The required infrastructure can affect [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) through the increased area of [impervious surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impervious_surface), redirecting the flow of [water runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff).

### Mitigation efforts

Many resorts are taking steps to reduce their energy and water consumption and waste production, increase recycling, and restore habitats. Initiatives aimed at addressing environmental concerns include:

- [Alpine Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Convention)

- Snowsports Industries America (SIA) ClimateUnited

- NSAA Sustainable Slopes Initiative

- [National Forest Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Forest_Foundation) Ski Conservation Fund

- [Ceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceres_\(organization\)) [Business for Innovative Climate and Energy Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_for_Innovative_Climate_and_Energy_Policy) (BICEP) Network

- FESI Winter Sports Sustainability Network (WSN)

- [Protect Our Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protect_Our_Winters) (POW)
