**23XI Racing** (pronounced **twenty-three eleven**) is an American professional [auto racing](1022.md) organization that competes in the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md). It is owned and operated by [Hall of Fame basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame) player [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan), along with current [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md) driver and three-time [Daytona 500](39021.md) winner [Denny Hamlin](2148196.md). The team name represents a combination of the number 23 Michael Jordan wore during most of his [NBA](22093.md) career and the number 11 which Denny Hamlin has driven his entire Cup series career.[^cite_note-Long-3] The organization fields three full-time entries. They are the No. 23 [Toyota Camry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry) for [Bubba Wallace](35761776.md), No. 35 Camry for [Riley Herbst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riley_Herbst), and No. 45 Camry for [Tyler Reddick](41108702.md). Additionally, the team fields the No. 67 Camry part-time for [Corey Heim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Heim). They have a technical alliance with [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md).[^cite_note-23XI_JGR-4]

## History



The previous logo for 23XI Racing.

During the summer of 2020, rumors swirled that Michael Jordan intended on purchasing an ownership stake in [Richard Petty Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Petty_Motorsports), who fielded the only black driver in the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md), [Bubba Wallace](35761776.md). Such claims were repeatedly denied by Jordan's management team.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

On September 8, 2021, veteran motorsports executive Steve Lauletta was named team president after serving as the interim president since the team's foundation.[^cite_note-Lauletta-1]

On July 12, 2022, 23XI Racing announced that two-time [NASCAR Xfinity Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Xfinity_Series) champion [Tyler Reddick](41108702.md) signed with the team for the 2024 season.[^cite_note-7] On July 23, 2022, Hamlin announced that starting in 2023, 23XI would have its own pit crews instead of leasing them from Joe Gibbs Racing.[^cite_note-8]

In May, 2024, 23XI opened its doors to their brand-new race shop and team headquarters, named "Airspeed", in Huntersville, North Carolina.[^cite_note-9]

### Antitrust lawsuit with Front Row Motorsports against NASCAR

Main article: [23XI Racing v. NASCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23XI_Racing_v._NASCAR)

On October 2, 2024, it was announced that 23XI Racing, along with [Front Row Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_Row_Motorsports), filed an antitrust lawsuit against NASCAR over the terms of the updated charter agreement, as well as anti-competitive practices committed by the [France family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_family).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] On December 18, both teams were granted a motion of preliminary injunction, allowing them to race as chartered entries in 2025 while continuing their legal battle with NASCAR. The injunction also allowed the transfer of the two [Stewart–Haas Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart%E2%80%93Haas_Racing) charters to both teams.[^cite_note-12] On June 5, 2025, the U.S. Court of Appeals overturned the preliminary injunction ruling.[^cite_note-13] On August 25, NASCAR filed a legal notice of its agreement to issue one of the charters to a [redacted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redaction) entity.[^cite_note-14] On December 11, 2025, after eight days in court, the case was settled.[^cite_note-15]

## NASCAR Cup Series

### Car No. 23 history

Bubba Wallace (2021–present)



Wallace's No. 23 car during the [2021 Toyota/Save Mart 350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Toyota/Save_Mart_350)

On September 21, 2020, Jordan and [Denny Hamlin](2148196.md) announced the formation of a Cup team to begin competition in 2021 with Jordan as majority owner, Hamlin as minority owner, and Wallace, who eleven days prior had announced his departure from Richard Petty Motorsports, as their first official driver.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The team bought a guaranteed starting spot in every 2021 race by purchasing a charter from [Germain Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germain_Racing).[^cite_note-18] The team's name, car number and manufacturer were not revealed at the time of the announcement.[^cite_note-19] Hamlin later said that the two had discussed owning a team but that it never came to fruition.[^cite_note-20] On October 22, the team announced its name, 23XI Racing, and would use the number 23 (Jordan's number during his basketball career).[^cite_note-Long-3][^cite_note-21] On October 30, the team revealed that they will run [Toyotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) with a technical alliance with [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md) (whose No. 11 car is driven by Hamlin) and operate out of a shop owned (and formerly used) by [Germain Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germain_Racing).[^cite_note-23XI_JGR-4] On December 14, 23XI Racing announced that [DoorDash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoorDash), [McDonald's](2480627.md), [Columbia Sportswear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Sportswear), [Dr Pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Pepper), and [Root, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root,_Inc.) will be the sponsors of the No. 23 team in 2021. It was also announced that several crew members of the former Germain operation would join the team as a pit crew.[^cite_note-22] On February 3, it was announced that former Germain driver [Ty Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Dillon) (who attempted to make the Daytona 500 with fellow Toyota team [Gaunt Brothers Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaunt_Brothers_Racing)) would run the car in the [2021 Busch Clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Busch_Clash), as Wallace was not eligible to run the clash.[^cite_note-23]

Wallace finished second in his Duel and finished seventeenth at the [2021 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Daytona_500) after being involved in a last-lap crash. At the Daytona road course he had troubles all day and finished 26th. Wallace struggled out the gate until Phoenix, which was his first strong run of the season. Wallace was running top-fifteen for most of the day before working his way into the top-ten. Following a caution and most of the field pitting, while Wallace stayed out, he restarted first. This move did not work out well for Wallace. Due to him being on old tires and the rest of the field being on new tires, Wallace was quickly cycled out of the first position, dropped back, and wound up making contact with [Cole Custer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_Custer). He ended up finishing 16th.[^cite_note-24] At the [2021 GEICO 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_GEICO_500) at Talladega, Wallace had a strong car the entire day. Wallace was running top-ten for a good portion of the day and ended up winning stage 2. This was Wallace's first career stage win. After an overtime restart, Wallace was cycled to the back of the pack and wound up finishing nineteenth. At Dover, Wallace finished eleventh. At Pocono, Wallace finished the first race in fourteenth, but finished the second race fifth, scoring the team's best finish and their first top-five. He finished third at the [2021 Coke Zero Sugar 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Coke_Zero_Sugar_400) at Daytona, but he was officially scored with a second place finish after [Chris Buescher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Buescher) was disqualified post-inspection.[^cite_note-25] On September 16, [Bootie Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootie_Barker) was named crew chief for the remainder of the season after Wheeler was promoted to Director of Competition.[^cite_note-26] Wallace scored his first career win at the rain-shortened [2021 YellaWood 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_YellaWood_500) at Talladega, becoming only the second Black driver after [Wendell Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Scott) to win a Cup race.[^cite_note-27] Wallace finished the season 21st in points.

Wallace began the [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NASCAR_Cup_Series) by finishing in second place at the [2022 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Daytona_500).[^cite_note-28] On March 29, 2022, Barker was suspended for four races due to a tire and wheel loss during the [2022 Texas Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Texas_Grand_Prix) at [COTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_of_the_Americas). [Dave Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Rogers_\(NASCAR\)) was announced as Wallace's crew chief for Richmond, Martinsville, Bristol, and Talladega.[^cite_note-29] Later in the regular season, Wallace showed strong form, posting four consecutive top-10s at [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ambetter_301), [Pocono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_M\&M's_Fan_Appreciation_400), [Indianapolis road course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Verizon_200_at_the_Brickyard), and [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FireKeepers_Casino_400).[^cite_note-30]

After failing to make the driver playoffs at Daytona, it was announced that [Ty Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Gibbs) would switch to the No. 23 to allow Wallace to compete in the owners championship with the No. 45. At the [Texas playoff race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Autotrader_EchoPark_Automotive_500), Gibbs veered into [Ty Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Dillon) on pit road, nearly sending Dillon towards a NASCAR official and a group of pit crew members; he was subsequently fined US\$75,000 and the No. 23 was docked 25 owner points.[^cite_note-31] At Phoenix, [Daniel Hemric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hemric) substituted for Gibbs after the death of his father, [Coy Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coy_Gibbs).[^cite_note-32] Hemric finished seventeenth.[^cite_note-33]

Wallace started the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NASCAR_Cup_Series) with a twentieth place finish at the [2023 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Daytona_500). He improved his finishes enough to make the playoffs for the first time in his career.[^cite_note-34] Wallace was eliminated from the Round of 12 at the conclusion of the [Charlotte Roval race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Bank_of_America_Roval_400).[^cite_note-35]

During the [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NASCAR_Cup_Series), Wallace was fined US\$50,000 for intentionally door-slamming [Alex Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bowman) during the cool-down lap after the [Chicago street race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Grant_Park_165).[^cite_note-36] Following the [Martinsville playoff race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Xfinity_500), the No. 23 was docked fifty owner and driver points and Wallace and the team were each fined US\$100,000 for race manipulation, when Wallace faked a tire failure and slowed down to allow fellow Toyota driver [Christopher Bell](47313650.md) to pass him in an attempt to make the Championship 4. In addition, Barker was suspended for the [Phoenix finale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NASCAR_Cup_Series_Championship_Race).[^cite_note-37]

Wallace started the [2025 season](77079684.md) with a 29th place DNF at the [2025 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Daytona_500). He won at [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Brickyard_400) to break a 100-race winless streak and make the playoffs.[^cite_note-38]

### Car No. 45 history

See also: [Kurt Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Busch) and [Tyler Reddick](41108702.md)



[Kurt Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Busch) in the No. 45 at [Auto Club Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Club_Speedway) in 2022

Kurt Busch (2022)

On August 27, 2021, 23XI Racing announced that [Kurt Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Busch) will pilot the No. 45 Toyota Camry in 2022 (With the number 45 being a reference to a jersey number Jordan wore during his baseball career and for 23 games during the [1994–95 NBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_NBA_season).)[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] His long time sponsor [Monster Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Energy) would follow him over. Following the end of the 2021 season, 23XI purchased the former [StarCom Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCom_Racing)'s charter for US\$13.5 million, making it the most expensive charter purchase since the implementation of the system in 2016.[^cite_note-44]

Busch began his [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NASCAR_Cup_Series) with a nineteenth place finish at the [2022 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Daytona_500). Despite a promising start with top-five finishes at [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ruoff_Mortgage_500) and [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Folds_of_Honor_QuikTrip_500), he suffered from poor finishes at [COTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Texas_Grand_Prix), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Toyota_Owners_400), [Bristol dirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Food_City_Dirt_Race), [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_DuraMAX_Drydene_400), and [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Goodyear_400). However, he rebounded with a win at [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AdventHealth_400).[^cite_note-45] At the [Pocono race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_M\&M's_Fan_Appreciation_400), Busch was not medically cleared after a crash during qualifying, and subsequently missed the last five races of the regular season.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Xfinity Series driver [Ty Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Gibbs) substituted Busch for the next six races. He finished sixteenth at Pocono and seventeenth at [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Verizon_200_at_the_Brickyard).[^cite_note-50] A 10th-place finish at [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FireKeepers_Casino_400) earned Gibbs his first top-10 finish.[^cite_note-51] The following week at [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Federated_Auto_Parts_400), Gibbs finished 36th, last due to an engine failure.[^cite_note-52] Gibbs finished 26th at [Watkins Glen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Go_Bowling_at_The_Glen). On August 25, Busch announced he withdrew his request for a playoff waiver.[^cite_note-53]

Six days later, it was announced that [Bubba Wallace](35761776.md) would swap rides with Gibbs as the No. 45 entry was still eligible for the owner's championship.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Wallace finished ninth at [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Cook_Out_Southern_500) to open the playoffs. He scored his second career win at [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Hollywood_Casino_400).[^cite_note-56] On October 16, Busch announced he would step away from full-time competition in 2023, with the possibility of running the season on a part-time basis.[^cite_note-57] At [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_Point_400), [Kyle Larson](36163081.md) charged aggressively past [Kevin Harvick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Harvick) and Wallace, causing Wallace to scrape the outside wall. Wallace retaliated with a right rear hook on Larson, wrecking both cars down the frontstretch and severely damaging [Christopher Bell](47313650.md)'s car in the process. During the caution, Wallace engaged in a shoving match with Larson.[^cite_note-58] Wallace was suspended for one race for the incident; [John Hunter Nemechek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hunter_Nemechek) was assigned to drive the No. 45 at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Dixie_Vodka_400).[^cite_note-59]

Tyler Reddick (2023–present)



[Tyler Reddick](41108702.md) in the No. 45 at [Sonoma Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoma_Raceway) in 2023.

On October 15, 2022, 23XI Racing announced that it bought out the remainder of [Tyler Reddick](41108702.md)'s contract from [Richard Childress Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Childress_Racing) to replace Kurt Busch in the No. 45 for the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NASCAR_Cup_Series). Reddick originally signed with 23XI for 2024, but Busch's concussion and subsequent retirement from full-time competition led to this move.[^cite_note-60]

Reddick started the 2023 season with a 39th place DNF at the [2023 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Daytona_500). A month later, he scored his first win of the season at [COTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_EchoPark_Automotive_Grand_Prix) in triple overtime.[^cite_note-61] On May 16, NASCAR docked the No. 45 team ten owner and driver points for unapproved ballasts during inspection prior to qualifying at [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Goodyear_400).[^cite_note-62] During the playoffs, Reddick won at [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hollywood_Casino_400) to advance to the Round of 12.[^cite_note-63]

Reddick started the [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NASCAR_Cup_Series) with a 29th place DNF at the [2024 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Daytona_500). He scored his first win of the season at [Talladega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_GEICO_500) by overtaking [Brad Keselowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Keselowski) on the final stretch after Keselowski spun [Michael McDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McDowell_\(racing_driver\)) and triggered a multi-car crash.[^cite_note-64] He scored his second win of the season at [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FireKeepers_Casino_400).[^cite_note-65] Following the [Southern 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cook_Out_Southern_500), Reddick clinched the regular season championship, beating [Kyle Larson](36163081.md) by just one point.[^cite_note-66] During the playoffs, he locked himself into the Championship 4 by winning at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Straight_Talk_Wireless_400).[^cite_note-67]

Reddick started the [2025 season](77079684.md) with a runner-up finish at the [2025 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Daytona_500). Despite having no wins, he stayed consistent enough to make the playoffs until he was eliminated after the Round of 12, finishing ninth in the points standings.

Reddick began the [2026 season](79747972.md) by winning the [2026 Daytona 500](81427567.md), as well as [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Autotrader_400) and [COTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_DuraMAX_Texas_Grand_Prix), becoming the first driver in Cup Series history to win the first three races in a season.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] In addition, Reddick won at [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Goodyear_400) and [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_AdventHealth_400), becoming the first driver since [Dale Earnhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Earnhardt) in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series) to score five of the first nine races in a season.[^cite_note-70]

### Car No. 50 history



Heim at the [2024 Ally 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ally_400).

On March 13, 2024, it was announced that Kobayashi would once again compete in a race for the team, this time at [COTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_EchoPark_Automotive_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-71] The car number would race as the No. 50 instead of the No. 67 to celebrate the 50th anniversary of sponsor [Mobil 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobil_1). The team planned to run the No. 50 for two more races during the season with different drivers.[^cite_note-72]

On May 10, 2024, 23XI Racing announced that Corey Heim would drive the No. 50 Mobil 1 Toyota Camry at Nashville Superspeedway.[^cite_note-73] Heim finished 29th after crashing out in one of five overtimes.

On August 2, 2024, the team announced that [Juan Pablo Montoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pablo_Montoya) would make his NASCAR Cup Series return for 23XI at Watkins Glen on September 15.[^cite_note-74] This would be the 3rd and final start for the No. 50 Mobil 1 entry in 2024. Montoya finished 32nd in his return to stock car racing.

### Car No. 67 history

Part-time (2023–present)



[Corey Heim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Heim) performing a burnout after winning the [2026 Anduril 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Anduril_250)

On January 17, 2023, 23XI announced that [Travis Pastrana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Pastrana) would attempt to enter the No. 67 Toyota Camry for the [2023 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Daytona_500) with sponsorship coming from [Black Rifle Coffee Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Rifle_Coffee_Company).[^cite_note-75] On February 14, Pastrana made the entry field by scoring the second fastest lap among the non-chartered teams.[^cite_note-76] He suffered an accident in the qualifying duel and started in the back for the race.[^cite_note-77] Pastrana ended up finishing eleventh in the race.[^cite_note-78] On June 7, it was announced that [Kamui Kobayashi](9537340.md) would make his Cup debut in the No. 67 at the [Indianapolis Motor Speedway Road Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Verizon_200_at_the_Brickyard), he would end up finishing 33rd in the race after getting caught up in several wrecks.[^cite_note-79]

On February 23, 2025, 23XI announced that [Corey Heim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Heim) signed a developmental deal that includes Cup races in the No. 67, and Xfinity races in the [Sam Hunt Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hunt_Racing) No. 24 car.[^cite_note-80]

During the [2026 season](79747972.md), Heim scored his first career win at [Naval Base Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Anduril_250).[^cite_note-81] A month later, he scored his second win at [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Brickyard_400).[^cite_note-82]
