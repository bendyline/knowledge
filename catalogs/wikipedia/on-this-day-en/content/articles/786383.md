The **Indian Coast Guard** (**ICG**) is a [maritime law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_law_enforcement) and [search and rescue](172599.md) agency for India with jurisdiction over its [territorial waters](496983.md), including its contiguous zone and [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_India). It was started in February 1977 and formally established in August 1978 by the *Coast Guard Act, 1978* of the [Parliament of India](410238.md).[^cite_note-act-6] It operates under the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)).[^cite_note-mod-depts-7]

The ICG works in close cooperation with the [Indian Navy](604522.md), the [Department of Fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Fisheries,_Animal_Husbandry_and_Dairying), the [Department of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(India\)) [(Customs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Customs_services), and the Coastal Police of the [State Police Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_forces_of_the_states_and_union_territories_of_India), and the [Central Armed Police Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Armed_Police_Forces).

## History

The establishment of the Indian Coast Guard was first proposed by the [Indian Navy](604522.md) to provide non-military maritime services to the nation.[^cite_note-icg-mh-8] In the 1960s, sea-borne smuggling of goods was threatening to destroy India's domestic economy. The Indian Customs Department frequently called upon the Indian Navy for assistance with patrol and interception in the anti-smuggling effort.

The [Nagchaudhuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basanti_Dulal_Nagchaudhuri) Committee was constituted with participation from the Indian Navy and the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) to study the problem. In August 1971, the committee identified the requirement to patrol India's vast coastline, set up a registry of offshore fishing vessels to identify illegal activity, and establish a capable and well-equipped force to intercept vessels engaged in illegal activities. The committee also looked at the number and nature of the equipment, infrastructure, and personnel required to provide those services.[^cite_note-icg-mh-8]

Indian Coast Guard promotional movie launched on the eve of 46th raising day

By 1973, India had started a programme to acquire the equipment and started deputing personnel from the Indian Navy for these anti-smuggling and law enforcement tasks, under the provisions of the [Maintenance of Internal Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maintenance_of_Internal_Security_Act). The Indian Navy sensed that the [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) nature of these duties diverged from its core mission as a military service. [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(India\)) [Sourendra Nath Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourendra_Nath_Kohli), then [Chief of Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Staff_\(India\)), made a recommendation to the [Defence Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Secretary_\(India\)), outlining the need for a separate maritime service to undertake those duties and offering the Navy's assistance in its establishment. In August 1974, the Defence Secretary submitted a note to the [Cabinet Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_\(India\)) proposing cabinet action on Admiral Kohli's recommendation.

As a result, in September 1974, the Indian cabinet set up the *Rustamji Committee*, under the chairmanship of [Khusro Faramurz Rustamji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khusro_Faramurz_Rustamji), with participation from the Navy, the [Air Force](403355.md) and the Department of Revenue to examine gaps in security and law enforcement between the roles of the Indian Navy and the central and state police forces. The discovery of oil off [Bombay High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_High) further emphasised the need for a maritime law enforcement and protection service.[^cite_note-icg-mh-8]

In July 1975, the committee submitted its recommendation for the establishment of the Indian Coast Guard under the Ministry of Defence. Bureaucratic wrangling followed, with the Cabinet Secretary making a recommendation to place the service under the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_\(India\)). Then [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister) [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi) overruled the Cabinet Secretary and decided to accept the original recommendation of the Rustamji Committee to place the service under the Ministry of Defence.[^cite_note-icg-mh-8]

In February 1977, an interim Indian Coast Guard came into being, equipped with two small [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) and five [patrol boats](536773.md) transferred from the Navy. The duties and functions of the service were defined in the *Coast Guard Act*, which was passed by India's parliament in August 1978 and came into immediate effect.[^cite_note-act-6]

[Vice Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Admiral_\(India\)) [V. A. Kamath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._A._Kamath) of the Indian Navy was appointed the founding Director-General. Prime Minister [Morarji Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morarji_Desai) inspected the Guard of Honour at the service's inauguration. In 1984, Vice Admiral Kamath proposed a five-year plan to develop the ICG into a potent force. The full potential of this plan was not immediately realised due to an economic resource crunch.[^cite_note-icg-mh-8]

One of the historic operational successes of the ICG occurred in October 1999, with the recapture at high seas of a Panamanian-registered Japanese cargo ship, *MV Alondra Rainbow*, hijacked off Indonesia. Her crew was rescued off [Phuket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuket_\(city\)), Thailand. The ship had been repainted as MV *Mega Rama*, and was spotted off [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi), heading towards Pakistan. She was chased by *ICGS Tarabai* and [INS *Prahar* (K98)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Prahar) of the Indian Navy and apprehended.[^cite_note-saa-alondra-9] It was the first successful prosecution of armed pirates in over a century.



Indian Coast Guard ship and helicopter during the Search and Rescue Workshop and Exercise (SAREX), 2014

The Indian Coast Guard conducts exercises with the other coast guards of the world. In May 2005, the ICG agreed to establish liaison links with the [Pakistan Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Maritime_Security_Agency) (PMSA). In 2006, the Indian Coast Guard conducted exercises with its [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard) and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea_Coast_Guard) counterparts.

After the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks) in which the terrorists entered India from Pakistan via the sea route, the Indian government initiated a program to expand the ICG force, assets, and infrastructure for enhanced protection and surveillance of Indian waters.

The force aims to have 200 ships and 100 twin-engined aircraft by 2030 in its fleet. As of January 2025, ICG deploys 55 to 60 ships and 10 to 12 aircraft daily.[^cite_note-10]

### Current role

The Indian Coast Guard's motto is "वयम रक्षामः" (*Vayam Rakshamah*), which translates from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) as *"We Protect"*.

Missions of Indian Coast Guard:[^cite_note-icg-mission-11]

- Safety and protection of artificial islands, offshore terminals, and other installations

- Protection and assistance to fishermen and mariners at sea

- Preservation and protection of [marine ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ecology) and environment including pollution control

- Assistance to the Department of Customs and other authorities in anti-smuggling operations

- Law enforcement in territorial as well as [international waters](394357.md)

- Scientific data collection and support

- National defence during hostilities (under the operational control of the Indian Navy)

Additional responsibilities of the Indian Coast Guard:[^cite_note-12]

### Leadership and organisation

Main article: [Director General of the Indian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_General_of_the_Indian_Coast_Guard)



26th Director General of Indian Coast Guard Paramesh Sivamani

The Indian Coast Guard organisation is headed by the [Director-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_General_of_Indian_Coast_Guard) (DG ICG), who is located at Coast Guard Headquarters (CGHQ), New Delhi. At CGHQ, he is assisted by the Additional Director General Coast Guard (ADGCG) of the rank of ADG, four Deputy Director-Generals of the rank of [Inspector-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector-general_of_police), and other senior officers heading staff divisions.[^cite_note-13]

Director General Paramesh Sivamani is the 26th Director General of the Indian Coast Guard. The rank of Director General is equivalent to a [Vice Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral_\(India\)) of [Indian Navy](604522.md).[^cite_note-14]

The Indian Coast Guard has the Western and Eastern Seaboard, both commanded by [three-star officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-star_rank) designated Coast Guard Commander Western Seaboard and Coast Guard Commander Eastern Seaboard. The seaboards are divided into four regions. A fifth region, the Andaman & Nicobar Region, reports directly to the DGICG.&#x20;

Each region is headed by an officer of the rank of [Inspector-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector-general_of_police). Each of the regions is further divided into multiple districts, typically covering a coastal [state or a union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India).

### Coast Guard officers



&#x20;The Coast Guard Marching Contingent passes through the [Rajpath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajpath), on the occasion of the 68th Republic Day Parade 2017

The naming of ranks of officers in the Coast Guard is as same as the rank of [Central Armed Police Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Armed_Police_Forces). Officers are appointed in the Coast Guard in one of four branches, as either a General-Duty officer, Pilot officer, Technical officer, or Law officer. Lady Officers have two branches, i.e., General-Duty Officer or Pilot Officer, and serve on shore establishments/Air Stations/Headquarters. They are not deployed on board Indian Coast Guard ships.

Officers of the Indian Coast Guard undergo Basic Military Training at the [Indian Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Naval_Academy), [Ezhimala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezhimala), along with their counterparts of the Indian Navy. This helps in the mutual interchange of Officers among these two sister services. While the [Indian Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Coast_Guard_Academy) is under construction in [Mangaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangaluru), [Dakshina Kannada district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kannada_district), [Karnataka](16880.md).[^cite_note-hindu-cga-40]

General-Duty Officers

The command of ships at sea can only be exercised by officers of the General Duty (GD) branch. The key functions of a General-Duty Officer would be to operate weapons, sensors, and different kinds of equipment on board a ship. The safety of the ship and the men would be the GD officers' responsibility. All the District Commanders (COMDIS) and Commander of Coast Guard Region (COMCG) appointments are exercised by a GD Officer of the Indian Coast Guard.

Pilot Officers

Pilot Officers are also part of the GD branch. A Pilot Officer gets an opportunity to work at shore Air Stations along the Indian coasts and also on board ships. ICG operates fixed-wing aircraft for [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance) of the [Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_India). Helicopters are embarked on Coast Guard Offshore Patrol Vessels (OPV) to provide local surveillance and perform [search and rescue](172599.md) mission at sea.

Technical Officers

Technical Officers are responsible for the operation of advanced technology and sensor systems on board Coast Guard vessels and aircraft, as well as on shore installations. They also command the maintenance wings of the force.

Law Officers

Law Officers act as legal advisers to their respective commanders. They represent the Indian Coast Guard in legal actions filed by or against the organisation. They also perform the duties of trial law officers in Coast Guard courts, convened to try delinquent Coast Guard personnel. The Directorate of Law at Coast Guard Headquarters is headed by a Deputy Inspector-General and is designated as the Chief Law Officer. Section 115 of the Coast Guard Act, 1978, deals with the qualifications necessary to be appointed as the Chief Law Officer of the Indian Coast Guard. Section 116 of the Coast Guard Act, 1978, defines the functions of the Chief Law Officer.[^cite_note-act-6]

### Enrolled personnel

Enrolled personnel in the Coast Guard serve as either a *yantrik* (technician) or *navik* (sailor).[^cite_note-icg-jobs-41]

- *Yantrik*s responsible for operating and maintaining mechanical, electrical, or aeronautical equipment and systems on board the Coast Guard vessels and aircraft.

- *Navik*s may further serve in the General-Duty or Domestic branches.&#x20;

  - The General-Duty *navik*s serves as sailors, weapons systems operators, communication specialists, divers, etc., or in specific maritime or aviation support roles.

  - The Domestic branch's navik serves in roles such as stewards, cooks, etc., on board Coast Guard vessels.

Enrolled personnel of the Indian Coast Guard are trained along with Indian Naval sailors at the naval training establishment [INS Chilka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Chilka). All training undertaken by Coast Guard personnel is the same as that undertaken by sailors in the Indian Navy. All personnel are trained in the operation of weapons systems in cases of emergency.

## Equipment



[Dhruv MK.3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_Dhruv) helicopters flying alongside [ICGS Sajag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarth-class_patrol_vessel).

Main article: [List of equipment of the Indian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Indian_Coast_Guard)

In November 2024, the Indian Coast Guard had a strength of 182 ships and 78 aircraft. It plans to have 200 ships and 100 aircraft by 2030. At any given time, 55 to 60 surface platforms and 10 to 12 aircraft are deployed on patrol daily.[^cite_note-\:4-43][^cite_note-44]

## Future of the Indian Coast Guard



Bharatiya Tatrakshak Swarnim Stamp

Main article: [List of future equipment of the Indian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_future_equipment_of_the_Indian_Coast_Guard)

The Indian Coast Guard expects to become a leading player in [maritime security](8111405.md) around the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). With a target set to triple its surface fleet strength by the turn of this decade, ICG has placed orders for 131 surveillance platforms that are already under construction in [Indian shipyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_shipbuilders_and_shipyards) and is in the process of signing contracts for building another 200 platforms.[^cite_note-45] Contracts have been signed for state-of-the-art [air cushion vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovercraft) and advanced communication systems [^cite_note-46]

The Indian Coast Guard was allotted ₹9,676.7 crore (US\$1.0 billion) under the Capital and Revenue Head of the [Union Budget 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Union_budget_of_India), which is a 26.5% increase over previous years. The funds will be used for the acquisition of more [ALH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_Dhruv), [Dornier Do 228](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_228), fast patrol vessels (FPVs), training ships, and interceptor boats to augment current capacity.[^cite_note-47]
