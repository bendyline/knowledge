# 2025 Indiana redistricting attempt

See also: [2025–2026 United States redistricting](81439539.md)



Indiana's current congressional districts map since 2023\
[Interactive map version](81781219.md)

Between October and December 2025, [Indiana lawmakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly) considered redrawing the [state's congressional districts](4006882.md) to eliminate [Democratic](5043544.md) representation in [the U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md). The push followed calls from President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), and after other states, including [Texas](80621571.md), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_California_Proposition_50), [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Virginia_redistricting_amendment), and [Missouri](80917394.md), sought to [gerrymander](42223515.md) their [congressional districts](449478.md). The proposal was rejected in the [State Senate](4964463.md) on December 11.

In 2026, eight out of the nine Republicans who voted against redistricting retired or lost renomination.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Background

In 2025, after [Texas adopted a new congressional map](80621571.md), California, in response, [adopted their own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_California_Proposition_50).[^cite_note-2]

On October 10, 2025, Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md) met in [Indianapolis](57707.md) with Republican state legislators to discuss the "pros and cons" of redistricting.[^cite_note-3]

## Proposed legislation

A bill passed by the [State House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_House_of_Representatives) sought to amend Indiana's congressional districts to foster in more [Republican](32070.md) support. The legislation, which was passed by the House on December 5, 2025,[^cite_note-4] [cracked](12987.md#Tactics) Indiana's [1st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana's_1st_congressional_district) and [7th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana's_7th_congressional_district) districts. The 7th, which includes [Indianapolis](57707.md), was split between four different districts in the proposed map.[^cite_note-5]

The proposal ran into opposition in the Republican-[supermajority](638313.md) [Senate](4964463.md), with over a dozen Republican Senators voicing opposition to the move ahead of the vote. In an attempt to pressure holdouts to vote in favor of redrawing the map, Trump vowed to back primary challengers to all Republicans who vote against it.[^cite_note-7] Independent of these actions, a number of Senators were targets of threats of violence against them ahead of the vote, including [bomb threats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_threat) and [swatting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swatting) calls.[^cite_note-8] The bill failed on December 11, 2025, after opposition votes came from 21 Republicans and all 10 Democrats.[^cite_note-9]

## Impact

With the bill's failure, Indiana's congressional map will remain the same for the [2026 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Indiana), allowing both incumbent Democratic congressmen much easier paths to re-election. Republican [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Indiana) [Mike Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Braun) has vowed to assist Trump in primarying the Republican Senators who voted against redistricting in the [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Indiana_Senate_election) and 2028 primaries.[^cite_note-10]

In 2026, 5 out of the 7 Republicans who voted against redistricting lost renomination. [^cite_note-11]
