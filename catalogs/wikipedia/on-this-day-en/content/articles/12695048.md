The **2028 Summer Olympics**, officially the **Games of the XXXIV Olympiad** and commonly known as **Los Angeles 2028** or **LA28**, is an upcoming international [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) scheduled to take place from July 14 to 30, 2028, in the United States. Los Angeles will be the [host city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Olympic_Games_host_cities), with various events also scheduled to be held at other cities spread across the [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) area,[^cite_note-4] plus two subsites in [Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City).[^cite_note-venueplan-5][^cite_note-venueplan2-6]

Los Angeles had originally [bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_bid_for_the_2024_Summer_Olympics) for the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics). Following multiple withdrawals, the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC) approved a process to concurrently award the 2024 and 2028 Games to Los Angeles and [Paris](22989.md) as the two remaining candidates; Paris was preferred as host for 2024, while Los Angeles agreed to host in 2028. Los Angeles was formally awarded the Games at the [131st IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/131st_IOC_Session) in [Lima](85423.md), Peru, on September 13, 2017. They will mark the fifth Summer Olympics, and ninth Olympics overall to be hosted by the United States. Los Angeles has previously hosted the [Summer Olympics](27566.md) in [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Summer_Olympics) and [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics), and will become the third three-time host city after [London](17867.md) ([1908](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics), [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Summer_Olympics), [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics)) and Paris ([1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics), [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics), [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics)), and the first non-European city to do so. These will be the first Summer Olympics held under the [IOC presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee) of [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry).

After debuting in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) as optional events, [skateboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarding_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [sport climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_climbing_at_the_Summer_Olympics), and [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing_at_the_Summer_Olympics) have been promoted to the Summer Olympics' core event program. The Games will feature the debut of [flag football](224471.md) and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)) as optional sports, joined by the return of [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Summer_Olympics)/[softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_at_the_Summer_Olympics) (for the first time since 1900) and [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse_at_the_Summer_Olympics) (for the first time as a medal event since 1908).

## Bidding process

Main article: [Bids for the 2024 and 2028 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2024_and_2028_Summer_Olympics)

On September 16, 2015, the International Olympic Committee announced the candidature process and the five candidate cities for the 2024 Games: [Budapest](36787.md), [Hamburg](13467.md), Los Angeles, Paris, and [Rome](25458.md).[^cite_note-7] Budapest, Hamburg, and Rome eventually withdrew, leaving only Los Angeles and Paris.[^cite_note-8] A similar situation had already occurred during the [bidding for the 2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2022_Winter_Olympics) when [Kraków](16815.md), [Lviv](49968.md), [Oslo](22309.md), and [Stockholm](26741.md) withdrew, resulting in a two-way decision between [Beijing](18603746.md), China, and [Almaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaty), Kazakhstan, with Beijing ultimately declared the winner. On April 3, 2017, at the IOC convention in Denmark, Olympic officials met with bid committees from Los Angeles and Paris to discuss the possibility of naming two winners in the competition to host the 2024 Summer Games.

After these withdrawals, the IOC executive board met in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland, on June 9, 2017, to discuss the 2024 and 2028 bid processes.[^cite_note-9] The IOC formally proposed electing the 2024 and 2028 host cities at the same time in 2017, a proposal that an Extraordinary IOC Session approved on July 11, 2017, in Lausanne. The IOC set up a process where the Los Angeles and Paris 2024 bid committees and the IOC held meetings in July 2017 to decide which city would host in each of the two years.[^cite_note-10]

Following the decision to award the 2024 and 2028 Games simultaneously, Paris was understood to be preferred for the 2024 Games, as it would also mark the centennial of the [1924 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics) in Paris.[^cite_note-11] On July 31, 2017, the IOC announced Los Angeles as the sole candidate for 2028, with \$1.8 billion of additional funding from the IOC,[^cite_note-LA_2028-12] allowing Paris to be confirmed as the host for 2024. On August 11, 2017, the [Los Angeles City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_City_Council) voted unanimously to approve the bid.[^cite_note-13] On September 11, 2017, Los Angeles received formal approval from the IOC's evaluation commission.[^cite_note-14] On September 13, 2017, Los Angeles was formally awarded the 2028 Games following a unanimous vote by the IOC.[^cite_note-15] IOC president [Thomas Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bach) praised the LA bid for using a record-breaking number of existing and temporary facilities and for relying entirely on private funding.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

On October 16, 2017, LA 2028 received official endorsement from the state of [California](5407.md).[^cite_note-18] On August 29, 2018, Olympic officials arrived for a two-day visit that included meetings with local organizers and a tour of the city's newest venues.[^cite_note-19] At the time, [LMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_Marymount_University) and *[LA Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* polls stated that more than 88% of [Angelenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Los_Angeles) were in favor of the city hosting the 2028 Olympic and Paralympic Games.[^cite_note-20] In March 2023, a poll conducted by Suffolk University and the *LA Times* indicated 57% of Angelenos believed the Olympics would be good for the city, while 20% indicated they thought it would have a negative impact.[^cite_note-21]

## Development and preparations

### Transportation

Further information: [Twenty-eight by '28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-eight_by_'28)



[K Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) train in [Inglewood](107640.md), a major events cluster



[D Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) train at [Wilshire/La Cienega station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire/La_Cienega_station) in [Beverly Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills).



[LA Metro local bus service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_bus_fleet#Bus_fleet)

Los Angeles civic leaders initially announced a plan to make the 2028 Olympics "car-free", aiming to reduce congestion, shorten travel times, and comply with security restrictions that limit parking near venues.[^cite_note-\:0-22][^cite_note-\:1-23] The [Twenty-eight by '28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-eight_by_'28) initiative, launched by former Mayor [Eric Garcetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Garcetti), aimed to complete 28 infrastructure projects before the Games.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Most projects were already in planning stages, but the initiative accelerated their completion. Funding comes from [Measure R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Los_Angeles_County_Measure_R) and [Measure M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_M), countywide sales tax measures.[^cite_note-LACountyElection-26] Mayor Karen Bass also promoted strategies such as encouraging remote work during the event to reduce demand on the transportation network.[^cite_note-LAtimesCarFree-27][^cite_note-28]

By March 2024, the original list of 28 projects was significantly revised, with 11 projects, including 5 rail line expansions, replaced by smaller projects considered more feasible to meet deadlines, including some projects that had already been completed.[^cite_note-29] By late 2024, observers expressed concern that only 5.2% of LA Metro's \$3.3 billion Olympic-related project list was funded, and several projects were running behind schedule. Officials also noted uncertainties related to government agency staffing, interagency coordination, and the late finalization of the Olympic venue list.[^cite_note-\:1-23] These challenges led civic leaders to pivot away from a fully "car-free" Games toward a "transit-first" approach.[^cite_note-\:0-22][^cite_note-30]

#### Rail projects

Several major rail expansions are underway to support Olympic transportation. The [D Line extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D_Line_Extension) is being completed in three phases: the first phase extending service from [Wilshire/Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire/Western_station) to [Wilshire/La Cienega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire/La_Cienega_station) was completed in May 2026;[^cite_note-31] the second phase will continue to [Century City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_City_station) by late 2026; and the third phase will reach the [West Los Angeles VA Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westwood/VA_Hospital_station) by 2027, including [a station near UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westwood/UCLA_station) that will connect the Olympic Village to downtown venues.[^cite_note-thesource_2018-02-23-32][^cite_note-33] Construction began in 2014 and remains largely on schedule.[^cite_note-thesource_2018-02-23-32][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

The [Regional Connector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Connector), completed in 2023, links Metro's light rail lines in downtown Los Angeles, providing easier transfers between lines that serve Long Beach, the San Gabriel Valley, and Santa Monica, all of which will host Olympic competition venues.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-MetroProjectSite-37] The [K Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)), which opened in October 2022 after multiple delays,[^cite_note-38] connects the Crenshaw District, Inglewood, and Westchester, and will connect with [a people mover at LAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyLink_\(Los_Angeles_International_Airport\)), which is projected to open in January 6, 2027 after several postponements.[^cite_note-Harjai_2025-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

#### Bus fleet expansion

While many venues are located near existing rail lines, Los Angeles lacks the continuous, high-capacity subway system that supported past Olympic Games.[^cite_note-\:1-23] Consequently, buses are expected to serve as the primary mode of transportation for most spectators. Los Angeles Metro and Olympic organizers estimate that an additional 2,700 buses will be needed, effectively doubling the [current Metro fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_bus_fleet) of 2,320 vehicles. Metro has requested that transit agencies nationwide donate buses near the end of their usable life for temporary Olympic service, potentially adding hundreds of vehicles.[^cite_note-42] Costs are estimated at \$700 million to \$1 billion, and through the Vision 2028 plan, Metro is seeking federal grants and coordinating post-Games resale of buses to other cities.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] In February 2026, Metro announced that Congress has approved \$94.3 million in mobility-related funding, including service planning, station experience enhancements, development of mobility hubs, light rail improvements, and planning and design for pedestrian access near Games venues as part of the Games Enhanced Transit System (GETS).[^cite_note-46]

### Budget

Building on the legacy of the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics–the first to generate a profit through innovative corporate sponsorship and private funding–the 2028 Los Angeles Games are heavily relying on a similar, fully private financial model.[^cite_note-NYTimes-47][^cite_note-LATimes-48] In April 2019, the estimated cost of the Games was assessed as being approximately \$6.88 billion, with all the money coming from the private sector. The organizers adjusted the budget for inflation after LA, which originally bid for the 2024 Games, agreed to wait four more years.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-California_Legislative_Analyst's_Office,_March_2017-50]

The City of Los Angeles is the lead public guarantor. As of August 12 2026, the city finalized the Enhanced City Resources Master Agreement (ECRMA) with LAOCOG committing to provide \$270 million in money upfront for costs over runs. The city also committed an additional \$270 million to cover shortfalls, once the initial up-front \$270 is spent. The California legislature has empowered the governor to negotiate the next \$250 million in public backup. The Games are expected to generate as much as they cost, with \$2.5 billion coming in through sponsorships and nearly \$2 billion earned through ticket sales. Average sport ticket prices for the Games have ranged between \$28 in most sports and \$3,350 for the mens basketball final.[^cite_note-51] Over \$2 billion in sponsorships were secured by late 2025 per the OCOG, surpassing the [2024 Paris Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) more than two years early.[^cite_note-LATimes-48] The city councils concern in the ECRMA is the high cost for security. LAPD reports they will spent approximately \$1.1 billion in policing alone. LA28 OCOG reports they are contingent on promises made by the Trump Administration on providing funding. The city council reported they cannot take the risk on the federal government not providing security funds and the OCOG not being clear on how the cost will be paid.[^cite_note-audacy-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-California_Legislative_Analyst's_Office,_March_2017-50]

### Security

Security will be handled by the U.S. federal government with an agreement signed by the [Los Angeles Organizing Committee for the 2028 Olympic and Paralympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Organizing_Committee_for_the_2028_Olympic_and_Paralympic_Games) (LAOCOG) and [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) in February 2020, but it will not be involved in the Games' funding, covering only the aforementioned security costs.[^cite_note-54]

The Olympics are the second to take place in the United States following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001 and the [Boston Marathon bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon_bombings) in 2013. Like the [2002 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics) in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) will designate the Olympics a [National Special Security Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Special_Security_Event) (NSSE). Due to this designation, the [United States Secret Service](58221.md) will be in charge of security and will head a single chain of command.[^cite_note-California_Legislative_Analyst's_Office,_March_2017-50]

[Casey Wasserman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Wasserman) has stated that there will be no issues with the federal government on obtaining visas for athletes, security and delivery at the [144th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144th_IOC_Session), emphasizing that they have received bipartisan support from all previous U.S. presidents, including [Donald Trump](4848272.md). LA28 officials met with then President-elect Trump at [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) in January 2025 and his administration pledged to help Los Angeles recover from the [wildfires](78831062.md), and hold "the greatest games".[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] In July 2025, \$1 billion in funding was earmarked for a [Homeland Security Grant Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Grant_Program) for federal security and planning costs as part of the [One Big Beautiful Bill Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act).[^cite_note-58] LAPD reports estimates they will spent approximately \$1.1 billion in policing alone. LAOCOG reports the funding for security is contingent on promises made by the Trump Administration on providing money. The city council reported they cannot take the risk on the federal government not providing security funds for LAPD costs and the OCOG not being clear on how the cost will be reimbursed.[^cite_note-audacy-52][^cite_note-59]

### Venues

Main article: [Venues of the 2028 Summer Olympics and Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venues_of_the_2028_Summer_Olympics_and_Paralympics)





The [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum) (*left*) and [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) (named "2028 Stadium" during the games) (*right*) will host the ceremonies, as well as the athletics and swimming events respectively.

Like the [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics), a majority of the 2028 Summer Olympics will be hosted in venues around the [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) area. The city's bid relied primarily on existing venues and venues that had already been under construction or were planned regardless of the Games. The majority of venues were divided into clusters known as "sports parks" (changed to "zones" during the planning phase), situated in [downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley), [Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson,_California) (at [California State University, Dominguez Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Dominguez_Hills)), and [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_California). No new permanent venues are being built specifically for the Games. Like the 1984 Summer Games, the [Olympic Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Village) will be situated on the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) campus, while the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) will host the Media Village.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

The [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum) will host [athletics](43719.md) and the [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)) will host [football (soccer)](10568.md), respectively.[^cite_note-gamesplan-64] Both will become the first stadiums to have ever hosted three different Olympic Games.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] [Crypto.com Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto.com_Arena) will host gymnastics, and [Intuit Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Dome) will host basketball.[^cite_note-venueplan-5][^cite_note-venueplan2-6] [BMO Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMO_Stadium), which opened in 2018 as the home of [Major League Soccer](71802.md)'s [Los Angeles FC](44255607.md), will host the flag football and lacrosse.[^cite_note-gamesplan-64] [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md), which opened in 2020 as the home of the [NFL](21211.md)'s [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) and [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers), will host swimming events.[^cite_note-swimming-67] [Riviera Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riviera_Country_Club) will host golf.[^cite_note-68]

The Coliseum underwent a major renovation and restoration program from 2017 to 2019.[^cite_note-69] A new press box, [loge boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_\(theatre\)), and club seats were installed.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Crypto.com Arena underwent renovations completed in 2024,[^cite_note-72] and the [NBA](22093.md)'s [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md) opened a new arena in [Inglewood](107640.md) known as [Intuit Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Dome) the same year.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Cricket will be hosted at the [Knight Riders Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Riders_Cricket_Ground) at the [Fairplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairplex) in [Pomona, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona,_California).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

To reduce costs and use more pre-existing facilities, LA28 announced changes to its venue plans in June 2024. The temporary aquatics venue that was scheduled to be built at USC's [Dedeaux Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedeaux_Field) was scrapped, in favor of holding swimming at SoFi Stadium and artistic swimming in Long Beach. [Diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_at_the_2028_Summer_Olympics) was originally set for the historic [LA84 Foundation/John C. Argue Swim Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA84_Foundation/John_C._Argue_Swim_Stadium) (which was built for the 1932 Games) but was changed in September 2025 to the Rose Bowl Aquatic Center in Pasadena due to cost and safety concerns.[^cite_note-77] With a planned configuration for 38,000 spectators, SoFi Stadium will be the largest swimming venue in Olympic history. Equestrian will return to [Santa Anita Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Anita_Park) once again, as Arcadia had previously hosted the event in 1984, while [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball) and [canoe slalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoe_slalom) were moved to existing venues in [Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City), [Oklahoma](22489.md), instead of building new venues in Los Angeles County.[^cite_note-venueplan-5][^cite_note-venueplan2-6][^cite_note-swimming-67]

In February 2026, the six preliminary association football (soccer) venues were announced. [New York City](645042.md), [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville), [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio), [St. Louis](27687.md), [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California) and [San Diego](28504.md) are the six cities outside of the LA area set to host football matches.[^cite_note-78] The match schedule, unveiled in March 2026, will allow the competition to progressively move east to west as teams advance to the final stages, minimizing travel demands. [Etihad Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Park) in New York City will open in July of 2027, one year prior to the Olympics.[^cite_note-79]

In July 2026, the [Knight Riders Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Riders_Cricket_Ground) in [Pomona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona,_California) opened. The cricket ground serves as the home of the [Los Angeles Knight Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Knight_Riders) of [Major League Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Cricket). It is the only new permanent venue in the LA area which was built ahead of the games. The venue will host the [T20 cricket competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_at_the_2028_Summer_Olympics) during the games.[^cite_note-80]

The [Rose Bowl Aquatics Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Aquatics_Center) will undergo renovations starting in April of 2027 to prepare it for the diving competition.[^cite_note-81]

### Olympic torch relay

In July 2025, LAOCOG CEO [Reynold Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynold_Hoover) informed the [California State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Senate) that the torch relay for LA 2028 planned to visit all 50 [U.S. states](18618239.md), explaining that it would "bind the nation with LA and California".[^cite_note-82] In September 2026, it was announced that the torch relay will begin in [Atlanta](3138.md), which hosted the last Summer Games in the U.S. in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics). From there, the flame will visit all 50 U.S. states, before finally arriving in California in time for the opening ceremony.[^cite_note-83]

### Tickets

An estimated 14 million tickets will be available to purchase for the Games, two million more than that of Paris 2024. 1 million tickets will start at \$28, with a third being under \$100. Registration for the first ticket lottery opened in January 2026, with selected fans being able to purchase tickets starting in March and the general public from 9th-19th of April. The second general public ticket drop was between August 10th-20th.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] As of the end of August 2026, the LAOCOG announced they have sold a record 6 million tickets after the second drop closed.[^cite_note-87]

### Ceremonies

The opening ceremonies will be held on July 14, 2028, and will be co-hosted by the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum and SoFi Stadium. The Coliseum will also host the closing ceremonies.[^cite_note-88] Filmmaker and media executive [Peter Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Rice_\(executive\)) was named "head of ceremonies and content" for LA 2028, overseeing the ceremonies for both the Olympics and Paralympics. Wasserman did not specifically state a planned budget, but said that he would provide Rice with any resources he needs. Referencing the [U.S. film and television industry](418334.md)'s status of an American cultural export, Wasserman explained that "when you think about what we expect of ourselves and what the world is going to expect of us, clearly we're the creative capital of the world and [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles) is a big piece of that, but we better knock people's socks off."[^cite_note-creative-89]

[Ben Winston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Winston) will serve as executive producer and creative director, alongside veteran ceremony producer Scott Givens; production will be overseen by FulFive, a joint venture between Winston and Given's respective companies [Fulwell 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulwell_73) and FiveCurrents.[^cite_note-creative2-90]

The concept of using both stadiums had been discussed by the Los Angeles bid committee as early as 2017; it proposed that a [prologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prologue) to the opening ceremony could be held at the Coliseum (culminating with the launch of the final leg of the torch relay to Inglewood) with the rest of the protocol being [simulcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulcast) from SoFi Stadium on screens, and the closing ceremony likewise beginning at SoFi Stadium and concluding at the Coliseum.[^cite_note-simultaneous_ceremonies-3]

### Sports



[Skateboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarding) *(pictured)*, [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing), and [sport climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_climbing) will be inducted into the core Olympic program, after being selected as discretionary sports at the previous two editions of the games.

The 2028 Summer Olympics is expected to include 351 medal events in 36 sports, an increase of 22 events and four sports over the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics);[^cite_note-womenathletes-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] this includes 31 mandatory "core" sports, and five optional sports that were proposed by LAOCOG to help improve local interest. The core program consists of the 28 core sports contested since [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics), along with three newly-added sports—skateboarding, [sport climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_climbing_at_the_Summer_Olympics), and [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing_at_the_Summer_Olympics)—which had been contested as optional sports in 2020 and 2024.[^cite_note-weightlifting-94][^cite_note-optionalsports-95][^cite_note-boxing-96][^cite_note-coresports-97] The five optional sports will include the Olympic debuts of [flag football](224471.md) (a non-contact variant of [gridiron football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridiron_football)) and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)), the return of [baseball](3850.md)/[softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball) for the first time since 2020, the return of [cricket](25675557.md) for the first time since 1900, and [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse_at_the_Summer_Olympics) for the first time since 1908.[^cite_note-new_sports-98][^cite_note-99]

Cricket will consist of men's and women's tournaments using the [Twenty20](770690.md) (T20) format, contested by six teams each.[^cite_note-100] Flag football will consist of men's and women's tournaments.[^cite_note-101] The [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) and [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) (CFL, whose regular season falls during the Olympics) both announced resolutions to facilitate participation by its professional players in the tournament, with the NFL's participation pending negotiations with the league's [players union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Players_Association).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Lacrosse will utilize the [lacrosse sixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse_sixes) format.[^cite_note-lacrosse-106]

#### New sports

Since 2020, the program of the Summer Olympics has consisted of mandatory core sports that persist between Games, and up to six optional sports proposed by the organizing committee in order to improve local interest.[^cite_note-Charter-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] On December 9, 2021, the IOC executive board proposed that skateboarding, sport climbing, and surfing, which all successfully debuted as optional sports at the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics), and returned in the same capacity in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics),[^cite_note-modernpentathlon-117][^cite_note-118] be promoted to the core program of the 2028 Summer Olympics to replace [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [modern pentathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_pentathlon_at_the_Summer_Olympics), and [weightlifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlifting_at_the_Summer_Olympics), which were provisionally dropped from the program pending the resolution of governance issues, with the IOC setting a deadline of 2023:[^cite_note-coresports-97]

- The [International Boxing Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boxing_Association) (IBA) was suspended by the IOC in 2019 for governance, financial, and corruption issues, which resulted in boxing at the [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics) Games being overseen by an external, IOC task force. Governance concerns increased following the election of [Umar Kremlev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_Kremlev) as its president in December 2020 due to his opposition to the independent appointment of judges and officials, and allegations of increasing Russian influence (including moving some of its operations to Russia, and his appointment of state-owned oil and gas company [Gazprom](320489.md) as the organization's main sponsor in 2021). The IBA had also stifled attempts by candidates to challenge Kremlev's presidency, and lifted an IOC-recommended ban on Russian and Belarusian athletes (in response to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)) in October 2022.

  [^cite_note-WaPo-119]

  &#x20;A competing governing body known as [World Boxing](72568701.md) was established in April 2023 by a consortium of national federations that had expressed concerns over the impact of the Russian invasion of Ukraine on the IBA, and the future of boxing as an Olympic event. In June 2023, the IOC executive board voted to strip the IBA of its recognition, citing a lack of progress since the original suspension.

  [^cite_note-theguardian20190522-120]

  [^cite_note-iocstripsiba-121]

  [^cite_note-122]

- The [International Weightlifting Federation](3228206.md) (IWF) faced issues with [doping](860860.md) and governance, resulting in weightlifting being considered "provisional" for the 2024 Summer Olympics.

  [^cite_note-theguardian20190522-120]

  &#x20;In 2022, the IWF elected Mohammed Hasan Jalood as its new president, and he oversaw reforms to the organization, including agreeing to delegate its drug testing to the [International Testing Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Testing_Agency) and to participate in the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport).

  [^cite_note-weightlifting-94]

  [^cite_note-optionalsports-95]

- The [Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Internationale_de_Pentathlon_Moderne) (UIPM) was seeking approval for its proposed replacement of show jumping in the modern pentathlon with [obstacle course racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstacle_course_racing);

  [^cite_note-modernpentathlon-117]

  [^cite_note-newmodernpentathlon-123]

  [^cite_note-124]

  [^cite_note-new_sports2-125]

  &#x20;the campaign to replace riding was motivated primarily by incidents during the [women's modern pentathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_pentathlon_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's) at the 2020 Summer Olympics, which saw German team coach [Kim Raisner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Raisner) disqualified after she hit a horse that did not follow the instructions of jockey [Annika Schleu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annika_Schleu).

  [^cite_note-126]

  &#x20;The UIPM ratified the changes in November 2022, and they are expected to officially take effect for senior competition after the 2024 Summer Olympics.

  [^cite_note-newmodernpentathlon-123]

In August 2022, LAOCOG shortlisted nine proposed sports for consideration as optional events for these Olympics: [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Summer_Olympics)/[softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [breaking (breakdance)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics), [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [flag football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_football_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics), [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_at_the_Summer_Olympics), and [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport).[^cite_note-127] On October 9, 2023, LAOCOG announced that they had officially submitted baseball/softball, cricket, flag football, lacrosse, and squash for consideration by the IOC.[^cite_note-new_sports2-125] All five sports were approved at the [141st IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/141st_IOC_Session) in Mumbai, India.[^cite_note-new_sports-98] The IOC also reinstated modern pentathlon and weightlifting for the core program, citing that the sports' governing bodies had made sufficient efforts in carrying out reforms.[^cite_note-weightlifting-94][^cite_note-optionalsports-95] The IOC withheld an immediate decision on boxing due to the IBA's expulsion and insufficient reach by World Boxing;[^cite_note-weightlifting-94][^cite_note-optionalsports-95] in February 2025, the IOC granted provisional recognition to World Boxing, citing its progress on reach and commitments to integrity.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

On March 20, 2025, at the [144th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144th_IOC_Session) in Greece, the IOC voted unanimously to approve a recommendation by the IOC executive board to reinstate boxing, with the event being sanctioned by World Boxing.[^cite_note-boxing-96]

Notwithstanding the approval of cricket by the IOC, USA Cricket is not currently certified as a national governing body by the [United States Olympic & Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_&_Paralympic_Committee) (USOPC), which would be required in order for the sport to be contested. The ICC has previously warned that USA Cricket's governance would need to be reformed in order to meet USOPC standards, and began a process to "reset" the organization to meet them.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

## Calendar

On July 13, 2025, the organizing committee published the first version of the competition schedule.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] On November 12, 2025, an updated comprehensive competition schedule by event was released, outlining when every medal event will take place during the upcoming Olympic Games in Los Angeles, with details across all confirmed sports and venues.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

In order to facilitate use of SoFi Stadium for both the opening ceremonies and swimming, the scheduling of swimming and athletics events will be reversed. This will be the first time since the [1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics) in [Mexico City](18987.md) that athletics competitions will take place in the first week of the Games.[^cite_note-venueplan-5][^cite_note-venueplan2-6]

## Marketing

### Branding



A reference version of the Games' emblem; the "A" in "LA" is interchanged with a variety of different designs.

On September 1, 2020, LAOCOG unveiled the emblem for the 2028 Summer Olympics, featuring the characters "LA" and "28" in a stacked layout. The "A" in "LA" was designed to be interchangeable, with numerous variations created in collaboration with athletes, artists, designers, and celebrities, including [Billie Eilish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Eilish), [Lilly Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilly_Singh), and [Reese Witherspoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reese_Witherspoon). Variants have also included designs referencing the [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics)' "Stars in Motion" emblem, a version featuring the flags of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_France) and the [United States](11447.md) used during the Los Angeles handover presentation at the [2024 closing ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics_closing_ceremony), and a "[Black Mamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_mamba)"-inspired design paying tribute to basketball player and two-time gold medalist [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant).[^cite_note-timeout-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] For the first time, variants of the emblem were also designed for promotional use by official partners, such as [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) and broadcaster [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) (which replace the "A" with Delta's "widget" logo and the [NBC peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_logo) respectively).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

LAOCOG chair Casey Wasserman stated that the variations were intended to "showcase our community's collective creativity and celebrate the diversity that makes us strong", reflecting a city that "defies a singular identity". Chief marketing officer Amy Gleeson said the emblem was designed to connect with an audience who will be in their 20s and 30s when the Games take place.[^cite_note-emblem-147][^cite_note-timeout-142]

On March 28, 2026, LAOCOG unveiled the overall look of the Games, designed by the Culver City-based agency Koto. Entitled "LA in full bloom", the design is inspired by California's "[superbloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superbloom)" phenomenon, and includes 13 different "waves" of flora-inspired designs that can be assembled into looping patterns. It incorporates colors inspired by the [Strelitzia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strelitzia) (*Bird of paradise*)—the [official flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_emblem) of Los Angeles—including   *poppy*,   *scarlet flax*,   *bluebell*, and   *sagebrush,* and typefaces inspired by the city's [street signage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_name_sign). LAOCOG stated that the designs reflected "the people, life, and culture of Los Angeles", and paid homage to the "bold, optimistic, Californian and unapologetically joyful" looks of past Summer Olympics hosted by Los Angeles.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

### Corporate sponsorship

LAOCOG partnered with [NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal)—owner of the U.S. media rights to the Olympics—to coordinate sponsorship sales for the Games. As part of the agreement and its sponsorship with the [United States Olympic & Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_&_Paralympic_Committee) (USOPC), NBCU parent company [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) will be billed as a "Founding Partner" of LA 2028.[^cite_note-151]

For the first time in Olympic and Paralympic history, venues will be permitted to carry [naming rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights) during the Games, provided that their naming rights partner is a Worldwide Olympic and Paralympic Partner (TOP) or Founding Partner. The organising committee also plans to sell naming rights for the temporary venues. Executive director Paul Krekorian commented that the new policy is intended to "ensure the success of the Games while also protecting our taxpayers," while Wasserman expected the new naming rights arrangements to help build a new commercial model for the Olympics, just as Los Angeles had previously done in 1984.[^cite_note-NYTimes-47][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

## Broadcasting rights

Main article: [List of 2028 Summer Olympics broadcasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2028_Summer_Olympics_broadcasters)

In the United States, the Games will be [broadcast by NBCUniversal and Versant properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympics_on_NBC), as part of a long-term contract with the IOC until 2036 after it was extended for a further four years in 2024,[^cite_note-USA_NBC_2032-191] with parent company [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) agreeing to provide [Olympic Broadcasting Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Broadcasting_Services) (OBS) with support for in-venue distribution, as well as collaborating on digital advertising opportunities in the U.S. among others.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] In January 2026, Comcast and NBCUniversal spun off [CNBC](223441.md), [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network), and other cable operations into a new company called [Versant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versant), with an agreement being made for those cable channels to continue airing Olympics coverage.[^cite_note-194] [Warner Bros. Studios’ The Ranch Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Ranch) is planned to be the site of the [International Broadcast Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Broadcast_Centre) for the Games, while [Hollywood Park Studios](45525802.md#Hollywood_Park) by SoFi (2028) Stadium will host the Main Press Center.[^cite_note-195]
