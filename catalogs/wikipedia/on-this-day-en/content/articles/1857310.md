**Ganjam district** is a district in the [Indian](14533.md) [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India) of [Odisha](250724.md). Ganjam's total area is 8,206 km² (3,168 mi²). The district headquarters is [Chhatrapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapur). Ganjam is divided into three sub-divisions: [Chhatrapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapur), [Brahmapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmapur,_Odisha), and [Bhanjanagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhanjanagar). As of 2011, it is the most populous district of [Odisha](250724.md) (out of [30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Odisha)) and is 2nd most largest district by area in Odisha.[^cite_note-districtcensus-2]

## History

Ganjam was a part of ancient Dakshina [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(historical_region\)).[^cite_note-3] The Mauryan emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) inscribed his message at [Jaugada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaugada) near the banks of the [Rushikulya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushikulya_River), in the present-day Ganjam district. Scholars thus conclude that a significant part of Ganjam was under the rule of Ashoka. There are multiple theories regarding the name 'Ganjam'. During the 7th century, the region was known as [Kangoda or Kongoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongoda). Some scholars believe the word evolved from this ancient name while others suggest the name of the region likely derives from the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) word "Ganj", meaning 'granary' or 'market' thus signifying the importance of historical ports and being the respective center of trade and commerce.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Ganjam district is one of the oldest administrative units of Odisha. It was part of Madras presidency during British rule.[^cite_note-Maltby_Leman_1882_p.-6]

## Economy

An important contributor to the local economy is production of extracts from [Ganjam Kewda Rooh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganjam_Kewda_Rooh), which are used in perfumery.

Since Ganjam is a coastal district, it is well equipped with Port facilities. Port at Gopalpur and nearby industries cater economic activities of the district and Odisha state.[^cite_note-10]

According to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India) Ganjam district has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 3,529,031,[^cite_note-districtcensus-2] roughly equal to the nation of [Lithuania](17675.md)[^cite_note-cia-13] or the US state of [Connecticut](6466.md).[^cite_note-14] This gives it a ranking of 83rd in [India](14533.md) (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-2] The district has a population density of 430 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,100/sq mi).[^cite_note-districtcensus-2] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001–2011 was 11.37%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-2] Ganjam has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 983 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-2] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 71.88%. 21.76% of the population lives in urban areas. The [Imperial Gazetteer of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Imperial_Gazetteer_of_India) 1908 lists Ganjam, along with the Thanjavur and South Canara districts, as the three districts of the Madras Presidency where Brahmins were most numerous. As per the 2011 Census, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes make up 19.50% and 3.37% of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-2]

### Lok Sabha constituencies

Since 2008, Ganjam district is represented in [Berhampur (Lok Sabha constituency)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berhampur_Lok_Sabha_constituency) and [Aska (Lok Sabha constituency)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aska_Lok_Sabha_constituency).

Berhampur seat earlier Ganjam (Lok Sabha constituency) exist in 1952 and 1957 general elections as two seat constituency and Chatarpur (Lok Sabha constituency) exist in 1962, 1967 and 1971 general elections and later Berhanpur seat exist since 1977 general elections.

The Aska seat existed since the 1977 general elections and earlier as Bhanjanagar (Lok Sabha constituency) seat in 1962, 1967 and 1971 general elections.[^cite_note-angul-ls-19]
