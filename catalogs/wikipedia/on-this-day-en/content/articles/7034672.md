# Weapon storage area



Typical WSA

**Weapon storage areas (WSA)**, also known as **special ammunition storage (SAS)**, were extremely well guarded and well defended locations where [NATO](21133.md) [nuclear weapons](21785.md) were stored during the [Cold War](325329.md) era.[^cite_note-nuke_matters_handbook_2020-1]

In most situations, the WSA or SAS areas were located inside the perimeter of an army barracks or an air base in NATO territory, but in a few cases they were located deep inside wooded areas and miles away from a [military base](843566.md).

Due to changes in the political landscape, the number of special weapons in [Europe](9239.md) has been drastically decreased. Moreover, the introduction of the [WS3 Weapon Storage and Security System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WS3_Weapon_Storage_and_Security_System) has made WSAs obsolete.

At present, few WSAs are still operational as modern day special weapons are stored in the floors of concrete aircraft shelters and placed under 24/7 electronic surveillance.

## Examples

- [Bossier Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossier_Base)

- [Killeen Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killeen_Base)

- [Lake Mead Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mead_Base), aka Nellis Area 2

- [Manzano Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzano_Base)

- [Medina Annex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina_Annex) prior to NSA/CSS it was the Texas Cryptologic Center

- [Naval Submarine Base Kings Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Submarine_Base_Kings_Bay)

- [Naval Base Kitsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Kitsap), former Naval Submarine Base Bangor, Washington
