&#x20;The **United States Africa Command** (**USAFRICOM**, **U.S. AFRICOM**, and **AFRICOM**)[^cite_note-4] is one of the eleven [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command) of the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md), headquartered at [Kelley Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelley_Barracks) in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), Germany. It is responsible for U.S. military operations, including fighting regional conflicts[^cite_note-5] and maintaining military relations with 53 African nations.[^cite_note-6] Its [area of responsibility](9452512.md) covers all of [Africa](5334607.md) except [Egypt](8087628.md), which is within the area of responsibility of the [United States Central Command](338482.md). U.S. AFRICOM headquarters operating budget is based on the total DoD budget, usually totalling around 0.3%, currently, as of 2026.[^cite_note-about-2]

The Commander of U.S. AFRICOM [reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_of_command) to the [secretary of defense](43998.md).[^cite_note-factsheet-7] The current Commander of the U.S. Africa Command stated that the purpose of the command is to work alongside African military personnel to support their military operations.[^cite_note-\:0-8] In individual countries, [U.S. ambassadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._ambassador) continue to be the primary diplomatic representative for relations with host nations. The incumbent commander is [Dagvin Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagvin_Anderson).

## History

### Origins

Prior to the creation of AFRICOM, responsibility for U.S. military operations in Africa was divided across three unified commands: [United States European Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_European_Command) (EUCOM) for West Africa, [United States Central Command](338482.md) (CENTCOM) for East Africa, and [United States Pacific Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Command) (PACOM) for Indian Ocean waters and islands off the east coast of Africa.

A U.S. military officer wrote the first public article calling for the formation of a separate African command in November 2000.[^cite_note-9] Following a 2004 global posture review, the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) began establishing a number of [Cooperative Security Locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_Security_Location) (CSLs) and [Forward Operating Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Operating_Site) (FOSs) across the African continent, through the auspices of EUCOM which had nominal command of West Africa at that time. These locations, along with [Camp Lemonnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Lemonnier) in [Djibouti](17207794.md), would form the basis of AFRICOM facilities on the continent. Areas of military interest to the United States in Africa include the [Sahara](325363.md)/[Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region,[^cite_note-US-TARGETS-SAHARA-TERRORIST-HAVEN-10] over which [Joint Task Force Aztec Silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Task_Force_Aztec_Silence) is conducting anti-terrorist operations ([Operation Enduring Freedom - Trans Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom_-_Trans_Sahara)), Djibouti in the Horn of Africa, where [Combined Joint Task Force – Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJTF-HOA) is located (overseeing [Operation Enduring Freedom - Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom_-_Horn_of_Africa)), and the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea).

The website **Magharebia.com** was launched by USEUCOM in 2004 to provide news about North Africa in English, French and Arabic.[^cite_note-11] When AFRICOM was created, it took over operation of the website.[^cite_note-pincus2007-05-08-12] Information operations of the United States Department of Defense was criticized by the Senate Armed Forces Committee and defunded by Congress in 2011. The site was closed down in February 2015.[^cite_note-pincus2011-12-06-13][^cite_note-14]

In 2007, the [United States Congress](31756.md) approved \$500 million for the [Trans-Saharan Counterterrorism Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_Counterterrorism_Initiative) (TSCTI) over six years to support countries involved in [counterterrorism](83392.md) against threats of [Al Qaeda](1921.md) operating in African countries, primarily [Algeria](358.md), Chad, [Mali](19127.md), Mauritania, [Niger](21373.md), Senegal, [Nigeria](21383.md), and [Morocco](19291.md).[^cite_note-AFRICA-TO-GET-ITS-OWN-US-MILITARY-COMMAND-15] This program builds upon the former [Pan Sahel Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Sahel_Initiative) (PSI), which concluded in December 2004[^cite_note-EUCOM-OPERATIONS-AND-INITIATIVES-16] and focused on [weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon_trafficking) and drug trafficking, as well as counterterrorism.[^cite_note-PAN-SAHEL-INITIATIVE-17] Previous U.S. military activities in Sub-Saharan Africa have included Special Forces associated [Joint Combined Exchange Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Combined_Exchange_Training). Letitia Lawson, writing in 2007 for a [Center for Contemporary Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Contemporary_Conflict) journal at the [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School), noted that U.S. policy towards Africa, at least in the medium-term, looks to be largely defined by international terrorism, the increasing importance of African oil to American energy needs, and the dramatic expansion and improvement of [Sino-African relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-African_relations) since 2000.[^cite_note-Lawson07-18]

### Creation of the command (2006–2008)

In mid-2006, Defense Secretary [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld) formed a planning team to advise on requirements for establishing a new Unified Command for the African continent. In early December, he made his recommendations to President [George W. Bush](3414021.md).[^cite_note-PENTAGON-CREATES-MILITARY-COMMAND-FOR-AFRICA-19][^cite_note-AFRICA-COMMAND-PLANS-APPROVED-BY-BUSH-20]

On 6 February 2007, Defense Secretary [Robert Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gates) announced to the [Senate Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Armed_Services_Committee) that President George W. Bush had given authority to create the new African Command.[^cite_note-DOD-ESTABLISHING-US-AFRICA-COMMAND-21] [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) [Rear Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_Admiral) [Robert Moeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Moeller), the director of the AFRICOM transition team, arrived in Stuttgart, Germany to begin creating the logistical framework for the command.[^cite_note-US-CREATING-NEW-AFRICA-COMMAND-TO-COORDINATE-MILITARY-EFFORTS-22][^cite_note-AFRICOM-TRANSITION-TEAM-LEADER-ARRIVES-STUTTGART-23] The creation of the command was introduced to African military leaders by General [William E. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Ward) who traveled to various African countries.[^cite_note-\:0-8] On 28 September, the U.S. Senate confirmed General Ward as AFRICOM's first commander and AFRICOM officially became operational as a sub-unified command of EUCOM with a separate headquarters.[^cite_note-24] On 1 October 2008 it became a fully operational command and incorporated pre-existing entities, including the Combined Joint Task Force - Horn of Africa that was created in 2002.[^cite_note-\:0-8] At this time, the command also separated from USEUCOM and began operating on its own as a full-fledged combatant command.

## Function

In 2007, the White House announced that Africa Command "will strengthen our security cooperation with Africa and create new opportunities to bolster the capabilities of our partners in Africa. Africa Command will enhance our efforts to bring peace and security to the people of Africa and promote our common goals of development, health, education, democracy, and economic growth in Africa."[^cite_note-georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov-25]

General [Carter F. Ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_F._Ham) said in a 2012 address at [Brown University](4157.md) that U.S. strategy for [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) is to strengthen democratic institutions and boost broad-based economic growth.[^cite_note-about-2]

In 2017 the U.S. Africa Command was operating along five lines of effort:

1. Neutralize [al-Shabaab](8726463.md) and transition the security responsibilities of the [African Union Mission in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_in_Somalia) (AMISOM) to the [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md) (FGS)

2. Degrade violent extremist organizations in the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) and contain instability in [Libya](17633.md)

3. Contain and degrade [Boko Haram](23769768.md)

4. Interdict illicit activity in the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea) and [Central Africa](539606.md) with willing and capable African partners

5. Build [peacekeeping](84617.md), [humanitarian assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_assistance) and [disaster response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_response) capacity of African partners

   [^cite_note-\:1-26]

On 18 March 2019, AFRICOM conducted an airstrike over Mogadishu, Somalia aimed at "the terrorist network and its recruiting efforts in the region", specifically referencing al-Shabaab. AFRICOM reported that the number of terrorists killed by this airstrike was 3, but this fact, as well as how many civilian casualties there were is still under dispute.[^cite_note-27]

## Headquarters and facilities

The AFRICOM headquarters is located at [Kelley Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelley_Barracks), a small urban facility near [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), Germany, and is staffed by 1,500 personnel. In addition, the command has military and civilian personnel assigned at [Camp Lemonnier, Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Lemonnier); [RAF Molesworth, United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Molesworth); [MacDill Air Force Base, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacDill_Air_Force_Base); and in Offices of Security Cooperation and Defense Attaché Offices in about 38 African countries.[^cite_note-about-2]

### Selection of the headquarters

It was reported in June 2007 that African countries were competing to host the headquarters because it would bring money for the recipient country.[^cite_note-29] [Liberia](17791.md) has publicly expressed a willingness to host AFRICOM's headquarters, and in 2021 [Nigeria](21383.md) expressed a similar interest.[^cite_note-30] The U.S. declared in February 2008 that AFRICOM would be headquartered in Stuttgart for the "foreseeable future". In August 2007, Dr. Wafula Okumu, a research fellow at the Institute for Security Studies in South Africa, testified before the United States Congress about the growing resistance and hostility on the African continent.[^cite_note-31] [Nigeria](21383.md) announced it will not allow its country to host a base and opposed the creation of a base on the continent. South Africa and [Libya](17633.md) also expressed reservations of the establishment of a headquarters in Africa.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

The *Sudan Tribune* considered it likely that [Ethiopia](187749.md), a strong U.S. ally in the region, will house USAFRICOM's headquarters due to the collocation of AFRICOM with the [African Union](10634933.md)'s developing [peace and security apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Security_Council).[^cite_note-34] [Prime Minister](1768778.md) [Meles Zenawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meles_Zenawi) stated in early November that Ethiopia would be willing to work together closely with USAFRICOM.[^cite_note-35] This was further reinforced when a U.S. Air Force official said on 5 December 2007, that [Addis Ababa](52593.md) was likely to be the headquarters.[^cite_note-36]

On 18 February 2008, General Ward told an audience at the [Royal United Services Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_United_Services_Institute) in London that some portion of that staff headquarters being on the continent at some point in time would be "a positive factor in helping us better deliver programs."[^cite_note-37] General Ward also told the BBC the same day in an interview that there are no definite plans to take the headquarters or a portion of it to any particular location on the continent.[^cite_note-38]

President Bush denied that the United States was contemplating the construction of new bases on the African continent.[^cite_note-39] U.S. plans include no large installations such as [Camp Bondsteel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Bondsteel) in Kosovo, but rather a network of "cooperative security locations" at which temporary activities will be conducted. There is one U.S. base on the continent, [Camp Lemonnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Lemonnier) in Djibouti, with approximately 2,300 troops stationed there having been inherited from USCENTCOM upon standup of the command.

In general, U.S. Unified Combatant Commands have an HQ of their own in one location, subordinate service component HQs, sometimes one or two co-located with the main HQ or sometimes spread widely, and a wide range of operating locations, main bases, forward detachments, etc. USAFRICOM initially appears to be considering something slightly different; spreading the actually [COCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command) HQ over several locations, rather than having the COCOM HQ in one place and the putative "U.S. Army Forces, Africa", its air component, and "U.S. Naval Forces, Africa" in one to four separate locations. AFRICOM will not have the traditional J-type staff divisions, instead having outreach, plans and programs, knowledge development, operations and logistics, and resources branches.[^cite_note-40] AFRICOM went back to a traditional J-Staff in early 2011 after General [Carter Ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Ham) took command.[^cite_note-41]



U.S. Army element AFRICOM element SSI

In the summer of 2020, U.S. Defense Secretary [Mark Esper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Esper) directed AFRICOM leadership to study a possible headquarters relocation outside of Germany after plans were announced that neighboring U.S. European Command would relocate to [Belgium](3343.md).[^cite_note-42]

On 20 November 2020 a new Army service component command (ASCC), U.S. Army Europe and Africa (USAREUR-AF) consolidated USAREUR and USARAAF.[^cite_note-usaeur,af2020-43] The U.S. Army Africa/Southern European Task Force is now the U.S. Army Southern European Task Force, Africa (SETAF-AF).[^cite_note-usaeur,af2020-43]

## Personnel

U.S. Africa Command completed fiscal year 2010 with approximately 2,000 assigned personnel, which includes military, civilian, contractor, and host nation employees. About 1,500 work at the command's main headquarters in Stuttgart. Others are assigned to the command's units in England and Florida, along with security cooperation officers posted at U.S. embassies and diplomatic missions in Africa to coordinate Defense Department programs within the host nation.

As of December 2010, the command has five [Senior Foreign Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Foreign_Service) officers in key positions as well as more than 30 personnel from 13 U.S. Government Departments and Agencies serving in leadership, management, and staff positions. Some of the agencies represented are the United States Departments of [State](31975.md), [Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Treasury), and [Commerce](70243.md), [United States Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development), and the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md).

U.S. Africa Command has limited assigned forces and relies on the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) for resources necessary to support its missions.

## Components

On 1 October 2008, the [Seventeenth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeenth_Air_Force) was established at [Ramstein Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramstein_Air_Base), Germany as the [United States Air Force](32090.md) component of the Africa Command.[^cite_note-44] Brig. Gen. Tracey Garrett was named as commander of the new USMC component, U.S. Marine Corps Forces Africa (MARFORAF), in November 2008.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] MARFORAF is a dual-mission arrangement for [United States Marine Corps Forces, Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Forces,_Europe).

On 3 December 2008, the U.S. announced that Army and Navy headquarters units of AFRICOM would be hosted in [Italy](14532.md). The AFRICOM section of the Army's [Southern European Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_European_Task_Force) would be located in [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza) and Naval Forces Europe in [Naples](55880.md) would expand to include the Navy's AFRICOM component.[^cite_note-47] Special Operations Command, Africa (SOCAFRICA) is also established, gaining control over Joint Special Operations Task Force-Trans Sahara (JSOTF-TS) and Special Operations Command and Control Element – Horn of Africa (SOCCE-HOA).[^cite_note-48]

### U.S. Army Europe and Africa (USAREUR-AF)



U.S. Army Europe and Africa

Headquartered on [Lucius D. Clay Kaserne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_D._Clay_Kaserne) in Wiesbaden, Germany, [U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Europe_and_Africa) — Southern European Task Force - Africa (SETAF-AF), in concert with national and international partners, conducts sustained security engagement with African land forces to promote peace, stability, and security in Africa. As directed, it can deploy as a contingency headquarters in support of crisis response.[^cite_note-49] The commander of SETAF-AF is DCG for Africa.

The U.S. Army "regionally aligned" a brigade towards Africa Command in 2012–13.[^cite_note-50] As of March 2013, the [2nd Brigade Combat Team, 1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Brigade_Combat_Team,_1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), the "Dagger Brigade", is being aligned with AFRICOM.[^cite_note-51]

### U.S. Naval Forces Africa (NAVAF)

U.S. Naval Forces Africa

U.S. Naval Forces Africa

[U.S. Naval Forces Europe - Naval Forces Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Forces_Europe_-_Naval_Forces_Africa) (NAVEUR-NAVAF) area of responsibility (AOR) covers approximately half of the Atlantic Ocean, from the North Pole to Antarctica; as well as the Adriatic, Baltic, Barents, Black, Caspian, Mediterranean and North Seas.[^cite_note-52] NAVEUR-NAVAF covers all of [Russia](25391.md), Europe and nearly the entire continent of Africa. It encompasses 105 countries with a combined population of more than one billion people and includes a landmass extending more than 14 million square miles.

The area of responsibility covers more than 20 million square nautical miles of ocean, touches three continents and encompasses more than 67 percent of the Earth's coastline, 30 percent of its landmass, and nearly 40 percent of the world's population.[^cite_note-53]

[Task Force 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_60) will normally be the commander of Naval Task Force Europe and Africa. Any naval unit within the USEUCOM or USAFRICOM AOR may be assigned to Task Force 60 as required upon by the Commander of the [Sixth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Sixth_Fleet).

[Air Forces Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Forces_Africa) ([AFAFRICA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFAFRICA)) is located at [Ramstein Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramstein_Air_Base), Germany, and serves as the air and space component to U.S. Africa Command (AFRICOM) located at Stuttgart, Germany. [Air Forces Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Forces_Africa) shares a headquarters and units with [United States Air Forces in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Forces_in_Europe), and its component Air Force, 3AF (AFAFRICA) conducts sustained security engagement and operations as directed to promote air safety, security and development on the African continent. Through its Theater Security Cooperation (TSC) events, Air Forces Africa carries out AFRICOM's policy of seeking long-term partnership with the African Union and regional organizations as well as individual nations on the continent.[^cite_note-54]

Air Forces Africa works with other U.S. Government agencies, to include the State Department and the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID), to assist African partners in developing national and regional security institution capabilities that promote security and stability and facilitate development.[^cite_note-55]

3AF succeeds the [Seventeenth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeenth_Air_Force) by assuming the [AFAFRICA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFAFRICA) mission upon the 17AF's deactivation on 20 April 2012.[^cite_note-56]

### U.S. Marine Corps Forces, Africa (MARFORAF)

U.S. Marine Corps Forces Africa

U.S. Marine Corps Forces Africa

U.S. Marine Corps Forces, Africa conducts operations, exercises, training, and security cooperation activities throughout the AOR. In 2009, MARFORAF participated in 15 ACOTA missions aimed at improving partners' capabilities to provide logistical support, employ military police, and exercise command and control over deployed forces.

MARFORAF conducted military to military events in 2009 designed to familiarize African partners with nearly every facet of military operations and procedures, including use of unmanned aerial vehicles, tactics, and medical skills. MARFORAF, as the lead component, continues to conduct Exercise AFRICAN LION in Morocco—the largest annual Combined Joint Chiefs of Staff (CJCS) exercise on the African continent—as well as Exercise SHARED ACCORD 10, which was the first CJCS exercise conducted in Mozambique.[^cite_note-57]

In 2013, the [Special Purpose Marine Air-Ground Task Force - Crisis Response - Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Purpose_Marine_Air-Ground_Task_Force_-_Crisis_Response_-_Africa) was formed to provide quick response to American interests in North Africa by flying marines in [Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Boeing_V-22_Osprey) aircraft from bases in Europe.[^cite_note-58]

### U.S. Space Forces – Europe and Africa (SPACEFOR–EURAF)

U.S. Marine Corps Forces Africa

U.S. Marine Corps Forces Africa

[U.S. Space Forces in Europe – Space Forces Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Forces_%E2%80%93_Europe_and_Africa) is the [U.S. Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) component field command charged with supporting both U.S. European Command and U.S. Africa Command, encompassing 51 countries in Europe and 54 African nations, respectively.[^cite_note-AutoAO-40-59]&#x20;

Activated at [Ramstein Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramstein_Air_Base) Dec. 8, 2023, SPACEFOREUR-AF is responsible for a wide range of space operations including the space situational awareness mission and security cooperation in support of the two combatant commands' objectives.[^cite_note-AutoAO-40-59]

SPACEFOREUR-AF is singularly responsible for integrating space forces with two combatant commands, enhancing the security of our nation and its allies from Iceland to South Africa. The mission scope spans two continents and has a vast importance to the international order and balance of rules governing use of space. SPACEFOREUR-AF is dedicated to building and improving on existing alliances and partnerships to enhance interoperability and resiliency, thereby bolstering the security and long-term sustainability of space.[^cite_note-AutoAO-40-59]

[Combined Joint Task Force – Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Joint_Task_Force_%E2%80%93_Horn_of_Africa) (CJTF-HOA) conducts operations in the East Africa region to build partner nation capacity in order to promote regional security and stability, prevent conflict, and protect U.S. and coalition interests. ## Programs and operations

The programs conducted by AFRICOM, in conjunction with African military forces focus on reconnaissance and direct action. However, AFRICOM's directives are to keep American military forces out of direct combat as best as possible. Despite this, the United States has admitted to American troops  being involved in direct action during missions with African military partners, namely in classified [127e programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/127e_programs).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-Turse-64] As of 2023, there have been at least 315 confirmed drone strikes from AFRICOM operations in Somalia. Estimates place the total number of deaths at least 1,668, with at least 33 civilians killed.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Each AFRICOM operations has a specific mission. Some of the operations in North and West Africa target ISIS, and Boko Haram. In East Africa, missions focus on targeting terrorist group Al-Shabaab and piracy.[^cite_note-Turse-64]

### Joint Exercise Programs

#### African Lion

Training exercises sponsored by the United States through AFRICOM and Morocco. Participants of this program came from Europe and Africa to undergo training in various military exercises and skills. Exercises conducted during African Lion included "command-and-control techniques, combat tactics, peacekeeping, and humanitarian assistance operations". Reported by AFRICOM to have improved the quality of operations conducted between the North African and United States military.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

#### Western Accord

Training exercises sponsored by AFRICOM, European, and Western African countries for the first time in 2014. The goal of this exercise was to improve African forces' skills in conducting peace support operations. An ebola epidemic occurring from 2014 to 2015 resulted in the exercises being hosted by the Netherlands. During this exercise the mission command for the "United Nations Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission" in Mali was replicated. Name of exercise changed to United Accord some time later.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

#### Central Accord

Training exercises conducted with the goal of increasing both the military knowledge and efficacy of collaborative interactions of the participating groups. Emphasis placed on crisis response tactics and fostering strong partnerships between participating groups. Forces came from Africa, the United States, and Europe. The Lake Chad Basin is an example of a regional mission conducted by The Multi-National Joint Task Force.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

#### Eastern Accord

Series of training exercises originally began in 1998 with a series of exercises titled "Natural Fire". The Justified Accord was a further continuation of the large group of exercises conducted under the name Eastern Accord. Participating forces came from the United States and various African allies. Conducted with the goal of improving coordinated operations in East Africa. Notable aspects of the training included discussion-based modules focused on peace-keeping endeavors.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

#### Southern Accord

Annual training exercise sponsored by AFRICOM in conjunction with allied African forces over several years. In 2014 partners also included the United Nation Integrated Training Service and U.S. Army Peacekeeping and Stability Operations Institute. Exercises focused around the goal of peacekeeping. In 2017, Southern Accord was renamed as United Accord.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

#### Cutlass Express

Series of training exercises held at sea off the coast of East Africa. The Cutlass Express series was conducted by the United States Naval Forces Africa, a group within AFRICOM. Exercises performed at this time focused on maritime security, piracy countermeasures and interception of prohibited cargo. Express series included operations Obangame Express, Saharan Express, and Phoenix Express.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

#### Obangame Express

Annual maritime exercise held at sea and ashore in Western and Central Africa. In 2023, participating nations included countries from North America, South America, Africa, and Europe, as well as the Economic Community of West African States and the Economic Community of Central African States.[^cite_note-72] The exercise aims to assist Gulf of Guinea nations in monitoring and enforcing their respective territorial waters.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

## Proposed reorganization (2025)

In 2025, the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_administration) proposed a significant reorganization of U.S. military combatant commands that would effectively downgrade AFRICOM. Under the plan, Africa would be folded into a command structure alongside the Americas rather than maintaining its own dedicated geographic combatant command. The restructuring was described as part of a broader shift in U.S. strategic priorities toward the [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) region. A senior AFRICOM official stated in January 2025 that no official decision had yet been made at that time.[^cite_note-77] Critics argued the move would create strategic voids that Russia and China could exploit, particularly in the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region, where jihadist groups linked to [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and the [Islamic State](9087364.md) have expanded significantly.
