The **European Aquatics Championships** (commonly referred to as **Europeans**) is the continental [Aquatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_sports) championship for Europe, which is organised by [European Aquatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_Europ%C3%A9enne_de_Natation) (LEN) – the governing body for aquatic sport in Europe. The Championships are currently held every two years (in even years) and since 2022, they have included 5 aquatics disciplines: [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)) (long course/50m pool), [diving](8402.md), [synchronised swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronised_swimming), [open water swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_water_swimming) and [high diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_diving). Prior to 1999, the championships also included [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo), which beginning in 1999 LEN split-off into a [separate championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Water_Polo_Championship). The open water events are not held during the Olympic year.

The Championships are generally held over a two-week time-period in mid-to-late Summer, but in the most recent Summer Olympics years (2004, 2008, 2012, 2016 and 2020), the Championships were moved to the Spring to be moved away from the Summer Olympic Games.

The [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)) portion of these championships is considered one of the pre-eminent swimming competitions in the world. LEN also conducts an annual [short-course (25 meters) swimming championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Short_Course_Swimming_Championships), which is a completely separate, distinct event (typically held in early December).

## Championships

Historically, the Championships were first held in 1926, and included [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo) prior to 1999 when the discipline was moved to the [European Water Polo Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Water_Polo_Championship). From 1973-1999 Europeans were held in years without a Summer Olympics or [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_World_Aquatics_Championships), save 1979 (1973 being the inception year of the World Championships; and 1999 being the last year before Worlds moved from even-years between Summer Olympics to every-odd year beginning in 2001). Women were first allowed to participate at the second Championships in 1927.[^cite_note-1]
