**Silesian Voivodeship** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *województwo śląskie* [\[vɔjɛˈvut͡stfɔ ˈɕlɔ̃skʲɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0c/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo\_%C5%9Bl%C4%85skie.wav/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo\_%C5%9Bl%C4%85skie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0c/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_%C5%9Bl%C4%85skie.wav/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_%C5%9Bl%C4%85skie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is an administrative province in southern [Poland](22936.md). With over 4.2 million residents and an area of 12,300 square kilometers, it is the second-most populous, and the most-densely populated and most-urbanized region of Poland. It generates 11.9% of Polish GDP[^cite_note-4] and is characterized by a high life satisfaction, low income inequalities, and high wages.[^cite_note-5]

The region has a diversified geography. The Beskid Mountains cover most of the southern part of the voivodeship, with the highest peak of [Pilsko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilsko) on the Polish–Slovak border reaching 1,534 m (5,033 ft) above sea level. [Silesian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Upland) dominates the central part of the region, while the hilly, limestone [Polish Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w-Cz%C4%99stochowa_Upland) closes it from the northeast. [Katowice urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice_urban_area), located in the central part of the region, is the second most-populous urban area in Poland after [Warsaw](32908.md), with 2.2 million people, and one of Poland's seven supra-regional metropolises, while [Rybnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rybnik), [Bielsko-Biała](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielsko-Bia%C5%82a) and [Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa) and their respective urban areas are classified among the country's 15 regional agglomerations.[^cite_note-6]

Despite the voivodeship's name, only the western half of its area is considered to be a part of the historical region of [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia). The eastern part of Silesian Voivodeship was historically part of [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland), while a small part in the north of the region was historically considered a part of [Greater Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland).

## History

### Interwar Poland

Silesian Voivodeship was first created in 1920 when the newly independent Polish state established an [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Voivodeship_\(1920%E2%80%931939\)) for all historical lands of Upper Silesia that were to end up in Poland. At the time, Upper Silesia was under international control, and a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_Upper_Silesia_plebiscite) was to be held in 1921 to divide the region between Germany and Poland following local results. Katowice has been chosen to be the provincial capital. In 1938, following the annexation of [Trans-Olza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Olza) region by Poland, the voivodeship's area was expanded to include these new territories.

The interwar region did not include more than half of its current area, which were parts of the [Kielce voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kielce_Voivodeship_\(1919%E2%80%931939\)) ([Sosnowiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnowiec) and [Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa) areas), [Kraków voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w_Voivodeship_\(1919%E2%80%931939\)) ([Jaworzno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaworzno) and [Żywiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBywiec) areas) or [Germany](11867.md) (cities of [Zabrze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabrze) and [Bytom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bytom) as well as [Gliwice](74600.md) and [Racibórz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racib%C3%B3rz) areas).

### German occupation

After the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) in 1939, Polish administrative divisions ceased to exist. Nazi Germany annexed most of the current voivodeship's area directly into the German [province of Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gau_Silesia) (*Gau Schlesien*) with capital in [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw) (*Breslau*) as the governmental district of Katowice (*Regierungsbezirk Kattowitz*). This new district included both historically Silesian areas as well as western parts of Lesser Poland. Northern parts of what is the Silesian Voivodeship today, with Częstochowa, found themselves in the [General Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government) area.

In 1941, the province of Silesia was split into Lower Silesia (with a capital in Wrocław) and Upper Silesia (with a capital in Katowice), the latter of which included the governmental district of Opole in addition to the one of Katowice.

### Socialist Poland

Following [World War II](32927.md), the new communist government of Poland cancelled the autonomous status of the Silesian voivodeship and established a new [Silesian-Dabrowa voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Voivodeship_\(1945%E2%80%931950\)) (*województwo śląsko-dąbrowskie*), the area of which roughly corresponded to the German province of Upper Silesia. The name of this region reflected both the Silesian part and the [Dąbrowa Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%85browa_Basin) part.

In 1950, Opole voivodeship was created from the western part of the Silesian-Dabrowa voivodeship, and the name of the remaining area changed to [Katowice Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice_Voivodeship). The new region's borders included, for the first time, the Częstochowa area, and roughly resembled the contemporary Silesian Voivodeship. Between 1953–56, the name of the region was changed to Stalinogród voivodeship (*województwo stalinogrodzkie*), reflecting Katowice's forced name change to Stalinogród following the death of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin).

In 1975, a new [administrative reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland_\(1975%E2%80%931998\)) introduced 49 new voivodeships. The area of today's Silesian Voivodeship was divided between [Częstochowa voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa_Voivodeship) (*województwo częstochowskie*) in the north, [Katowice voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice_Voivodeship) (*województwo katowickie*) in the center, and [Bielsko-Biała voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielsko_Voivodeship) (*województwo bielskie*) in the south.

### After 1989

As Poland aimed to join the European Union, European negotiators named administrative reform as one of the conditions for accession. As such, in 1999, a new administrative division was introduced, reducing the number of voivodeships from 49 to 16. A Silesian voivodeship has emerged from the reform, with its capital in Katowice, and consisting of most municipalities of the former Katowice, Częstochowa, and Bielsko-Biała voivodeships.

## Geography

The Silesian Voivodeship borders both the [Moravian-Silesian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian-Silesian_Region) ([Czech Republic](5321.md)), [Žilina Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDilina_Region) ([Slovakia](26830.md)) to the south. It is also bordered by four other Polish voivodeships: those of [Opole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opole_Voivodeship) (to the west), [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA_Voivodeship) (to the north), [Świętokrzyskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awi%C4%99tokrzyskie_Voivodeship) (to the north-east), and [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship) (to the east).

The region includes the Silesian Upland (*_Wyżyna Śląska_*) in the centre and north-west, and the Krakowsko-Częstochowska Upland (*_[Jura Krakowsko-Czestochowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Krakowsko-Czestochowska)_*) in the north-east. The southern border is formed by the [Beskidy Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskidy_Mountains) ([Beskid Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskid_%C5%9Al%C4%85ski) and [Beskid Żywiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskid_%C5%BBywiecki)).

The current administrative unit of Silesian Voivodeship is just one fraction of the historical [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia) region which is within the borders of today's Poland (there are also fragments of Silesia in the Czech Republic and Germany). Other parts of today's Polish Silesia are administered as the [Opole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opole_Voivodeship), the [Lower Silesian Voivodeships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolny_%C5%9Al%C4%85sk) and the [Lubusz Voivodeship](75149.md). On the other hand, a large part of the current administrative unit of the Silesian Voivodeship is not part of historical Silesia (e.g., [Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa), [Zawiercie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zawiercie), [Myszków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myszk%C3%B3w), [Jaworzno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaworzno), [Sosnowiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnowiec), [Żywiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBywiec), [Dąbrowa Górnicza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%85browa_G%C3%B3rnicza), [Będzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C4%99dzin) and east part of [Bielsko-Biała](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielsko-Bia%C5%82a), which were historically parts of [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland)).

### Population

More than one out of every nine of Poland's residents live in the Silesian voivodeship. According to the Polish Statistics Office, the region's population was 4.32 million at the end of 2023,[^cite_note-8] a decrease of 6% from 10 years earlier.

Similarly to Poland, the Silesian voivodeship has suffered extremely low fertility and an intensifying natural population decrease. In 2023, the total fertility rate was only 1.10, well below the 2.1 required to sustain a population size and slightly below the Polish average of 1.11. 27,641 people were born while 51,723 died, a natural decrease of -24,082. In 2023, 19.1% of the population was under 20, 32.2% was 20–44 years old, 27.4% was 45–64, while 21.3% were 65 or older.[^cite_note-9]

Silesian voivodeship is the most densely populated and most urbanized region of Poland. More than three-fourths of residents live in urban areas, compared to less than 60% on average in Poland.[^cite_note-10] Population density is nearly three times the average, with more than 350 people per square kilometer (nearly 1,000 per square mile).[^cite_note-11] Population density is particularly high in the central part of the region where the polycentric Katowice urban area is located.



Population density in the region

Aside from Polish citizens, a large immigrant population resides in the region and is not counted towards official population statistics in Poland. As of November 2024, 99,542 foreigners living in the region paid into social security,[^cite_note-12] and in the 2023/24 academic year, 31,111 foreign students attended primary and secondary schools in the region.[^cite_note-13] In addition, following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), the region has attracted many refugees. As of January 2025, 99,545 Ukrainian refugees settled in the Silesian voivodeship.[^cite_note-14]

### Religion

Silesian voivodeship is the most religiously diverse region of Poland.[^cite_note-15] In the 2021 census, 71.39% of residents declared they have belonged to a religion, of which [Roman Catholicism](606848.md) was the largest denomination with 3.063 million adherents (69.57% of total). The region is divided into five ecclesial provinces:

- [Archdiocese of Katowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Katowice) with its suffragan [Diocese of Gliwice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Gliwice) covers all of the former [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) lands in the voivodeship

- [Diocese of Bielsko-Żywiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bielsko%E2%80%93%C5%BBywiec) covers all of the former [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) lands in the voivodeship and is a suffragan to the [Archdiocese of Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Krak%C3%B3w)

- [Archdiocese of Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Cz%C4%99stochowa) with its suffragan [Diocese of Sosnowiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Sosnowiec) covers all of the former [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) lands in the voivodeship

[Jasna Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasna_G%C3%B3ra_Monastery) in Częstochowa, located in the northern part of the voivodeship, is the most-visited shrine in Poland and features the [Black Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Madonna) icon.

Silesian voivodeship is also the center of [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), in particular [Lutheranism](23371382.md), in Poland. [Wisła](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wis%C5%82a) and [Goleszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_Golesz%C3%B3w) are the only municipalities in Poland where Lutheranism is the plurality religion, at 46.7% and 37.47% of the total population, respectively. In total, there are 53,980 Protestants in the region (1.23% of the region's population and 42.7% of all protestants in Poland).

### Origins

In terms of nationality and ethnicity, the 2021 Polish census allowed responders to select up to two nationalities and ethnicities. [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) nationality was selected by 95.49% of residents, while 13.08% indicated other nationalities; in addition to the Polish one or separately. [Silesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesians) and [German](152735.md) nationalities were the largest, declared by 517,100 and 27,923 residents, respectively.

The 2021 census did not count most of recent immigrants towards the resident population but instead considered them as temporary residents. According to the census, 119,594 of such temporary residents lived in the Silesian voivodeship, and the majority of them were Ukrainians.[^cite_note-16]

## Tourism



["Szyndzielnia" gondola lift](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolej%20gondolowa%20na%20Szyndzielni%C4%99) in [Bielsko-Biała](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielsko-Bia%C5%82a), north part of [Beskid Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Beskids)

Both the northern and southern parts of the voivodeship are surrounded by a [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt). [Bielsko-Biała](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielsko-Bia%C5%82a) is enveloped by the [Beskidy Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskidy_Mountains) which are popular with winter sports fans. It offers over 150 ski lifts and 200 kilometres of ski routes. More and more slopes are illuminated and equipped with artificial snow generators. [Szczyrk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczyrk), [Brenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenna,_Poland), [Wisła](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wis%C5%82a) and [Ustroń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustro%C5%84) are the most popular winter mountain resorts. Rock climbing sites can be found in [Jura Krakowsko-Czestochowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Krakowsko-Czestochowska). In the south-western part of the voivodeship are parks and old monasteries ([Rudy Raciborskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Raciborskie), [Wodzisław Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodzis%C5%82aw_%C5%9Al%C4%85ski)). Along the [Oder River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder) are interesting natural reserves and places for swimming during the summer.

There are numerous castles and palaces in the voivodeship, including the medieval castles of the [Piast dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_dynasty) in [Będzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C4%99dzin_Castle), [Gliwice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliwice_Castle), [Racibórz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racib%C3%B3rz_Castle), and the castles forming the [Trail of the Eagle's Nests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_of_the_Eagle's_Nests), including at [Bobolice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobolice_Castle), [Mirów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir%C3%B3w_Castle), [Ogrodzieniec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogrodzieniec_Castle) and [Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn_Castle_\(Silesian_Voivodeship\)). The best-preserved palaces include those at [Brynek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brynek), [Kłobuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%82obuck), [Koniecpol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koniecpol), [Kończyce Wielkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%84czyce_Wielkie), [Pławniowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%82awniowice), [Sosnowiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnowiec) and [Złoty Potok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C5%82oty_Potok,_Silesian_Voivodeship).

Often visited is the [Black Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Madonna)'s [Jasna Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasna_G%C3%B3ra) Sanctuary in [Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa) – the annual destination of over 4 million pilgrims from all over the world. Another local pilgrimage destination is the [Basilica of St. Mary and St. Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Mary_and_St._Bartholomew) in [Piekary Śląskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piekary_%C5%9Al%C4%85skie). Other notable historic churches include the St. Nicholas' Chapel in [Cieszyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cieszyn), a [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture_in_Poland) rotunda, depicted on the [20 złotych note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_z%C5%82otych_note), and the St. Mary Magdalene Church in Cieszyn, which contains several sarcophagi of Polish dukes from the [Piast dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_dynasty).

There are three [spa towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_town) in the voivodeship: [Goczałkowice-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gocza%C5%82kowice-Zdr%C3%B3j), [Jastrzębie-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jastrz%C4%99bie-Zdr%C3%B3j), and [Ustroń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustro%C5%84).

With its more than two centuries of industrial history, the region has many technical heritage memorials. These include narrow and standard gauge railways, coal and silver mines, and shafts and their equipment from the 19th and 20th centuries. The historic coal mine complex in [Zabrze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabrze) is listed as a [Historic Monument of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Monument_\(Poland\)),[^cite_note-17] and the [Historic Silver Mine in Tarnowskie Góry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Silver_Mine_in_Tarnowskie_G%C3%B3ry) is listed as both a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) and Historic Monument of Poland.

There are numerous memorials to Polish uprisings against foreign rule, including the [January Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising) of 1863–1864 and [Silesian Uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Uprisings) of 1919–1921, and [Świętochłowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awi%C4%99toch%C5%82owice) hosts the Silesian Uprisings Museum.

There are numerous [World War II](32927.md) memorials in the voivodeship, including at the sites of Nazi massacres of Poles and Jews, and the sites of former Nazi German [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) camps and prisons. The [Gliwice Radio Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliwice_Radio_Tower) and [Katowice Parachute Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute_Tower_in_Katowice) are local symbols of German provocation and Polish resistance during the war, respectively.

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the province was 61 billion € in 2018, accounting for 12.3% of the Polish economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 22,200 € or 74% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 83% of the EU average. Silesia Voivodeship is the province with the fourth-highest GDP per capita in Poland.[^cite_note-18]

The Silesian voivodeship is predominantly an industrial region. Most of the mining is derived from one of the world's largest bituminous [coalfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) of the Upper Silesian Industrial District (*_Górnośląski Okręg Przemysłowy_*) and the [Rybnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rybnik) Coal District (*_Rybnicki Okręg Węglowy_*) with its major cities [Rybnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rybnik), [Jastrzębie-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jastrz%C4%99bie-Zdr%C3%B3j), [Żory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBory) and [Wodzisław Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodzis%C5%82aw_%C5%9Al%C4%85ski). [Lead](17747.md) and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) can be found near [Bytom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bytom), [Zawiercie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zawiercie) and [Tarnowskie Góry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnowskie_G%C3%B3ry); [iron ore](145352.md) and raw materials for building – near [Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa). The most important regional industries are: mining, iron, lead, and zinc metallurgy, power industry, engineering, automobile, chemical, building materials, and textile. In the past, the Silesian economy was determined by coal mining. Now, considering the investment volume, car manufacturing is becoming more and more important. The most profitable company in the region is [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat) Auto-Poland S.A. in Bielsko-Biała with a revenue of [PLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_z%C5%82oty) 6.2 billion in 1997. Recently a new car factory has been opened by [GM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Corporation) [Opel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel) in Gliwice. There are two [Special Economic Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zone) in the area: Katowice and Częstochowa. The voivodeship's economy consists of about 323,000, mostly small and medium-sized enterprises, employing over 3 million people. The biggest Polish steelworks, "Huta Katowice", is situated in [Dąbrowa Górnicza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%85browa_G%C3%B3rnicza).

The unemployment rate stood at 3.9% in 2017 and was lower than the national average.[^cite_note-19]

## Transport



Terminal A at [Katowice International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice_International_Airport)

[Katowice International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice_International_Airport) (in [Tarnowskie Góry County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnowskie_G%C3%B3ry_County)) is used for domestic and international flights, with the other nearby airports being [John Paul II International Airport Kraków-Balice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II_International_Airport_Krak%C3%B3w-Balice). The Silesian agglomeration railway network has the largest concentration in the country.

The voivodship capital enjoys good railway and road connections with [Gdańsk](12099.md) ([motorway A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_autostrada_\(Poland\))) and [Ostrava](464078.md) (motorway A1), [Kraków](16815.md) ([motorway A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A4_\(Poland\))), [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw) (motorway A4), [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA) (motorway A1) and [Warsaw](32908.md). It is also the crossing point for many international routes like [E40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E40) connecting [Calais](85468.md), [Brussels](3708.md), [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden), [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw), [Kraków](16815.md) and [Kyiv](585629.md) and [E75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E75) from [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) to the [Balkans](4829.md). A relatively short distance to [Vienna](55866.md) facilitates cross-border co-operation and may positively influence the process of European integration.

[Linia Hutnicza Szerokotorowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Gauge_Metallurgy_Line) (known by its acronym *LHS*, English: *Broad gauge metallurgy line*) in [Sławków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82awk%C3%B3w) is the longest [broad gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_gauge) [railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_tracks) in Poland. The line runs on a single track for almost 400 km from the Polish-[Ukrainian](31750.md) border, crossing it just east of [Hrubieszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrubiesz%C3%B3w). It is the westernmost broad-gauge railway line in Europe that is connected to the broad-gauge rail system of the countries of the former Soviet Union.

A large part of the Upper Silesia conurbation features the [Silesian Interurbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Interurbans), the longest tram network in Poland, and one of the largest in the world. Bus and tram transport in and around Katowice and surrounding cities has been managed by the Metropolitan Transport Authority (ZTM) since 2019.

## Politics



Silesian Regional Assembly

Main article: [Silesian Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Regional_Assembly)

The Silesian voivodeship's government is headed by the province's *_voivode_* ([governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodes_of_Poland_\(since_1999\))) who is appointed by the [Polish Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Prime_Minister). The *_voivode_* is then assisted in performing his duties by the voivodeship's marshal, who is the appointed speaker for the voivodeship's executive and is elected by the *_sejmik_* (provincial assembly). The current *_voivode_* of Silesia is Jarosław Wieczorek, whilst the present marshal is Wojciech Saługa.

The Sejmik of Silesia consists of 48 members.

## Administrative division

## Curiosities

- [Bytom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bytom), [Cieszyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cieszyn), [Racibórz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racib%C3%B3rz) and [Siewierz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siewierz) are former medieval ducal seats of the [Piast dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_dynasty).

- One of the three parish churches of the [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) in Poland is located in [Gliwice](74600.md) (see also: *[Armenians in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Poland)*).
