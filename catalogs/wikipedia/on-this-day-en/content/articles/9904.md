The **England national football team**, nicknamed **the Three Lions**, have represented [England](9316.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) since the first international match in 1872. They are governed by [The Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) (FA), the governing body for [football in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_England), which is affiliated with [UEFA](32332.md) and [FIFA](11049.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] England competes in three major international tournaments: the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) and [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League).

England and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_men's_national_football_team) are together the oldest national football teams in the world, having played in the world's [first international football match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1872_Scotland_v_England_football_match) in 1872. England plays home matches at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) in London, and their training headquarters is [St George's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's_Park_National_Football_Centre) in Burton upon Trent. [Thomas Tuchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel) is the current head coach.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

England won their lone [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) title on home soil in [1966](61629.md), making them one of eight nations to have won the World Cup. They have qualified for the World Cup seventeen times, finishing in third place in [2026](36581929.md), and fourth place in [1990](157232.md) and [2018](13327177.md). England have never won the European Championship, with their best performances to date being runners-up finishes in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). As a constituent [country of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries_of_the_United_Kingdom), England are not a member of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (as English athletes compete for [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_at_the_Olympics)), and so do not compete at the [Olympic Games](22576.md). England are the only team to have won the World Cup at senior level but not their own major continental title, and the only team representing a non–sovereign state to have won the World Cup. England shares football rivalries [with Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England%E2%80%93Scotland_football_rivalry), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England%E2%80%93Germany_football_rivalry), and [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93England_football_rivalry).

## History

Main article: [History of the England national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_England_national_football_team)

### Early years

The England men's national football team is the joint-oldest in the world; it was formed at the same time as [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_men's_national_football_team). A [representative match between England and Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_v_Scotland_representative_football_matches_\(1870%E2%80%931872\)) was played on 5 March 1870, having been organised by [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association).[^cite_note-5] A return fixture was organised by representatives of Scottish football teams on 30 November 1872. This match, played at [Hamilton Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Crescent) in Scotland, is viewed as the first official international football match, because the two teams were independently selected and operated, rather than being the work of a single football association.[^cite_note-6] Over the next 40 years, England played exclusively with the other three [Home Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Nations)—Scotland, [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_football_team_\(1882%E2%80%931950\))—in the [British Home Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Home_Championship).

At first, England had no permanent home stadium. They joined [FIFA](11049.md) in 1906 and played their first games against countries other than the Home Nations on a tour of Central Europe in 1908.[^cite_note-A_history_of_the_FA-7] [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)) was opened in 1923 and became their home ground.[^cite_note-A_history_of_the_FA-7] The relationship between England and FIFA became strained, and this resulted in their departure from FIFA in 1928, before they rejoined in 1946.[^cite_note-8] As a result, they did not compete in a [World Cup](11370.md) until [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), in which they were [beaten 1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v_England_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\)) by the [United States](582488.md), failing to get past the first round in one of the most embarrassing defeats in the team's history.[^cite_note-9]

Their first defeat on home soil to a foreign team was a [2–0 loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_England_v_Ireland_football_match) to [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), on 21 September 1949 at [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park).[^cite_note-10] A [6–3 loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_of_the_Century_\(1953_England_v_Hungary_football_match\)) in 1953 to [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) was their second defeat by a foreign team at Wembley.[^cite_note-11] In the return match in [Budapest](36787.md), Hungary [won 7–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Hungary_v_England_football_match). This stands as England's largest ever defeat. After the game, a bewildered [Syd Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syd_Owen) said, "it was like playing men from outer space".[^cite_note-12] In the [1954 FIFA World Cup](60523.md), England reached the quarter-finals for the first time, and lost 4–2 to reigning champions [Uruguay](679782.md).[^cite_note-13]

### Walter Winterbottom and Alf Ramsey

Although [Walter Winterbottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Winterbottom) was appointed as England's first full-time manager in 1946, the team was still picked by a committee until [Alf Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Ramsey) took over in 1963.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The [1966 World Cup](61629.md) was hosted in England and Ramsey guided England to victory with a [4–2 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final) against [West Germany](250204.md) after [extra time](633242.md) in the final, during which [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) scored a [hat-trick](411541.md).[^cite_note-16] At [UEFA Euro 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968) in Italy, the team reached the semi-finals for the first time, being eliminated by [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-17]

England qualified automatically for the [1970 World Cup](157227.md) in Mexico as reigning champions, and reached the quarter-finals, where they were knocked out by West Germany. England had been 2–0 up, but were eventually beaten 3–2 after [extra time](633242.md).[^cite_note-18] They then failed to qualify for [Euro 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972) and the [1974 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), leading to Ramsey's dismissal by the [FA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association).[^cite_note-19]

### Don Revie, Ron Greenwood and Bobby Robson

Following Ramsey's dismissal, [Joe Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Mercer) took immediate temporary charge of England for a seven-match spell until [Don Revie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Revie) was appointed as new permanent manager in 1974.[^cite_note-20] Under Revie, the team underperformed and failed to qualify for either [Euro 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1976) or the [1978 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-21] Revie resigned in 1977 and was replaced by [Ron Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Greenwood), under whom performances improved. The team qualified for [Euro 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980) without losing any of their games, but exited in the group stage of the final tournament in Italy.[^cite_note-22] They also qualified for the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) in Spain; despite not losing a game, they were eliminated at the second group stage.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

[Bobby Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Robson) managed England from 1982 to 1990.[^cite_note-25] Although the team failed to qualify for [Euro 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984), they reached the quarter-finals of the [1986 World Cup](157231.md), losing 2–1 to [Argentina](454699.md) in a [game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)) made famous by two highly contrasting goals scored by [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) – the first being blatantly knocked in by his hand, prompting his "[Hand of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_hand_of_God)" remark, the second being an outstandingly skilful individual goal, involving high speed dribbling past several opponents.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] England striker [Gary Lineker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Lineker) finished as the tournament's top scorer with six goals.[^cite_note-28]

England went on to lose every match at [Euro 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988).[^cite_note-29] They next achieved their second best result in the [1990 World Cup](157232.md) by finishing fourth – losing again to West Germany after a closely contested semi-final finishing 1–1 after [extra time](633242.md), then 3–4 in England's first [penalty shoot-out](511302.md).[^cite_note-30] Despite losing to [Italy](362466.md) in the match for third place, the members of the England team were given bronze medals identical to the Italians'. Due to the team's good performance at the tournament against general expectations, and the emotional nature of the narrow defeat to West Germany,[^cite_note-31] the team were welcomed home as heroes and thousands of people lined the streets for an open-top bus parade.[^cite_note-32]

### Graham Taylor, Terry Venables, Glenn Hoddle and Kevin Keegan

The 1990s saw four England managers follow Robson, each in the role for a relatively brief period. [Graham Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Taylor_\(footballer\)) was Robson's immediate successor.[^cite_note-33] England failed to win any matches at [Euro 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1992), drawing with tournament winners [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and later with [France](250197.md), before being eliminated by host nation [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team). The team then failed to qualify for the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) after losing a controversial game against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in Rotterdam, which resulted in Taylor's resignation. Taylor faced much newspaper criticism during his tenure for his tactics and team selections.[^cite_note-34]

Between 1994 and 1996, [Terry Venables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Venables) took charge of the team. Hosting [Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996), they equalled their best performance at a [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship), reaching the semi-finals as they did in 1968, before exiting via another penalty shoot-out loss to Germany.[^cite_note-35] England striker [Alan Shearer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shearer) was the tournament's top scorer with five goals.[^cite_note-36] At Euro 96, the song "[Three Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Lions_\(song\))" by [Baddiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baddiel), [Skinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Skinner) and [the Lightning Seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lightning_Seeds) became the definitive anthem for fans on the terraces,[^cite_note-37] and popularised the chant "it's coming home".[^cite_note-38] Venables announced before the tournament that he would resign at the end of it, following investigations into his personal financial activities and ahead of upcoming court cases. Due to the controversy around him, the FA stressed that he was the coach, not the manager, of the team.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Under Venables's successor, [Glenn Hoddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Hoddle), England won the [1997 Tournoi de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Tournoi_de_France), which remains their only tournament win since the 1966 World Cup.[^cite_note-Brewin_2024-41] He then took the team to the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) — in which England were eliminated in the second round, again by Argentina and again on penalties (after a 2–2 draw).[^cite_note-42] In February 1999, Hoddle was sacked by the FA due to controversial comments he had made about disabled people to a newspaper.[^cite_note-43] [Howard Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Wilkinson) took over as caretaker manager for two matches.[^cite_note-44] [Kevin Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Keegan) was then appointed as the new permanent manager and took England to [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), but the team exited in the group stage and he unexpectedly resigned shortly afterwards.[^cite_note-45]

### Sven-Göran Eriksson and Steve McClaren



The England team at the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)

[Peter Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Taylor_\(footballer,_born_1953\)) was appointed as caretaker manager for one match, before [Sven-Göran Eriksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven-G%C3%B6ran_Eriksson) took charge between 2001 and 2006, and was the team's first non-English manager.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Although England's players in this era were dubbed a "[golden generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Generation_\(English_football\))" and only lost five competitive matches during Eriksson's tenure,[^cite_note-48] they exited at the quarter-finals of the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), [Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) and the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-49] In January 2006 it was announced that Eriksson would leave the role following that year's World Cup.[^cite_note-50] [Steve McClaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_McClaren) was selected to succeed Eriksson, but was sacked on 22 November 2007 after just 18 matches in charge as England failed to qualify for [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008).[^cite_note-51] McClaren was criticised for his team selection in his final game – a decisive qualifier against [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) which England lost 3–2 – particularly the decision to select inexperienced goalkeeper [Scott Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Carson), whose mistake lead to Croatia's first goal.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Fabio Capello, Roy Hodgson and Sam Allardyce

On 14 December 2007, Italian manager [Fabio Capello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Capello) was appointed as McClaren's successor, becoming only the second foreign coach to take the job.[^cite_note-54] At the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), England were considered favourites to top [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C)[^cite_note-55] but drew their opening two games against the United States and [Algeria](1029528.md); this led to questions about the team's spirit, tactics and ability to handle pressure.[^cite_note-56] Despite this, England progressed to the round of 16, where they were beaten 4–1 by [Germany](250204.md), their heaviest defeat in a World Cup finals tournament match.[^cite_note-57] This match became infamous for a [ghost goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_goal#England_v_Germany_at_the_2010_World_Cup) when [Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard) hit a shot from outside the penalty area that bounced down off the crossbar and over the goal line before being cleared by German goalkeeper [Manuel Neuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Neuer), with neither the referee nor the assistant opting to award a goal. Had it been given, the goal would have tied the game 2–2 with England coming from two goals down. This incident – along with similar mistakes at the tournament – led to an apology from FIFA president [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter) and was a factor in the subsequent decision to introduce [goal-line technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal-line_technology) into football.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-58][^cite_note-59] Capello continued as England manager, leading the team's successful [qualifying campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying_Group_G) for [Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_2012), before resigning from the role in February 2012 following a disagreement with the [FA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Football_Association) over their request to remove [John Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Terry) from the team captaincy following [accusations of racial abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_v_Terry) against the player.[^cite_note-60]

Following Capello's departure, [Stuart Pearce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Pearce) was appointed as caretaker manager for one match, after which in May 2012, [Roy Hodgson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Hodgson) was announced as the new manager, just six weeks before Euro 2012.[^cite_note-61] England managed to finish top of [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_Group_D), but exited the European Championship in the quarter-finals via a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) against [Italy](362466.md).[^cite_note-62] In the [2014 World Cup](656933.md), England were eliminated at the group stage for the first time since 1958.[^cite_note-63] At [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016), England were eliminated in the round of 16, [losing 2–1 to Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_v_Iceland_\(UEFA_Euro_2016\))[^cite_note-64] in a result that has been described as among their worst ever defeats.[^cite_note-65] Hodgson tendered his resignation shortly after the full-time whistle,[^cite_note-66] with [Sam Allardyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Allardyce) announced as his successor in July 2016.[^cite_note-67] After one match and only 67 days in charge, Allardyce resigned from his managerial post by mutual agreement following an [alleged breach of FA rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_English_football_scandal). This makes Allardyce the shortest serving permanent England manager.[^cite_note-BBC_News_27_September_2016-68]

### Gareth Southgate and Thomas Tuchel



The England line-up before the last match of group G against [Belgium](679716.md), 28 June 2018

After Allardyce's resignation, [Gareth Southgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gareth_Southgate), then the coach of the [England under-21 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_under-21_team), was put in temporary charge of the national team until November 2016,[^cite_note-Gareth_Southgate:_Interim_England_manager_wants_future_decided_within_a_month-69] before being given the position on a permanent basis at the end of that period.[^cite_note-Former_defender_signs_four-year_deal_to_lead_Three_Lions-70] At the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md), England reached the semi-finals for the third time. After finishing second in their group, England faced [Colombia](680175.md) in the round of 16 where they won on penalties for the first time at a World Cup, before beating [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] In the semi-final, they were beaten 2–1 in extra time by [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) and finished fourth after losing the match for third place against [Belgium](679716.md).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] England striker [Harry Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Kane) finished the tournament as top scorer with six goals and was awarded the [golden boot](13327177.md#Awards).[^cite_note-76]

On 14 November 2019, England played their 1000th international match, defeating [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro_national_football_team) 7–0 at Wembley in a [Euro 2020 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying) match.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]



England cap awarded to [Harry Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Kane) for his appearance against Germany at Euro 2020, his 58th overall

At the delayed [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), England reached their first European Championship final, and their first final at a major tournament since 1966.[^cite_note-bbc_2021-07-07-79] After finishing top of their group above Croatia, [Czechia](729739.md), and Scotland, the Three Lions beat Germany, [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team) and [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) in the knockout rounds to advance to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_final).[^cite_note-bbc_2021-07-07-79] In the final held at Wembley, England were defeated by Italy on penalties after a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-80]

At the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md), England defeated [Iran](272865.md) and Wales in the group stage to qualify for the round of 16.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] In the round of 16, the Three Lions defeated [Senegal](1017436.md) 3–0,[^cite_note-83] but were then eliminated by reigning world champions France in the quarter-finals, 2–1.[^cite_note-84] In that match, Harry Kane scored his 53rd goal for England, equalling the all-time record at the time.[^cite_note-85]

At [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), England finished top of [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_Group_C) above Denmark, [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team), and [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team). In the round of 16, England defeated [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team) 2–1 after extra time, with [Jude Bellingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Bellingham) scoring with a [bicycle kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_kick) in second-half stoppage time to equalise the match.[^cite_note-86] In the quarter-final, England beat [Switzerland](681862.md) on penalties after the game finished 1–1.[^cite_note-87] The Three Lions reached their second consecutive European Championship final after defeating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) 2–1 in the semi-final.[^cite_note-88] In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_final), England were defeated 2–1 by [Spain](609825.md), becoming the first team to lose consecutive European Championship finals.[^cite_note-89] With three goals, Harry Kane was the joint top scorer at the tournament and shared the [golden boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship_awards#Top_goalscorer) with five other players.[^cite_note-90]

Whilst the FA were willing to extend his contract further, Southgate announced his resignation as England manager on 16 July 2024, saying that it was "time for change, and for a new chapter".[^cite_note-Steinberg_July_2024-91] On 16 October 2024, the FA announced that German manager [Thomas Tuchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel) would take over as manager from 1 January 2025, becoming the third foreign coach to take up the position.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

Under Tuchel, England became the first European nation to secure qualification for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after winning all six of their qualification matches without conceding a goal as of 14 October 2025.[^cite_note-92] In the tournament, England were ultimately eliminated by the defending champions Argentina in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-93] In the [third place match](81667005.md#Match_for_third_place) versus [France](250197.md) on 18 July, England won 6–4, achieving their best finish at the tournament since 1966.[^cite_note-94]

#### Crest



The lion *passant guardant* used in the logo of the England national football team

The motif of the England national football team has three lions *passant guardant*, the emblem of King [Richard I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England), who reigned from 1189 to 1199.[^cite_note-105] In 1872, English players wore white jerseys emblazoned with the three lions crest of the Football Association.[^cite_note-kits-106] The lions, often blue, have had minor changes to colour and appearance.[^cite_note-107] Initially topped by a crown, this was removed in 1949 when the FA was given an official coat of arms by the [College of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Arms); this introduced ten [Tudor roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_rose), one for each of the regional branches of the FA.[^cite_note-kits-106][^cite_note-108] Since 2003, England top their logo with a [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_\(football_badge\)) to recognise their World Cup win in 1966; this was first embroidered onto the left sleeve of the home kit, and a year later was moved to its current position, first on the away shirt.[^cite_note-109]

#### Colours

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [England national football team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/England%20national%20football%20team%20kits).



England shirt for the [1966 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final)

England's traditional home colours are white shirts, navy blue shorts and white or black socks. The team has periodically worn an all-white kit.

Although England's first [away kits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_kit) were blue, England's traditional away colours are red shirts, white shorts and red socks. In 1996, England's away kit was changed to grey shirts, shorts and socks. This kit was only worn three times, including against [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_National_Football_Team) in the semi-final of [Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_1996) but the deviation from the traditional red was unpopular with supporters and the England away kit remained red until 2011, when a navy blue away kit was introduced. The away kit is also sometimes worn during home matches, when a new edition has been released to promote it.

England have occasionally had a third kit. At the 1970 World Cup, England wore a third kit with pale blue shirts, shorts and socks against [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team). They had a kit similar to [Brazil](149286.md)'s, with yellow shirts, yellow socks and blue shorts which they wore in the summer of 1973. For the 1986 World Cup, England had a third kit of pale blue, imitating that worn in Mexico 16 years earlier and England retained pale blue third kits until 1992, but they were rarely used.

[Umbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbro) first agreed to manufacture the kit in 1954 and since then has supplied most of the kits, the exceptions being from 1959 to 1965 with [Bukta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukta) and 1974–1984 with [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Sportswear). [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) purchased Umbro in 2008 and took over as kit supplier in 2013 following their sale of the Umbro brand.[^cite_note-110]

### Home stadium

Main article: [England national football team home stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_football_team_home_stadium)



[Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) during a friendly match between England and [Germany](250204.md)

For the first 50 years of their existence, England played their home matches all around the country. They initially used [cricket](25675557.md) grounds before later moving on to football club stadiums. The original [Empire Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)) was built in [Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley), London, for the [British Empire Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire_Exhibition).[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

England played their first match at the stadium in 1924 against Scotland[^cite_note-113] and for the next 27 years Wembley was used as a venue for matches against Scotland only. The stadium later became known simply as Wembley Stadium and it became England's permanent home stadium during the 1950s. In October 2000, the stadium closed its doors, ending with a [defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_England_v_Germany_football_match) against Germany.[^cite_note-114]

This stadium was demolished during the period of 2002–03, and work began to completely rebuild it.[^cite_note-115] During this time, England played at venues across the country, though by the time of the [2006 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_6), this had largely settled down to having [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.)'s [Old Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford) stadium as the primary venue, with [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.)'s [St. James' Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._James'_Park,_Newcastle) used on occasions when Old Trafford was unavailable.[^cite_note-116]

Their first match in the new [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) was in March 2007 when they drew with Brazil.[^cite_note-117] The stadium is now owned by the Football Association, via its subsidiary Wembley National Stadium Limited.[^cite_note-118]

### Rivalries

England's three main rivalries are with [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team), [Germany](250204.md), and [Argentina](454699.md).[^cite_note-119] Smaller rivalries with [France](250197.md), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team), and the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) have also been observed.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

#### Scotland

Main article: [England–Scotland football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England%E2%80%93Scotland_football_rivalry)

England's rivalry with Scotland is one of the fiercest international rivalries that exists.[^cite_note-HistoryFierce-123][^cite_note-124] It is the oldest international fixture in the world, [first played in 1872](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1872_Scotland_v_England_football_match) at [Hamilton Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Crescent), Glasgow.[^cite_note-125] The [history of the British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_British_Isles) has led to much rivalry between the nations in many forms, and the social and cultural effects of centuries of antagonism and conflict between the two has contributed to the intense nature of the sporting contests. [Scottish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_national_identity) has also been a factor in the Scots' desire to defeat England above all other rivals, with Scottish [sports journalists](558219.md) traditionally referring to the English as the "Auld Enemy".[^cite_note-126] The footballing rivalry has diminished somewhat since the late 1970s, particularly since the annual fixture stopped in 1989. For England, games against Germany and Argentina are now considered to be more important than the historic rivalry with Scotland.[^cite_note-127]

#### Germany

Main article: [England–Germany football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England%E2%80%93Germany_football_rivalry)

England's rivalry with Germany is considered to be mainly an English phenomenon—in the run-up to any competition match between the two teams, many UK newspapers will print articles detailing results of previous encounters, such as England's win in the [1966 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final) and the semi-final penalty shoot-out defeats of the [1990 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#West_Germany_vs_England) and [Euro 96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996_knockout_stage).[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] This rivalry has diminished significantly since the early 2020s.[^cite_note-130] Germans consider [Italy](362466.md), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), and France to be their greater rivals.

#### Argentina

Main article: [Argentina–England football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93England_football_rivalry)

England's rivalry with Argentina is highly competitive. Games between the two teams, even those that are only [friendly matches](977090.md), are often marked by notable and sometimes controversial incidents such as the [hand of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_hand_of_God) in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)).[^cite_note-goal-131][^cite_note-Arg-132] The rivalry is unusual in that it is an intercontinental one; typically such footballing rivalries exist between bordering nations. England is regarded in Argentina as one of the major rivals of the national football team, matched only by [Brazil](149286.md) and [Uruguay](679782.md).[^cite_note-Arg-132] The rivalry is, to a lesser extent reciprocal in England, locally described as a *grudge match* although matches against Germany carry a greater significance in popular perception. The rivalry emerged across several games during the latter half of the 20th century, even though as of 2008 the teams have played each other on only 14 occasions in full internationals.[^cite_note-11v11-133] The rivalry was intensified, particularly in Argentina, by non-footballing events, especially the 1982 [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) between Argentina and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-134] England and Argentina last met in the [2026 FIFA World Cup semi-final](81667005.md#England_vs_Argentina), with Argentina winning 2–1 to reach the [final](75845879.md), after going a goal down.[^cite_note-11v11-133]

### Media coverage

All England matches are broadcast with full commentary on [Talksport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talksport) and [BBC Radio 5 Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_5_Live). From the [2008–09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_in_English_football) season until the 2017–18 season, England's home and away qualifiers, and friendlies both home and away were broadcast live on [ITV Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Sport) (often with the exception of [STV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STV_\(TV_network\)), the ITV franchisee in central and northern Scotland). England's away qualifiers for the 2010 World Cup were shown on [Setanta Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setanta_Sports) until that company's collapse. As a result of Setanta Sports's demise, England's World Cup qualifier in Ukraine on 10 October 2009 was shown in the United Kingdom on a pay-per-view basis via the internet only. This one-off event was the first time an England game had been screened in such a way. The number of subscribers, paying between £4.99 and £11.99 each, was estimated at between 250,000 and 300,000 and the total number of viewers at around 500,000.[^cite_note-135] In 2018, [Sky Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sports) broadcast the England UEFA Nations League and in-season friendlies, until 2021 and [ITV Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Sport) broadcast the European qualifiers for Euro-World Cups and pre-tournament friendlies (after the Nations League group matches end), until 2022.[^cite_note-136] In April 2022, [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4) won the rights for England matches until June 2024, including [2022–23 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Nations_League) matches, [Euro 2024 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying) games, and friendlies. 2022 World Cup rights remained with the BBC and ITV.[^cite_note-137]

### FIFA World Cup

Main article: [England at the FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_at_the_FIFA_World_Cup)



1966 World Cup final lineups: England (red) vs. West Germany

England first appeared at the [1950 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), and have subsequently qualified for a total of 17 World Cup tournaments, tied for sixth best by [number of appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_team_appearances_in_the_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] They are also placed sixth by number of wins, with 38. The national team is one of only eight nations to have won at least one FIFA World Cup title.[^cite_note-162] The England team won their first and only World Cup title in [1966](61629.md).[^cite_note-WC_1966-163] The tournament was played on home soil, and England defeated [West Germany](250204.md) 4–2 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-WC_1966-163] Their highest placing since occurred in [2026](36581929.md), where they placed third. In [1990](157232.md), England finished in fourth place, losing 2–1 to host nation [Italy](362466.md) in the match for third place, following defeat on penalties, after extra time, to champions [West Germany](250204.md) in the semi-final.[^cite_note-164] They also finished in fourth place in [2018](13327177.md), losing 2–0 to [Belgium](679716.md) in the match for third place, following a 2–1 defeat to [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), again after extra time, in the semi-final.[^cite_note-165] The team also reached the quarter-final stage in [1954](60523.md), [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), [1970](157227.md), [1986](157231.md), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2022](17742072.md).[^cite_note-166]

England failed to qualify for the World Cup in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-167] The team's earliest exit in the finals tournament was elimination in the first round in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) and, most recently, [2014](656933.md).[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-Brazil_2014-169] This was after being defeated in both their opening two matches for the first time, against [Italy](362466.md) and [Uruguay](679782.md) in Group D.[^cite_note-Brazil_2014-169] In 1950, four teams remained after the first round, in 1958 eight teams remained and in 2014 sixteen teams remained. In [2010](168079.md), England suffered its most resounding World Cup defeat, 4–1 to [Germany](250204.md), in the round of 16 stage.[^cite_note-170]

### UEFA European Championship

Main article: [England at the UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_at_the_UEFA_European_Championship)



Euro 2020 final lineups: England (white) vs. Italy



Euro 2024 final lineups: England (white) vs. Spain

England first entered the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) in 1964,[^cite_note-EUROapps-177] and have since qualified for eleven tournaments,[^cite_note-EUROapps-177] tied for [fourth-best by number of finals appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship_records_and_statistics#Ranking_of_teams_by_number_of_appearances). England's best results at the tournament were finishing as runners-up in both the [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) (held in 2021) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) editions, followed by a third-place finish in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968)[^cite_note-UEFA-178] and reaching the semi-finals of [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996), a tournament they hosted.[^cite_note-179] In addition, England have reached the quarter-finals on two further occasions, in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012).[^cite_note-UEFA-178]

England's worst results in the finals tournament to date have been first round eliminations in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980), [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988), [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1992) and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), whilst they failed to qualify for the finals in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1964), [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972), [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1976), [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984) and [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008).[^cite_note-EUROapps-177]
