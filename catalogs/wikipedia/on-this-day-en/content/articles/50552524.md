From March 9 to March 28, 2026, members of the [New Democratic Party of Canada](19283982.md) (NDP) voted in a [leadership election](4527291.md) to choose a permanent leader to replace [Don Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Davies), who became leader on an interim basis following the resignation of [Jagmeet Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagmeet_Singh). Singh announced his resignation after being defeated in the [2025 Canadian federal election](68483222.md).[^cite_note-6]

On March 29, [Avi Lewis](547837.md), the grandson of former party leader [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lewis_\(Canadian_politician\)) and son of former Ontario NDP leader [Stephen Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lewis), was announced as the winner of the [ranked-choice voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting) (RCV) election with 56% of the first ballot vote. The election saw the highest number of votes cast and largest margin of victory in an NDP leadership election under the [one member, one vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_member,_one_vote) system.[^cite_note-7]

## Background

[Jagmeet Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagmeet_Singh) was elected as the [leader of the New Democratic Party](79890122.md) (NDP) in the [2017 leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_New_Democratic_Party_leadership_election).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] He led the party in the [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Canadian_federal_election), [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_federal_election), and [2025 federal elections](68483222.md). On March 22, 2022, the NDP reached [a confidence and supply agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal%E2%80%93NDP_confidence_and_supply_agreement) with the governing [Liberal Party of Canada](149536.md), agreeing to support the government until June 2025 in exchange for specific policy commitments.[^cite_note-10] On September 4, 2024, the NDP withdrew from their confidence-and-supply agreement with the Liberals, though they did not commit to a [motion of non-confidence](208101.md).[^cite_note-11]

While the NDP under Singh stagnated in [most opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_45th_Canadian_federal_election) following the [2021 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_federal_election), the party experienced a brief jump in support to second place following the collapse of the governing [Liberal Party](149536.md), during the [2024–2025 political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Canadian_political_crisis). However, following Prime Minister [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau)'s resignation in January and [Mark Carney](13571049.md)'s [election as Liberal leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Liberal_Party_of_Canada_leadership_election), the NDP collapsed in most polls, with most of its support going to the Liberals.[^cite_note-12]

At the [2025 federal election](68483222.md), Singh led the NDP to its worst result in party history, both in seat count and popular vote, losing [official party status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_party_status) and [himself having been defeated in the riding of Burnaby Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby_Central_in_the_2025_Canadian_federal_election). On election night, he announced that he would resign as party leader.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] On May 5, 2025, he was replaced by [Vancouver Kingsway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Kingsway) MP [Don Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Davies) on an interim basis until a new party leader is elected.[^cite_note-auto-15]

## Rules

Under rules set out in the party's constitution, every member is entitled to cast a secret ballot for the selection of the leader. The new leader will be chosen at a leadership convention through a combination of ranked ballots and round-by-round voting. If a leader is not chosen in the first round, additional vote counts will occur until a candidate obtains 50 per cent plus one vote and is declared the leader. Voters can vote online, by telephone, by mail, and in-person at the convention, with the voting period being from March 9 to 28.

Candidates will be required to pay a \$100,000 entry fee, which will be due in four instalments,[^cite_note-Rules-4] and abide by a \$1,500,000 spending limit. The \$100,000 entrance fee has been criticized by some of the party's grassroots.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Jacobin-18] The entrance fee marked a \$70,000 increase over the prior \$30,000 entrance fee in the 2017 NDP leadership election.[^cite_note-Jacobin-18] One quarter of all donations to candidates will be paid to the party. To be nominated, candidates require at least 500 signatures from party members, at least half of which must be from female-identified members and at least 100 from "other equity-seeking groups," including Indigenous people, LGBTQIA2S+ people, persons with disabilities, and visible minorities. At least 50 signatures will be required from each of five regions: the [Atlantic](71095.md), [Quebec](7954867.md), [Ontario](22218.md), [the Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies), and [British Columbia](3392.md)/[the North](79987.md). In addition, 10 per cent of the signatures must come from [Canada's Young New Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada's_Young_New_Democrats).&#x20;

Members must join the party at least 60 days prior to the vote, an earlier eligibility cutoff compared to the 45 days prescribed by the rules of the 2017 contest and the 35 days in the 2012 contest.[^cite_note-NDPrules-19][^cite_note-postrules-20][^cite_note-globerules-21][^cite_note-22]

Because membership is controlled by provincial and territorial branches, the [voting age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_age) varies from 12 to 14 years old, depending on where the member lives.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

Due to allegations of [foreign interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_interference_in_elections) in past Canadian federal leadership races, the party's federal council announced in July 2025 that "strong regulations will govern the role of third parties in the race."[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Campaign

Following the party's poor election showing, several commentators and party members pointed to organizational and strategic shortcomings, including leadership fatigue, messaging issues, overreliance on social media and [identity politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics),[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] and difficulty balancing support between [urban progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Progressivism) and [working-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) voters.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Some called for renewed grassroots engagement and stronger ties to [organized labour](17626.md),[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] while others suggested the creation of a New Progressive Party, possibly including closer cooperation or a merger with the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Canada) to consolidate progressive support.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Failure to obtain over 10% of the vote in all but 46 [ridings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democratic_Party_candidates_in_the_2025_Canadian_federal_election) meant many candidates did not receive campaign reimbursements, exacerbating concerns about financial challenges.[^cite_note-36] Internal disputes and a shrinking activist base further complicated the party's path forward ahead of the 2026 leadership race.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Following Singh's announcement, political commentators suggested that several figures were likely candidates: [Alexandre Boulerice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Boulerice), [Nathan Cullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Cullen), [Leah Gazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leah_Gazan), [Matthew Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Green_\(Canadian_politician\)), [Jenny Kwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Kwan), [Avi Lewis](547837.md), [Heather McPherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_McPherson_\(politician\)), [Rachel Notley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Notley), and [Valérie Plante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val%C3%A9rie_Plante).[^cite_note-\:0-41][^cite_note-\:3-42] Boulerice, Cullen, Green, Notley, and Plante all declined to run before the start of the campaign period. McPherson's supporters publicly called on her to run in the election shortly following Singh's resignation.[^cite_note-43] In late August 2025, representatives on behalf of Lewis and McPherson began collecting signatures before the launch of the leadership election on September 2.[^cite_note-Ramzy-44]

On June 23, Tony McQuail, an environmentalist and farmer, announced his campaign. He was approved to run on October 9. He suspended fundraising on November 20, to prevent splitting grassroots donations with Tanille Johnston.[^cite_note-\:12-45][^cite_note-46] Shortly before the first debate on November 27, he resumed fundraising. McQuail would again suspend fundraising on December 23, with him resuming on December 28.[^cite_note-\:13-47][^cite_note-\:TM-48]

On July 3, activist [Yves Engler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yves_Engler) announced his candidacy, backed by the [NDP Socialist Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDP_Socialist_Caucus); he submitted his application to the NDP's leadership vote committee for vetting on November 10.[^cite_note-Engler-49] His application was rejected on December 8, 2025. However, his wife Bianca Mugyenyi announced her candidacy shortly before the deadline to apply on January 1, 2026.[^cite_note-\:14-50] Her application was also rejected on January 28 on the grounds of being a "proxy candidate".[^cite_note-\:21-51]

On September 19, [Avi Lewis](547837.md), an activist and journalist, announced his campaign.[^cite_note-\:5-52] Lewis is also the son of former [Ontario NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_New_Democratic_Party) leader [Stephen Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lewis) and grandson of former federal NDP leader [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lewis_\(Canadian_politician\)). His campaign launch was held in [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-\:5-52]

In an interview on May 20 with [CBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News)' *[Power & Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_&_Politics)*, [Heather McPherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_McPherson_\(politician\)), the MP for [Edmonton Strathcona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Strathcona), confirmed she was seriously considering running for the leadership.[^cite_note-53] She announced her leadership bid on September 28, at an event in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton).[^cite_note-54] During the campaign, she faced criticism due to her comments on "purity tests" in the party.[^cite_note-55]

On September 30, it was reported that McPherson had raised nearly \$100,000, followed by Lewis with around \$55,500, Ashton with around \$25,000, and Johnston with around \$1,700. Lewis reported having the most individual donors with 353, followed by McPherson with 231 and Ashton with 45.[^cite_note-56]

On October 1, Rob Ashton, president of the [International Longshore and Warehouse Union Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Longshore_and_Warehouse_Union_Canada), announced his campaign in [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-57] His campaign would notably be endorsed by the [United Steelworkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Steelworkers) trade union.

On October 8, Tanille Johnston, [city councillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Canada) in [Campbell River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_River,_British_Columbia), announced her campaign in [Nanaimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaimo).[^cite_note-\:8-58] Johnston, a member of the [We Wai Kai First Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Wai_Kai_Nation),[^cite_note-59] is the first Indigenous woman to seek the NDP leadership.

The first all-candidates forum took place in [Nanaimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaimo) on October 16,[^cite_note-60] with Rob Ashton and Tanille Johnston attending in person, and the other candidates speaking via Zoom.[^cite_note-61] The [Canadian Labour Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Labour_Congress) hosted the first fully in-person forum on October 22, in [Ottawa](22219.md).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-auto3-63] Ashton, Lewis, and McPherson gathered in [Ottawa](22219.md) for the [Douglas Coldwell Layton Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Coldwell_Layton_Foundation) leadership forum on October 28, while McQuail and Johnston attended virtually.[^cite_note-64] On November 22, all the candidates, except Tony McQuail, gathered virtually in [Gibsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibsons) for a leadership forum.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-\:11-66] On November 27, the candidates all gathered in [Montreal](7954681.md) for the first debate; it was held using a mix of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The debate was noted for none of the candidates being fluent in French, with most reading prepared French statements before switching to English.[^cite_note-p574-69] The debate was followed by an all-candidates forum hosted by the [Ontario NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_NDP)'s northern caucus in December 2025, and a town hall hosted by the [Canadian Union of Public Employees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Union_of_Public_Employees) on January 7, 2026.[^cite_note-y551-70] On January 10, the [Ontario New Democratic Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_New_Democratic_Youth) hosted an in-person debate in [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-\:17-71] Johnston, Lewis, and McQuail attended a forum hosted by the Democratic Socialists of [Vancouver](32706.md) on January 15.[^cite_note-\:19-72] The [Canadians for Justice and Peace in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians_for_Justice_and_Peace_in_the_Middle_East) hosted a virtual debate on January 21, with all candidates except Ashton attending. Another forum was held in [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary), on February 12.[^cite_note-\:20-73] The second official leadership debate took place in [New Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Westminster) on February 19.[^cite_note-74] The next day on February 20, an in-person debate was held in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia).[^cite_note-\:22-75] The [Elmwood—Transcona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmwood%E2%80%94Transcona) NDP [EDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding_association) hosted an all-candidates forum on February 25.[^cite_note-\:23-76] On March 2, [Leadnow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadnow) hosted a virtual debate.[^cite_note-\:24-77] The NDP Equity Commissions hosted a virtual forum on March 11.[^cite_note-\:26-78] The [Edmonton Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Centre) NDP [EDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding_association) hosted an all-candidates forum in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton) on March 13.[^cite_note-\:27-79] The Jeunes néodémocrates du Québec (Young New Democrats of Quebec) hosted a virtual all-candidates forum on March 15.[^cite_note-\:28-80]

At the end of December 2025, [Elections Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_Canada) reported that Lewis had raised \$778,869, with McPherson trailing at \$415,490. Ashton had raised \$231,095, Johnston had raised \$142,129, and McQuail had raised \$95,093.[^cite_note-81] By the end of January 2026, it was reported that Lewis's campaign had raised over \$1,000,000.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

On January 11, Ashton's campaign was criticized for [answering questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R/IAmA) on [Reddit](3829005.md) using [generative AI](73291755.md), despite his criticism of the technology.[^cite_note-\:25-84] He previously made headlines for stating that Lewis's style of leadership would "divide" the party.[^cite_note-85]

In March 2026, the party reported that its membership had risen to approximately 100,000 before the vote.[^cite_note-VotingBegins-86] On March 11, 2026, [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut_\(electoral_district\)) MP [Lori Idlout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Idlout) defected from the NDP to the [Liberal Party](149536.md); she had previously appeared at a Lewis campaign event in Ottawa.[^cite_note-87] On March 16, 2026, the *[Toronto Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Star)* reported that McPherson's campaign had approached Ashton to form an electoral pact; Ashton's campaign declined the request.[^cite_note-88] Tony McQuail and Tanille Johnston had previously cross endorsed each other.[^cite_note-89] On March 28, a candidate showcase took place during the party's convention in Winnipeg, in the last event before the leadership announcement. Shortly before the end of the campaign, it was reported that Lewis had raised nearly \$1.5 million with over 11,000 donors, followed by McPherson with over \$700,000 and over 4,000 donors. Ashton raised nearly \$400,000 with over 2,300 donors, Johnston raised nearly \$200,000 with nearly 2,000 donors, and McQuail raised over \$120,000 with over 1,000 donors.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

Lewis got over 56% of the vote on the first ballot, meaning subsequent ballots did not need to be counted, but it was reported he was the second vote of most Johnston, McQuail, and McPherson voters.[^cite_note-200] He received the highest number of votes for a leadership candidate in NDP history.

## Aftermath

[Prime Minister](24135.md) [Mark Carney](13571049.md) and [leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Official_Opposition_\(Canada\)) [Pierre Poilievre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Poilievre) congratulated Lewis on his victory.[^cite_note-201]

[Alberta NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_New_Democratic_Party) leader [Naheed Nenshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naheed_Nenshi) and [Saskatchewan NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_New_Democratic_Party) leader [Carla Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Beck) criticized the direction of the federal party, particularly Lewis's natural resource policies. Meanwhile, [Ontario NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_NDP) leader [Marit Stiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marit_Stiles), [Manitoba NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_NDP) leader [Wab Kinew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wab_Kinew), [Nova Scotia NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia_NDP) leader [Claudia Chender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Chender), [Yukon NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon_NDP) leader [Kate White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_White_\(politician\)), [Newfoundland and Labrador NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_and_Labrador_NDP) leader [Jim Dinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Dinn), [New Brunswick NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick_NDP) leader [Alex White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_White_\(Canadian_politician\)) and [British Columbia NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_NDP) leader [David Eby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Eby) congratulated Lewis on his victory.[^cite_note-202]

Just two days after Avi Lewis's victory, his father, former [Ontario NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_New_Democratic_Party) leader and former [Ambassador to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_Canada_to_the_United_Nations) [Stephen Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lewis), died following a several-year bout with abdominal cancer.[^cite_note-203]
