**Kamui Kobayashi** ([Japanese](15606.md): 小林 可夢偉, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Kobayashi Kamui*; born 13 September 1986) is a Japanese [racing driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_driver) and [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport) executive who competes in the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) for [Toyota](3796361.md) and in [Super Formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Formula) for [TGMGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGMGP). Kobayashi competed in [Formula One](10854.md) from [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Formula_One_World_Championship). In [endurance racing](699018.md), Kobayashi has won two [FIA World Endurance Championship titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIA_World_Endurance_champions) and is a [two-time winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans_winners) of the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) and [2026](77197114.md), all with Toyota. He is also a two-time winner of the [24 Hours of Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Hours_of_Daytona) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_24_Hours_of_Daytona) and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_24_Hours_of_Daytona) with [WTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Taylor_Racing). Since 2022, Kobayashi has served as team principal of Toyota in WEC, winning three consecutive [World Manufacturers' Championship titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIA_World_Endurance_champions) from [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) to [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).

Kobayashi also serves as team principal for [Toyota Gazoo Racing Europe](3796361.md) for their [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) team. Kobayashi previously competed in [Formula One](10854.md), [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_E), the [GP2 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Series), and the [GP2 Asia Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Asia_Series). He became champion of the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) alongside co-drivers [Mike Conway](5506888.md) and [José María López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_L%C3%B3pez) in the [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) season and in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), where he also won the [2021 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans).

Kobayashi is the third FIA world champion from Japan after [Toshi Arai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshi_Arai) and [Kazuki Nakajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuki_Nakajima), and became the third [Asian](689.md)-born driver after countrymen [Aguri Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguri_Suzuki) and [Takuma Satō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuma_Sato) to score a [Formula One](10854.md) podium finish, doing so at the [2012 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Japanese_Grand_Prix) for [Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber_Motorsport).

## Junior single-seater career

### Early years

Kobayashi was born in [Amagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagasaki) in [Hyōgo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo_Prefecture), near [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe). His father owns a [sushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushi) restaurant.[^cite_note-1] He began his career in motorsport in 1996 when he was nine years old, finishing third in his first season of [karting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kart_racing) in the *SL Takarazuka Tournament Cadet Class*. During the following seven years, Kobayashi took four karting titles, winning the *[Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) SL All Japan Tournament Cadet Class* series twice.

In 2004, Kobayashi signed for Toyota's Driver Academy and soon began his career in [open wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_wheel_car) racing. His next step was [Formula Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault), entering the [Asian](689.md), [German](11867.md), [Italian](14532.md) and [Dutch](21148.md) championships and taking two race victories in the Italian championship. Kobayashi continued in the [Formula Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault) class, entering the Italian and European championships and with six wins in both championships, he won both titles.

In 2006, Kobayashi entered the [Formula 3 Euro Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_3_Euro_Series) with [ASM Formule 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ART_Grand_Prix) alongside [Paul di Resta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_di_Resta), [Giedo van der Garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giedo_van_der_Garde) and [Sebastian Vettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Vettel). He took three podium positions in his debut season, coming eighth in the Drivers' Championship and first in the Rookie's Championship. Kobayashi also entered the [Macau Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Grand_Prix) and the [Masters of Formula 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_of_Formula_3), which are annual Formula Three events. Kobayashi started in tenth place and finished the race a place lower in 11th, while at the Macau Grand Prix, he started the race in [pole position](630713.md) but finished in 19th place.

At the beginning of 2007, Kobayashi, along with [Kōhei Hirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohei_Hirate), was named as one of the [Toyota Formula One team's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Racing_\(Formula_One_team\)) test drivers. He stayed in the Euro Series for the upcoming season and had an impressive start, taking two podiums in the first four rounds. He achieved his first race victory in Formula 3 at [Magny-Cours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Nevers_Magny-Cours), in the tenth round, a support race for the [Formula One](10854.md) [French Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_French_Grand_Prix). Kobayashi finished fourth in the Drivers' Championship.

Kobayashi driving for [DAMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAMS) at the [Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring) round of the [2009 GP2 Series season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_GP2_Series_season)

Following a successful [GP2 Asia Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Asia_Series) campaign in early 2008, Kobayashi won his first [GP2 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Series) race in only the second race of the season. After a strong start from pole in the sprint race at the [Circuit de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Catalunya), Kobayashi took the chequered flag in first place. This was after a controversial piece of defensive driving from his former Euro Series teammate [Romain Grosjean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romain_Grosjean) after a safety car period. At the end of the pit straight, Kobayashi attempted a pass on Grosjean. However, the Frenchman moved more than once to defend his position, forcing Kobayashi into evasive action. This resulted in a drive through penalty for the Frenchman, handing the win to Kobayashi. However, he only finished in the points on one further occasion, restricting him to sixteenth in the championship.

Another successful GP2 Asia campaign followed over the winter months of 2008 and 2009, with Kobayashi winning two races en route to the championship, with a round to spare. Kobayashi could not repeat his form in the main series, finishing sixteenth again.

## Formula One (2007–2012)

On 16 November 2007, it was confirmed that Kobayashi would replace the departing [Franck Montagny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franck_Montagny) as the Toyota F1 team's third driver.[^cite_note-2] He was the team's test and reserve driver during the [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Formula_One_World_Championship) seasons.

### Toyota (2009)



Kobayashi deputising for [Timo Glock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timo_Glock) during practice for the [2009 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Japanese_Grand_Prix)

#### 2009

At the [2009 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Japanese_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi competed in the first two free practice sessions in place of [Timo Glock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timo_Glock), who was ill.[^cite_note-3] Glock recovered in time to take part in the third free practice session and qualifying, but was injured after crashing in the final session and had to miss the race. Toyota asked the [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile](248019.md) (FIA) for permission to run Kobayashi in the race, but this was refused as the regulations state a driver must run in at least one session on Saturday to be eligible to start the race.[^cite_note-4]

Kobayashi made his Formula One debut at the [2009 Brazilian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Brazilian_Grand_Prix), following a complication to Glock's injury that was initially not detected.[^cite_note-5] He qualified eleventh in a chaotic session that lasted for over two and half hours and was red-flagged twice due to accidents caused by torrential rain. Early in the race, while running in sixth place, he held off for several laps a challenge by [Jenson Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenson_Button), who needed to finish well to clinch the world championship. He finished the race in tenth place, and was later promoted to ninth when [Heikki Kovalainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heikki_Kovalainen) was penalised. Button jokingly described Kobayashi as "absolutely crazy, very aggressive".[^cite_note-Brazil-Results-6] He also competed in the [2009 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Abu_Dhabi_Grand_Prix), as Glock's injury had not healed sufficiently.[^cite_note-7] Kobayashi qualified twelfth and finished sixth, scoring his first World Championship points, in the inaugural day-night race in Abu Dhabi.[^cite_note-8] Before Toyota decided to withdraw from Formula One, Kobayashi was expected to be given a full-time seat at Toyota for the 2010 Formula One Season.[^cite_note-9]

### Sauber (2010–2012)

#### 2010



Kobayashi driving for [Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber) at the [2010 Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Australian_Grand_Prix)



Kobayashi became another victim of the "[Wall of Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Gilles_Villeneuve#The_Wall_of_Champions)" at the [2010 Canadian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Canadian_Grand_Prix)

Following Toyota's withdrawal, Kobayashi faced an uncertain future, but he was mentioned in lists of probable drivers for the series' new teams for 2010.[^cite_note-10] After weeks of speculation, it was confirmed on 17 December 2009, he would drive for [Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber_Motorsport) for the 2010 season. Despite the team having been sold by BMW back to founder [Peter Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sauber) after the 2009 season, and no longer using BMW components, the team was still named [BMW Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Sauber) as it had been known for the past four seasons.[^cite_note-KobayashiSauber-11] His teammate was former [McLaren](20994.md) tester [Pedro de la Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_la_Rosa). Kobayashi completed his first laps in the new [Sauber C29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber_C29) chassis during F1 winter testing on 2 February.

During the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Australian_Grand_Prix), a front wing failure on his Sauber caused him to hit the barrier, rebounding off it to cause a three-car crash taking out [Nico Hülkenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_H%C3%BClkenberg) and [Sébastien Buemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_Buemi). In the next race in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Malaysian_Grand_Prix) he qualified in ninth place, his best grid position up to that point, however he suffered an engine failure early in the race. In [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Chinese_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi was involved in a three-way collision with Buemi and [Vitantonio Liuzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitantonio_Liuzzi) on the first lap, making him the only driver to retire from the first four races. In [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Turkish_Grand_Prix), he won his first points of the season, coming home tenth after being promoted a place due to Vettel's retirement after a collision with teammate Webber and Petrov's puncture. In [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_European_Grand_Prix), he finished seventh by passing both [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) and [Sébastien Buemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_Buemi) in the final laps on fresh tyres, after driving the majority of the race in third position on his first set of tyres. He followed that with sixth place in [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_British_Grand_Prix), eleventh in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_German_Grand_Prix), ninth in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Hungarian_Grand_Prix) and eighth in [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Belgian_Grand_Prix).

At the [Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Italian_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi suffered a gearbox failure and retired from the [Singapore Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Singapore_Grand_Prix) after hitting a track-side barrier. Kobayashi's teammate changed in Singapore as [Pedro de la Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_la_Rosa) was removed in favour of [Nick Heidfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Heidfeld). In [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Japanese_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi qualified 14th and finished seventh, passing several drivers along the way including his teammate, in a very impressive fashion. He finished eighth in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Korean_Grand_Prix) and tenth in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Brazilian_Grand_Prix), eventually finishing the season with 32 points. In his review of the season, former TV commentator [Murray Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Walker) stated that Kobayashi is "without a doubt Japan's best \[F1 driver] yet".[^cite_note-12] Kobayashi gained a reputation during the season as a highly skilled overtaker, being able to outbrake drivers several car lengths in front of him. His aggressive overtaking style was described by [Martin Brundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brundle) as, "He gets to the normal braking point and then goes, 'Now, which one is the brake again? That's right, it's on the left,' and he just sails past people!"[^cite_note-13] He qualified well against his more experienced teammates, outqualifying de la Rosa and Heidfeld eleven times to eight over the season.

#### 2011



Kobayashi at the [2011 Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Malaysian_Grand_Prix)

Kobayashi remained with Sauber (renamed from BMW Sauber to Sauber F1 Team) in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Formula_One_World_Championship),[^cite_note-14] where he was partnered by GP2 graduate [Sergio Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_P%C3%A9rez). Kobayashi finished eighth in the season opening [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Australian_Grand_Prix), but he and Pérez – who had finished seventh – were disqualified after the race due to an irregularity with the car's rear-wing. The next race of the season in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Malaysian_Grand_Prix) was another strong showing for Kobayashi, finishing eighth in the race, eventually classified seventh after [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton) received a penalty. He finished tenth in his next three races, before a career-high fifth place in an incident-packed Monaco Grand Prix. In an extremely wet [Canadian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Canadian_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi worked his way up from thirteenth place to second having not decided to change to extreme wet tyres before the race was red-flagged, as many other drivers had. This essentially gave him a free pit stop while the race was suspended. After the restart, the track began to dry out, and after changing to intermediate tyres and finally slicks, Kobayashi dropped several places, including having a spin whilst lapping a backmarker and being rear-ended by Nick Heidfeld. He eventually finished seventh, 0.045 seconds behind Felipe Massa, who passed him on the final straight.

#### 2012



Kobayashi during pre-season testing at [Jerez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuito_de_Jerez)

On 28 July 2011, it was announced that Kobayashi would remain with Sauber into the [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Formula_One_World_Championship) season, alongside teammate Pérez.[^cite_note-15]



Kobayashi driving for Sauber at the [2012 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Japanese_Grand_Prix)

Kobayashi started the season with sixth place at the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Australian_Grand_Prix),[^cite_note-16] and a retirement at the [Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Malaysian_Grand_Prix), due to a problem with his car's brakes.[^cite_note-17]



Kobayashi at the [2012 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Grand_Prix)

Kobayashi then started third at the [Chinese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Chinese_Grand_Prix) behind the Mercedes of [Nico Rosberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Rosberg) and [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher). He dropped to tenth but managed to set the fastest lap. At the [Spanish Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Spanish_Grand_Prix), he finished fifth after overtaking Jenson Button and Nico Rosberg. Kobayashi finished in the points once in the next four races, finishing ninth in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Canadian_Grand_Prix). Kobayashi finished in a then-career-best fourth place at the [German Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_German_Grand_Prix) – having finished fifth on-the-road – as he was helped by a post-race penalty for second-placed [Sebastian Vettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Vettel).[^cite_note-18] After retiring late in the race at the [Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Hungarian_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi qualified a career-best second for the [Belgian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Belgian_Grand_Prix) but was caught in a first-corner accident along with four other drivers. Kobayashi was the only one of the five to continue in the race, and finished thirteenth.[^cite_note-19]

Kobayashi took his maiden podium in Formula One with third place at the [Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Japanese_Grand_Prix), after lasting through race-long pressure from Jenson Button. Kobayashi became the first Japanese driver to finish on a Formula One podium in Japan in 22 years, after [Aguri Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguri_Suzuki) in the [1990 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Japanese_Grand_Prix), and was the third Japanese driver to finish on a Formula One podium after Suzuki and [Takuma Sato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuma_Sato) in the [2004 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-20]

On 23 November 2012, Sauber announced that Kobayashi would not be a part of the team's line-up for the [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Formula_One_World_Championship) season, as [Nico Hülkenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_H%C3%BClkenberg) and [Esteban Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Guti%C3%A9rrez) would form the race team and [Robin Frijns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Frijns) as reserve driver.[^cite_note-21] Kobayashi ultimately finished the season in twelfth place in the Drivers' Championship, with sixty points.[^cite_note-22] Despite raising around [€](9472.md)8 million in sponsorship, Kobayashi elected to focus on gaining a competitive drive for the [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Formula_One_World_Championship) season rather than a [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Formula_One_World_Championship) drive.[^cite_note-23]

## WEC debut (2013)

### AF Corse



Kobayashi's [Ferrari 458 GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_458_Italia) during the [2013 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans)

On 11 March 2013, it was confirmed that Kobayashi would drive for [AF Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AF_Corse) in the [2013 FIA World Endurance Championship season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship_season). He competed in the LMGTE-Pro class in the [Ferrari 458 GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_458_Italia) for what is expected to be all of the season's eight rounds, including the [2013 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans).[^cite_note-24]

Kobayashi also tested a [2010 Formula One Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_F10) in preparation for a promotional event in Moscow, where he crashed in the wet.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

In the 81st edition of the 24 Hours of Le Mans 2013, Kobayashi and the AF Corse team scored fifth place in the GTE-Pro class along with his co-drivers [Olivier Beretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Beretta) and [Toni Vilander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Vilander), their Ferrari 458 GT covered a total of 312 laps in the [Circuit de la Sarthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_la_Sarthe). The race was run in very difficult weather conditions and several serious accidents bringing out a record of twelve [safety car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_car) caution periods.[^cite_note-ACO_stats-27][^cite_note-Race_result-28][^cite_note-29]

## Return to Formula One (2014)

### Caterham



Kobayashi at the [2014 Singapore Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Singapore_Grand_Prix)

On 21 January 2014, it was confirmed that Kobayashi would return to Formula One with the [Caterham F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterham_F1) team for the 2014 season partnering Swedish driver [Marcus Ericsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Ericsson) after considerable speculation about the team's all new line-up for the new season.[^cite_note-Caterham_2014-30] At the first race of the season, the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Australian_Grand_Prix), Kobayashi crashed into Massa at the start due to a brake failure. However, in the following [Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Malaysian_Grand_Prix), he was running as high as eighth, ahead of his teammate, the Marussias, and several other cars. He however finished the race in thirteenth, promoting Caterham to tenth in the Constructors' standings. However, later in the season Caterham were demoted to 11th due to [Jules Bianchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Bianchi) achieving [Marussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marussia_F1)'s first points finish in the [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Monaco_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-skysports-31]

On 20 August 2014, it was announced that German driver [André Lotterer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Lotterer) would replace Kobayashi for the [Belgian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Belgian_Grand_Prix) race weekend.[^cite_note-BBC-28869449-32] He returned to racing action at the [Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Italian_Grand_Prix) after Lotterer declined a further offer due to the seat being taken in practice by [Roberto Merhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Merhi), who was attempting to qualify for an [FIA Super Licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_Super_Licence).[^cite_note-33] Kobayashi declared his unhappiness at the situation, with the team's driver plans changing at short notice and his own future uncertain.[^cite_note-34]

### Haas F1 test (2025)

Kobayashi returned to the wheel of a [Formula One car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_car) in June 2025 after eleven years, driving the [Haas VF-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_VF-23) for the [Haas F1 Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_F1_Team) at [Circuit Paul Ricard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Paul_Ricard).[^cite_note-35] The test is due to Haas' close ties with Kobayashi's [WEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) team, [Toyota Gazoo Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Gazoo_Racing), with [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) willing to give Kobayashi an opportunity to test a modern Formula One car.[^cite_note-36]

## Super Formula and WEC split

### Super Formula and Super GT (2015–present)

#### Super Formula

On 30 January 2015, it was confirmed that Kobayashi would drive for Team LeMans in the [2015 Super Formula season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Super_Formula_Championship).[^cite_note-37] He scored three podiums on his way to a fifth-place finish in the drivers' championship during his first year in the series. His second year with Team LeMans was less successful, only managing to score one point and finishing seventeenth in the championship.

Kobayashi moved to [KCMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC_Motorgroup) at the start of the [2017 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Super_Formula_Championship).[^cite_note-38] Kobayashi stayed in the team for over seven years, where he clinched four podiums of second place. But for 2020 and 2021, he did not compete full-time as he had clashing races where he prioritized WEC, his replacements were [Yuichi Nakayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuichi_Nakayama) for 2020, and [Kazuto Kotaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuto_Kotaka) for 2021.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-\:2-40] Kobayashi then continued to race with KCMG, and he clinched his first podium since 2019 with third place at Fuji Speedway in 2024.

Kobayashi left KCMG after competed with them for eight seasons, as he moves to [KDDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDDI) [TGMGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGMGP) [TGR-DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGR-DC) with rookie [Rikuto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikuto_Kobayashi).[^cite_note-41]

#### Super GT

Kobayashi made his one off debut in Super GT GT500 class with [Racing Project Bandoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Project_Bandoh) in 2017. Kobayashi made full season debut in 2018, as he raced with [SARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARD) alongside former F1 driver and the series champion [Heikki Kovalainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heikki_Kovalainen).[^cite_note-Toyota18-42]

On 4 February 2016, Kobayashi was confirmed as a LMP1 driver for [Toyota Gazoo Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Motorsport_GmbH) in the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).

Kobayashi scored his first WEC race victory at the [2016 6 Hours of Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Fuji), finishing ahead of the No. 8 [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joest_Racing) and the No. 1 [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche).[^cite_note-43]

In 2017, Kobayashi achieved the current lap record at the [Circuit de la Sarthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_la_Sarthe) with a lap time of 3\:14.791.[^cite_note-44]

Kobayashi won the [2021 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) from pole after numerous attempts, alongside [Mike Conway](5506888.md) and [Jose Maria Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Maria_Lopez). Kobayashi is the fourth [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) driver to win the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md), the first being [Masanori Sekiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masanori_Sekiya), and is only the second to do so for a [Japanese](15573.md) manufacturer.

In December 2021, Toyota announced that Kobayashi would succeed Hisatake Murata as team principal of the manufacturer's WEC programme, combining the management position with his role as a driver for the team.[^cite_note-45]

### NASCAR (2023)



Kobayashi driving in the [Verizon 200 at the Brickyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon_200_at_the_Brickyard)

On 7 June 2023, it was announced that Kobayashi would make his debut in the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md), driving the No. 67 [Toyota Camry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry) for [23XI Racing](65517461.md) at the [Indianapolis Motor Speedway road course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway). He finished in 33rd.[^cite_note-NASCAR-46]

In 2024, Kobayashi would return to the NASCAR Cup Series and race again for 23XI Racing at [Circuit of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_of_the_Americas) for the [2024 EchoPark Automotive Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_EchoPark_Automotive_Grand_Prix), finishing 29th.[^cite_note-47]

## Personal life

Kobayashi was named after [Kamuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamuy), a divine being in Ainu mythology, and the letter of the name imitated the sound citing three [Kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji) from the sentence "Enabling great dream(s)".[^cite_note-48]

In April 2013, Kobayashi was awarded the Outstanding Achievement in Sport Award at [The Asian Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Asian_Awards) in London.[^cite_note-49]
