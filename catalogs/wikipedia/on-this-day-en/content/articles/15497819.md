# Immigration to Europe



Line of Syrian and refugees between the border Hungary and Austria 2015.

**Immigration to Europe** has a long history, but increased substantially after [World War II](32927.md). [Western European](33800.md) countries, especially, saw high growth in immigration [post 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_history), and many European nations today (particularly those of the [EU-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_enlargement_of_the_European_Union)) have sizeable immigrant populations, both of European and non-European origin. In contemporary globalization, migrations to Europe have accelerated in speed and scale. Over the last decades, there has been an increase in negative attitudes towards immigration, and many studies have emphasized marked differences in the strength of anti-immigrant attitudes among European countries.[^cite_note-1]

Beginning [in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Movement_of_Citizens_Directive), the [European Union](9317.md) granted [EU citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_European_Union) [freedom of movement and residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market) within the EU, and the term "immigrant" has since been used to refer to non-EU citizens, meaning that EU citizens are not to be defined as immigrants within EU territory. The European Commission defines "immigration" as the action by which a person from a non-EU country establishes their usual residence in the territory of an EU country for a period that is or is expected to be at least twelve months. Between 2010 and 2013, around 1.4 million non-EU nationals, excluding asylum seekers and refugees, immigrated into the EU each year using regular means, with a slight decrease since 2010.[^cite_note-2]

The [Yamnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture), a Bronze Age pastoralist culture from the [Pontic–Caspian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic%E2%80%93Caspian_steppe) (modern Russia/Ukraine), migrated into Europe around 3000 BC, drastically transforming its genetic, linguistic, and cultural landscape. This migration is considered the third major [prehistoric migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_human_migrations) event in Europe, following the initial settlement by hunter-gatherers and the arrival of [early farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers) from Anatolia.[^cite_note-4] The [Yamnaya migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations) in particular showed a strong, sex-biased, male-driven replacement, with newcomers replacing local male lineages while marrying local women.[^cite_note-5]

Historical migration into or within Europe has mostly taken the form of [military invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_invasion), but there have been exceptions; this concerns notably population movements within the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) under the *[Pax Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Romana)*; the [Jewish diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora) in Europe was the result of the [First Jewish–Roman War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War) of AD 66–73.

With the collapse of the Roman Empire, migration was again mostly coupled with warlike invasion, not least during the so-called [migration period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_period) (Germanic), the [Slavic migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_migrations), the [Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_conquest_of_the_Carpathian_Basin), the [Islamic conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) and the [Turkic expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_expansion) into Eastern Europe ([Kipchaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipchaks), [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars), [Cumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumans)). The [Ottomans](22278.md) once again established a multi-ethnic imperial structure across Western Asia and Southeastern Europe, but [Turkification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkification) in Southeastern Europe was due more to cultural assimilation than to mass immigration. In the late medieval period, the [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) moved into Europe both via Anatolia and the Maghreb.[^cite_note-6]

There were substantial population movements within Europe throughout the [Early Modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_period), mostly in the context of the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) and the [European wars of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wars_of_religion), and again [as a result of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_evacuation_and_expulsion).

From the late 15th century until the late 1960s and early 1970s, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Germany, Norway,[^cite_note-7] Sweden,[^cite_note-8] Denmark, Belgium, Portugal, [Spain](26667.md) and the United Kingdom[^cite_note-9] were primarily sources of [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration), sending large numbers of emigrants to the [Americas](29833.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [Siberia](29639.md) and [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa). A number also went to other European countries (notably France, Switzerland, Germany and Belgium). As living standards in these countries have risen, the trend has reversed and they were a magnet for immigration (most notably from Morocco, Somalia, Egypt to Italy and Greece; from Morocco, Algeria and the Americas to Spain and Portugal; and from Ireland, India, [Pakistan](23235.md), Germany, the United States, Bangladesh, and Jamaica to the United Kingdom).

## Mobility within Europe after the 1985 Schengen Agreement

Article 17 of the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md) aims at moving internal borders to external borders.[^cite_note-10]

Mobility or mobility of workers is the name given to ease people from one EU country to work into another EU country. The Maastricht treaty name it "the free movement of persons".[^cite_note-11] This is one of the key principles of the European Union.

As a result of the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md), signed on 14 June 1985,[^cite_note-12] there is free travel within part of Europe — known as the [Schengen area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_area)[^cite_note-Schengen_Area-13] — for all citizens and residents of all 27 member states;[^cite_note-14] however, non-citizens may only do so for tourism purpose, and for up to three months.[^cite_note-Schengen_Area-13][^cite_note-15] Moreover, [EU citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_citizenship) and their families have the right to live and work anywhere within the EU;[^cite_note-16] citizens of non-EU or non-EEA states may obtain a [Blue Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Card_\(European_Union\)) or long-term residency.[^cite_note-17]

A large proportion of immigrants in western European states have come from former eastern bloc states in the 1990s, especially in Spain, Greece, Germany, Italy, Portugal and the United Kingdom. There are frequently specific migration patterns, with geography, language and culture playing a role. For example, there are large numbers of [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) who have moved to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Ireland](147575.md) and [Iceland](14531.md), while [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) and also [Bulgarians](151876.md) have chosen [Spain](26667.md) and [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-book-19]

With the earlier of the two recent enlargements of the EU although most countries restricted free movement by nationals of the acceding countries, the United Kingdom did not restrict for the [2004 enlargement of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) and received Polish, Latvian and other citizens of the new EU states.

Spain was not restricted for the [2007 enlargement of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_enlargement_of_the_European_Union), when 9 countries entered Schengen area in 2007, and received many Romanians and Bulgarians as well other citizens of the new EU states.[^cite_note-20]

Many of these Polish immigrants to the UK have since returned to Poland, after the serious economic crisis in the UK. Nevertheless, free movement of EU nationals is now an important aspect of migration within the EU, since there are now 27 member states,[^cite_note-21] and has resulted in serious political tensions between Italy and Romania, since Italy has expressed the intention of restricting free movement of EU nationals (contrary to Treaty obligations and the clear jurisprudence of the European Court of Justice).[^cite_note-22]

Another migration trend has been that of [Northern Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe) moving toward [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe). Citizens from the European Union make up a growing proportion of immigrants in Portugal, Spain, South of France, Italy and Greece.[^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-23][^cite_note-BBC_NEWS_-_UK_-_Brits_Abroad:_Count-24][^cite_note-Giles_Tremlett-25][^cite_note-byebyeblighty.com-26][^cite_note-Jason_Burke-27][^cite_note-BBC_NEWS_-_UK_-_5.5m_Britons_'opt_t-28][^cite_note-BBC_NEWS_-_UK_-_More_Britons_consid-29]

In 2023 and 2024, 3 new countries entered the Schengen area, including Romania and Bulgaria.

Many immigrants and their descendants have ancestral origins outside the continent. For the former colonial powers France, Britain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Spain, and Portugal, most immigrants and their descendants have ties to former colonies in Africa, the Americas, and Asia. In addition, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, the Netherlands, and Belgium recruited Turkish and Moroccan [guest workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastarbeiter) beginning in the 1960s, and many current immigrants in those countries today have ties to such recruitment programs.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Moroccan immigrants also began migrating substantially to Spain and Italy for work opportunities in the 1980s.[^cite_note-33] In the Nordic countries of Sweden, Denmark, Norway, and Finland, the bulk of non-Western immigrants are refugees and asylum seekers from the Middle East, East Africa, and other regions of the world arriving since the 1980s and 1990s. Increasing globalization has brought a population of students, professionals, and workers from all over the world into major European cities, most notably London, Paris, and Frankfurt. The introduction of the [EU Blue Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Card_\(European_Union\)) in May 2009 further increased the number of skilled professional immigrants from outside of the continent.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

Irregular immigration and asylum-seeking in Europe from outside the continent have been occurring since at least the 1990s. While the number of migrants was relatively small for years, it began to rise in 2013. In 2015, the number of asylum seekers arriving from outside Europe increased substantially during the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md) (see [timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_European_migrant_crisis)). However, the EU-Turkey deal enacted in March 2016 dramatically reduced this number, and anti-immigrant measures starting in 2017 by the Italian government further cut irregular migration from the Mediterranean route.



Rally for refugees in [Brussels](3708.md), Belgium, 11 September 2015

Scholars say that the increase in immigration flows from the 1980s is due to [global inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization) between poor and rich countries.[^cite_note-Calefato94p80-36]

In 2017, approximately 825,000 persons acquired citizenship of a member state of the European Union, down from 995,000 in 2016.[^cite_note-37] The largest groups were nationals of [Morocco](19291.md), [Albania](738.md), [India](14533.md), [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-38]

2.4 million non-EU migrants entered the EU in 2017.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] In addition, cheaper transportation and more advanced technology have further aided migration.

European Commission President [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) praised the [EU Asylum and Migration Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Pact_on_Migration_and_Asylum) as a "huge achievement for Europe".[^cite_note-41] The provisions of the EU Asylum and Migration Pact apply to migrants caught illegally crossing an [external EU border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_border_of_the_European_Union), such as those reaching the shores of Greece, Italy or Spain via the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) or [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) on boats provided by smugglers; estimated at 300,000 migrants in 2023.[^cite_note-m887-42] Provisions do not apply to legal migrants to the EU (\~3.5 million in 2023) and migrants who arrived legally but overstayed their visas (\~700,000 in 2023).[^cite_note-m887-42]

In January 2026, [India](14533.md) and the European Union signed a mobility and migration partnership agreement alongside a [free trade agreement](82235527.md) to substantially expand and simplify legal pathways to the EU for [Indian students and skilled workers](26998691.md).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-telegraph-mobility-44]

### Immigrants in the Nordic countries in 2000–2025

The Nordic countries have differed in their approach to immigration. While Norway and Sweden used to have generous immigration policies, Denmark and Finland had more restricted immigration. Although both Denmark and Finland have experienced a significant increase in their immigrant populations between 2000 and 2020 (6.8% points in Denmark and 5.0% in Finland), Norway (11.9%) and Sweden (11.0%) have seen far greater relative increases.&#x20;

From 2020 to 2025, Iceland saw the highest increase in their immigrant population (+6.1% points), followed by Finland (+3.8%), Norway (+3.6%) and Denmark (+2.9%), while Sweden saw the lowest increase (+2.2%). In 2024, the Swedish Government reported that, owing to their more restrictive immigration policy, more were leaving Sweden than arriving.[^cite_note-45] This was disputed by the source of their data, [Statistics Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Sweden).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

#### Denmark

Main article: [Immigration to Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Denmark)

For decades, Danish immigration and integration policy were built upon the assumption that with the right kind of help, immigrants and their descendants will eventually tend to the same levels of education and employment as Danes. This assumption was doubted by a 2018 study by the [Danish Immigration Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Immigration_Service) and the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Denmark\)). There are only small differences between children of descendants, descendants and immigrants (arrived in Denmark as 0-12 year olds) of non-Western origin in terms of labor market attachment and education.[^cite_note-63] One of the reasons was that second-generation immigrants from non-Western countries marry someone from their country of origin and so Danish is not spoken at home which disadvantages children in school. Thereby the process of integration has to start from the beginning for each generation.[^cite_note-64]

#### Norway

Main article: [Immigration to Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Norway)



Immigration to Norway has increased the amount of religious minorities, such as these [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Norway) in [Oslo](22309.md)

In January 2015 the "immigrant population" in Norway consisted of approximately 805,000 people, including 669,000 foreign-born and 136,000 born in Norway to two immigrant parents. This corresponds to 15.6% of the total population.[^cite_note-65] The cities with the highest share of immigrants are [Oslo](22309.md) (32%) and [Drammen](178144.md) (27%).[^cite_note-66]

In 2024, approximately 931,081 individuals (16.8% of the population) of the population of Norway were immigrants. Of these, 386,559 (41.5%) had a [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) background, while 544,521 (58.5%) had a [non-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South) background (Asia, [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_immigration_to_Norway), South and Central America). 221,459 individuals (4% of the population) were children of immigrants, born in Norway.[^cite_note-SSB-2024-67] The six largest immigrant groups in Norway are from Poland, Lithuania, Sweden, [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrians_in_Norway), Ukraine and Somalia.[^cite_note-SSB-2024-67] Children of [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians_with_Pakistani_background), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Norway), [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalis_in_Norway), [Iraqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqis_in_Norway) and [Eritrean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritreans_in_Norway) parents made up the largest groups of all Norwegians born to immigrant parents.[^cite_note-SSB-2024-67]

In the years since 1970, the largest increase in the immigrant population has come from countries in [Asia](689.md) (including [Turkey](11125639.md)), [Africa](5334607.md) and [South America](26769.md), increasing from about 3500 in 1970 to about 300,000 in 2011. In the same period, the immigrant population from other Nordic countries and [Western Europe](33800.md) has increased modestly from around 42,000 to around 130,000.[^cite_note-68]

#### Sweden

Main article: [Immigration to Sweden](24537353.md)



Immigrants (red) and emigrants (blue), Sweden 1850-2007

In 2014 the "immigrant population" in Sweden consisted of approximately 2.09 million people, including 1.60 million foreign-born and 489,000 born in Sweden to two immigrant parents. This corresponds to 21.5% of the total population.[^cite_note-scb.se-69]

Of the major cities [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) has the largest immigrant population, estimated to be 41.7% in 2014.[^cite_note-70] However, the smaller municipalities [Botkyrka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botkyrka) (56.2%), [Haparanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haparanda) (55.5%) and [Södertälje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6dert%C3%A4lje) (49.4%) all have a higher share of immigrants. In the Swedish capital [Stockholm](26741.md) 31.1% (in 2014) of the population are either foreign-born or born in Sweden by two foreign-born parents.[^cite_note-71]

In 2014 127,000 people immigrated to Sweden, while 51,000 left the country. Net immigration was 76,000.[^cite_note-scb.se-69][^cite_note-72]

Sweden has been transformed from a nation of [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration) ending after World War I to a nation of [immigration](4599312.md) from World War II onwards. In 2009, Sweden had the fourth largest number of asylum applications in the [EU](9317.md) and the largest number per capita after [Cyprus](5593.md) and [Malta](19137.md).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Immigrants in [Sweden](5058739.md) are mostly concentrated in the [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) of [Svealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svealand) and [Götaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6taland) and the five largest foreign born populations in Sweden come from [Finland](10577.md), [Yugoslavia](34244.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Poland](22936.md) and [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-75]

#### Finland

Main article: [Immigration to Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Finland)



A [pro-Palestinian protest](75044900.md) in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), Finland, was attended by people of immigrant background, 21 October 2023.

Immigration has been a major source of [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) and cultural change throughout much of the [history of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Finland).[^cite_note-76] The economic, social, and political aspects of immigration have caused controversy regarding ethnicity, economic benefits, jobs for non-immigrants, settlement patterns, impact on upward [social mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_mobility), crime, and voting behavior.

At the end of 2025, there were 614,401 foreign born people residing in [Finland](10577.md),[^cite_note-77] which corresponds to 10.9% of the population, while there are 660,800 people with a foreign background, corresponding to 11.7% of the population. The largest groups come from [Russia](25391.md), [Estonia](28222445.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Somalia](27358.md), [Ukraine](31750.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), [India](14533.md), former [Yugoslavia](34244.md), [China](5405.md) and [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-78] Proportionally speaking, Finland has had one of the fastest increases in its foreign-born population between 2000 and 2010 in all of Europe. The majority of immigrants in Finland settle in the Helsinki area, although Tampere, Turku, and Kuopio have had their share of immigrants in recent years. In the city of [Vantaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vantaa), 41% of 30-44 year olds speak a native language other than Finnish or Swedish.[^cite_note-79]

### France

Main article: [Immigration to France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_France)

In 2024, the [United Nations](31769.md) estimated 12 million immigrants (foreign-born people) in [France](5843419.md), representing 17.2% of the total population, making France the [second largest foreign born population in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_by_immigrant_population) after [Germany](11867.md) and tied with the [United Kingdom](31717.md). This is an increase from the United Nations statistics in 2018 in which there were 9 million immigrants (foreign-born people) in France, which at the time represented 14% of the country's total population.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-r147-81] As of 2024, around 12 million people living in France, or 17.2% of the population, are [first-generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_generations) immigrants, while the population with a [migrant background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_background) in the wider sense stood at approximately 19.2 million, accounting for 30.2% of the total population of 69.4 million.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

As of 2008, the French national institute of statistics ([INSEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSEE)) estimated that 5.3 million foreign-born immigrants and 6.5 million direct descendants of immigrants (born in France with at least one immigrant parent) lived in France. This represents a total of 11.8 million, or 19% of the population. In terms of origin, about 5.5 million are [European](9239.md), four million [Maghrebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebis), one million [Sub-Saharan African](27067.md), and 400,000 [Turkish](11125639.md). Among the 5.3 million foreign-born immigrants, 38% are from Europe, 30% from Maghreb, 12.5% from Sub-Saharan Africa, 14.2% from [Asia](689.md) and 5.3% from [America](29833.md) and [Oceania](22621.md)[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-Insee_1-85] The most significant countries of origin as of 2008 were Algeria (713,000), Morocco (653,000), Portugal (580,000), Italy (317,000), Spain (257,000), Turkey (238,000) and Tunisia (234,000). However, immigration from Asia (especially China, as well as the former French colonies of Vietnam, Cambodia and Laos), and from Sub-Saharan Africa (Senegal, Mali and others), is gaining in importance.



Mosque in [Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis), Paris, 2019

The region with the largest proportion of immigrants is the [Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele-de-France_\(region\)) (Greater Paris), where 40% of immigrants live. Other important regions are [Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes) (Lyon) and [Provence-Alpes-Côte d’Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d%E2%80%99Azur) (Marseille).[^cite_note-86]

Among the 802,000 newborns in [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) in 2010, 27.3% had at least one foreign-born parent and about one quarter (23.9%) had at least one parent born outside Europe.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Including grandparents; almost 40% of newborns in France between 2006 and 2008 had at least one foreign-born grandparent. (11% were born in another European country, 16% in Maghreb, and 12% in another region of the world.)[^cite_note-89]

In 2022, the total number of new foreigners coming to France rose above 320,000 for the first time, with nearly a majority coming from Africa. A significant increase in students, family reunification and labor migration occurred under the presidency of [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md).[^cite_note-90]

### Germany

Main article: [Immigration to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Germany)



Migrants arriving in Germany during the [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md)

Nearly 1.1 million refugees and migrants registered in Germany in 2015, mostly people fleeing wars in [Syria](7515849.md), [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-91]

In 2019, 21.2 million people with a [migration background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_background) lived in Germany, which corresponded to a population share of around 26%.[^cite_note-92]

In December 2021, German Chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) promised to reduce barriers to immigration and make it easier for immigrants to obtain German citizenship.[^cite_note-93] 352,000 people applied for asylum in Germany in 2023, the highest number since 2016, when 722,370 people applied for asylum. People from Ukraine are not included among asylum seekers. Most asylum seekers in 2023 were from [Turkey](11125639.md), Syria and Afghanistan.[^cite_note-94]

In 2023, 1,933,000 people immigrated to Germany, including 276,000 from [Ukraine](31750.md) and 126,000 from Turkey, while 1,270,000 people emigrated. Net immigration to Germany was 663,000 in 2023, down from a record 1,462,000 in 2022.[^cite_note-95]

On 14 September 2024, Scholz and Kenyan President [William Ruto](2577967.md) signed an agreement that opened the German labor market to up to 250,000 skilled and semi-skilled [migrant workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastarbeiter) from [Kenya](188171.md).[^cite_note-96] Scholz's government has already signed migration partnerships with several other countries,[^cite_note-97] including [Morocco](19291.md), [Nigeria](21383.md) and [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-98]

### United Kingdom

[London](17867.md) has become multiethnic as a result of immigration.

In 2014, the number of people who became naturalised [British](31717.md) citizens rose to a record 140,795 - a 12% increase from the previous year, and a dramatic increase since 2009. Most new citizens came from [Asia](689.md) (40%) or [Africa](5334607.md) (32%); the largest three countries of origin were [India](14533.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), and [Bangladesh](3454.md)[^cite_note-100] with Indians making the largest group. In 2005, an estimated 565,000 migrants arrived to live in the United Kingdom for at least a year, primarily from Asia and Africa,[^cite_note-101] while 380,000 people emigrated from the country for a year or more, chiefly to [Australia](4689264.md), [Canada](5042916.md) and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-102]

In 2014 the net increase was 318,000: immigration was 641,000, up from 526,000 in 2013, while the number of people emigrating (for more than 12 months) was 323,000.[^cite_note-103]

In 2021, the government launched a [scheme for Hongkongers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_National_\(Overseas\)#Expanded_access_to_UK_residence), with more than 200,000 Hong Kong residents immigrating to the UK.[^cite_note-104]

Long-term net migration (the number of people immigrating minus the number emigrating) reached a record high of 764,000 in 2022,[^cite_note-bbc-23-05-2024-106] with immigration at 1.26 million and emigration at 493,000.[^cite_note-107] Of the 1,218,000 immigrants who came to the United Kingdom in 2023, only 126,000 were [EU](9317.md) nationals. Around 250,000 people came from [India](14533.md), 141,000 from [Nigeria](21383.md), 90,000 from [China](5405.md) and 83,000 from [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-bbc-23-05-2024-106]

### Italy

Main article: [Immigration to Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Italy)



Foreign residents in Italy as a percentage of the regional population, 2011

In 2021, [Istat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istat) estimated that 5,171,894 foreign citizens lived in [Italy](14532.md), representing about 8.7% of the total [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Italy). These figures include [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized) [foreign-born](4599312.md) residents (about 1,620,000 foreigners acquired [Italian citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_citizenship) from 1999 to 2020, of whom 130,000 did so in 2020[^cite_note-108]) as well as [illegal immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigrants), the so-called *clandestini*, whose numbers, difficult to determine, are thought to be at least 670,000.[^cite_note-109]

In 2021, around 6,260,000 people residing in Italy have an immigration background (around the 10.6% of the total Italian population).[^cite_note-appsso.eurostat.ec.europa.eu-110][^cite_note-ISTAT2015-111][^cite_note-Bilancio2015-112]

Starting from the early 1980s, until then a linguistically and culturally homogeneous society, Italy began to attract substantial flows of foreign immigrants.[^cite_note-dati.istat.it-113][^cite_note-114] After the [fall of the Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Berlin_Wall) and, more recently, the [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) and [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) enlargements of the European Union, large waves of migration originated from the former socialist countries of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) (especially [Romania](25445.md), [Albania](738.md), [Ukraine](31750.md), [Moldova](19260.md) and [Poland](22936.md)). Another source of immigration is neighbouring North Africa (in particular, [Morocco](19291.md), [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Tunisia](30188.md)), with soaring arrivals as a consequence of the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring). Furthermore, in recent years, growing migration fluxes from Asia-Pacific (notably [China](5405.md),[^cite_note-115] [South Asia](21566765.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md)) and [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas) have been recorded.

Since the expansion of the [European Union](9317.md), the most recent wave of migration has been from surrounding European states, particularly Eastern Europe, and increasingly Asia,[^cite_note-116] replacing North Africa as the major immigration area.[^cite_note-dati.istat.it-113]

[Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) made up the largest foreign community in the country (1,077,876; around 10% of them being ethnic [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people)[^cite_note-117]) followed by [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians) (433,130) and [Moroccans](5639315.md) (428,940).[^cite_note-118] The fourth largest, but the fastest growing, community of foreign residents in Italy was represented by the [Chinese](372983.md); as of 2021 there were 330,495 foreigners holding Chinese citizenship.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] The majority of Chinese living in Italy are from the city of [Wenzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzhou) in the province of [Zhejiang](96602.md).[^cite_note-121] As of 2021, foreign citizens' origins were subdivided as follows: Europe (47,6%), Africa (22.25%), Asia (22.64%), The Americas (7.49%), and Oceania (0.04%).[^cite_note-idos2-122]

The distribution of foreigners is largely uneven in Italy: in 2020, 61.2% of foreign citizens lived in [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) (in particular 36.1% in [Northwestern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Italy) and 25.1% in [Northeastern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Italy)), 24.2% in [Central Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Italy), 10.8% in [Southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy) and 3.9% in [Insular Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Italy).[^cite_note-123]

The children born in Italy to foreign mothers were 102,000 in 2012, 99,000 in 2013 and 97,000 in 2014.[^cite_note-istat2015-124]

The [Meloni government](72067724.md) authorised more than 450,000 entries for non-EU workers between 2023 and 2025. In July 2025, the government approved plans to authorise 497,550 entries for non-EU workers for the 2026–2028 period, including 164,850 in 2026.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] The quotas are reserved for selected countries, primarily located in [Asia](689.md), [Africa](5334607.md), and the [Western Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Balkans).[^cite_note-128]

### Portugal

Main article: [Immigration to Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Portugal)

Portugal, long a country of emigration,[^cite_note-145] has now become a country of net immigration, from both its former [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_empire) and other sources. In 2022, legal immigrants represented about 7% of the population, and the largest communities were from [Brazil](3383.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Cabo Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabo_Verde), [India](14533.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Angola](701.md), [France](5843419.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] As of 2024, according to the latest official report, legal immigrants now represent 14.4% of the population. ### Slovenia



Middle East migrants pass through Slovenia on their way to [Germany](11867.md), 22 October 2015.

### Greece

In March 2020, migrants set fires and threw [Molotov cocktail](20922.md) firebombs over to the Greek side in order to break down the border fence. Greek and European forces responded with tear gas and by trying to keep the fence intact. By 11 March 348 people had been arrested and 44,353 cases of unlawful entry had been prevented.[^cite_note-176]

### Italy

According to poll published by *[Corriere della Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriere_della_Sera)*, one of two respondents (51%) approved closing Italy's ports to further boat migrants arriving via the Mediterranean, while 19% welcomed further boat migrants.[^cite_note-182]

### Poland

A 2015 opinion poll conducted by the [Centre for Public Opinion Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Public_Opinion_Research) (CBOS) found that 14% thought that Poland should let asylum-seekers enter and settle in Poland, 58% thought Poland should let asylum-seekers stay in Poland until they can return to their home country, and 21% thought Poland should not accept asylum-seekers at all. Furthermore, 53% thought Poland should not accept asylum-seekers from the Middle East and North Africa, with only 33% thinking Poland should accept them.[^cite_note-183]

Another opinion poll conducted by the same organisation found that 86% of Poles think that Poland does not need more immigrants, with only 7% thinking Poland needs more immigrants.[^cite_note-184]

Despite Polish public sentiment, 683,000 immigrants from outside of the EU arrived in Poland in 2017, with 87.4% of them immigrating for work. This was the highest number of residence permits granted by an EU member state that year. [^cite_note-185]

### Portugal

This section is an excerpt from [Opposition to immigration § Portugal](6491626.md#Portugal).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Opposition_to_immigration\&action=edit)]

Portugal had little immigration until a sudden influx in the 1970s, as ex-colonists, most of them ethnically white, returned.[^cite_note-186] After the former Portuguese African colonies gained independence, and because nationals of Portuguese-speaking nations can freely live and work in Portugal without much bureaucracy, an incremental growth of immigration from Portugal's former overseas possessions was observed over the past few decades, primarily from [Brazil](3383.md), [Cape Verde](18962637.md), [Angola](701.md) and [Mozambique](19301.md).[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] The country now has nearly 240,000 Brazilians[^cite_note-189] and about 350,000 people born in an African country.[^cite_note-190] Although immigrants are mostly concentrated in urban and suburban areas, mainly on Portugal's coast, Portuguese authorities have in recent times encouraged immigration, notably from Brazil, to rural areas, in an effort to increase an ever shrinking population.[^cite_note-191] The growth of the number of immigrants has been linked to an escalation of anti-immigration sentiments and protests throughout Portugal since the mids 2000's.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193]

Until recently, far-right party "[National Renewal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renewal_Party)", known as PNR, was the only one in Portugal which actively targeted the mass-immigration and ethnic minorities (mainly related to [Gypsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsy) and African communities) issues.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] After years of growing support—0.09% 4,712 [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_legislative_election,_2002), 0.16% 9,374 [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_legislative_election,_2005), 0.20% 11,503 [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_legislative_election,_2009), 0.31% 17,548 [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_legislative_election,_2011)— it managed 0.50%, or 27,269, of the electorate in the [2015 Portuguese legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Portuguese_legislative_election). Since 2019, far-right political party [Chega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chega_\(political_party\)) has gained traction in the country. Following the [2019 Portuguese legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Portuguese_legislative_election), the party's president, [André Ventura](60708639.md), assured a seat in [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md), after having received over 66,000 votes, 1,3% of the electorate.[^cite_note-196] In the [2020 Azorean regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Azorean_regional_election), the party secured two assemblyman to the regional parliament[^cite_note-197] and, during the [2021 Portuguese presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_presidential_election), André Ventura managed to gather approximately 500,000 votes, 12% of the total.[^cite_note-198] The party opposes immigration and has been described by the media and mainstream parties as xenophobic.[^cite_note-199] Chega has an estimated 28,000 militant members[^cite_note-200] and is expected to continue to rise in popularity and political force.[^cite_note-201]

### Slovakia

In October 2024, Slovak Prime Minister [Robert Fico](4127226.md) criticized the European Union's migration policies, specifically targeting the low rate of deportations for individuals residing in the bloc illegally.[^cite_note-202] Speaking at a summit on 22 October 2024, alongside Hungarian Prime Minister [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) and Serbian President [Aleksandar Vučić](13349267.md), Fico emphasized that the "fight against illegal migration" is an absolute priority. During this summit, Orbán corroborated Fico's stance by stating that out of approximately 430,000 decisions to expel illegal migrants in the EU in the previous year, only 84,000 were actually implemented—supporting Fico's claim that roughly 80% of those ordered to leave remain in Europe while only 20% are successfully returned.[^cite_note-203]

### Spain

This section is an excerpt from [Opposition to immigration § Spain](6491626.md#Spain).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Opposition_to_immigration\&action=edit)]

A January 2004 survey by Spanish newspaper *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)* found that the "majority" of Spaniards believed [immigration](10499603.md) was too high.[^cite_note-204]

In Spain, as of 2005, surveys found "in descending order, jobs, crime and housing" were the primary concerns for citizens opposed to immigration.[^cite_note-205]

In 2024, [Alberto Núñez Feijóo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_Feij%C3%B3o), leader of Spain's conservative [Popular Party](404727.md) (PP) and head of the main opposition group, criticized the immigration policy of the [Sánchez government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez). He accused Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) of "promoting Spain as a destination" for migrants rather than addressing the root causes by going to Africa to "combat the (migration) mafia."[^cite_note-206] In 2026, Feijóo further condemned the Spanish government's proposal to legalize approximately 500,000 undocumented immigrants.[^cite_note-Opposition_to_immigration_dw-spain-207]

In 2026, the [Vox party](41928488.md) launched a legal challenge in Spain's [Supreme Court](8327853.md) against the government's plan to regularize hundreds of thousands of undocumented migrants.[^cite_note-Opposition_to_immigration_dw-spain-207]

In July 2026, following the [Morocco–Spain border incident](83824713.md), Italian Prime Minister [Giorgia Meloni](29228976.md) called for Spain to be suspended from the EU's visa-free [Schengen Area](16565139.md). Writing on [X](9988187.md), she said that "uncontrolled illegal immigration poses a real threat to the security of Europe's borders."[^cite_note-Opposition_to_immigration_PoliticoEU-208] Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez rejected the criticism and argued on X that Italy had received significantly more migrants than Spain since 2021, citing approximately 478,000 undocumented migrants arriving in Italy as opposed to 234,000 in Spain.[^cite_note-209]

According to an October 2024 survey for the *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)* newspaper and [Cadena SER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadena_SER) radio station, 57% of Spaniards believe there is "too much" immigration to Spain.[^cite_note-210]

### Sweden

In response to the high immigration of 2015, the anti-immigration party [Sweden Democrats](65873.md) rose to 19.9% in the [Statistics Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Sweden) poll.[^cite_note-211]

In late 2015, Sweden introduced temporary border checks on the [Øresund Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Bridge) between Denmark and Sweden and public transport operators were instructed to only let people with residence in Sweden board trains or buses. The measures reduced the number of asylum seekers from 163 000 in 2015 to 29 000 in 2016.[^cite_note-212]

In 2018, a poll by [Pew Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) found that a small majority (52%) wanted fewer immigrants to be allowed into the country, 33% wanted to keep the current level and 14% wanted to increase immigration.[^cite_note-\:13-213]

According to a 2018 Yougov poll, 60% of Swedes opposed accepting more migrants into the country.[^cite_note-\:1-161]

In February 2020 finance minister [Magdalena Andersson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Andersson_\(Social_Democrat\)) encouraged migrants to head for other countries than Sweden. Andersson stated in an interview that integration of immigrants in Sweden wasn't working since neither before nor after 2015 and that Sweden cannot accept more immigration than it is able to integrate.[^cite_note-214]

After the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Swedish_general_election), the Swedish government saw a surge in support for right-wing parties, notably bolstered by the rise of [anti-immigration sentiments](6491626.md).[^cite_note-215]

### Switzerland

During the 1990s under [Christoph Blocher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Blocher), the [Swiss People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) started to develop an increasingly eurosceptic and anti-immigration agenda.[^cite_note-216] In 2014, they launched a popular initiative titled "[Against mass immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_popular_initiative_%22Against_mass_immigration%22)" that was narrowly accepted. They are currently the largest party in the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) with 53 seats.

### United Kingdom

Anti-immigration sentiment in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) has historically focused on non-indigenous [African](5334607.md), [Afro-Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Caribbean) and especially [South Asian](21566765.md) migrants, all of whom began to arrive from the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) in greater numbers following [World War II](32927.md). Since the fall of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the [enlargement of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_European_Union), the increased movement of people out of countries such as [Poland](22936.md), [Romania](25445.md) and [Lithuania](17675.md) has shifted much of this attention towards migrants from [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). While working-class migrants tend to be the focus of anti-immigration sentiment, there is also some discontent about [Russian](25391.md), [Chinese](5405.md), [Singaporean](27318.md) and [Gulf Arab](3088555.md) multimillionaires resident in the UK, particularly in [London](17867.md) and [South East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England). These residents often invest in property and business, and are perceived as living extravagant "jet-set" lifestyles marked by conspicuous consumption while simultaneously taking advantage of tax loopholes connected to [non-dom status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-dom).

Policies of reduced immigration, particularly from the European Union, are central to the manifestos of parties such as the [UK Independence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Independence_Party). Such policies have also been discussed by some members of the largest parties in Parliament, most significantly the [Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)).

Following revised November 2024 data showing [net immigration to the United Kingdom](1247786.md) reached a record 906,000 for the year ending June 2023, [Reform UK](59757202.md) leader [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) described the figures as "horrendous," stating he had had "enough of being lied to" by the Conservatives and claiming the situation would be "even worse" under Labour.[^cite_note-bbc_news2024-217] Conservative leader [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch) stated that the Conservative Party had "got it wrong" on immigration during their time in power because post-[Brexit](41688778.md) openings of new non-EU visa routes caused net migration to reach record highs despite ending [EU freedom of movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_movement_for_workers_in_the_European_Union), prompting her to announce a new policy under her leadership that will include a "strict numerical cap" on immigration, tighter access to British passports, and a "zero tolerance" policy on foreign criminals remaining in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

## Great Replacement

This section is an excerpt from [Great Replacement conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory\&action=edit)]

The [Great Replacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *grand remplacement*), also known as replacement theory or great replacement theory,[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_Bowles-221][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_\:8-222] is a debunked[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224] [white nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism)[^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_\:7-225] [far-right](221220.md) [conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory)[^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_\:8-222][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_taguieff71-226][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_Baldauf_2017-227][^cite_note-228] coined by French author [Renaud Camus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaud_Camus). Camus's theory states that, with the complicity or cooperation of "replacist" elites,[^cite_note-transl-rempla-229][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_taguieff71-226][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_fourquet29-230] the ethnic French and white European populations are deliberately being replaced by non-white peoples—especially [from Muslim-majority countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_diaspora)—through mass migration, demographic growth, and a drop in the birth rate of white Europeans.[^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_taguieff71-226][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_Froio-231][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_\:5-232] Similar claims have been advanced in other national contexts, [notably in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-233] A consensus of academic scholars have dismissed these claims of a conspiracy of "replacist" elites as rooted in a misunderstanding of demographic statistics and premised upon an unscientific, racist worldview.[^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_Jenkins2017-234][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_\:9-236]

While similar themes have characterized various far-right theories since the late 19th century, the particular term was popularized by Camus in his 2011 book *Le Grand Remplacement*. The book associates the presence of [Muslims in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_in_France) with danger and destruction of [French culture and civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_France). Camus and other conspiracy theorists attribute recent demographic changes in Europe to intentional policies advanced by global and liberal elites (the "replacists") from within the [Government of France](3398091.md), the [European Union](9317.md), or the [United Nations](31769.md); they describe it as a "genocide by substitution".[^cite_note-Great_Replacement_conspiracy_theory_taguieff71-226]

## Border crises



Migrants in the Mediterranean Sea sailing to the Italian port of [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), 5 September 2015

The [EU](9317.md) [Border and Coast Guard Agency (Frontex)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Border_and_Coast_Guard_Agency) uses the terms "illegal" and "irregular" border crossings for crossings of an EU external border but not at an official [border-crossing point](288329.md).[^cite_note-Frontex2016,p.22-283] These include illegal migrants interdicted at sea.[^cite_note-Frontex2016,p.20-284]

### 2015 European migrant crisis Hungarian border barrier



Hungarian border barrier

In 2015, [Hungary](13275.md) erected a [border barrier on its border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_border_barrier) with [Serbia](29265.md) and [Croatia](5573.md). The fence was constructed by prison labour during the European migrant crisis, with the aim of preventing illegal entry by migrants from entering.[^cite_note-285] Attempted border entries fell following the barrier's construction. During the month of September 2015 there was a total number of 138,396 migrant entries, and within the first two weeks of November the average daily number of intercepted migrants decreased to only 15, which is a daily reduction of more than 4,500.[^cite_note-286]

### Mediterranean Sea trafficking

See also: [Mediterranean Sea migrant smuggling](63864017.md)

### Belarusian migrant smuggling

See also: [Belarus–European Union border crisis](68384300.md)

In August 2021, the [government of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Belarus) began sponsoring an influx of migrants, mostly from the Middle East and North Africa, to the borders of [Lithuania](17675.md), [Poland](22936.md) and [Latvia](17514.md).[^cite_note-296] Although Belarus denied involvement, both the [European Union](9317.md) and independent observers viewed it as [hybrid warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_warfare) undertaken in response to the deterioration in [Belarus–European Union relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus%E2%80%93European_Union_relations) following the [2020 Belarusian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Belarusian_presidential_election) and the [2020–2021 Belarusian protests](64399515.md).[^cite_note-297][^cite_note-298] Between August and December 2021, tens of thousands of unauthorized border crossing attempts were recorded, peaking in October.[^cite_note-\:11-299][^cite_note-lt-migrants-stats-300] Poland, Lithuania, and Latvia each declared states of emergency and announced their intentions to build border walls.[^cite_note-301] The EU sent supporting officers and patrol cars to Lithuania,[^cite_note-302] and 12 EU governments stated their support for a physical barrier along the border.[^cite_note-303] After the EU refused to finance protective structures on the external borders, Poland and Lithuania completed their barriers on the border with Belarus on their own.[^cite_note-lt-completes-barrier-304][^cite_note-lt-barrier-surveilance-305] In 2024, there was a 192 percent increase in illegal crossings from Belarus into the EU.[^cite_note-306] In May 2025, Lithuania filed a case against Belarus at the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) for "orchestrating a migrant crisis that endangered lives and violated international law".[^cite_note-307] In 2025, four concrete-reinforced tunnels to smuggle migrants to the European Union were found on the Belarusian-Polish border in [Podlaskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaskie_Voivodeship), representing a new front in Belarusian [hybrid warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_warfare) tactics.[^cite_note-308]

### Ukrainian conscription evasion

See also: [Ukrainian conscription crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_conscription_crisis) and [Ukrainian desertion crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_desertion_crisis)

Following the February 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and the subsequent travel restrictions imposed on men aged 18 to 60 under the [2022 Ukrainian mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_mobilization),[^cite_note-309] which were later narrowed to ages 23 to 60, tens of thousands of Ukrainian men have evaded conscription by illegally crossing into neighbouring European Union member states, often undertaking hazardous transits through the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) and across the [Tisza River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza_River) that have resulted in numerous rescues and fatalities. According to Romanian border police, more than 30,000 Ukrainian men have entered the country illegally since the start of the conflict, with Romanian authorities granting them temporary protection status under European Union measures.[^cite_note-310]

In June 2026, European Commission President [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) signaled plans to alter the terms of the [Temporary Protection Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Protection_Directive), an EU emergency mechanism granting immediate collective [protection to displaced persons](1621432.md). Consequently, EU member states began debating the exclusion of newly arriving Ukrainian men aged 23 to 60 who were subject to mobilization requirements, with the stated aim of avoiding any undermining of Ukraine's self-defence capacity.[^cite_note-311][^cite_note-312] This initiative aligns with the stance of German Chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md), who stated during a bilateral meeting with Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) that Berlin would actively support Kyiv's efforts to limit the exodus of military-age men and facilitate their return, emphasizing their necessity for Ukraine's defense and future reconstruction.[^cite_note-313][^cite_note-314]

## Gallup study, 2010



Eurostat: Non-EU citizens found to be illegally present in the EU-28 and EFTA, 2015

[Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)) has published a study estimating potential migrants in 2010.[^cite_note-315] The study estimated that 700 million adults worldwide would prefer to migrate to another country. Potential migrants were asked for their country of preference if they were given free choice.

The total number of potential migrants to the [European Union](9317.md) is estimated at 200 million, comparable to the number for North America ([USA](15051.md) and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada)). In addition, an estimated 40 million potential migrants within the EU desire to move to another country within the EU, giving the EU the highest intra-regional potential migration rate.[^cite_note-316] The study estimates that from 2015 to 2017, there were about 750 million potential migrants. One in five potential migrants (21%), or about 158 million adults worldwide name the U.S. as their desired future residence. Canada, Germany, France, Australia and the United Kingdom each appeal to more than 30 million adults. Apart from the United States, the top desired target countries were: [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada) (47 million), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Germany) (42 million), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_France) (36 million), [Australia](1100670.md) (36 million) and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_United_Kingdom) (34 million).[^cite_note-317]
