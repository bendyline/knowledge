**Uttar Pradesh** ([*UTT-ər prə-DESH*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), abbr. **UP**; [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_language): *Uttar Pradēś*, pronounced [\[ˈʊt̪ːəɾ pɾə.ˈdeːɕ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/30/LL-Q1571\_%28mar%29-Aparna_Gondhalekar-%E0%A4%89%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B0\_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B6.wav/LL-Q1571\_%28mar%29-Aparna_Gondhalekar-%E0%A4%89%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B0\_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B6.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/30/LL-Q1571_%28mar%29-Aparna_Gondhalekar-%E0%A4%89%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B0_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B6.wav/LL-Q1571_%28mar%29-Aparna_Gondhalekar-%E0%A4%89%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B0_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B6.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, lit. 'Northern Province') is a [state](375986.md) in [northern India](1552939.md). With over 241 million inhabitants, it is India's [most populated state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population).[^cite_note-13] The state is bordered by [Rajasthan](26291.md) to the west, [Haryana](14189.md), [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) and [Delhi](37756.md) to the northwest, [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) and [Nepal](171166.md) to the north, [Bihar](442482.md) to the east, [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) and [Jharkhand](197225.md) to the south. It is the [fourth-largest Indian state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_of_India_by_area) covering 243,286 km<sup>2</sup> (93,933 sq mi), accounting for 7.3 per cent of the total area of India. [Lucknow](392095.md) serves as the [state capital](181337.md), with [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj) being the [judicial capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_High_Court). It is divided into 18 [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Uttar_Pradesh) and 75 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Uttar_Pradesh). Uttar Pradesh is the Indian state with the highest number of bordering states, sharing its boundaries with nine other states and one union territory.

Uttar Pradesh was established in 1950 after India had become a republic. It is a successor to the [United Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_\(1937%E2%80%931950\)), established in 1935 by renaming the [United Provinces of Agra and Oudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Agra_and_Oudh), in turn established in 1902 from the [North-Western Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Western_Provinces) and the [Oudh Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Commissioners_of_Oudh). The two major rivers of the state, the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) and its tributary [Yamuna](354286.md), meet at the [Triveni Sangam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triveni_Sangam) in Prayagraj, a Hindu pilgrimage site. Other notable rivers are [Gomti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomti_River) and [Sarayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaghara). Most of the state is covered by the Indo Gangetic plain which is intensely farmed. The forest cover in the state is 6.1 per cent of the state's geographical area, mainly in the northern Terai. The cultivable area is 82 per cent of the total geographical area, and the net area sown is 68.5 per cent of the cultivable area.[^cite_note-Cultivation-14]

Uttar Pradesh encompasses several distinct cultural regions, forming a continuum from [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj) and [Rohilkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohilkhand) in the west to [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand) and [Awadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadh) in the centre and [Purvanchal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purvanchal) in the east. [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) is the official and most widely-spoken language, with [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) as an additional official language. The region has been a major centre of political and cultural development throughout Indian history. It was the core area of several dominant Indian dynasties, including the [Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire), [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire), [Vardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushyabhuti_dynasty), [Pala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire), [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate), and [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) empires. During the British colonial period, the area contained several princely states, notably [Rampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampur_State), [Benares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benares_State), and Ramgadi, and played a central role in the Indian independence movement, including the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857).

Though long known for sugar production, the state's economy is now dominated by the services industry. The service sector comprises travel and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism), [hotel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_industry), [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate), [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance) and financial consultancies. The [economy of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Uttar_Pradesh) is just the [third-largest state economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) in India, with [₹](490783.md)39.8 [trillion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,000,000,000,000) (US\$410 billion)[^cite_note-PRS-15] in [gross domestic product](12594.md) and a per capita GSDP of ₹152,366[^cite_note-PRS-15] The [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_High_Court) of the state is located in [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj). The state plays an important role in national politics, sending 80 seats to the lower house [Lok Sabha](275456.md) and 31 seats to the upper house [Rajya Sabha](232210.md). Uttar Pradesh is significant for several major world religions. It is home to many of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism)'s most important pilgrimage sites, including [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), [Mathura](283812.md), [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya), and Prayagraj. The state is also important to [Buddhism](3267529.md); [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) delivered his first sermon at [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath) near Varanasi. Several sites in the state are associated with Jainism, and Uttar Pradesh has been an important centre of Islamic culture, architecture, and learning since the medieval period. The state contains three [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](44940.md).

## History

Main article: [History of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Uttar_Pradesh)

### Prehistory

The region of present-day Uttar Pradesh has been inhabited since [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) times, with evidence of [modern human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_human) hunter-gatherers dating to between approximately 85,000 and 72,000 years ago.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Archaeological finds from the Middle and Upper Paleolithic periods (c. 31,000–21,000 years ago) have been reported, while [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) or [microlithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microlithic) settlements, such as those at Sarai Nahar Rai in [Pratapgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratapgarh,_Uttar_Pradesh), date to around 10,550–9,550 BCE and indicate subsistence patterns based on hunting, fishing, and early burial practices.[^cite_note-20]

Early [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) remains have also been identified in the Belan Valley, where tools such as hand axes and cleavers reflect long-term habitation by hunter-gatherer communities. [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) sites, including [Koldihwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koldihwa), provide evidence of early agriculture, including rice cultivation, alongside domestication of cattle, sheep, and goats, with village settlements emerging as early as 6000 BCE and gradually developing between c. 4000 and 1500 BCE.[^cite_note-21] This developmental phase overlaps with broader cultural transitions from the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) and [Harappan traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Harappa) to the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period).[^cite_note-Allchin1982-22]

During the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) phase, the use of copper alongside stone tools is reflected in the Ochre Coloured Pottery culture at sites such as Atranjikhera, along with copper hoards found across the Ganga–Yamuna Doab.[^cite_note-Allchin1982-22] The subsequent Iron Age is marked by the Painted Grey Ware culture at sites including Hastinapur and Ahichchhatra, followed by the [Northern Black Polished Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Black_Polished_Ware) (NBPW) culture at urban centres such as [Kaushambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosambi) and Varanasi, reflecting increasing urbanisation and socio-economic complexity in the region.[^cite_note-23]

### Ancient and classical period



The [Dhamekh Stupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamekh_Stupa) in Sarnath is where [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) first taught the [Dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma), and where the Buddhist [Sangha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangha_\(Buddhism\)) came into existence through the [enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism) of [Kondanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondanna_Buddha).

Out of the sixteen *[mahajanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapadas)* (lit. 'great realms') or [oligarchic](22315.md) [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) that existed in ancient India, seven fell entirely within the present-day boundaries of the state.[^cite_note-24] The kingdom of [Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosala), in the [Mahajanapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapada) era, was also located within the regional boundaries of modern-day Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-Sen1999-25] According to Hinduism, the divine King [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama) of the [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana) epic reigned in [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya), the capital of Kosala.[^cite_note-Buck2000-26]

[Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna), another divine king of Hindu legend, who plays a key role in the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata) epic and is revered as the eighth reincarnation ([Avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar)) of the Hindu god [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu), is said to have been born in the city of [Mathura](283812.md).[^cite_note-Sen1999-25] The aftermath of the [Kurukshetra War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukshetra_War) is believed to have taken place in the area between the [Upper Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doab#Upper_Doab) and [Delhi](37756.md), in what was [Kuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_Kingdom) Mahajanapada, during the reign of the [Pandava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandava) King [Yudhishthira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yudhishthira). The kingdom of the Kurus corresponds to the [Black and Red Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_Red_Ware) and [Painted Gray Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painted_Gray_Ware) culture and the beginning of the Iron Age in northwest India, around 1000 BCE.[^cite_note-Sen1999-25]

Control over Gangetic plains region was of vital importance to the power and stability of all of India's major empires, including the [Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) (320–200 BCE), [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) (100–250 CE), [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) (350–600), and [Gurjara-Pratihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjara-Pratihara) (650–1036) empires.[^cite_note-White2010-27] Following the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns)' invasions that broke the Gupta empire, the [Ganges-Yamuna Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doab#The_Doab) saw the rise of [Kannauj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannauj).[^cite_note-Corporation2007-28] During the reign of [Harshavardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harshavardhana) (590–647), the Kannauj empire reached its zenith.[^cite_note-Corporation2007-28] It spanned from [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) in the north and [Gujarat](53707.md) in the west to [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) in the east and [Odisha](250724.md) in the south.[^cite_note-Sen1999-25] It included parts of central India, north of the [Narmada River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) and it encompassed the entire [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain).[^cite_note-Chopra2003-29]

Many communities in various parts of India claim descent from the migrants of Kannauj.[^cite_note-Bowman2000-30] Soon after Harshavardhana's death, his empire disintegrated into many kingdoms, which were invaded and ruled by the Gurjara-Pratihara empire, which challenged Bengal's [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) for control of the region.[^cite_note-Chopra2003-29] Kannauj was several times invaded by the South Indian [Rashtrakuta dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty), from the 8th century to the 10th century.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] After the fall of the Pala empire, the [Chero dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chero_dynasty) ruled from the 12th century to the 18th century.[^cite_note-33]

#### Delhi Sultanate

Main article: [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate)

Uttar Pradesh was partially or entirely ruled by the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) for 320 years (1206–1526). Five dynasties ruled over the Delhi Sultanate sequentially: the [Mamluk dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_dynasty_\(Delhi\)) (1206–90), the [Khalji dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalji_dynasty) (1290–1320), the [Tughlaq dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaq_dynasty) (1320–1414), the [Sayyid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_dynasty) (1414–51), and the [Lodi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi_dynasty) (1451–1526).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-Unesco1998-35]

The first Sultan of Delhi, [Qutb ud-Din Aibak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_ud-Din_Aibak), conquered some parts of Uttar Pradesh, including [Meerut](413695.md), [Aligarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligarh), and [Etawah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etawah). His successor, [Iltutmish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iltutmish), expanded the Sultanate's rule over Uttar Pradesh by defeating the King of [Kannauj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannauj). During the reign of Sultan [Balban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balban), the Mamluk dynasty faced numerous rebellions in the state, but he was able to suppress them and establish his authority. [Alauddin Khilji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Khilji), extended his conquests to various regions in the state, including [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) and [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj).[^cite_note-36]

Apart from the rulers, the Delhi Sultanate era also saw the growth of [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) in Uttar Pradesh. Sufi saints, such as [Nizamuddin Auliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamuddin_Auliya) and [Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbuddin_Bakhtiar_Kaki), lived during this period and their teachings had a significant impact on the people of the region. Sultanat era in the state also witnessed the construction of mosques and tombs, including the [Atala Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atala_Masjid) in [Jaunpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaunpur,_Uttar_Pradesh), the [Jama Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jama_Masjid,_Fatehpur_Sikri) in [Fatehpur Sikri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri), and the [Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghiyath_al-Din_Tughluq's_Tomb) in [Tughlaqabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaqabad).[^cite_note-Atala-37][^cite_note-Tughluq_Tomb-38]

### Medieval and early modern period

In the 16th century, [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur), a [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty) descendant of [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) and [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent_from_Genghis_Khan) from [Fergana Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergana_Valley) (modern-day [Uzbekistan](31853.md)), swept across the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass) and founded the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire), covering [India](14533.md), along with modern-day [Afghanistan](737.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Bangladesh](3454.md).[^cite_note-39] The Mughals were descended from [Persianised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianised) Central Asian [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_people) (with significant [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) admixture). In the Mughal era, Uttar Pradesh became the heartland of the empire.[^cite_note-Bowman2000-30] Mughal emperors Babur and Humayun ruled from Delhi.[^cite_note-Schimmel2004-40][^cite_note-HindustanHiro2006-41]

In 1540 an [Afghan](2235.md), [Sher Shah Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Shah_Suri), took over the reins of Uttar Pradesh after defeating the Mughal King Humanyun.[^cite_note-Ramirez-Faria2007-42] Sher Shah and his son Islam Shah ruled Uttar Pradesh from their capital at [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior).[^cite_note-Hindustan-43] After the death of [Islam Shah Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Shah_Suri), his prime minister [Hemu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemu) became the *de facto* ruler of Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, and the western parts of Bengal. He was bestowed the title of *[Hemchandra Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemu)* (title of [Vikramāditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram%C4%81ditya) adopted from [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period)) at his coronation at [Purana Qila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purana_Qila) in Delhi on 7 October 1556. A month later, Hemu died in the [Second Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Panipat), and Uttar Pradesh came under Emperor [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar)'s rule.[^cite_note-Agrawal1983-44] Akbar ruled from [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra) and [Fatehpur Sikri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri).[^cite_note-45]

In the 18th century, after the fall of Mughal authority, the power vacuum was filled by the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire), in the mid-18th century, the Maratha army invaded the Uttar Pradesh region, which resulted in [Rohillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohilla) losing control of [Rohilkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohilkhand) to the Maratha forces led by [Raghunath Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunath_Rao) and [Malha Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar). The conflict between Rohillas and Marathas came to an end on 18 December 1788 with the arrest of [Ghulam Qadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghulam_Kadir), the grandson of Najeeb-ud-Daula, who was defeated by the Maratha general [Mahadaji Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadji_Scindia). In 1803–04, following the [Second Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Maratha_War), when the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) defeated the Maratha Empire, much of the region came under British suzerainty.[^cite_note-46]

Starting from [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) in the second half of the 18th century, a series of battles for north Indian lands finally gave the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) accession over the state's territories.[^cite_note-Kudaisya2006-48] [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer) and [Jaipur](166537.md) kingdoms were also included in this northern territory, which was named the "[North-Western Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Western_Provinces)" (of Agra). Although UP later became the fifth-largest state of India, NWPA was one of the smallest states of the British Indian empire.[^cite_note-Sivaramakrishnan1999-49] Its capital shifted twice between Agra and Allahabad.[^cite_note-50]

Due to dissatisfaction with British rule, a serious rebellion erupted in parts of North India, which became known as the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). The [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) [regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment)'s sepoy stationed at [Meerut](413695.md) [cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonment), [Mangal Pandey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal_Pandey), is widely considered as its starting point.[^cite_note-Mukherjee2005-51] After the revolt failed, the British divided the most rebellious regions by reorganising their administrative boundaries. The Delhi region was split from 'NWFP of Agra' and merged with [Punjab Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_\(British_India\)). The [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer)–[Marwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwar) region was merged with [Rajputana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajputana) and [Oudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudh_State) was incorporated into the state. The new state was called the North Western Provinces of Agra and Oudh, which in 1902 was renamed as the [United Provinces of Agra and Oudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Agra_and_Oudh).[^cite_note-(India)Drake-Brockman1934-52] It was commonly referred to as the United Provinces or its acronym UP.[^cite_note-Chakrabarti1997-53][^cite_note-Cohn1996-54]

In 1920, the capital of the province was shifted from Allahabad to [Lucknow](392095.md).[^cite_note-Wilkinson-Weber1999-55] The high court continued to be at Allahabad, but a bench was established at Lucknow.[^cite_note-Lucknow_Bench-56] Allahabad continues to be an important administrative base of today's Uttar Pradesh and has several administrative headquarters.[^cite_note-Nair2004-57] Uttar Pradesh continued to be central to Indian politics and was especially important in modern Indian history as a hotbed of the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement). The state hosted modern educational institutions such as the [Aligarh Muslim University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligarh_Muslim_University), [Banaras Hindu University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaras_Hindu_University) and [Darul Uloom Deoband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Uloom_Deoband).[^cite_note-Śekhara2004-58]

Nationally known figures such as [Ram Prasad Bismil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Prasad_Bismil) and [Chandra Shekhar Azad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandra_Shekhar_Azad) were among the leaders of the movement in Uttar Pradesh. [Motilal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motilal_Nehru), [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), [Madan Mohan Malaviya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Mohan_Malaviya) and [Govind Ballabh Pant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govind_Ballabh_Pant) were important national leaders of the [Indian National Congress](149333.md). The [All India Kisan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Kisan_Sabha) was formed at the Lucknow session of the Congress on 11 April 1936, with the famous nationalist [Sahajanand Saraswati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahajanand_Saraswati) elected as its first president,[^cite_note-Śekhara2004-58] to address the longstanding grievances of the peasantry and mobilise them against the [zamindari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamindari) landlords' attacks on their occupancy rights, thus sparking the farmers movements in India.[^cite_note-59] During the [Quit India Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quit_India_Movement) of 1942, [Ballia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballia) district overthrew the colonial authority and installed an independent administration under [Chittu Pandey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittu_Pandey). Ballia became known as "Baghi Ballia" (Rebel Ballia) for this significant role in India's independence movement.[^cite_note-Chatterji2006-60]

#### Post-independence

After India's independence, the United Provinces were renamed "Uttar Pradesh" (lit. 'northern province'), preserving UP as the abbreviation of the state's name,[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] with the change coming into effect on 24 January 1950.[^cite_note-UPDay-1] The new state was formed after the merger of several princely states and territories, including the [United Provinces of Agra and Oudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Agra_and_Oudh), and the Delhi territory. The state has provided nine of India's prime ministers, which is more than any other state, and is the source of the largest number of seats in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). Despite its political influence since ancient times, its poor record in economic development and administration, poor governance, organised crime and corruption have kept it among India's backward states. The state has been affected by repeated episodes of [caste-related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste-related_violence_in_India) and [communal violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_violence).[^cite_note-63] In December 1992 the disputed [Babri Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_of_the_Babri_Masjid) located in [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya) was demolished by Hindu activists, leading to widespread violence across India.[^cite_note-communal_violence-64] In 2000, northern districts of the state were separated to form the state of [Uttarakhand](1429154.md).[^cite_note-separation-65]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Uttar_Pradesh)

The Indo-Gangetic Plain, also known as the Northern Plain or North Indian River Plain

A part of the Gangetic Plain

Uttar Pradesh, with a total area of 240,928 km<sup>2</sup> (93,023 sq mi), is India's [fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area) state in terms of land area and is roughly of same size as United Kingdom. It is situated on the northern spout of India and shares an international boundary with Nepal. The Himalayas border the state on the north,[^cite_note-land-66] but the plains that cover most of the state are distinctly different from those high mountains.[^cite_note-Geography-67] The larger [Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangetic_Plain) region is in the north; it includes the [Ganges-Yamuna Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doab#The_Doab), the Ghaghra plains, the Ganges plains and the [Terai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terai).[^cite_note-Terai-68] The smaller [Vindhya Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya_Range) and plateau region are in the south.[^cite_note-autogenerated8-69] It is characterised by hard rock strata and a varied topography of hills, plains, valleys and plateaus. The [Bhabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhabar) tract gives place to the Terai, which is covered with tall [elephant grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saccharum_ravennae) and thick forests interspersed with [marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshes) and [swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamps).[^cite_note-Terai_Area-70][^cite_note-Bhabhar-71] The sluggish rivers of the bhabhar deepen in this area, their course running through a tangled mass of thick undergrowth. The Terai runs parallel to the Bhabhar in a thin strip. The entire alluvial plain is divided into three sub-regions.[^cite_note-Great_Plains-72] The first in the [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Uttar_Pradesh) tract consisting of 14 districts which are subject to periodical [floods](50482.md) and [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts) and have been classified as scarcity areas. These districts have the highest [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), which gives the lowest per capita land. The other two regions, the [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Uttar_Pradesh) and the [western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Uttar_Pradesh), are comparatively better with a well-developed irrigation system.[^cite_note-Western_-_Eastern_Irrigation-73] They suffer from waterlogging and large-scale user tracts.[^cite_note-Meena-74] In addition, the area is fairly arid. The state has more than 32 large and small rivers; of them, the [Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges), [Yamuna](354286.md), [Saraswati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_river), [Sarayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarayu), [Betwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betwa), and [Ghaghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaghara) are larger and of religious importance in [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism).[^cite_note-rivers-75]

[Cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillage) is intensive in the state.[^cite_note-glossary-76] Uttar Pradesh falls under three agro-climatic zones viz. Middle Gangetic Plains region (Zone–IV), Upper Gangetic Plains region (Zone–V) and Central Plateau and Hills region (Zone–VIII).[^cite_note-Agro-Climatic-77] The [valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley) areas have fertile and rich soil. There is intensive cultivation on terraced hill slopes, but irrigation facilities are deficient.[^cite_note-Irrigation-78] The [Siwalik Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwalik_Range) which forms the southern foothills of the [Himalayas](37966.md), slopes down into a boulder bed called 'bhabhar'.[^cite_note-Preface-79] The transitional belt running along the entire length of the state is called the Terai and Bhabhar area. It has rich forests, cutting across it are innumerable streams which swell into raging [torrents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrent_\(stream\)) during the [monsoon](57630.md).[^cite_note-Singh1998-80]

Uttar Pradesh has a humid subtropical climate and experiences four seasons.[^cite_note-Board2008-81] The winter in January and February is followed by summer between March and May and the monsoon season between June and September.[^cite_note-Climate-82] Summers are extreme with temperatures fluctuating anywhere between 0–50 °C (32–122 °F) in parts of the state coupled with dry hot winds called the *[Loo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loo_\(wind\))*.[^cite_note-rana2007-83] The Gangetic plain varies from semiarid to sub-humid.[^cite_note-Climate-82] The mean annual rainfall ranges from 650 mm (26 in) in the southwest corner of the state to 1,000 mm (39 in) in the eastern and south eastern parts of the state.[^cite_note-rainfall-84] Primarily a summer phenomenon, the Bay of Bengal branch of the [Indian monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_of_India) is the major bearer of rain in most parts of state. After summer it is the southwest monsoon which brings most of the rain here, while in winters rain due to the *[western disturbances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_disturbances)* and north-east monsoon also contribute small quantities towards the overall precipitation of the state.[^cite_note-Board2008-81][^cite_note-WesDist-85]

The rain in Uttar Pradesh can vary from an annual average of 170 cm (67 in) in hilly areas to 84 cm (33 in) in [Western Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Uttar_Pradesh).[^cite_note-Board2008-81] Given the concentration of most of this rainfall in the four months of the monsoon, excess rain can lead to floods and then the shortage of rain can lead to droughts. As such, these two phenomena, floods and droughts, commonly recur in the state. The climate of the Vindhya Range and [plateau](538005.md) is subtropical, with a mean annual rainfall between 1,000 and 1,200 mm (39 and 47 in), most of which comes during the monsoon.[^cite_note-Climate-82] Typical summer months are from March to June, with maximum temperatures ranging from 30–38 °C (86–100 °F). There is a low relative humidity of around 20 per cent and dust-laden winds blow throughout the season. In summer, hot winds called *[loo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loo_\(wind\))* blow all across Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-Board2008-81]

Uttar Pradesh has an abundance of natural resources.[^cite_note-Flora-88] In 2011, the recorded forest area in the state was 16,583 km<sup>2</sup> (6,403 sq mi) which is about 6.9 per cent of the state's geographical area.[^cite_note-fsiwbforest-89] In spite of rapid deforestation and poaching of wildlife, a diverse flora and fauna continue to exist in the state. Uttar Pradesh is a [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat) for 4.2 per cent of all species of [Algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae) recorded in India, 6.4 per cent of [Fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi), 6.0 per cent of [Lichens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichens), 2.9 per cent of [Bryophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryophytes), 3.3 per cent of [Pteridophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pteridophytes), 8.7 per cent of [Gymnosperms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnosperms), 8.1 per cent of [Angiosperms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiosperms).[^cite_note-Plant_Diversity-90] Several species of trees, large and small [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammals), [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptiles), and insects are found in the belt of [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate) upper mountainous forests. Medicinal plants are found in the wild[^cite_note-91] and are also grown in [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations). The [Terai–Duar savanna and grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terai%E2%80%93Duar_savanna_and_grasslands) support cattle. Moist [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) trees grow in the upper Gangetic plain, especially along its riverbanks. This plain supports a wide variety of plants and animals. The Ganges and its tributaries are the habitat of large and small reptiles, [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibians), freshwater fish, and crabs. [Scrubland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrubland) trees such as the [Babool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babool) (*Vachellia nilotica*) and animals such as the [Chinkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara) (*Gazella bennettii*) are found in the arid Vindhyas.[^cite_note-animals-92][^cite_note-Predominant-93] Tropical dry deciduous forests are found in all parts of the plains. Since much sunlight reaches the ground, shrubs and grasses are also abundant.[^cite_note-Vegetation_and_Flora-94] Large tracts of these forests have been cleared for cultivation. Tropical thorny forests, consisting of widely scattered thorny trees, mainly [babool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia_nilotica) are mostly found in the southwestern parts of the state.[^cite_note-Thorny_Forest-95]

Uttar Pradesh is known for its extensive [avifauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avifauna).[^cite_note-Avifauna-96] The most common birds which are found in the state are [doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbidae), [peafowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peafowl), [junglefowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junglefowl), black partridges, [house sparrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_sparrow), [songbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songbirds), [blue jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_jay), [parakeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakeet), [quails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quail), [bulbuls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulbul), [comb ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knob-billed_duck), [kingfishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingfisher), [woodpeckers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodpecker), [snipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snipe), and parrots. Bird sanctuaries in the state include [Bakhira Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhira_Sanctuary), [National Chambal Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Chambal_Sanctuary), [Chandra Prabha Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandra_Prabha_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Hastinapur Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastinapur_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Kaimoor Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaimoor_Wildlife_Sanctuary), and [Okhla Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhla_Sanctuary).[^cite_note-Bakhira-97][^cite_note-Chambal_Gharial-98][^cite_note-Chandra_Prabha-99][^cite_note-Hastinapur_Wild_Life-100][^cite_note-Kaimoor_Wild_Life-101][^cite_note-102]

Other animals in the state include reptiles such as [lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizard), [cobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobra), [kraits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungarus), and [gharials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharial). Among the wide variety of fishes, the most common ones are [mahseer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahseer) and [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout). Some animal species have gone extinct in recent years, while others, like the [Indian rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rhinoceros) in the Terai region, [Ganges river dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_river_dolphin) primarily in the Ganges have become [endangered](20110668.md).[^cite_note-S.k.agarwal-103] Many species are vulnerable to poaching despite regulation by the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh).[^cite_note-fauna-104]

Each district is administered by a [District Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_magistrate) (DM), who is an [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS) officer appointed [Government of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh) and reports to [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)) of the division in which his/her district falls.[^cite_note-panchayat-107] The Divisional Commissioner is an IAS officer of high seniority, and part of revenue department.[^cite_note-108] Each district is divided into subdivisions, administered by a [Sub-Divisional Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Divisional_Magistrate), and again into [Tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) for land revenue administration. Each district is divided into [Blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_development_block), primarily for rural development administration, and consists of gram panchayats (village councils).[^cite_note-blocks-109] These blocks consists of urban units viz. [census towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_towns) and rural units called [gram panchayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_panchayat).[^cite_note-panchayat-107]

Local governance comprises Panchayati Raj Institutions in rural areas[^cite_note-110]—[Zilla Parishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_council_\(India\)), [Kshettra Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kshetra_panchayat), and [Gram Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_panchayat)—and urban local bodies such as Nagar Nigams (*[municipal corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation_\(India\))*), Nagar Palikas (*[municipal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council_\(India\))*), and [Nagar Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_panchayat), all governed by elected councils with five-year terms.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

Uttar Pradesh has more [metropolitan cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_populous_cities_in_India) than any other state in India.[^cite_note-metropolitan_cities-114][^cite_note-Metro_cities-115] The absolute urban population of the state is 44.4 million, which constitutes 11.8 per cent of the total urban population of India, the second-highest of any state.[^cite_note-Population_info-116] In the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), there were 15 urban agglomerations with a population greater than 500,000.[^cite_note-Datasheet-117] Uttar Pradesh has a complex system of municipalities. [Nagar Nigam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_Nigam) (Municipal Corporation) are urban local bodies in large cities such as Lucknow, Kanpur, Varanasi and cities having population more than 4 million.[^cite_note-ULB-118] These governed by a mayor and councillors elected from wards. [Nagar Palika Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_Palika_Parishad) or Municipal Council, serves medium-sized towns like [Bela Pratapgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bela_Pratapgarh), [Jalaun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalaun), or [Bisalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisalpur,_Pilibhit) and are governed by a chairperson and councillors.[^cite_note-Nagar_Palika-119] [Nagar Panchayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_Panchayat) which operate in smaller towns and semi-urban areas like [Badlapur, Jaunpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badlapur,_Jaunpur), [Bikapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikapur), or [Chilkana Sultanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilkana_Sultanpur), are governed by a chairman and councillors.[^cite_note-Nagar_Palika-119] There are 14 [Municipal Corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Corporations_in_India).[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-GENERAL_AMENDMENT-121] [Noida](986046.md) and [Greater Noida](4942600.md) in [Gautam Budha Nagar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautam_Buddha_Nagar_district) are specially administered by statutory authorities under the Uttar Pradesh Industrial Development Act of 1976.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

In 2011, state's cabinet ministers headed by the then Chief Minister [Mayawati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayawati) announced the separation of Uttar Pradesh into four different states of Purvanchal, Bundelkhand, Avadh Pradesh and Paschim Pradesh with twenty-eight, seven, twenty-three and seventeen districts, respectively, later the proposal was turned down when the [Akhilesh Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhilesh_Yadav)–led [Samajwadi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samajwadi_Party) came to power in the [2012 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Uttar_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election).[^cite_note-124]

Uttar Pradesh has a very large population and a high population growth rate. From 1991 to 2001 its population increased by over 26 per cent.[^cite_note-population-126] It is the [most populous state in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population), with 199,581,477 people in March 2011.[^cite_note-autogenerated7-127] The state contributes to 16.2 per cent of India's population. As of 2023, the estimated population of the state is around 235.7 million people.[^cite_note-NITI1-128] The population density is 828 people per square kilometre, making it one of the most densely populated [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India) in the country.[^cite_note-Statistics-129] It has the largest [scheduled caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_caste) population, making up 23 per cent of the state's population, whereas [scheduled tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scheduled_Tribes_in_India) are less than 1 per cent of the total population.[^cite_note-Tribe-130][^cite_note-Indigenous-131]

The [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sex_ratio) in 2011, at 912 women to 1,000 men, was lower than the national figure of 943.[^cite_note-pc-census2011-132] The low sex ratio in Uttar Pradesh is a result of various factors, such as [sex-selective abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex-selective_abortion), [female infanticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_infanticide), and discrimination against girls and women.[^cite_note-UP_Foeticide-133][^cite_note-Uttar_Pradesh21-134] The state's 2001–2011 decennial growth rate (including Uttrakhand) was 20.1 per cent, higher than the national rate of 17.64 per cent.[^cite_note-Decennial-135][^cite_note-decennialgrowth-136]

The 2011 literacy rate was 67.7 per cent, which was below the national average of 74 per cent.[^cite_note-Literacy_rate-137][^cite_note-literacy_rates-138] The literacy rate for men is 79 per cent and for women 59 per cent. The 2001 literacy rate was 56 per cent overall, 67 per cent for men and 43 per cent for women.[^cite_note-Literacy-139] A report based on a [National Statistical Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Statistics_Office_\(India\)) (NSO) survey[^cite_note-remark1-140] revealed that Uttar Pradesh's literacy rate is 73 per cent, less than the national average of 77.7 per cent. According to the report, in the rural region, the literacy rate among men is 80.5 per cent and women is 60.4 per cent, while in urban areas, the literacy rate among men is 86.8 per cent and women is 74.9 per cent.[^cite_note-NSO-141]

Economically, a large number of the population lives below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_threshold).[^cite_note-poverty-142] As per a [World Bank](36752.md) document released in 2016, the pace of poverty reduction in the state has been slower than the rest of the country.[^cite_note-World_Bank-143] Estimates released by the [Reserve Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Bank_of_India) for the year 2011–12 revealed that the state had 59.819 million people below the poverty line, the most for any state in India.[^cite_note-poverty-142][^cite_note-commission-144] The central and eastern districts in particular have very high levels of poverty. The state is also experiencing widening consumption inequality. As per the report of the [Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Statistics_and_Programme_Implementation) released in 2020, the state per capita income is below ₹80,000 (US\$830) per annum.[^cite_note-Annum-145]

### Religion

Religion in Uttar Pradesh (2011)

[^cite_note-religion_census-146]

1. [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (79.7%)

2. [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Uttar_Pradesh) (19.3%)

3. [Sikhism](27964.md) (0.32%)

4. Other[^cite_note-147] (0.40%)

5. Not stated (0.29%)

Uttar Pradesh is home to the largest populations of both Hindus and Muslims in India, with the two communities comprising approximately 80 per cent and 19 per cent of the state's population, respectively. The state's Hindu population is estimated at 186 million, making it the largest Hindu population of any state in the country. Uttar Pradesh also has an estimated 44 million Muslims, a figure that exceeds the Muslim populations of several Muslim‑majority countries, including [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Syria](7515849.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), and [Libya](17633.md). Hinduism is the predominant religion and is especially strong in rural areas. Islam is more prominent in urban areas and northwestern parts of the state. [Moradabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moradabad) and [Rampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampur_district) are the districts in which Muslims constitute an absolute majority, accounting for 50.80 per cent and 50.57 per cent of the population, respectively.[^cite_note-w745-148][^cite_note-s751-149]

Uttar Pradesh has historically been an important centre of both [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism). In Buddhism, [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) delivered his first sermon at [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath) after attaining enlightenment, making it one of the religion’s most significant pilgrimage sites. Other major Buddhist centres, including [Kushinagar](33686220.md), where Buddha attained [Mahaparinirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahaparinirvana), are located in the state.&#x20;

[Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) [Tirthankaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankaras) are believed to have been born in the state. According to Jain traditions and scriptures, [Ajitanatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajitanatha), the second Tirthankara, is closely associated with Ayodhya, which is also traditionally regarded as the birthplace of other [Tirthankaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankaras), including [Abhinandananatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhinandananatha) and [Sumatinatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatinatha).[^cite_note-Jainism_Birth-150] Important Jain pilgrimage sites in the state includes [Deogarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deogarh,_Uttar_Pradesh), [Ahichchhatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahichchhatra) (present-day [Ramnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramnagar,_Bareilly)). A sizeable population of Jains lives in urban areas in the northwest part of the state.&#x20;

The [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) history of Uttar Pradesh is closely linked to the travels of the Sikh Gurus and later post-Partition migrations. [Guru Nanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Nanak) traveled extensively through Uttar Pradesh during his first and third *[Udasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udasis)* (spiritual journeys).[^cite_note-Sikh_Initials-151] Sikh Gurus visited and established sangats (congregations) in the state.[^cite_note-congregations-152] Guru Nanak visited Mathura (*Guru Nanak Bagichi*) and Varanasi (*Guru Bagh*). [Guru Hargobind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Hargobind) sent representatives to Lucknow around 1580 to establish Sikh congregations.[^cite_note-n048-153] Sikhs, mainly descendants of refugees from Partition, are settled in [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj), [Sultanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanpur,_Uttar_Pradesh), [Benaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benaras) and [Jaunpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaunpur,_Uttar_Pradesh), and northern districts bordering Delhi .[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-h002-155]

### Language

2. [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language) (10.9%)

3. [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) (5.42%)

Uttar Pradesh is a linguistically diverse state where several [Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages) and dialects are widely spoken. The primary official language is [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), used in government, education, and media, and it is also the most widely spoken language across the state.[^cite_note-2011lang-7] A significant portion of the population speaks various regional languages grouped under the Hindi. These include [Awadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadhi_language), spoken in [Awadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadh) in central Uttar Pradesh; [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language) spoken in [Purvanchal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purvanchal) in eastern Uttar Pradesh; and [Braj Bhasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj_Bhasha) spoken in the [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj) region in Western Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-Experts-158]

These languages have been recognised by the state government for official use in their respective regions. Bhojpuri is the second most spoken language in the state, with nearly 11 per cent of the population using it. This is partly due to the high [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) in eastern Uttar Pradesh, where Bhojpuri is predominantly spoken, compared to the central and western regions of the state.[^cite_note-Experts-158]

[Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) was granted the status of a second official language in 1989, through the Official Languages Act.[^cite_note-Second-159][^cite_note-Approves-160] It is spoken by approximately 5.4 per cent of the population.[^cite_note-2011lang-7][^cite_note-census2011-langreport-161] [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is used as a means of communication for education, commerce, and governance. It is commonly spoken and employed as a language of instruction in educational institutions, as well as for conducting business transactions and managing administrative affairs. Other notable languages spoken in the state include [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (0.3 per cent) and [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) (0.1 per cent).[^cite_note-census2011-langreport-161]

### Human Development Index

In 2026, Uttar Pradesh’s [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI), which is calculated on the basis of [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy), education, and per capita income, stands at approximately 0.600, placing the state in the medium human development category.[^cite_note-HDI1-162] While the state has shown consistent, steady improvements across health, education, and per capita income over the last decade, it continues to rank in the lower third among Indian states.[^cite_note-HDI_Rank-163]

## Governance and administration

Main articles: [Government of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh) and [Vidhan Bhavan, Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Bhavan,_Lucknow)

refer caption

Uttar Pradesh Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha), the lower house of the bicameral legislature

The state is governed by a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) of [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy). Uttar Pradesh is one of the seven states in India, where the state legislature is bicameral, comprising two houses: the [Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Sabha) (Legislative Assembly) and the [Vidhan Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Parishad) (Legislative Council).[^cite_note-vidhan_parishad-164][^cite_note-Vidhan_Sabha-165] The [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly) consists of 404 members who are elected for five-year terms. The [Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Legislative_Council) is a permanent body of 100 members with one-third (33 members) retiring every two years. The state sends the largest number of legislators to the national Parliament.[^cite_note-elections-166] The state contributes 80 seats to [Lok Sabha](275456.md), the lower house of the [Indian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Parliament), and 31 seats to [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), the upper house.[^cite_note-Grover1989-167][^cite_note-168]

The [Government of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh) is a [democratically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) elected body in India with the [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Uttar_Pradesh) as its constitutional head and is appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) for a five-year term.[^cite_note-169] The leader of the party or coalition with a majority in the Legislative Assembly is appointed as the [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_chief_ministers_of_Uttar_Pradesh) by the governor, and the council of ministers is appointed by the governor on the advice of the chief minister. The governor remains a ceremonial head of the state, while the chief minister and his council are responsible for day-to-day government functions. The Council of Ministers includes Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State (MoS), with the latter sometimes holding independent charge of specific departments. The Secretariat headed by the [Chief Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Secretary_\(India\)) assists the council of ministers. The Chief Secretary is also the administrative head of the government. Each government department is headed by a minister, who is assisted by an [Additional Chief Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_Chief_Secretary_\(India\)) or a [Principal Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Secretary_\(India\)), who is usually an officer of [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS), the Additional Chief Secretary/Principal Secretary serves as the administrative head of the department they are assigned to. Each department also has officers of the rank of Secretary, Special Secretary, Joint Secretary etc. assisting the Minister and the Additional Chief Secretary/Principal Secretary.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-\:64-171]

For administration, the state is divided into 18 divisions and 75 districts. [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)), an IAS officer is the head of revenue administration on the divisional level.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-\:42-172][^cite_note-\:52-173] The administration in each district is headed by a [District Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_collector) (DM), who is also an IAS officer, and is assisted by several officers belonging to state services.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-\:022-174] The District Magistrate, as the head of the district administration, is responsible for revenue administration, maintenance of law and order, disaster management, conducting elections, coordination among various departments, and delivery of public services in the district.[^cite_note-175] At the [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_development_block) level, the [Block Development Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Development_Officer) (BDO) is responsible for the overall development of the [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(district_subdivision\)). The [Uttar Pradesh Police](14570210.md) is headed by an IPS officer of the rank of [Director general of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_general_of_police) (DGP). A [Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Police) (SP), an IPS officer assisted by the officers of the Uttar Pradesh Police Service, is entrusted with the responsibility of maintaining law and order and related issues in each district. The Divisional Forest Officer (DFO), an officer belonging to the [Indian Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Forest_Service) manages the forests, environment, and wildlife of the district, assisted by the officers of [Provincial Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Forest_Service_\(Uttar_Pradesh\)) and Uttar Pradesh Forest Subordinate Service.[^cite_note-Provincial_Forest-176]

refer caption

Allahabad High Court

The judiciary in the state consists of the [Allahabad High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_High_Court) in [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj), the [Lucknow](392095.md) Bench of Allahabad High Court, district courts and session courts in each district or Sessions Division, and lower courts at the [tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) level.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-courts-177] The president of India appoints the chief justice of the High Court of the Uttar Pradesh judiciary on the advice of the [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India) of the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) as well as the governor of Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-Appointment-178] Subordinate Judicial Service, categorised into two divisions viz. Uttar Pradesh civil judicial services and Uttar Pradesh higher judicial service are another vital part of the judiciary of Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-presiding-179] While the Uttar Pradesh civil judicial services comprise the Civil Judges (Junior Division)/Judicial Magistrates and civil judges (Senior Division)/Chief Judicial Magistrate, the Uttar Pradesh higher judicial service comprises civil and sessions judges.[^cite_note-\:2-170] The Subordinate judicial service (viz. The district court of [Etawah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etawah) and the district court of Kanpur Dehat) of the judiciary at Uttar Pradesh is controlled by the District Judge.[^cite_note-\:2-170][^cite_note-presiding-179][^cite_note-Civil_Judiciary-180]

Politics in Uttar Pradesh has been dominated by four political parties – the [Samajwadi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samajwadi_Party), the [Bahujan Samaj Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahujan_Samaj_Party), the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md), and the [Indian National Congress](149333.md). The political landscape of the state is often characterised by intense competition and [polarisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_polarization), leading to [caste-based tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste-related_violence_in_India) and [communal conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_violence).[^cite_note-State_Tensions-181] Critics often suggest that despite Uttar Pradesh's significant political legacy of producing eight [Prime Ministers](24452.md), the state continues to struggle with issues that hinder its overall advancement.[^cite_note-politically_crucial_state-182]

### Crime and accidents

According to the [National Human Rights Commission of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Human_Rights_Commission_of_India) (NHRC), Uttar Pradesh tops the list of states of [encounter killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encounter_killings_by_police) and custodial deaths.[^cite_note-Express-183] In 2014, the state recorded 365 judicial deaths out of a total 1,530 deaths recorded in the country.[^cite_note-deaths-184] NHRC further said, of the over 30,000 murders registered in the country in 2016, Uttar Pradesh had 4,889 cases.[^cite_note-NHRC-185] A data from [Minister of Home Affairs](1974473.md) (MHA) avers, [Bareilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bareilly) recorded the highest number of custodial death at 25, followed by [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra) (21), [Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj) (19) and [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) (9). [National Crime Records Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Crime_Records_Bureau) (NCRB) data from 2011 says, the state has the highest number of crimes among any state in India, but due to its [high population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), the actual per capita crime rate is low.[^cite_note-186] The state also continues to top the list of states with maximum [communal violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_violence_in_India) incidents. An analysis of Ministers of State of Home Affairs states (2014), 23 per cent of all incidents of communal violence in India took place in the state.[^cite_note-rise_violence-187][^cite_note-violence_raise-188] According to a research assembled by [State Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Bank_of_India), Uttar Pradesh failed to improve its [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI) ranking over a period of 27 years (1990–2017).[^cite_note-SBI-189] Based on sub-national human development index data for [Indian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_Human_Development_Index) from 1990 to 2017, the report also stated that the value of human development index has steadily increased over time from 0.39 in 1990 to 0.59 in 2017.[^cite_note-Index-190][^cite_note-HDI2-191][^cite_note-growth-192] The [Uttar Pradesh Police](14570210.md), governed by the [Department of Home and Confidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Home_and_Confidential), is the largest [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) force in the world.[^cite_note-armed_force-193][^cite_note-194][^cite_note-Highlight-195]

Uttar Pradesh also reported the highest number of deaths – 41,746 – due to road accidents till December 2022, according to "Road Accidents in India" report of [Union Ministry of Road Transport and Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Road_Transport_and_Highways).[^cite_note-Road_Deaths-196][^cite_note-Highest-197] The [UP Transport Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) report also indicates that the primary cause of road accident fatalities was over-speeding, which accounted for 40 per cent of deaths. Drunken driving contributed to 10 per cent of fatalities, while 12 per cent of deaths were due to driving on the wrong side. Additionally, 10 per cent of accident deaths were caused by the use of mobile phones, and 5 per cent resulted from running red lights. The remaining 23 per cent of deaths were attributed to factors such as losing control of the vehicle, drowsiness, poor road visibility, and engineering defects.[^cite_note-198]

Between 2006 and 2010, the state has been hit with three terrorist attacks, including explosions in a landmark holy place, a court and a temple. The [2006 Varanasi bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Varanasi_bombings) were a series of bombings that occurred across the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) holy city of [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) on 7 March 2006. At least 28 people were killed and as many as 101 others were injured.[^cite_note-bomb09-199][^cite_note-200] In the afternoon of 23 November 2007, within a span of 25 minutes, six consecutive [serial blasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Uttar_Pradesh_bombings) occurred in the Lucknow, Varanasi, and Faizabad courts, in which 28 people were killed.[^cite_note-blast_responsible-201][^cite_note-2007_bombing-202][^cite_note-bombings-203] Another blast occurred on 7 December 2010, the [blast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Varanasi_bombing) occurred at Sheetla Ghat in Varanasi in which more than 38 people were killed.[^cite_note-people_killed-204][^cite_note-terror_attack-205] In February 2016, a series of bomb blasts occurred at the Jhakarkati Bus Station in [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur), killing 2 people and injuring more than 30.[^cite_note-Kanpur16-206]

In terms of [net state domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_domestic_product) (NSDP), Uttar Pradesh is the [fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) economy in India, with an estimated gross state domestic product of ₹14.89 lakh crore (US\$150 billion),[^cite_note-\:0-208] contributing 8.4 per cent of India's gross domestic product.[^cite_note-RBI2023-209] According to the report generated by India Brand Equity Foundation (IBEF), in 2014–15, Uttar Pradesh has accounted for 19 per cent share in the country's total food grain output.[^cite_note-IBEF2015-210] About 70 per cent of India's sugar comes from Uttar Pradesh. The state has significant reserves of cement-grade [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and crystalline [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), in districts of [Mirzapur](638650.md) and [Sonbhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonbhadra), supporting a growing cement and refractory industry. With 359 manufacturing clusters, cement is the top sector of [SMEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Micro,_Small_and_Medium_Enterprises) in Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-ET-20130620-SMEs-212] The Uttar Pradesh Financial Corporation (UPFC) was established in 1954 under the SFCs Act of 1951 mainly to develop small- and medium-scale industries in the state.[^cite_note-UPFC-213] The UPFC also provides working capital to existing units with a soundtrack record and to new units under a single window scheme.[^cite_note-statements-214] In July 2012, due to financial constraints and directions from the state government, lending activities were suspended except for State Government Schemes.[^cite_note-budget-215] The state has reported total private investment worth over Rs. 25,081 crores during the years of 2012 and 2016.[^cite_note-216]

According to the 2024 [Ease of Doing Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_states_ranking_by_ease_of_doing_business) ranking by the [Commerce and Industry ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_and_Industry_\(India\)), Uttar Pradesh ranked among the top 10 states in India and secured the highest position among all northern states.[^cite_note-i321-217] The state is home to over 11,500 leather production units, primarily concentrated in Agra and Kanpur, making it one of India's leading leather manufacturing regions.[^cite_note-leather-218] Uttar Pradesh also contributes around 15 per cent of India's total fabric production and employs nearly 30 per cent of the country's artisan workforce.[^cite_note-BS-219]

According to the Uttar Pradesh Budget Documents (2019–20), Uttar Pradesh's debt burden is 29.8 per cent of the [GSDP](12594.md).[^cite_note-Debt_HT-220] The state's total financial debt stood at ₹2.09 lakh crore (US\$22 billion) in 2011.[^cite_note-debt_burden-221] Uttar Pradesh has not been able to witness double-digit economic growth despite consistent attempts over the years.[^cite_note-Debt_HT-220] The GSDP is estimated to have grown 7 per cent in 2017–18 and 6.5 per cent in 2018–19 which is about 10 per cent of India's GDP. According to a survey conducted by the [Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Monitoring_Indian_Economy) (CMIE), Uttar Pradesh's [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) increased 11.4 percentage points, rising to 21.5 per cent in April 2020.[^cite_note-Unemployment-222] Uttar Pradesh has the largest number of [net migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration) migrating out of the state.[^cite_note-223] The 2011 census data on migration shows that nearly 14.4 million (14.7 per cent) people had migrated out of Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-224] Marriage was cited as the predominant reason for migration among females. Among males, the most important reason for migration was work and employment.[^cite_note-Reasons-225] Uttar Pradesh continues to have regional disparities, particularly with the western districts of the state showing higher development indicators such as per capita district development product (PCDDP) and gross district development product (GDDP) compared to other regions.[^cite_note-TOI_Regions-226] Due to inadequate infrastructure and a dense population, [Purvanchal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purvanchal) faces notable socio-economic disparities.[^cite_note-NG_Journal-227] For 2021–22 the GDDP for Purvanchal it is ₹5.37 lakh crore, while for [Western Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Uttar_Pradesh) (including the [Upper Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Doab), [Rohilkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohilkhand) and [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj)) it is ₹9.44 lakh crore. For the [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand) and [Awadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadh) regions, the GDDP remained ₹99,029.34 crore and ₹3.36 lakh crore, respectively. As of 2021–22, the per capita annual income in eastern districts is about one-fourth of the national average at ₹12,741 while the state's average stood at ₹17,349.[^cite_note-HT_Data-228]

In 2009–10, the [tertiary sector of the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) (service industries) was the largest contributor to the gross domestic product of the state, contributing 44.8 per cent of the state domestic product compared to 44 per cent from the primary sector (agriculture, forestry, and tourism) and 11.2 per cent from the secondary sector (industrial and manufacturing).[^cite_note-Investment-230][^cite_note-Service_sector-231] [Noida](986046.md), [Meerut](413695.md), and [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra) rank as the top 3 districts with the highest per capita income, whereas [Lucknow](392095.md) and [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur) rank 7th and 9th in per capita income.[^cite_note-232] During the 11th five-year plan (2007–2012), the average [gross state domestic product](12594.md) (GSDP) growth rate was 7.3 per cent, lower than 15.5 per cent, the average for all states of the country.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-State_Finances-234] The state's per capita GSDP was ₹29,417 (US\$310), lower than the national per capita GSDP of ₹60,972 (US\$630).[^cite_note-Ministry_of_statistics_and_Program_Implementation_Govt._Of_India-235] Labor efficiency is higher at an index of 26 than the national average of 25. Textiles and sugar refining, both long-standing industries in Uttar Pradesh, employ a significant proportion of the state's total factory labour. The economy also benefits from the state's tourism industry.[^cite_note-Small_Scale_Industries-236]

## Transportation

Further information: [List of state highways in Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Uttar_Pradesh), [List of airports in Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Uttar_Pradesh), and [Uttar Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_State_Road_Transport_Corporation)



A section of the Delhi–Noida Direct Flyway

Uttar Pradesh has the largest railway network in the country, but in relative terms has only sixth-highest railway density despite its plain topography and largest population. As of 2015, there were 9,077 km (5,640 mi) of rail in the state.[^cite_note-route_length-237][^cite_note-Network-238] The railway network in the state is controlled by two divisions of the [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways) viz. North Central Railway and North Eastern Railway. [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj_Junction_railway_station) is the headquarters of the [North Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Railway_zone)[^cite_note-North_Central_Railway-239] and [Gorakhpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorakhpur_Junction_railway_station) is the headquarters of the [North Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Eastern_Railway_zone).[^cite_note-North-Eastern_Railways-240][^cite_note-security_system-241] Lucknow and Moradabad serve as divisional headquarters of the Northern Railway Division.[^cite_note-North-Eastern_Railways-240] The [Lucknow Swarna Shatabdi Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow_Swarna_Shatabdi_Express), the second fastest [Shatabdi Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatabdi_Express) train, connects the Indian capital of [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi_railway_station) to [Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow_Charbagh_railway_station) while [Kanpur Shatabdi Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur%E2%80%93New_Delhi_Shatabdi_Express), connects New Delhi to [Kanpur Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur_Central_railway_station). This was the first train in India to get the new German [LHB coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LHB_coaches).[^cite_note-train-242] The railway stations of [Prayagraj Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj_Junction), [Agra Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra_Cantonment), Lucknow Charbagh, [Gorakhpur Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorakhpur_Junction), Kanpur Central, [Mathura Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_Junction) and [Varanasi Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi_Junction) are included in the Indian Railways list of 50 world-class railway stations.[^cite_note-Railway_Budget-243] The [Lucknow Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow_Metro), along with the [Kanpur Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur_Metro) (Orange line), are rapid transit systems that serve Lucknow and Kanpur, respectively.

The state has a large, multimodal transportation system with the largest road network in the country.[^cite_note-ROAD_NETWORK-244] It has 42 national highways, with a total length of 4,942 km (3,071 miles) comprising 8.9 per cent of the total national highways length in India.[^cite_note-NH_Share-245] The [Uttar Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) (UPSRTC) was established in 1972 to provide transportation in the state with connecting services to adjoining states.[^cite_note-246] The UPSRTC's fleet consists of 11,238 buses operating on 2,762 routes across a total distance of 768,065 km (477,253 mi) throughout the state, generating an average daily income of ₹16 crore (equivalent to ₹17 crore or US\$1.8 million in 2026).[^cite_note-Fleet_Profite-247] Despite its extensive operation, many of UPSRTC buses are now outdated and unreliable, raising concerns about their condition and the impact on passenger safety.[^cite_note-Fleets-248] All cities are connected to [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highways_in_India), and all district headquarters are being connected with four lane roads which carry traffic between major centres within the state. One of them is [Agra–Lucknow Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra%E2%80%93Lucknow_Expressway), which is a 302 km (188 miles) [controlled-access highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) constructed by [UPEIDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Expressways_Industrial_Development_Authority).[^cite_note-249] Uttar Pradesh has the highest road density in India – 1,027 km (638 miles) per 1,000 km<sup>2</sup> (390 square miles) – and the largest surfaced urban-road network in the country – 50,721 km (31,517 miles).[^cite_note-250]

One of the Socata TB-20 that IGRUA operates

IGRUA's [Trinidad TB-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCATA_TB_family) aircraft taking off

By passenger traffic in India, [Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhary_Charan_Singh_International_Airport) in Lucknow and [Lal Bahadur Shastri Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri_Airport) in Varanasi, are the major international airports and the main gateway to the state.[^cite_note-Airports-251] Another international airport has been built at [Kushinagar](33686220.md). However, since its inauguration, [Kushinagar International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushinagar_International_Airport) has not yet seen any outbound flights to international destinations.[^cite_note-Kushinagar_IA-252][^cite_note-253] [Ayodhya Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya_Airport), officially known as Maharshi Valmiki International Airport, was inaugurated on 30 December 2023 and currently facilitates domestic air travel.[^cite_note-Ayodhya_Airport-254] The newly constructed [Noida International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noida_International_Airport) commenced commercial operations in 2026, becoming the second major airport serving the [Delhi](37756.md) [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)).[^cite_note-Noida_Airport-255]

Uttar Pradesh has six domestic airports located at [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra_Airport), [Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_Airport), [Bareilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bareilly_Airport), [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindon_Airport), [Gorakhpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorakhpur_Airport) and [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur_Airport).[^cite_note-DFCCIL-256][^cite_note-257] Under the collaboration with [civilian aviation authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Authority_of_India), these domestic airports are primarily used by the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) for operational flexibility, especially during emergencies or natural disasters. Developed under Regional Connectivity Scheme ([UDAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDAN)), [Chitrakoot Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitrakoot_Airport) is the first and only airport in the [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand) region. Although inaugurated in 2024, the airport has remained largely inactive due to low passenger traffic, and flight operations were suspended in December 2024 owing to poor visibility and operational challenges.[^cite_note-BT_UP-258][^cite_note-closed-259] Similarly, the airports in [Azamgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azamgarh_Airport), [Aligarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligarh_Airport), [Moradabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moradabad_Airport), [Shravasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravasti_Airport), and [Saharanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarsawa_Airport) have faced temporary closures due to limited commercial viability, low passenger traffic, and a shortage of suitable aircraft.[^cite_note-BT_UP-258][^cite_note-closed-259] [Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_Rashtriya_Uran_Akademi) (IGRUA) is a premier pilot training institute located in [Amethi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amethi).[^cite_note-IGRUA-260] Established in 1985 under the [Ministry of Civil Aviation](10257026.md), IGRUA provides commercial pilot training programs.[^cite_note-pilot-261]

## Sports

See also: [Indian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Grand_Prix) and [Uttar Pradesh Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Cricket_Association)

Traditional sports, now played mostly as a pastime, include [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)), [kabaddi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaddi), and track-sports or [water-sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_water_sports) played according to local traditional rules and without modern equipment. Some sports are designed to display martial skills such as using a sword or 'Pata' (stick).[^cite_note-Rao2005-262] Due to a lack of organised patronage and requisite facilities, these sports survive mostly as individuals' hobbies or local competitive events. Among modern sports, [field hockey](10886.md) is popular and Uttar Pradesh has produced top-level players in India, such as Nitin Kumar. and Lalit Kumar Upadhyay.[^cite_note-player-263]

Recently, [cricket](25675557.md) has become more popular than [field hockey](10886.md).[^cite_note-Cricket_Legacy-264] Uttar Pradesh won its first [Ranji Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranji_Trophy) tournament in February 2006, beating [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) in the final.[^cite_note-Ranji_Trophy-265] [Shaheed Vijay Singh Pathik Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaheed_Vijay_Singh_Pathik_Sports_Complex) is a newly built international cricket stadium with a capacity of around 20,000 spectators.[^cite_note-266] Wrestling has deep roots in Uttar Pradesh, with many [akharas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhara) (traditional wrestling schools) spread across the state.[^cite_note-Kushti-267]

The [Uttar Pradesh football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_football_team) (UPFS) serves as the governing body for football in Uttar Pradesh. It holds authority over the Uttar Pradesh football team and is officially affiliated with the [All India Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Football_Federation).[^cite_note-Football_Sangh-268] The UPFS participates in sending state teams to compete in all National Football Championships organised by the All India Football Federation.[^cite_note-Official_AIFF-269] Additionally, the UPFS oversees two Mandal Football Associations: the Aligarh Football Association and the Kanpur Football Association.[^cite_note-UPFS-270] The Uttar Pradesh Badminton Association is a sports body affiliated to [Badminton Association of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_Association_of_India) responsible for overseeing players representing Uttar Pradesh at the national level.[^cite_note-TOI_2023-271]

The [Buddh International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddh_International_Circuit) hosted India's inaugural [F1 Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_Grands_Prix) race on 30 October 2011.[^cite_note-Indian_Grand_Prix-272] Races were only held three times before being cancelled due to falling attendance and lack of government support. The government of Uttar Pradesh considered [Formula One](10854.md) to be entertainment and not a sport, and thus imposed taxes on the event and participants.[^cite_note-bbc.co.uk-273]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Uttar_Pradesh)

See also: [List of institutions of higher education in Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_institutions_of_higher_education_in_Uttar_Pradesh)

refer caption

The [Central Drug Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Drug_Research_Institute), an autonomous multidisciplinary research institute

Uttar Pradesh has a prolonged tradition of education, although historically it was primarily confined to the elite class and religious schools.[^cite_note-schools-274] Sanskrit-based learning formed the major part of education from the [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period) to the [Gupta periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_period). As cultures travelled through the region they brought their bodies of knowledge with them, adding [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) scholarship to the community. These formed the core of Hindu-Buddhist-Muslim education until the rise of British colonialism. The present schools-to-university system of education owes its inception and development in the state (as in the rest of the country) to foreign [Christian missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_missionaries) and the [British colonial administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).[^cite_note-Education_System-275]

Schools in the state are either managed by the government or by private trusts. [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) is used as a medium of instruction in most of the schools except those affiliated to the [CBSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) or the council for [ICSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education) boards.[^cite_note-Facts-276] Under the [10+2+3 plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10+2+3_plan), after completing secondary school, students typically enroll for two years in a [junior college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_college), also known as pre-university, or in schools with a higher secondary facility affiliated with the [Uttar Pradesh Board of High School and Intermediate Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_High_School_and_Intermediate_Education_Uttar_Pradesh) (commonly referred to as U.P. Board) or a central board. Students choose from one of three streams, namely [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts), commerce, or science. Upon completing the required coursework, students may enrol in general or professional degree programs. In a study done by [Child Rights and You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_Rights_and_You) (CRY) and the Centre for Budgets, Governance, and Accountability (CBGA), Uttar Pradesh spent ₹9,167 per [pupil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student), which is below the national average of ₹12,768.[^cite_note-pupil_ratio-277]

The pupil/teacher ratio is 39\:1,[^cite_note-278] lower than the national average of 23\:1.[^cite_note-NBER_Report-279] According to the [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research), the state reported the second-highest teacher [absenteeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absenteeism) (31%) in rural public schools among 19 surveyed states.[^cite_note-NBER_Data-280] According to an answer given by the [Union Education Minister](49127628.md) in 2020 in the Lok Sabha, about 17.1 per cent of all elementary teacher posts in government schools in Uttar Pradesh are vacant. In terms of absolute numbers, the figure stands at 210,000.[^cite_note-Vacant_posts-281] In February 2024, the [Uttar Pradesh government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_government) informed [legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly) that, 85,152 posts of headmasters and assistant teachers are vacant in the state.[^cite_note-Deccan_Herald-282] According to data presented by the [Union Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(India\)) in [Parliament](410238.md), the number of government schools in Uttar Pradesh decreased by approximately 26,000 between the 2017–18 and 2023–24 academic years.[^cite_note-Schools_closed-283] The state also recorded closures of educational institutions at the school and higher education levels. [All India Council for Technical Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Council_for_Technical_Education) (AICTE) data showed that 12 engineering colleges in Uttar Pradesh closed during the 2025–26 academic year, the highest number among the states, alongside Maharashtra.[^cite_note-AICTE-284]

refer caption

[La Martiniere college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Martini%C3%A8re_College,_Lucknow), Lucknow

Uttar Pradesh has more than 45 universities,[^cite_note-universities-285] including six [central universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_University,_India), twenty eight [state universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_university_\(India\)), eight [deemed universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deemed_university), two [IITs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IITs) in [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_\(BHU\)_Varanasi) and [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Kanpur), [AIIMS Gorakhpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIIMS_Gorakhpur) and [AIIMS Rae Bareli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIIMS_Rae_Bareli), an [IIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institutes_of_Management) in [Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management_Lucknow)[^cite_note-Universities-286][^cite_note-IIM-287] Founded in 1845, [La Martinière Girls' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Martiniere_Lucknow) in Lucknow, stands as one of the oldest schools in India.[^cite_note-288] Located in Amethi, [Rajiv Gandhi Institute of Petroleum Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_Institute_of_Petroleum_Technology) (RGIPT), provides education and training in [STEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science,_technology,_engineering,_and_mathematics) fields, particularly emphasising the petroleum industry. With deemed university status, the RGIPT awards degrees in its own right. [King George's Medical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George's_Medical_University) (KGMU), located in Lucknow, is an institution for medical education, research, and healthcare services. [The Integral University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_University_\(Lucknow\)), a state level institution, was established by the [Uttar Pradesh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh) to provide education in different technical, [applied science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_science), and other [disciplines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_discipline).[^cite_note-institution-289] The [Central Institute of Higher Tibetan Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Institute_of_Higher_Tibetan_Studies) was founded as an autonomous organisation by the national [ministry of culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(India\)). [Jagadguru Rambhadracharya Handicapped University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagadguru_Rambhadracharya_Handicapped_University) is the only university established exclusively for the disabled in the world.[^cite_note-jansatta-290]

As of 2023, the state has 573 public libraries.[^cite_note-Libraries-291][^cite_note-Library_network-292] Established in 1875, [Maulana Azad Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Azad_Library) is one of the oldest and is the largest university library in Asia. [Rampur Raza Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampur_Raza_Library) is a repository of Indo-Islamic cultural heritage established in the last decades of the 18th century.[^cite_note-Library_network-292] It was established in 1774 by nawab [Faizullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faizullah_Khan) and now an autonomous body under the [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(India\)).[^cite_note-public_libraries-293] [Thornhill Mayne Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thornhill_Mayne_Memorial) also known as Allahabad Public Library, has an approximate collection of 125,000 books, 40 types of magazines, and 28 different newspapers in Hindi, English, Urdu and Bangla and it also contains 21 [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) manuscripts.[^cite_note-Thornhill-294] A large number of Indian scholars are educated at different universities in Uttar Pradesh. Notable scholars who were born, worked or studied in the geographic area of the state include [Harivansh Rai Bachchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harivansh_Rai_Bachchan), [Motilal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motilal_Nehru), [Harish Chandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harish_Chandra) and [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi).[^cite_note-scholars-295]

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Uttar_Pradesh)

See also: [Kumbh Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbh_Mela) and [Architecture of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Uttar_Pradesh)

refer caption

[Kumbh Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbh_Mela) at [Sangam, Allahabad, 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triveni_Sangam)

Uttar Pradesh ranks first in domestic tourist arrivals among all states of India.[^cite_note-Board2010-296][^cite_note-Tourism-297] According to the [Ministry of Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism_\(India\)) (Government of India), 1.6 million foreign tourists and approximately 317.91 million domestic tourists visited the state in 2023.[^cite_note-Tourist_Data-298] The Taj Mahal attracts some 7 million people a year, earning almost [₹](490783.md)98 [crore](121420.md) (US\$10 million) in ticket sales in 2023–24.[^cite_note-Taj_Revenue-299] The state is home to three [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites): the [Taj Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_Mahal),[^cite_note-300] [Agra Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra_Fort),[^cite_note-301] and the nearby [Fatehpur Sikri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri).[^cite_note-302]

[Religious tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tourism) plays a significant role in the state's economy. [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) is a major religious hub and one of the seven sacred cities (*[Sapta Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapta_Puri)*) in [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism).[^cite_note-303][^cite_note-304][^cite_note-305] [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan) is considered to be a holy place for [Vaishnavism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism).[^cite_note-Hawley-306][^cite_note-Madan-307] [Sravasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravasti) generally considered as revered sites in [Buddhism](3267529.md), believed to be where the Buddha taught many of his *[Suttas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suttas)* (sermons).[^cite_note-PIB_2019-308] Owing to the belief as to the birthplace of [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama), [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya) (Awadh) has been regarded as one of the seven most important pilgrimage sites.[^cite_note-Paramasivan-309][^cite_note-AboutDistrict-310][^cite_note-311] Millions gather at [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj) to take part in the [Magh Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magh_Mela) festival on the banks of the Ganges.[^cite_note-MacLean2008-312] This festival is organised on a larger scale every 12th year and is called the [Kumbh Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbh_Mela), where over 10 million Hindu pilgrims congregate in one of the largest gatherings of people in the world.[^cite_note-Magh_Mela-313]

refer caption

[Deogarh temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashavatara_Temple,_Deogarh) is an early example of [Nagara-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagara_Style) Hindu architecture

Buddhist attractions in Uttar Pradesh include [stupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupas) and [monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasteries). The historically important towns of [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath) where [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) gave his first sermon after his enlightenment and died at [Kushinagar](33686220.md); both of which are important pilgrimage sites for [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists).[^cite_note-SARNATH-314] Also at Sarnath are the [Pillars of Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Ashoka) and the [Lion Capital of Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Capital_of_Ashoka), both important archaeological artefacts with national significance. At a distance of 80 km (50 miles) from Varanasi, [Ghazipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazipur_district) is famous for its [Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghat) on the Ganges and for the tomb of [Lord Cornwallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Cornwallis), the 18th-century Governor of [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) ruled [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency). The tomb is maintained by the [Archaeological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Survey_of_India).[^cite_note-Joon-315]

[Jhansi Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhansi_Fort), located in the city of [Jhansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhansi), is closely associated with the "First War of Indian Independence", also known as the "Great Rebellion" or the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857).[^cite_note-Jhansi_Fort-316] The fort is constructed in accordance with medieval Indian military architecture, featuring thick walls, bastions, and various structures within its complex. The architecture reflects a blend of Hindu and Islamic styles.[^cite_note-UP_Monuments-317]

## Healthcare



District Hospital, [Kanpur Dehat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur_Dehat_district)

Uttar Pradesh has a mix of [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicly_funded_health_care) as well as private healthcare infrastructure. Public healthcare in Uttar Pradesh is provided through a grid of primary health centres, [community health centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_health_centres), [district hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_hospital), and [medical colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_colleges). Although an extensive network of public and private sector healthcare providers has been built, the available [health infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_India) is inadequate to meet the demand for health services in the state.[^cite_note-318] In 15 years to 2012–13, the population increased by more than 25 per cent. The public health centres, which are the frontline of the government's health care system, decreased by 8 per cent.[^cite_note-health-319]

Smaller sub-centres, the first point of public contact, increased by no more than 2 per cent over the 25 years to 2015, a period when the population grew by more than 51 per cent.[^cite_note-health-319] The state is also facing challenges such as a shortage of healthcare professionals, increasing cost of healthcare, a lack of essential medicines and equipment, the mushrooming of private healthcare and a lack of planning.[^cite_note-WHO-320] The number of doctors registered with State Medical Councils or the [Medical Council of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Council_of_India) in Uttar Pradesh was 77,549.[^cite_note-Doctors-321]

According to the [Ministry of Health and Family Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Family_Welfare)'s *Health Dynamics of India 2022–23* report, Uttar Pradesh had 3,055 rural Primary Health Centres (PHCs), 598 urban PHCs, 939 rural Community Health Centres (CHCs), 11 urban CHCs, and 125 district hospitals. These public health facilities, along with medical colleges, had a combined bed capacity of 77,243, comprising 13,548 beds in PHCs, 26,157 beds in CHCs, 22,812 beds in district hospitals, and 14,726 beds in medical colleges.[^cite_note-Beds-322] The average population served per government hospital stands at 47,782 individuals.[^cite_note-Hospitals-323] As of December 2023, [Out-of-pocket expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-pocket_expense) in Uttar Pradesh is ₹60,883 crore (US\$6.3 billion), highest in India.[^cite_note-OOP-324]

A newborn in Uttar Pradesh is expected to live four years fewer than in the neighbouring state of [Bihar](442482.md), five years fewer than in [Haryana](14189.md) and seven years fewer than in [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md). The state contributed to the largest share of almost all [communicable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contagious_disease) and noncommunicable disease deaths, including 48 per cent of all [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid) deaths (2014); 17 per cent of cancer deaths and 18 per cent of [tuberculosis](30653.md) deaths (2015).[^cite_note-health-319] Its [maternal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_mortality) ratio is higher than the national average at 285 maternal deaths for every 100,000 live births (2021), with 64.2 per cent of pregnant women unable to access minimum [ante-natal care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ante-natal_care).[^cite_note-Maternal-325][^cite_note-NITI2-326][^cite_note-Mortality-327] Around 42 per cent of pregnant women, more than 1.5 million, [deliver babies at home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_birth). About two-thirds (61 per cent) of childbirths at home in the state are unsafe.[^cite_note-Health_and_Family-328] It has the highest [child mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_mortality) indicators,[^cite_note-Child_mortality-329] from the [neonatal mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_mortality_rate) to the under-five mortality rate of 64 children who die per 1,000 live births before five years of age, 35 die within a month of birth, and 50 do not complete a year of life.[^cite_note-Rural-330]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Uttar_Pradesh)

### Language and literature

Main article: [Languages of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Uttar_Pradesh)



The [battle of Kurukshetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kurukshetra), folio from the Mahabharata

Several texts and hymns of the Vedic literature were composed in Uttar Pradesh. Renowned Indian writers who have resided in Uttar Pradesh were [Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabir), [Ravidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravidas), and [Tulsidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsidas), who wrote much of his *Ram Charit Manas* in Varanasi. The festival of *[Guru Purnima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima)* is dedicated to Sage [Vyasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyasa), and also known as *Vyasa Purnima* as it is the day which is believed to be his birthday and also the day he divided the Vedas.[^cite_note-ci-331]

[Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) became the language of state administration with the Uttar Pradesh Official Language Act of 1951.[^cite_note-332] A 1989 amendment to the act added [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), as an additional language of the state.[^cite_note-333] There are numerous language varieties spoken in the state. The north and northwestern parts of the state speak [Khadi Boli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauravi_dialect), the language variety from which Standard Hindi and Urdu developed. In the southwest is spoken [Braj Bhasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj_Bhasha), seen as the language of Krishna and an important literary language for Krishna Bhakti literature. To the east of Braj and Khadi Boli is [Kannauji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannauji_language), a transitional dialect between western and eastern Hindi varieties.[^cite_note-Ethnologue-334][^cite_note-335]

In the southwest of the state, south of the Yamuna, is spoken [Bundeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeli_language). [Awadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadhi_language), the language of the Ram Charit Manas, is spoken in the centre of the state with small pockets of [Bagheli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagheli_language) speakers in the far southeast. East of Awadhi is spoken [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language). Rather than having clean linguistic boundaries, these language varieties form part of one large dialect continuum covering north and east India, Pakistan and Bangladesh. In urban areas, Standard Hindi and Urdu are dominant, with Lucknow being a major centre for Urdu literature.[^cite_note-Ethnologue-334][^cite_note-336]

### Music and dance

Main article: [Music of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Uttar_Pradesh)

refer caption

Richa Jain performing *Chakkarwala tukra*, a [Kathak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathak) rhythmic composition featuring rapid spins (*chakkars*)

With each district of Uttar Pradesh having its unique music and tradition, traditional folk music in Uttar Pradesh has been categorised in three different ways including music transmitted orally, music with unknown composers and music performed by custom. During the [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_India) period, two distinct types of music began to emerge in Uttar Pradesh. One was the courtly music, which received support from cities like Agra, [Fatehpur Sikri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri), Lucknow, Jaunpur, Varanasi, and [Banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda,_Uttar_Pradesh). The other was the religious music stemming from the [Bhakti Cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakti_movement), which thrived in places like Mathura, Vrindavan, and Ayodhya.[^cite_note-Hindustani_Music-337]

The popular folk music of Uttar Pradesh includes sohar, which is sung to celebrate the birth of a child. Evolved into the form of semi-classical singing, [Kajari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajari) sung during the rainy season, and its singing style is closely associated the Benares gharana.[^cite_note-Kajari_Origin-338] [Ghazal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazal), [Thumri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thumri) and [Qawwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali) which is a form of [Sufi poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi_poetry) is popular in the Awadh region, [Rasiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasiya) (especially popular in [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj)), which celebrate the divine love of [Radha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radha) and [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna). [Khayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khayal) is a form of semi-classical singing which comes from the courts of Awadh. Other forms of music are [Biraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biraha), [Chaiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaiti), [Chowtal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowtal), [Alha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alha-Khand), and [Sawani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawani).[^cite_note-Hindustani_Music-337]

[Kathak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathak), a classical dance form, owes its origin to the state of Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-walker100-339] [Ramlila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramlila) is one of the oldest dramatic folk dances; it depicts the life of the Hindu deity [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama) and is performed during festivals such as [Vijayadashami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami).[^cite_note-Schechner_Hess_1977-340] [Nautanki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautanki) is a traditional form of folk theatre that originated in Uttar Pradesh. It typically portrays a variety of themes ranging from historical and mythological tales to social and political commentary.[^cite_note-Nautanki-341] In the [gharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharana) dance form, both the [Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow_gharana) and the [Benares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benares_gharana) gharanas are situated in the state.[^cite_note-342] [Charkula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charkula) is popular dance of the Braj region.[^cite_note-343]

### Fairs and festivals

[Chhath Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhath) is the biggest festival of [eastern Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purvanchal).[^cite_note-344] The [Kumbh Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbh_Mela), organised in the month of [Maagha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maagha) (February—March), is a major festival held every twelve years in rotation at Prayagraj on the river Ganges.[^cite_note-maclean2003-345] [Lathmar Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathmar_Holi) is a local celebration of the Hindu festival of [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi). It takes place well before the actual Holi in the town of [Barsana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barsana) near Mathura.[^cite_note-346] [Taj Mahotsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_Mahotsav), held annually at Agra, is a colourful display of the culture of the Braj area.[^cite_note-Braj_Holi-347] [Ganga Mahotsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev_Deepawali_\(Varanasi\)), a festival of [Kartik Purnima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartik_Purnima), is celebrated fifteen days after Diwali.[^cite_note-traditions-348]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Cuisine of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Uttar_Pradesh)



Uttar Pradeshi thali with [naan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naan), [sultani dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal), [raita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raita), and [shahi paneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahi_paneer)

[Mughlai cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughlai_cuisine) is a style of cooking developed in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) by the [imperial](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/imperial) kitchens of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). It represents the cooking styles used in [North India](1552939.md), especially Uttar Pradesh, and has been strongly influenced by [Central Asian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_cuisine). [Awadhi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadhi_cuisine) from the city of [Lucknow](392095.md) consists of both vegetarian and non-vegetarian dishes. It has been greatly influenced by Mughlai cuisine.[^cite_note-349] [Gulgula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulgula_\(doughnut\)), a traditional sweet made from [jaggery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaggery) and [wheat flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_flour), is a popular dish in [Awadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadh).

[Bhojpuri cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_cuisine) is a style of food preparation common in the districts located near the Bihar border. Bhojpuri foods are mostly mild and tend to be less hot in terms of spices used. The cuisine consists of both vegetable and meat dishes.[^cite_note-Bhojpuri_Cuisine-350]
