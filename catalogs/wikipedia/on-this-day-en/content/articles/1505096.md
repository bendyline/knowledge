# Military prison

Not to be confused with [Prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner-of-war_camp).

A **military prison** is a [prison](19008450.md) operated by a [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military). Military prisons are used variously to house [prisoners of war](25008.md), [unlawful combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlawful_combatants), those whose freedom is deemed a [national security](240468.md) risk by the military or national authorities, and members of the military found guilty of [military offenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_offense). There are two types: penal and confinement-oriented, where captured enemy combatants are confined for military reasons until hostilities cease. Most militaries have some sort of [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police) unit operating at the divisional level or below to perform many of the same functions as civilian police, from traffic-control to the arrest of violent offenders and the supervision of [detainees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detainees) and [prisoners of war](25008.md).

## Australia

The [Australian Defence Force](67538.md) states it has no prisons.[^cite_note-Meechan-1] Instead they have a single facility, the [Defence Force Correctional Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Force_Correctional_Establishment_\(Australia\)), which aims to rehabilitate members who have been sentenced to detention for breaching military regulations or law; employees of the establishment are considered "instructors" rather than guards.[^cite_note-correct_approach-2] Military personnel may be sent there for between 14 days' to two years' rehabilitation before returning to active duty; the average sentence is about 23 days. In addition, there are 15 detention centres located within military bases across Australia.[^cite_note-3]

## Canada

The [Canadian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces) have one military prison, the [Canadian Forces Service Prison and Detention Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces_Service_Prison_and_Detention_Barracks) (CFSPDB) (colloquially known as Club Ed), located at [Canadian Forces Base Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces_Base_Edmonton). Canadian Forces personnel who are convicted by military courts and receive a sentence of 14 days or more are [incarcerated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarcerated) at CFSPDB. Men, although in the same prison, are kept separate from women. The prison is maintained and controlled by the [Canadian Forces Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces_Military_Police), although [NCOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer#Canada) from various branches of the Canadian Forces serve at the prison as staff. Service personnel who are convicted of less serious offences are considered to be in "detention", and undergo a strict military routine aimed at rehabilitation for their return to regular military service, whereas personnel convicted of more serious offences are considered to be in "prison" and upon completion of their sentence they are released from the military. Serious offenders with sentences longer than two years are transferred to the Canadian federal prison system after serving 729 days, to complete their sentence in the civilian prison system, followed by release from the Canadian Forces. Any service personnel serving a sentence of 14 days or less are held in local base Military Police Detachment cells at the various Canadian Forces Bases within Canada.[^cite_note-4]

## Israel

The Israeli military prison of [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek_Prison) (also known as *Prison 10* or *Detention Base 416*) is located near [Kfar Yona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Yona), inside the [Camp Gur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Gur?action=edit\&redlink=1) military complex, and is used for the detention of military personnel who committed crimes during their service. There are also at least two [Incarceration facilities](13588562.md) run by the [Israeli Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defence_Force), (Etzion Facility, near [Gush Etzion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Etzion) and Shomron Facility, in [Samaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria)) used to hold Palestinian prisoners.

## Italy

In Italy the only military prison is the *Carcere Militare Giudiziario* located at the "Ezio Andolfato" barracks in [Santa Maria Capua Vetere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Capua_Vetere), part of the *[Organizzazione Penitenziaria Militare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizzazione%20Penitenziaria%20Militare)*.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] It is tasked with the detention of all military personnel under custody of the Military Justice Authority, either waiting for trial or serving a *[Reclusione Militare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reclusione_Militare)* ("Military detention") term,[^cite_note-7] and of personnel of the civilian police forces under custody of the ordinary civilian justice that opt for the detention in the military prison instead of a civilian one[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## New Zealand

The only military prison in [New Zealand](4913064.md) is the [Services Corrective Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Services_Corrective_Establishment) (SCE) at [Burnham Military Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Military_Camp) near [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch).[^cite_note-HRC_2019/20-10] Prior to 1995, the SCE was based at [Ardmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardmore,_New_Zealand), [Auckland](18660332.md). The prison hosts enlisted personnel who are serving sentences with a maximum of two years. Commissioned officers and enlisted personnel sentenced to a prison term exceeding two years are sent to civilian prisons.[^cite_note-Wilkinson_24_Aug_2025-11] According to lawyer Matthew Hague, the prison's regime consist of a mixture of corrective discipline and rehabilitation.[^cite_note-Wilkinson_24_Aug_2025-11]

Between 2013 and 2018, nearly 140 people were detained at SCE, with an average stay of 14 days. Half of inmates were sent there for drug and alcohol offences, while 20 were sent there for assault-related offences. In August 2025, a [New Zealand Defence Force](21359.md) soldier, who became the first New Zealander convicted of attempted espionage, was sentenced to a prison term of two years at the facility.[^cite_note-Wilkinson_24_Aug_2025-11]

In addition to the Services Corrective Establishment, the NZDF has maintained cell facilities at [RNZAF Base Ohakea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNZAF_Base_Ohakea), [RNZAF Base Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNZAF_Base_Auckland), [HMNZS *Philomel*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Philomel) and [Linton Military Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linton_Military_Camp).[^cite_note-HRC_2019/20-10]

## Portugal

The only military prison in [Portugal](23033.md) is the *[Estabelecimento Prisional Militar](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estabelecimento%20Prisional%20Militar)*, located at [Tomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomar).[^cite_note-12]

## Spain

The only military prison in [Spain](26667.md) is named (*Establecimiento Penitenciario Militar*) and is located at [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares), near [Madrid](41188263.md).[^cite_note-13]

## United Kingdom

The [United Kingdom](31717.md) has one military correctional facility. (It has no establishments that would be considered prisons.) The [Military Corrective Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester_Garrison#MCTC) (colloquially known as the [Glasshouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasshouse_\(British_Army\)) after the former [military prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldershot_military_prison) in [Aldershot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldershot)), in the town of [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester), is where non-commissioned servicemen and women who are convicted by military courts and sentenced to more than 28 days, but less than three years, will be [incarcerated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarcerated). Women and men, although in the same prison, are kept separate from each other. The facility is maintained and controlled by the British Army's [Military Provost Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Provost_Staff) (Adjutant General's Corps). More serious offenders with longer sentences are transferred to [HM Prison Service](429448.md) as part of their [dishonourable discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishonourable_discharge). There are three categories of prisoner:

- Those from the [Royal Navy](26061.md) (RN), [Royal Marines](93985.md) (RM), [British Army](4887.md), and the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) who are to remain in the Services after sentence and will serve their detention in A Company.

- Those from the RN, RM, British Army and RAF who are to be discharged after their sentence and will serve their detention in D Company.

- Those held in Military custody awaiting the outcome of an investigation, or awaiting HM Prison or [YOI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty's_Young_Offender_Institution) placement.

### Organization

Today's American military prison systems are designed to house people who commit a criminal offense while in service.[^cite_note-17] There is a distinction in the male and female prison organization system. Male military prisons have a tier system that is based on the length of a prisoner's sentence. Tier I prisoners have been sentenced up to one year.[^cite_note-Haasenritter2003-18] The [army](32087.md) does not operate any tier I prisons. Tier II prisoners, with sentences of up to seven years, make up 65% of the incarcerated.[^cite_note-Haasenritter2003-18] Men sentenced to more than 10 years, are confined at the [United States Disciplinary Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Disciplinary_Barracks), located on [ Fort Leavenworth, KS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth). This tier system based on sentence length differs from typical American prisons which are characterized by their [level of security](1021698.md). For women this tier system does not exist. Women convicted of felonies are housed at [Naval Consolidated Brig, Miramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Consolidated_Brig,_Miramar) located at the [Marine Corps Air Station Miramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Miramar) near [San Diego, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Composition

Data from the Bureau of Justice Statistics breaks down military prisoners by five different military branches. As of 2020 the confined population by branch was 557 prisoners from the Army, 253 prisoners from the Marine Corps, 156 prisoners from the Navy, 7 prisoners from the Coast Guard, and 227 prisoners from the Air Force.[^cite_note-Carson2020-16] 44 of these prisoners were military officers. A significant number of these prisoners are males, with only 54 being female.[^cite_note-bjs.gov-21] A plurality were Caucasian, followed by African Americans and Hispanics.[^cite_note-bjs.gov-21] Most of the crimes committed by military prisoners are violent offenses, with violent sexual crimes being 41.1% of the crime.[^cite_note-Carson2020-16] The next most frequent crimes committed by military prisoners are drug-related offenses, followed by property offenses, such as theft.[^cite_note-Carson2020-16] There are a small percentage of other crimes committed, such as military offenses.[^cite_note-Carson2020-16] [Military offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCMJ#Punitive_articles) examples are disrespect, [insubordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubordination), and false offense statements. The most recent data from 2020 of military prisoners has shown a small drop from 1214 prisoners in 2019 to 1180 in 2020.[^cite_note-Carson2020-16]

## Incarceration of prisoners-of-war

The [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions) provides an international protocol defining minimum requirements and safeguards for prisoners of war. Prisoners are often kept in *ad hoc* camps near the battlefield, guarded by military police until they can be transferred to more permanent barracks for the duration of the conflict. Treatment has varied from age to age and nation to nation, the quality of conditions for prisoners often being linked with the intensity of the conflict and the resources of the warring parties.

## In popular culture

Military prisons and the treatment of military prisoners have often figured prominently in modern literature, cinema and even politics. In the 19th century, written accounts of the barbaric treatment accorded prisoners on both sides during the Napoleonic and Crimean wars helped lead to the founding of the [Red Cross](15487.md) and the promulgation of the [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions).

There are numerous examples of 20th and 21st-century cinema dealing with military prisons. *[Stalag 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_17)* (1953) portrays the struggles of a group of American airmen in a German [Luftwaffe](17885.md) prison and is based on the play of the same name written by former prisoners of war. *[The Caine Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Caine_Mutiny_\(1954_film\))* (1954) deals with the military legal system during World War II. *[The Great Escape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Escape_\(film\))* (1963) details the true-life adventures of a mixed group of Allied prisoners attempting to escape from a German Luftwaffe [stalag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag). *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(1965_film\))* (1965) was set in a [British](31717.md) military penal camp in [North Africa](21714.md) during [World War II](32927.md). *[The Last Detail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Detail)* (1973) is a film that tells the story of two sailors assigned to a temporary detail transporting a prisoner. *[Andersonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andersonville_\(film\))* (1996) and *[The Andersonville Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Andersonville_Trial)* (1970), both TV movies, dealt with the conditions at [Andersonville Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andersonville_Prison) and its aftermath. *[The Last Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Castle)* (2001) shows a former U.S. Army general who is sent to a military prison after contradicting a direct order. *[Hart's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart's_War)* (2002) features American POWs in a German prison camp.

Some of the late-20th-century military novels of American writer [W. E. B. Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._E._B._Griffin) make mention of the former [Portsmouth Naval Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Prison) facility.
