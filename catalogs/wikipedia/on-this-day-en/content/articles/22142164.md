An annular [solar eclipse](5024887.md) occurred at the Moon's [descending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit on Tuesday, April 29, 2014,[^cite_note-April_29,_2014-1][^cite_note-Browns20140430p21-2] with a [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_of_eclipse) of 0.9868. A [solar eclipse](5024887.md) occurs when the [Moon](19331.md) passes between [Earth](9228.md) and the [Sun](26751.md), thereby totally or partly obscuring the image of the Sun for a viewer on Earth. An annular solar eclipse occurs when the Moon's [apparent diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_diameter) is smaller than the Sun's, blocking most of the Sun's light and causing the Sun to look like an [annulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annulus_\(mathematics\)) (ring). An annular eclipse appears as a partial eclipse over a region of the Earth thousands of kilometres wide. The Moon's apparent diameter was near the average diameter because it occurred 6.2 days after [perigee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on April 23, 2014, at 1\:20 UTC) and 7.2 days before [apogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on May 6, 2014, at 11\:20 UTC).[^cite_note-3]

This eclipse's gamma value was closer to 1 than any other eclipse from 2000 B.C. to 3000 A.D. This means the center of the Moon's shadow passed almost exactly at the surface of the Earth, barely missing the Antarctic continent by a few kilometers, but an annular eclipse was visible from a small part of [Antarctica](18959138.md), and a partial eclipse was visible from parts of [Antarctica](18959138.md) and [Australia](4689264.md).

## Eclipse season

See also: [Eclipse cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_cycle)

This eclipse is part of an [eclipse season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_season), a period, roughly every six months, when eclipses occur. Only two (or occasionally three) eclipse seasons occur each year, and each season lasts about 35 days and repeats just short of six months (173 days) later; thus two full eclipse seasons always occur each year. Either two or three eclipses happen each eclipse season. In the sequence below, each eclipse is separated by a [fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnight).

### Saros 148

This eclipse is a part of [Saros series 148](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Saros_148), repeating every 18 years, 11 days, and containing 75 events. The series started with a partial solar eclipse on September 21, 1653. It contains annular eclipses on [April 29, 2014](22142164.md) and [May 9, 2032](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_May_9,_2032); a hybrid eclipse on [May 20, 2050](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_May_20,_2050); and total eclipses from [May 31, 2068](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_May_31,_2068) through August 3, 2771. The series ends at member 75 as a partial eclipse on December 12, 2987. Its eclipses are tabulated in three columns; every third eclipse in the same column is one [exeligmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeligmos) apart, so they all cast shadows over approximately the same parts of the Earth.&#x20;

The longest duration of annularity will be produced by member 22 at 22 seconds (by default) on [May 9, 2032](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_May_9,_2032), and the longest duration of totality will be produced by member 54 at 5 minutes, 23 seconds on April 26, 2609. All eclipses in this series occur at the Moon’s [descending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit.[^cite_note-6]

### Inex series

This eclipse is a part of the long period [inex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inex) cycle, repeating at alternating nodes, every 358 [synodic months](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synodic_month) (≈ 10,571.95 days, or 29 years minus 20 days). Their appearance and longitude are irregular due to a lack of synchronization with the [anomalistic month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalistic_month) (period of perigee). However, groupings of 3 inex cycles (≈ 87 years minus 2 months) comes close (≈ 1,151.02 anomalistic months), so eclipses are similar in these groupings.
