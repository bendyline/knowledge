**Han Seong-sook** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 한성숙; born 20 June 1967) is a South Korean businesswoman and politician[^cite_note-\:0-1] who has served as the [prime minister of South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_South_Korea) since 2026.[^cite_note-2] She was previously the [chief executive officer](52234.md) (CEO) of [Naver Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naver_Corporation) from 2017 to 2021 and served as the [minister of SMEs and startups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_SMEs_and_Startups) from 2025 to 2026. She is the first woman to hold the position since [Han Myeong-sook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Myeong-sook) in 2006, 20 years prior.

## Early life and education

Han Seong-sook was born on 20 June 1967 in [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md). She received a [Bachelor's degree](188874.md) in English Literature from [Sookmyung Women's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sookmyung_Women's_University) in 1989.[^cite_note-\:2-3]

## Career

### Early career

In 1989, Han began working as a reporter at [Mincom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincom_\(company\)). She later reported for PC Line. In 1995, she led public relations for Nanum Technology.[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:2-3]

### Naver

In 1997, Han was a founding member of [Empas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empas),[^cite_note-\:1-4] one of South Korea's most popular [search engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine) in the early 2000s, and led its search business.[^cite_note-\:3-5] After Empas was sold to [SK Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Communications) in 2007,[^cite_note-6] Han began to serve in executive roles at [Naver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naver) under its original incarnation as [NHN Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHN_Corporation).[^cite_note-\:2-3]

In 2017, Han, a vice president at the time,[^cite_note-7] became CEO of Naver Corporation after [Kim Sang-hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Sang-hun?action=edit\&redlink=1) stepped down from the role. As the company's first female CEO, Han lead the development of [Naver Pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naver_Pay) and [V Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_Live) and Naver's acquisition of [Wattpad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wattpad).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In 2021, Han stepped down as CEO after a Naver employee committed suicide, citing [workplace bullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workplace_bullying) as the motive,[^cite_note-\:3-5][^cite_note-10] and was replaced by [Choi Soo-yeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choi_Soo-yeon_\(executive\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-11] Han later led Naver's expansion in [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-12]

### Appointment

On 7 June 2026, President [Lee Jae Myung](50305655.md) nominated Han to succeed [Kim Min-seok](63697473.md) as [Prime Minister](606354.md).[^cite_note-13] Preparation for her confirmation hearing began on 8 June.[^cite_note-14] The two-day confirmation hearing was held on 25 and 26 June.[^cite_note-15]

On 30 June 2026, Han was confirmed as prime minister by the [National Assembly](601423.md), with 166 votes in favor and one invalid vote. The [People Power Party](63143730.md) boycotted the vote.[^cite_note-16] She became the second female prime minister in South Korean history, following [Han Myeong-sook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Myeong-sook), who served as Prime Minister from 2006 to 2007.[^cite_note-17] She is also the first business executive to assume the position of prime minister.[^cite_note-18] Her term as prime minister began on 1 July 2026, following the approval of National Assembly's appointment by President Lee.[^cite_note-19]

### Tenure

Since taking office on 1 July 2026, Han has focused her tenure on making AI transformation a central government priority, expanding AI-based government systems and increasing the use of public data for AI development. She has also pushed for greater investment in AI and advanced industries, while seeking to remove regulations that could hinder technological innovation.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

Han has additionally prioritized economic stability and inflation control, directing the government to closely monitor prices of essential goods and respond to pressures from high oil prices, a weaker won and rising consumer costs.[^cite_note-22]

## Recognition

From 2017 to 2021, *[Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_\(magazine\))* included Han on its "Most Powerful Women International" list.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-\:3-5] In 2018, Han was listed on *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*' "The Emergent 25 Asia's Latest Star Businesswomen" list.[^cite_note-25]
