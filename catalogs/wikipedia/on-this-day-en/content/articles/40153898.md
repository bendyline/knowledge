The **Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Siły Zbrojne Rzeczypospolitej Polskiej*, pronounced [\[ˈɕiwɨ ˈzbrɔjnɛ ʐɛt͡ʂpɔsˈpɔlitɛj ˈpɔlskʲɛj\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish); abbreviated **SZ RP**), also called the **Polish Armed Forces** (*Wojsko Polskie*, [\[ˈvɔj.skɔ ˈpɔl.skjɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish), WP, lit. 'Polish Military'), are the national [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) of the [Republic of Poland](22936.md). The name has been used since the early 1800s, but can also be applied to [earlier periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).

The [Polish Legions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Legions_in_World_War_I) and the [Blue Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Army_\(Poland\)), composed of Polish volunteers from the [United States](3434750.md) and those who switched sides from the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers), were formed during [World War I](4764461.md). In the war's aftermath, the Polish Army was reformed from the remnants of the partitioning powers' forces and expanded significantly during the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Soviet_War) of 1920. [World War II](32927.md) dramatically impacted Polish military structures, with the initial defeat by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union invasions leading to the dispersion of Polish forces into the [underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Army). After the war, the [Polish People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Army) (LWP) was formed, and its standards aligned with those of the former [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact). The LWP's reputation suffered due to its role in political suppression both domestically and abroad, such as during the [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring). Following the fall of communism, Poland shifted towards Western military standards, joining [NATO](21133.md) in 1999, participating in missions in [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md), and undertaking substantial modernisation of its forces.

The Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland comprise five main service branches: the [Polish Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Land_Forces) (*Wojska Lądowe*), the [Polish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Navy) (*Marynarka Wojenna*), the [Polish Air Force](602835.md) (*Siły Powietrzne*), the [Polish Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Special_Forces) (*Wojska Specjalne*), and the [Polish Territorial Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Defence_Force_\(Poland\)) (*Wojska Obrony Terytorialnej*), under the command of the [Ministry of National Defence of Poland](7425373.md). According to the [Stockholm International Peace Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_International_Peace_Research_Institute), Poland spent \$46.8 billion on its defense budget in 2025, ranking [14th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures) globally in terms of military expenditures.

In 2025, Poland spent the greatest share of its GDP for military expenditures (4.2%) among all NATO members.[^cite_note-5] With over 216,100 active personnel in 2024, the Polish Armed Forces are the third-largest military in NATO, right after [Turkey](30205.md) and the [United States](32212.md).[^cite_note-6]

## Mission



Shoulder sleeve insignia of the Polish [Territorial Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Defence_Force_\(Poland\)) Command

Pursuant to the national security strategy of Poland, the supreme strategic goal of Poland's military forces is to ensure favourable and secure conditions for the realisation of national interests by eliminating external and internal threats, reducing risks, rightly assessing undertaken challenges, and ably using existing opportunities.[^cite_note-7] The Republic of Poland's main strategic goals in the area of defence include:

- Ensuring the independence and sovereignty of the Republic of Poland, as well as its integrity and the inviolability of its borders

- Defence and protection of all the citizens of the Republic of Poland

- Creating conditions to ensure the continuity of the implementation of functions by public administration authorities and other entities competent in the area of national security, including entities responsible for running the economy and for other areas important for the life and security of its citizens

- Creating conditions for the improvement of the state's national defence capabilities and ensuring defence readiness in allied structures

- Developing partnership military cooperation with other states, especially neighbouring ones

- Implementing commitments arising from Poland's [NATO](21133.md) and [European Union](9317.md) membership

- Engaging in international crisis response operations led by NATO, the EU, the UN, and as a part of emergency coalitions

## History

Main article: [History of the Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Polish_Army)

### Origins and establishment

The [List of Polish wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Polish_wars) chronicles Polish military involvement in armed conflicts since the year 972. The present armed forces trace their roots to the early 20th century, yet the history of Polish armed forces in their broadest sense stretches back much further. After the [partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland), during the period from 1795 to 1918, the Polish military was recreated several times during national insurrections that included the [November Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising) of 1830, the [January Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising) in 1863, and the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) that saw the formation of the [Polish Legions in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Legions_in_Italy). [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland), being part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) with a certain degree of autonomy, had a separate Polish army in the years 1815–1830, which was disbanded after the unsuccessful November Uprising. Large numbers of Poles also served in the armies of the partitioning powers, the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), and the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire).

During [World War I](4764461.md), the [Polish Legions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Legions_in_World_War_I) were set up in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)), the southern part of Poland under Austrian occupation. They were both disbanded after the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) failed to provide guarantees of Polish independence after the war. General [Józef Haller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Haller), the commander of the Second Brigade of the Polish Legion, switched sides in late 1917 and, via [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk), took part of his troops to [France](5843419.md), where he created the [Blue Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Army_\(Poland\)). It was joined by several thousand Polish volunteers from the [United States](3434750.md). It fought on the French front in 1917 and 1918.

The Polish Army was recreated in 1918 from elements of the three separate Russian, Austro-Hungarian, and German armies, and armed with equipment left following World War I. The force expanded during the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Soviet_War) of 1919–1922 to nearly 800,000 men, but was then reduced after peace was reestablished.



[President of Poland](43581.md) inspecting troops during the [Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Poland\)) parade in [Warsaw](32908.md), 2007

At the onset of [World War II](32927.md), on 1 September 1939, Nazi Germany invaded Poland. Polish forces were overwhelmed by the [German attack in September 1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), which was followed on 17 September 1939 by [an invasion by the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland). Some Polish forces escaped from the occupied country and joined Allied forces fighting in other theaters, while those that remained in Poland splintered into guerrilla units of the *Armia Krajowa* ("[Home Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Army)") and [other partisan groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) which fought in clandestine ways against the foreign occupiers. Thus, there were three threads to the Polish armed forces from 1939: the [Polish Armed Forces in the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Armed_Forces_in_the_West), the *Armia Krajowa* and other resistance organizations fighting the Germans in Poland, and the [Polish Armed Forces in the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Armed_Forces_in_the_East), which later became the post-war communist [Polish People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Army) (LWP).

Until the [fall of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989), the army's prestige under communist rule continued to fall, as it was used by the government to resettle ethnic minorities immediately after the war ([Operation Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vistula)), and to violently suppress opposition several times, during the [1956 Poznań protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84_1956_protests), the [1970 Polish protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Polish_protests), and during [martial law in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland) in 1981–1983. The LWP also took part in the suppression of the 1968 democratisation process of Czechoslovakia, commonly known as the [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring). That same year, Marshal of Poland Marian Spychalski was asked to replace [Edward Ochab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Ochab) as chairman of the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Council_of_State), and General [Wojciech Jaruzelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Jaruzelski), at that time the [Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_General_Staff), was named to replace him.[^cite_note-8] Jaruzelski, a known Soviet loyalist, was put in place by the Soviets in order to ensure that a trusted group of officers was in control of one of the least trusted armies in the Warsaw Pact.[^cite_note-9]

### Republic of Poland



Polish military interventions since 1990: [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), [Operation Uphold Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uphold_Democracy), [war on terror](13425800.md), and [Operation Serval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Serval)

After January 1990 and the collapse of the communist bloc, the name of the armed forces was changed to "Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland" to accord with the Polish State's new official name. Following the subsequent disbandment of the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact), Poland was admitted into [NATO](21133.md) on 12 March 1999, and the Polish armed forces began a major reorganisation effort in order to conform to the new Western standards.

#### Involvement in Afghanistan (2002-2014)

From 2002 until 2014, Polish military forces were dispatched to the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) mission in [Afghanistan](737.md), led by [NATO](21133.md). Poland's contribution to ISAF was the country's largest since its entrance into NATO. Polish forces also took part in the [Iraq War](5043324.md). From 2003 to 2008, Polish military forces commanded the [Multinational Division Central-South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Division_Central-South) (MND-CS) located in the South-Central Occupation Zone of Iraq. The division consisted of troops from 23 states and totaled as many as 8,500 soldiers.

#### Invasion of Iraq (2003)

See also: [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md)



[GROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GROM) special operations unit secures a section of the port of [Umm Qasr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Qasr) in [Iraq](7515928.md), 2003

In March 2003, the Polish Armed Forces took part in the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), [deploying special forces and a support ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_involvement_in_the_2003_invasion_of_Iraq).[^cite_note-10] Following the destruction of Saddam's regime, the [Polish Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Land_Forces) supplied a brigade and a division headquarters for the 17-nation [Multinational Division Central-South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Division_Central-South), part of the U.S.-led [Multi-National Force – Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Force_%E2%80%93_Iraq). At its peak, Poland had 2,500 soldiers in the south of the country.

#### Peacekeeping missions

Other completed operations include the 2005 'Swift Relief' in Pakistan, in which [NATO Response Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Response_Force)-allocated personnel were dispatched. [Polish Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Land_Forces) personnel sent to Pakistan included a [military engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineering) company, a platoon of the [1st Special Commando Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JW_Komandos%C3%B3w), and a logistics component from the 10th Logistics Brigade. Elsewhere, Polish forces were sent to [MINURCAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_the_Central_African_Republic_and_Chad) in Chad and the [Central African Republic](5478.md) (2007–2010).

As of 2008, Poland had deployed 985 personnel in eight separate UN peacekeeping operations (the [United Nations Disengagement Observer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Disengagement_Observer_Force), [MINURSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_for_the_Referendum_in_Western_Sahara), [MONUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO), [UNOCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire), [UNIFIL](787766.md), [UNMEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea), [UNMIK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Interim_Administration_Mission_in_Kosovo), [UNMIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_Liberia), and [UNOMIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Observer_Mission_in_Georgia)).[^cite_note-11]

#### Fully professional armed forces (2010)



Polish army's [Rosomak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTO_Rosomak) armoured vehicle on patrol in [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni), Afghanistan, 2010

Formerly set up according to [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) standards, the Polish armed forces are now fully organised according to [NATO](21133.md) requirements. Poland is also playing an increasingly larger role as a major European peacekeeping power in the world through various UN peacekeeping actions, and cooperation with neighboring nations through multinational formations and units such as the [Multinational Corps Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Corps_Northeast) and [POLUKRBAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Ukrainian_Peace_Force_Battalion). As of 1 January 2010, the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland have transitioned to a completely contract-based manpower supply system.

On 10 April 2010, a [Polish Air Force Tu-154M crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Polish_Air_Force_Tu-154_crash) near Smolensk, Russia, while it was travelling to a ceremony commemorating the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre). On board the plane were the President (Commander-in-Chief), the Chief of Staff, all four Branch Commanders of the Polish Military, and a number of other military officials; all were killed.

In 2014–2015, the [Armed Forces General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_General_Command_\(Poland\)) and [Armed Forces Operational Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Operational_Command_\(Poland\)) were both established, superseding the previous individual service branch command structures.

#### Homeland Defence Act (2022)

Prompted in part by the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the [Homeland Defence Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Defence_Act) was unanimously passed by the Polish parliament on March 17, 2022, and signed into law by President [Andrzej Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Duda) the following day. In accordance with the act, Poland intends to roughly double the size of the armed forces to 300,000 personnel, and to spend at least 3% of GDP on the defence budget in 2023.[^cite_note-polramp-12] This includes increasing the size of the tank fleet by adding approximately 1,000 new tanks and adding 600 new howitzers to Poland's ground forces.[^cite_note-13] Poland's Deputy Prime Minister and Defense Minister [Mariusz Błaszczak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariusz_B%C5%82aszczak) said that it is Poland's goal to build the most powerful ground forces of all the North Atlantic Treaty Organisation members in Europe.[^cite_note-14]

A [Polish Air Force](602835.md) [F-16](11642.md)C Fighting Falcon during a military exercise, 2017

Since 2011, the Armed Forces have been in the middle of a long-term modernisation program. Plans involve new anti-aircraft missile systems, ballistic missile defense systems, a Lead-In Fighter Trainer (LIFT) aircraft, medium transport and combat helicopters, submarines, unmanned aerial vehicles, as well as self-propelled howitzers. Technical modernisation plans for the years 2013 through to 2022 have been put in place.[^cite_note-\:0-15] During the 2013 to 2016 period of the plan, 37.8 billion PLN, or 27.8% of the period's military budget of 135.5 billion PLN, was invested into technical modernisation.[^cite_note-\:0-15]



ORP *Ślązak* [offshore patrol vessel](536773.md) in the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), 2015

Significant military equipment acquisitions are also planned for through the 2022 period, with the Ministry of Defence outlining 61 billion [złoty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_z%C5%82oty) to be spent on further modernisation. A major feature of the program is the acquisition of around 1,200 unmanned aerial vehicles, including at least 1,000 with combat capabilities.

Additionally, new helicopters and air defence systems are to be procured along with five light vessels for the navy. A new submarine force is to be jointly operated with a NATO partner, and general upgrade and modernisation efforts are aimed at the country's air defenses, naval forces, cyber warfare capabilities, armoured forces, and territorial defence forces (to have 50,000 volunteer members).[^cite_note-DW20161207-16]

### Rise in arms imports



Polish M1 Abrams tanks during the [Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Poland\)) parade in [Warsaw](32908.md), 2023

According to data published by the [Stockholm International Peace Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_International_Peace_Research_Institute) (SIPRI) in 2026, Poland became one of Europe's largest arms importers amid a large-scale military modernisation programme launched following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and largely supported by the EU.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] SIPRI reported that Poland's arms imports increased by 852% between the periods 2016–2020 and 2021–2025, making it the largest arms importer among European members of [NATO](21133.md).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

The increase reflected Poland's accelerated efforts to strengthen the capabilities of the Polish Armed Forces and reinforce NATO's eastern flank.[^cite_note-auto-21] During the period, Poland expanded procurement of tanks, artillery systems, missile launchers and combat aircraft through major defence agreements with the [United States](3434750.md) and [South Korea](27019.md), including acquisitions of [M1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_Abrams) and [K2 Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K2_Black_Panther) tanks, [K9 Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K9_Thunder) self-propelled howitzers, [M142 HIMARS](147011.md) rocket systems and [F-35 Lightning II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35_Lightning_II) fighter aircraft.[^cite_note-auto-21]

## Traditions

See also: [Polish Armed Forces rank insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Armed_Forces_rank_insignia), [Polish military awards and decorations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_military_awards_and_decorations), and [Polish military eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_military_eagle)



[Honour Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Honour_Guard_Regiment_of_the_Polish_Armed_Forces?action=edit\&redlink=1) at the [Tomb of the Unknown Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier_\(Warsaw\)) in Warsaw's [Piłsudski Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi%C5%82sudski_Square)

The Polish armed forces have consistently held two yearly military parades ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Defilada wojskowa*) on [Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Poland\)) and [National Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Independence_Day_\(Poland\)). These parades take place on [Ujazdów Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujazd%C3%B3w_Avenue) and near the [Tomb of the Unknown Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier,_Warsaw) on [Piłsudski Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi%C5%82sudski_Square), respectively. The Armed Forces Day Parade was introduced in 2007 and 2008 as the first grand military parades since the holiday was reinstated and has been held yearly since 2013. The first Polish military parade took place on 17 January 1945, and as of 2019, the [3 May Constitution Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_May_Constitution_Day) parade was officially reinstated.

*[Marsz Generalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsz_Generalski)* and *[Warszawianka (1831)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warszawianka_\(1831\))* are the main military musical pieces performed at ceremonial events. While the former is a solemn march used during inspections and the march on of the Polish flag, the latter is a march strictly used for marches pasts, [military parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_parades), and other processions.

The Polish Armed Forces are the only military entity in the world to use a [two-finger salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-finger_salute), which is only used while wearing a hat (it refers to the fact that the salute is given to the emblem itself) with the emblem of the Polish eagle, such as military hat, [rogatywka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogatywka). The salute is performed with the middle and index fingers extended and touching each other, while the ring and little fingers are bent and touched by the thumb. The tips of the middle and index fingers touch the peak of the cap, two fingers supposedly meaning Honour and Fatherland (Honor i Ojczyzna).

*Czołem Żołnierze* (the [Polish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) version of *Greetings Soldiers*) is the official military greeting of the armed forces, usually given by the members of the government or military establishment, as well as visiting dignitaries during ceremonial occasions. The soldiers will usually respond with *Czołem (States title/rank of dignitary[^cite_note-28])*.
