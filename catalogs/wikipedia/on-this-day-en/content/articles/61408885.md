**Antifa** (German pronunciation: [\[ˈantifa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/eb/De-Antifa.ogg/De-Antifa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/eb/De-Antifa.ogg/De-Antifa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [political movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_movement) in Germany composed of multiple [militant](310790.md) groups and individuals on the [political left](18499.md), who describe themselves as [anti-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascist); its primary activity has been combating [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism): initially [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism), and later [neo-Nazism](54361.md). The antifa movement in Germany has existed in different eras and incarnations, dating back to *[Antifaschistische Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifaschistische_Aktion)*, from which the moniker *antifa* came.

*Antifaschistische Aktion* was set up by the then-[Stalinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist) [Communist Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany) (KPD) during the late history of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic). After the forced dissolution in the wake of *[Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung)* ('power seizure') in 1933, the movement went underground.[^cite_note-ddr-biografien_9589137ed36-1] In the postwar era, *Antifaschistische Aktion* inspired a variety of different autonomous movements, groups and individuals in Germany as well as other countries which widely adopted variants of its aesthetics and some of its tactics.&#x20;

Known as the [wider antifa movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93World_War_II_anti-fascism), the contemporary antifa groups have no direct organisational connection to *Antifaschistische Aktion*.[^cite_note-Grunenberg-2] The contemporary antifa movement has its roots in the West German *[Außerparlamentarische Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Au%C3%9Ferparlamentarische_Opposition)* [left-wing](18499.md) [student movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_German_student_movement) and largely adopted the aesthetics of the first movement while being ideologically somewhat dissimilar. The first antifa groups in this tradition were founded by the [Maoist](46636.md) [Communist League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_League_\(West_Germany\)) in the early 1970s. From the late 1980s, West Germany's [squatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatter) scene and left-wing [autonomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism) movement were the main contributors to the new antifa movement and in contrast to the earlier movement had a more [anarcho-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-communist) leaning. The contemporary movement has splintered into different groups and factions, including one [anti-imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-imperialist) and [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionist) faction and one [anti-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Germans_\(political_current\)) faction who strongly oppose each other, mainly over their views on [Israel](9282173.md).

[German government](1327803.md) institutions such as the [Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Office_for_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution) and the [Federal Agency for Civic Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Agency_for_Civic_Education) describe the contemporary antifa movement as part of the extreme left and as partially violent. Antifa groups are monitored by the federal office in the context of its legal mandate to combat [extremism](428002.md).[^cite_note-bpb-3][^cite_note-GermanGovernment-4][^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5][^cite_note-Verfassungsschutz2-6] The federal office states that the underlying goal of the antifa movement is "the struggle against the [liberal democratic basic order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democratic_basic_order)" and capitalism.[^cite_note-GermanGovernment-4][^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5] In the 1980s, the movement was accused by German authorities of engaging in [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) acts of violence.[^cite_note-Schöppner-7] According to the these agencies, the use of the epithet *[fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_\(insult\))* against opponents and the view of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) as a form of fascism are central to the movement.[^cite_note-bpb-3][^cite_note-GermanGovernment-4][^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5]

## *Antifaschistische Aktion*

Main article: [Antifaschistische Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifaschistische_Aktion)



The [Karl-Liebknecht-Haus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Liebknecht-Haus) in 1932, the historical seat of the Communist Party of Germany and *Antifaschistische Aktion*, with prominently displayed its logo

*Antifaschistische Aktion* was established by the [Communist Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany) (KPD) based on the principle of a [communist front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_front) and its establishment was announced in the party's newspaper *[Die Rote Fahne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Rote_Fahne)* (*The Red Flag*) in 1932. It functioned as an integral part of the KPD during its entire existence from 1932 to 1933.[^cite_note-Moreau-8] A member of the [Comintern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comintern), the KPD under the leadership of [Ernst Thälmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Th%C3%A4lmann) was loyal to the Soviet government headed by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) to the extent that the party had been directly controlled and funded by the Soviet leadership in Moscow since 1928.[^cite_note-Hoppe-9][^cite_note-Moreau-8] The KPD described *Antifaschistische Aktion* as a "red [united front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_front) under the leadership of the only anti-fascist party, the KPD".[^cite_note-10]

During the Comintern's [Third Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Period) (1928–1931), the [Social Democratic Party of Germany](60585.md) (SPD) was included by the KPD in the category of "fascists"[^cite_note-Draper-11] based on the theory of "[social fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_fascism)" supported by the Comintern in the early 1930s, according to which social democracy was a variant of fascism and even more dangerous and insidious than open fascism.[^cite_note-Moreau-8] The KPD doctrine held that the communist party was "the only anti-fascist party" while all other parties were "fascist".[^cite_note-Bois-12][^cite_note-Hoppe-9]

The KPD in the Third Period did not view [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) as a specific political movement, but primarily as the final stage of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and the KPD's anti-fascism was therefore synonymous with [anti-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalism).[^cite_note-Langer-13] Throughout this period, the KPD regarded the centre-left SPD as its main adversary.[^cite_note-Moreau-8][^cite_note-newstatesman-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Langer-13]

Consequently, *anti-fascism* and *anti-fascist action* in the language of the Third Period KPD also included the struggle against the social democrats.[^cite_note-Moreau-8] In the early 1930s, the KPD had stated that "fighting fascism means fighting the SPD just as much as it means fighting [Hitler](2731583.md) and the parties of [Brüning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Br%C3%BCning)".[^cite_note-Bois-12] While some KPD members initially believed *Antifaschistische Aktion* should include other leftists, this opinion was quickly suppressed by the KPD leadership which made it clear that *Antifaschistische Aktion* would also oppose the SPD and that "Anti-Fascist Action means untiring daily exposure of the shameless, treacherous role of the SPD and [ADGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_German_Trade_Union_Federation) leaders who are the direct filthy helpers of fascism".[^cite_note-TheLeftBerlin-16]

Occasionally between late 1931 and late 1932, the KPD cooperated with the Nazis in attacking the SPD.[^cite_note-TheLeftBerlin-16] In 1931, the KPD under the leadership of Ernst Thälmann internally used the slogan "[After Hitler, our turn!](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Ernst%20Th%C3%A4lmann)", strongly believing that a united front against Nazis was not needed and that a Nazi dictatorship would ultimately crumble due to flawed economic policies and lead the KPD to power in Germany when the people realized that their economic policies were superior.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The relationship between the KPD and the SPD was characterised by mutual hostility. The SPD had itself adopted the position that both the Nazis and the KPD posed an equal danger to liberal democracy[^cite_note-19] and SPD leader [Kurt Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Schumacher) famously described the KPD as "red-painted Nazis" in 1930.[^cite_note-Richter-20] The SPD-dominated [Reichsbanner Schwarz-Rot-Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsbanner_Schwarz-Rot-Gold) described itself as a "protection organization of the Republic and democracy in the fight against the swastika and the Soviet star" and both the Reichsbanner and the [Iron Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Front) opposed both the Nazis and the anti-fascist KPD.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Potthoff-22]

In 1929, the KPD's paramilitary organisation, *[Roter Frontkämpferbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roter_Frontk%C3%A4mpferbund)* (Alliance of Red Front-Fighters), an effective predecessor of *Antifaschistische Aktion*, had been banned as extremist by the governing SPD.[^cite_note-23] In December 1929, the KPD founded *Antifaschistische Junge Garde* as a successor to *Roter Frontkämpferbund*, which was banned.[^cite_note-24]

Despite this animosity between party leaderships, on the ground there was considerable co-operation against the Nazis between rank and file activists of the KPD, SPD and other left groups such as in local anti-fascist committees and militias, particularly in 1932 as the fascists gained ground and calls for a [united front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_front) by [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky), [August Thalheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Thalheimer) and other left leaders became more urgent.[^cite_note-Bois-12] It was in this context that the KPD began to emphasise the specific threat of Nazism, leading to the formation of *Antifaschistische Aktion* and later the turn away from the "social fascism" doctrine.&#x20;



The congress organised by the KPD in 1932, with *Antifaschistische Aktion*' logo flanked by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) banners (centre), imagery showing the KPD fighting capitalism (right) and imagery mocking the centre-left [SPD](60585.md) (left), regarded by the KPD as *[social fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_fascists)*

&#x20;The 1932 congress organised by KPD dedicated energy to attacking the SPD. It featured a large *Antifaschistische Aktion* logo flanked by imagery that showed the KPD fighting the capitalists next to imagery openly mocking the SPD.[^cite_note-25] The two-flag logo, designed by [Association of Revolutionary Visual Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Revolutionary_Visual_Artists) members [Max Gebhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Gebhard?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Max Keilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Keilson?action=edit\&redlink=1), remains a widely used symbol of militant anti-fascism.[^cite_note-Balhorn-26]

How many people belonged to *Antifaschistische Aktion* is difficult to determine because there were no membership cards. Rather, it developed out of the practical participation.[^cite_note-27] The Red Mass Self-Defence (RMSS) units were absorbed into *Antifaschistische Aktion*, forming the nuclei of the latter's Unity Committees, organised on a micro-local basis, e.g. in apartment buildings, factories or allotments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenhaft198397–98-28]

As well as being involved in political streetfights, the RMSS and *Antifaschistische Aktion* used their militant approach to develop a comprehensive network of self-defence for communities targeted by the Nazis such as in "tenant protection" (*Mieterschutz*), action against evictions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenhaft198354,_98-29] Initially, the RMSS units had minimal formal membership, but in the second half of 1932 local executive boards were created to co-ordinate the activities of the KPD, the Kampfbund, the RMSS and the now illegal [Roter Frontkämpferbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roter_Frontk%C3%A4mpferbund), with the RMSS given a more distinct and almost paramilitary defence role, often co-operating on an ad hoc basis with the Reichsbanner.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenhaft198398-30] At a local level, *Antifaschistische Aktion* worked with other left groups, including joint assemblies and demonstrations with SPD and other socialist groups, but the national KPD leadership continued to describe social democracy as the "main enemy".[^cite_note-FoDEx-31][^cite_note-32]

After the forced dissolution in the wake of the [Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung) in 1933, the movement went underground.[^cite_note-ddr-biografien_9589137ed36-1] [Theodore Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Draper) argued that "the so-called theory of social fascism and the practice based on it constituted one of the chief factors contributing to the victory of German fascism in January 1933".[^cite_note-Draper-11][^cite_note-newstatesman-14]

## Post-war committees

After the defeat of [Nazi Germany](21212.md), groups called *Antifaschistische Aktion*, *Antifaschistische Ausschüsse*, or *Antifaschistische Kommittees*, all typically abbreviated to *[antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-World_War_II_anti-fascism)*, spontaneously re-emerged in Germany in 1944, mainly involving veterans of pre-war [KPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPD), [KPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany_\(Opposition\)) and [SPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPD) politics[^cite_note-auto-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] as well as some members of other democratic political parties and [Christians who opposed the Nazi régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessing_Church).[^cite_note-Pritchard2012-37] Communists tended to make up at least half of the committees.[^cite_note-Pritchard2012-37]

In the western zones, these anti-fascist committees began to recede by the late summer of 1945, marginalized by [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) bans on political organization and by re-emerging divisions between [communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communists) and others and the emerging state doctrine of [anti-communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism) in what became [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany).[^cite_note-38] In [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), the antifa groups were absorbed into the new [Stalinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist) state.[^cite_note-auto-33]

## Cold War

### East Germany



The [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall) was officially referred to as the Anti-Fascist Protection Wall by [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany).

In the [Soviet occupation zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_zone) which later became East Germany, the Soviet occupation authorities pressured the KPD and the remaining [Social Democratic Party of Germany](60585.md) (SPD) to merge into the [Socialist Unity Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Unity_Party_of_Germany) (SED) while those within the SPD who resisted the [Stalinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinization) were persecuted and often fled to the western zones.[^cite_note-Malycha-39] The repression in the Soviet occupation zone and the onset of the [Cold War](325329.md) quickly exacerbated the conflict between the SED and the SPD. The term *anti-fascism* was widely used by [Marxist–Leninists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist%E2%80%93Leninists) to smear their opponents, including [democratic socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialists), [social democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democrats) and other [anti-Stalinist leftists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Stalinist_left).[^cite_note-Malycha-39]

Anti-fascism was part of the official ideology and language of the [communist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state)[^cite_note-bpb-3] and *[Antifaschistische Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifaschistische_Aktion)* was considered an important part of the heritage of the governing SED along with the KPD itself. [Eckhard Jesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckhard_Jesse) notes that *anti-fascism* was ubiquitous in the language of the SED and used to justify repression such as the crackdown on the [East German uprising of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German_uprising_of_1953).[^cite_note-Jesse-40][^cite_note-Agethen-41] *Anti-fascism* generally meant the struggle against the [Western world](21208200.md) and [NATO](21133.md) in general and against the western-backed [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) and its main ally the United States in particular which were seen as the main fascist forces in the world by the SED.[^cite_note-Richter-20] From 1961 to 1989, the SED used Anti-Fascist Protection Wall ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Antifaschistischer Schutzwall*) as the official name for the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall). This was in sharp contrast to the [West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Berlin) city government which would sometimes refer to the same structure as the [Wall of Shame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_of_Shame).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionist) struggle was seen as an important part of the anti-fascist struggle and [Israel](9282173.md) was regarded by East Germany as a "fascist state"[^cite_note-44] alongside the United States and West Germany. Anti-fascism as interpreted by East Germany served as a "legitimizing ideology" and "state doctrine" of the regime.[^cite_note-bpb-3][^cite_note-Grunenberg-2][^cite_note-Agethen-41] When the regime crumbled during the [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989), the SED intensified its use of anti-fascist rhetoric directed at the West to justify its existence.[^cite_note-Jesse-40][^cite_note-Agethen-41]

### West Germany



The contemporary antifa logo, based on the logo of *Antifaschistische Aktion* including a red flag representing communism and socialism and a black flag representing [anarchism](12.md) and [autonomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism) (added in the 1980s)

See also: [Autonomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomen)

The contemporary antifa movement has its origins in West Germany, in the student-based *[Außerparlamentarische Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Au%C3%9Ferparlamentarische_Opposition)* (extra-parliamentary opposition) of the 1960s and early 1970s which opposed the alleged "fascism" of the West German government.[^cite_note-Grunenberg-2] Major factors that formed the backdrop of this movement were criticism of the [Vietnam War](32611.md) and the [United States](3434750.md), students' anti-authoritarian rebellion against their parents' generation, criticism of professors' dominance of universities and continuity of the societal relations of power, especially the continuity in the civil service since the Nazi era, and the criticism of the centre-left [SPD](60585.md) by those to the left of the SPD.[^cite_note-45]

The earliest contemporary antifa groups that were inspired by the left-wing student movement were founded by the [Maoist](46636.md) [Communist League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_League_\(West_Germany\)) in the early 1970s. During the 1970s, parts of the *Außerparlamentarische Opposition* were [radicalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalized), culminating in the formation of militant groups like the [Red Army Faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_Faction), the [2 June Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_June_Movement) and the [Revolutionary Cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Cells_\(German_group\)).[^cite_note-46] Some of the more radical elements within antifa groups of the late 1970s had contact with the Red Army Faction and the Revolutionary Cells.[^cite_note-47] From the late 1980s, the [squatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatter) scene and [autonomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism) movement were important in an upswing of the antifa movement.[^cite_note-auto-33]

## Contemporary groups



An antifa protester in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), 2008

The contemporary antifa movement in Germany comprises different [anti-fascist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascist_groups) which usually use the abbreviation *[antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-World_War_II_anti-fascism)* and regard *[Antifaschistische Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifaschistische_Aktion)* of the early 1930s as an inspiration. Contemporary antifa "has no practical historical connection to the movement from which it takes its name, but is instead a product of West Germany's squatter scene and autonomist movement in the 1980s".[^cite_note-auto-33] Many new antifa groups formed from the late 1980s onwards. One of the biggest antifascist campaigns in Germany in recent years was the ultimately successful effort to block the annual [Neo-Nazi marches in Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Nazi_marches_in_Dresden) which had grown into "Europe's biggest gathering of Nazis".[^cite_note-48]

Unlike *Antifaschistische Aktion* which had links to the [Communist Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany) and which was concerned with industrial working-class politics, the late 1980s and early 1990s autonomists were instead independent anti-authoritarian [libertarian Marxists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Marxists) and [anarcho-communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-communists) not associated with any particular party. The publication *[Antifaschistisches Infoblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifaschistisches_Infoblatt)*, in operation since 1987, sought to expose [radical nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_nationalists) publicly.[^cite_note-Bray-49]



A demonstration by antifa on [May Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day) 2014 in Berlin in which *[Bereitschaftspolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereitschaftspolizei)* are in the foreground

Most contemporary antifa groups were formed after the [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990, mainly in the early part of the 1990s. In 1990, *Autonome Antifa (M)* was established in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ttingen). *Antifaschistische Aktion Berlin*, founded in 1993, became one of the more prominent groups. *[Antifaschistische Aktion/Bundesweite Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifaschistische_Aktion/Bundesweite_Organisation?action=edit\&redlink=1)* was an umbrella organisation at the federal level that coordinated these groups across Germany. Aside from their violent clashes with [ultra-nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-nationalists), these groups participated in the annual [May Day in Kreuzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day_in_Kreuzberg) which resulted in large-scale riots in 1987 and which have been characterized by a significant police presence.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] In 2003, *Antifaschistisches Infoblatt* joined [*Antifa-Net*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifa-Net?action=edit\&redlink=1), part of an international network, including the likes of Britain's *[Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Searchlight_\(magazine\))* and Sweden's *[Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_\(magazine\))* magazine.[^cite_note-52]

[Steffen Kailitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffen_Kailitz) asserted in 2013 that "the difference between the autonomist scene and terrorist networks gradually lost importance from the 1990s" and that a number of antifa groups were involved in violent activities from the 1990s.[^cite_note-53] In October 2016, antifa in Dresden campaigned on the occasion of the anniversary of the reunification of Germany on 3 October for "turning Unity celebrations into a disaster" to protest this display of new German nationalism whilst explicitly not ruling out the use of violence.[^cite_note-54] Antifa protesters were involved during the [2017 G20 Hamburg summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_G20_Hamburg_summit) confrontations.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

### Main factions and ideology



Protesters belonging to the [Anti-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Germans_\(political_current\)) wing of antifa, carrying the slogan "Down with Germany/Solidarity with Israel/For Communism" with an antifa logo

After [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), the antifa movement gradually fractured into three main camps:[^cite_note-57]

- Anti-imperialists, the largest group, who take an [anti-imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-imperialist) position and most closely adhere to the traditional position taken within the movement and communist parties generally, tending to view politics in terms of how a country relates to the West and seeing [anti-Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) as part of the anti-fascist struggle.

- [Anti-Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Germans_\(political_current\)), who emphasize their opposition to Germany as a country, [support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) [Israel](9282173.md) and oppose the anti-imperialist antifa and the mainstream left.

- Those who have no position on Israel or who see it as irrelevant to questions of contemporary anti-fascism in Germany.

Diverging opinions on Israel has caused a split in the movement since the 2000s.[^cite_note-Peters-58] The Antifaschistische Aktion/Bundesweite Organisation dissolved in 2001 and it splintered into different groups and factions as a result of these political differences.

Writing in 1993, political scientist [Antonia Grunenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Grunenberg) described "anti-fascism" as a "strange term, that expresses opposition to something, but no political concept" and points out that while all democrats are against fascism, not everyone who is against fascism is a democrat. In this sense, Grunenberg argues that the term obscures the difference between democrats and non-democrats.[^cite_note-Grunenberg-2]

In German, the terms *antifa* and *anti-fascism* are often used interchangeably.[^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5] According to the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution, many contemporary antifa groups include their understanding of various forms of oppression or general and loosely defined topics such as homophobia, racism, sexism and war in their understanding of fascism. Frequently, corporate interests, the government and especially the police and military are also included in their understanding of fascism.[^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5] According to political scientist and [CDU](60584.md) politician [Tim Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Peters_\(political_scientist\)), writing in 2007, usage of the term *anti-fascism* in contemporary Germany is mainly limited to the far-left while the term and ideology are viewed critically by many.[^cite_note-Peters-58]

### Symbolism



Antifa protesters with a banner reading: "Red & Anarchist Skinheads Hannover; as long as you don't resist you're supporting the system"

Many contemporary antifa groups have adopted variants of the aesthetics of *Antifaschistische Aktion*. Its two-flag logo was originally designed by [Max Gebhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Gebhard?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Max Keilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Keilson?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the KPD-associated [Association of Revolutionary Visual Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Revolutionary_Visual_Artists).[^cite_note-59] While the original logo of *Antifaschistische Aktion* featured two [red flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_\(politics\)) representing [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) and [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), contemporary antifa logos since the 1980s usually feature a [black flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_flag_\(anarchism\)) representing [anarchism](12.md) and [autonomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism), in addition to the red flag.[^cite_note-Bray-49]

### Government and police monitoring and prosecution



Antifa protest in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden), 15 February 2020

[German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Germany) institutions such as the [Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Office_for_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution) and the [Federal Agency for Civic Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Agency_for_Civic_Education) describe the contemporary antifa movement as part of the [extreme left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_left) and antifa groups are monitored by the federal office in the context of its legal mandate to combat [extremism](428002.md) under the provisions allowed for by the German system of a *[Streitbare Demokratie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streitbare_Demokratie)* ("fortified democracy").[^cite_note-bpb-3][^cite_note-GermanGovernment-4][^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5][^cite_note-Verfassungsschutz2-6]



Antifa [black block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_block) in Leipzig, 17 June 2020

The Federal Agency for Civic Education claims that antifa groups sometimes call for violence not only against police or [skinheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinhead) but also against bishops and judges. According to the agency, there are slogans such as "antifascism means attack" not only against the far-right but also against the political system of the Federal Republic of Germany.[^cite_note-bpb-3] Writing for the Federal Agency for Civic Education, extremism expert [Armin Pfahl-Traughber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Pfahl-Traughber), a former director with the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution, notes that "even if every convinced democrat is an opponent of fascism, *anti-fascism* is not per se a democratic position". According to Pfahl-Traughber, one must distinguish between "fascism in a scholarly sense" and "fascism in a far-left extremist sense".[^cite_note-bpb-3]



Antifa black block in [Frankfurt](10992.md), 14 November 2020

The Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution describes the field of "anti-fascism" or "Antifa" as extremist[^cite_note-GermanGovernment2-5] and includes it and associated groups in its [annual public reports on extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Report_on_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution) as part of the topic "far-left extremism".[^cite_note-Verfassungsschutz2-6] The federal office further says that "\[t]he field of 'anti-fascism' has for years been a central element of the political activity of far-left extremists, especially violent ones. \[...] Far-left extremists within this tradition only superficially claim to fight far-right activities. In reality the focus is the struggle against the liberal democratic basic order, which is smeared as a 'capitalist system' with 'fascist' roots".[^cite_note-GermanGovernment-4]



Antifa demonstration in support of [Liebig 34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebig_34)

The contemporary antifa or anti-fascist movement in the Federal Republic of Germany has been mentioned in the [Annual Report on the Protection of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Report_on_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution) since 1986 as part of the main chapter on "far-left extremism" and was described as a group engaged in [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) acts of violence.[^cite_note-Schöppner-7] In 1995, public prosecutors in [Lower Saxony](18435.md) [charged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_charge) 17 members of antifa with belonging to a [criminal organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_organization) ("Antifa") and with supporting terrorism as part of a sweeping investigation into antifa by Lower Saxon police and security agencies known as the anti-antifa investigation that started in 1991 until the case was dropped in 1996.[^cite_note-60] A report by the German [Bundestag](3768.md) from 2018 determined that due to the lack of a formal organizational structure or leadership, it is only possible to prosecute members of antifa on terrorism charges in individual cases.[^cite_note-61]

According to the 2018 Annual Report on the Protection of the Constitution, antifa's actions against [right-wing extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_extremists) included arson, the outing of personal information, vandalism and more rarely causing personal injuries.[^cite_note-62] In 2020, *[Die Welt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Welt)* reported that at least 47 organised antifa groups are monitored by German federal and state offices for the protection of the constitution and labelled as "extremist". However, not all monitored groups are mentioned in the federal or state annual reports on the protection of the constitution and the list is therefore not exhaustive.[^cite_note-63]
