**Aarhus Municipality** ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Aarhus Kommune*), known as **Århus Municipality** ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Århus Kommune*) until 2011, is a *[kommune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Denmark)* in the [Central Denmark Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Denmark_Region), on the east coast of the [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) peninsula in central [Denmark](76972.md).

The municipality covers an area of 469.56 km<sup>2</sup> (181.30 sq mi), and has a population of 378,361 as of 2026.[^cite_note-BY2-1] The main town and the site of its [municipal council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council) is the city of [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus).[^cite_note-Århus-2] Neighbouring municipalities are [Syddjurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syddjurs_Municipality) to the north, [Favrskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favrskov_Municipality) to the northwest, [Skanderborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderborg_Municipality) to the southwest, and [Odder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odder_Municipality) to the south.

Aarhus Municipality was not merged with other municipalities in the nationwide [*Kommunalreformen* ("The Municipal Reform" of 2007)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Denmark#Municipal_Reform_2007) due to its already relatively large size and population.

The municipality is part of [Business Region Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Region_Aarhus) and of the [East Jutland metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jutland_metropolitan_area), which had a total population of 1.378 million in 2016.[^cite_note-Business_Region_Aarhus-3][^cite_note-East_Jutland-4]

## Politics

Aarhus City Council (*Aarhus Byråd*) is also the municipal government. The city council consists of 31 members elected for four-year terms. Administratively the municipality is divided into six minor agencies which together constitute the [magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate) led by a mayor and five aldermen as administrative directors. It is the only Danish city with a magistrate structure.[^cite_note-5] The six departments of the city are the *Mayor's Department*, *Social Affairs and Employment*, *Technical Services and Environment*, *Health and Care*, *Culture and Citizens Services* and *Children and Young People* which handle all the day-to-day operations of the city.[^cite_note-6]

The current [mayor](101146.md) of Aarhus municipality is [Jacob Bundsgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Bundsgaard) of the [Social Democrats](403131.md),[^cite_note-7] who took over after [Nicolai Wammen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolai_Wammen) in August 2011,[^cite_note-8] with Wammen wanting to focus on the upcoming election for Parliament.

Politically Aarhus leans [left](18499.md) and the largest political party has historically been the [Social Democratic Party](403131.md) which has also held the mayor's office for all terms with the notable exception of the 2001–2005 term when Louise Gade of [Venstre](403109.md) became both the first female and non-Social Democratic mayor. The gender composition of the city council has risen steadily in favour of women through the 20th century and currently 9 of 31 members are women.[^cite_note-9]

The first publicly elected mayor of Aarhus was appointed in 1919. In the [1970 Danish Municipal Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Danish_Municipal_Reform) the current Aarhus municipality was created by merging 20 municipalities.[^cite_note-10] Aarhus (from 1948 to 2011 spelled Århus) was the seat of [Århus County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85rhus_County) (spelled Århus all the years it existed) until the 2007 Danish municipal reform, which substituted the Danish counties with five regions and made the former Århus County a part of [Central Denmark Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Denmark_Region) (*Region Midtjylland*), seated in [Viborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viborg,_Denmark).[^cite_note-11]

### Districts and subdivisions



[Locator map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locator_map) of districts in [Aarhus municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_municipality) and neighbourhoods in *Midtbyen*

See also: [Administrative divisions of Aarhus Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Aarhus_Municipality)

Aarhus Municipality has 45 [electoral wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_wards) and [polling stations](763947.md) in four [electoral districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_districts) for the [folketing](429743.md).[^cite_note-15] The [diocese of Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Aarhus) has four [deaneries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deanery) composed of 60 [parishes](23623.md) within Aarhus municipality.[^cite_note-16] Aarhus municipality contains 21 [postal districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_code) and some parts of another 9.[^cite_note-17] The urban area of Aarhus and the immediate suburbs are divided into the districts [Aarhus C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_C), [Aarhus N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_N), [Aarhus V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_V), [Viby J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viby_J), [Højbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8jbjerg) and [Brabrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabrand).

Aarhus is segmented into districts on different levels often containing several distinct neighbourhoods within them. The historical centre, known as *Indre By* (lit. [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), "Inner City"), includes the neighbourhoods *Centralværkstedet*, the [Latin Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinerkvarteret,_Aarhus) and the areas around the [Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_Central_Station) and [Aarhus Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_Concert_Hall).[^cite_note-18] The inner city beltway [Ring 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_1_\(Aarhus\)) contains the larger district [Midtbyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtbyen,_Aarhus) (lit. Town Centre), roughly equivalent to postal district Aarhus C, and is composed of the neighbourhoods Indre By, [Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Aarhus), [Frederiksbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksbjerg), [Aarhus Ø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_Docklands), [Marselisborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marselisborg) and [Nørre Stenbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rre_Stenbro).[^cite_note-19]

North of the inner beltway lies the district Aarhus N, containing the neighbourhoods [Trøjborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%B8jborg), [Risskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risskov), [Vejlby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vejlby), [Skejby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skejby), [Christiansbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_N#Christiansbjerg) and Vorrevangen and to the west lies [Langenæs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langen%C3%A6s), [Åbyhøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85byh%C3%B8j) and Aarhus V, comprising the neighbourhoods [Hasle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasle,_Aarhus), [Frydenlund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frydenlund), Herredsvang and Møllevangen, while the district of [Højbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8jbjerg) lies to the south. Districts and suburbs further out are [Brabrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabrand), [Egå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eg%C3%A5), [Gellerup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gellerup), [Hasselager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasselager), [Holme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holme,_Aarhus), [Kolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolt,_Denmark), [Rosenhøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenh%C3%B8j), [Skødstrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%B8dstrup), [Skæring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%A6ring), [Skåde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%A5de), [Slet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slet,_Aarhus), [Stavtrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavtrup), [Tilst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilst) and [Tranbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranbjerg_J).

## Cities and towns



Municipal map

See also: [Administrative divisions of Aarhus Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Aarhus_Municipality) and [List of towns and cities in Aarhus Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_and_cities_in_Aarhus_Municipality)

[Statistics Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danmarks_Statistik) defines towns or cities as areas with more than 200 residents in a continuous settlement with no more than 200 meters between residential structures. 1 January 2020 there were 19 such areas in Aarhus Municipality, the largest being the city of [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus) with a population of 280,534, while some 50,000 people lived in urban areas elsewhere in the municipality. The city of Aarhus is the second largest [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) in Denmark, the principal port on the east coast of [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland), and the seat of Denmark's second-largest bishopric. In 2013 [Beder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beder,_Denmark) and [Malling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malling,_Denmark) was officially counted as a single urban area for the first time.[^cite_note-StatDanBY12015-20] Towns in the municipality are generally considered [satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town) of Aarhus.

## Twin towns – sister cities

See also: [List of twin towns and sister cities in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_twin_towns_and_sister_cities_in_Denmark)

Aarhus is [twinned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_city) with:[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

- [Norway](21241.md) [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen), Norway (1946)

- [Sweden](5058739.md) [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg_Municipality), Sweden (1946)

- [China](5405.md) [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin), China (1984)

- [Germany](11867.md) [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel), Germany (2019)

- [Greenland](12118.md) [Kujalleq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kujalleq), Greenland (1962)

- [Germany](11867.md) [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock), Germany (2006)

- [Finland](10577.md) [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku), Finland (1946)

- [Ukraine](31750.md) [Lviv](49968.md), Ukraine (2023)

Formerly, [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) was twinned with Aarhus, but following the [2022 Russian Invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), this relationship was terminated on 9. March 2022.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]
