# Palm oil

For oil created from the palm seed, rather than the palm fruit, see [palm kernel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_kernel_oil).



Palm oil

**Palm oil** is an edible [vegetable oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil) derived from the reddish [mesocarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_anatomy#Mesocarp) (pulp) of the fruit of the [African oil palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_oil_palm).[^cite_note-1] The oil is used in food manufacturing, in beauty products, and as [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel). Palm oil accounted for about 36% of global oils produced from oil crops in 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-2] Palm oils are easier to stabilize and maintain quality of flavor and consistency in [ultra-processed foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-processed_foods), so they are frequently favored by food manufacturers.[^cite_note-3] Globally, humans consumed an average of 7.7 kg (17 lb) of palm oil per person in 2015.[^cite_note-bloomberg-4] Demand has also increased for other uses, such as cosmetics and biofuels, encouraging the growth of palm oil [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations) in tropical countries.

The mass production of palm oil in the tropics is a topic for substantial concern for [environmental and human rights groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_environmental_impact_of_palm_oil). The palm oil industry is a significant contributor to [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) in the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic) where palms are grown and has been cited as a factor in social problems due to allegations of human rights violations among growers.[^cite_note-5]

In 2018, a report by the [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature) acknowledged that palm oil is much more efficient than other oils in terms of land and water usage; however, deforestation causes more biodiversity loss than switching to other oils.[^cite_note-6] The biggest global producers of palm oil are Indonesia, which produced 60% of it in 2022,[^cite_note-7] followed by Malaysia, Thailand, and Nigeria. Indonesia produces [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel) primarily from palm oil.[^cite_note-deforestation-8]

## History

Humans used oil palms as far back as 5,000 years. In the late 1800s, archaeologists discovered a substance that they concluded was originally palm oil in a tomb at [Abydos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abydos,_Egypt) dating back to 3,000 BCE.[^cite_note-Kiple-9]

Palm oil from *[Elaeis guineensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaeis_guineensis)* has long been recognized in West and Central African countries used widely as a [cooking oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil). European merchants trading with West Africa occasionally purchased palm oil for use as a cooking oil in Europe.

Palm oil became a highly sought-after commodity by British traders for use as an industrial [lubricant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubricant) for machinery during Britain's [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).[^cite_note-10] Palm oil formed the basis of [soap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap) products, such as [Lever Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever_Brothers)' (now [Unilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilever)) "[Sunlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunlight_\(cleaning_product\))" soap, and the American [Palmolive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmolive_\(soap\)) brand.[^cite_note-11]

By around 1870, palm oil constituted the primary export of some West African countries, which often accompanied oppressive labor practices, as highlighted in the account of Abina Mansah's life (and, human rights group argue, still does).[^cite_note-12] However, this was overtaken by [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean) in the 1880s with the introduction of colonial European cocoa plantations.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Uses



Palm oil production is done in some parts of the world artisanally, and the locally produced oil is used for food, handicrafts and other products. This woman in the Democratic Republic of the Congo is showing the palm fruit above a pot for processing the fruit.

### Food

The highly saturated nature of palm oil renders it solid at room temperature in temperate regions, making it a cheap [substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_substitute) for [butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter) or [hydrogenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation) [vegetable oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil) in uses where solid fat is desirable, such as the making of [pastry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastry) dough and baked goods. Palm oil is used in West African cuisine such as [egusi soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egusi_soup) and [okra soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okra_soup).[^cite_note-15] Palm oil is sometimes used as a minor ingredient in calf [milk replacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_replacer).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Non-food consumer products

Palm oil is pervasively used in personal care and cleaning products, and it provides the foaming agent in nearly every soap, shampoo, or detergent. Around 70% of personal care products including soap, shampoo, makeup, and lotion, contain ingredients derived from palm oil. However, there are more than 200 different names for these palm oil ingredients and only 10% of them include the word "palm".[^cite_note-18]

### Biomass and biofuels

Palm oil is used to produce both methyl ester and hydrodeoxygenated [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel).[^cite_note-Rojas-19] Palm oil methyl ester is created through a process called [transesterification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transesterification). Palm oil biodiesel is often blended with other fuels to create palm oil biodiesel blends.[^cite_note-20] Palm oil biodiesel meets the European [EN 14214](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EN_14214) standard for biodiesels.[^cite_note-Rojas-19] Hydrodeoxygenated biodiesel is produced by direct [hydrogenolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenolysis) of the fat into alkanes and propane. The world's largest palm oil biodiesel plant is the €550 million Finnish-operated [Neste Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neste_\(company\)) biodiesel plant in [Singapore](27318.md), which opened in 2011 with a capacity of 800,000 tons per year and produces hydrodeoxygenated [NEXBTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXBTL) biodiesel from palm oil imported from Malaysia and Indonesia.[^cite_note-Yahya-21][^cite_note-22]

Significant amounts of palm oil exports to Europe are converted to biodiesel (as of early 2018: Indonesia: 40%, Malaysia 30%).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] In 2014, almost half of all the palm oil in Europe was burned as car and truck fuel.[^cite_note-25] As of 2018, one-half of Europe's palm oil imports were used for biodiesel.[^cite_note-26] Use of palm oil as biodiesel generates three times the carbon emissions as using fossil fuel,[^cite_note-27] and, for example, "biodiesel made from Indonesian palm oil makes the global carbon problem worse, not better."[^cite_note-28]

There are pressures for increased oil palm production from Indonesian palm-based biodiesel programs. The biodiesel currently contains a 30\:70 palm oil to conventional diesel ratio (known as B30) at the gas pumps. The Indonesian government is aiming to produce 100% palm oil biodiesel (or B100) to transition out of using conventional diesel. The Indonesian government has estimated it would need to establish approximately 15 million hectares of oil palm plantations to meet these future demands.[^cite_note-29]

The organic waste matter that is produced when processing oil palm, including oil palm shells and oil palm fruit bunches, can also be used to produce energy. This waste material can be converted into pellets that can be used as a biofuel.[^cite_note-Choong-30] Additionally, palm oil that has been used to fry foods can be converted into methyl esters for biodiesel. The used cooking oil is chemically treated to create a biodiesel similar to petroleum diesel.[^cite_note-JOPR_2006-31]

## Social and environmental impact

Main article: [Social and environmental impact of palm oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_environmental_impact_of_palm_oil)

Forests have been cleared in parts of Indonesia and Malaysia to make space for oil-palm [monoculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoculture).[^cite_note-33] This has [significant impacts on the local ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_environmental_impact_of_palm_oil) leading to [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss). For example, these processes have resulted in significant acreage losses of the natural habitat of the three surviving species of [orangutan](22433.md). One species in particular, the [Sumatran orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_orangutan), has been listed as [critically endangered](7451605.md) because of habitat loss due to palm oil cultivation.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-Gilbert-35]

### Social



In [Borneo](4518.md), the forest (F), is being replaced by oil palm plantations (G). These changes are irreversible for all practical purposes (H).

In addition to environmental concerns, palm oil development in specific regions that produce it has also led to significant [social conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conflict). Journalists and NGO researchers have found that regions with fast growing palm oil production have experienced significant violations of indigenous tribe land rights, human trafficking, sexual abuses, and other related human rights violations.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

The palm oil industry has had both positive and negative impacts on workers, [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples), and residents of palm oil producing communities. Palm oil production provides employment opportunities, and has been shown to improve [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), social services and also help reduce poverty.[^cite_note-Budidarsono-39][^cite_note-Norwana-40][^cite_note-Ismail-41] However, in some cases, oil palm plantations have developed lands without consultation or compensation of the indigenous people inhabiting the land, resulting in social conflict.[^cite_note-IDMC-42][^cite_note-Colchester-43][^cite_note-FPP-44] The use of [illegal immigrants](7124412.md) in Malaysia has also raised concerns about working conditions within the palm oil industry.[^cite_note-BMS_2012-45][^cite_note-ABC_2014-46][^cite_note-BBC_2013-47]

Some social initiatives use palm oil cultivation as part of poverty alleviation strategies. Examples include the UN Food and Agriculture Organisation's hybrid oil palm project in Western Kenya, which improves incomes and diets of local populations, and Malaysia's [Federal Land Development Authority](27694519.md) and Federal Land Consolidation and Rehabilitation Authority, which both support rural development.[^cite_note-Ibrahim-48]

#### Food vs. fuel

Main article: [Food vs. fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_vs._fuel)

The use of palm oil in the production of biodiesel has led to concerns that the need for fuel is being placed ahead of the need for food, leading to [malnutrition](258979.md) in developing nations. This is known as the food versus fuel debate. According to a 2008 report published in the *[Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_and_Sustainable_Energy_Reviews)*, palm oil was determined to be a sustainable source of both food and biofuel, and the production of palm oil biodiesel does not pose a threat to edible palm oil supplies.[^cite_note-Man_Kee_Lam-49] According to a 2009 study published in the *Environmental Science and Policy* journal, palm oil biodiesel might increase the demand for palm oil in the future, resulting in the expansion of palm oil production, and therefore an increased supply of food.[^cite_note-50]

#### Human rights abuses



The palm oil industry has a history of violating labor-related human rights, indigenous territorial right and environmental rights of communities in the contexts where the industry is prominent. Child labor violations are common in smallholder farming in many of the post-colonial contexts (such [as Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labour_in_Africa)) in which palm oil is produced.&#x20;

One report indicated numerous allegations of [human rights](13831.md) abuses in the production of palm oil in Indonesia and Malaysia, including exposure to hazardous [pesticides](48340.md), [child labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labor), [rape](7980471.md) and [sexual abuse](7397019.md), and unsafe carrying loads, among other human rights violations. These incidents may receive no response by the company, police, or other law enforcement group, or are left unreported because victims fear retaliation from their abuser. Pesticides used by palm oil plantations include [paraquat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraquat), which has been banned in Europe over links to [Parkinson's disease](22228064.md), and [glyphosate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyphosate), which has been involved in a US class-action lawsuit regarding cancer[^cite_note-ap-51] (the [European Chemicals Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Chemicals_Agency) (ECHA) classified glyphosate as causing serious eye damage but did not find evidence implicating it as a carcinogen).[^cite_note-52]

Reports of indigenous peoples and communities in Indonesia, indicate losing farmland and traditionally significant land due to palm oil industry expansion. In 2017, there were over 650 different land disputes between palm oil plantations and indigenous landowners.[^cite_note-hrw\.org-53] Indigenous communities also expressed concern over the loss of natural resources, such as wild rubber, reed, and *adat* forests (communal forests).[^cite_note-hrw\.org-53] Indigenous communities have made some ground when it comes to land disputes, either through protest or legal means.[^cite_note-hrw\.org-53][^cite_note-54]

Other concerns when it comes to indigenous communities being impacted include lack of government oversight on palm oil plantations, political corruption, or the lack of enforcement of laws meant to protect indigenous lands.[^cite_note-55] In countries such as Guatemala, palm oil plantations have significant leverage within the local justice system, leading local police to disregard land claims, going as far as using force to break up protests, and even murdering local leaders.[^cite_note-56]

### Environmental

See also: [2015 Southeast Asian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Southeast_Asian_haze)

While only 5% of the world's vegetable oil farmland is used for palm plantations, palm cultivation produces 38% of the world's total vegetable oil supply.[^cite_note-spinks-57] In terms of oil yield, a palm plantation is 10 times more productive than [soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean), [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) or [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed) cultivation because the palm fruit and [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed) both provide usable oil.[^cite_note-spinks-57] Palm oil has garnered criticism from environmentalists due to the environmental importance of where it is grown. However, it is indisputably more efficient in comparison to other oil-producing plants. In 2016, it was found that palm oil farms produce around 4.17 metric tons of oil per hectare. By contrast other oils, such as sunflower, soybean, or peanut only produce 0.56, 0.39, and 0.16 metric tons respectively per hectare.[^cite_note-58] Palm oil is the most sustainable vegetable oil in terms of yield, requiring one-ninth of land used by other vegetable oil crops.[^cite_note-jp-59] In the future, laboratory-grown microbes like [torula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torula) oil might achieve higher yields per unit of land at comparable prices.[^cite_note-doi10.1016/j.rser.2012.01.003-60][^cite_note-61]

However, palm oil cultivation has been criticized for its impact on the natural environment,[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-Greenpeace_Cooking-63] including [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), loss of natural habitats,[^cite_note-64] and [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md)[^cite_note-Foster-65][^cite_note-66] which have threatened [critically endangered species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critically_endangered_species), such as the [orangutan](22433.md)[^cite_note-cspi-67] and [Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger).[^cite_note-WWF_2010-68] [Slash-and-burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn) techniques are still used to create new plantations across palm oil producing countries. From January to September 2019, 857,000 hectares of land was burned in Indonesia; peatlands accounted for more than a quarter of the burned area.[^cite_note-69] The widespread deforestation and other environmental destruction in Indonesia, much of which is caused by palm oil production has often been described by academics as an [ecocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

Furthermore, a study of palm oil in Cameroon showed that land occupied by palm oil plantations can reduce ecosystem quality by 30–45% compared to the potential natural vegetation state. This land use change also creates a [carbon debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_Debt), which can take approximately 45–53 years to offset greenhouse gas emissions reductions.[^cite_note-\:3-75]



[Deforestation in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Indonesia), to make way for an [oil palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_palm) plantation

[^cite_note-deforestation-8]

Environmental groups such as [Greenpeace](12233.md) and [Friends of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Earth) oppose the use of palm oil biofuels, claiming that the [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) caused by oil palm plantations is more damaging for the climate than the benefits gained by switching to biofuel and using the palms as [carbon sinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sink).[^cite_note-76]

A 2018 study by the [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature) (IUCN) concluded that palm oil is "here to stay" due to its higher productivity compared with many other vegetable oils. The IUCN maintains that replacing palm oil with other vegetable oils would necessitate greater amounts of agricultural land, negatively affecting [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity).[^cite_note-jp-59][^cite_note-\:0-77] The IUCN advocates better practices in the palm oil industry, including the prevention of plantations from expanding into forested regions and creating a demand for certified and sustainable palm oil products.[^cite_note-\:0-77]

In 2019, the [Rainforest Action Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest_Action_Network) surveyed eight global brands involved in palm oil extraction in the [Leuser Ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuser_Ecosystem), and said that none was performing adequately in avoiding "conflict palm oil".[^cite_note-78] Many of the companies told [The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian) they were working to improve their performance.[^cite_note-79] A [WWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature) scorecard rated only 15 out of 173 companies as performing well.[^cite_note-80]

In 2020 a study by [Chain Reaction Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_Reaction_Research) concluded that NDPE (No Deforestation, No Peat, No Exploitation) policies cover 83% of palm oil refineries. NDPE policies are according to the Chain Reaction Research the most effective private mechanism to cut the direct link with deforestation, due to the economic leverage refineries have over palm oil growers.[^cite_note-81]

Palm oil is one of seven commodities included in the 2023 [EU Regulation on Deforestation-free products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Regulation_on_Deforestation-free_products) (EUDR), which aims to guarantee that the products [European Union](9317.md) (EU) citizens consume do not contribute to deforestation or [forest degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_degradation) worldwide.[^cite_note-eudef-82]

#### Comparative environmental impacts with fossil fuels

Compared to fossil fuels, palm oil systems have lower non-renewable energy consumption, [global warming potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_potential) (GWP, excluding land-use change), and acidification potential than fossil fuel alternatives. As a biofuel, palm oil increases [eutrophication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutrophication) potential (EP).[^cite_note-\:3-75]

## Production

In 2023–2024, world production of palm oil was 77.3 million [tonnes](31185.md), led by [Indonesia](14579.md) with 57% of the total.[^cite_note-fas-83] The annual production of palm oil is projected to reach 240 million tonnes by 2050.[^cite_note-84] During the [2022 food crises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_food_crises) instigated by the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) and crop failures in other parts of the world due to [extreme weather caused by climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_agriculture) leading to [food inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_inflation), the Indonesian government banned exports of palm oil.[^cite_note-\:4-85] This combined with a reduced harvest in Malaysia greatly increased global prices, while reducing availability causing ripple effects in the global supply chain.[^cite_note-\:4-85] On 23 May 2022, the Indonesian government reopened trading hoping to balance supplies.[^cite_note-86]

### Malaysia

A palm oil mill located on a palm oil plantation in Malaysia



A satellite image showing deforestation in [Malaysian Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Borneo) to allow the plantation of [oil palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_palm)

[Malaysia](3607937.md) is the world's second-largest producer of palm oil, producing 19.7 million tonnes in 2023–24.[^cite_note-fas-83]

In 1992, in response to [concerns about deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_environmental_impact_of_palm_oil#Deforestation), the [Government of Malaysia](9660028.md) pledged to limit the expansion of palm oil plantations by retaining a minimum of half the nation's land as [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover).[^cite_note-Morales-87][^cite_note-Scott-Thomas2-88]

### Nigeria

As of 2023–24, [Nigeria](21383.md) was the fifth-largest producer, with 1.5 million tonnes.[^cite_note-fas-83] Both small- and large-scale producers participate in the industry.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] In much of the [Niger Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_Delta), palm oil is commonoly referred to as "red oil" (or red gold) to distinguish it from the "black oil" ([crude oil](23195.md)) which dominates production.[^cite_note-91]

### Thailand

[Thailand](30128.md) is the world's third-largest producer of crude palm oil, producing 3.6 million tonnes in 2023–24.[^cite_note-fas-83] Nearly all of Thai production is consumed locally. Almost 85% of palm plantations and extraction mills are in south Thailand. At year-end 2016, 4.7 to 5.8 million [rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_\(unit\)) (750,000 to 930,000 hectares; 1,900,000 to 2,300,000 acres) were planted in oil palms, employing 300,000 farmers, mostly on small landholdings of 20 rai (3.2 hectares; 7.9 acres). [ASEAN](106539.md) as a region accounts for 52.5 million metric tons (57.9 million short tons) of palm oil production, about 85% of the world total and more than 90% of global exports. Indonesia accounts for 52% of world exports. Malaysian exports total 38%. The biggest consumers of palm oil are India, the European Union, and China, with the three consuming nearly 50% of world exports. Thailand's Department of Internal Trade (DIT) usually sets the price of crude palm oil and refined palm oil Thai farmers have a relatively low yield compared to those in Malaysia and Indonesia. Thai palm oil crops yield 4–17% oil compared to around 20% in competing countries. In addition, Indonesian and Malaysian oil palm plantations are 10 times the size of Thai plantations.[^cite_note-BP-20180128-92]

### Benin

Palm is native to the wetlands of western Africa, and south [Benin](3459.md) hosts many palm plantations. Its 'Agricultural Revival Programme' has identified many thousands of hectares of land as suitable for new oil palm export plantations. [Non-governmental organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organisation) (NGOs), such as [Nature Tropicale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Tropicale?action=edit\&redlink=1), claim biofuels will compete with domestic food production in some existing prime agricultural sites. Other areas comprise [peat land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat), whose drainage would have a deleterious [environmental impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact). There is also concern that [genetically modified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_modification) plants will be introduced into the region, jeopardizing the current premium paid for their non-GM crops.[^cite_note-wrm-93][^cite_note-94]

According to recent article by [National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic), most palm oil in Benin is still produced by women for domestic use.[^cite_note-95] The FAO additionally states that peasants in Benin practice [agroecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agroecology).[^cite_note-96] They harvest palm fruit from small farms and the palm oil is mostly used for local consumption.

### Cameroon

[Cameroon](5447.md) had a production project underway initiated by Herakles Farms in the United States.[^cite_note-97] However, the project was halted under the pressure of civil society organizations in Cameroon. Before the project was halted, Herakles left the Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil early in negotiations.[^cite_note-98] The project has been controversial due to opposition from villagers and the location of the project in a sensitive region for biodiversity.

### Colombia

In 2018, total palm oil production in [Colombia](5222.md) reached 1.6 million metric tons (1.8 million short tons), representing some 8% of national agricultural [GDP](12594.md) and benefiting mainly [smallholders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallholder) (65% of Colombia's palm oil sector).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] According to a study from the Environmental, Science and Policy, Colombia has the potential to produce sustainable palm oil without causing [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation).[^cite_note-101] In addition, palm oil and other crops provide a productive alternative for illegal crops, like [coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca).[^cite_note-102]

### Ecuador

Ecuador aims to help palm oil producers switch to sustainable methods and achieve RSPO certification under initiatives to develop greener industries.[^cite_note-103]

### Ghana

[Ghana](12067.md) has a lot of [palm nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae) species, which may become an important contributor to the agriculture of the region. Although Ghana has multiple palm species, ranging from local palm nuts to other species locally called agric, it was only marketed locally and to neighboring countries. Production is now expanding as major investment funds are purchasing plantations because Ghana is considered a major growth area for palm oil.

### Kenya

[Kenya](188171.md)'s domestic production of edible oils covers about a third of its annual demand, estimated at 380,000 metric tons (420,000 short tons). The rest is imported at a cost of around US\$140 million a year, making edible oil the country's second most important import after petroleum. Since 1993 a new [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)) variety of cold-tolerant, high-yielding oil palm has been promoted by the [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization_of_the_United_Nations) in western Kenya. As well as alleviating the country's deficit of edible oils while providing an important cash crop, it is claimed to have environmental benefits in the region, because it does not compete against food crops or native vegetation and it provides stabilisation for the soil.

### Myanmar

Palm oil was introduced to [British Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma) (now Myanmar) in the 1920s.[^cite_note-\:5-104] Beginning in the 1970s, smaller-scale palm oil plantations were developed in [Tanintharyi Region](15162240.md), and [Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_State), [Kayin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayin_State), and [Rakhine States](493373.md).[^cite_note-\:5-104] In 1999, the ruling military junta, the [State Peace and Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council), initiated the large-scale development of such plantations, especially in Tanintharyi, the southernmost region of Myanmar.[^cite_note-\:5-104] As of 2019, over 401,814 ha of palm oil concessions have been awarded to 44 companies.[^cite_note-\:5-104] 60% of the awarded concessions consist of forests and native vegetation, and some concessions overlap with national parks, including [Tanintharyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanintharyi_Nature_Reserve) and [Lenya National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenya_National_Park), which have seen deforestation and threaten conservation efforts for endemic species like the [Indochinese tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochinese_tiger).[^cite_note-\:5-104][^cite_note-105]

## Markets



Palm oil is one of the most commonly produced vegetable oils

According to the Hamburg-based *Oil World* trade journal,[^cite_note-106] in 2008 global production of oils and fats stood at 160 million tonnes. Palm oil and palm kernel oil were jointly the largest contributor, accounting for 48 million tonnes, or 30% of the total output. [Soybean oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean_oil) came in second with 37 million tonnes (23%). About 38% of the oils and fats produced in the world were shipped across oceans. Of the 60 million tonnes of oils and fats exported around the world, palm oil and palm kernel oil made up close to 60%; Malaysia, with 45% of the market share, dominated the palm oil trade. Production of palm oil that complies with voluntary sustainability standards is growing at a faster rate than conventional production. Standard-compliant production increased by 110% from 2008 to 2016, while conventional production increased by 2%.[^cite_note-107] The production of vegetable oils as a whole went up 125% between 2000 and 2020, driven by a sharp increase in palm oil.[^cite_note-\:14-108]

### Food label regulations

Previously, palm oil could be listed as "vegetable fat" or "vegetable oil" on food labels in the European Union (EU). From December 2014, [food packaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_packaging) in the EU is no longer allowed to use the generic terms "vegetable fat" or "vegetable oil" in the ingredients list. Food producers are required to list the specific type of vegetable fat used, including palm oil. Vegetable oils and fats can be grouped together in the ingredients list under the term "vegetable oils" or "vegetable fats" but this must be followed by the type of vegetable origin (e.g., palm, sunflower, or rapeseed) and the phrase "in varying proportions".[^cite_note-GAIN_Report-109]

In Malaysia, it is illegal to label products in ways that discriminate against palm oil. Offenders can be fined up to [RM](360278.md)250,000 or sent to prison for up to five years. One convenience store was raided for selling "no palm oil" ice cream, with all products seized.[^cite_note-110]

#### Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil (RSPO)

This section is an excerpt from [Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil\&action=edit)]



Roundtable No 2 (RT2) in Zürich in 2005

The [Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil) (RSPO) was established in 2004 with the objective of promoting the growth and use of [sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable) palm oil products through global standards and [multistakeholder governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multistakeholder_governance_model). The seat of the association is in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), Switzerland, while the secretariat is currently based in [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md), with a satellite office in [Jakarta](16275.md). RSPO currently has 5,650 members from 94 countries.[^cite_note-113] These members include palm oil producers, manufacturers who use palm oil in their products, and some environmental groups.

The RSPO was established following concerns raised by non-governmental organizations about [environmental impacts resulting from palm oil production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_environmental_impact_of_palm_oil).[^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Gunasegaran-114]

51,999,404 tonnes of palm oil fruit produced in 2016 was RSPO certified.[^cite_note-115] Products containing Certified Sustainable Palm Oil (CSPO) can carry the RSPO trademark.[^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Yulisman-116] In 2014, Indonesia accounted for 40% of global palm oil production and 44% of the total RSPO-certified areas.[^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_\:3-117]

After the meeting in 2009, a number of environmental organisations were critical of the scope of the agreements reached.[^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Browne-118] Palm oil growers who produce CSPO have been critical of the organization because, though they have met RSPO standards and assumed the costs associated with certification, the market demand for certified palm oil remains low.[^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Gunasegaran-114][^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Yulisman-116] Even though deforestation has decreased in RSPO-certified oil palm plantations, peatlands continue to be drained and burned for the creation of new RSPO-certified palm plantations.[^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_\:3-117] Additionally, no mention to well-documented health effects of palm oil is made by the organization.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Browne-118][^cite_note-Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Palm_Oil_Gunasegaran-114]

In 2019, RSPO launched the [Independent Smallholder Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Smallholder_Standard?action=edit\&redlink=1) (ISH). By simplifying documentation and cost requirements, it expanded the RSPO system to smallholder farmers not affiliated with large corporations or cooperatives.This enhanced smallholder management awareness and skills, improved sustainable palm oil market opportunities, and enhanced palm oil community development and income.[^cite_note-120]

## Processing



Oil palm fruits on the tree



An oil palm stem, weighing about 10 kg, with some of its fruits picked

Palm oil is naturally reddish in color because of a high [beta-carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-carotene) content. It is not to be confused with [palm kernel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_kernel_oil) derived from the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_kernel) of the same fruit[^cite_note-121] or [coconut oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_oil) derived from the kernel of the coconut palm (*[Cocos nucifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_nucifera)*). The differences are in color (raw palm kernel oil lacks [carotenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotenoids) and is not red), and in [saturated fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat) content: palm mesocarp oil is 49% saturated, while palm kernel oil and coconut oil are 81% and 86% saturated fats, respectively. However, crude red palm oil that has been refined, neutralized, bleached and deodorized, a common [commodity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_\(type_of_good\)) called RBD (refined, bleached, and deodorized) palm oil, does not contain carotenoids.[^cite_note-nag-122] Many industrial food applications of palm oil use fractionated components of palm oil (often listed as "modified palm oil") whose saturation levels can reach 90%;[^cite_note-123] these "modified" palm oils can become highly saturated, but are not necessarily [hydrogenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation).

The oil palm produces bunches containing many fruits with the fleshy mesocarp enclosing a kernel that is covered by a very hard shell. The [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) considers palm oil (coming from the pulp) and palm kernels to be primary products. The oil extraction rate from a bunch varies from 17 to 27% for palm oil, and from 4 to 10% for palm kernels.[^cite_note-124]

Along with coconut oil, palm oil is one of the few highly [saturated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat) [vegetable fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_fat) and is semisolid at room temperature.[^cite_note-Plant_cholesterol-125] Palm oil is a common cooking ingredient in the tropical belt of Africa, Southeast Asia and parts of Brazil. Its use in the commercial food industry in other parts of the world is widespread because of its lower cost[^cite_note-126] and the high oxidative stability ([saturation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat)) of the refined product when used for frying.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] One source reported that humans consumed an average 17 pounds (7.7 kg) of palm oil per person in 2015.[^cite_note-bloomberg-4]

### Extraction



Red palm oil being sold on the roadside in plastic bottles in Ghana. Artisanal production of palm oil is common there, providing a key staple food stuff in most traditional cooking.

Palm oil is traditionally, and still industrially, produced by [milling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_\(grinding\)) the fruits of oil palm.[^cite_note-FAO-129]

Besides milling, palm oil is produced by [cold-pressing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil#Mechanical_extraction) the fruit of the oil palm since the 1990s. This type of artisanal palm oil is usually not further refined, so it keeps the natural red color. It is bottled for use as a [cooking oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil), in addition to other uses such as being blended into [mayonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayonnaise) and [vegetable oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salad_oil).[^cite_note-nag-122]

The result of milling or cold-pressing is a mixture of water, crude palm oil, and fibers from the palm fruit. A minimum degree of processing is required to obtain the oil. The mixture is first passed through a filter to remove the solids, then separated by density to remove the water. Density treatment can also act as a basic form of degumming, provided that the fruit is steamed before milling to hydrolyze the gum, at a cost of also losing some triglycerides to hydrolysis.[^cite_note-FAO-129]

The result of basic processing is called a "crude palm oil"[^cite_note-FAO-129] or a "red palm oil", referring to its intense color due to the high [carotenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotenoid) content.[^cite_note-Oguntibeju-130] Red palm oil is a traditional cooking oil in West Africa. The free fatty acids within provide a "bite" to the flavor.[^cite_note-FAO-129] The triglyceride part is around 50% [saturated fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat)—considerably less than [palm kernel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_kernel_oil)—and 40% [monounsaturated fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monounsaturated_fat) and 10% [polyunsaturated fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyunsaturated_fat). It is a source of Vitamin A and Vitamin E.[^cite_note-Oguntibeju-130]

### Refining

See also: [Edible oil refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edible_oil_refining)



A worker walking near the border of an oil mill in Malaysia. Palm oil production is an important part of economies in many parts of rural Malaysia, but is also a source of [environmental conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_conflict).

Crude PO can be refined to remove its non-triglyceride components.

1. Bleaching removes color from the oil. This is achieved by adding a clay absorbent called [bleaching earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleaching_earth) in a vacuum mixer.

   [^cite_note-Lidu2016-131]

2. Filters remove the clay from the oil.

   [^cite_note-Lidu2016-131]

3. The oil enters the deodorizer, which is responsible for removing [free fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_fatty_acid) (FFA) generated by hydrolysis. One type of deodorizer works by distillating out the FFAs using a set of different temperatures. The FFA is collected as "palm fatty acid distillate" (PFAD).

   [^cite_note-Lidu2016-131]

   &#x20;PFAD is itself a valuable product used in the manufacture of [soaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap), [washing powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washing_powder) and other products.

4. The final, refined oil is called "refined, bleached and deodorized palm oil" (RBD PO). RBD PO is the basic palm oil sold on the world's commodity markets.

RBD PO is also known as white palm oil. It can be further [fractionated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractionation) using the different melting points of its components. The part with a higher melting point, which crystalizes out as a solid earlier, is called [palm stearin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_stearin). It consists of mostly saturated fats. The remaining liquid part is called [palm olein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_olein?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-132] It is also possible to fractionate at a different point of processing, even with crude palm oil.[^cite_note-133]

RBD PO, or "palm [shortening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortening)", is extensively used in food manufacture. It is valued for its low polyunsaturated fat content, which offers high stability against [rancidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancidity) and allows it to replace hydrogenated fats in a variety of baked and fried products.

## Nutrition, composition and health

Palm oil is a food staple in many cuisines, contributing significant calories and plant lipids.[^cite_note-WHO_Technical_Report-134][^cite_note-Economist_2011-135][^cite_note-Bradsher-136] Globally, humans consumed an average of 7.7 kg (17 lb) of palm oil per person in 2015.[^cite_note-bloomberg-4] Although the relationship of palm oil consumption to disease risk has been previously assessed, the quality of the [clinical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_research) specifically assessing palm oil effects has been generally poor.[^cite_note-137] Consequently, research has focused on the deleterious effects of palm oil and palmitic acid consumption as sources of saturated fat content in edible oils, leading to conclusions that palm oil and saturated fats should be replaced with [polyunsaturated fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyunsaturated_fat) in the diet.[^cite_note-aha-138][^cite_note-ejcn-139]

A 2015 [meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis) and 2017 advisory from the [American Heart Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Heart_Association) indicated that palm oil is among foods supplying dietary saturated fat which increases blood levels of [LDL cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-density_lipoprotein) and increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases, leading to recommendations for reduced use or elimination of dietary palm oil in favor of consuming [unhydrogenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation#Food_industry) [vegetable oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil).[^cite_note-aha-138][^cite_note-Sun-140] The British [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service) provided a 2023 guideline recommending that palm oil foods be replaced by those made with unsaturated fats.[^cite_note-NHS-141] A 2019 meta-analysis found no association between total fat, saturated fatty acids, monounsaturated fatty acid, and polyunsaturated fatty acid intake with risk of cardiovascular disease.[^cite_note-142]

Glycidyl fatty acid esters (GE), [3-MCPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-MCPD) and 2-MCPD, are found especially in palm oils and palm fats because of their refining at high temperatures (approx. 200 °C (392 °F)).[^cite_note-\:1-143] Since [glycidol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycidol), the parent compound of GE, is considered genotoxic and carcinogenic, the [EFSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Food_Safety_Authority) did not set a safe level for GE. According to the chair of the CONTAM (EFSA's expert Panel on Contaminants in the Food Chain), "The exposure to GE of babies consuming solely [infant formula](65330.md) is a particular concern as this is up to ten times what would be considered of low concern for public health".[^cite_note-\:1-143] The EFSA's tolerable daily intake (TDI) of [3-MCPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-MCPD) and its fatty acid esters was set to 0.8 micrograms per kilogram of body weight per day (μg/kg bw/day) in 2016 and increased to 2 μg/kg bw/day in 2017, based on evidence linking this substance to organ damage in animal tests and on possible adverse effects on the [kidney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney) and on [male fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male_fertility).[^cite_note-\:1-143][^cite_note-144] According to the EFSA, there is not enough data to set a safe level for 2-MCPD.[^cite_note-\:1-143] As of December 2022, the [Malaysian Palm Oil Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Palm_Oil_Board) issued an amendment to its palm oil licensing conditions to include maximum limits of 1.25 ppm and 1 ppm, respectively, to the amount of 3-MCPDE and GE that can be found in processed palm oil.[^cite_note-145]

### Key components

#### Fatty acids

Main article: [Fatty acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid)

Palm oil, like all fats, is composed of [fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid), [esterified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ester) with [glycerol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycerol). Palm oil has an unusually high concentration of saturated fat, specifically the 16-carbon saturated fatty acid, [palmitic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmitic_acid), to which it gives its name. Monounsaturated [oleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleic_acid) is also a major constituent of palm oil. Unrefined palm oil is a significant source of [tocotrienol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocotrienol), part of the [vitamin E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_E) family.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-oil-147]

The linoleic acid content of palm oil is about 6.4–15%.[^cite_note-148]

#### Carotenes

Red palm oil is rich in carotenes, such as [alpha-carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-carotene), [beta-carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-carotene) and [lycopene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycopene), which give it a characteristic dark red color.[^cite_note-oil-147][^cite_note-150] However, palm oil that has been refined, bleached and deodorized from crude palm oil (called "RBD palm oil") does not contain carotenes.[^cite_note-nag-122]

#### Palmitic acid

Excessive intake of [palmitic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmitic_acid), which makes up 44% of palm oil, increases blood levels of [low-density lipoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-density_lipoprotein) (LDL) and total cholesterol, and so increases risk of [cardiovascular diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_diseases).[^cite_note-aha-138][^cite_note-ejcn-139][^cite_note-CSPI-151] Other reviews, the [World Health Organization](33583.md), and the US [National Heart, Lung and Blood Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Heart,_Lung_and_Blood_Institute) have encouraged consumers to limit the consumption of palm oil, palmitic acid and foods high in saturated fat.[^cite_note-WHO_Technical_Report-134][^cite_note-aha-138][^cite_note-CSPI-151]
