**Weligama** ([Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language): වැලිගම, [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language): வெலிகாமம்) is a town on the south coast of [Sri Lanka](26750.md), located in [Matara District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matara_District), [Southern Province, Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Province,_Sri_Lanka), governed by an [Urban Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Council). The name *Weligama*, literally means "sandy village" which refers to the area's sandy sweep bay. It is approximately 144 kilometres (89 mi) south of [Colombo](56636.md) and is situated at an elevation of 9 metres (30 ft) above the sea level.

## History

The main industries are tourism and fishing. Weligama is a popular tourist destination and hosts several boutique hotels including an off shore [islet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet) known as *[Taprobane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taprobane_Island_\(Weligama\))*, which houses a villa constructed by the French Count de Mauny, and is currently owned by Geoffrey Dobbs. It was the birthplace of the scholar monk [Weligama Sri Sumangala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weligama_Sri_Sumangala).

Weligama was significantly affected by the [tsunami](31161.md) caused by the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_of_the_2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_on_Sri_Lanka), with 15% of the area destroyed, with over 2,200 houses damaged or washed away, and 469 reported deaths.[^cite_note-1]

There are a number of sites of historical importance within Weligama and its vicinity, including a 3 metres (9.8 ft) high [bas-relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-relief) statue of [Bodhisattva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva) [Avalokiteśvara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalokite%C5%9Bvara), carved into the surrounding rock, between the 6th-9th century AD.[^cite_note-2] It is known locally as *Kusta Raja Gala* or *Rock of the Leper King* and is thought to represent a king smitten with a skin disease (possibly [leprosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leprosy) "kusta"), who was prompted in a vision to take coconut pulp and water for three months as a cure. When he fulfilled the vision his health was restored, he then commissioned his figure to be carved on the rock commemorating this miraculous cure. This sculpture is believed to be all that is left of the old Agrabodhi Vihara that was located there.[^cite_note-3]

Weligama is recognised for its beeralu [bobbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobbin_lace) [lace-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lace). First introduced by the Portuguese in the 16th century, lace-making has remained a traditional handicraft along the coastal area of Weligama, with a number of households producing [crochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocheted_lace) and [tatting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatting) lace.[^cite_note-4]

The area is also famous for its distinct [stilt fishermen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan_fishing),[^cite_note-5] who erect a single pole in the chest-deep water on the beach, just few meters off-shore, where they perch on a cross bar and using bamboo fishing rods cast their lines out beyond the surf break to catch small fish.

## Transport

Weligama is located on the [Coastal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Line_\(Sri_Lanka\)) or Southern Rail Line (connecting [Colombo](56636.md) through to [Matara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matara,_Sri_Lanka)), and the [A2 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_highway_\(Sri_Lanka\)), connecting [Colombo](56636.md) to Weligama.

## Demographics

The Weligama Urban Council area is [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) Majority with a large [Sri Lankan Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Muslim) population, and a small number of [Sri Lankan Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils). Others including [Indian Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Tamils_of_Sri_Lanka), [Burgher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgher_people), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Malays).
