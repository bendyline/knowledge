**Michael Robert Conway** (born 19 August 1983) is a British professional racing driver competing in the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) with [Toyota Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Motorsport_GmbH).

In [endurance racing](699018.md), Conway has been a [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Motorsport_GmbH) factory driver since 2014. He won two [FIA World Endurance Championship titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIA_World_Endurance_champions) in [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), as well as the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) and [2026](77197114.md). In [formula racing](1618495.md), Conway was test driver for [Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_in_Formula_One) in [Formula One](10854.md), and won multiple [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) races between 2009 and 2014. He also briefly raced in [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_E).

## Early career

Conway who was born in [Bromley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromley), [London](17867.md), attended Sevenoaks Prep School from 1986 to 1996.

Conway began racing in [karting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kart_racing) at the age of eight at [Rye House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye_House_Kart_Circuit) in [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire). After that, he went on to be the Formula A British Karting Champion, then raced in [Formula Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Ford) with [Van Diemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen). He was the [Formula Renault UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Formula_Renault_Championship#UK_series) Champion in 2004 and then entered the [British F3 International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Formula_3_Championship) in 2005, with the same [Fortec Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortec_Motorsport) team with which he had competed in Formula Renault UK.

In British F3, Conway was the highest placed rookie and finished third behind [Alvaro Parente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvaro_Parente) and [Charlie Kimball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kimball), as well as managing 13th in the [BP Ultimate Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_of_Formula_3) at [Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Park_Zandvoort), having started 16th. At the [Macau Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Grand_Prix), Conway ended up fourth in both the qualifying and the qualifying race around the Circuito da Guia, before a problematic Grand Prix saw him retire with engine failure, but classified 14th.

Following that success, Conway was signed by 2MB Sports Management run by former Grand Prix drivers [Martin Brundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brundle) and [Mark Blundell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Blundell), and signed for the [Räikkönen Robertson Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_R_Racing) team, owned by then [McLaren](20994.md) driver [Kimi Räikkönen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimi_R%C3%A4ikk%C3%B6nen) and his race-manager [Steve Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Robertson_\(racing_driver\)). In the 2006 season, Conway dominated the British F3 International Series, and clinched the title with three races remaining, as well as finishing first of the British F3 drivers in Race Two of the prestigious [Pau Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau_Grand_Prix), in France, a round of the [British F3 International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Formula_3_Championship) for 2006 - [Romain Grosjean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romain_Grosjean) won the race. While at the [Macau Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Grand_Prix), after qualifying 11th, he finished seventh in the qualifying race, before becoming the first British driver to win the Grand Prix since [Darren Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Manning) in 1999.

### GP2

#### 2006



Conway driving for [Trident Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_Racing) at the [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverstone_Circuit) round of the [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_GP2_Series_season) [GP2 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Series) season

Conway made his [GP2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Series) debut at [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverstone_Circuit) in June 2006, when he replaced the injured [Olivier Pla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Pla) in a [DPR Direxiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Price_Racing) car. Having stalled at the start of race one, he battled to 11th place. In race two, he also finished 11th.

Conway won the National Racing Driver of the Year Award at the 2006 McLaren [Autosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosport) Awards.

#### 2007

Conway then signed to drive a full season for [Super Nova Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Nova_Racing) in the [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_GP2_Series_season) [GP2 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2_Series), taking a podium finish at [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverstone_Circuit), and signed as a test driver with the [Honda Racing F1 Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_in_Formula_One#Honda_Racing_F1_Team) <http://www.f1network.net/main/s169/st112910.htm>. He remained in the series for [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_GP2_Series_season), having signed for the [Trident Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_Racing) team,[^cite_note-1] after testing for several other teams <http://www.crash.net/motorsport/gp2/feature/12319-0/qa_mike_conway.html>. At [Monaco](19261.md), he took his first GP2 series win (and first 2008 podium) in the sprint race, earning pole position by finishing eighth in the feature race, in which he ran third before being hit by backmarker [Javier Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Villa) on the final lap <https://web.archive.org/web/20080531235757/http://www.itv-f1.com/news_article.aspx?id=42743>. At [Magny-Cours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magny-Cours), he finished eighth in the feature race, but faded to finish sixth in a wet sprint race. He eventually finished twelfth in the drivers' championship, an improvement of two positions on the previous year.

## IndyCar career



Conway at [Sonoma Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoma_Raceway) in 2014

During 2008, Conway was given an opportunity to test an IndyCar Series car at [Infineon Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infineon_Raceway) and surprised several series regulars by being top of the times during one session.[^cite_note-2] Conway signed with [Dreyer & Reinbold Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreyer_&_Reinbold_Racing) to compete in the IndyCar Series full-time in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_IndyCar_Series_season).

On the final lap of the [2010 Indianapolis 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indianapolis_500), Conway was battling side by side with [Ryan Hunter-Reay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Hunter-Reay) when the two cars became entangled as Hunter-Reay's [Dallara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallara) ran low on fuel and stuttered between turns 3 and 4, resulting in Conway's Dallara-[Honda](13729.md) being launched into the air. Conway suffered a broken leg during the crash and was flown straight to the [Methodist Hospital of Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Hospital_of_Indianapolis).[^cite_note-3] Conway also received a compression fracture of one of his [thoracic vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_vertebrae) and was fitted with a back brace. The injuries effectively ended his participation in the rest of the season.[^cite_note-4]

On 1 February 2011, [Andretti Autosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andretti_Autosport) announced that Conway had been signed to a full-time ride for the 2011 IndyCar season.[^cite_note-5] He won his first IndyCar race at Long Beach on 17 April 2011, but the remainder of the year was disappointing, as Conway only managed to score three more top-ten finishes and failed to qualify for the Indianapolis 500; he finished 17th in the championship.



Conway driving in the 2012 Detroit Grand Prix

Conway moved to the [A. J. Foyt Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Foyt_Enterprises) team for the [2012 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_IndyCar_Series_season).[^cite_note-6] He also made his racing return to the Indianapolis 500, having failed to qualify the year before. He qualified near the back but appeared to have a strong car coming up several positions during the race. On lap 79, Conway entered the pits during a scheduled green flag pit stop period. He made contact with one of his crew members damaging his front wing but not injuring the crew member. The damage went unnoticed by the crew who hurried up the pit stop to keep Conway in contention. A few laps later, Conway lost control of his car, spinning in front of [Will Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Power) and making contact with Power causing him to hit the inside wall. At the same time, Conway's car turned around backwards and flew airborne into the fence topside first. Conway was uninjured in the incident, but due to damage he was unable to finish the race and was scored in 29th position. He later finished in third place in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Honda_Indy_Toronto), his best result of the season.

In the week leading up to the [final race of the 2012 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_MAV_TV_500_IndyCar_World_Championships)—held at the [Fontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Club_Speedway) superspeedway—Conway informed the Foyt team that he no longer felt comfortable competing on oval tracks, and asked not to race. He was replaced by [Wade Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Cunningham). Conway's decision stemmed from injuries after a crash at the [2010 Indianapolis 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indianapolis_500) and the debate over IndyCar competing on oval tracks after the October 2011 death of [Dan Wheldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Wheldon).[^cite_note-7]

In [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_IndyCar_Series_season), Conway ran a one race deal with Rahal-Letterman-Lanigan Racing at Long Beach finishing 25th with an electrical issue, then signed with Dale Coyne Racing for the doubleheader events of the year, winning his first race out at the Belle Isle Park Course. In [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_IndyCar_Series_season), he ran only the road courses and street circuits for [Ed Carpenter Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Carpenter_Racing) winning both the Toyota Grand Prix of Long Beach as well as the Honda Indy Toronto Doubleheader.[^cite_note-8]

## Sportscar career

### 2013



Conway driving the No. 26 [Oreca 03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreca_03) at the [2013 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), where they would be disqualified from the final classification

In 2013, Conway decided to enter sports car racing to supplement his IndyCar career, becoming a full-time driver for [G-Drive Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Docking_Racing) in the [LMP2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMP2) class of the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).[^cite_note-Conway-9] Having started the campaign with two points finishes, Conway and co-drivers [John Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Martin_\(Australian_racing_driver\)) and [Roman Rusinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rusinov) were excluded from a third place at [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) due to a fuel tank infraction.[^cite_note-GDriveDisqualified-10] They then found form, dominantly winning from pole at [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_6_Hours_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo) and [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_6_Hours_of_Circuit_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] After finishing second in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_6_Hours_of_Fuji), the trio won the final two races to finish third in the category standings.[^cite_note-13]

### 2014

The following year, Conway joined [LMP1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans_Prototype) outfit [Toyota Gazoo Racing](3796361.md) as a test and reserve driver in the [WEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), driving for the team in three races besides his IndyCar commitments.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-COTA_entry-16] He would score his first overall win in sportscars at [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_6_Hours_of_Bahrain), beating the Porsche entries on pace during the middle of the contest.[^cite_note-17] In addition to his duties in sportscars and IndyCar, Conway was announced as one of [Dragon Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Racing)'s drivers for the inaugural [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Formula_E_Championship) season in July, though he left the team on 5 September before the first round.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### 2015–2016

Conway would become a full-time driver at Toyota for the [2015 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), racing with the experienced pairing of [Stéphane Sarrazin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A9phane_Sarrazin) and [Alexander Wurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Wurz).[^cite_note-Toyota_lineup-20] Over the course of the year, Toyota was unable to keep up with its rivals, as Conway and his teammates finished sixth in the standings, level on points with the sister car. With the retirement of Wurz, Conway and Sarrazin were joined by [Kamui Kobayashi](9537340.md) ahead of [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).[^cite_note-Toyota_lineup2-21] After being promoted to second at [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Silverstone) due to a disqualification for Audi, Conway made a mistake at [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps), hitting the No. 37 LMP2 entry and being forced to serve a drive-through penalty.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The car was later retired due to an engine failure.[^cite_note-\:15-25] Conway began the [Le Mans 24 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) well by overtaking the Porsches of [Timo Bernhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timo_Bernhard) and [Neel Jani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neel_Jani) to take the lead in the opening hour.[^cite_note-DSCH1-26] He lost the lead to Jani during the night but emerged ahead due to a superior strategy in the morning; a chance of victory went begging however when the No. 6 had to enter the garage to fix floor damage, thus losing a place to the No. 2 Porsche and eventually ending up second after the No. 5's retirement on the final lap.[^cite_note-AutosportH9-27][^cite_note-DSCChaos-28][^cite_note-AutosportH22-29][^cite_note-30] From the middle part of the campaign the No. 6 would become a constant on the podium, finishing third in [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Mexico) and [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Circuit_of_the_Americas) before winning its home race at [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Fuji) after a strong performance by Kobayashi.[^cite_note-31] The trio finished second in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_6_Hours_of_Shanghai) despite suffering two punctures but lost out on the title in the final round, eventually finishing third overall.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

### 2017

In [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), Conway's teammates changed again, as he and Kobayashi were now partnering [José María López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_L%C3%B3pez) in the No. 7.[^cite_note-GazooWEC-34] An early lead battle at the season opener was ended when López crashed heavily, though the team bounced back to claim second at [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps).[^cite_note-35] Drama followed at [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans): Conway had re-taken a lead he had lost during the second hour, which later became substantial because of reliability struggles for the entire LMP1 field.[^cite_note-AutosportH2-36] The No. 7 would not be spared however, as the car's clutch broke during the night and forced them to retire.[^cite_note-TSReport-37] Over the rest of the campaign, Conway and his teammates only scored two more podiums and finished last of the four full-time LMP1 entries in the standings.

### 2019–20



The race-winning No. 7 car in Silverstone

For the fifth full season in succession, Conway returned to Toyota in [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship). The final season of LMP1 regulations began well, as Conway and Kobayashi combined for pole at [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_4_Hours_of_Silverstone) before winning the race along with López.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] A second place at [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_6_Hours_of_Fuji) and third place to the sister car and the winning [Rebellion Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion_Racing) entry in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_4_Hours_of_Shanghai) preceded the team's second victory at the [2019 8 Hours of Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_8_Hours_of_Bahrain), where Conway was able to avoid the first lap chaos to establish an early advantage.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The No. 7 would retain the championship, as they followed a third place in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Lone_Star_Le_Mans) with a commanding win at [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps) after the [COVID-induced](62750956.md) break.[^cite_note-57] However, they once again lost out on a [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) victory by finishing second to the sister crew; they lost the lead this time thanks to a loss of power caused by a fracture in an exhaust manifold during the early hours of Sunday.[^cite_note-ToyotaConsole-58][^cite_note-SpeedcafeConway-59] Consolation would come at the final round in [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_8_Hours_of_Bahrain), where a pole position from Conway and López led to a victory and, eventually, the WEC title.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

### 2021

In 2021, the new [Le Mans Hypercar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans_Hypercar) formula would be introduced, forcing Conway and his teammates to pilot the new [Toyota GR010 Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR010_Hybrid) in the [WEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).[^cite_note-ToyotaLMHLineup-62] Having experienced a troubled run at [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps) which relegated the No. 7 to third behind the grandfathered LMP1 of [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Elf_Matmut) and finished second at [Portimão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_8_Hours_of_Portim%C3%A3o), the trio drove an untroubled [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_6_Hours_of_Monza) race and won following a short battle with the Alpine.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] At [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), Kobayashi qualified the car on pole.[^cite_note-66] Conway then went on to take a dominant lead after the #8 suffered a crash on the first safety car restart; the No. 7 team experienced few troubles and won, giving Conway, Kobayashi, and López their first respective victories at the 24 Hours.[^cite_note-S365H1-67][^cite_note-AutosportLMHReport-68][^cite_note-ConwayMCOM-69] The championship lead they had gained would be solidified at the first [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_6_Hours_of_Bahrain) event where the #7 outpaced the #8 to win for the third time running.[^cite_note-70] Despite losing first place to the sister car at the [season finale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_8_Hours_of_Bahrain), Conway and his teammates ended up retaining the World Championship with a second-place finish, therefore becoming the first titlists in the Hypercar era.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

### 2022



Conway piloting the No. 7 GR010 Hybrid at the [2022 24 Hours of Le Mans](68512218.md)

The [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) proved to be more difficult: the team suffered a heavy crash at [Sebring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_1000_Miles_of_Sebring) at the hands of López, though they bounced back to win at [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps) amidst race-ending issues for the No. 8.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Conway had multiple fights for the lead with Buemi at [Le Mans](68512218.md), but the team was forced to settle for second after encountering a front-axle [motor–generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor%E2%80%93generator) issue during the morning hours.[^cite_note-R\&TLive-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-MCOMWin-78] The No. 7 then finished third at [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_6_Hours_of_Monza), where a fight with the Alpine resulted in a collision, a penalty for Kobayashi, and a third-place finish.[^cite_note-79] Despite Kobayashi's pole at [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_6_Hours_of_Fuji) the team was unable to keep up with the sister car and came second; Conway and his teammates finished the season on a positive note by winning in [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_8_Hours_of_Bahrain), where Conway's pace allowed the team to issue orders to [Brendon Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendon_Hartley) to let the Brit past.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] The No. 7's drivers finished third in the overall table, sixteen points behind their teammates.[^cite_note-83]

### 2023



Conway and his No. 7 teammates took victory in Spa-Francorchamps

With an influx of new manufacturers, [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) would bring stronger opposition for Toyota.[^cite_note-TGRDrivers-84] Still, the team managed to start well, as Conway and the No. 7 won the opening round in [Sebring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_1000_Miles_of_Sebring).[^cite_note-85] [Portimão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_6_Hours_of_Portim%C3%A3o) however brought problems, with a failure of a torque sensor necessitating a swap of the left-side driveshaft and resulting in ninth place at the flag.[^cite_note-86] A victory at [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps) was followed up by a retirement at [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), where Kobayashi had to retire at midnight following a crash caused by an LMP2 car.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-DSCH9-88] Despite the virtual impossibility of winning the title at that point, the #7 crew persevered, coming out on top in a lead battle with the No. 50 [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_499P) at [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_6_Hours_of_Monza).[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] They also triumphed at [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_6_Hours_of_Fuji), once again winning from pole but this time after a long chase of the No. 6 [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Penske_Motorsport).[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] A runner-up spot at [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_8_Hours_of_Bahrain) was enough for Conway and his teammates to finish second, though they lost out on both the race win and the championship to the No. 8.[^cite_note-93]

### 2024

Toyota made a change to its driver lineup for the [2024 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship): López left the team, with Conway and Kobayashi now partnering former reserve driver [Nyck de Vries](28892600.md).[^cite_note-TGRDrivers2-94] However, Conway would be forced to miss [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) after he fractured his ribs and collarbone in a cycling accident a day before the race. [José María López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_L%C3%B3pez) replaced Conway for the Le Mans weekend.[^cite_note-95] Conway returned at [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_6_Hours_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo) and controlled the race's early stages, before dropping off the lead lap with a fuel pressure monitor problem.[^cite_note-\:1-96] They eventually recovered to fourth by the flag.[^cite_note-\:1-96] The team then finished second at [Lone Star Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Lone_Star_Le_Mans), battling for the race lead against the No. 83 [AF Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AF_Corse) car throughout but losing out due to a drive-through penalty for Kobayashi.[^cite_note-97] Following the race, Conway described the penalty as "bizarre".[^cite_note-98] The season ended discretely: at [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_6_Hours_of_Fuji), a collision between Kobayashi and [Matt Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Campbell_\(racing_driver\)) forced Toyota out of the race.[^cite_note-99] Though Conway went up to third in the opening hour of the [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_8_Hours_of_Bahrain) season finale, the No. 7 retired from the lead in hour 5 with a fuel pump issue.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

### 2025



Conway's No. 7 car at the 2025 24 Hours of Le Mans

Conway and his teammates remained unchanged in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).[^cite_note-TGRDrivers3-102] Sixth in [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Qatar_1812_km) was followed by seventh in [Imola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_6_Hours_of_Imola), where Conway damaged the car after a collision with [René Rast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Rast).[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] At [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps), Conway recovered to sixth during the first hour after suffering a puncture; the team eventually finished seventh.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] Conway and his teammates then ended up sixth at [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), being the last car on the lead lap, before being promoted to fifth following a penalty to Ferrari's No. 50.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] A slump in performance followed at [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_6_Hours_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo), where both Toyotas finished outside of the points.[^cite_note-109] After suffering a collar bone injury in another bike crash, Conway was ruled out of the [Lone Star Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Lone_Star_Le_Mans) weekend, once again being replaced by former teammate López.[^cite_note-110] Having tempered expectations for the teams performance ahead of his return in [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_6_Hours_of_Fuji), Conway finished the race in eighth.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] At the final round in [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_8_Hours_of_Bahrain), Conway held the lead for the majority of the first two hours, paving the way for a comfortable victory for the No. 7 crew.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]
