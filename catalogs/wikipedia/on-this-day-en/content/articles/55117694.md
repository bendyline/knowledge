**Super Micro Computer, Inc.**, [doing business as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_business_as) **Supermicro**, is an American [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) company based in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California). The company is one of the largest producers of high-performance and high-efficiency [servers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_\(computing\)),[^cite_note-3] while also providing server management software, and storage systems for various markets, including [enterprise data centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_IT_management), [cloud computing](19541494.md), [artificial intelligence](1164.md), [5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G), and [edge computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_computing).[^cite_note-\:5-4][^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-anandtech-supermicro01-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Supermicro was founded in 1993, and has manufacturing operations in [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley), the [Netherlands](21148.md), and in [Taiwan](25734.md) at its Science and Technology Park.

## History

In 1993, Supermicro began as a five-person business operation run by [Charles Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Liang), a [Taiwanese-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese-American), alongside his wife and company treasurer, Chiu-Chu Liu, known as Sara.[^cite_note-Family-10] Prior to founding Supermicro, Liang earned a B.S. in electrical engineering from the [National Taiwan University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University_of_Science_and_Technology) and an M.S. in electrical engineering from the [University of Texas at Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Arlington). Liang holds several patents for server technology and was previously the president and chief design engineer of Micro Center Computer, a motherboard design and manufacturing company, from July 1991 to August 1993.[^cite_note-11] Liang stated that the company was able to turn a profit after six months of operation.[^cite_note-taiwan-panorama-12]

The same year it was founded, Supermicro established its first European partnership with Boston Limited, a UK-based IT solutions provider, which launched Supermicro products into the UK and European markets. The continued collaboration has seen several Supermicro technologies introduced to Europe, including the P5EXTRA in the 1990s, the 1U Twin in 2006, and innovations such as the Supercool 1U Twin in 2007[^cite_note-13] and high-performance GPU computing systems in 2009.[^cite_note-14]



Supermicro PCs in 2009

In 1996, the company opened a manufacturing subsidiary, Ablecom, in [Taiwan](30092.md), which is run by Charles's brothers, Steve Liang and Bill Liang. Charles Liang and his wife own close to 31 percent of Ablecom, while Steve Liang and other members of the family own close to 50 percent.[^cite_note-\:5-4]

In 1998, Supermicro opened a subsidiary in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Netherlands).[^cite_note-Family-10] In May 2010, Supermicro further expanded into Europe with the opening of its system integration logistics center in the Netherlands.[^cite_note-15]

In 2012, the company opened its Taiwan Science and Technology Park, totaling \$99 million in construction costs.[^cite_note-16] The Taiwanese factory was expanded in 2019 and came online in 2021.[^cite_note-taiwan-panorama-12]

In September 2014, Supermicro moved its corporate headquarters to the former *[Mercury News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mercury_News)* headquarters in [North San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_San_Jose), along [Interstate 880](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_880_\(California\)), naming the campus Supermicro Green Computing Park.[^cite_note-Ridder_Park_history-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Pizarro-19] In 2017, the company completed a new 182,000 square-foot manufacturing building on the campus, which was designed to meet [LEED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_in_Energy_and_Environmental_Design) gold certification.[^cite_note-\:1-20][^cite_note-21] The company expanded its San Jose campus in September 2021 with a manufacturing facility for advanced storage and server equipment. Supermicro was reported to have 2,400 people working in San Jose.[^cite_note-22]

In February 2025, Supermicro began building its third California-based manufacturing campus. The new campus is being developed with the intention to increase production of liquid-cooled services for data centers.[^cite_note-23] The company produces a majority of its servers in California. Following a push for more state-side manufacturing by American President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), Supermicro considered expanding server production in states like Mississippi and Texas.[^cite_note-24] A few months later, in July 2025, Supermicro expressed its interest in expanding investment regarding manufacturing in Europe to meet artificial intelligence demand in the area.[^cite_note-25]

In October 2025, Supermicro created a subsidiary focusing on American federal agencies, which would provide cloud-services and data center materials manufactured from its facilities in Silicon Valley, California.[^cite_note-26] From November 2025 to June 2026, Supermicro added eight additional production facilities in the United States,[^cite_note-Taipei-27] including its Data Center Building Block Solutions campus in San Jose, which was launched at the end of April 2026.[^cite_note-28] As of June 2026, the company planned to open two additional facilities in Taiwan. Supermicro also expanded its Netherlands-based operations.[^cite_note-Taipei-27] In addition, it operates a Malaysian facility, catering towards Southeast Asia and parts of Europe.[^cite_note-Taipei-27]

In 1995, Supermicro released a dual-CPU motherboard for servers, becoming one of the first companies to do so. The motherboard was followed up by a four-CPU server board.[^cite_note-taiwan-panorama-12]

In 2004, Supermicro began to develop energy-saving servers, which was partially influenced by Liang watching the movie *[The Day After Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Day_After_Tomorrow)* with his family.[^cite_note-taiwan-panorama-12]

In 2008, Supermicro was among ten computing companies brought on to the Hyperion project by the [National Nuclear Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Nuclear_Security_Administration)'s [Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Livermore_National_Laboratory). The companies helped to develop a testbed for high-performance computing technologies for the purpose of maintaining the U.S. nuclear weapons stockpile while avoiding underground nuclear testing, and improving the industry's ability to make petaFLOP/s (quadrillion [floating operations per second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_point_operations_per_second)) computing and storage more accessible.[^cite_note-29]

In 2012, Supermicro debuted its new 2U and 4U/Tower platforms.[^cite_note-30]



Supermicro booth at [COMPUTEX Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMPUTEX_Taipei) in 2019

In 2016, Supermicro sent 30,000 MicroBlade servers to a Silicon Valley data center with a claimed [power usage effectiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_usage_effectiveness) (PUE) of 1.06.[^cite_note-31] While Supermicro did not name the customer, it was likely Intel, who opened a similar data center in November 2015 with a PUE of 1.06.[^cite_note-32]

In April 2020, Supermicro announced the H12 A+ Superblade, a blade server based on the 2nd gen Epyc 1P family of CPUs. It was the first blade server platform to implement [AMD](2400.md)'s [Epyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epyc) processors.[^cite_note-33]

In April 2021, Supermicro introduced over 100 application-optimized server product SKUs using (new at the time) 3rd Gen [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) Xeon Scalable processors, including Hyper, SuperBlade, the Twin Product Family (BigTwin, TwinPro, and FatTwin), Ultra, CloudDC, GPU, Telco/5Gand Edge servers.[^cite_note-34]

In July 2024, *[VentureBeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VentureBeat)* reported that Supermicro would be providing half the servers for [Elon Musk](909036.md)'s artificial intelligence start-up, [xAI](74330685.md), with [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell) providing the other half.[^cite_note-35] The project was completed in 122 days according to the company, resulting in the creation of a 750,000 square foot Memphis-based data center to host [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_\(supercomputer\)), a supercomputer.[^cite_note-36] Supermicro also supplied servers for Tesla's [Gigafactory Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_Texas).[^cite_note-37]

In November 2025, Supermicro reported fiscal first-quarter 2026 results under expectations, stating that "design win upgrades" on server projects, where \$1.5 billion in expected revenue shifted due to a high-volume customer requesting last minute updates, which extended integration, testing, and validation times for new GPU-based systems.[^cite_note-reuters-quarterly-38] From the last two years until then, Supermicro's revenue had grown from \$7 billion to \$22 billion.[^cite_note-39] Additionally, the company received \$13 billion in orders on systems based on the Nvidia Blackwell Ultra-based GB300 architecture.[^cite_note-reuters-quarterly-38] In the same month, Supermicro displayed new products at the 2025 Supercomputing Conference, including the Nvidia GB300 NVL72 rack, 8U and 6U SuperBlade multi-node systems, 2U FlexTwin, HGX B300 4U rack, GB200 NVL4 1U server, and GB300-based developer workstation. Updates to the modular and blade families such as BigTwin, MicroBlade, and MicroCloud were also shown at the conference.

In January 2026, Supermicro announced it had expanded manufacturing capacity surrounding it's liquid-cooling technology in support of Nvidia's [Vera Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubin_\(microarchitecture\)) (including the Vera Rubin NVL72 SuperCluster and the HGX Rubin NVL8) and Rubin AI platforms. In addition to Supermicro's close ties to Nvidia, the company also has relationships with AMD, [Broadcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcom), Intel, [Samsung](46445426.md) and [Micron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micron_Technology).[^cite_note-40]

In June 2026, during CEO Charles Liang's keynote speech at Computex, Supermicro demonstrated its VR200 NVL72 rack using Vera CPUs and Rubin GPUs, which featured liquid-cooling through the company's newly updated proprietary coolant.[^cite_note-41] Also in that month, Supermicro released a series of [ARM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_Holdings)-based rack-scale AI systems, featuring [ARM AGI CPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture_family). The series consists of the 2U Hyper Server, 5U GPU Server, 2U4N Liquid-Cooled Server, 2U Hyper-E Server, and the 1U 4N in an [OCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Compute_Project) ORW rack.[^cite_note-42]

In September 2026, Supermicro released a workstation built around Nvidia's GB 300 Grace Blackwell Ultra superchip, containing 748 GB of RAM split between 498 GB of system memory on the Grace CPU, and 252 GB of [HBM3e memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Bandwidth_Memory) on the Blackwell Ultra GPU, intended for "research labs and enterprises running large models locally".[^cite_note-43]

### Partnerships

In 2023, Supermicro partnered with [Rakuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakuten) Symphony on high-performing Open RAN technologies and storage systems for operators of cloud-based mobile services.[^cite_note-44] Later in the year, Supermicro debuted servers with liquid cooling, focusing on [ESG policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental,_social,_and_corporate_governance). The servers save approximately 40% of the power expended on air-cooled data centers.[^cite_note-45] In June 2023, Supermicro saw increased demand for its large language model optimized AI systems, featuring [NVIDIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NVIDIA) chips.[^cite_note-46]

In October 2024, Supermicro announced a partnership with [Fujitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujitsu), a Japanese IT services company, where the two companies will jointly develop energy-efficient servers and liquid-cooling systems for high-performance computing, generative artificial intelligence, and data centers.[^cite_note-47]

On December 25, 2024, Super Micro Computer announced a joint venture with Taiwanese development company Guo Rui to build an [artificial intelligence](1164.md) [data center](579730.md) powered solely by [renewable energy](25784.md) at an undisclosed location in Taiwan.[^cite_note-48]

In May 2025, Supermicro announced a \$20 billion partnership with DataVolt, a Saudi Arabian data center company. Under the agreement, Supermicro will supply GPU platforms and rack systems for DataVolt's AI campuses in both Saudi Arabia and the United States.[^cite_note-49]

In September 2025, Supermicro announced an artificial intelligence-based partnership with [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia), focusing on integrated data center network products.[^cite_note-50] In the following month, Supermicro would partner with [Hitachi Vantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi), each allowing the other company to offer their products through the opposing companies, focusing on artificial intelligence applications and [data lakehouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_lake#Data_lakehouses).[^cite_note-51]

On May 6 2026, *[BNN Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNN_Bloomberg)* reported that Supermicro and Nano Nuclear Energy signed a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) to provide power to AI data centers through the creation of on-site nuclear power.[^cite_note-52] Later in the month, Supermicro announced a collaboration with Verda, with the main goal of producing rack-scale systems designed for AI cloud infrastructure.

## Corporate affairs

### Board of directors

As of March 2026, Supermicro's board of directors consists of co-founder Charles Liang, co-founder Sara Liu, Tally Liu, Sherman Tuan, Judy Lin, Robert Blair, Susie Giordano, and Scott Angel.[^cite_note-53] Giordano joined the board in August 2024 and served as the only member of Supermicro's special committee to oversee an internal audit of concerns raised by EY.[^cite_note-GeneralCounsel-54] In April 2025, the company's former senior vice president of corporate development, Yitai Hu, became its chief legal officer, serving as Supermicro's general counsel.[^cite_note-GeneralCounsel-54] In March 2026, DeAnna Luna was promoted to the role of chief compliance officer at the company.[^cite_note-55]

### Financials

On March 8, 2007, Supermicro raised \$64 million in an [initial public offering](63879.md), selling 8 million shares at \$8 a share.[^cite_note-56] Supermicro's stock trades under the ticker symbol SMCI[^cite_note-57] on the [Nasdaq](21559.md) exchange. In 2009, Supermicro sold about \$720 million worth of computer servers and related products and employed almost 1,100 people.[^cite_note-58] By the end of 2023, the company had reported a [fiscal year](5752099.md) 2023 revenue of \$7.1 billion and employed over 5,000 workers globally.[^cite_note-10K-2023-59] Supermicro replaced [Whirlpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlpool_Corporation) in the S\&P 500 after a large rally in the company's stock lifted its market cap from \$4.5B at the end of 2022 to \$60B in March 2024.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] In June 2024, Supermicro made its debut on the [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) list, reaching number 498.[^cite_note-63] By 2025, the company had climbed 206 spots, earning the 292nd position on the list.[^cite_note-64] On July 22, 2024, Supermicro became a [Nasdaq-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq-100) company, replacing [Walgreens Boots Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walgreens_Boots_Alliance) in the index.[^cite_note-65] Supermicro was removed from the index in December 2024.[^cite_note-66]

In June 2026, Supermicro raised \$7 billion "through a series of equity and equity-linked financing transactions" to fund the purchasing of components related to demand for its AI servers. The components used will fulfill orders worth \$39 billion.[^cite_note-67] Additionally, in Supermicro's fiscal third quarter of 2026, the company reported net sales of \$10.2 billion, and forecasted revenue between \$11 billion and \$12.5 billion.[^cite_note-BizJournal-1] Supermicro reported a gross margin of 10.1% in that quarter, topping analyst estimates of 6.75%, attributing the margin recovery and sales to its "combined hardware, software, and services offerings", referring to the company's Data Center Building Block Solutions (DCBBS).[^cite_note-68]

## Controversy

In November 2021, the joint venture of Super Micro Computer and Fiberhome Telecommunication Technologies won a contract for supplying servers to the [Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Production_and_Construction_Corps) for 'public safety purposes', which is associated with the [persecution of Uyghurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China) and construction of a [mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance_in_China) system in the province of [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

### Server tampering allegation

On October 4, 2018, *[Bloomberg Businessweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Businessweek)* published a report, citing unnamed corporate and governmental sources, which claimed that the Chinese [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) had forced Supermicro's Chinese sub-contractors to add microchips with [hardware backdoors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_backdoor) to its servers. The report claimed that [the compromised servers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_and_network_surveillance) had been sold to U.S. government divisions (including the [CIA](5183633.md) and [Department of Defense](7279897.md)), contractors, and at least 30 commercial clients.[^cite_note-\:2-73][^cite_note-\:3-74][^cite_note-\:4-75] Supermicro denied the report, stating that it had not been contacted by government agencies and were unaware of any investigation.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-ars-bb-77][^cite_note-zd-bb-78][^cite_note-79] The report was also disputed by sources and companies who were named therein.[^cite_note-zd-bb-78][^cite_note-ars-bb-77]

On October 9, 2018, *Bloomberg* issued a second report, alleging that Supermicro-manufactured datacenter servers of a U.S. telecom firm had been compromised by a hardware implant on an [Ethernet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethernet) connector.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] In response to the article, Supermicro announced that it would review its motherboards for potential [spy chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_spying),[^cite_note-83] later filing a letter with the [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) stating that it was "confident" that "no malicious hardware chip had been implanted" during the manufacture of its motherboards.[^cite_note-84]

In February 2021, *Bloomberg Business* reported that [U.S. intelligence](501244.md) used the altered servers to gather intelligence about China, warning only a small number of potential targets, despite those servers allegedly having been compromised since 2011.[^cite_note-85]

### Accounting practices

Supermicro settled with the SEC in August 2020 over violations in accounting practices between 2014 and 2017 by the company and its former chief financial officer, and agreed to pay \$17.5 million in penalties. In 2018, the company was briefly delisted from the Nasdaq after delaying to file financial reports by nearly two years.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

#### 2024 Hindenburg Research short-seller report

In 2024, short-seller [Hindenburg Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindenburg_Research) alleged that Supermicro continued to engage in accounting violations, adding that the company rehired executives who were involved in the accounting scandal. The report also alleged that the company was evading U.S. export restrictions by shipping advanced technology products to Russia,[^cite_note-Hindenburg-88] similar to a 2021 *[Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Post)* report co-written by Russian dissident authors [Andrei Soldatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Soldatov) and [Irina Borogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Borogan), accusing the company of supplying servers to a Moscow control center for [Internet censorship in Russia](37529240.md). In the latter instance, Supermicro responded by stating the following: "Supermicro complies with applicable laws and regulations, and our policies are consistent with international principles of human rights. We act appropriately to ensure this is the case."[^cite_note-89] Hindenburg's report also pointed out alleged "circular" financial relationship with Supermicro's suppliers, Ablecom and Compuware, which are controlled and partially owned by the brothers of Supermicro's CEO.[^cite_note-Hindenburg-88] The next day, Supermicro said it would delay the filing of its annual report.[^cite_note-90]

The [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) (DOJ) opened a preliminary probe into the company a month after the report, according to *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*.[^cite_note-91] Supermicro was subpoenaed by the DOJ and the Securities and Exchange Commission, seeking documents related to the report.[^cite_note-92]

In October 2024, Supermicro's auditors, [Ernst & Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_&_Young) resigned after raising significant concerns over the company's internal controls, board independence and accounting practices.[^cite_note-93] The company created an independent special board committee comprising board members and external counsel to evaluate issues raised by Ernst & Young, which would later find no evidence of misconduct.[^cite_note-94] The board also started searching to replace the CFO.[^cite_note-95] In the next month, Supermicro hired a new auditor, [BDO Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BDO_Global).[^cite_note-auditor-96] On February 25, 2025, the company filed its annual report, just before the end of its extended deadline. Supermicro included an additional note in its filing, saying "it had identified material weaknesses in internal controls over financial reporting", and in response to those issues, "plans to hire additional accounting and audit employees, and to upgrade IT systems."[^cite_note-97]

### Export violations

In 2006, Supermicro pleaded guilty to a felony charge and paid a \$150,000 fine due to a violation of a United States embargo against the sale of computer systems to Iran.[^cite_note-Iran-98] In a plea agreement, it was acknowledged that Supermicro became aware of the investigation in February 2004 and set up an export-control program that same year.[^cite_note-Iran-98]

In March 2026, three individuals affiliated with Supermicro, including co-founder and Senior Vice President of Business Development, Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw; general manager in Supermicro's Taiwan office, Ruei-Tsang "Steven" Chang; and a third-party contractor/broker,[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Ting-Wei "Willy" Sun were indicted in a U.S. federal court for conspiring to smuggle billions of dollars of AI chips to China.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-ReutersChina-102][^cite_note-103] Super Micro servers with AI chips were reported to have been smuggled to universities linked to China's [People's Liberation Army](66890.md).[^cite_note-104] A Southeast Asian company was directed by the individuals to place orders with Supermicro, who then repackaged the servers to divert them to China.[^cite_note-WSJResignation-105] The company was reviewed by compliance team members from Supermicro and a [United States Department of Commerce](70243.md) official, who visited the site in December 2025.[^cite_note-WSJResignation-105] The Southeast Asian company purchased approximately \$2.5 billion-worth of servers.[^cite_note-WSJResignation-105] Supermicro was not named as a defendant in the case and the company stated it's intent to cooperate with on-going investigations.[^cite_note-ReutersChina-102] Yih-Shyan Liaw was initially placed on leave[^cite_note-ReutersChina-102] and eventually resigned after being arrested,[^cite_note-106] having plead not guilty to charges against him. Liaw was released on a \$5 million bond.[^cite_note-107] Sun's contract with Supermicro was terminated after the company learned of the charges,[^cite_note-ReutersChina-102] and he was arrested in Taiwan.[^cite_note-ChipArrests-108] Chang, a Taiwanese citizen, remains a fugitive as of the same date of the others' arrests.[^cite_note-ChipArrests-108]

Supermicro opened an internal investigation, which was led by independent board members Scott Angel and Tally Liu and the law firm [Munger, Tolles & Olson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munger,_Tolles_&_Olson), and launched an internal review of its global trade compliance program.[^cite_note-109] The investigation was completed, determining that "the company nor its current senior executives" were involved in the re-distribution of AI chips. The investigation resulted in the termination of several employees for breaking company policies and code of conduct, and the adoption of all recommendations to "enhance its export compliance programs".[^cite_note-110]

At the end of May 2026, Supermicro assisted Taiwanese authorities in an investigation leading to the arrest of three individuals attempting to divert shipments of servers to China.[^cite_note-111] In June 2026, Taiwanese government agencies accessed a local Supermicro office and multiple local affiliates (including Chief Telecom Inc. and Albatron Technology Co.) as part of its probe, "working closely" with Supermicro in its investigation.[^cite_note-112] On July 1, 2026, Supermicro re-affirmed its collaboration with Taiwanese officials, publishing an open letter to its customers and partners denying that its offices had been raided or that it was the target of an investigation, and clarifying that the company had worked with Taiwanese authorities to permissively grant access to the work stations and devices of four Supermicro employees who had been detained for questioning as a part of its ongoing collaboration with authorities.[^cite_note-113]
