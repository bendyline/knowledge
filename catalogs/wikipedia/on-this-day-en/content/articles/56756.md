The **politics of [Uganda](31816.md)** occurs in an [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) context. Since its independence, Uganda has not experienced a peaceful democratic transfer of power, with 3 dictators [Milton Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Obote), [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin) and [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) dominating its politics.[^cite_note-1] Since assuming office in 1986 at the end of the [Ugandan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Bush_War), [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) has ruled Uganda as a [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator).[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3] [Political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) were banned from 1986 to 2006 in the wake of the [2005 Ugandan multi-party referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Ugandan_multi-party_referendum) which was won by pro-democracy forces.[^cite_note-\:0-2] Since 2006, Museveni has used legal means, patronage, and violence to maintain power.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

Under the [Ugandan constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Uganda), Uganda is a [presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) in which the [President](450146.md) is the head of state and the prime minister is the head of government business. There is a multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the government. Legislative power is given to both the government and the National Assembly. The system is based on a democratic parliamentary system with equal rights for all citizens over 18 years of age. The [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) rated Uganda a "[hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index#Definitions)" in 2022.[^cite_note-democracy-index-4]

## Political culture



[Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin), one of the most notable Ugandan presidents.

After taking power after a five-year civil war in 1986, the authoritarian Yoweri Museveni regime banned political parties from campaigning in elections or field candidates directly (although electoral candidates could belong to political parties). A [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_multi-party_referendum,_2005) canceled this 19-year ban on multi-party politics in July 2005.[^cite_note-5]

Presidential elections were held in February 2006. Museveni ran against several candidates, of whom the most prominent was the exiled Dr. [Kizza Besigye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizza_Besigye). Museveni was declared the winner. Besigye alleged fraud, and rejected the result. The [Supreme Court of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Uganda) ruled that the election was marred by intimidation, violence, [voter disenfranchisement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_disenfranchisement), and other irregularities. However, the Court voted 4-3 to uphold the results of the election.[^cite_note-6]

The [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) in [Uganda](31816.md) is the [President](450146.md), who is elected by a popular vote to a five-year term. This is currently Yoweri Museveni, who is also the head of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_People's_Defense_Force). The previous presidential elections were in February 2011, and in the election of February 2016, Museveni was elected with 68 percent of the vote. The cabinet is appointed by the president from among the elected legislators. The [prime minister of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Uganda), [Robina Nabbanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinah_Nabbanja), assists the president in the supervision of the cabinet.

The [Cabinet of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Uganda), according to the Constitution of Uganda, "shall consist of the President, the [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Uganda) and such number of Ministers as may appear to the President to be reasonably necessary for the efficient running of the State."[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Judiciary

The Ugandan judiciary operates as an independent branch of government and consists of magistrate's courts, high courts, courts of appeal (which organizes itself as the [Constitutional Court of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Uganda) when hearing constitutional issues), and the Supreme Court. Judges for the High Court are appointed by the president; Judges for the Court of Appeal are appointed by the president and approved by the legislature.[^cite_note-13]

[Pius Bigirimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_Bigirimana) is the permanent secretary since 2019[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]
