**Herceg Novi** ([Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script): Херцег Нови, pronounced [\[xěrtseɡ nôviː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian)) is a town in [Coastal region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Montenegro) of [Montenegro](20760.md) located at the Western entrance to the [Bay of Kotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Kotor) and at the foot of Mount [Orjen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orjen). It is the administrative center of the [Herceg Novi Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herceg_Novi_Municipality) with around 33,000 inhabitants. The town was founded as a fortress in 1382 by the King of Bosnia, [Tvrtko I Kotromanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvrtko_I_of_Bosnia), and named after [Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen) but the name did not stick, instead it became known as Novi (transl. New), also Castelnuovo in Italian (transl. New Castle). Between 1482 and 1687 it was part of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and then from 1687 to 1797 the [Albania Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_Veneta) of the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice). It was a Catholic bishopric and remains a Latin [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see) as Novi. Herceg Novi has had a turbulent past, despite being one of the youngest settlements on the [Adriatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic). A [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Montenegro) of varied occupations has created a blend of diverse and picturesque architectural styles in the city.

## Names and etymology

[Tvrtko I of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvrtko_I_of_Bosnia) founded the town and named it after [Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen), the name that from the beginning gave way to a name *Novi*, which literally translates to "New", as in Newtown; also known as *Castelnuovo* in Italian, *New Castle* in English. Later the town came into the [Kosača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosa%C4%8Da) noble family's possession and become their winter seat. During this era, the town was renamed again by adding [Stjepan Vukčić Kosača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stjepan_Vuk%C4%8Di%C4%87_Kosa%C4%8Da)'s title *herceg* (*prince*, Serbo-Croatian pronunciation of German *herzog*) to the name *Novi*, which gave it the current name of *Herceg Novi*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVego195744–45-2]

In [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian), the town is known as *Herceg Novi* or *Херцег Нови*; in [Italian](14708.md) as *Castelnuovo*; and in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) as *Neókastron* (Νεόκαστρον), Ottoman Turkish as *Kala-i Novi*, and modern Turkish as *Kastelnovo*, all except the first meaning 'new castle'. The name Herceg Novi, in its modern form, was first mentioned in a Turkish document in 1726.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVasić200595-3]

Archeological findings from the Luštica peninsula and the Vranjaj cavern imply that the area was populated during the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age). In the 3rd century BC, after their victory over the [Illyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians), the area was ruled by the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic). After the split of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the area fell under the rule of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) and after its fall, the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire) also known as [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). Slavic tribes began inhabiting these lands during the 7th century AD. During these times the small settlement was part of Byzantine-held [Dračevica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dra%C4%8Devica_\(%C5%BEupa\)) district, which in turn later belonged to the Principality of [Travunija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travunija). During the 10th century, Dračevica came under the control of various Dioclean/Zetan dukes, who were in turn incorporated into [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia_\(medieval\)) ruled by the [Nemanjić dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemanji%C4%87_dynasty). After the death of Emperor [Stefan Dušan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Du%C5%A1an), the Serbian Empire began to fracture into smaller principalities and districts, with Dračevica being administered by duke [Vojislav Vojinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojislav_Vojinovi%C4%87). After the rulership of Vojinović, the area, along with most of modern Montenegro, came under the rule of the [Balšić noble family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal%C5%A1i%C4%87_noble_family).

### Foundation under Bosnia



The fortress of Novi built by Tvrtko I, overlooking 'Stari Grad' (Old Town of Herceg Novi) and the Adriatic in Bay of Kotor

Bosnia could not make economical use of [Bosansko Primorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosansko_Primorje), as its share of the [Adriatic coast](23275478.md) from the river [Neretva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neretva) to the [Bay of Kotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Kotor) lacked any major settlements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEĆirković1964144-4][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEĆirković1964142-5] In the second half of the 14th century area in Bay of Kotor also became part of the [Kingdom of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bosnia). The Bosnian king, [Tvrtko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvrtko_I_of_Bosnia), embroiled in the [War of Chioggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Chioggia), which erupted between the old-time rival [Republics of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republic_of_Genoa) in 1378 will, as a result, find himself pressured by the circumstances of failure to seize [Kotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotor), the damage to the Bosnian economy from the Ragusan embargo, and the need for easy access to maritime trade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEĆirković1964145,_146,_147-6][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEĆirković1964149-7] This and the fact that Bosansko Primorje had no significant port led Tvrtko to found the youngest medieval town on the eastern Adriatic coast.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEĆirković1964148-8] In early 1382, Tvrtko constructed a new fortress in the Bay of Kotor on the place of a small fishing village and decided that it should form the basis of a new [salt trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_trading) center. Initially named after [Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen), the city immediately came to be known as Novi (meaning "new").[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEĆirković1964148-8]

After the death of Tvrtko, Duke [Sandalj Hranić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandalj_Hrani%C4%87) of the Hum lords, [Kosačas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosa%C4%8Da), acquired *Novi*. During his reign, the town picked up trading salt. When Hranić died, his nephew, Duke [Stjepan Vukčić Kosača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stjepan_Vuk%C4%8Di%C4%87_Kosa%C4%8Da) inherited it. During his reign, the town grew in importance and became Stjepan's winter seat, getting a new name in the process: Herceg Novi.

### Under the Ottomans

The [Ottomans](22278.md) conquered Herceg Novi before 14 December 1481, and ruled for 200 years. The Ottomans built Kanli Tower on the upper edge of the city. However, there was a short pause between 1538 and 1539 when it was held by the Spaniards before they were defeated in the [Siege of Castelnuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Castelnuovo). In their brief overlordship, the Spanish built a Hispaniola fort above the city that is well-preserved today. [Evliya Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_%C3%87elebi) visits the Bay of Kotor and mentions Croats, Bosnians and Albanians (Arnauts) who live in the Herceg Novi.[^cite_note-9]

In 1608 an earthquake struck Herceg Novi and devastated it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVasić200599-10]



Herceg Novi in 1700, [Albania Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_Veneta)

### Modern Age

In 1687, the city was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Castelnuovo_\(1687\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) by the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) under [Girolamo Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Corner), and included it into [Albania Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_Veneta), an administrative unit on the territory of present-day coastal Montenegro. In Venice, the city was known as Castelnuovo. The Venetians refortified the old town walls and towers and reinforced the fortress with a Citadella Tower (destroyed in an earthquake in 1979). On 24 August 1798, Herceg Novi was annexed by Habsburg [Austria](26964606.md) but was then ceded to Russia as per the [Treaty of Pressburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Pressburg_\(1805\)) on 26 December 1805. The Russians officially occupied Herceg Novi between 28 February 1806 and 12 August 1807.

On 7 July 1807, Herceg Novi was ceded to [Napoleon I Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I_Bonaparte)'s [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) as per the [Treaty of Tilsit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tilsit). Official French rule over Herceg Novi began on 12 August 1807, when the Russians left the city. The city was part of Dalmatia until 14 October 1809, when it was annexed to the newly created [Illyrian Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_Provinces).



Herceg Novi in 1837

Herceg Novi, as well as the rest of the Bay of Kotor, was overtaken by Montenegrin forces in 1813. It was under control of a temporary government based in Dobrota between 11 September 1813 and 10 June 1814, which was supported by [Montenegro](20760.md). The appearance of Austrian forces in 1814 caused the [Prince-Bishop of Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1) to turn over the territory to Austrian administration on 11 June. After Herceg Novi was retaken, as well as the rest of the bay, it became part of the [Dalmatian crownland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dalmatia). The bay was under Austro-Hungarian control until 1918.

### 20th century



Austrian KK stamp issued in 1867 cancelled Castelnuovo in Dalmazia



Former coat of arms of Herceg Novi

In 1900, the two names ERZEG NOVI and CASTELNUOVO PRESSO CATTARO were used in bilingual cancellations.[^cite_note-11]

The [Kingdom of Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Montenegro) attempted to retake the Bay of Kotor during [World War I](4764461.md), it was bombarded from [Lovćen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lov%C4%87en), but by 1916 Austria-Hungary defeated Montenegro.

On 7 November 1918, the Serbian Army entered the bay and were greeted by the people as Slavic liberators. The bay later became a part of the self-proclaimed [State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Slovenes,_Croats_and_Serbs). Within a month, this region united with Serbia as part of the [Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbs,_Croats_and_Slovenes), renamed to Yugoslavia in 1929. The bay was a municipality of [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia) until it was, like all historic entities, abolished in 1922. It was incorporated into the Zeta Oblast (province), from 1929 style [Zeta Banate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_Banate).

Herceg Novi was annexed by Mussolini's fascist Italy during [World War II](32927.md) in 1941. It became a part of the province of Cattaro.

Herceg Novi was later retaken by [Yugoslav Partisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisans_\(Yugoslavia\)) forces on 10 September 1943. Within Tito's Communist reformed [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFR_Yugoslavia), Herceg Novi became part of the [People's Republic of Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Montenegro).

On 8 April 1970, following the [USSR vs. Rest of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_\(USSR\)_vs_Rest_of_the_World) 'Match of the Century' hosted in [Belgrade](55904.md), Yugoslavia, a blitz [chess](5134.md) tournament was held in Herceg Novi, featuring four World Champions, the field was considered the strongest of any blitz tournament in modern chess history.

### Titular see

In 1933 the diocese was nominally restored by establishing in the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) of the [Catholic Church](606848.md) a [titular bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_bishopric) of Novi (in [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia)), listed as [suffragan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan) of the [Archdiocese of Doclea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Doclea) (which in [Classical times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_times) controlled its region in the [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Dalmatia Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_Superior)).

## Tourism



Mirište Beach.



Statue of Tvrtko I of Bosnia in the downtown harbour of Herceg Novi.

Herceg Novi is a major Montenegrin tourist destination. It is well known as a [spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destination_spa) and health center; nearby Igalo has an abundance of [healing sea mud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_bath) called "igaljsko blato" (Igalo mud) and mineral springs called "igaljske slatine" (Igalo springs). The most famous tourist attractions in Herceg Novi are castle Forte Mare built by the Bosnian king [Tvrtko I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvrtko_I) in 1382, a clock tower built by Austrians in the 19th century, the Kanli tower built by Turks. Other famous attractions include the various ancient [Serbian Orthodox churches and monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church), which include the Church of St. Ilija, the Church of Preobraženja, the Church of St. Đorđa, the Church of the Holy Salvation (St. Spas), the Church of St. Archangel Michael, and the [Savina Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savina_Monastery_\(Montenegro\)).

Whilst the city itself is not a major destination for [sunbathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_tanning), with no long sandy [beaches](40630.md) along the rest of the Bay of Kotor, many beaches are reachable by [boat](3996.md). Tourist companies organise one-day boat trips to [Luštica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C5%A1tica) peninsula, which lies opposite to the town. Popular [Luštica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C5%A1tica) peninsula beach sites include *Žanjic*, *Mirište* and *Rose*. Herceg Novi accounted for one-third of overnight stays in Montenegro before the [Yugoslav wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_wars), but the situation has changed since in favour of [Budva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budva), [Kotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotor) and other resorts.

Local bus station will charge doubtfully legal fee of €2 for entering its platform with printed e-ticket.

## Culture



A walkway in Herceg Novi

"JUK Herceg-Fest" This cultural events center was established on 24 February 1992. Two years later the cultural center joined with the "Orijen" movie distribution and production company. Together they have played a crucial role in enhancing the city's cultural life. Among many annual festivities are the mimosa celebration, local theatrical events, and a film and music fest.

"The [Operosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operosa) Opera Festival" takes place during the summer in the [Kanli Kula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanli_Kula) amphitheater inside the fortress of Stari Grad (Old Town) and in the Forte Mare fortress.

The Herceg Novi City Archive was returned in 1956 the Archive to Herceg Novi after being temporarily relocated to [Zadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadar), Croatia. The Archive was relocated to Zadar by Italians during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). The original archive building, built in 1885, suffered severe damage from the 1979 earthquake. Shortly afterward, the building was renovated. Today, the Archive is 700 m<sup>2</sup> (7,535 sq ft) in size. The Archive features modern equipment and a library open to the public. The oldest document in the Archive originated in 1685. The library contains approximately 30,000 volumes and 1,000 periodicals.

The Historical Museum was established in 1949 and officially opened in 1953. The museum building, a gift to the city from the former mayor, Mirko Komnenović (1870–1941), and his wife Olga, is at least 150 years old.

The City library contains at least 30,000 volumes. Among the contributors to this collection are Dušan Petković (5,000 books), Veljka Radojević (1,500), Doklestić, Daljev, Lučić, Subotić and others.

## Transportation



August 2017 panoramic view of Herceg Novi.

Herceg Novi or Igalo is usually the final destination of buses that come from inland Montenegro and [Serbia](29265.md). The [Adriatic Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Motorway), a two-lane motorway that extends for the length of the Montenegrin coast, goes through Herceg Novi before it merges with the Croatian road network at the [Debeli Brijeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debeli_Brijeg) border crossing.

The ferry operates on the Kamenari – Lepetane line at Verige Strait, eliminating the need to go all the way around Boka Kotorska bay in order to reach Tivat, Kotor, Budva and inland Montenegro.

[Tivat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivat_Airport) is 23 km (14 mi) away (via the ferry). There are regular flights to Belgrade and Zürich, and dozens of charter planes land daily on Tivat airport during the summer season. [Dubrovnik Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik_Airport) in Croatia is some 30 km (19 mi) away, and it maintains regular flights to many European destinations.

## Climate



A pomegranate tree near the bus station in Herceg Novi. September 2018.

The area of the Bay of Kotor has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*) with significantly more rain in the winter than in the summer. Herceg Novi has a specific microclimate, which is a result of southern exposition, proximity to the sea, limestone substratum and mountainous hinterland which prevents the breakthrough of cold air masses.

Herceg Novi has approximately 200 sunny days a year. In July and August there are approximately 11 sunny hours per day. Average annual temperature is 16.2 °C (61.2 °F) (similar to that of [Naples](55880.md) and [Lisbon](18091.md)). There are frequent slight temperature oscillations; the average daily temperature fluctuation is only 4 °C (7.2 °F). Average temperature from May to September is about 25 °C (77 °F), and the average summer sea temperature is rather high, between 22 and 26 °C (72 and 79 °F).

The annual average precipitation is 1,930 mm (76.0 in). Relative air humidity is at its highest level, 80%, in the fall. Its lowest level, 63%, comes in the summer.
