The **Popular Congress Party** (PCP, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حزب المؤتمر الشعبي) is an [Islamist political party in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamism_in_Sudan). The party was founded by [Hassan al-Turabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_al-Turabi).[^cite_note-Turabi-1]

The party emerged from a split within the ruling [National Congress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_Party_\(Sudan\)) (NCP) in 1999, due to differences between Turabi and President [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md).[^cite_note-Turabi-1] The party is one of the most outspoken against the NCP, advocating a popular uprising to overthrow the government.[^cite_note-2] The party relies heavily on displaced [Western Sudanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sudan) living in Khartoum's shanty towns for support.[^cite_note-Turabi-1]

The party is a member of the [National Consensus Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Consensus_Forces) opposition alliance.[^cite_note-3]

## History

Al-Turabi had a falling out with al-Bashir in 1999, when al-Turabi again began to spend time in jail or under [house arrest](247643.md).[^cite_note-loc2015-4] The power struggle between al-Bashir and al-Turabi resulted in al-Turabi’s expulsion from the NCP.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] As a consequence, al-Turabi established the PCP (initially called the Popular National Congress) in August 2000 in opposition to the NCP.[^cite_note-loc2015-4]

After al-Turabi created the PCP, al-Bashir’s security forces regularly harassed its meetings and arrested participants.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] In February 2001, the PCP signed a "memorandum of understanding" in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) with the [Sudan People's Liberation Movement](2691327.md).[^cite_note-loc2015-4] Among other things, the memorandum noted that self-determination is a legitimate right of the people of [South Sudan](32350676.md).[^cite_note-loc2015-4] Al-Turabi saw the memorandum as a way to undermine al-Bashir and improve his own position; it resulted in al-Turabi’s arrest.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] After three months in prison, the government released al-Turabi from prison and put him under house arrest.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] PCP activity virtually came to a halt; al-Bashir dropped charges against PCP supporters near the end of 2001.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] Released from detention, al-Turabi was rearrested in March 2003 on charges of masterminding a coup attempt.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] The government dismissed these charges against al-Turabi and the PCP in December 2004 but continued to keep him in detention and banned party activity.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] Al-Turabi managed, however, to have a significant impact on political developments in Sudan from jail or house arrest and eventually was released.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] There is strong evidence that the PCP established an alliance with the rebel [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) in [Darfur](605837.md) in order to put additional pressure on the government.[^cite_note-loc2015-4] In the flawed [April 2010 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Sudanese_general_election), the PCP candidate for president, [Abdullah Deng Nhial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_Deng_Nhial), a [Muslim](19541.md) from the [Dinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinka_people) tribe, received only 4 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-loc2015-4]

The party newspaper, *Ray al-Shaab*, has been banned since 2012.[^cite_note-5]
