The **2030 FIFA World Cup** is scheduled to be the 24th [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the quadrennial international [football](10568.md) tournament that is contested by the [men's national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams) of the member associations of [FIFA](11049.md). The tournament is planned to be jointly hosted by Morocco, Portugal, and Spain. In honour of the 100th anniversary of the [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) in 1930, a special match and celebration are scheduled to be held at [Estadio Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Centenario) in Montevideo, Uruguay—host stadium of the [1930 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup_final)—as well as one match each in [Estadio Monumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Monumental_\(Buenos_Aires\)) in Buenos Aires, Argentina, and [Estadio Osvaldo Domínguez Dibb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Osvaldo_Dom%C3%ADnguez_Dibb) in Asunción, Paraguay.

The tournament will be the first World Cup held in more than one confederation, the first in Africa since [2010](168079.md), and the first ever in North Africa. It will be the first held in Morocco, Paraguay, and Portugal, and the second in Uruguay, Argentina, and Spain, after 1930, [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) respectively.

[Morocco](1024865.md), [Portugal](576328.md), [Spain](609825.md), [Argentina](454699.md), [Paraguay](804025.md), and [Uruguay](679782.md) all automatically qualified for the tournament as hosts, with Spain becoming the first ever [defending champions](75845879.md) to host the tournament after winning their second World Cup in 2026.

## Possible format and expansion

At the March 2025 [FIFA Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Council) meeting, [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) proposed a one-off 64-team expansion to honour the tournament's centennial anniversary.[^cite_note-6] Later that year, CONMEBOL leaders met directly with FIFA president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) in [New York City](645042.md) to discuss the expansion. In July 2026, Infantino stated that he would consider this expansion.[^cite_note-7] On 20 July 2026, CONMEBOL president [Alejandro Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Dom%C3%ADnguez_\(football_executive\)) announced that the tournament would feature 64 teams in a post on [X](9988187.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] This has not been confirmed by FIFA. Critics claim increasing the number of qualified teams to 64 would devalue the qualification process and reduce competition quality.[^cite_note-10]

## Host selection

Further information: [2030 FIFA World Cup bids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bids), [Morocco–Portugal–Spain 2030 FIFA World Cup bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco%E2%80%93Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bid), [Uruguay–Argentina–Chile–Paraguay 2030 FIFA World Cup bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay%E2%80%93Argentina%E2%80%93Chile%E2%80%93Paraguay_2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bid), and [Bulgaria–Greece–Romania–Serbia UEFA Euro 2028 and 2030 FIFA World Cup bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria%E2%80%93Greece%E2%80%93Romania%E2%80%93Serbia_UEFA_Euro_2028_and_2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bid)

FIFA launched the bidding process in 2022.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Because of the rule preventing countries belonging to confederations that hosted the two preceding tournaments to host the next,[^cite_note-rotation-13] members of [AFC](715249.md) and [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) could not bid to host the 2030 FIFA World Cup[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-telegraph.uk_14_Jan_2017-15]–AFC as hosts of the World Cup in [2022](17742072.md) (Qatar), and CONCACAF as hosts in [2026](36581929.md) (United States, Canada, and Mexico).

During a friendly match between [Portugal](576328.md) and [Spain](609825.md) on 7 October 2020, the [FPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Football_Federation) and [RFEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) announced their intentions to produce a joint bid for the tournament.[^cite_note-16] Almost two years later, on 5 October 2022, the federations held a joint press conference alongside the [Ukrainian Association of Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Association_of_Football) president [Andriy Pavelko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Pavelko). On 29 November, The Guardian reported the Ukrainian part of the bid was likely dead due to Pavelko's arrest on suspicion of [embezzlement](209411.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The [Royal Moroccan Football Federation](1905329.md)–failing to secure hosting rights in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts#1994_FIFA_World_Cup), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts#1998_FIFA_World_Cup), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts#2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts#2010_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_2026_FIFA_World_Cup_bid)–bid for the sixth time.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] On 14 March 2023, King [Mohammed VI](226113.md) announced that [Morocco](1024865.md) would join the Portuguese–Spanish bid.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

On 11 December 2024, FIFA confirmed the 2030 World Cup would be jointly hosted by Morocco, Portugal, and Spain. During an extraordinary FIFA Congress meeting, this announcement was made alongside the decision to award the [2034 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_FIFA_World_Cup) to Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-23]

#### Format changes

Two regional confederations have announced changes to their qualifying format for this tournament:

##### CONCACAF

On 6 February 2026, [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) announced their qualifying format for the 2030 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-36]

- Round 1: The 22 teams ranked 14–35 in the CONCACAF rankings will be drawn into pairs and play a home-and-away matchup during the first half of the September–October 2027 international window with the 11 winners advancing to Round 2.

- Round 2: The top 13 ranked associations in CONCACAF will be joined by the 11 winners from the Round 1 to create six groups of four teams with each facing each other home-and-away. The top two from each group advance to the final round. Round 2 will begin during the second half of the September–October 2027 international window with later matches in November 2027 and March 2028.

- Final round: The 12 teams that advanced from Round 2 will be divided up into three groups of four with each team facing each other home-and-away. The top two teams in each group will automatically qualify for the World Cup while the two better third-placed finishers will qualify for the play-in. The final round will be played in June 2028 and in the 2029 September–October international window.

- Play-in: The two teams will play each other home-and-away with the aggregate winner qualifying for the FIFA intercontinental play-offs. The two matches will take place in November 2029.

##### UEFA

On 20 May 2026, UEFA announced a new qualification format mirroring both the [Champions League](44220.md) and [Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League). A two-tier qualification process will be created where major teams will no longer play minor teams to remove uncompetitive games. Under the new rules, the 36 highest-ranked countries—as determined by the [2028–29 Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028%E2%80%9329_UEFA_Nations_League)—will be drawn into three groups of 12 in League 1. Teams in those groups would each play six games against six different opponents and be ranked in a 12-team league table. The best-ranked teams of each group of League 1 will qualify for the World Cup, with the remaining places allocated via play-offs. The remaining 18 lower-ranked countries will take part in League 2, split into three groups. All teams in both Leagues will play six home-and-away matches and the system of play-offs will involve teams from both Leagues—those who fall short of direct qualification in League 1 along with top teams from League 2. More details are expected to be released in late 2026 or early 2027.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Controversies

### Manipulation of Spanish venues

On 23 March 2025, it was reported that the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) manipulated the classification scores when choosing the venues in order to exclude [Balaídos Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala%C3%ADdos_Stadium) and include [Anoeta Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoeta_Stadium).[^cite_note-61]

### Inclusion of centenary match hosts

With the FIFA rotation system, which prevents continental associations from hosting consecutive or near-consecutive World Cups,[^cite_note-rotation-13] [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) (which won the rights to host the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md)), [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL), [UEFA](32332.md), and [CAF](715265.md) were all unable to bid for the [2034](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_FIFA_World_Cup), leaving it open only for the [AFC](715249.md) and [OFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation). This led to accusations that FIFA intentionally selected these countries, especially the centenary match hosts in the CONMEBOL region, to ensure that [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) would win [its bid for the 2034 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_2034_FIFA_World_Cup_bid) unopposed.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

### Animal welfare concerns

Animal rights organisations have accused Morocco of killing stray dogs ahead of its co-hosting of the FIFA World Cup. These groups estimate the stray dog population at 3 million and have alleged that methods used include poisoning and shooting.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-newsCull-65]

In response, Moroccan authorities adopted Law 19-25 in 2025, which established a legal framework for the management of stray animals. The law introduced measures such as sterilisation, vaccination, identification, and the use of shelters, and restricted the killing of stray animals.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

### Moroccan occupation of Western Sahara

[Western Sahara](33220.md) is a non-self-governing territory that has been [under Moroccan military occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara) since 1975. Analysts expect that Morocco will use the World Cup to bolster its claim over Western Sahara,[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] such as through [sportswashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportswashing).[^cite_note-71] Morocco initially planned to build a stadium in [Dakhla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakhla,_Western_Sahara) for the World Cup,[^cite_note-72] though ultimately no matches will take place in the territory.[^cite_note-ElInd-73] Morocco regularly expels journalists who bring attention towards the occupation.[^cite_note-74] It is doubling its green energy production in the territory to meet its own demands for the World Cup.[^cite_note-75]

In their 2030 bid document, Morocco, Spain, and Portugal included a map that depicted Western Sahara as Moroccan territory; FIFA's bid evaluation report did not reproduce this map.[^cite_note-ElInd-73]

### Gen Z 212 protests

Main article: [2025 Moroccan Gen Z protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Moroccan_Gen_Z_protests)

In late September 2025, a series of protests erupted in several cities in Morocco led by the youth movement known as Gen Z 212.[^cite_note-\:15-76][^cite_note-77] The protests were sparked by the deterioration of the country's health and education systems and the government's excessive spending on sports infrastructure in preparation for hosting the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md) and the upcoming 2030 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-CNN-78][^cite_note-AlJazeera-79][^cite_note-Reuters-80][^cite_note-\:11-81]

### UEFA boycott

On 30 July 2026, following FIFA's announcement of [FIFA Forward Enterprise](83820435.md) (a proposal to sell stakes in FIFA's tournament operations, including for the World Cup, to private investors), all 55 [UEFA](32332.md) member associations agreed on an indefinite boycott of FIFA competitions until the proposal was shelved and assurances were made that such a proposal would not be tabulated again. Two of the 2030 FIFA World Cup hosts – Spain and Portugal – are involved in the boycott.[^cite_note-FFEBBC-82][^cite_note-83] On 31 July, FIFA scrapped the proposal,[^cite_note-84] although UEFA stated that, regardless, it no longer trusts FIFA's governance.[^cite_note-85] After receiving written assurances from FIFA that similar plans would not be explored in the future, UEFA ended the boycott on 26 August.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

### Calls for Morocco's removal as a host

On 5 August 2026, Portuguese left-wing political party [LIVRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIVRE) called for Morocco to be removed from hosting the 2030 FIFA World Cup following the [Ceuta migration crisis](83824713.md). The party urged the Portuguese and Spanish governments, as well as the [European Union](9317.md) and [UEFA](32332.md), to support a hosting arrangement involving only Portugal and Spain, citing Morocco's handling of migration at the [Ceuta](6443.md) border and broader human rights concerns.[^cite_note-88] LIVRE spokesperson [Jorge Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Pinto) described the crisis as "the last straw" and questioned FIFA's governance and the transparency of its host selection process. The party had also previously criticized Morocco's involvement in the bid over its policies regarding Western Sahara.[^cite_note-89] On the same day, Spanish left-wing political party and member of the Spanish government [Sumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumar_\(electoral_platform\)) registered a non-binding motion calling on the government to discuss with the Spanish Royal Football Federation the need to "review and reevaluate" the current joint organization model with Morocco amid criticism over "grave breaches" on international agreements and human rights violations.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] Later, far-left party [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_\(Spanish_political_party\)) echoed the call to exclude Morocco from the organization after stating that the country "attacked Spain’s sovereignty".[^cite_note-92] On 6 August, far-right party [Vox](41928488.md) submitted another non-binding motion asking for the same.[^cite_note-93]
