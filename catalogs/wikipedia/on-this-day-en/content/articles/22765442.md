**Ilham Heydar oghlu Aliyev**[^cite_note-2] (born 24 December 1961) is an Azerbaijani politician and statesman who has been the fourth [president of Azerbaijan](938372.md) since 2003. He is the son of the previous president, [Heydar Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev), and has led the [New Azerbaijan Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Azerbaijan_Party) since 2005.

Aliyev briefly served as [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Azerbaijan) for his father before being elected to office in [a presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Azerbaijani_presidential_election) defined by irregularities, shortly before his father's death. He was re-elected for a second term in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Azerbaijani_presidential_election) and was allowed to run in elections indefinitely after the [2009 constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Azerbaijani_constitutional_referendum) removed presidential [term limits](505825.md), winning subsequent elections in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Azerbaijani_presidential_election), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Azerbaijani_presidential_election) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Azerbaijani_presidential_election). His wife, [Mehriban Aliyeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehriban_Aliyeva), has been [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Azerbaijan) since 2017.

[Azerbaijan](746.md)'s oil-rich territory has significantly strengthened the stability of Aliyev's regime and enriched the nation's ruling elite, enabling it to host lavish international events, as well as engage in extensive [lobbying efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caviar_diplomacy). Aliyev's family has also enriched itself through ties to state-run businesses. They hold significant stakes in several major Azerbaijani banks, construction and telecommunications companies, as well as in the country’s oil and gas sector. In 2012, Aliyev was named the "Person of the Year" by [Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project) for featuring prominently in stories on crime and corruption.[^cite_note-\:2-3] In 2017, it was revealed that Aliyev and his family were involved in the [Azerbaijani laundromat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_laundromat), a complex [money-laundering](19390.md) scheme to pay off prominent European politicians to deflect criticism of Aliyev and promote a positive image of his regime.

Aliyev is widely considered a [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator),[^cite_note-10] presiding over an authoritarian government characterized by the absence of free and fair elections, pervasive corruption, and serious [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Azerbaijan) violations. Aliyev’s regime is also known for promoting [Sunnification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnification), and the suppression of [Shia Islam](26961.md) to tighten control, and align with Turkey.[^cite_note-Ahmed_2014,_p._87-11][^cite_note-12] His regime is known for the [harassment of journalists](43480308.md) and [non-governmental organizations](46539.md), as well as the [ethnic cleansing of the Armenians of Nagorno-Karabakh](74922032.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-cbc1-14] During Aliyev's presidency, the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict escalated into a [full-fledged war in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) and — following a [military siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) and renewed [offensive](74857564.md) in 2023 — [expelled all Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh](74922032.md) and reincorporated the territory into Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-15]

## Early life and career

Ilham Aliyev is the son of [Heydar Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev), president of Azerbaijan from 1993 to 2003.[^cite_note-HRW_AZ_pres_elec2003-16] His mother [Zarifa Aliyeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarifa_Aliyeva) was an Azerbaijani [ophthalmologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophthalmology). He also has an older sister, [Sevil Aliyeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevil_Aliyeva).[^cite_note-17] In 1977, Ilham Aliyev entered the [Moscow State Institute of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Institute_of_International_Relations) (MGIMO-MSIIR) and in 1982, continued his education as a [postgraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate).[^cite_note-Pres-18] In 1985, he received a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) degree in history.[^cite_note-Pres-18] From 1985 to 1990 Aliyev lectured at MSIIR.[^cite_note-Pres-18] From 1991 to 1994, he led a group of private industrial-commercial enterprises.[^cite_note-\:1-19] In 1994–2003, he was vice-president, and later the first vice-president of [SOCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAR), the [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned) [Azerbaijani oil and gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Azerbaijan) company.[^cite_note-\:1-19] Since 1997, Aliyev has been the president of the [National Olympic Committee of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee_of_the_Republic_of_Azerbaijan).[^cite_note-\:1-19][^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-20]

## Early political career

In 1999, Ilham Aliyev was elected as the deputy chair of the ruling [New Azerbaijan Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Azerbaijan_Party) and in 2001, he was elected to the post of first deputy chair at the Second Congress of the Party.[^cite_note-Chair_NewAzParty-21] At the third Congress of the New Azerbaijan Party held on March 26, 2005, President Aliyev and the first deputy of the Party was unanimously elected to the post of chairman of the Party. The fourth and fifth congresses of the party held in 2008 and 2013 unanimously supported his nomination for the next presidential term.[^cite_note-22] In 1995, Aliyev was elected to the [Parliament of the Republic of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Azerbaijan\)); later he became president of the National Olympic Committee (still incumbent). From 2001 to 2003, Aliyev was head of the Azerbaijani delegation to the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) (PACE).[^cite_note-\:1-19][^cite_note-23]

### Prime Minister of Azerbaijan (2003)

In 2002, a [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Azerbaijani_constitutional_referendum) in Azerbaijan made the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Azerbaijan) next in line for the presidency if the president became incapacitated. By early 2003, Heydar Aliyev's health had significantly declined, fueling speculation about his ability to continue serving office.[^cite_note-24]

On 4 August 2003, Ilham Aliyev, his son, was appointed prime minister amidst his Heydar's illness, absence from public life, and rumours of his death, a move that many saw as preparing him to eventually succeed his father as president.[^cite_note-HRW_AZ_pres_elec2003-16][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] After his appointment, Ilham Aliyev dismissed the rumours about his Heydar's death, emphasizing his father's recovery in a televised address.

Ilham Aliyev's appointment as prime minister was confirmed by a National Assembly vote, with 101 out of 125 deputies supporting his nomination. However, opposition parties boycotted the vote, expressing concerns that this move was part of an effort to establish a political dynasty and limit democratic processes. They argued that Ilham's appointment was an attempt to maintain power within the Aliyev family, bypassing a more open and competitive political system.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

During his brief tenure as prime minister, Ilham Aliyev focused on continuing policies implemented by Heydar, particularly in terms of Azerbaijan's economy and foreign relations. He assured both the public and international community that there would be no significant changes in government direction. Despite this, the opposition criticized the appointment, calling it an "undemocratic power grab". Observers also pointed to the ongoing concentration of power within the ruling elite as a concern for Azerbaijan's political future.[^cite_note-30]

Ilham Aliyev sought for the presidency in the [October 2003 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Azerbaijani_presidential_election), but Azerbaijan's election law prohibited him from doing so while serving as prime minister. As a result, he remained in the position, but his duties were temporarily assumed by [Artur Rasizade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Rasizade), who became acting prime minister following Aliyev's leave of absence on August 6, 2003.[^cite_note-31] This arrangement allowed Aliyev to focus on his presidential campaign while retaining his position as prime minister until the election.[^cite_note-32]

## President of Azerbaijan (2003–present)

### First term (2003–2008)

Main article: [2003 Azerbaijani presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Azerbaijani_presidential_election)



President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) welcomes President Ilham Aliyev

The official results of the October 15, 2003 elections gave victory to Ilham Aliyev, who earned 76.84% of the votes.[^cite_note-33] The election was defined by election fraud.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-HRW_AZ_pres_elec2003_full_pdf-35][^cite_note-IDEE_AZ_preselec2003-36][^cite_note-HRW_AZ_pres_elec2003-16] [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) and the [Institute for Democracy in Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_in_Support_of_Solidarity) documented arrests of opposition candidates, police violence against journalists and participants in election rallies, and "widespread fraud and abuse" in the conduct of the election itself.[^cite_note-HRW_AZ_pres_elec2003_full_pdf-35][^cite_note-IDEE_AZ_preselec2003-36] Despite these allegations, Aliyev was inaugurated as President of Azerbaijan on 31 October 2003. Following his inauguration, Aliyev appointed [Artur Rasizade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Rasizade) as Prime Minister, whose nomination was approved by the parliament on 4 November 2003. Rasizade had previously held the position of Prime Minister under Aliyev's father, Heydar Aliyev, and continued to serve in an acting capacity.

Aliyev worked to consolidate his power both politically and economically. One of his first major actions was the creation of the "Foundation for Combating Corruption in Azerbaijan" in 2004. This initiative was aimed at addressing widespread corruption within government institutions and improving public sector accountability. Despite the establishment of this foundation, critics argued that the reforms were insufficient, and corruption remained prevalent within the ruling elite.

In the energy sector, Aliyev focused on solidifying Azerbaijan's role as an important player in the global oil market. A key milestone in his first term was the completion of the [Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline](397567.md) in May 2005.[^cite_note-37] This major infrastructure project allowed Azerbaijan to export oil to international markets, particularly to Europe and the United States, and significantly boosted the country's economic profile. The pipeline further reinforced Azerbaijan's strategic importance as an energy supplier, linking the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) region to the global market while bypassing Russia and Iran.

Politically, Aliyev faced significant challenges from opposition groups, particularly in the [2005 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Azerbaijani_parliamentary_election). The elections were marred by widespread allegations of fraud, electoral malpractice, and the restriction of opposition parties’ activities.[^cite_note-38] Despite the opposition's gains in certain regions, the elections were widely considered unfair. Protests erupted in response to the results, with demonstrators accusing the government of rigging the vote.[^cite_note-39] However, the protests were quickly suppressed by the government's security forces, and many opposition leaders were arrested. Aliyev's government responded by tightening control over the media and cracking down on dissent.[^cite_note-40]

### Second term (2008–2013)

Main article: [2008 Azerbaijani presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Azerbaijani_presidential_election)

Ilham Aliyev was [re-elected in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Azerbaijani_presidential_election) with 87% of the vote. Seven candidates registered to run, each required to gather 40,000 voter signatures.[^cite_note-41] According to the European Parliament Election Observation Delegation, the election was conducted without major unrest and with only minor procedural issues. The delegation acknowledged reforms to the Electoral Code, such as the use of ink to prevent repeat voting, improved transparency in voter lists, and legal provisions limiting government involvement in the electoral process.[^cite_note-42] During the 2008 election, the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) (PACE) included members with differing views. A draft statement by delegation head Andreas Herkel, which contained critical remarks, was not adopted after opposition from several members including Michael Hancock, Eduard Lintner, and Paul Ville. Herkel reportedly offered to resign if the delegation’s findings were not fully reflected.

In March 2009, a [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Azerbaijani_constitutional_referendum) under Aliyev's administration removed presidential term limits, allowing him to seek re-election indefinitely. This move was praised by some PACE delegates as a step towards democratic development but raised serious concerns from the Council of Europe’s [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Commission) and the European Commission about the potential consolidation of executive power and erosion of democratic safeguards.[^cite_note-VULNERABLE-43] The referendum was conducted without broad public consultation, drawing criticism from opposition parties who argued it violated both domestic constitutional norms and Azerbaijan’s international commitments.[^cite_note-44]

During Aliyev's second term, Azerbaijan experienced significant [pro-democracy protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Azerbaijani_protests) in 2011, as citizens demanded political reforms, economic changes, the release of political detainees and resignation of Aliyev.[^cite_note-45] The government responded with force, dispersing protests and detaining hundreds, which prompted condemnation from numerous international human rights organizations for restricting peaceful assembly and curtailing media freedoms.[^cite_note-46]

Economically, Azerbaijan’s growth during this period remained heavily dependent on its oil and gas sector. However, concerns were raised by [Global Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Witness) and other watchdogs about governance and transparency in the energy sector, including allegations of mismanagement of revenues and the use of offshore financial mechanisms.[^cite_note-47]

### 2013 election

Main articles: [2013 Azerbaijani presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Azerbaijani_presidential_election) and [caviar diplomacy § 2013 Presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caviar_diplomacy#2013_Presidential_elections)

In the [2013 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Azerbaijani_presidential_election) held on 9 October, Aliyev claimed victory with 85 percent of the vote, securing a third five-year term.[^cite_note-48] The election results were accidentally released before the polls opened.[^cite_note-49] Election observation delegations from the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe and the European Parliament claimed to have observed a free, fair and transparent electoral process with no evidence of voter intimidation.[^cite_note-50] A day before voting began, however, the Central Election Commission released a new smartphone application intended to allow citizens to watch the ballot counting in real time, and instead the app accidentally showed the results of the election before the election had taken place.[^cite_note-51] The Central Election Commission tried to justify the incident by saying that the initially displayed results were those of the 2008 election, even though the candidates listed, including [Jamil Hasanli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamil_Hasanli) in second place, were from the 2013 ballot.[^cite_note-wapo_oops-52] Aliyev's main rivals in the election were Jamil Hasanli and Iqbal Agazade. Observers from the [OSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSCE) / [ODIHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ODIHR) spoke of restrictions on freedom of speech during elections.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The US State Department described the elections as not meeting international standards, and expressing solidarity with the ODIHR's assessment.[^cite_note-55] In 2013, [Amnesty International](18947898.md) called on western leaders to speak up against the arrest of [Anar Mammadli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anar_Mammadli), prominent human rights activist and head of the independent [Election monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_monitoring) and Democracy Studies Centre, who was falsely charged with tax evasion and illegal business activity, after his organisation had reported widespread violations during the election.[^cite_note-Dictator-56] There was a controversy over election observers who had allegedly been paid by the Azerbaijani regime through the [Azerbaijani laundromat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_laundromat) scandal. A German former lawmaker [Eduard Lintner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Lintner) led a mission that claimed that the elections were up to "German standards"; however, Lintner's group had been paid 819,500 euros through the laundromat money laundering scheme.[^cite_note-\:15-57][^cite_note-58] According to the [OCCRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project), there is "ample evidence of its connection to the family of President Aliyev."[^cite_note-\:15-57]

Economically, Aliyev's third term was significantly affected by a [sharp decline in global oil prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010s_oil_glut) beginning in 2014, severely impacting Azerbaijan's oil-dependent economy.[^cite_note-59] The resulting drop in government revenues contributed to inflation and depreciation of the national currency, the [manat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_manat).[^cite_note-60] In response, the government pursued diversification efforts, targeting agriculture, tourism, and information technology sectors, though hydrocarbons remained the dominant economic force.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Notably, the [Southern Gas Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Gas_Corridor) project advanced substantially during this period, aiming to boost Azerbaijan's role as an energy supplier to Europe through pipelines such as TANAP and TAP.[^cite_note-63]

Politically, the Aliyev administration maintained a firm grip on power, continuing to restrict political freedoms and civil society. Opposition parties faced repression, with numerous arrests of journalists, activists, and dissidents. While the government justified these measures as necessary to ensure stability and combat corruption, international watchdogs like Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International criticized these practices as severe setbacks to democratic development.\[11]\[12]

Social unrest surfaced intermittently, including [protests in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Azerbaijani_protests) across Baku and other cities, calling for political reform and improved economic conditions. These demonstrations were met with police crackdowns. Labour strikes and protests over unpaid wages and poor working conditions further highlighted underlying social and economic grievances.[^cite_note-64]

The longstanding [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](4695860.md) with Armenia remained unresolved. Despite ongoing negotiations under the [OSCE Minsk Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSCE_Minsk_Group), ceasefire violations persisted, culminating in a notable escalation in April 2016 known as the [Four-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nagorno-Karabakh_conflict). Azerbaijani forces sought to regain territory, leading to significant casualties before a ceasefire was reinstated.[^cite_note-65] Aliyev consistently emphasized Azerbaijan's territorial integrity and advocated for a peaceful resolution respecting its sovereignty.[^cite_note-66]

### 2018 election



Aliyev celebrates victory in 2018 elections.

Main article: [2018 Azerbaijani presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Azerbaijani_presidential_election)

Ilham Aliyev got 86.02% of votes in the 2018 presidential election.[^cite_note-67] Major opposition parties did not participate in the election,[^cite_note-68] and evidence indicates that the election was fraudulent.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-observation-70]

In January 2019, thousands rallied in Baku calling for the release of political prisoners like blogger Mehman Huseynov, in a permitted protest organized by the National Council of Democratic Forces. Protests escalated in October 2019, with peaceful demonstrations on the 8th, 19th, and 20th triggered by demands for fair elections, economic justice, and greater political freedoms. Police forcibly dispersed crowds, detained approximately 60 protesters including opposition leader Ali Karimli, and cracked down on women's rights activists on 20 October.

In 2020, amid the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the government enacted emergency measures that critics said were used to suppress dissent and limit civil liberties. Later that year, Azerbaijani forces regained territories in the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War), followed by a high‑profile Victory Parade in December, attended by Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan), and signaling strengthened ties with Ankara Crisis Group. Throughout his term, Aliyev pursued economic diversification, expanding agriculture, tourism, logistics, and small‑business sectors, while major energy infrastructure projects like the Southern Gas Corridor continued as core state priorities Wikipediahrw\.org.

From late 2023 into 2024, authorities intensified repression of independent media. At least six journalists from outlets such as [Abzas Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abzas_Media) and [Toplum TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toplum_TV?action=edit\&redlink=1) were arrested on smuggling or tax‑related charges, widely viewed as politically motivated. Journalist trials and detention conditions drew international condemnation, including allegations of ill‑treatment and torture.

### Nagorno-Karabakh

As president, Aliyev has presided over two conflicts involving the region of [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md). The [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) in 2020 resulted in a ceasefire agreement that left Azerbaijani forces in control of significant portions of the territory which had been previously held by ethnic Armenians and the Republic of Armenia.[^cite_note-71] Another [offensive](74857564.md) in 2023 resulted in the entire territory coming under Azerbaijani control.[^cite_note-72]

### Economic policy

investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) outside the [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) sector.[^cite_note-Azer25-140] Persistent pledges by Aliyev gas.[^cite_note-BBCOil-145] The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) has urged Azerbaijan to diversify its economy.[^cite_note-BagirovaAntidze-146] Efforts to economically liberalize were inhibited ### Sunnification efforts

The secular government of Azerbaijan officially promoted Turkish-influenced Sufism over both Iranian-influenced Shia Islam and Arab-influenced Salafism, but maintained vigilance over religious activities of any sect.[^cite_note-Ahmed_2014,_p._87-11] Many Turkish missionaries in Azerbaijan directly worked for the Turkish government religious institutions.[^cite_note-148] There was another rise in conversions to Sunni Islam after the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war, which was seen as a symbol of growing closeness with Turkey.[^cite_note-149]

When Azerbaijani Sunnis became a significant minority, many traditional Shia clerics accused Azerbaijani Sunnis of having shamelessly sold their ancestral religion merely for Turkish approval and only symbolically converting without actually believing in it.[^cite_note-150] Many Azerbaijani Shia clerics were pressured into political quietism or pragmatic collaboration with pro-state religious institutions.[^cite_note-151] Many religious Shia Azerbaijanis criticized the rise of Sunni Islam as a betrayal of the historically Shia identity of Azerbaijan and as a means of submission to Turkey after the Ottoman-Safavid conflict which lasted centuries. In 2018, an Azerbaijani Shia cleric stated that they were "trading Karbala for Ankara".[^cite_note-152]

### Wealth and corruption

The Aliyev family have enriched themselves through their ties to state-run businesses. They own significant parts of several major Azerbaijani banks, construction firms and telecommunications firms, as well as partially own the country's oil and gas industries. Much of the wealth is hidden in offshore companies.[^cite_note-\:16-153] The 2021 [Pandora Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandora_Papers) leaks showed that the Aliyev family built a vast offshore network to hide their money. The family and their close associates have secretly been involved in property deals in the UK worth more than £400 million.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]



Aliyev family in 2018

Andrew Higgins, writing in *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*, stated in 2010 that Azerbaijanis with the same names as Ilham Aliyev's three children owned real estate in [Dubai](211583.md) worth about \$75 million. Higgins stated that some members of the family are indeed wealthy, such as the president's older daughter, Leyla, married to Emin Agalarov, a Russian billionaire, and relatives of the first lady who have businesses in Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-wapo_dubai_deals-157]

In 2012, the [Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project) called Ilham Aliyev the person of the year in organized crime and corruption.[^cite_note-\:2-3] Also in 2012, [CNBC](223441.md) produced the film *Filthy Rich* about corruption which also mentioned the Aliyev family.[^cite_note-158]

According to a 2013 investigation by the [International Consortium of Investigative Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Consortium_of_Investigative_Journalists) (ICIJ), the Aliyev family owned at least four offshore companies directly connected with Hassan Gozal.[^cite_note-159] ICIJ stated that family members never declared the Aliyevs' offshore companies, that Ilham and Mehriban Aliyevs had no legal right to open offshore companies, and that when these companies were opened, measures were taken to conceal the real owners. ### Lobbying and money laundering

In 2017, the Aliyev family was implicated in the [Azerbaijani laundromat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_laundromat) scandal, which was a complex [money-laundering](19390.md) scheme to pay off prominent European politicians to deflect criticism of Aliyev and promote a positive image of his regime.[^cite_note-\:11-74][^cite_note-\:13-75] Much of Aliyev's and his families wealth is hidden through an elaborate network of offshore companies.

### Authoritarianism

Aliyev rules an [authoritarian regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) in Azerbaijan,[^cite_note-173] as elections are not free and fair, power is concentrated in the hands of Aliyev and his extended family, corruption is rampant, and [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Azerbaijan) violations are severe (which included [torture](47702.md),[^cite_note-\:7-174][^cite_note-Musicians-175] [arbitrary arrests](13435705.md), as well as harassment of journalists and [non-governmental organizations](46539.md)).[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-\:8-178][^cite_note-\:5-126][^cite_note-\:9-179][^cite_note-\:10-180][^cite_note-hrw2013-181][^cite_note-NYT_Ilham_Aliyev_profile-131] Many observers see Aliyev as a dictator.[^cite_note-auto2-4][^cite_note-auto1-5][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

#### Human rights violations

[Human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Azerbaijan) violations in Azerbaijan during Aliyev's presidency include [torture](47702.md),[^cite_note-Musicians-175] [arbitrary arrests](13435705.md) and harassment of journalists and [non-governmental organizations](46539.md).[^cite_note-hrw2013-181]

#### Suppression of opposition parties and free press

In a speech delivered on 15 July 2020, during the [2020 Armenian–Azerbaijani clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Armenian%E2%80%93Azerbaijani_clashes), President Aliyev targeted the largest opposition party, the [Popular Front Party of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Popular_Front_Party). He declared that "we need to finish with the '[fifth column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_column)" and the Popular Front "worse than the Armenians". According to Azerbaijani sources as many as 120 people are currently held in jail, including some deputy leaders of the party as well as journalists. On 20 July the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) urged Azerbaijan to avoid using the pandemic to silence "civil society advocacy, opposition voices, or public discussion".[^cite_note-186] These actions are widely seen as an attempt "to eliminate pro-democracy advocates and political rivals once and for all". According to [RFE/RL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFE/RL), "Aliyev's authoritarian rule has shut down independent media outlets and suppressed opposition parties while holding elections deemed neither free nor fair by international monitoring groups".[^cite_note-187]

According to [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md), Aliyev launched "a new wave of fierce repression against the country’s last remaining journalists" in late 2023.[^cite_note-188] Journalists from the independent [Abzas Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abzas_Media), Toplum TV, and [Meydan TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meydan_TV) were prosecuted in 2024 and 2025 in trials that international human rights organizations described as unfair.[^cite_note-189] The long prison sentences for seven journalists from Abzas Media are widely seen as retaliation for the outlet's investigations into corruption in the family of Ilham Aliyev and his inner circle.[^cite_note-190]

### Ramil Safarov repatriation

Main article: [Ramil Safarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramil_Safarov)

In 2012, Aliyev convinced the government of [Hungary](13275.md) to transfer convicted murderer [Ramil Safarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramil_Safarov) to Azerbaijan to complete the rest of his prison term. While attending a NATO-sponsored English-language course in Hungary, Safarov had murdered an Armenian lieutenant, [Gurgen Margaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgen_Margaryan), who was also taking the course, while Margaryan was asleep. Safarov admitted that he committed the crime and surrendered himself to the police. Safarov has justified the act based over the [Naghorno Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Republic) conflict between the two countries from 1988 to 1994.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] Safarov had been tried and sentenced to life imprisonment in Hungary. However, after being extradited to Azerbaijan, Safarov received a hero's welcome, promoted to the rank of major, and given an apartment and over eight years of back pay, covering the time he had spent in jail.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194]

### Statements about conflict with Armenians

See also: [Western Azerbaijan (irredentist concept)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Azerbaijan_\(irredentist_concept\))



Aliyev in a speech on 4 October 2020, during the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) between Armenia, Artsakh and Azerbaijan

While in office, Aliyev has made numerous [anti-Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment) statements,[^cite_note-195] and claiming historically Armenian territory for Azerbaijan. Shortly after thousands of Azerbaijani people gathered to commemorate the [Khojaly massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khojaly_massacre), in which Azerbaijan claims that 613 Azerbaijanis had been massacred by the Armenian irregular forces and the [366th CIS regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/366th_Guards_Motor_Rifle_Regiment) of the Soviet Union 20 years earlier, Human Rights Watch claims that 200+ Azerbaijanis were massacred, Aliyev posted on his official website: "Our main enemies are [Armenians of the world](214413.md) and the hypocritical and corrupt politicians that they control".[^cite_note-Enemy_1-196][^cite_note-Enemy_2-197] During his speech, Ilham Aliyev noted: "I once said that the Armenian people should not be afraid of us, they should be afraid of their own leadership".[^cite_note-198]

In 2008, Aliyev declared that "[Nagorno Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Republic) will not be independent; the position is backed by international mediators as well; Armenia has to accept the reality", and also stated that "in 1918, [Yerevan](34352.md) was granted to the [Armenians](387816.md). It was a great mistake. The [khanate of Iravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_khanate) was the [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis) territory, the Armenians were guests there."[^cite_note-regnum.ru-199] President Ilham Aliyev stated, "the occupation of the territory of the sovereign State with its internationally recognized boundaries – our territorial integrity is recognized by the United Nation and has been left out of due attention of the international community. All these facts are the ever seen injustice."[^cite_note-\:3-200] "No project can be carried out in the Armenian occupied Azerbaijani territories without the consent and participation of Azerbaijan".[^cite_note-201]



Aliyev, Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) and Pakistani Prime Minister [Shehbaz Sharif](6502561.md) in [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert), Nagorno-Karabakh, July 2025

Aliyev said that "Armenians living in Karabakh must either accept Azerbaijani citizenship or look for another place to live"[^cite_note-\:26-202][^cite_note-\:27-203][^cite_note-\:662-204] adding that "I am sure that the majority of the Armenian population living in Karabakh today is ready to accept Azerbaijani citizenship. It's just that these leeches, these wild animals, the separatists \[referring to the de facto Republic of Artsakh representatives] don't allow it."[^cite_note-\:662-204][^cite_note-205] Aliyev's irredentist statements have drawn criticism from EU officials and human rights organizations specializing in genocide studies.[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-\:32-207][^cite_note-Genocide_Warning:_Nagorno_Karabakh-208][^cite_note-\:162-209][^cite_note-\:91-210][^cite_note-\:92-211][^cite_note-\:93-212][^cite_note-\:94-213][^cite_note-214]

During a speech on 10 November 2020, Aliyev directly referred to [Prime Minister of Armenia](483914.md) [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) by mockingly saying *Nə oldu Paşinyan?* ("What happened Pashinyan?"), which became an Internet meme in Azerbaijan and Turkey.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216] In 2023, experts in genocide prevention stated that Azerbaijan's [blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) and sabotage of public infrastructure constitutes genocide according to the [Genocide Convention](143988.md): "Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction" and that there are various indicators that Azerbaijan possesses [genocidal intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocidal_intent): President [Aliyev's public statements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan#Official_statements), his regime's [openly Armenophobic practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan#Destruction_of_cultural_heritage) and noncompliance with the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) orders to end the blockade.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

#### Denying Armenia's right to exist

See also: [Falsification of history in Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsification_of_history_in_Azerbaijan) and [Anti-Armenian sentiment in Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan)

Aliyev has repeatedly made substantial territorial claims over Armenia proper.[^cite_note-Rubin21-220] In 2012 he stated that "Armenia as a country is of no value. It is actually a colony, an outpost run from abroad, a territory artificially created on ancient Azerbaijani lands."[^cite_note-Rubin21-220] In 2013 he defined much of Armenia as "historical lands" of the [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis), promising them that they will live there "in the future", including in "Irevan and Zangezur regions", to which he sometimes adds "Geicha", [Meghri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghri), and [Goris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goris).[^cite_note-Rubin21-220] The first three of these are irredentist terms by which he means the Armenian capital [Yerevan](34352.md), the southern [Syunik Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province), and [Lake Sevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sevan) in [Gegharkunik Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gegharkunik_Province), all of them in Armenia, while the latter two are towns in Syunik Province. In 2018, when he became the [New Azerbaijan Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Azerbaijan_Party)'s presidential candidate, he repeated his call for "the return of Azerbaijanis to these lands" and declared this to be "our political and strategic goal, and we must gradually approach it."[^cite_note-221] In December 2016, he clarified that he does not "intend to reclaim \[these lands] ... through military force" in the immediate future, but that the "time will come" when "we, Azerbaijanis, will return to all our historic lands", wherefore "\[t]he main factor \[for success] is strength... a more powerful army."[^cite_note-Rubin21-220]
