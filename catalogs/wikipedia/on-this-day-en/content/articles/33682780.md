The rights of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, and queer ([LGBTQ](78174325.md)) people are protected under the [European Union](9317.md)'s (EU) [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_European_Union) and [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law). Same-sex sexual activity is legal in all [EU member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_member_states) and discrimination in employment has been banned since 2000. However, EU states have different laws when it comes to any greater protection, [same-sex civil union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union), [same-sex marriage](92656.md), and [adoption by same-sex couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_adoption). On 25 November 2025, the European Court of Justice (ECJ) ruled that member states must recognise same-sex marriages lawfully concluded in another member state, even though they are not obliged to legalise same-sex marriage domestically.[^cite_note-1]

## Treaty protections

The [Treaty on European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_European_Union), in its last version as updated by the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) in 2007 and in force as of 2009, makes the [Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_of_the_European_Union) legally binding for the [member states of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_European_Union) when applying EU law, and the European Union itself. In turn, [Article 21](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Charter%20of%20Fundamental%20Rights%20of%20the%20European%20Union#CHAPTER_III._EQUALITY) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union includes an anti-discrimination provision that states that "any discrimination based on any ground such as \[...] sexual orientation shall be prohibited."[^cite_note-Treaties-2][^cite_note-Babel-3][^cite_note-Charter-4]

Furthermore, the [Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Functioning_of_the_European_Union) provides in [Articles 10](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Consolidated%20version%20of%20the%20Treaty%20on%20the%20Functioning%20of%20the%20European%20Union/Part%20One:%20Principles#Article_10) that the European Union has a positive duty to combat discrimination (among other things) on the grounds of sexual orientation and provides in [Article 19](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Consolidated%20version%20of%20the%20Treaty%20on%20the%20Functioning%20of%20the%20European%20Union/Part%20Two:%20Non-Discrimination%20and%20Citizenship%20of%20the%20Union#Article_19) ways for the [Council](5866.md) and the [European Parliament](9581.md) to actively propose pass legislation to do so. These provisions were enacted by the [Treaty of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Amsterdam) in 1999.[^cite_note-Treaties-2][^cite_note-Babel-3]

### Discrimination in employment

Following the inclusion in the Treaties of the above-mentioned provisions, [Directive 2000/78/EC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_2000/78/EC) "Directive establishing a general framework for equal treatment in employment and occupation" was enacted in 2000. This [framework directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_Directive_\(disambiguation\)) compels all EU states to adopt anti-discrimination legislation in employment. That legislation has to include provisions to protect people from discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation.[^cite_note-Babel-3]

In practice, this protects EU citizens from being refused a job, or from being fired, because of their sexual orientation; it also protects them from being harassed by a colleague due to their sexual orientation.[^cite_note-5]

#### EU staff

While Directive 2000/78/CE does not technically apply to the [EU institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions_of_the_European_Union) themselves,[^cite_note-6] the EU Staff Regulations[^cite_note-7] provide in Article 1d:

In the application of these Staff Regulations, any discrimination based on any ground such as sex, race, colour, ethnic or social origin, genetic features, language, religion or belief, political or any other opinion, membership of a national minority, property, birth, disability, age, or sexual orientation shall be prohibited.

For the purposes of these Staff Regulations, non-marital partnerships shall be treated as marriage provided that all the conditions listed in Article 1(2)(c) of Annex VII are fulfilled.

And the conditions listed in Article 1(2)(c) of Annex VII are the following:

(i) the couple produces a legal document recognised as such by a Member State, or any competent authority of a Member State, acknowledging their status as non-marital partners,

(ii) neither partner is in a marital relationship or in another non-marital partnership,

(iii) the partners are not related in any of the following ways: parent, child, grandparent, grandchild, brother, sister, aunt, uncle, nephew, niece, son-in-law, daughter-in-law,

(iv) the couple has no access to legal marriage in a Member State; a couple shall be considered to have access to legal marriage for the purposes of this point only where the members of the couple meet all the conditions laid down by the legislation of a Member State permitting marriage of such a couple

Thereby, the Staff Regulations outlaw discrimination of EU staff based on sex (which includes gender reassignment[^cite_note-8]) or sexual orientation and guarantee that registered same-sex couples are not excluded from benefits awarded to married couples due to not having access to the institution of marriage.

### Discrimination in the provisioning of goods and services

Directive 2000/78/EC does not cover being refused medical services or treatment, refusal of being given a double room in a hotel, protection from bullying in a school and refusal of social security schemes (e.g. survivors' pensions and financial assistance to carers). Protection under [EU law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law) in these circumstances exists, but is granted on the grounds of race or gender only.[^cite_note-current-9]

As such, in 2008, a [proposal of a Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposal_2008/0426/COM_of_a_Directive_on_implementing_the_principle_of_equal_treatment_between_persons_irrespective_of_religion_or_belief,_disability,_age_or_sexual_orientation?action=edit\&redlink=1) to more broadly fight [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-discrimination_law) has been introduced, which would outlaw discrimination in the areas of social protection, social advantages, education and access to supply of goods, on the basis of religious belief, disability, age, and sexual orientation.[^cite_note-10] However, despite strong support from the [European Parliament](9581.md), the directive has since been stalled in the [Council](5866.md).[^cite_note-11]

### Transgender rights

EU law currently takes a different approach to transgender issues. Despite the European Parliament adopting a resolution on transgender rights as early as 1989, transgender identity is not incorporated into any EU funding and was not mentioned in the law establishing the [European Institute for Gender Equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Institute_for_Gender_Equality) (EIGE) as sexual orientation was. However, the [case law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_law) of the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) (ECJ) provides some protection by interpreting discrimination on the basis of 'sex' to also refer to people who have had '[gender reassignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_reassignment_therapy)'. Thus all EU sex discrimination law applies to transgender people.[^cite_note-Babel-3] However, the literature criticised this approach, and there are calls to protect transgender people against discrimination based on their gender identity instead of sex.[^cite_note-12] In 2002, the [1976 equal treatment directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Treatment_Directive_of_1976) was revised to include discrimination based on [gender identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity), to reflect case law on the directive.[^cite_note-ILGA2-13] Transgender EU citizens have faced difficulties when exercising their right to free movement and other rights associated with their Union citizenship status.[^cite_note-14] In October 2024, the ECJ ruled that EU member states must recognise changes to names and gender markers obtained in other member states, based on the right to free movement.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Intersex rights

In February 2019, the [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted a resolution on the rights of [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex) people. The resolution called European Union member states to legislate better policies that protected intersex individuals, especially from unnecessary surgery and discrimination. It stated that the parliament "strongly condemns sex-normalizing treatments and surgery; welcomes laws that prohibit such surgery, as in Malta and Portugal, and encourages other member states to adopt similar legislation as soon as possible." The resolution also urged legal gender recognition based on self-determination. It also confirms that intersex people are "exposed to multiple instances of violence and discrimination in the European Union" and calls on the European Commission and the Members States to propose legislation to address these issues. It also includes the need of adequate counselling and support for intersex people and their families, measures to end the stigma and pathologisation intersex people face and increased funding for intersex-led civil society organisations.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## Other actions

Between 2001 and 2006, a Community Action Programme to Combat Discrimination involved the expenditure of €100 million to fight discrimination in a number of areas, including sexual orientation.[^cite_note-ILGA2-13]

In 2009, the [European Commission](9974.md) has acted to tone down a law in Lithuania that included homophobic language and also aimed to support the [gay pride parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_pride_parade) in the country and others under threat of banning.[^cite_note-Babel-3]

## Foreign relations

In June 2010, the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) adopted a non-binding toolkit to promote LGBTQ people's human rights.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In June 2013, the Council upgraded it to binding LGBTI [Guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guideline) instructing EU diplomats around the world [to defend the human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_defenders) of LGBTI people.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Same-sex marriage has been legalised in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Austria), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Belgium), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Denmark), [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Estonia), [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Finland), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_France), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Germany), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Greece), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Ireland), [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Luxembourg), [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Malta), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_Netherlands), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Portugal), [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Slovenia), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Spain), and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Sweden). Same-sex civil unions have been legalised in Austria, Belgium, [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Croatia), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Cyprus), [Czechia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_the_Czech_Republic), [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Estonia), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_solidarity_pact), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Greece), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Hungary), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Italy), [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Latvia), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Lithuania), Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, and Spain. In Denmark, Sweden, Finland, and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Partnership_and_Certain_Rights_and_Obligations_of_Cohabitants_Act_2010), civil partnerships were legal between 1989 and 2012, and between 1995 and 2009, and between 2002 and 2017, and between 2011 and 2015, respectively. In Germany, registered life partnerships were legal between 2001 and 2017. However, existing civil unions/registered life partnerships are still recognised in all of these countries.

[Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Bulgaria), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Lithuania), [Poland](1238328.md) and [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Slovakia) have constitutionally defined marriage as being between a man and a woman. In December 2020, [Hungary](13275.md) also explicitly legally banned adoption for same-sex couples within its constitution.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

European Union law (the [Citizens’ Rights Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%E2%80%99_Rights_Directive) 2004/38/EC) requires those member states that legalised same-sex partnerships to recognise each other's partnerships for the purpose of freedom of movement.[^cite_note-27] The European Parliament has however approved a report calling for mutual recognition.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

According to [European Court of Justice](87187.md) case law based on the [Employment Equality Framework Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_Equality_Framework_Directive), employees in a civil partnership with a same-sex partner must be granted the same benefits as those granted to their colleagues upon their marriage, where marriage is not possible for same-sex couples. The Court established this principle in 2008 in the case of *Tadao Maruko v. Versorgungsanstalt der deutschen Bühnen* with regards to a German [registered life partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Germany). In December 2013, the Court confirmed this in the case of *Frédéric Hay v. Crédit agricole mutuel* (C-267/12) with regards to a French [civil solidarity pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_solidarity_pact), which is significantly inferior to marriage than a German registered life partnership.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Also, according to the European Court of Justice in the case of *[Coman and Others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coman_and_Others_v_Inspectoratul_General_pentru_Imigr%C4%83ri_and_Ministerul_Afacerilor_Interne)*, by judgement of 5 June 2018, a "spouse" (or partner or any other family member) in the [Free Movement Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%E2%80%99_Rights_Directive) (2004/38/EC) includes a (foreign) same-sex spouse; member states are required to confer the right of residence on the (foreign) same-sex spouse of a citizen of the European Union.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] EU member countries that do not recognise same-sex unions themselves are still bound by a ruling by the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) to recognise same-sex marriages performed within the EU and including an EU citizen for the purposes of granting legal residence,[^cite_note-bbc050618-34] though they do not always respect this ruling in practice (in case of Romania is still ignoring implementation of the ruling).[^cite_note-boffey-35]

On 25 November 2025, the European Court of Justice ruled in *[Cupriak-Trojan and Trojan v Wojewoda Mazowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupriak-Trojan_and_Trojan_v_Wojewoda_Mazowiecki)* that member states must recognise same-sex marriages lawfully concluded in another member state. The judges note that their decision does not oblige member states to amend their domestic legislation to recognise same-sex marriage, instead simply requiring them to recognise marriages lawfully concluded in another EU country. The procedure must be applied "without distinction or additional hurdles". The ruling expanded upon *Coman and Others,* which had been ignored by countries like Romania, Poland, Slovakia, Hungary, and Bulgaria.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Family rights

In 2021, 10 EU member states refused to recognize same-sex couples as joint parents to their children. This leads to situations where two people recognized as parents in one country would have their family ties legally dissolve after crossing a border. A frequently encountered issue is that birth certificates issued in one member state and listing two people of the same sex as parents are not recognized in other countries. Some children do not have passports as a result.[^cite_note-obstacles-38] The pending CJEU case *[V.M.A. v. Stolichna Obsthina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V.M.A._v._Stolichna_Obsthina?action=edit\&redlink=1)* involves a child who could not claim Bulgarian nationality because her parents were a lesbian couple.[^cite_note-39] A policy brief commissioned by [European Parliament Committee on Petitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_Committee_on_Petitions) recommends that the European Commission or the CJEU should clarify that Directive 2004/38 on free movement also applies to rainbow families, who should not be discriminated against in their exercise of EU free movement rights.[^cite_note-obstacles-38] The case was finally decided on 14 December 2021, with the CJEU accepting the position of the European Parliament Committee on Petitions, and finding Bulgaria in breach of EU law for not issuing documents to the child of the lesbian couple. The decision points out that while it is still a Member State's prerogative to decide whether or not to extend [same-sex marriage](92656.md) and [LGBTQ adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_adoption) rights to its own citizen, this choice cannot come at the expense of the child being deprived of the relationship of one of her parents while exercising her rights to freedom of movement within the EU.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

## Conversion therapy

In March 2018, a majority of representatives in the [European Parliament](9581.md) passed a resolution in a 435–109 vote condemning conversion therapy and urging [European Union](9317.md) member states to ban the practice.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] A report released by the [European Parliament Intergroup on LGBT Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_Intergroup_on_LGBT_Rights) after the measure was passed stated that "Currently, only Malta and some regions in Spain have explicitly banned LGBTI conversion therapies."[^cite_note-45] Conversion therapy for minors was banned in Germany in 2020,[^cite_note-46] in France in 2022,[^cite_note-47] and in Belgium, Cyprus, and Portugal in 2023. Bans have also been proposed in Ireland, Netherlands and Austria.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 24 January 2024, the European Commission registered a European Citizens' Initiative to ban conversion therapy and the signature collection period started on 17 May of the same year. For the initiative to be successful, organizers have until 17 May 2025 to gather at least one million statements of support, as well as reach a minimum threshold in at least seven countries.[^cite_note-50]

### Historical evolution

While earlier versions of the [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) already polled [European Union citizens](1882861.md) on their general attitude towards LGBTQ people, the first Eurobarometer to specifically ask questions on LGBTQ rights was the "*Standard Eurobarometer 66*" published in November 2006.[^cite_note-\:1-145] Subsequent surveys on discrimination (2008-2012) focused on the perception of discrimination rather than support for specific rights.

In February 2014, the [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted the [Lunacek Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunacek_Report?action=edit\&redlink=1), which was a resolution calling for an *EU roadmap against homophobia* *and discrimination on grounds of sexual orientation and gender identity*.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] Following the start of the [Juncker Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juncker_Commission) the [Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate-General_for_Justice_and_Consumers) commissioned the "*Special Eurobarometer 437: Discrimination in the EU in 2015*" that was published in October 2015. Using data from that Eurobarometer, in December 2015, the [European Commission](9974.md) published its [List of Actions to Advance LGBTI Equality (2015–2019)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Actions_to_Advance_LGBTI_Equality_\(2015%E2%80%932019\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) which was seen as a direct response to the Lunacek Report.[^cite_note-\:2-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] Since 2015 EU-level polling on LGBTQ rights as such has continued regularly. The latest *Special Eurobarometer on Discrimination in the EU* was published in 2023 and the next one is expected for 2027.[^cite_note-151]

## Notes

**[^a.](#ref_a)** In 2023 the question was altered slightly from previous years to specify "marriage, adoption, and parental rights" for the first time. In light of the increases in support for the other unaltered questions, the Eurobarometer study likely attributes the decrease in percentage points regarding "equal rights" from 2019 to 2023 to this wording change.

**[^b.](#ref_b)** In 2015 and 2019 the question only concerned equal rights and did not specify these rights any further.

**[^c.](#ref_c)** In 2006 the question did not ask about equal rights in general, but only whether the adoption of children by same-sex couples should be legalised throughout Europe.
