**Dame Parmjit Kaur** "**Bobbie**" **Cheema-Grubb**, [DBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dame_Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Cheema**; born 6 October 1966), styled **Mrs Justice Cheema-Grubb**, is a [judge](5093797.md) of the [King's Bench Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Bench_Division) of the [High Court of Justice of England and Wales](26569402.md).[^cite_note-1]**[^cite_note-2]** She is the first Asian woman to serve as a [High Court judge](5093797.md) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).

## Early life and education

Parmjit Kaur Cheema was born in 1966 to [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_people) parents who emigrated from [India](14533.md) to Great Britain in the 1960s.[^cite_note-3] She grew up in [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire), and attended [City of Leeds School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Leeds_School) before reading law at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London).[^cite_note-Law_Diary-4][^cite_note-BBC-5]

## Legal career

[Called to the Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Called_to_the_Bar) at [Gray's Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray's_Inn) in 1989,[^cite_note-Appointment-6][^cite_note-7] Cheema-Grubb, in 2006, became the first Asian woman to be appointed a Junior Treasury Counsel.[^cite_note-Law_Diary-4] In 2007, she was appointed to sit as a [Recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorder_\(judge\)).

In 2013, Cheema-Grubb [took silk (QC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Counsel)[^cite_note-Appointment-6] serving as Senior Treasury Counsel before receiving authorisation to sit as a deputy High Court judge.[^cite_note-Appointment-6] She chaired an Advocacy Training Council working group which produced the report "Raising the Bar: The Handling of Vulnerable Witnesses, Victims and Defendants in Court".[^cite_note-8]

Cheema-Grubb successfully acted for the prosecution against retired Anglican Bishop [Peter Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Ball_\(bishop\)) for sexual abuse[^cite_note-9] and against barrister and Recorder [Constance Briscoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_Briscoe) for perverting the course of justice.[^cite_note-10]

### Judiciary

Appointed as a [High Court judge](5093797.md) on 22 October 2015,[^cite_note-Appointment-6] Cheema-Grubb was sworn in to the Queen's Bench on 25 November 2015,[^cite_note-BBC-5] receiving the customary [accolade of the Realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damehood) as [DBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire_\(DBE\)).[^cite_note-11]

Cheema-Grubb is one of a small number of High Court judges who are selected to preside over trials involving terrorism offences.&#x20;

In 2025, Cheema-Grubb was judge at the trial of [Nathan Gill](42863259.md), the former leader of [Reform UK](59757202.md) in Wales, whom she sentenced to 10-and-a-half years for taking pro-Russia bribes.[^cite_note-12]



[DBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire_\(DBE\)) [insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insignia)

In 2026, following a ruling by the Court of Appeal that contempt proceedings against defence barrister Rajiv Menon KC had been initiated using the wrong procedure, the President of the King's Bench Division referred the matter for reconsideration under the statutory procedure governing contempt in the Crown Court. The application was assigned to Cheema-Grubb, who held that there was sufficient evidence to establish a prima facie case of contempt and that it was in the public interest for proceedings to be instituted. She directed that the allegations should proceed to a substantive hearing before her sitting as a judge of the Crown Court.[^cite_note-13]

The hearing was originally scheduled to take place on 28 July 2026. However, on 24 July 2026, the High Court of England and Wales ruled that an appeal challenging the jurisdiction of the High Court to rule on contempt should be allowed to proceed, resulting in an adjournment of proceedings until such time as the appeal could be heard.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Personal life

In 1990, she married Russell Grubb and they have three children.[^cite_note-16] They are practising [Christians](5211.md).[^cite_note-17]
