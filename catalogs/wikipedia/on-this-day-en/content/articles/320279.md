**Groupe Union Défense** (originally named *Groupe Union Droit*), better known as **GUD**, was a French [far-right](221220.md) [students' union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students'_union) formed in the 1960s. The GUD was based in [Panthéon-Assas University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panth%C3%A9on-Assas_University),[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] a law school in [Paris](22989.md). After a period of inactivity it relaunched in 2022.[^cite_note-Plottu_Macé_2022-5][^cite_note-B.Corson_2022-6][^cite_note-Macé_Plottu_2022-7] On 26 June 2024, the French government ordered the dissolution of the GUD.[^cite_note-auto-1]

## Ideology

Formed as a [far-right](221220.md), [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism) youth organization, in the mid-1980s, the GUD turned toward support of the [Third Position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Position) movements and "[national revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_nationalism)" theories,[^cite_note-8] as well as embracing [anti-Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism), [anti-Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism) and [Assadism](36629941.md).[^cite_note-Blast_le_souffle_de_l’info_2022-9]

## Culture

GUD took as symbol the [Celtic cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_cross) and the [comic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comics) *black rats* (*rats noirs*).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Some music groups of [Rock identitaire français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_identitaire_fran%C3%A7ais) had connections with GUD.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## History



Members of the GUD during demonstration in [Paris](22989.md) in 2012

GUD was founded in December 1968 under the name *Union Droit* at Panthéon-Assas University[^cite_note-Blast_le_souffle_de_l’info_2022-9] by [Alain Robert (homme politique)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Robert_\(homme_politique\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Gérard Longuet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Longuet),[^cite_note-15] Gérard Ecorcheville and some members of the [political movement *Occident*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occident_\(far-right_group\)). In its early period, it was a reactionary bourgeois student movement, and some of its early members went on to become mainstream conservative politicians, including [Gérard Longuet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Longuet), [Hervé Novelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herv%C3%A9_Novelli) and [Alain Madelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Madelin).[^cite_note-Blast_le_souffle_de_l’info_2022-9][^cite_note-Henley_2002-16]

Members of the GUD participated in the 1969 founding of [Ordre Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_Nouveau_\(1960s\)).[^cite_note-auto1-17]

During the 1970s and early 1980s, linked to the [Parti des forces nouvelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_des_forces_nouvelles) (PFN), the GUD published the [satiric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satire) monthly *Alternative*.[^cite_note-auto1-17] Members in this period included [Alain Orsoni](77915489.md), a Corsican nationalist linked to organised crime and suspected of the murder of [Marie-Jeanne Bozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Jeanne_Bozzi).[^cite_note-Blast_le_souffle_de_l’info_2022-9]

On 9 May 1994 GUD member [Sébastien Deyzieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_Deyzieu?action=edit\&redlink=1) died after clashes between nationalists and [riot police](1148542.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Following these event, some French nationalist groups formed an umbrella organization [Comité du 9-Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comit%C3%A9_du_9-Mai) (C9M) and holds yearly a commemorative marches in Paris on May 9.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

In 1998, the Group united itself with *Jeune Résistance* and the *Union des cercles résistance*, offshoots of [Nouvelle Résistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouvelle_R%C3%A9sistance) group, under the name [Unité Radicale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit%C3%A9_Radicale), but it was dissolved[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] after [Maxime Brunerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxime_Brunerie)'s failed assassination attempt on president [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac).[^cite_note-24]

In 2004, the GUD reformed under the name [Rassemblement étudiant de droite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rassemblement_%C3%A9tudiant_de_droite?action=edit\&redlink=1). Its publication was *Le Dissident*.[^cite_note-25]

In 2017 members of the GUD [squatted a building in Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_social_centres) and founded political movement [Social Bastion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Bastion).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In late 2022, [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) appeared in educational institutions in Paris (including the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Normale_Sup%C3%A9rieure)) saying "GUD is back"; a video was released on [Ouest Casual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouest_Casual?action=edit\&redlink=1), a [Telegram](41501305.md) channel used by the far right, commemorating some Greek neo-Nazis; and the GUD slogan “Europe, Youth, Revolution” appeared on stickers in Paris and chants at a right-wing demonstration in [Lyon](8638634.md). Its activists were reported to be drawn from far-right trade union La Cocarde Étudiante, the ultra-right group the [Zouaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zouaves), [traditionalist Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholics) from Versailles, and [football hooligans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_hooligans).[^cite_note-Blast_le_souffle_de_l’info_2022-9]

## Members

Successive leaders of the GUD were: Alain Robert, [Jack Marchal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Marchal), Jean-François Santacroce, Serge Rep, Philippe Cuignache, Charles-Henri Varaut, [Frédéric Chatillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chatillon), William Bonnefoy, Benoît Fleury.

### Military volunteers

Some GUD members have fought in [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) with the [Kataeb Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kataeb_Party)[^cite_note-29] in 1976, [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence)[^cite_note-30] in the 1990s and in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) during [Karen conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_conflict).[^cite_note-31] In 1985 member of the GUD Jean-Philippe Courrèges was killed in action fighting for the [Karen National Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_National_Liberation_Army).[^cite_note-32]

GUD members have had links with the *[Department for Protection and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Protection_and_Security)*, which is the security organization of the far-right political party [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_\(France\)).[^cite_note-33]

Former member of the GUD [Alain Orsoni](77915489.md) was member of the [FLNC](786920.md).[^cite_note-34]
