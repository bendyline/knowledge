The **president of Portugal**, officially the **president of the Portuguese Republic**,[^cite_note-3] is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and highest office of [Portugal](23033.md).

The powers, functions and duties of prior presidential offices, and their relation with the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Portugal) and cabinets have over time differed with the various [Portuguese constitutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Portugal). Currently, in the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Portuguese_Republic), a [semi-presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system), the president holds no direct executive power, unlike his counterparts in the [United States](24113.md) and [France](24899.md). However, even though he is in general a ceremonial figure,[^cite_note-4] he holds some powers less-commonly found in [parliamentary systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_systems): one of his most significant responsibilities is the [promulgation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promulgation) of all laws enacted by the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md) (parliament) or the [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_government) (an act without which such laws have no legal validity), with an alternative option to [veto](74661.md) them (although this veto can be overcome in the case of laws approved by Parliament) or send them to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_\(Portugal\)) for appreciation of whether they violate the Constitution. This and other abilities imply that the president of Portugal does not fit clearly into either of the [three traditional powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) – legislative, executive and judicial – acting instead as a sort of "moderating power" among the traditional three.[^cite_note-Presidency-5]

The current president of Portugal is [António José Seguro](35591042.md), who took office on 9 March 2026.

## Role

The Portuguese Third Republic is a [semi-presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system). Despite being a rather ceremonial figure, unlike most European presidents, who are largely ceremonial figures, the Portuguese president is vested with more extensive powers. Although the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Portugal) and [parliament](1123482.md) oversee and direct much of Portugal's actual governmental affairs, the president wields significant influence and authority, especially in the fields of [national security](240468.md) and [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Portugal), however, always on the advice of the Government and the approval of Parliament. The president is the supreme commander of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Armed_Forces), holds the nation's most senior office, and outranks all other politicians.[^cite_note-pt-6]

Prior to the Carnation Revolution, the powers of the presidency varied widely; some presidents were virtual dictators (such as [Pais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sid%C3%B3nio_Pais), and [Carmona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Fragoso_Carmona) in his early years), while others were little more than [figureheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurehead) (such as Carmona in his later years, [Craveiro Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Craveiro_Lopes), and [Américo Tomás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9rico_Tom%C3%A1s)). During the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)), the president was nominally vested with near-dictatorial powers, but in practice supreme power was held by the [president of the Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Portugal) ([António de Oliveira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Oliveira_Salazar) and [Marcelo Caetano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Caetano)).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

### Government nomination

The president's greatest power is their ability to appoint the prime minister. However, since the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md) has the sole power to dismiss the prime minister's government, the prime minister named by the president must have the confidence of a majority of representatives in the assembly, otherwise the prime minister may face a [motion of no confidence](208101.md). The president has the discretionary power to dissolve parliament when he/she sees fit (colloquially known as the "atomic bomb" in Portugal),[^cite_note-9] and President [Jorge Sampaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Sampaio) made use of this prerogative in late 2004 to remove the controversial government of [Pedro Santana Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Santana_Lopes), despite the absolute majority of deputies supporting the government.[^cite_note-10]

### Armed Forces

In 2003, President Sampaio also intervened to limit the Portuguese participation in the [Iraq War](5043324.md) – as Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces he forbade the deployment of the [Portuguese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Army) in a war that he personally disagreed with, clashing with the then–prime minister [José Manuel Barroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Barroso).[^cite_note-11] Because of this, the Government eventually deployed 128 members of the [National Republican Guard (GNR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Republican_Guard_\(Portugal\)) to [Iraq from 2003 to 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Force_%E2%80%93_Iraq), this being possible because the GNR, despite being a military force, was not part of the Armed Forces.[^cite_note-12]

## Powers

The constitution grants the following powers to the president:[^cite_note-CRP-EN-13]

- The president of the republic exercises the functions of Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces and Grand Master of the Three Orders, and appoints and dismisses, on a proposal from the Government, the chief of the General Staff of the Armed Forces and the heads of General Staff of the three branches of the Armed Forces.

- The president of the republic can dissolve the Assembly of the Republic, which implies the need to call new legislative elections and, after these have been held, the resignation of the Government.

- The president of the republic appoints the prime minister taking into account the electoral results and appoints the remaining members of the Government on the proposal of the prime minister. The president can, on the other hand, dismiss the Government when this becomes necessary to ensure the regular functioning of democratic institutions.

- The governing bodies of the autonomous regions may be dissolved by the president of the republic, for carrying out serious acts contrary to the Constitution.

- The president of the republic declares the state of siege and emergency, after hearing the Government and under authorization from the Assembly of the Republic.

- Upon a proposal from the Government and with authorization from the Assembly of the Republic, the president of the republic may declare war in the event of effective or imminent aggression and make peace.

- The president of the republic promulgates or signs and, consequently, can veto the promulgation or signature of laws, decree-laws, regulatory decrees and other Government decrees.

- In the domain of his competences in international relations, the president of the republic ratifies international treaties.

- The president of the republic decides on the convening of the referendum whose holding is proposed by the Assembly of the Republic.

- The president of the republic may request the Constitutional Court to pre-empt the constitutionality of norms contained in international conventions or decrees that have been sent to him for promulgation as an organic law, law or decree-law.

- The president of the republic appoints and exonerates, in some cases on a proposal from the Government, holders of important state bodies such as the representatives of the Republic for the autonomous regions, the president of the Court of Auditors and the attorney general of the republic, five members of the Council of State and two members of the Superior Council of the Judiciary.

- The president of the Republic appoints the ambassadors and extraordinary envoys, on a proposal from the Government, and accredits the foreign diplomatic representatives.

- The president of the republic, after hearing the Government, pardons and commutes sentences.

## Election

Under the [Portuguese Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Constitution) adopted in 1976, in the wake of the 1974 [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution), the president is elected to a five-year term. In order to be eligible, any citizen has to be of Portuguese origin and above 35 years old.[^cite_note-pt-6] He may be reelected any number of times, but not more than twice in a row.[^cite_note-CRP-EN-13] The official residence of the Portuguese president is the [Belém Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_Palace) in [Lisbon](18091.md).[^cite_note-14]

The president is elected in a [two-round system](25932.md): if no candidate reaches 50% of the votes during the first round, the two candidates with the most votes face each other in a second round held two weeks later. However, the second round have only been needed twice, during the [1986 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Portuguese_presidential_election) and [2026 presidential election](68734153.md). To date, all of the elected presidents since the Carnation Revolution have served for two consecutive terms, and presidents consistently rank as the most popular political figure in the country. During his time in office, however, the popularity of former president [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%ADbal_Cavaco_Silva) plummeted, making him the second-least popular political figure in the country, just above the then-prime minister, and the first Portuguese president after 1974 to have a negative popularity.[^cite_note-15] By 2024, [Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa](1652059.md) also displayed negative popularity ratings.[^cite_note-16]

After the election, the President[-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-elect) usually takes an office in the [Queluz Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Queluz) in [Sintra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintra) (this having been the case for Aníbal Cavaco Silva, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa and António José Seguro).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Under article 132 of the Constitution, if the president dies or becomes incapacitated while in office, the [president of the Assembly of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Assembly_of_the_Republic_\(Portugal\)) assumes the office with restricted powers until a new president can be inaugurated following fresh elections.[^cite_note-CRP-20] In case there's no president of the Assembly to assume the office of President, the regiment of the Assembly of the Republic takes into effect and the vice presidents of the Assembly assume the office by their order and, if none are available, the member of Parliament with the longest tenure assumes the office until the election of a new president of the Assembly.[^cite_note-CRP-20][^cite_note-RAR-21]

Since 1976, four [Presidents of the Assembly of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Assembly_of_the_Republic_\(Portugal\)) have served as interim presidents: [Vasco da Gama Fernandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama_Fernandes) in May 1978,[^cite_note-22] [Teófilo Carvalho dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te%C3%B3filo_Carvalho_dos_Santos) in June 1979,[^cite_note-23] [Francisco de Oliveira Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Oliveira_Dias) in December 1981[^cite_note-24] and [António de Almeida Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Almeida_Santos) in July 1996.[^cite_note-25]

## President's residence

Main article: [Belém Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_Palace)

[Belém Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_Palace) is the official residence of the president of the Portuguese republic since 1910. Built in the 16th century by a high ranking diplomat named [Manuel de Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_de_Portugal_\(poet\)), was bought by King [John V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_V_of_Portugal) in the 18th century and served as one of the residences of the [Royal Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Braganza) until the early 20th century.[^cite_note-26]

[António José Seguro](35591042.md), the current president, does not live in the palace.[^cite_note-27]
