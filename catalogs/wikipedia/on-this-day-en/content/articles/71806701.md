# Oil and natural gas refining in Turkmenistan

[Turkmenistan](198149.md), a country rich in both oil and natural gas, has developed a sub-sector in [its economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Turkmenistan) related to the refining of those two sorts of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel).[^cite_note-1]

## Oil refining

There are two [oil refineries](195137.md) in [Turkmenistan](198149.md), located in the cities of [Türkmenbaşy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkmenba%C5%9Fy,_Turkmenistan) and [Seýdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Se%C3%BDdi).[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3]

The Türkmenbaşy oil refinery is the larger of the two refineries,[^cite_note-4] with a capacity of more than 10 million tons of oil per year. The refinery produces a range of products, including [unleaded gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unleaded_Gasoline), [petroleum coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_coke), [asphalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen), laundry detergent, [hydro-treated diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodiesel), and [lube oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lube_oil). The Turkmen government has demonstrated interest in attracting foreign investment to build factories producing end-user petroleum based products such as detergents and tires. The refinery reported that its products are exported to Russia, China, Iran, Afghanistan, Turkey, Pakistan, Tajikistan, and Japan.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

Turkmenistan has invested US\$900 million in a number of projects designed to help increase the country's refining capacity by 95 percent by 2030. The projects include the construction of a facility for coking (carbonization) and tar de-asphalting with annual capacity of 900,000 and 500,000 tons, respectively. The government of Turkmenistan also constructed a facility to produce asphalt with an annual capacity of 38,000 tons as well as a facility to produce polypropylene film and an oil refinery with a capacity of 3 billion tons per year. Turkmenistan has commissioned a feasibility study regarding the construction of a new oil refinery in its Balkan province.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

## Natural gas refining

Sysmic and geological research was conducted in the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) with the help of the United States in 2000, which showed the presence of 11,000,000,000 tons of oil and 5,500,000,000,000 cubic metres (1.9×10<sup>14</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>) of gas on the coast of the country.[^cite_note-5] Historically, Turkmenistan has been a heavy exporter of natural gas, exporting nearly 80% of its raw material produced.[^cite_note-6] As of the 2010s, however, the country has increasingly faced a difficult time in increasing its exports of natural gas.[^cite_note-7] In response, the government plans to refine [natural gas](22131.md) to make chemicals such as [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol), [synthetic rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_rubber), and materials for paint. In October 2018, the government opened a chemical facility in the [Balkan Region of Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Region) to create [polyethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene) and [polypropylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypropylene), in contract with [LG International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Corporation), [Toyo Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyo_Engineering_Corporation), and [Hyundai Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Engineering_&_Construction). The facility cost US\$3.4 billion to build, and is capable of converting 5,000,000,000 cubic metres (1.8×10<sup>11</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>) of natural gas into 81,000 tons of polypropylene as well as 386,000 tons of polyethylene every year.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

The government also created a facility to convert natural gas to gasoline, in the [Ahal Region of Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahal_Region). It was first conceived in 2013 by a contract including [Türkmengaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkmengaz), [Rönesans Türkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6nesans_Holding), and [Kawasaki Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_Heavy_Industries). It cost US\$1.7 billion to build, and was opened in 2019. The plant is capable of converting 1,785,000,000 cubic metres (6.30×10<sup>10</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>) of natural gas into 600,000 tons of [A-92 gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octane_rating#Regional_variations) every year.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

In addition to gasoline, the government has sought to [convert natural gas to liquid petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_to_liquids). A new facility was announced in April 2016 by the Turkmenistan Ministry of Oil & Gas, created by a contract that includes South Korean LG International Corp., Hyundai Engineering Co, and the Japanese [Itochu Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itochu_Corporation). It is projected to convert 3,700,000,000 cubic metres (1.3×10<sup>11</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>) into 1.1 million tons of [diesel fuel](81761.md) and over 400,000 tons of [naphtha](184342.md) every year. The country has plans to build additional gas to liquids plants in the coming years.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

For decades, Turkmenistan had a dispute with Azerbaijan regarding the ownership of a large oil and gas block located inside the [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) of both countries in the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). In 2021, the two countries signed a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) in [Ashgabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashgabat) to conduct joint exploration and field development activities.[^cite_note-8] Following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) and the [subsequent gas dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russia%E2%80%93European_Union_gas_dispute), Turkmenistan was considered as an alternative supplier to Europe.[^cite_note-9]

In July 2022, the Turkmenistan started extraction from a new gas plant in the [Mary Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Region).[^cite_note-10]
