**Maritime Launch Services (MLS)** is a Canadian [space transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_transport) services company founded in 2016 and headquartered in [Halifax, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia), [Canada](5042916.md). MLS is building a commercial launch facility near [Canso, Nova Scotia](681561.md) called Spaceport Nova Scotia. Three [sub-orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital) flights have been launched from this site.[^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch_Canadian_Press-1][^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch_Globe_and_Mail-2]

Originally, MLS was to rely on Ukrainian-built [Cyclone-4M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone-4M) rockets by [Pivdenne Design Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KB_Pivdenne) to launch polar and [Sun-synchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit) from [Canso, Nova Scotia](681561.md).[^cite_note-CBC_News_2017_Announcement-3] Due to supply problems caused by the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), the agreement was terminated.[^cite_note-CAN-USA_Tech_Deal_2024-4]

In 2024, Maritime Launch Services transitioned to an airport model, planning to lease facilities and services to launch vehicle providers.[^cite_note-Airport_Model-5] The company has signed agreements with [Netherlands](21148.md)-based [T-Minus Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Minus_Engineering), [Quebec](7954867.md)-based [Reaction Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_Dynamics_\(company\)), and [Germany](11867.md)-based [Isar Aerospace](65261460.md) to provide launch services. The company has signed Letters of Intent with [UK](31717.md)-based [Skyrora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyrora) and [South Korea](27019.md)-based [Innospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innospace).

## Launch site

On 14 March 2017, MLS selected [Canso, Nova Scotia](681561.md) as its launch site.[^cite_note-Canso_Gets_Selected_2017-6] MLS applied to lease 15 hectares (37 acres) of land outside the town from the provincial Department of Natural Resources.[^cite_note-cbc_lease-7] This leased area was later increased to 335 acres.[^cite_note-entervestor-8] The launch site is approximately 3.5 km south of Canso, with the Vehicle Processing Facility located approximately 2 km south-west of Canso.[^cite_note-9] After delays, MLS received final approval for construction in August 2022 and began work in September 2022.[^cite_note-10]

The site is slated to include a 10 to 15 metre-tall control centre and rocket assembly facility, with a launch pad positioned 2.4 kilometres away, linked by a custom rail system for rocket transportation.[^cite_note-Spaceport_Details_2017-11] It will be the only operational spaceport in Canada, after the abandonment of the [Churchill Rocket Research Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Rocket_Research_Range) in the 1990s, and the first commercial spaceport for orbital launches in the country.[^cite_note-Spaceport_Details_2017-11] It was estimated that construction of the spaceport would take three or four years to complete, but the first pad was not ready until 2024.[^cite_note-gmk-20210409-12]

#### First launch

The first rocket launch that occurred from Spaceport Nova Scotia was the Goose 3 [amateur rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_rocketry) developed by Arbalest Rocketry, a student group from [York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_University). The Goose 3 rocket's first launch attempt occurred on 5 July 2023, but was delayed by one day, due to weather. Goose 3 was successful launched on 6 July 2023, reaching 13.4 kilometers.[^cite_note-Goose_3_Launch-13]

#### Second launch

The second rocket launch that occurred from Spaceport Nova Scotia was the Barracuda hypersonic test platform by T-Minus Engineering. Two Barracudas were originally scheduled to be launched in October 2025, this was reduced to one Barracuda due to T-Minus Engineering not having completed the second rocket, and the launch was delayed to a 18-24 November 2025 launch window due to a European port strike.[^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch-17] The Barracuda was successful launched on 20 November 2025 at 10\:54 a.m. Eastern time, but did not cross the [Kármán line](738149.md).[^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch_SpaceQ-15]

#### Third launch

The third rocket launch that occurred from Spaceport Nova Scotia was the Barracuda hypersonic test platform by T-Minus Engineering. Two Barracudas were scheduled to be launched in a 8-14 June 2026 window.[^cite_note-June2026SpaceQ-19] One Barracuda was successful launched on 10 June 2025 at 8\:51 a.m. Atlantic time, but did not cross the [Kármán line](738149.md). The second launch was scrubbed for a later date.[^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch_Globe_and_Mail-2]

### Launch Vehicle Partnerships



Cyclone-4M graphic

#### Cyclone-4M

MLS originally planned to utilize the Ukrainian-built [Cyclone-4M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone-4M) medium-lift launch vehicle designed by [Pivdenne Design Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KB_Pivdenne).[^cite_note-Cyclone_4M_Description_2019-23] It was expected to use a first-stage derived from the Soviet-era [ Zenit launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenit_\(rocket_family\)). It was to be powered by four Ukrainian-built RD-870 kerosene/LOX engines. These engines were originally designed for the vacuum of space on a second-stage for Soviet-era rockets.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-Cyclone_Ref_Guide_V.2-25] The upper stage stack was developed from the original hypergolic Cyclone 4 rocket. The first launch of the Cyclone-4M was originally expected to take place at Canso in 2025.[^cite_note-Precious_Payload_Announcement_2022-26] However, because of the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, and the uncertainty the prolonged war caused, MLS terminated the agreement to launch Cyclone from Spaceport Nova Scotia in third quarter 2024.[^cite_note-CAN-USA_Tech_Deal_2024-4]

Before the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), MLS originally had planned to launch eight Cyclone-4M rockets annually. The launch site has two southward launch [orbital inclination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_inclination) options:&#x20;

**Option 1** is a [Sun-synchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit) launch between 600–800 km in altitude, for smaller satellites, with a payload up to 3350 kg for \$45 million [USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD).

**Option 2** is an [ equatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-equatorial_orbit) [low Earth orbit](47568.md) launch, below 600 km in altitude, that will allow a payload up to 5000 kg also for \$45 million USD.

#### Skyrora XL

In September 2022, Maritime Launch Services signed a Letter of Intent with the [UK](31717.md)-based company [Skyrora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyrora) to launch that company's Skyrora XL small class launch vehicle from Spaceport Nova Scotia.[^cite_note-Skyrora-28]

#### Barracuda

In June 2025, Maritime Launch Services reached an agreement with the [Dutch](21148.md) company [T-Minus Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Minus_Engineering) to perform two launches of that company's Barracuda hypersonic test platform from Spaceport Nova Scotia in October 2025.[^cite_note-Barracuda-29] The Barracuda was first successful launched from Spaceport Nova Scotia on 20 November 2025.[^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch_SpaceQ-15]. On 5 January 2026, Steve Matier stated that Maritime Launch Services was planning to launch two more Barracuda rockets from Spaceport Nova Scotia in May or June 2026, with a fourth launch being planned for late 2026.[^cite_note-Strait_Talk-30] In May 2026, Matier stated that these launches would occur in June and October.[^cite_note-Taking_Shape-20] One Barracuda was successfully launched on 10 June 2026, with the second launched scrubbed to a later date.[^cite_note-Barracuda_Launch_Globe_and_Mail-2][^cite_note-31]

#### Aurora-8

In August 2025, Maritime Launch Services reached an agreement with the [Quebec](7954867.md)-based firm [Reaction Dynamics (RDX)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_Dynamics_\(company\)) to launch that company's Aurora-8 small-class orbital launch vehicle from Spaceport Nova Scotia no earlier than 2028.[^cite_note-RDX_stock_agreement_2025-21]

#### HANBIT

In March 2026, Maritime Launch Services signed a Letter of Intent with the [South Korea](27019.md)-based [Innospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innospace) to evaluate hosting that company's HANBIT launch system from Spaceport Nova Scotia, with a deadline of December 2026 to finalize an agreement.[^cite_note-HANBIT_loi_2026-32]

#### Spectrum

In May 2026, Maritime Launch Services signed a Letter of Intent with the [German](11867.md)-based [Isar Aerospace](65261460.md) to bring together Isar Aerospace's [Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_\(rocket\)) orbital launch system and Spaceport Nova Scotia.[^cite_note-Isar_loi_2026-33] In July 2026, Maritime Launch Services reached an agreement with [Isar Aerospace](65261460.md) for a dedicated launch complex to perform orbital launches beginning in 2028.[^cite_note-Isar_agreement_2026-22]

## Ground Control Site

In June 2021,[^cite_note-2025_MD\&A-34] Maritime Launch Services acquired a 6.87 acre piece of private land at 233 Dover Road [^cite_note-rezoning_request-35]. On 8 January 2025, Maritime Launch Services requested to rezone the property from mixed-use rural residential (MRR-1) to commercial.[^cite_note-rezoning_request-35]

### Launch Control Centre

On 19 November 2021, Maritime Launch Services announced plans to construct a Launch Control Centre on the land the company had acquired.[^cite_note-LCC_announcement-36] This LCC is planned to contain launch control functionality, a press briefing room, and a visitors centre to include interactive exhibits, a theatre, a gift store, and a public launch viewing area.[^cite_note-LCC_announcement-36]

### Ground Station

On 25 September 2024, Maritime Launch Services announced an agreement with [Italy](14532.md)-based ground segment as-a-service (GSaaS) provider [Leaf Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_Space?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Leaf_space_agreement-37] This 10-year agreement was to build and host a new ground station at Spaceport Nova Scotia. The station was expected to be operation in Q1 2025,[^cite_note-Leaf_space_agreement-37] the completed installation was announced in August 2026.[^cite_note-MLS_updates-38]
