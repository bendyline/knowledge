**Freight transport**, also referred to as **freight forwarding**, is the physical process of [transporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) commercial [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(business\)) and [merchandise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchandise) goods and [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo).[^cite_note-1] The term **shipping** originally referred to [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) by sea but in [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English), it has been extended to refer to transport by land or air (International English: "carriage") as well. "[Logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics)", a term borrowed from the military environment, is also used in the same sense.

## History

### Prehistoric era

Initial human civilization relied heavily on [domesticated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication) animals, such as [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse), [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel), and [donkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey), to transport their goods. The invention of the [wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel) in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) in 5000 B.C.E improved this efficiency by allowing for carts and carriages to be created, which animals could pull.[^cite_note-2]

Water transport also developed, with a terra-cotta model of a boat from the Ubaid period, circa 4000 BCE, providing some of the earliest evidence for sailboats.[^cite_note-3]

### Classical era

#### Romans

The [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) built a vast network of roads, which facilitated trade across the numerous cities in its empire.[^cite_note-4]

#### Silk Road

Main article: [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road)

Transport along the silk road, a land-based route, was generally done through caravans, equipped with camels that would carry goods. It was uncommon for traders to traverse the entirety of the silk road - they would pass on their goods to other traders along different legs of the journey.[^cite_note-5]

While silk flowed westward from China, other goods such as wools, gold, and silver were transported east from the West.[^cite_note-6]

Freight transport in the UK was shaped significantly by the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution). The midlands of England saw a sophisticated [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) system, which allowed freight to be transported easily over long distances.[^cite_note-7] Additionally, [railway systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) were popularized, initially consisting of horse-drawn carts, but switched to [steam powered trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine) later on.[^cite_note-8]

#### Aviation

After the invention of the plane by the [Wright Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers), the first air freight shipment was delivered in 1910.[^cite_note-9] While the industry and demand for air freight delivery remained low throughout much of the 20th century, the creation of [FedEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx) in 1973 revolutionised the industry by introducing specialised [cargo](2375692.md) flights, rather than aggregating passenger and cargo flights in one trip.[^cite_note-10]

#### Autonomous transportation

Presently, [autonomous vehicles](5071866.md) have been trialled by many companies as a method of transportation, intending to reduce labour costs.[^cite_note-11] Additionally, robots designed to automate the '[last-mile delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_mile_\(transportation\))' stage have also been considered by businesses.[^cite_note-12]

## Modes of shipment



Global freight volumes according to mode of transport in trillions of [tonne-kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne-kilometre) in 2010

In 2015, 108 trillion [tonne-kilometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne-kilometer) were transported worldwide (anticipated to grow by 3.4% per year until 2050 (128 Trillion in 2020)): 70% by sea, 18% by road, 9% by rail, 2% by [inland waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_waterway) and less than 0.25% by air.[^cite_note-13]

Land or "ground" shipping can be made by [train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train) or by [truck](31456.md) (British English: [lorry](31456.md)). Ground transport is typically more affordable than air, but more expensive than sea, especially in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries), where inland [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) may not be efficient. In air and sea shipments, ground transport is required to take the cargo from its place of origin to the [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) or [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) and then to its destination because it is not always possible to establish a production facility near ports due to the limited coastlines of countries.

Much freight transport is done by [cargo ships](786056.md). An individual nation's fleet and the people that crew it are referred to as its [merchant navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_navy) or merchant marine. According to a 2018 report from the [United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_Trade_and_Development), merchant shipping (or seaborne trade) carries 80–90% of [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade) and 60–70% by value.[^cite_note-14]<sup>: 4 </sup> On rivers and [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal), [barges](4177.md) are often used to carry [bulk cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_cargo).

Cargo is commonly transported by [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air) in specialized cargo aircraft and in the luggage compartments of passenger aircraft, while sometimes [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopters) and drones become the alternative air transportations. Air freight is typically the fastest mode for long-distance freight transport, but it is also the most expensive.[^cite_note-15]

### Space

Main article: [Space logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_logistics)

### Multimodal

Main article: [Multimodal transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimodal_transport)

Cargo is exchanged between different modes of transportation via [transport hubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub), also known as [transport interchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub) or [Nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_transport#Nodes) (e.g. train stations, airports, etc.). Cargo is shipped under a single contract but performed using at least two different modes of transport (e.g. ground and air). Cargo may not be containerized.

### Intermodal

Main article: [Intermodal freight transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_freight_transport)

Multimodal transport featuring containerized cargo (or [intermodal container](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_container)) that is easily transferred between ship, rail, plane and truck.

For example, a shipper works together with both ground and air transportation to ship an item overseas. Intermodal freight transport is used to plan the route and carry out the shipping service from the manufacturer to the door of the recipient.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

Main article: [Incoterm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoterm)

The [Incoterms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoterms) (or International Commercial Terms) published by the [International Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Chamber_of_Commerce) (ICC) are accepted by governments, legal authorities, and practitioners worldwide for the interpretation of the most commonly used terms in international trade. Common terms include:

- [Free on Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoterms#FOB_%E2%80%93_Free_on_Board_\(named_port_of_shipment\)) (FOB)

- [Cost and Freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoterms#Cost_and_Freight) (CFR, C\&F, CNF)

- [Cost, Insurance and Freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoterms#CIF_%E2%80%93_Cost,_Insurance_&_Freight_\(named_port_of_destination\)) (CIF)

The term "best way" generally implies that the shipper will choose the carrier that offers the lowest rate (to the shipper) for the shipment. In some cases, however, other factors, such as better insurance or faster transit time, will cause the shipper to choose an option other than the lowest bidder.

## Door-to-door shipping

**Door-to-door** (**DTD** or **D2D**) **shipping** refers to the domestic or international shipment of cargo from the point of origin (POI) to the destination while generally remaining on the same piece of equipment and avoiding multiple transactions, trans-loading, and cross-docking without interim storage.

International DTD is a service provided by many [international](214491.md) shipping companies and may feature [intermodal freight transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_freight_transport) using [containerized cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_container). The quoted price of this service includes all shipping, handling, import and customs duties, making it a hassle-free option for customers to import goods from one [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) to another. This is compared to standard shipping, the price of which typically includes only the expenses incurred by the shipping company in transferring the object from one place to another. [Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs) fees, [import taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import_taxes) and other tariffs may contribute substantially to this base price before the item ever arrives.[^cite_note-18]

## Digitalization and logistics marketplaces

The freight transport industry has witnessed a substantial change with the rise of integrated digital marketplaces. International freight transport is a traditionally fragmented industry; however, with the rise of global digital [marketplaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_marketplace), the industry is witnessing a move towards a centralized platform. Such a move is enabling the integration of maritime, air, and land freight into a single marketplace. This is enabling small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to access international [supply chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain) through Less than Container Load (LCL) and multimodal solutions traditionally adopted by industrial shippers.[^cite_note-WorldBank-19][^cite_note-ResearchGate-20]
