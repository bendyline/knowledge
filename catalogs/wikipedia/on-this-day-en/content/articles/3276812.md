In [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law), a **trial** is a coming together of [parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_\(law\)) to a [dispute](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/dispute), to present information (in the form of [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence_\(law\))) in a [tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribunal), a formal setting with the [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority) to [adjudicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjudication) claims or disputes. One form of tribunal is a [court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court). The tribunal, which may occur before a [judge](44719.md), [jury](44720.md), or other designated [trier of fact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trier_of_fact), aims to achieve a resolution to their dispute.[^cite_note-1]

## Types by finder of fact

Where the trial is held before a group of members of the community, it is called a [jury trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jury_trial). Where the trial is held solely before a judge, it is called a [bench trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_trial).[^cite_note-2] [Hearings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_\(law\)) before [administrative bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_body) may have many of the features of a trial before a court, but are typically not referred to as trials. An [appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) (appellate proceeding) is also generally not deemed a trial, because such proceedings are usually restricted to a review of the evidence presented before the [trial court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_court), and do not permit the introduction of new evidence.

### Criminal



The [Old Bailey](229046.md) in [London](17867.md), 1808

A [criminal trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_trial) is designed to resolve accusations brought (usually by a [government](12229.md)) against a person accused of a [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime). In [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) systems, most criminal [defendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defendant) are entitled to a trial held before a jury. Because the state is attempting to use its power to deprive the accused of life, liberty, or property, the rights of the accused afforded to criminal defendants are typically broad. The rules of criminal procedure provide rules for criminal trials.

### Civil

A [civil trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(common_law\)) is generally held to settle [lawsuits](113258.md) or civil claims—non-criminal disputes. In some countries, the government can both sue and be sued in a civil capacity. The rules of [civil procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_procedure) provide rules for civil trials.

### Administrative

Although [administrative hearings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_hearing) are not ordinarily considered trials, they retain many elements found in more "formal" trial settings. When the dispute goes to a judicial setting, it is called an administrative trial, to revise the administrative hearing, depending on the jurisdiction. The types of disputes handled in these hearings are governed by [administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law) and auxiliarily by civil trial law.

### Labor

Main article: [Labor and employment law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_and_employment_law)

Labor law (also known as [employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment) law) is the body of laws, administrative rulings, and precedents which address the legal rights of, and restrictions on, working people and their organizations. Collective labour law relates to the tripartite relationship between employee, employer, and union. Individual labour law concerns employees' rights at work also through the contract for work. Employment standards are social norms (in some cases also technical standards) for the minimum socially acceptable conditions under which employees or contractors are allowed to work. [Government agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agencies) (such as the former US Employment Standards Administration) enforce labour law (legislature, regulatory, or judicial).

## Systems

### Adversarial

Main article: [Adversarial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adversarial_system)

In [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) systems, an adversarial or accusatory approach is used to adjudicate [guilt](8033188.md) or [innocence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innocence). The assumption is that the truth is more likely to emerge from the open contest between the [prosecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecution) and the [defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_\(legal\)) in presenting the [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence_\(law\)) and opposing legal arguments, with a [judge](44719.md) acting as a neutral referee and as the arbiter of the law. In several jurisdictions in more serious cases, there is a [jury](44720.md) to determine the facts, although some common law jurisdictions have abolished the jury trial. This polarizes the issues, with each competitor acting in its own self-interest, and so presenting the facts and interpretations of the law in a deliberately biased way.&#x20;

The intention is that through a process of argument and counter-argument, [examination-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examination-in-chief) and [cross-examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-examination), each side will test the truthfulness, relevancy, and sufficiency of the opponent's evidence and arguments. To maintain fairness, there is a [presumption of innocence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presumption_of_innocence), and the [burden of proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_burden_of_proof) lies on the prosecution. Critics of the system argue that the desire to win is more important than the search for truth. Further, the results are likely to be affected by [structural inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_inequalities). Those defendants with resources can afford to hire the best [lawyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyers). Some trials are—or were—of a more summary nature, as certain questions of evidence were taken as resolved (see [handhabend and backberend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backberend_and_handhabend)).[^cite_note-Hale-3][^cite_note-Richards-4][^cite_note-Care-5]

### Inquisitorial

Main article: [Inquisitorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisitorial_system)



The [Trial of the 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_the_66) in *[Le Monde illustré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde_illustr%C3%A9)* of 27 January 1883, under the French inquisitorial system

In [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) legal systems, the responsibility for supervising the investigation by the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) into whether a crime has been committed falls on an [examining magistrate or judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examining_magistrate) who then conducts the trial. The assumption is that the truth is more likely to emerge from an impartial and exhaustive investigation, both before and during the trial itself. The examining magistrate or judge acts as an inquisitor who directs the fact-gathering process by questioning [witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witnesses), [interrogating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interrogating) the suspect, and collecting other evidence.&#x20;

The lawyers who represent the interests of the state and the accused have a limited role to offer legal arguments and alternative interpretations to the facts that emerge during the process. All the interested parties are expected to cooperate in the investigation by answering the magistrate or judge's questions and, when asked, supplying all relevant evidence. The trial only takes place after all the evidence has been collected and the investigation is completed. Thus, most of the factual uncertainties will already be resolved, and the examining magistrate or judge will already have resolved that there is *[prima facie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prima_facie)* of guilt.&#x20;

Critics argue that the examining magistrate or judge has too much power with the responsibilities of both investigating and adjudicating [on the merits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merit_\(law\)) of the case. Although [lay assessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_assessors) do sit as a form of jury to offer advice to the magistrate or judge at the conclusion of the trial, their role is subordinate. Further, because a professional has been in charge of all aspects of the case to the conclusion of the trial, there are fewer opportunities to appeal the conviction alleging some procedural error.[^cite_note-6]

## Mistrials

"Mistrial" redirects here. For other uses, see [Mistrial (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistrial_\(disambiguation\)).

A judge may cancel a trial prior to the return of a verdict; legal parlance designates this as a "**mistrial**". A judge may declare a mistrial due to:

- The court determining that it lacks [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) over a case.

- Evidence being admitted improperly, or new evidence that might seriously affect the outcome of the trial being discovered.

- Misconduct by a party, [juror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juror_misconduct),

  [^cite_note-7]

  &#x20;or an outside actor, if it prevents [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process).

- A [hung jury](621236.md) which cannot reach a verdict with the required degree of unanimity. In a criminal trial, if the jury is able to reach a verdict on some charges but not others, the defendant may be retried on the charges that led to the deadlock, at the discretion of the prosecution.

- Disqualification of a juror after the jury is empaneled, if no alternative juror is available and the litigants do not agree to proceed with the remaining jurors, or the remaining jurors not meeting the required number for a trial.

- The illness or death of a juror or attorney.

Either side may submit a motion for a mistrial; on occasion, the presiding judge may declare one on a motion of their own. If a mistrial is declared, the case at hand may be retried at the discretion of the plaintiff or prosecution, as long as [double jeopardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_jeopardy) does not bar that party from doing so.

## Other types

Some other kinds of processes for resolving conflicts are also expressed as trials. For example, the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) requires that, following the [impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_impeachment_in_the_United_States) of the president, a judge, or another federal officer by the [House of Representatives](19468510.md), the subject of the impeachment may only be removed from office by an [impeachment trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_impeachment_trial_in_the_United_States) in the [Senate](24909346.md), a practice which evolved from the British parliament's powers of [impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_in_the_United_Kingdom), wherein the House of Lords would also try public officials. In earlier times, disputes were often settled through a [trial by ordeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_by_ordeal), where parties would have to endure physical suffering in order to prove their righteousness; or through a [trial by combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_by_combat), in which the winner of a physical fight was deemed righteous in their cause.
