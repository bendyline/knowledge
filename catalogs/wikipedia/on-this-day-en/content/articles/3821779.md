**Kurds in Iran** ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): کورد لە ئێران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kurdish): *Kurdên Îranê*,[^cite_note-5] [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): کردها در ایران)[^cite_note-6] constitute a large minority in the country with a population of around 9 and 10 million people.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Most Iranian Kurds are bilingual in Kurdish and Persian.

## Geography

Kurds celebrating [Newroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newroz_as_celebrated_by_Kurds) in Iranian Kurdistan, 2024

[Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md) or Eastern Kurdistan (*_Rojhilatê Kurdistanê_*), is the parts of western [Iran](14653.md) inhabited by [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) which borders [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-Kurdish_Awakening_2014-9] It includes the [Kurdistan province](448112.md), [Kermanshah province](448078.md), [West Azerbaijan province](47567329.md), [Ilam province](448068.md), and [Lorestan province](433072.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

[Shia](26961.md) [Feyli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyli_Kurds) Kurds inhabit Kermanshah province, except for those parts where people are [Jaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaff), and Ilam province; as well as some parts of [Kurdistan](448112.md) and [Hamadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadan_province) provinces. The [Kurds of Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_of_Khorasan), in the [North Khorasan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Khorasan_province) of northeastern Iran, are [Shi'ite](26961.md) Muslims.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The [Lak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laks_\(Iran\)) tribe populate parts of [Ilam province](448068.md) and [Lorestan province](433072.md), while [Chegini Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chegini_Kurds) reside in central [Lorestan](433072.md).

## Religion

Main article: [Religion in Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Kurdistan)

The two major religions among Kurds in Iran are [Islam](6037917.md) and [Yarsanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism), while fewer Kurds adhere to [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) and [Judaism](15624.md).[^cite_note-14] There is disagreement on which is the largest denomination among Kurds; experts such as [Richard N. Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_N._Frye) and [Martin van Bruinessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_van_Bruinessen) argue that [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) (the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) branch[^cite_note-Shafii-3]) is the majority religion,[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] while researcher Anu Leinonen believes it is the [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver) branch of Shia Islam.[^cite_note-17]

Pockets of Sunni Kurds belong to the [Qadiriyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadiriyya) [tariqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqa) (around [Marivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marivan) and [Sanandaj](29953972.md)). These orders have experienced repression from the state, including the destruction of their [places of worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Places_of_worship).[^cite_note-Rudaw-4][^cite_note-18] Yarsanis are also targeted by the central government.[^cite_note-19]

## Political history

### Emergence of Kurdish nationalism

While Ottoman Kurdistan has been identified as the source of Kurdish national inspiration, Iranian Kurdistan has been identified as the ideological cradle for the emergence of Kurdish nationalism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202121–22-20]

In Iran, Kurdish intellectual writings and poetry from the 16th and 17th century indicate that the Kurdish population in the country was aware of the necessity of Kurdish unity and the need to form political and administrative entities for Kurds. However, these calls for Kurdish unity did not reach the broader Kurdish population until the 20th century when it awakened and diffused as a response to the implementation of nation-state policies ([Persianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianization)) by changing Iranian rulers. These policies not only alienated Kurds but also excluded them from equal access to citizenship. An example was the [Constitutional Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Constitutional_Revolution) of 1905–1911, which elevated [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) above Kurdish by asserting it as official language, language of administration and language of education.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202118–20-21] Unlike other minorities in Iran, the Kurds were historically fascinated with Iranian intellectuals, chiefly because the Pahlavi government emphasizing on pre-Islamic Iran had also strengthened Kurdish claims of [Median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes) descent.[^cite_note-22]

### Cross-border interaction (1918–1979)

Kurds have a strong cross-border ethnic linkage and few historical Kurdish rebellions were limited to the borders of a single country. For example, the rebellion of [Sheikh Ubeydullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Ubeydullah) in [Turkish Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Kurdistan) around 1880 inspired [Simko Shikak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak) to rebel in 1918, while the various [Barzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Barzani) rebellions in [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md) became a source of support for the [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202121-23] Other examples of cross-border interaction include the subjugation of the [Simko Shikak revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak_revolt_\(1918%E2%80%931922\)) forcing Simko to flee to [Rawandiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawandiz) in [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md) – where he sought the support of Sheikh [Mahmud Barzanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Barzanji).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202158–59-24] Following the fall of the [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad) in 1946, some of its leaders also fled to Iraqi Kurdistan where they were sheltered by the son of Sheikh Mahmud Barzanji. [Mustafa Barzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Barzani) had also supported the Republic of Mahabad by sending 2,100 soldiers which in turn also increased Kurdish self-confidence. Many teachers and military officers from Iraqi Kurdistan moreover crossed the border to support the republic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202158–59-24]

In 1944, the Society for the Revival of the Kurds/Kurdistan (JK) considered the first Kurdish nationalist movement met with a [Turkish Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_Turkey) delegation and an [Iraqi Kurdish](35423251.md) delegation at the border area near Mount Dalanpar where they signed the *Pact of Three Borders* which demonstrated the existence of a strong Kurdish sense of cross-border solidarity and sentiment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202158-25]

Cross-border interaction became difficult to sustain in the 1950s due to repression from [SAVAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAVAK) on the Iranian side. However, Kurds were able to reinforce the cross-border political activity, when the [First Iraqi–Kurdish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Iraqi%E2%80%93Kurdish_War) commenced in 1961, as the [Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan](3197182.md) (KDPI) gave financial support and loyalty to their counterpart in Iraq, the [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) (KDP), while KDPI themselves accessed spatial resources. Relations between KDP and KDPI would later deteriorate greatly as KDP became a close ally of SAVAK against Iraq. CIA documents from 1963 show that the KDP rebuffed support from KDPI due to the desire to maintain close relations with Iran.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202162–63-26]

In the 1970s, KDPI with [Komalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komalah_\(CPI\)) and the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md) (PUK) fought around [Piranshahr](30865712.md), [Sardasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardasht,_West_Azerbaijan), [Baneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baneh) in the northern parts of Iranian Kurdistan against Iranian forces who received support from KDP.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202164-27]

### Cross-border interaction after 1979

After the [Iranian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_revolution) in 1979, political infighting among Kurds increased and KDPI and Komala fought over political and spatial influence in Iranian Kurdistan as they were fighting Iran together. In the 1980s, the two political and military groups had become powerful and cross-border interaction was therefore less important.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHassaniyan202164–65-28]

## Separatism or autonomy

Main articles: [Iranian–Kurdish conflict](35032860.md), [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad), [Western Iran clashes (2016–2023)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\)), and [federalism in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Iran)

**Kurdish separatism in Iran**[^cite_note-habeeb-29] or the **Kurdish–Iranian conflict**[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] is an ongoing,[^cite_note-cornell10-32][^cite_note-uarkansas-33][^cite_note-habeeb-29][^cite_note-elling-34] long running dispute for full or partial autonomy between the Kurdish opposition in Western [Iran](14653.md) and the governments of Iran,[^cite_note-habeeb-29] lasting since the emergence of [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah) Pahlavi in 1918.[^cite_note-cornell10-32]

During the [Iranian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_revolution), Kurdish nationalist political parties were unsuccessful in attracting support, who at that time had no interest in autonomy.[^cite_note-David_Romano_page_235-35][^cite_note-McDowall_page_270-278-36] Since the 1990s, [Kurdish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_nationalism) in the region grew, partly due to anger at the government's violent suppression of Kurdish activism.[^cite_note-McDowall_page_278-37]
