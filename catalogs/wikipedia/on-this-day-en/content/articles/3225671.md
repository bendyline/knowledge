# List of active Royal Australian Navy ships

&#x20;  The [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) (RAN) fleet is made up of 32 [commissioned warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_commissioning) and 11 non-commissioned as of February 2026, with the non-commissioned vessels using the prefix of ADV (Australian Defence Vessel).

The main strength consists of three destroyers and seven frigates of the surface combatant force: three [*Hobart* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart-class_destroyer) destroyers and seven [*Anzac* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzac_class_frigate) frigates. Six [*Collins*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_class_submarine) boats make up the [submarine service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Navy_Submarine_Service). [Amphibious warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) assets include two [*Canberra*-class landing helicopter dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra-class_landing_helicopter_dock) ships and the [Bay-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay-class_landing_ship) landing ship [HMAS *Choules*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Choules). One [*Arafura-*&#x63;lass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura-class_offshore_patrol_vessel) and six [Cape-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape-class_patrol_boat) patrol boats perform coastal and [economic exclusion zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_exclusion_zone) patrols, and two [*Huon*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huon-class_minehunter) vessels are used for minehunting and clearance (four have been decommissioned). Replenishment at sea is provided by two [*Supply-*&#x63;lass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-class_replenishment_oiler) replenishment oilers, while the final remaining [*Leeuwin*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeuwin-class_survey_vessel) hydrographic survey vessel, [HMAS *Melville*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Melville_\(A_246\)), perform survey and charting duties.

In addition to the commissioned warships, the RAN operates the sail training ship [*Young Endeavour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Young_Endeavour), five non-commissioned Cape-class patrol boats and eight individual ships make up the National Support Squadron where vessels are owned by the RAN but manned by a mix of RAN officers and Teekay Shipping Australia personnel under the Australia Defence Maritime Support Services Program (DMSSP). Other auxiliaries and small craft are not operated by the RAN, but by [DMS Maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMS_Maritime), who are contracted to provide support services.[^cite_note-SaundersPhilpott35-1]

The majority of the RAN fleet is divided between Fleet Base East ([HMAS *Kuttabul*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Kuttabul_\(naval_base\)), in Sydney) and Fleet Base West ([HMAS *Stirling*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Stirling), near Perth). Mine warfare assets are located at [HMAS *Waterhen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Waterhen_\(naval_base\)) (also in Sydney), while [HMAS *Cairns*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Cairns_\(naval_base\)) in Cairns and [HMAS *Coonawarra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Coonawarra) in Darwin host the navy's patrol and survey vessels.

For over 30 years, the [Osborne Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborne_Naval_Shipyard) in [South Australia](26716.md) has been the main supplier of 'Tier 1 surface combatant' vessels to the RAN, having constructed the entire [Collins-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins-class_submarine) fleet and [Hobart-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart-class_destroyer) fleet. Osborne will further supply the navy with the currently under-construction [Hunter-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-class_frigate), as well as [SSN-AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN-AUKUS) [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine) [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine) and [Hobart-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart-class_destroyer) [guided-missile destroyer](232005.md) replacements. The [Henderson Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Marine_Complex) in [Western Australia](33613.md) will supply the RAN with smaller 'Tier 2 surface combatant' vessels. These include [Australian general purpose frigates](76691941.md), [Large Optionally-Crewed Surface Vessels (LOSV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Unmanned_Surface_Vehicle), and [Evolved Cape-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape-class_patrol_boat) and [Arafura-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura-class_offshore_patrol_vessel) patrol boats.

## Submarines

### *Collins* class

Main article: [Collins-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins-class_submarine)



[HMAS *Collins*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Collins), lead ship of her class

Australia operates a single class of diesel-electric submarines, the six [*Collins*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins-class_submarine) boats which began entering service in 1993. The *Collins* was designed by the Swedish submarine builder [Kockums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kockums) as the Type 471 specifically to meet Australian requirements, many of which were derived from Australia's need for great range without utilizing a nuclear propulsion system. The boats themselves were built in Australia by the [Australian Submarine Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASC_Pty_Ltd) in [Adelaide](1148.md). The submarines are classified by the RAN as guided missile submarines (SSG), but are often referred to as hunter-killer submarines (SSK) in the international press. While these vessels represented a major increase in capability for the RAN, they have found themselves mired in numerous technical and operational problems. Meanwhile, the RAN has struggled to sufficiently crew their submarine fleet, with at times no more than two qualified crews available.[^cite_note-did-collins-2]

The *Collins* was to be replaced by twelve [Shortfin Barracudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracuda-class_submarine_\(France\)#Shortfin_Barracuda_conventional_variant) a conventionally-powered design of the [Barracuda-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracuda-class_submarine_\(France\)) nuclear submarine by French shipbuilder [Naval Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Group) that had been selected in 2016 and named the [*Attack*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack-class_submarine).[^cite_note-3] In September 2021, in a joint announcement, it was announced that the *Attack*-class contract had been cancelled, and that Australia would acquire up to eight nuclear-powered submarines with US and UK support through a new trilateral security partnership between Australia, the UK and the US named [AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUKUS).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Pursuenuclear-5] In March 2023, AUKUS announced that Australia will buy three [*Virginia* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine) submarines from the US, with the option to buy two more, with the first boat planned to be delivered to Australia in the early 2030s.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-AUKUSMar23-7] AUKUS also announced that Australia and the UK will commence construction of a new nuclear-powered submarine class known as the [SSN-AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN-AUKUS) with the first Australian built SSN-AUKUS boat to be delivered by the early 2040s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinistry_of_Defence20237-8][^cite_note-AUKUSMar23-7] Australia will reportedly build five SSN-AUKUS boats.[^cite_note-9] The entire *Collins*-class fleet will now receive a Life-of-Type Extension.[^cite_note-Pursuenuclear-5]

## Amphibious warfare

### *Canberra* class

Main article: [Canberra-class landing helicopter dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra-class_landing_helicopter_dock)



[HMAS *Adelaide*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Adelaide_\(L01\))

The *Canberra* class are [landing helicopter dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_helicopter_dock) ships based on the design of [Spanish ship *Juan Carlos I*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_ship_Juan_Carlos_I). The hull of each ship was built by the designer, [Navantia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navantia), then was transported to Australia by [heavy lift ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_lift_ship) for internal fitout and installation of the superstructure by [BAE Systems Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Australia). Designed to transport and land an amphibious force of up to 1,600 soldiers by landing craft and helicopter, the *Canberra*s are the largest ships ever operated by the RAN. Lead ship [HMAS *Canberra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Canberra_\(L02\)) was commissioned into the RAN in late 2014. The second ship of the class, [*Adelaide*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Adelaide_\(L01\)), was commissioned at the end of 2015.

### *Choules*

Main article: [HMAS Choules (L100)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Choules_\(L100\))



[HMAS *Choules*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Choules) in 2012

The [Bay-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_class_landing_ship_dock) landing ship dock [HMAS *Choules*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Choules) was acquired by the RAN in 2011. The ship was originally built by [Swan Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Hunter) for the British [Royal Fleet Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Fleet_Auxiliary), and entered British service in 2006 as RFA *Largs Bay*. She was made redundant in the 2011 [Strategic Defence and Security Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_and_Security_Review_2010) and sold to Australia. *Choules* represents a major increase in [sealift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sealift) capability for the RAN, particularly after mechanical issues in 2010 and 2011 forced the early retirement of the navy's two [*Kanimbla*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanimbla-class_landing_platform_amphibious) vessels, and put [HMAS *Tobruk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Tobruk_\(L_50\)) in dock for an extensive refit.

Main article: [Hobart-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart-class_destroyer)



[HMAS *Hobart*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Hobart_\(DDG_39\)) in December 2017

These combined with the [SM-2 missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard) provide an advanced air defence system capable of engaging enemy aircraft and missiles at ranges in excess of 150 km (93 mi).

### *Anzac* class

Main article: [Anzac-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzac-class_frigate)



*Anzac*-class frigate [HMAS *Perth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Perth_\(FFH_157\)) (post-ASMD configuration)

There are seven [Frigates](11464.md) of the [*Anzac* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzac-class_frigate) (the lead ship of the class has been retired). These were commissioned from 1996 to 2006 as part of a joint program with [New Zealand](4913064.md), whose navy operates an additional two examples. Derived from [Blohm + Voss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blohm_+_Voss)' [MEKO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEKO) modular ship family and designated the [MEKO 200 ANZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEKO_200) by that company, the ships were built in Australia by [Tenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenix) in [Williamstown, Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamstown,_Victoria). They are designated as helicopter frigates (FFH) by the [RAN](26327.md), and are designed to be capable of both [mid-level patrol](536773.md) and [blue water operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy). In 2010, these vessels began to receive upgrades to their anti-ship missile defence (ASMD) capabilities.

## Patrol and defence

### *Arafura* class

Main article: [Arafura-class offshore patrol vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura-class_offshore_patrol_vessel)

The *Arafura-*&#x63;lass is a new fleet of offshore patrol vessels being built for the Royal Australian Navy. Originally, the plan was to replace 26 older ships from four different classes with 20 versatile vessels under the SEA 1180 project, each designed to take on a range of tasks like border patrol, mine clearance, and hydrographic surveys using modular mission systems. However, in 2013, the government decided to fast-track the replacement of the aging [*Armidale*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armidale-class_patrol_boat) patrol boats using an existing ship design, while extending the service life of the other vessels. This led to the current Offshore Patrol Vessel (OPV) program, which initially aimed 12 ships and a later proposal of expanded to it 14 with the addition of 2 mine countermeasure variants under a separate project, SEA 1905. This number was then reduced to just 6 of the original design as part of the 2024 Defence Strategic Review.

### *Cape* class

Main article: [Cape-class patrol boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape-class_patrol_boat)

Eight Cape-class patrol boats were built for the [Australian Customs and Border Protection Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Customs_and_Border_Protection_Service) (now the [Australian Border Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Border_Force)) by [Austal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austal) between 2012 and 2015, as replacements for the [Bay class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay-class_patrol_boat).[^cite_note-ASMcape-12][^cite_note-AusAwarded-13] Following the loss of [HMAS *Bundaberg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Bundaberg_\(ACPB_91\)) and hull issues with the [*Armidale*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armidale-class_patrol_boat) requiring an intense remedial maintenance program, two Cape-class patrol boats were leased to the RAN from late 2015 until the end of 2016.[^cite_note-McPhedranLease-14] The patrol boats operate from [HMAS *Cairns*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Cairns_\(naval_base\)) and [HMAS *Coonawarra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Coonawarra) in Darwin. These vessels are identified with the Australian Defence Vessel (ADV) [prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_prefix); ADV *Cape Fourcroy* and ADV *Cape Inscription* retain the blue-and-red customs colour scheme whilst the Evolved Cape-class ships have a grey naval colour scheme.[^cite_note-ParozCapeJoin-15] Two new boats were ordered in 2017, and the two leased vessels were returned to the ABF. These two were removed from service in 2026. 10 more were ordered between 2018 to 2023 to replace the ageing *Armidale-*&#x63;lass.

### *Huon* class

Main article: [Huon-class minehunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huon-class_minehunter)



*Huon*-class minehunter [HMAS *Gascoyne*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Gascoyne_\(M_85\))

Mine countermeasures at sea are handled by the *Huon*-class [minehunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minehunter), which began to enter RAN service from 1999. The class was based on the [Italian Navy](1126939.md)'s [*Gaeta*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaeta-class_minehunter) minehunter developed by [Intermarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermarine). Development was undertaken in partnership between Intermarine and [Australian Defence Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Australia) (ADI). The first hull was built in Italy, with fitting out the first and construction of the remaining five vessels of the class done by ADI in [Newcastle](52665.md), replacing the problematic [Bay-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay-class_minehunter) minehunters. In addition to the mine warfare role, they have been deployed on multiple occasions to support Northern Australian patrol and border protection operations. Two vessels operate out of [HMAS *Waterhen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Waterhen_\(naval_base\)), in [Sydney](27862.md). The others has been decommissioned and either sold (*Norman* and *Hawkesbury*) or awaiting further options (*Huon* and *Gascoyne*).

## Replenishment

### *Supply* class

Main article: [Supply-class replenishment oiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-class_replenishment_oiler)



The *Supply*-class replenishment oiler [HMAS *Supply*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Supply_\(A195\)) in 2022

## Hydrographic survey

### *Leeuwin* class

Main article: [Leeuwin-class survey vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeuwin-class_survey_vessel)



*Leeuwin*-class survey vessel [HMAS *Leeuwin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Leeuwin_\(A_245\))

Only one of the two *Leeuwin*-class survey ships is still active as of 3 November 2024. They were built for the RAN by [NQEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NQEA) of [Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairns). Ordered in 1996, the ships were commissioned in a joint ceremony in 2000. They are capable of charting waters up to 6,000 metres (20,000 ft) deep, and carry three [*Fantome*-class survey boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantome-class_survey_motor_boat) for shallow-water work. In addition to [hydrographic surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrographic_survey) duties, since 2001 both vessels have also operated in support of the RAN patrol force.

## Non-commissioned vessels

### *Young Endeavour*

Main articles: [STS Young Endeavour (1987)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Young_Endeavour_\(1987\)) and [STS Young Endeavour II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Young_Endeavour_II)



The youth crew of *Young Endeavour* manning the mast after the ship's arrival at the [Australian National Maritime Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_Maritime_Museum)

The [sail training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail_training) ship [*Young Endeavour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Young_Endeavour_\(1987\)) was built as a gift from the United Kingdom to Australia for the latter's 1988 [bicentenary of colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bicentenary). Built by British shipbuilder [Brooke Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke_Marine), the [brigantine rig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigantine_rig) vessel is operated by the RAN, but is used to facilitate the Young Endeavour Youth Scheme; a sail training program for Australian youth aged between 16 and 23. A 10-strong RAN crew is supplemented by 24–30 youth on ten-day voyages, with 500 applicants selected every year through two ballots.

### National Support Squadron



[ADV *Guidance*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADV_Guidance) at Fleet Base East in November 2023

In addition to commissioned ships, the RAN is supported by the [National Support Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Support_Squadron), which operates a variety of vessels by civilian companies under various contract arrangements, with their taskings and operations in support of Navy capabilities. This includes [Teekay Shipping Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teekay), which operates with mainly civilian mariners and naval support staff for the tasking being undertaken. All ships within the National Support Squadron feature a uniform saphire blue hull and white superstructure.
