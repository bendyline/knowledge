**Soyuz MS-28**, identified by NASA as **Soyuz 74S**, was a Russian crewed [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_\(spacecraft\)) spaceflight launched from [Site 31/6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome_Site_31) at the [Baikonur Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome) on 27 November 2025 to the [International Space Station](15043.md), where it docked to Russia's Rassvet docking port.

## Mission

The Soyuz mission was initially scheduled to use [Soyuz MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS) vehicle No. 759, which was next in line for assembly at [Energia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energia_\(corporation\))'s factory in [Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korolyov,_Moscow_Oblast). During post-production testing, the spacecraft reportedly sustained major damage to its [heat shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ablative_heat_shield) that could not be repaired in time for the planned launch at the end of 2025. Reports suggested that the shield was either detached when its [pyrotechnic bolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrotechnic_fastener) were inadvertently triggered (similar to the procedure that occurs shortly before landing) or that its thermal layers delaminated during a poorly executed test. As of October 2025, [Roscosmos](406573.md) had not acknowledged the incident, but photographs released from the Baikonur Cosmodrome showed that Soyuz MS vehicle No. 753 was being prepared for flight.[^cite_note-Zak_2025-1]

Vehicle No. 753, along with No. 752, had originally been allocated for prospective commercial missions. Vehicle No. 752 was used for the [Soyuz MS-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS-20) space tourism flight, but following [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), commercial contracts with the Russian space program were cancelled. As a result, No. 753 was reassigned to regular International Space Station operations. The substitution also allowed Roscosmos to utilize the older spacecraft before its onboard systems exceeded their certified service life.[^cite_note-Zak_2025-1]

Preparations for launch began in early October 2025 at the Baikonur Cosmodrome, including testing of the spacecraft's [Kurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurs-NA) rendezvous system, leak checks in a vacuum chamber, and verification of propulsion, guidance, and communications systems.[^cite_note-Zak_2025-1]

The [Soyuz-2.1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-2.1a) launch vehicle and payload fairing for the mission arrived by rail at Baikonur on 22 October 2025.[^cite_note-Zak_2025-1]



Launch of Soyuz MS-28

The mission launched successfully on 27 November 2025 at 09\:27\:57 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time) (14\:27\:57 [AQTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Kazakhstan), local time at the launch site),[^cite_note-spacefacts-2] and the crew arrived at the ISS a little over three hours later at 12\:34 UTC. However, the launch pad at [Site 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome_Site_31) was damaged during the launch, with the mobile service platform beneath the pad appearing to have collapsed into the flame trench. The extent of the damage temporarily rendered Russia's only operational crewed launch facility unusable. The next planned launch from the site, the [Progress MS-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS-33) cargo mission scheduled for late December, was delayed to 22 March 2026.[^cite_note-nextspaceflight-3] In the hours following the incident, Roscosmos issued a statement saying it was assessing the situation, had the necessary spare parts, and expected repairs to be completed soon.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Repairs to the pad were completed on 3 March 2026.[^cite_note-Papachristou_2026-03-03-6][^cite_note-7]

On the other side, the arrival of Soyuz MS-28 meant that all eight docking ports aboard the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS) are occupied following the reinstallation of [Cygnus NG-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_NG-23) to [Unity module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_\(ISS_module\)) on 1 December 2025. It was held on [Canadarm2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm2) away from its docking port on 24 November 2025 to provide docking clearance to MS-28. The space station currently hosts: two SpaceX [Dragon 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2) spacecraft ([Crew-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew-11) and [CRS-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_CRS-33)), the Northrop Grumman [Cygnus XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_\(spacecraft\)) freighter ([NG-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_NG-23)), the JAXA's [HTV-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTV-X) cargo ship ([HTV-X1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTV-X1)), Roscosmos [Soyuz MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS) crew vehicles ([MS-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS-27) and MS-28), and two [Progress MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS) cargo spacecraft ([MS-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS-31) and [MS-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS-32)).[^cite_note-8]

On the lower stage of this rocket, the Unity Foundation in collaboration with Roscosmos, wrapped the fuselage with art painted by children from across the world. This project, dubbed "The Art Rocket" and "Rocket of Dreams", was an artistic expression for children with cancer, enabling them to send their dreams to space.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Crew

The mission was slated to be the first to launch after the termination of a NASA/Roscosmos barter agreement, where one Russian cosmonaut flies on a NASA spacecraft in exchange for one NASA astronaut flying on a Soyuz. Consequently, as of 2024, this mission was scheduled to transport three Russian cosmonauts. However, NASA and Roscosmos were negotiating to extend their seat exchange program beyond 2025,[^cite_note-12] and in April 2025, NASA announced that [Christopher Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Williams_\(astronaut\)) had been assigned to the crew of Soyuz MS-28, serving as a flight engineer.[^cite_note-13]
