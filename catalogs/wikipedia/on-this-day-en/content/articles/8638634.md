**Lyon**[^cite_note-12] ([Franco-Provençal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Proven%C3%A7al): *Liyon*; formerly *Lyons* in English)[^cite_note-14] is a city in [France](5843419.md). It is located at the confluence of the rivers [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne) and [Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne), to the northwest of the [French Alps](961317.md), 391 km (243 mi) southeast of Paris, 278 km (173 mi) north of [Marseille](40888948.md), and 113 km (70 mi) southwest of [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), Switzerland.

The City of Lyon is the [third-largest city](76050805.md) in France[^cite_note-15] with a population of 519,127 as of 2023 within its small municipal territory of 48 km<sup>2</sup> (19 sq mi),[^cite_note-pophist-16] but together with its suburbs and [exurbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurb) the Lyon [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area_\(France\)) had a population of 2,338,789 that same year,[^cite_note-compterr-5] the [second largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area_\(France\)#List_of_functional_areas_\(AAV\)) in France. Lyon and 58 suburban municipalities have formed since 2015 the [Metropolis of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metropolis), a directly elected metropolitan authority now in charge of most urban issues, with a population of 1,436,354 in 2023.[^cite_note-compterr-5] Lyon is the [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) of the [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](45093325.md) [region](58846.md) and seat of the [Departmental Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_council_\(France\)) of [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_\(department\)) (whose jurisdiction, however, no longer extends over the Metropolis of Lyon since 2015).

The capital of the [Gauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls) during the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), Lyon is the seat of an [archbishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Lyon) whose holder bears the title of Primate of the Gauls. Lyon became a major economic hub during the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Renaissance). The city is recognised for its [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyonnaise_cuisine) and [gastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomy), as well as historical and architectural landmarks; as such, the districts of [Old Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieux_Lyon), the [Fourvière hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourvi%C3%A8re), the [Presqu'île](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presqu'%C3%AEle) and the slopes of the [Croix-Rousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Croix-Rousse) are inscribed on the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage List](44940.md). Lyon was historically an important area for the production and weaving of silk. Lyon played a significant role in the [history of cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_film) since [Auguste and Louis Lumière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_and_Louis_Lumi%C3%A8re) invented the [cinematograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematograph) there. The city is also known for its light festival, the [Fête des lumières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_of_Lights_\(Lyon\)), which begins every 8 December and lasts for four days, earning Lyon the title of "Capital of Lights".

Economically, Lyon is a major centre for banking, [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical), [pharmaceutical](560876.md) and [biotech](4502.md) industries. The city contains a significant software industry with a particular focus on video games; in recent years it has fostered a growing local start-up sector.[^cite_note-17] The home of renowned universities and higher education schools, Lyon is the second-largest student city in France, with a university population of nearly 200,000 students within the Metropolis of Lyon.[^cite_note-18] Lyon hosts the international headquarters of [Interpol](14338129.md), the [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer), as well as [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews). According to the [Globalization and World Rankings Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network), Lyon is considered a [Beta city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city), as of 2018.[^cite_note-19] It ranked second in France and 40th globally in Mercer's 2019 [liveability rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_quality_of_living).[^cite_note-20] Lyon will be the centre of the [2030 Winter Olympics](57658858.md).

### Toponymy

The name of the city has evolved from the Gallic *Lugdunon* to the intermediate forms *Lugdon* to *Luon*, and developing the present form, *Lyon*, during the 13th century.

The Gallic *Lugdun* or *Lugdunon* that was Latinized in Roman as [Lugdunum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugdunum) is composed of two words. The first may be the name of the Celtic god [Lug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugh) (in charge of order and law), or the derived word *lugon*, meaning "crow" (the crow being the messenger of Lug), but might also be another word *lug*, meaning "light". The second is *dunos* ('fortress', 'hill'). The name thus may designate the [hill of Fourvière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourvi%C3%A8re), on which the ancient city of Lyon is founded, but could mean "hill of the god Lug", "hill of the crows" or "shining hill".[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Alternatively [Julius Pokorny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Pokorny) associates the first part of the word with the Indo-European radical \**lūg* ('dark, black, swamp'), the basis of the toponyms [Ludza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludza) in Latvia, [Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusatia) in Germany (from [Sorbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbian_language) *Łužica*), and several places in the Czech Republic named Lužice;[^cite_note-23] it could then also be compared to [Luze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luze,_Haute-Sa%C3%B4ne) in Franche-Comté and various hydronyms such as [Louge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louge).

Further down, in the current Saint-Vincent district, was the Gallic village of Condate, probably a simple hamlet of sailors or fishermen living on the banks of the Saône. *Condate* is a Gallic word meaning "confluence", from which the Confluence district gets its name.

In [Roman times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lyon#Antiquity) the city was called *Caput Galliae*, meaning "capital of the Gauls". As an homage to this title, the [Archbishop of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Lyon) is still called the Primate of Gaul.

During the revolutionary period, Lyon was renamed *Commune-Affranchie* ("[Emancipated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affranchi) [Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_\(France\))") on 12 October 1793 by a decree of the [Convention Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Convention). It resumed its name in 1794, after the end of [the Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign_of_Terror).

### Ancient Lyon

Main article: [Lugdunum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugdunum)

According to the historian [Dio Cassius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dio_Cassius), in 43 BC, the [Roman Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate) ordered the creation of a settlement for Roman refugees of war with the [Allobroges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allobroges). These refugees had been expelled from [Vienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienne,_Isere) and were now encamped at the confluence of the [Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne) and [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne) rivers. The foundation was built on Fourvière hill and officially called *Colonia Copia Felix Munatia*, a name invoking prosperity and the blessing of the gods. The city became increasingly referred to as *Lugdunum* (and occasionally *Lugudunum*[^cite_note-25]).[^cite_note-26] The earliest translation of this Gaulish place-name as "Desired Mountain" is offered by the 9th-century *[Endlicher Glossary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endlicher_Glossary?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.[^cite_note-27] In contrast, some modern scholars have proposed a Gaulish hill-fort named Lug\[o]dunon, after the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_mythology) god [Lugus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugus) (cognate with [Old Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language) *Lugh*, Modern Irish *Lú*), and *dúnon* (hill-fort).



[The Roman-era Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Theatre_of_Fourvi%C3%A8re) on the Fourvière Hill

The Romans recognised that Lugdunum's strategic location at the convergence of two navigable rivers made it a natural communications hub. The city became the starting point of main [Roman roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_roads) in the area, and it quickly became the capital of the province, [Gallia Lugdunensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallia_Lugdunensis). Two Emperors were born in this city: [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius), whose speech is preserved in the [Lyon Tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Tablet) in which he justifies the nomination of Gallic Senators, and [Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracalla).

[Early Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christians) in Lyon were martyred for their beliefs under the reigns of various Roman emperors, most notably [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius) and [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus).[^cite_note-28] Local saints from this period include [Blandina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blandina), [Pothinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pothinus), and [Epipodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipodius_and_Alexander), among others. The Greek [Irenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus) was the second bishop of Lyon during the latter part of the second century.[^cite_note-29] To this day, the archbishop of Lyon is still referred to as "*[Primat des Gaules](24672.md)*".[^cite_note-30]

[Burgundians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundians) fleeing the destruction of [Worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worms,_Germany) by the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) in 437 were re-settled in eastern Gaul. In 443 the Romans established the Kingdom of the Burgundians, and Lugdunum became its capital in 461. In 843, under the [Treaty of Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Verdun), Lyon went to the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) [Lothair I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothair_I). It later was made part of the Kingdom of Arles which was incorporated into the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1033. Lyon did not come under French control until the 14th century.

### Modern Lyon

[Fernand Braudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_Braudel) remarked, "Historians of Lyon are not sufficiently aware of the bi-polarity between Paris and Lyon, which is a constant structure in French development...from the late Middle Ages to the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution)".[^cite_note-31] In the late 15th century, the [fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairs) introduced by Italian merchants made Lyon the economic [counting house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counting_house) of France. Even the *Bourse* (treasury), built in 1749, resembled a public bazaar where accounts were settled in the open air. When international banking moved to [Genoa](47332321.md), then [Amsterdam](844.md), Lyon remained the banking centre of France.

During the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), the city's development was driven by the [silk trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_trade), which strengthened its ties to Italy. Italian influence on Lyon's architecture is still visible among historic buildings.[^cite_note-32] In the late 1400s and 1500s Lyon was also a key centre of literary activity and book publishing, both of French writers (such as [Maurice Scève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Sc%C3%A8ve), [Antoine Héroet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_H%C3%A9roet), and [Louise Labé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Lab%C3%A9)) and of Italians in exile (such as [Luigi Alamanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Alamanni) and [Gian Giorgio Trissino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Giorgio_Trissino)).



Lyon under siege in 1793



Massacre during the [Canut rebellion of 1834](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canut_revolts)

In 1572, Lyon was a scene of mass violence by Catholics against Protestant [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) in the [St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Bartholomew's_Day_Massacre). Two centuries later, Lyon was again convulsed by violence during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), when the citizenry rose up against the National Convention and supported the [Girondins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girondins). The city was besieged by Revolutionary armies for over two months before it surrendered in October 1793. Many buildings were destroyed, especially around the [Place Bellecour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_Bellecour), and [Jean-Marie Collot d'Herbois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Collot_d'Herbois) and [Joseph Fouché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Fouch%C3%A9) administered the execution of more than 2,000 people. The Convention ordered that its name be changed to "Liberated City", and a plaque was erected that proclaimed "Lyon made war on Liberty; Lyon no longer exists". A decade later, [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) ordered the reconstruction of all the buildings demolished during that period.

The convention was not the only target within Lyon during the French Revolution. After the Convention faded into history, the [French Directory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Directory) appeared and days after the 4 September 1797 [Coup of 18 Fructidor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_of_18_Fructidor), a Directory's commissioner was assassinated in Lyon.

The city became an important industrial town in the 19th century. In 1831 and 1834, the *[canuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canut)* (silk workers) of Lyon staged [two major uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canut_revolts) for better working conditions and pay. In 1862, the first of [Lyon's extensive network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funiculars_of_Lyon) of [funicular railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_railway) began operation. During the [repression of January and February 1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repression_of_January_and_February_1894), the police conducted at least dozens of raids targeting the [anarchists](12.md) living there, without much success.[^cite_note-\:12-33][^cite_note-\:22-34][^cite_note-\:32-35] Some months later, in June 1894, [companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_companionship) [Sante Caserio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sante_Geronimo_Caserio) [assassinated the French president responsible for the repression, Sadi Carnot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Sadi_Carnot).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBouhey2009294-298-36]

During [World War II](32927.md), Lyon was a centre for the occupying [Nazi](21212.md) forces, including [Klaus Barbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Barbie), the infamous "Butcher of Lyon". However, the city was also a stronghold of the [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance), the many secret passages known as *[traboules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traboule)*, enabled people to escape [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) raids. On 3 September 1944, Lyon was liberated by the [1st Free French Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Free_French_Division) and the [Forces Françaises de l'Intérieur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_Fran%C3%A7aises_de_l'Int%C3%A9rieur). The city is now home to a Resistance museum.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

## Geography



The Saône-Rhône confluence

The Rhône and Saône converge to the south of the historic city centre, forming a peninsula – the "*[Presqu'île](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presqu'%C3%AEle_\(Lyon\))*" – bounded by two large hills to the west and north and a large plain eastward. Place Bellecour is located on the Presqu'île between the two rivers and is the third-largest public square in France. The broad, pedestrian-only Rue de la République leads north from Place Bellecour.

The northern hill is [La Croix-Rousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Croix-Rousse), known as "the hill that works" because it is traditionally home to many small silk workshops, an industry for which the city has long been renowned.[^cite_note-39]

The western hill is Fourvière, known as "the hill that prays" because it is the location for [Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Notre-Dame_de_Fourvi%C3%A8re), several convents, and [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop)'s residence. The district, [Vieux Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieux_Lyon), also hosts the [Tour métallique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallic_tower_of_Fourvi%C3%A8re) (a highly visible TV tower, replicating the last stage of the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower)) and one of the city's railways.[^cite_note-40] Fourvière, along with portions of the Presqu'île and much of La Croix-Rousse, is designated as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-UNESCO-41]

East of the Rhône from the Presqu'île is a large flat area upon which sits much of modern Lyon and contains most of the city's population. Situated in this area is [La Part-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Part-Dieu) urban centre, which clusters the landmark structures [Tour To-Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_To-Lyon), [Tour Incity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_Incity), [Tour Part-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_du_Cr%C3%A9dit_Lyonnais), [Tour Oxygène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_Oxyg%C3%A8ne), and [Tour Swiss Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_Swiss_Life), as well as the city's primary railway station, [Gare de Lyon-Part-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_de_Lyon-Part-Dieu).

North of this district lays the sixth [arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement), which is home to one of Europe's largest urban parks, the [Parc de la Tête d'or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_la_T%C3%AAte_d'or), as well as [Lycée du Parc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_du_Parc) and [Interpol](14338129.md)'s world headquarters.

### Climate



Ice on the Saône, 2012

Lyon has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*).[^cite_note-42] The mean temperature in Lyon in the coldest month is 4.1 °C (39.4 °F) in January and in the warmest month in July is 22.6 °C (72.7 °F). Precipitation is adequate year-round, at an average of 820 mm (32.3 in); the winter months are the driest. The highest recorded temperature was 41.4 °C (106.5 °F) on 24 August 2023 while the lowest recorded temperature was −24.6 °C (−12.3 °F) on 22 December 1938.[^cite_note-MFrecords-43]

## Administration

### Commune

Main article: [Arrondissements of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Lyon)



Map of the City of Lyon divided into 9 [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Lyon)

Like Paris and [Marseille](40888948.md), the [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) (municipality) of Lyon is divided into a number of [municipal arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_arrondissements_of_France), each of which is identified by a number and has its own council and town hall. Five arrondissements were originally created in 1852, when three [neighbouring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood) communes (La Croix-Rousse, La Guillotière, and Vaise) were annexed by Lyon. Between 1867 and 1959, the third arrondissement (which originally covered the whole of the Left Bank of the Rhône) was split three times, creating a new arrondissement in each case. Then, in 1963, the commune of Saint-Rambert-l'Île-Barbe was annexed to Lyon's fifth arrondissement. A year later, in 1964, the fifth was split to create Lyon's 9th – and, to date, final – arrondissement. Within each arrondissement, the recognisable *quartiers* or neighbourhoods are:

- [1st arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_arrondissement_of_Lyon): Slopes of La Croix-Rousse, [Terreaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_des_Terreaux), Martinière/St-Vincent

- [2nd arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_arrondissement_of_Lyon): [Cordeliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Cordeliers), Bellecour, [Ainay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainay), [Perrache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perrache_\(quarter\)), Confluence, Sainte-Blandine

- [3rd arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_arrondissement_of_Lyon): Guillotière (north), Préfecture, Part-Dieu, Villette, Dauphiné/Sans Souci, Montchat, Grange Blanche (north), Monplaisir (north)

- [4th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_arrondissement_of_Lyon): Plateau de la Croix-Rousse, Serin

- [5th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_arrondissement_of_Lyon): Vieux Lyon ([Saint-Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Paul_\(Lyon\)), [Saint-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Cathedral), [Saint-Georges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Georges_de_Lyon)), [Saint-Just](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Just_\(Lyon\)), Saint-Irénée,

  [^cite_note-48]

  &#x20;Fourvière, Point du Jour, Ménival, Battières, Champvert (south)

- [6th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_arrondissement_of_Lyon): [Brotteaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Brotteaux), Bellecombe, Parc de la Tête d'or, Cité Internationale

- [7th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_arrondissement_of_Lyon): Guillotière (south), Jean Macé, Gerland

- [8th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_arrondissement_of_Lyon): Monplaisir (south), [Bachut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Bachut), États-Unis, Grand Trou/Moulin à Vent, Grange Blanche (south), [Laënnec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La%C3%ABnnec), Mermoz, Monplaisir-la-Plaine

- [9th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_arrondissement_of_Lyon): [Vaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaise), Duchère, Rochecardon, St-Rambert-l'Île-Barbe, Gorge de Loup, Observance, Champvert (north)

Geographically, Lyon's two main rivers, the Saône and the Rhône, divide the arrondissements into three groups:

- To the west of the Saône, the fifth arrondissement covers the old city of Vieux Lyon, Fourvière hill and the plateau beyond. The 9th is immediately to the north, and stretches from Gorge de Loup, through Vaise to the neighbouring suburbs of Écully, Champagne-au-Mont-d'Or, Saint-Didier-au-Mont-d'Or, Saint-Cyr-au-Mont-d'Or and Collonges-au-Mont-d'Or.

- Between the two rivers, on the Presqu'île, are the second, first, and fourth arrondissements. The second includes most of the city centre, Bellecour and Perrache railway station, and reaches as far as the confluence of the two rivers. The first is directly to the north of the second and covers part of the city centre (including the [Hôtel de Ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Ville,_Lyon)) and the slopes of La Croix-Rousse. To the north of the Boulevard is the fourth arrondissement, which covers the Plateau of La Croix-Rousse, up to its boundary with the commune of Caluire-et-Cuire.

- To the east of the Rhône, are the third, sixth, seventh, and eighth arrondissements.

### Metropolis



Map of the [Metropolis of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Lyon) and its 59 communes (The commune of Lyon is in red.)



Map showing the 14 electoral wards of the Metropolis of Lyon

Since 2015, the commune of Lyon (48 km<sup>2</sup>; 19 sq mi in land area) and 58 suburban communes have formed the Metropolis of Lyon (534 km<sup>2</sup>; 206 sq mi in land area), a directly elected metropolitan authority now in charge of most urban issues. The Metropolis of Lyon is the only metropolitan authority in France which is a [territorial collectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_collectivity), on par with French communes and [departments](38523.md). Its metropolitan council was for the first time directly elected by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) in 2020 within 14 electoral wards, the only directly elected metropolitan council in France.

The 14 electoral wards are the following (see map for location):

&#x20; Porte des Alpes

&#x20; Val de Saône

&#x20; Villeurbanne

The six wards with names starting with "Lyon" are all located within the commune of Lyon. The [Villeurbanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeurbanne) ward is coterminous with the namesake commune. All other seven wards each group various suburban communes.

The division of the Metropolis of Lyon in large electoral wards often grouping various communes and dividing the commune of Lyon into six wards was criticized by the suburban mayors, as it ended the rule of 'one commune, one metropolitan councilor'. The goal of this electoral division of the metropolis was to focus metropolitan elections more on metropolitan issues than parochial communal issues, and ensure the 'one person, one vote' rule be respected, by creating electoral wards of more homogeneous population sizes. Opponents said it diluted the voice of the small suburban communes, which are now part of large electoral wards and do not each possess a representative in the metropolitan council anymore.

#### Presidents of the Metropolitan Council

The two first presidents of the Metropolis of Lyon's metropolitan council were chosen by indirectly elected metropolitan councilors. The current president since July 2020 was elected by new metropolitan councilors following their election by universal suffrage in March (1st round) and June (2nd round) 2020, the first direct election of a metropolitan council in France.

## Economy

See also: [Industrial history of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_history_of_Lyon)



[La Part-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Part-Dieu), the city's central business district

The GDP of Lyon was 124 billion US dollars in 2019,[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] making it the second richest city in France after Paris. Lyon and its region [Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes) represent one of the most important economies in Europe and, according to Loughborough University, can be compared to Philadelphia, Mumbai or Athens with regard to its international position. The city of Lyon is working in partnership to more easily enable the establishment of new headquarters in the territory (ADERLY, [Chambre du commerce et d'industrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambre_de_commerce_et_d'industrie_de_Lyon), [Grand Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Community_of_Lyon)...). High-tech industries such as biotechnology, software development, video game ([Arkane Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkane_Studios), [Ivory Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Tower_\(company\)), [Eden Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Games), [EA France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_France?action=edit\&redlink=1), Bandai Namco Entertainment Europe), and internet services are also growing. Other important sectors include medical research and technology, non-profit institutions, and universities. Lyon is home to the P4-Inserm–ean Merieux Laboratory which conducts top-level vaccine research.[^cite_note-52]

The city is home to the headquarters of many large companies such as [Groupe SEB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_SEB), [Sanofi Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanofi_Pasteur), [Renault Trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trucks), [Norbert Dentressangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Dentressangle), [LCL S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCL_S.A.), [Descours & Cabaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descours_&_Cabaud?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Merial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merial), [Point S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_S?action=edit\&redlink=1), [BioMérieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioM%C3%A9rieux), [Iveco Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveco_Bus), [Compagnie Nationale du Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_Nationale_du_Rh%C3%B4ne), [GL Events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GL_Events?action=edit\&redlink=1), [April Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Group), [Boiron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiron), Feu Vert, [Panzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzani), [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat), [Lyon Airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Airports), LVL Medical, and inter-governmental agencies [IARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer) and Interpol. The specialisation of some sectors of activities has led to the creation of many main business centres: La Part-Dieu, located in the 3rd arrondissement is the second biggest business quarter after [La Défense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_D%C3%A9fense) in Paris with over 1,600,000 m<sup>2</sup> (17,222,256.67 sq ft) of office space and services and more than 55,000 jobs.[^cite_note-53] *Cité Internationale*, created by the architect Renzo Piano is located in the border of the Parc de la Tête d'Or in the 6th arrondissement. The worldwide headquarters of Interpol is located there. The district of *Confluence*, in the south of the historic centre, is a new pole of economical and cultural development.

Tourism is an important part of the Lyon economy, with one billion euros in 2007 and 3.5 million hotel-nights in 2006 provided by non-residents. Approximately 60% of tourists visit for business, with the rest for leisure. In January 2009, Lyon ranked first in France for hostels business. The festivals most important for attracting tourists are the *[Fête des lumières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_of_Lights_\(Lyon\))*, the *[Nuits de Fourvière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuits_de_Fourvi%C3%A8re)* every summer, the *Biennale d'art contemporain* and the *[Nuits Sonores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuits_Sonores)*.

## Culture



[Guignol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guignol), created in the early 19th century, associated with the silk-workers

Since the Middle Ages, the region residents have spoken several dialects of [Franco-Provençal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Proven%C3%A7al). The Lyonnais dialect was replaced by the French language as the importance of the city grew. However some "frenchified" Franco-Provençal words can also be heard in the French of the Lyonnais, who call their little boys and girls "gones" and "fenottes" for example.[^cite_note-Onofrio-54]

- The [Lumière brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_and_Louis_Lumi%C3%A8re) pioneered cinema in the town in 1895. The [Institut Lumière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Lumi%C3%A8re), built as Auguste Lumiere's house, and a fascinating piece of architecture in its own right, holds many of their first inventions and other early cinematic and photographic artifacts.

- 8 December each year is marked by the Festival of Lights (la Fête des lumières), a celebration of thanks to the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_\(mother_of_Jesus\)), who purportedly saved the city from a deadly plague in the Middle Ages. During the event, the local population places candles (*luminions*) at their windows and the city of Lyon organizes large-scale light shows onto the sides of important Lyonnais monuments, such as the medieval [Cathédrale St-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Cathedral).

- The [Saint Francis of Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_de_Sales) church is famous for its large and unaltered [Cavaillé-Coll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Cavaill%C3%A9-Coll) pipe organ, attracting audiences from around the world.

- The Opéra Nouvel (New Opera House) is the home of the [Opéra National de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_National_de_Lyon). The original opera house was re-designed by the distinguished French architect Jean Nouvel between 1985 and 1993 and is named after him.

- Lyon is also the French capital of "*[trompe l'œil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trompe-l'%C5%93il)*" walls, a very ancient tradition. Many are to be seen around the city. This old tradition is now finding a contemporary expression, for example in the art of Guillaume Bottazzi.

### UNESCO World Heritage Site



Passage de l'Argue

The historic site of Lyon was designated a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1998. In its designation, UNESCO cited the "exceptional testimony to the continuity of urban settlement over more than two millennia on a site of great commercial and strategic significance."[^cite_note-UNESCO-41] The specific regions comprising the historic site include the Roman district and Fourvière, the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) district (Vieux Lyon), the silk district (slopes of Croix-Rousse), and the Presqu'île, which features architecture from the 12th century to modern times.[^cite_note-58]

Both Vieux Lyon and the slopes of Croix-Rousse are known for their narrow passageways (named *traboules*) that pass through buildings and link streets on either side. The first examples of traboules are thought to have been built in Lyon in the 4th century.[^cite_note-59] The traboules allowed the inhabitants to get from their homes to the Saône quickly and allowed the canuts on the Croix-Rousse hill to get from their workshops to the textile merchants at the foot of the hill.

### Gastronomy

Main article: [Lyonnaise cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyonnaise_cuisine)



Île Barbe bakery at the [Halles de Lyon-Paul Bocuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halles_de_Lyon-Paul_Bocuse?action=edit\&redlink=1)

Lyon has a long and chronicled culinary arts tradition. The noted food critic [Curnonsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curnonsky) referred to the city as "the gastronomic capital of the world",[^cite_note-60] a claim repeated by later writers such as [Bill Buford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Buford).[^cite_note-61] Renowned 3-star Michelin chefs such as [Marie Bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bourgeois)[^cite_note-62] and [Eugénie Brazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A9nie_Brazier)[^cite_note-63] developed Lyonnaise cuisine into a national phenomenon favoured by the French elite; a tradition which [Paul Bocuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bocuse) later turned into a worldwide success.[^cite_note-64] The *[bouchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouchon)* is a traditional Lyonnais restaurant that serves local fare such as sausages, duck pâté or roast pork, along with local wines. Two of France's best known wine-growing regions are located near the city: the [Beaujolais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaujolais_\(wine\)) region to the north and the [Côtes du Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4tes_du_Rh%C3%B4ne_AOC) region to the south. Another Lyon tradition is a type of [brunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunch) food called "mâchons", made of local [charcuterie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcuterie) and usually accompanied by Beaujolais red wine. Mâchons were the customary meal of the canuts, the city's silk workers, who ate a late-morning meal after they finished their shifts in the factories.[^cite_note-65]

Other traditional local dishes include [quenelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quenelle); [gras double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gras_double); [salade lyonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salade_lyonnaise?action=edit\&redlink=1) (lettuce with bacon, croûtons and a poached egg); and the sausage-based [rosette lyonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosette_de_Lyon) and [andouillette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andouillette). Popular local confections include [marron glacé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marron_glac%C3%A9) and [coussin de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coussin_de_Lyon). [Cervelle de canut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervelle_de_canut) (literally, "silk worker's brains") is a cheese spread/dip made of a base of [fromage blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fromage_frais), seasoned with chopped herbs, shallots, salt, pepper, olive oil and vinegar.

More recently, [French tacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_tacos) appears to have been invented in the early 2000s in the Lyon suburb [Vaulx-en-Velin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaulx-en-Velin), though it might plausibly have been invented in nearby [Grenoble](50972.md). This [street food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_food) became popular in the Lyon region and then across France; it is now famous worldwide.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

[Parc Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_Olympique_Lyonnais)

Lyon is home to the [football](10568.md) club [Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) (OL), whose men's team plays in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) and has won the championship of that competition seven times, all consecutively from 2002 to 2008.[^cite_note-68] OL played until December 2015 at the 43,000-seat [Stade de Gerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_Gerland), which also hosted matches of the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). Since 2016, the team has played at the Parc Olympique Lyonnais, a 59,000-seat stadium located in the eastern suburb of [Décines-Charpieu](5146934.md).[^cite_note-69] OL operates a women's team, [OL Lyonnes](8587183.md), which competes in and dominates the [Première Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premi%C3%A8re_Ligue). They won fourteen consecutive top-flight championships (2007–2020), and additionally claim the four titles won by the original incarnation of FC Lyon, a women's football club that merged into OL in 2004 (the current FC Lyon was founded in 2009). OL Lyonnes have also won the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) eight times, including in five consecutive editions from 2016 to 2020, and hosted the [2019 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) semi-finals as well as the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Final) on 7 July at Stade de Lyon.



[Stade de Gerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_Gerland)

Lyon has a [rugby union](25405.md) team, [Lyon OU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_OU), in the [Top 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_14), which moved into Stade de Gerland full-time in 2017–18. In addition, Lyon has a [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) side called [Lyon Villeurbanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Villeurbanne) that plays in the [French rugby league championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_rugby_league_championship). The club's home is the [Stade Georges Lyvet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Georges_Lyvet?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Villeurbanne.

Lyon is also home to the [Lyon Hockey Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Hockey_Club), an [ice hockey](14790.md) team that competes in France's national ice hockey league. The [Patinoire Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patinoire_Charlemagne) is the seat of Club des Sports de Glace de Lyon, the club of Olympic [ice dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_dancing) champions [Marina Anissina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Anissina) and [Gwendal Peizerat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwendal_Peizerat), and world champions [Isabelle Delobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Delobel) and [Olivier Shoenfelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Shoenfelder).[^cite_note-70] Lyon-Villeurbanne also has a basketball team, [ASVEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASVEL_Basket), that plays at the [Astroballe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astroballe) arena.

Lyon is will host the indoor ice during the [2030 Winter Olympics](57658858.md) following the decision to move those events out of [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice,_France).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Several venues in Lyon are set to be used.[^cite_note-73]

## Transport

Further information: [Lyon Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metro), [Lyon tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_tramway), [Trolleybuses in Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Lyon), [Buses in Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_Lyon), [Transport in Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes), and [TER Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TER_Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes)

[Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon%E2%80%93Saint-Exup%C3%A9ry_Airport), located east of Lyon, serves as a base for domestic and international flights. It is a key transport facility for the entire Rhône-Alpes region, with coach links to other cities in the area. The in-house train station Gare de Lyon Saint-Exupéry connects the airport to the nationwide [TGV](57212.md) network. The [Rhônexpress tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4nexpress) monopoly links the airport with the business quarter of [La Part Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Part-Dieu) in less than 30 minutes, and offers connections with Underground A & B, Tramway T1, T3 & T4, and bus lines. Lyon public transport Sytral offers a bus service, Route 47, that links the airport to Meyzieu[^cite_note-82] where passengers can change onto Tram T3. The regular price of public transport is €1.90, as opposed to €15 one way for the Rhonexpress. In the suburb of [Bron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bron), the smaller [Lyon-Bron Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Bron_Airport) provides an alternative for domestic aviation.

Lyon has two major railway stations: [Lyon-Part-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Part-Dieu_station), which was built to accommodate the TGV, and [Lyon-Perrache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Perrache_station), an older station that now provides mostly regional service. Smaller railway stations include [Lyon-Gorge-de-Loup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Gorge-de-Loup_station), [Lyon-Vaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Vaise_station), [Lyon-Saint-Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Saint-Paul_station) and [Lyon-Jean Macé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Jean_Mac%C3%A9_station). Lyon was the first city to be connected to Paris by the TGV in 1981.[^cite_note-83] Since that time the TGV train network has expanded and links Lyon directly to Perpignan, Toulouse, Nice, Marseille, Strasbourg, Nantes and Lille. International trains operate directly to Madrid, Barcelona, Milan, Turin, Geneva, Frankfurt, Luxembourg, Brussels (formerly to London, now withdrawn).

The city is at the heart of a dense road network and is located at the meeting point of several highways: [A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_autoroute_\(France\)) to Paris, [A7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A7_autoroute) Marseille, [A42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A42_autoroute) to [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), and [A43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A43_autoroute) to [Grenoble](50972.md). The city is now bypassed by the [A46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A46_autoroute). A double motorway tunnel passes under Fourvière, connecting the A6 and the A7 autoroutes, both forming the "Autoroute du Soleil".

The Perrache railway station serves as an intermodal transportation hub for tramways, local and regional trains and city buses, the terminus of Metro line A, of the Tramway T2, the bicycle service Vélo'v, and taxis.

The [Transports en commun lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transports_en_commun_lyonnais) (TCL), Lyon's public transit system, consisting of metro, tramways and buses, serves 62 communes of the Lyon metropolis.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-\:0-85] The metro network has four lines ([A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metro_Line_A), [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metro_Line_B), [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metro_Line_C) and [D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metro_Line_D)), 42 stations, and runs with a frequency of up to a train every 2 minutes. There are eight [Lyon tram lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_tramway) since november 2020: T1 from *Debourg* in the south to *IUT-Feyssine* in the north, Tram T2 from *Hôtel de région Montrochet* to [Saint-Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Priest,_Rh%C3%B4ne) in the south-east, Tram T3 from Part-Dieu to [Meyzieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meyzieu), Tram T4 from 'Hôptial Feyzin Venissieux' to La Doua Gaston Berger. Tram T5 from Grange Blanche, in the south-east to Eurexpo in the south-west. Tram T6 from Debourg, in the south to Hôpitaux Est-Pinel in the east. Tram T7 from Vaux-en-Velin la soie, in the north-east to Décines – OL Vallée in the east.[^cite_note-\:0-85] And Rhône Express tramline from Part-Dieu to Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] The [Lyon bus network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_Lyon) consists of the [Lyon trolleybus system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Lyon), [motorbuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorbuses), and coaches for areas outside the centre. There are also two funicular lines from Vieux Lyon to Saint-Just and Fourvière. The ticketing system is relatively simple as the city has only one public transport operator, the SYTRAL.

The public transit system was complemented in 2005 by *[Vélo'v](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9lo'v)*, a bicycle network providing a low-cost bicycle-hire service made up of 340 stations throughout the city. Borrowing a bicycle for less than 30 minutes is free. Free rental time can be extended for another 30 minutes at any station. Lyon was the first city in France to introduce this bicycle renting system. In 2011, the [Auto'lib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autolib') car rental service was introduced. It worked much the same way as the Velo'v but for cars. It closed in 2018.

The average amount of time people spend commuting with public transit in Lyon on a weekday is 45 minutes. The average amount of time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 11 min, while 17% of riders wait for over 20 minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 4.7 km, while 4% travel for over 12 km in a single direction.[^cite_note-88]
