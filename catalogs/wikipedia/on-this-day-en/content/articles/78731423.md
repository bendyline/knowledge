***Debí Tirar Más Fotos*** (Spanish: [\[deˈβi tiˈɾaɾ mas ˈfotos\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); transl. *I Should Have Taken More Photos*, stylized as ***DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOToS*** and shortened to ***DtMF***) is the sixth solo studio album (seventh overall) by the Puerto Rican rapper and singer [Bad Bunny](55943877.md). It was released on January 5, 2025, through [Rimas Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimas_Entertainment) and follows his previous record *[Nadie Sabe Lo Que Va a Pasar Mañana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadie_Sabe_Lo_Que_Va_a_Pasar_Ma%C3%B1ana)* (2023). It features collaborations with [Chuwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuwi_\(band\)), [Omar Courtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Courtz), [Los Pleneros de la Cresta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pleneros_de_la_Cresta), [Dei V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei_V), and [RaiNao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RaiNao).

An [alternative reggaeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_reggaeton) record, the album heavily blends together diverse elements of [traditional Puerto Rican music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Puerto_Rico) that Bad Bunny listened to when growing up, including [plena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plena), [jíbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Puerto_Rico#J%C3%ADbaro_music), [salsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_music), and [bomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_\(Puerto_Rico\)), as well as influences of other musical styles, similar to *[Un Verano Sin Ti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un_Verano_Sin_Ti)* (2022). While most of the lyrical content is based on love, heartbreak, partying, and family, some of the lyrics focus on the complexities that arise from [Puerto Rico's political status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico's_political_status) as an [unincorporated territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) under U.S. [sovereignty](28791.md), including [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) and loss of cultural identity.&#x20;

*Debí Tirar Más Fotos* was supported by several singles, including the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) and [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md) number-one hit "[DTMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTMF_\(song\))". It was also promoted with an accompanying [short film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film) of the same name uploaded onto [YouTube](3524766.md). Following its release, the album received critical acclaim from music critics, who considered it Bad Bunny’s most personal album and an homage to Puerto Rico.[^cite_note-5] The album peaked at number one on the U.S. [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200#All-Spanish-language_albums_to_reach_number_one_on_Billboard_200), becoming his fourth consecutive number-one album to top the chart. From July to September 2025, he hosted the concert residency [No Me Quiero Ir de Aquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Me_Quiero_Ir_de_Aqu%C3%AD) in Puerto Rico in support of the album, and performed multiple songs from the album during the [Super Bowl LX halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LX_halftime_show). He embarked on the all-stadium [Debí Tirar Más Fotos World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb%C3%AD_Tirar_M%C3%A1s_Fotos_World_Tour), which started in November 2025 and concluded in August 2026.

*Debí Tirar Más Fotos* achieved various notable accolades and milestones. It received twelve nominations at the [26th Latin Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Annual_Latin_Grammy_Awards), where it won five awards, including [Best Urban Music Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Grammy_Award_for_Best_Urban_Music_Album) and [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year), marking Bad Bunny’s first win in the latter category after four nominations. At the [68th Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/68th_Grammy_Awards), the album received six nominations, including [Album of the Year](196513.md) as well as [Record of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_of_the_Year) and [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_the_Year) for "DTMF", making Bad Bunny the first Latino, Hispanic, and Spanish-language artist to be simultaneously nominated in the three most [prestigious categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_\(Grammy_Awards\)).[^cite_note-6] The album won Album of the Year, [Best Música Urbana Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_M%C3%BAsica_Urbana_Album), and [Best Global Music Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Global_Music_Performance) for "[Eoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eoo_\(song\))". It became the first Spanish-language album to win Album of the Year.[^cite_note-7] It also became the first album to win Album of the Year at both the Grammys and Latin Grammys.[^cite_note-8]

## Background

On October 13, 2023, Bad Bunny released his fifth solo studio album, *[Nadie Sabe Lo Que Va a Pasar Mañana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadie_Sabe_Lo_Que_Va_a_Pasar_Ma%C3%B1ana)*, which debuted at number one on the US [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) with 184,000 units and reached the top 10 in five more countries.[^cite_note-9] The album release was followed by his fifth concert tour titled the [Most Wanted Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Wanted_Tour_\(Bad_Bunny\)), spanning dates from February to June 2024, all of which sold out and broke multiple records.[^cite_note-10] Outside of touring, Bad Bunny released collaborations with [Myke Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myke_Towers)[^cite_note-11] and [Rauw Alejandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauw_Alejandro)[^cite_note-12] as well as the standalone single "[Una Velita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_Velita)", co-produced by long-time collaborator [Tainy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tainy).[^cite_note-13]

He announced his sixth solo studio album, *Debí Tirar Más Fotos*, and supported it with two singles, "[El Clúb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%BAb)" and "[Pitorro de Coco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitorro_de_Coco)".[^cite_note-DTMF-1-14] His decision on giving the album that title is because of him initially hating taking photos but eventually getting used to it, additionally stating that the idea is "enjoying the moment when I could and valuing memories".[^cite_note-interview-1-15]

## Music and themes

*Debí Tirar Más Fotos* is primarily a [reggaeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggaeton) album which heavily incorporates several elements of [traditional Puerto Rican music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Puerto_Rico), such as [bomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_\(Puerto_Rico\)), [jíbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Puerto_Rico#J%C3%ADbaro_music), [plena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plena), and [salsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_music); the album also contains influences of [a cappella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_cappella), [bolero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolero), [cha-cha-chá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha-cha-ch%C3%A1_\(music\)), [dembow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_dembow), [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music), [acoustic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_music), [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock), [ambient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_music), [ballad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad), [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae), [dancehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancehall), [bossa nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossa_nova), [boogaloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogaloo), [rumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_rumba), [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop), [bachata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachata_\(music\)), [merengue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merengue_music), [indie pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_pop), [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz), [R\&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R\&B), [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music), [spoken word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoken_word), [sandungueo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandungueo), and [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop).[^cite_note-30] Production for the album was handled by Bad Bunny's frequent collaborators [Tainy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tainy), La Paciencia, and MAG, along with new collaborators such as Big Jay and Saox.

### Songs

The album's opening track, "[Nuevayol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevayol)", begins with a sample of "[Un Verano en Nueva York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un_Verano_en_Nueva_York)", originally performed by [Andy Montañez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Monta%C3%B1ez) and [El Gran Combo de Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Gran_Combo_de_Puerto_Rico),[^cite_note-samples-31] later switching to a fusion of reggaeton and [dembow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dembow) rhythms, similar to the singer's 2022 single "[Tití Me Preguntó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tit%C3%AD_Me_Pregunt%C3%B3)".[^cite_note-Rolling_Stone-18][^cite_note-Billboard_track_ranking-32] During an exclusive video interview with *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, Bad Bunny stated that "Nuevayol" was one of the first songs written for the album, noting that the song represents the cultural and historical importance of [Nuyoricans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuyorican). He also explained its placement as the opening track, due to New York City being the gathering point of "the whole Latin American community".[^cite_note-33] The third track, "[Baile Inolvidable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baile_Inolvidable)", runs at a length of six minutes and features a live salsa orchestra consisting of students from the Escuela Libre de Música in [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico).[^cite_note-Pitchfork-34][^cite_note-35]

"[Veldá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veld%C3%A1)," which features fellow Puerto Rican singers and rappers [Omar Courtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Courtz) and [Dei V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei_V), is a hardcore reggaeton track that features a sandungueo beat and lyrics about online love exchanges, with a surprise appearance by fellow rapper [Wisin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisin) towards the end of the track.[^cite_note-Billboard_track_ranking-32] The track samples [Plan B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_B_\(duo\))'s "No Voy a Esperar Por Ti" throughout.[^cite_note-samples-31] "[El Clúb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%BAb)" is a [house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_music) track with a fusion of [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music) and plena.[^cite_note-El-Club-01-36][^cite_note-Forbes-16] On the [bachata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachata_\(music\))-influenced "Bokete", he compares an ex-girlfriend to a titular [pothole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothole), simultaneously functioning as a critique of the island's infrastructure.[^cite_note-AllMusic-37][^cite_note-Pitchfork-34] "Turista" is a slow-paced [bolero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolero) with lyrics that reflect on a brief relationship, comparing it to the perspective of travelers who arrive in a country to briefly "enjoy what is there, yet, once they leave, they are not confronted with the struggles that local residents must face".[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

"[Lo Que Le Pasó a Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Que_Le_Pas%C3%B3_a_Hawaii)" features instrumentals composed of [güiros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCiro) and guitars, with Bad Bunny musically calling out the gentrification being faced in Puerto Rico and expressing his concern over the potential negative impact of the island's [admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) in the [context of Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Admission_Act).[^cite_note-NME-29][^cite_note-LQLPAH-Pitchfork-40][^cite_note-41] The track gained significant attention on social media sites such as [Twitter](9988187.md) and has been denounced by [pro-statehood advocates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_statehood_movement) (*estadistas*), some of whom continue to see [Hawaii](13270.md) as a successful model for [Puerto Rican statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_state#Puerto_Rico).[^cite_note-42] Statehood advocates have occasionally accused Bad Bunny of supporting [independence for Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_movement_in_Puerto_Rico), particularly after his support for candidates linked to the [Puerto Rican Independence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_Independence_Party) and [Citizens' Victory Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Victory_Movement) such as [Juan Dalmau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Dalmau) for governor and [Manuel Natal Albelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Natal_Albelo) for the [mayorship of San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico), respectively.[^cite_note-43]

"[Eoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eoo_\(song\))" evokes the reggaeton scene of the mid-1990s and 2000s, with its chorus being built around a sample of [Héctor & Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_&_Tito)'s track "Perreo Baby".[^cite_note-NME-29][^cite_note-Pitchfork-34] Midway through the track, there is a sample taken from the final seconds of the singer's 2018 single "[Solo de Mí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_de_M%C3%AD)," which is a recording of a woman saying "¡Mira, puñeta, no me quiten el perreo!";[^cite_note-Pitchfork-34] the usage of this sample on both mentioned tracks would lead to a lawsuit filed by Tainaly Serrano Rivera, who is the woman in the recording, claiming this was done without permission.[^cite_note-44]

The title track, "[DTMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTMF_\(song\))", is a "nostalgic" track[^cite_note-NME-29] that blends "[Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo)-inspired beats", reminiscent of his 2020 album *[YHLQMDLG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YHLQMDLG)*, with "lively [plena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plena)", a Puerto Rican "folk call-and-response" genre.[^cite_note-Rolling_Stone-18] On a "mellow beat" with occasional choir chants, Bad Bunny displays his emotions and regrets not having taken more pictures of loved ones or people with whom he had spent periods of time.[^cite_note-45]

## Marketing

### Promotion and release



The logo used to promote the album, showing its title, stylized in [alternating caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_caps).



The [Puerto Rican crested toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_crested_toad) inspired the creation of *Concho*, the promotional [mascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascot) of the album.

On December 5, 2024, Bad Bunny released the lead single "[El Clúb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%BAb)". The album's title was teased through the music video, simply displaying "DTmF 2025".[^cite_note-46] On December 25, he tweeted a numbered list of 17 songs, all labeled as "bomba", a tactic also used to promote his previous albums.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-DTMF-1-14] The album was officially announced the following day, alongside the release of the second single "[Pitorro de Coco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitorro_de_Coco)".[^cite_note-48] The album's tracklist was revealed on January 3, 2025, along with an accompanying short film on [YouTube](3524766.md), co-directed by Bad Bunny and starring actor, filmmaker and playwright [Jacobo Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobo_Morales).[^cite_note-49]

The album was released on Sunday, January 5, 2025, at 12 p.m. [ET](496028.md), a day before [Three Kings Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kings_Day).[^cite_note-HNHH-50][^cite_note-El_Universal-51] In further promotion of the album, he served as a co-host on *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)* on January 13, additionally performing "[Voy a Llevarte Pa' PR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voy_a_Llevarte_Pa'_PR)".[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] The same day, he announced the Puerto Rican-exclusive 2025 concert residency [No Me Quiero Ir de Aquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Me_Quiero_Ir_de_Aqu%C3%AD) (transl. "I don't want to leave here"), which took place at [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico)'s [Coliseo de Puerto Rico José Miguel Agrelot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coliseo_de_Puerto_Rico_Jos%C3%A9_Miguel_Agrelot) for 30 dates from early July to mid-September.[^cite_note-54] He also hinted a worldwide tour across Latin America, Europe and Asia,[^cite_note-55] which was officially announced on May 5, 2025, as the [Debí Tirar Más Fotos World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb%C3%AD_Tirar_M%C3%A1s_Fotos_World_Tour).[^cite_note-56] He performed an [NPR Tiny Desk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR_Tiny_Desk) concert on April 7 alongside his backing band Los Sobrinos.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-LS-58] On September 28, 2025, the rapper was announced as the headliner of the [Super Bowl LX halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LX_halftime_show) at [Levi's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi's_Stadium) in [Santa Clara, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara,_California).[^cite_note-Rys-59]

Following the album's release, multiple songs received music videos, including "Baile Inolvidable" on January 9,[^cite_note-60] "Turista" on [Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine's_Day),[^cite_note-61] "La Mudanza" on March 11, his 31st birthday,[^cite_note-62] "Bokete" on April 25,[^cite_note-63] "Ketu Tecré" on May 16,[^cite_note-64] and "Nuevayol" on [July 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-65]

On February 6, 2026, the album was re-issued as 2-LP vinyl. In the United States, an eighteenth bonus track, "Alambre Púa" was included. This song was previously released July 14, 2025, on streaming services.

### Artwork and visuals

The artwork for *Debí Tirar Más Fotos* was revealed on January 3, 2025.[^cite_note-66] It features two [Monobloc chairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monobloc_\(chair\)) placed on a dirt-and-grass field with [banana trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_%C3%97_paradisiaca) in the background.[^cite_note-67] According to Bianca Betancourt of *[Harper's Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper's_Bazaar)*, the album cover is "meant to resonate" with both [stateside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateside_Puerto_Ricans) and islander Puerto Ricans.[^cite_note-68] His appearance in album promos notably included doning the [*pava*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pava_\(Puerto_Rico\)) hat traditional to Puerto Rico, which he also wore to the 2025 Met Gala as a tie-in.[^cite_note-69]

The [visualizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_visualization) for each track on YouTube display the [history of Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Puerto_Rico) as a slide presentation, with the content being compiled by Puerto Rican professor of the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison), Jorell Meléndez Badillo.[^cite_note-70] These discuss moments such as the Spanish colonization, the acquisition of Puerto Rico by the United States, the Puerto Rican diaspora, repression and surveillance through the 20th century, the killing of student activist [Antonia Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Mart%C3%ADnez), and the creation of the [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Puerto_Rico) and [*Grito de Lares*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grito_de_Lares_flag) flags.[^cite_note-71]

Additionally, Bad Bunny created the character *Concho*, which is a [Puerto Rican crested toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_crested_toad), locally known as *sapo concho*.[^cite_note-72] The toad is an endangered species, and its use as a promotional image of the album has had a strong impact on its conservation.[^cite_note-Trapp-73]

*Debí Tirar Más Fotos* was highly acclaimed by music critics. According to the review aggregator [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), *Debí Tirar Más Fotos* received "universal acclaim" based on a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean) score of 95 out of 100 from 8 critic scores.[^cite_note-MC-75] It is also currently the fifteenth highest-scoring album on the website.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-80]

In a five-star review, Maya Georgi of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* called it a "homegrown, jubilant, and fresh" album and concluded her review by stating "\[i]n *Nadie Sabe*, he claimed he was in his prime. Now, on *Debí Tirar Más Fotos*, it’s clear he finally is."[^cite_note-Rolling_Stone-18] *[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME)*'s Lucas Villa commended the album for "revolutionizing Puerto Rico's folk music", as well as blending nostalgic sounds recalled from Bad Bunny's childhood with contemporary production.[^cite_note-NME-29]

Critics lauded the album for approaching political issues. Tatiana Lee Rodriguez of *[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_\(website\))* praised the narration of Puerto Rico's struggle for sovereignty "rooted in compounded centuries of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas), then [American, colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism)".[^cite_note-Pitchfork-34] Writing for [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic), David Crone called *Debí Tirar Más Fotos* a "Puerto Rican triumph".[^cite_note-AllMusic-37] In a positive review, *[Clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clash_\(magazine\))*'s Robin Murray considered the album to be "a love letter to \[Bad Bunny's] heritage" and additionally stated that it is "potent and personal".[^cite_note-Clash-76]

## Commercial performance

### United States

*Debí Tirar Más Fotos* debuted at number two on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) for the week of January 18, 2025, behind [Lil Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Baby)'s *[WHAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHAM_\(album\))*, with 122,000 [album-equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Album-equivalent_units) (of which only 8,000 were from pure album sales), making it Bad Bunny's seventh top-ten entry on the chart and his first album to not debut atop since *[YHLQMDLG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YHLQMDLG)* (2020).[^cite_note-B200-122] This was attributed to its release on a Sunday as opposed to the industry-standard Friday, resulting in only five tracked days of activity.[^cite_note-HNHH-50] The following week, the album peaked at number one on the *Billboard* 200, earning 203,500 additional units mostly through streaming activity, with 7,500 from traditional album sales. It became Bad Bunny's fourth number-one album, as well as the sixth Spanish language album and the 28th non-English language album to top the chart.[^cite_note-BB200-Wk2-123] Additionally, it debuted atop both the US [Top Latin Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Latin_Albums) and [Latin Rhythm Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Rhythm_Albums) charts, becoming his ninth number-one album on both charts.[^cite_note-BTLA-124]

Seventeen of the album tracks (excluding the U.S.-only vinyl bonus track "Alambre Púa") were also charting on the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md). With this feat, Bad Bunny became the first Latin artist to attain over 100 song entries on the chart, and the eleventh artist with the most chart entries, totaling up to 113 entries.[^cite_note-125] On the [Hot Latin Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Latin_Songs) chart, all of its tracks were also charting, with ten of its tracks charting in the entirety of the top-10 on the latter chart.[^cite_note-BTLA-124]

### International markets

In Spain, the album debuted atop its album chart published by [Productores de Música de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Productores_de_M%C3%BAsica_de_Espa%C3%B1a), becoming Bad Bunny's sixth consecutive album to peak at number one in the country.[^cite_note-Spain-126] All of its tracks also charted in the country's accompanying singles chart, with nine of its tracks appearing in the top-10 section.[^cite_note-Spain-126] In the Netherlands, *Debí Tirar Más Fotos* debuted at number one on the [Dutch Album Top 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Album_Top_100), becoming Bad Bunny's first chart-topping project and the 1,000th to reach this milestone in the country.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] On the [Swiss Albums Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Albums_Chart), the album marked Bad Bunny's second consecutive number-one record in Switzerland after *Nadie Sabe Lo Que Va a Pasar Mañana* (2023).[^cite_note-129] In the United Kingdom, the album debuted on 13. However, following the [2026 Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Grammy_Awards) and the [Super Bowl LX halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LX_halftime_show), the album re-entered 44 and the next week peaked at number 2, barred from the top spot by [Olivia Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Dean)'s *[The Art of Loving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Loving_\(album\))*, becoming his first top 10 album ever in the country.[^cite_note-130] Also, In France reached the number one on French Album Charts whit 14,000 equivalent units sold following the halftime show.[^cite_note-131]

## Impact

The promotional cycle surrounding *Debí Tirar Más Fotos* generated substantial economic impact across multiple countries. A study by Indira Luciano Montalvo, associate professor of economics at the [University of Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Puerto_Rico) at [Río Piedras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Puerto_Rico,_R%C3%ADo_Piedras_Campus), estimated that the [No Me Quiero Ir de Aquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Me_Quiero_Ir_de_Aqu%C3%AD) residency generated a minimum of \$176.6 million in economic activity through production revenue, employment, and audience spending, excluding ticket prices and in-venue expenses; Luciano Montalvo characterized this figure as "very conservative."[^cite_note-RS-economic-132] [Discover Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discover_Puerto_Rico?action=edit\&redlink=1), the island's nonprofit [destination marketing organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destination_marketing_organization), estimated the residency brought in approximately \$200 million in tourist spending on lodging, transportation, and food,[^cite_note-CNN-economic-133] while a report from Gaither International estimated \$733 million in total gains when accounting for increased international exposure and shifts in global perception of Puerto Rico.[^cite_note-RS-economic-132] The 31 sold-out shows attracted over 500,000 attendees, with the first nine shows reserved exclusively for residents of Puerto Rico.[^cite_note-RS-economic-132] The [Puerto Rico Tourism Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Tourism_Company) reported that hotel occupancy rose 6 percent in July, reaching 85 percent compared to 79 percent the previous year, with projected increases of 10.1 percent in August and 12.2 percent in September.[^cite_note-WJournal-134] Approximately two million passengers passed through [Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mu%C3%B1oz_Mar%C3%ADn_International_Airport) during the residency period, with 40 percent composed of international visitors.[^cite_note-WJournal-134]

The residency also had a measurable effect on Puerto Rican artists featured on the album. [Los Pleneros de la Cresta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pleneros_de_la_Cresta), who performed at all 31 shows and collaborated with Bad Bunny on "Café con Ron", grew from tens of thousands of monthly [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify) listeners to 12 million; the group used the increased revenue to fund cultural preservation projects through their nonprofit Acción Valerosa, including the restoration of the Yerba Bruja cultural center in [Ciales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciales).[^cite_note-RS-economic-132] Local artisans reported a 340 percent increase in the sale of [vejigante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vejigante) masks in [Ponce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponce,_Puerto_Rico), bookstores reported a 280 percent increase in sales of Puerto Rican authors, and local [salsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_music), [bomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_\(Puerto_Rico\)), and [plena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plena) artists reported an average income increase of 65 percent during the residency period.[^cite_note-RS-economic-132]

The subsequent Debí Tirar Más Fotos World Tour sold over 2.6 million tickets within one week across 54 stadium shows in 18 countries, which [Live Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation) compared to the touring scale of [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay), [Michael Jackson](14995351.md), [Taylor Swift](5422144.md), and [the Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones).[^cite_note-LiveNation-tour-135] According to [Billboard](18309966.md) Boxscore, the eight concerts at [Estadio GNP Seguros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_GNP_Seguros) in [Mexico City](18987.md) (December 10–21, 2025) grossed \$86.7 million from 518,000 tickets, making it the second highest-grossing concert series at a single venue, behind [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay)'s ten shows at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md).[^cite_note-BB-Boxscore-136] The Mexico City engagement reportedly generated \$177 million in broader economic activity according to the National Chamber of Commerce, Services and Tourism of Mexico City, with approximately 45 percent of attendees traveling from abroad.[^cite_note-RS-economic-132] The tour's first 12 shows collectively grossed \$107 million from 697,000 tickets, surpassing the entire Latin American gross of Bad Bunny's previous [World's Hottest Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Hottest_Tour) (\$81.7 million across 22 dates).[^cite_note-BB-Boxscore-136]
