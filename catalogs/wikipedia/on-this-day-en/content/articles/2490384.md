**Muzaffarpur district** is one of the thirty-eight [districts of Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Bihar) [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India), India. Muzaffarpur district is part of and also the headquarters of [Tirhut division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut_division). Muzaffarpur district is the financial and unofficial capital of [North Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bihar)[^cite_note-1] Tirhut is the ancient name of all of northern Bihar.[^cite_note-2] The district headquarters is [Muzaffarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur).

## History

Muzaffarpur district, popularly known as the "Land of Litchi," was created in 1875 for administrative convenience by splitting the earlier district of Tirhut district. The district is named after [Muzaffar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffar_Khan), an [Amil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amil) (Revenue Officer) under the [British administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj).

Muzaffarpur has gained international recognition for its delicious [litchis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litchi), including [Shahi litchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahi_litchi) and China litchi [^cite_note-3]

### Ancient

The history of Muzaffarpur can be traced back to the ancient Indian epic *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana)*. According to legend, [Rajarshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajarshi) [Janak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janaka) ruled [Videha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videha), a region that included present-day [Nepal](171166.md) and northern [Bihar](442482.md). [Sitamarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitamarhi), a town in the district, is sacred to [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) as the birthplace of [Sita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sita), also known as Vaidehi, who is said to have emerged from an earthen pot while Janak was tilling the land.[^cite_note-4]

The recorded history of the district begins with the rise of the [Vrijjan Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajjika_League). This confederation consisted of eight clans, with the [Licchavis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licchavis_of_Vaishali) being the most powerful. Even the powerful kingdom of [Magadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadh) entered into matrimonial alliances with the Licchavis in 519 B.C. [Ajatshatru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajatshatru) invaded [Vaishali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishali_\(ancient_city\)) and extended his rule over [Tirhut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut). [Patliputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patliputra) (modern-day [Patna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patna)) was established on the banks of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) during this period, along with a fortress to monitor the Licchavis.

Ambarati], 40 km from Muzaffarpur, is believed to be the village home of [Amrapali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrapali), the famous royal court dancer of [Vaishali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishali_\(ancient_city\)).

### Influence of Buddhism and Jainism

Muzaffarpur was also influenced by [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism). [Vaishali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishali_\(ancient_city\)), a center of religious renaissance, is the birthplace of [Mahavir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavir), the 24th Jain Tirthankara and a contemporary of [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha). The region attracted pilgrims and scholars from across the subcontinent and beyond.

### Medieval

From the 7th century A.D., Muzaffarpur came under the rule of [Maharaja Harsha Vardhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha_Vardhan). After his reign, local chiefs governed the area, and in the 8th century, the [Pala dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) established control over [Tirhut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut) until 1019 A.D. Later, the [Chedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chedi_Kingdom) kings of [Central India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India) and the [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty) also influenced the region.

Between 1211 and 1226, Ghais-u-ddin Iwaz, the ruler of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), was the first Muslim invader of [Tirhut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut), although he only succeeded in extorting tributes. In 1323, [Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghiyasuddin_Tughlaq) established his control over the district. The Simraon dynasty, founded by Nanyupa Deva, ruled the northeastern part of [Champaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaran), including parts of [Mithila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithila_\(region\)) and [Nepal](171166.md), until [Tughlaq Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaq_Shah)’s invasion.

By the late 14th century, the [Jaunpur kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaunpur_Sultanate) controlled [North Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bihar), including [Tirhut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut), until [Sikandar Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_Lodi) defeated them. Later, the [Nawabs of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs_of_Bengal), particularly [Hussain Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Shah), exerted influence over the region before it became part of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). Despite Mughal annexation, local [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) chiefs continued to exercise significant autonomy.[^cite_note-5]

### Colonial Era

After the [Battle of Buxar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buxar) in 1764, Muzaffarpur came under the control of the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company). During the [1857 revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), the district witnessed revolutionary activities. Muzaffarpur was also the site of the famous Muzaffarpur bomb case of 1908, where 18-year-old [Khudi Ram Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khudi_Ram_Bose) was hanged for throwing a bomb at the carriage of [Pringle Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pringle_Kennedy), mistakenly believed to be carrying Douglas Kingsford, the [District Judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_courts_of_India) of Muzaffarpur. A memorial for Bose stands in the district to this day.

### Struggle for Independence

The nationalist movement in Muzaffarpur gained momentum after [World War I](4764461.md). Visits by [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) in December 1920 and January 1927 had a significant impact on the political consciousness of the people. The district played an important role in [India’s struggle for freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement).

### Cultural Significance

Muzaffarpur is known for its rich cultural heritage and the unique blending of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) and [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic) traditions. The district has historically been a meeting point for diverse spiritual and cultural influences, making it a center of learning and the birthplace of several notable personalities.

In 1972, the districts of [Sitamarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitamarhi_district) and [Vaishali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishali_district) were separated from Muzaffarpur to form independent administrative units.[^cite_note-6]

## Geography

Muzaffarpur district lies in the fertile plains of northern Bihar and forms part of [Tirhut division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut_division). The district is drained by the [Gandak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandak), Burhi Gandak, [Bagmati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagmati_River) and [Lakhandei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhandei_River) rivers.

The district has an extensive road and railway network connecting [Muzaffarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur) with neighbouring districts and major urban centres. [Mehsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehsi), in [Purbi Champaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purbi_Champaran_district), is connected with Muzaffarpur by [National Highway 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_27_\(India\)). The [National Highways Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Authority_of_India) identifies the Kotwa (Motihari) – Mehsi – Muzaffarpur section as an 80-km stretch of NH 27, linking Muzaffarpur with Mehsi and the East Champaran region.[^cite_note-7]

Muzaffarpur district covers an area of 3,122.56 square kilometres (1,205.63 sq mi). It is bordered by [Purbi Champaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purbi_Champaran_district) and [Sitamarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitamarhi_district) districts to the north, [Vaishali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishali_district) and [Saran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saran_district) districts to the south, [Darbhanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darbhanga_district) and [Samastipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samastipur_district) districts to the east, and [Saran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saran_district) and [Gopalganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopalganj_district,_India) districts to the west.[^cite_note-8]

## Demographics

### Population

According to the 2011 Census of India, Muzaffarpur district has a population of 4,801,062, comprising 2,527,497 males and 2,273,565 females, living in 945,703 households. With this population, the district ranks 24th in India (out of 640 districts), and is comparable in population to the nation of Singapore and the U.S. state of Alabama. The district has a population density of 1,514 persons per sq km over a total area of 3,172 sq km. Muzaffarpur experienced a population growth rate of 28.14% between 2001 and 2011.[^cite_note-religion-9][^cite_note-districtcensus-10][^cite_note-cia-11]

The overall sex ratio in Muzaffarpur district is 900 females for every 1,000 males. The district records a child sex ratio (0–6 years) of 915, which is higher than the overall average.

## Administration

[Muzaffarpur district](2490384.md) is an important administrative district in North Bihar and forms part of the [Tirhut division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirhut_division). The district administration is headed by the District Magistrate and operates under the Government of Bihar.

### Subdivisions

The district is divided into two administrative subdivisions:

- **Subdivision East -** Aurai, Bandra, Bochahan, Gaighat, Mushahari, Katra, Minapur, Muraul and Sakra.

- **Subdivision West -** Kanti, Kurhani, Marwan, Paroo, Sahebganj, Motipur and Saraiya.

### Revenue Administration (Anchal/Tehsil)

For land and revenue purposes, the district is divided into 16 Anchal (Tehsils): Aurai, Bandra, Bochahan, Gaighat, Kanti, Katra, Kurhani, Marwan, Minapur, Motipur, Muraul, Mushahari, Sahebganj, Sakra, Paroo and Saraiya.

Each Anchal is headed by a Circle Officer responsible for land records and revenue administration.

### Civic Administration

The urban areas of the district are governed primarily by the [Muzaffarpur Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur_Municipal_Corporation) (MMC), which is responsible for civic infrastructure and public service delivery within the city limits.

- **Current Area:** Approximately 32 km²

- **Total Wards:** 49 administrative wards

- **Establishment:** Municipality formed in 1864, upgraded to Municipal Corporation in 1981

- **Proposed Expansion -** To accommodate rapid urban growth, the Government of Bihar has proposed expansion of municipal limits. Proposed area expansion to approximately 53 km² Ward increase from 49 to around 72–75 wards

  [^cite_note-17]

### Smart City Initiative

Muzaffarpur is one of the cities selected under the Government of India's [Smart Cities Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Cities_Mission).[^cite_note-18]

Smart City projects are implemented by **Muzaffarpur Smart City Limited**, the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) formed to execute mission projects in coordination with the Municipal Corporation and the Government of Bihar.

Key focus areas include integrated traffic management, sewerage and drainage improvement, smart roads, urban beautification, and digital governance systems.

## Economy

Muzaffarpur is one of the most economically important districts of Bihar. According to the Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26, it ranks among the top three districts in terms of Gross District Domestic Product (GDDP) and per-capita income, and also records one of the highest Net District Domestic Product (NDDP) figures in the state. This highlights the district's strong and diverse economy and its role as a key contributor to Bihar’s overall growth.

The district is a major industrial and commercial hub in North Bihar. It is known for textiles, bag manufacturing, agro-based industries, dairy, and lychee cultivation. Important industrial areas like Bela and Bariyarpur host companies such as Bihar Drugs & Organic Chemicals Ltd and Muzaffarpur Dairy. The Sutapatti clothing bazar is a popular retail and wholesale market, attracting shoppers from nearby districts.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

### Economic Indicators

Muzaffarpur’s per capita GDP in 2024 was around US\$402.22 (approximately ₹33,600), contributing nearly 5% to Bihar’s overall GDP. The district consumed 1,807 million units of energy in 2024–25, had over 97,500 registered vehicles, and generated ₹440 crore in revenue from stamp duty and registration fees. These figures reflect the district’s industrial activity and commercial vibrancy.

### Industry and Commerce

The district’s industrial areas and markets form the backbone of its economy. Bela and Bariyarpur host major companies and agro-industries, while Motijheel Market and Sutapatti Bazar support wholesale and retail trade. The city also has more than six malls, the highest number in Bihar with multiplexes, reflecting a growing commercial and consumer sector.

### Real Estate

Muzaffarpur is among the top cities in Bihar for real estate investment, with 102 ongoing projects, ranking third in the state after Patna and Gaya.

### Retail and Payments

Muzaffarpur has 8,584 POS machines, showing a thriving retail ecosystem and increasing purchasing power among residents.[^cite_note-23]

## Defence Establishments in Muzaffarpur

### Military Station

Muzaffarpur Military Station, also known as the Muzaffarpur Cantonment, Muzaffarpur Army Area or Station Headquarter Muzaffarpur, is a defence establishment of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) located in Shrirampuri, near Circuit House Road, in the Muzaffarpur district.

The station provides administrative, logistic, and welfare facilities for serving and retired defence personnel in North Bihar. It is associated with the [151 Infantry Battalion (Territorial Army) – Jat Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Army_\(India\)), which operates under the Territorial Army framework of the Indian Army.[^cite_note-Jagran2020MuzaffarpurCanteen-27]

- Overview

The military station includes an Army Canteen that has served defence personnel and ex-servicemen of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), [Indian Air Force](403355.md), and [Indian Navy](604522.md) in the region for over a century. It is among the oldest canteens in eastern India, established around 1859–1860, providing subsidized goods and amenities to military personnel. The station also houses an Ex-Servicemen Contributory Health Scheme (ECHS) Polyclinic, which provides medical care and health services to retired defence personnel and their dependents.[^cite_note-LiveHindustan2021MuzaffarpurCanteen-28]

- Location

Address: Circuit House Road, Shrirampuri, Muzaffarpur, Bihar – 842001

- Nearest railway station: [Muzaffarpur Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur_Junction)

### CRPF Group Centre – Muzaffarpur

**[CRPF Group Centre, Muzaffarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Reserve_Police_Force)** is a major administrative and logistical hub of the Central Reserve Police Force in [North Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bihar), located at Jhapahan/Umanagar, Muzaffarpur district, Bihar (PIN 842004). It falls under the CRPF Bihar Sector and coordinates deployments, logistics, accommodation, and welfare for CRPF personnel. The centre also serves as a base for Rapid Action Force units and hosts Kendriya Vidyalaya CRPF Jhapahan for children of CRPF personnel. It is one of the main paramilitary installations in [North Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bihar), supporting internal security and rapid deployments.[^cite_note-29]

### Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)

**[Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sashastra_Seema_Bal), Muzaffarpur** is a regional headquarters of the [Central Armed Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Armed_Police_Force) located at Umanagar, Muzaffarpur district, Bihar (PIN 842004). It serves as an administrative and operational hub for SSB units in the region, coordinating deployments, training, logistics, and welfare of personnel. The centre also supports local internal security operations and engages in community development activities such as school tree plantations. Equipped with offices, barracks, training facilities, and other infrastructure, SSB Muzaffarpur is a key facility for border security, internal security, and civic engagement in North Bihar.[^cite_note-30]

## Transport

Muzaffarpur is a major railway junction in northern Bihar and is connected by rail with several parts of Bihar and other states.

The district is also connected by a network of national and state highways. The [National Highways Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Authority_of_India) identifies the [NH 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_27_\(India\)) section between Kotwa (Motihari), [Mehsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehsi) and [Muzaffarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur) as an 80-kilometre highway section, extending from km 440.000 to km 520.000.[^cite_note-36]

The NH 27 corridor connects [Muzaffarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur) with [Mehsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehsi) and [Motihari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motihari) in [Purbi Champaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purbi_Champaran_district), forming an important road link between Muzaffarpur and the East Champaran region. The [National Highways Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Authority_of_India) records the Kotwa (Motihari)–Mehsi–Muzaffarpur section as an 80-kilometre stretch of NH 27.[^cite_note-37]

### Railways

[Muzaffarpur Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur_Junction) is the principal railway station serving Muzaffarpur and is part of the [East Central Railway zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Central_Railway_zone). It provides rail connections to major cities and regions of India.

There are daily and weekly trains connecting Muzaffarpur with [New Delhi](51585.md), [Patna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patna), [Secunderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunderabad), [Kolkata](47905.md), [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), [Ahmedabad](206337.md), [Mumbai](19189.md), [Howrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howrah), [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar), [Pune](164634.md) and other major cities.[^cite_note-38]

### Roadways

 [NH 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_27_\(India\)) passes through Muzaffarpur and forms part of the [East–West highway corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_and_East%E2%80%93West_Corridor). Muzaffarpur is connected with other parts of Bihar and India through [NH 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_27_\(India\)) and other national and state highways.[^cite_note-39]

The NH 27 route continues through [Mehsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehsi) towards [Kotwa (Motihari)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotwa_\(Motihari\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and provides a direct road connection between Muzaffarpur and [East Champaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Champaran_district). The [National Highways Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Authority_of_India) identifies the [Kotwa (Motihari)–Mehsi–Muzaffarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotwa_\(Motihari\)%E2%80%93Mehsi%E2%80%93Muzaffarpur?action=edit\&redlink=1) section as an 80-kilometre stretch of NH 27.[^cite_note-40]

### Metro Rail

[Muzaffarpur Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur_Metro) is a proposed metro railway project in Muzaffarpur district, Bihar. The proposal envisages metro services within Muzaffarpur city.

For the proposed project, [RITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_India_Technical_and_Economic_Service) conducted a survey and prepared a proposed route and station plan for consideration by the Bihar government.[^cite_note-41]

### Airport

[Patahi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarpur_Airport), located in Muzaffarpur, is not currently operational. Plans for its development and expansion have included improvements to the runway and facilities for small passenger aircraft.[^cite_note-42]
