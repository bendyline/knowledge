The **Aare** (Swiss Standard German: [\[ˈaːrɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c2/De-Aare2.ogg/De-Aare2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c2/De-Aare2.ogg/De-Aare2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) or **Aar** (French: [\[aʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/96/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my-G%C3%BCnther-Heinz_J%C3%A4hnick-Aar.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my-G%C3%BCnther-Heinz_J%C3%A4hnick-Aar.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/96/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my-G%C3%BCnther-Heinz_J%C3%A4hnick-Aar.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my-G%C3%BCnther-Heinz_J%C3%A4hnick-Aar.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; Swiss Standard German: [\[aːr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bd/De-Aar2.ogg/De-Aar2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bd/De-Aar2.ogg/De-Aar2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the main [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [High Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Rhine) (its [discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)) even exceeds that of the latter at their [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence))[^cite_note-2] and the longest [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River) that both rises and ends entirely within [Switzerland](26748.md).[^cite_note-CVDE-3][^cite_note-GH-4]

Its total length from its source to its junction with the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) comprises about 295 kilometres (183 mi),[^cite_note-CVDE-3][^cite_note-eb-5] during which distance it descends 1,565 m (5,135 ft), draining an area of 17,779 km<sup>2</sup> (6,865 sq mi), almost entirely within Switzerland, and accounting for close to half the area of the country, including all of [Central Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Switzerland).[^cite_note-eb-5]

There are more than 40 [hydroelectric plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_plant) along the course of the Aare.[^cite_note-cohen-6]

The [river's name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydronymy) dates to at least the [La Tène period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_period), and it is attested as *Nantaror* "Aare valley" in the [Berne zinc tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berne_zinc_tablet).

The name was Latinized as *Arula*/*Arola*/*Araris*.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-9]

## Course

The Aare rises in the great [Aargletschers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aargletschers) (Aare Glaciers) of the [Bernese Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Alps), in the [canton of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern) and west of the [Grimsel Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsel_Pass).[^cite_note-CVDE-3] The [Finsteraargletscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finsteraarhorn) and [Lauteraargletscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauteraarhorn) come together to form the [Unteraargletscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unteraargletscher) (Lower Aar Glacier), which is the main source of water for the [Grimselsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimselsee) (Lake of Grimsel).[^cite_note-GH-4][^cite_note-cohen-6] The [Oberaargletscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberaargletscher) (Upper Aar Glacier) feeds the [Oberaarsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberaarsee), which also flows into the Grimselsee.[^cite_note-GH-4] The Aare leaves the Grimselsee just to the east to the Grimsel Hospiz, below the [Grimsel Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsel_Pass), and then flows northwest through the [Haslital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haslital), forming on the way the magnificent Handegg Waterfall, 46 m (151 ft), past [Guttannen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guttannen).

Right after [Innertkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innertkirchen) it is joined by its first major tributary, the Gamderwasser. Less than 1 kilometre (0.62 mi) later the river carves through a [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) ridge in the [Aare Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aare_Gorge) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Aareschlucht*).[^cite_note-CVDE-3] It is here that the Aare proves itself to be more than just a river, as it attracts thousands of tourists annually to the causeways through the gorge.[^cite_note-GH-4] A little past [Meiringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiringen), near [Brienz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brienz), the river expands into [Lake Brienz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Brienz). Near the west end of the lake it indirectly receives its first important [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary), the [Lütschine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCtschine), by the Lake of Brienz. It then runs across the swampy plain of the Bödeli (Swiss German diminutive for ground) between [Interlaken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlaken) and [Unterseen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterseen) before flowing into [Lake Thun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Thun).[^cite_note-CVDE-3]

Near the west end of Lake Thun, the river indirectly receives the waters of the [Kander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kander_\(Switzerland\)), which has just been joined by the [Simme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simme), by the Lake of Thun. Lake Thun marks the [head of navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_navigation).[^cite_note-cohen-6] On flowing out of the lake it passes through [Thun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thun), and then flows through the city of [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), passing beneath [eighteen bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Aare_bridges_in_Bern) and around the steeply-flanked peninsula on which the [Old City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Bern\)) is located. To the south of the Old City peninsula is the [Mattenschwelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattenschwelle?action=edit\&redlink=1), a [weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weir) which provides water for the small Matte [hydroelectric power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power_plant). [River swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_water_swimming) in the Aare is popular in Bern, and the river is sometimes full of bathers on summer days. The river soon changes its northwesterly flow for a due westerly direction, but after receiving the [Saane or La Sarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saane/Sarine) it turns north until it nears [Aarberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarberg). There, in one of the major Swiss engineering feats of the 19th century, the [Jura water correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_water_correction), the river, which had previously rendered the countryside north of Bern a [swampland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swampland) through frequent flooding, was diverted by the Aare-Hagneck Canal into the [Lac de Bienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac_de_Bienne). From the upper end of the lake, at [Nidau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidau), the river issues through the [Nidau-Büren Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidau-B%C3%BCren_Canal), also called the Aare Canal,[^cite_note-GH-4] and then runs east to [Büren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCren,_Solothurn). The lake absorbs huge amounts of eroded gravel and snowmelt that the river brings from the Alps, and the former swamps have become fruitful plains: they are known as the "vegetable garden of Switzerland".

From here the Aare flows northeast for a long distance, past the ambassador town [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solothurn)[^cite_note-CVDE-3] (below which the Grosse [Emme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emme_\(river\)) flows in on the right), [Aarburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarburg) (where it is joined by the [Wigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigger_\(river\))), [Olten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olten), [Aarau](2466.md),[^cite_note-CVDE-3] near which is the junction with the [Suhre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhre), and Wildegg, where the Seetal [Aabach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aabach_\(Seetal\)) falls in on the right. A short distance further, below [Brugg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brugg), it receives first the [Reuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuss_\(river\)), its major tributary, and shortly afterwards the [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat), its second strongest tributary. It now turns due north, and soon becomes itself a tributary of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), which it even surpasses in volume when the two rivers unite downstream from [Koblenz (Switzerland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koblenz,_Switzerland), opposite [Waldshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldshut-Tiengen) in Germany. The Rhine, in turn, empties into the [North Sea](21179.md) after crossing into the [Netherlands](21148.md).

## Incidents

On 26 May 2022, Indonesia [West Java](314356.md) Governor [Ridwan Kamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridwan_Kamil)'s eldest child, Emmeril Kahn Mumtadz,[^cite_note-\:kompas-13] was declared missing after being swept away by the river current. Eril, aged 22, was swimming in the river with his sister and friends.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The search efforts involving the police search and rescue team, maritime police, fire department, and authority of the city of Bern.[^cite_note-\:kompas-13] One week after he was declared missing, Emmeril Kahn Mumtadz was declared [dead *in absentia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presumption_of_death).[^cite_note-16] On 9 June 2022, Eril's body was located.[^cite_note-17] The funeral procession of Emmeril “Eril” Kahn Mumtadz took place in the family's burial ground located in Cimaung, Bandung regency, West Java.[^cite_note-18] Soon after news about Eril's body brought back to his home, Indonesian netizens [review bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_bomb) Aare River's Google listing, leaving negative comments and one-star ratings as if the waterway was fully to blame for the tragedy.[^cite_note-19]
