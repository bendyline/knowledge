A **toll** is a [fee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fee) charged for the use of a road or waterway.

## History

Main article: [Road toll (historical)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_toll_\(historical\))

Tolls usually had to be paid at strategic locations such as bridges (sometimes called a bridge toll) or gates. In Europe, the road toll goes back to the practice of the [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_tribes), who charged fees to travellers if they wanted to cross over mountain passages. From that time, road tolls became commonplace in [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) times, especially in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). The Empire had a "passage system" whereby a number of toll stations would be established on a route where small tolls were collected. Examples were the [Ochsenweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochsenweg) in Schleswig-Holstein which had toll stations at [Königsau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konge%C3%A5) and [Rendsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendsburg), [Neumünster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neum%C3%BCnster), [Bramstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramstedt_\(Hagen_in_Bremen\)) and [Ulzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulzburg),[^cite_note-1] as well as the [Gabler Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabler_Road?action=edit\&redlink=1) with the [Karlsfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsfried?action=edit\&redlink=1) Castle as its toll station. Another form of road tax was *[Liniengeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liniengeld)*, which had to be paid when entering the city of [Vienna](55866.md) from the beginning of the 18th century.

A special form of road toll was the *[Pflasterzoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pflasterzoll?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, which had to be paid to fund the initial cobbling of a road and its subsequent upkeep.

## Electronic collection

Main article: [Electronic toll collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_toll_collection)

See also: [Road pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_pricing)

Electronic toll collection is a wireless system to automatically collect the usage fee or toll charged to vehicles using [toll roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road), [HOV lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOV_lane), [toll bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_bridge), and [toll tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_tunnel). It is a faster alternative to [toll booths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_booth), where vehicles must stop and the driver manually pays the toll with cash or a card. In most systems, vehicles using the system are equipped with an automated radio [transponder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder) device. When the vehicle passes a roadside toll reader device, a [radio signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_signal) from the reader triggers the transponder, which transmits back an identifying number which registers the vehicle's use of the road, and an [electronic payment system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_payment_system) charges the user the toll. A major advantage is the driver does not have to stop, reducing traffic delays. Electronic tolling is cheaper than a staffed toll booth, reducing transaction costs for government or private road owners. The ease of varying the amount of the toll makes it easy to implement [road congestion pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_congestion_pricing), including for [high-occupancy lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-occupancy_toll_lane), toll lanes that bypass congestion, and city-wide congestion charges. The payment system usually requires users to sign up in advance and load money into a declining-balance account, which is debited each time they pass a toll point.
