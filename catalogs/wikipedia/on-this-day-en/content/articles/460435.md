**Holguín** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[olˈɣin\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is one of the [provinces of Cuba](239171.md), the third most populous after [Havana](49719.md) and [Santiago de Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Cuba). It lies in the southeast of the country. Its major cities include [Holguín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holgu%C3%ADn) (the capital), [Banes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banes,_Cuba), [Antilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilla,_Cuba), [Mayarí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayar%C3%AD,_Cuba), and [Moa](4428432.md).

The province has a population of slightly over one million people. Its territory exceeds 9,300 km<sup>2</sup> (3,600 sq mi), 25 percent of which is covered by forest.

## History



&#x20;Statue of Christopher Columbus near [Guardalavaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardalavaca)&#x20;

[Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) landed in what is believed to have been today's Holguín province on October 27, 1492. He declared that it was "the most beautiful land human eyes had ever seen".

The Holguín province was established in 1978, when it was split from the [Oriente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriente_Province) region.

## Economy

Like much of Cuba, Holguín's economy is based around [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), though other crops such as [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), as well as [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), are also large earners for the province.

A large [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) plus [cobalt](24580536.md) processing plant with shipping facilities was built in [Moa](4428432.md), using foreign investment, much of it from [Canada](5042916.md). [Chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) and [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) plants dot the province as well.

[Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) has only recently begun to be developed, offering beach resorts in the outskirts of the region, with a number of hotels around the [Guardalavaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardalavaca) area, Playa Esmeralda, Playa Yuraguanal, Playa Blanca, Playa Pesquero, and [Cayo Saetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayo_Saetia). The [Cuchillas del Toa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuchillas_del_Toa) [Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_Reserve), [Sierra Cristal National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Cristal_National_Park) and [Alejandro de Humboldt National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_de_Humboldt_National_Park) are partly located in the province. [Gibara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibara) is a little visited historical port located west of the main [Guardalavaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardalavaca) [resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resorts). Recently a large number of tourists are reported to have visited the city and its rich culture and beauty. Direct flights to [Canada](5042916.md) and [Europe](9239.md) from Holguin airport limit transfer time to around 1 hour to most resorts on the Costa Holguinera. [Canadians](19851291.md) and [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans) share the [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral) beaches with [Cubans](1288758.md).

## Demographics

In 2004, the province of Holguin had a population of 1,029,083.[^cite_note-atenas-3] With a total area of 9,292.83 km<sup>2</sup> (3,587.98 sq mi),[^cite_note-census2002-6] the province had a population density of 110.7/km<sup>2</sup> (287/sq mi).

Per the 2012 census,[^cite_note-census2012-4] the population was 1,035,072, and a similar population density of 111.38/km<sup>2</sup> (288.5/sq mi).
