**Mark Richard Walter** (born May 22, 1960) is an American businessman, philanthropist, and the chief executive officer of [Guggenheim Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Partners), a privately held global financial services firm with more than \$345 billion in [assets under management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assets_under_management).[^cite_note-1] He is also the CEO and co-chairman of [TWG Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWG_Global), an American multinational holding company.

Walter is best known for his investments in [professional sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_sports). He is the owner and chairman of [Major League Baseball](38776.md)'s [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md), the [Women's National Basketball Association](57721.md)'s [Los Angeles Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Sparks), the motorsports organization [TWG Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWG_Motorsports), and the [Professional Women's Hockey League](74699992.md) (PWHL). In addition, he owns part of the [Billie Jean King Cup](1108893.md).[^cite_note-2]

## Early life and education

Walter grew up in [Cedar Rapids, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Rapids,_Iowa). His father, Ed, worked at a local concrete block manufacturing plant.[^cite_note-\:5-3] He graduated from [Cedar Rapids Jefferson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_High_School_\(Cedar_Rapids,_IA\)) in 1978.[^cite_note-\:5-3]

Walter attended [Creighton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creighton_University), where he studied accounting and graduated with a bachelor's degree in business in 1982.[^cite_note-\:13-4] He graduated from the [Northwestern University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University_Law_School) in 1985.[^cite_note-\:13-4][^cite_note-\:0-5]

## Career

In 1996, Walter, Tom Irvin, and Steven E. Johnson co-founded the Liberty Hampshire Company.[^cite_note-6] In 1999, he helped found [Guggenheim Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Partners) with assistance from [Guggenheim family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_family) heir Peter Lawson-Johnson II;[^cite_note-\:13-4][^cite_note-7] he now serves as its CEO.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:18-9] Through his work at Guggenheim, he got to know future investment partner [Todd Boehly](35675191.md), who spent 14 years with the firm.[^cite_note-\:1-10][^cite_note-\:10-11]

In May 2024, Walter co-founded [TWG Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWG_Global)[^cite_note-\:18-9] and is its CEO, co-chairman, and 21% owner.[^cite_note-\:6-12][^cite_note-13] He is the controlling shareholder of Delaware Life Holdings, LLC, and serves on the board of EquiTrust Life.[^cite_note-14]

Walter has a personal stake in [Beyond Meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_Meat).[^cite_note-\:0-5]

In 2021, it was reported that Walter had purchased numerous commercial and historic buildings in [Crested Butte, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crested_Butte,_Colorado), where his family frequently takes vacations.[^cite_note-\:13-4][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

In September 2025, the *[Bloomberg Billionaires Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index)* estimated that he had a net worth of \$13.3 billion.[^cite_note-17]

## Sports investments

Walter uses sports to advertise his insurance companies, particularly TWG Global subsidiary Gainbridge, headquartered in [Zionsville, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionsville,_Indiana).[^cite_note-18] It has sponsored racing team [Andretti Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andretti_Global) since 2018[^cite_note-\:14-19][^cite_note-20] and the [Indianapolis 500](159018.md).[^cite_note-\:14-19] In 2021, it bought the naming rights to the home arena of the [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) and [Indiana Fever](240214.md), now known as [Gainbridge Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainbridge_Fieldhouse).[^cite_note-21] It also signed Fever basketball player [Caitlin Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark) as a brand ambassador in 2024.[^cite_note-22]

### Los Angeles Dodgers

Walter owns 27% of the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md) baseball team.[^cite_note-\:6-12] In 2012, he led [Guggenheim Baseball Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Baseball_Management)'s \$2.15 billion purchase of the team[^cite_note-\:2-23] using cash from Guggenheim's investments in insurance and [annuities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annuities).[^cite_note-\:7-24][^cite_note-\:12-25] Walter and his partner investors provided collateral to the insurers, including Dodgers shares, Walter's stake in Carvana, and various [Wendy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy's) hamburger franchises.[^cite_note-\:8-26] His personal contribution was reportedly \$100 million,[^cite_note-\:4-27] with Guggenheim Partners-related businesses contributing another \$1.213 billion.[^cite_note-28] The precise amount from insurance companies has been disputed, with *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* estimating a contribution of "at least \$300 million"[^cite_note-\:8-26] and *[LA Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Weekly)* putting the figure at \$100 million.[^cite_note-\:12-25] Other members of the Guggenheim consortium include [Peter Guber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guber), [Earvin “Magic” Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earvin_%E2%80%9CMagic%E2%80%9D_Johnson), [Stan Kasten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Kasten), [Todd Boehly](35675191.md), [Bobby Patton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Patton), [Billie Jean King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Jean_King), [Ilana Kloss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilana_Kloss), [Alan Smolinisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Smolinisky), and Robert L. Plummer.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Guggenheim's investments in sports have been questioned by industry commentators as riskier investments than insurance.[^cite_note-\:9-32] After insurers reportedly contributed at least \$300 million to the Dodgers' bid, government regulators investigated the arrangement but declined to take further action.[^cite_note-\:8-26]



Mark Walter at the [White House](33057.md) at an event celebrating the 2024 World Series Champion Los Angeles Dodgers

Walter appointed Kasten to run the team.[^cite_note-\:7-24] Under Walter, Kasten, and president of baseball operations [Andrew Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Friedman),[^cite_note-33] the team became baseball's "most perennially competitive team"[^cite_note-\:3-34] and won the World Series in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_World_Series), [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Series) and [2025](79597307.md). Less than a year after Guggenheim bought the team, the Dodgers signed a television deal that increased their payroll.[^cite_note-\:7-24]

### Basketball

Walter is the majority owner of the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) basketball team, and a partial owner of the [Los Angeles Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Sparks) women's basketball team.[^cite_note-\:16-35]

In 2014, Walter joined [David Geffen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Geffen), [Oprah Winfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oprah_Winfrey), and [Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison)'s unsuccessful bid for the [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md),[^cite_note-36] which eventually went to [Steve Ballmer](29656.md).[^cite_note-\:4-27]

Since 2014, Walter and Johnson have led the ownership group for the Los Angeles Sparks, Sparks LA Sports, which also includes Dodgers partners Kasten, Boehly, Patton, and Eric Holoman.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-\:17-38] He has credited Johnson with giving him the idea to "save the Sparks" and to keep them in Los Angeles.[^cite_note-\:17-38]

In 2021, Walter and Boehly acquired a 27% stake in the Lakers from [Philip Anschutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Anschutz), the principal owner of the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) and a stakeholder in [Crypto.com Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto.com_Arena), where both the Lakers and Kings play.[^cite_note-\:6-12][^cite_note-39] Walter had previously led a consortium that bid for [Anschutz Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschutz_Entertainment_Group), though Anschutz ultimately decided to keep the company.[^cite_note-\:4-27]

On June 18, 2025, Walter agreed to buy majority control of the Lakers from the [Buss family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Buss) at a \$10 billion valuation.[^cite_note-\:16-35] The *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* reported that TWG Global would also be involved in the purchase.[^cite_note-40] On August 12, 2026, a year after purchasing the team, Walter agreed to sell the Lakers to [Josh Kushner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Kushner) and [Bob Iger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Iger) for \$12.5 billion.[^cite_note-Shelburne-41] At the time of sale, Walter was under federal investigation for alleged tax fraud.[^cite_note-Shelburne-41]

### European football

Walter owns 12.7% of [BlueCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlueCo),[^cite_note-\:6-12] a holding company that bought [English Premier League](11250.md) football club [Chelsea](7473.md) in 2022 and [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Strasbourg_Alsace) in 2023.[^cite_note-\:1-10] Walter serves on Chelsea's board[^cite_note-42] but does not play an active role in day-to-day operations.[^cite_note-\:1-10]

On September 16, 2026, Chelsea announced that Clearlake Capital acquired Mark Walter's ownership interest and therefore acquire full control of the club.[^cite_note-43]

### Women's hockey

Walter owns North America's top-level professional women's [ice hockey](14790.md) league, the [Professional Women's Hockey League](74699992.md) (PWHL).[^cite_note-PWHL_1stGame-44] In collaboration with Dodgers partners King and Kloss, Walter formed a unified league by consolidating the [Professional Women's Hockey Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Women's_Hockey_Players_Association) with the remnants of its rival, the [Premier Hockey Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Hockey_Federation).[^cite_note-salvian20220524-45][^cite_note-\:15-46] The purchase ended the PWHPA's boycott of the PHF, which the PWHPA was pressuring to invest more in the sport.[^cite_note-salvian20230630-47][^cite_note-48]

Walter invested in the league[^cite_note-\:15-46] and appointed Dodgers partner Kasten to oversee the league's business operations,[^cite_note-49] which began play in 2024.[^cite_note-PWHL_1stGame-44] The Mark Walter Group owns the inaugural six teams.[^cite_note-phf-sale-espn-50][^cite_note-phf-sale-phf-51] The league's [championship trophy](76537110.md) is named after Walter and his wife.[^cite_note-\:15-46]

### Squash

Walter and his wife were the primary financial backers of the [2018–19 PSA Men's World Squash Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_PSA_Men's_World_Squash_Championship); with their help, the event presented its largest winners' purses in history. They continue to back the event, as well as the [Windy City Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windy_City_Open).[^cite_note-52]

## Personal life

Walter is married to Kimbra Walter, an attorney. She attended [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) and [Southern Methodist University Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMU_Law_School).[^cite_note-53] They have a daughter and live in the [Lincoln Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Park_\(community_area\),_Chicago) neighborhood of Chicago.[^cite_note-\:13-4] Walter was an avid fan of the [Chicago Cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cubs) growing up. In 2020, *FiveThirtyEight* reported that he makes bipartisan contributions.[^cite_note-54] In 2011, Walter made a \$30,800 contribution to the [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee), as well as a \$5,000 contribution to [Obama for America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_2012_presidential_campaign).[^cite_note-\:13-4]

### Philanthropy

Walter serves as a trustee of the [Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_R._Guggenheim_Foundation),[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Creighton University,[^cite_note-57] Northwestern University,[^cite_note-58] and the [Field Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Museum).[^cite_note-59] His wife is a trustee of the [Lincoln Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Park_Zoo).[^cite_note-60]

Mark and Kimbra Walter invest together in numerous philanthropic causes, including the [White Oak Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Oak_Conservation).[^cite_note-61] In 2014, Walter contributed \$40 million to the [Northwestern University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University_Law_School) (now the [Pritzker School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pritzker_School_of_Law)), from where he had earned his [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) degree,[^cite_note-\:0-5] and endowed a law scholarship with his wife in 2015.[^cite_note-62]

Walter leads the Los Angeles Dodgers Foundation, which is committed to promoting education and health in the greater Los Angeles community.[^cite_note-63] He donated \$50 million at the start of the 2024 season and an additional \$50 million when the Dodgers qualified for the World Series.[^cite_note-64]

In October 2024, Walter was awarded the Billie Jean King Leadership Award by the [Women's Sports Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Sports_Foundation) for his role in starting the PWHL and pledging \$5.5 million to the foundation.[^cite_note-65] In 2025, he became a leader of LA Rises, a fundraising group assigned by California Governor [Gavin Newsom](418947.md) to support rebuilding after the area's wildfires early in the year, and organized the first \$100 million donation.[^cite_note-66]
