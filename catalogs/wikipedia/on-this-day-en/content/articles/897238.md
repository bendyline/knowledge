[Operation El Dorado Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_El_Dorado_Canyon) had among its goals the destruction of 3-5 [Ilyushin Il-76](229943.md). No regime change was sought, nor any land occupied.

In the [military of the United States](32212.md), **strikes and raids** are a group of military operations that, alongside quite a number of others, come under the formal umbrella of [military operations other than war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_operations_other_than_war_\(US\)) (MOOTW).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBonnBaker20003-2] What the definition of a military strike is depends on which particular branch of the military is using them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVego2009XG-88-3] However, they do have formal, general, definitions in the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md)'s *Joint Publication 1-02*:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVego2009XG-88-3]

strike

An attack to damage or destroy an objective or a capability.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDOD2006208-4]

raid

An operation to temporarily seize an area in order to secure information, confuse an adversary, capture personnel or equipment, or to destroy a capability culminating with a planned withdrawal.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDOD2006274-5]

For the [United States Air Force](32090.md), strikes and raids are the least common types of MOOTW, there only having been eight of them in the period from 1947 to 1997, including [Operation Just Cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Just_Cause), [Operation Urgent Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Urgent_Fury), and [Operation El Dorado Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_El_Dorado_Canyon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVick199711-6] For the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md), the latter was also a raid, and [Operation Praying Mantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Praying_Mantis) was a strike.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUSMC200710—14-7]
