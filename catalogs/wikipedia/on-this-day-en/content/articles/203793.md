The **Ministry of Defence** (**MOD** or **MoD**) is a [ministerial department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_the_Government_of_the_United_Kingdom) of the [Government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md). It is responsible for implementing the defence policy set by the government and serves as the headquarters of the [British Armed Forces](31730.md).

Its principal objectives are to defend the [United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Northern_Ireland) and its interests, [promote national interests globally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) and to strengthen international peace and stability.[^cite_note-mod-about-3] The MOD also manages day-to-day running of the armed forces, contingency planning and [defence procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_procurement).

The expenditure, administration and policy of the MOD are scrutinised by the [Defence Select Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Select_Committee),[^cite_note-4] except for [Defence Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Intelligence) which instead falls under the [Intelligence and Security Committee of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_and_Security_Committee_of_Parliament).[^cite_note-5]

The Ministry of Defence has been involved in commercial activities; an example of which is their 2025 announcement to supply Norway with warships, the UK's largest warship export deal by value.[^cite_note-6]

## History

During the 1920s and 1930s, British [civil servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_servant) and politicians, looking back at the performance of the state during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), concluded that there was a need for greater co-ordination between the three services that made up the armed forces of the United Kingdom: the [Royal Navy](26061.md), the [British Army](4887.md) and the [Royal Air Force](25679.md). The formation of a united ministry of defence was rejected by the coalition government of [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George) in 1921, but the [Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) was formed in 1923, for the purposes of inter-service co-ordination. As rearmament became a concern during the 1930s, [Stanley Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Baldwin) created the position of [Minister for Co-ordination of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Co-ordination_of_Defence). [Ernle Chatfield, 1st Baron Chatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernle_Chatfield,_1st_Baron_Chatfield) held the post until the fall of the [Chamberlain government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamberlain_war_ministry) in 1940. His success was limited by his lack of control over the existing Service departments, and his lack of political influence.

On forming his government in 1940, [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) created the office of Minister of Defence, to exercise ministerial control over the Chiefs of Staff Committee and to co-ordinate defence matters. The post was held by the Prime Minister of the day until [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee)'s government introduced the [Ministry of Defence Act of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(1947%E2%80%931964\)). After 1946, the three posts of [Secretary of State for War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War), [First Lord of the Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty), and [Secretary of State for Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Air) were formally subordinated to the new [Minister of Defence](302332.md), who had a seat in the Cabinet. The three service ministers – Admiralty, War, Air – remained in direct operational control of their respective services, but ceased to attend Cabinet.

From 1946 to 1964, five Departments of State did the work of the modern Ministry of Defence: the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Admiralty), the [War Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Office), the [Air Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Ministry), the [Ministry of Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Aviation), and [an earlier form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(1947%E2%80%9364\)) of the Ministry of Defence. The [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply) existed from 1939 to 1959. Those departments merged in 1964, and the defence functions of the [Ministry of Aviation Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Aviation_Supply?action=edit\&redlink=1) were merged into the Ministry of Defence in 1971.[^cite_note-7] Thereafter the [MoD Procurement Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoD_Procurement_Executive) was established as a separate organisation to supervise all military procurement. The unification of all defence activities under a single ministry was motivated by a desire to curb [interservice rivalries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interservice_rivalry) and followed the precedent set by the American [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947).[^cite_note-8]

### Controversies

### Fraud

Main article: [Gordon Foxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Foxley)

The most notable fraud conviction has been that of [Gordon Foxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Foxley), Director of Ammunition Procurement at the Ministry of Defence from 1981 to 1984. Police claimed he received at least £3.5m in total in corrupt payments, such as substantial bribes from overseas arms contractors aiming to influence the allocation of contracts.[^cite_note-Mike_Hall-9]

### Germ and chemical warfare tests

A government report covered by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* newspaper in 2002 indicated that between 1940 and 1979, the Ministry of Defence "turned large parts of the country into a giant laboratory to conduct a series of secret [germ warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_warfare) tests on the public" and many of these tests "involved releasing potentially dangerous chemicals and micro-organisms over vast swathes of the population without the public being told."[^cite_note-10] The Ministry of Defence claims that these trials were to simulate germ warfare and that the tests were harmless. However, families who have been in the area of many of the tests are experiencing children with birth defects and physical and mental handicaps and many are asking for a public inquiry. The report estimated these tests affected millions of people, including during one period between 1961 and 1968 where "more than a million people along the south coast of England, from [Torquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquay) to the [New Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest), were exposed to bacteria including *[E.coli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escherichia_coli)* and *[Bacillus globigii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_atrophaeus)*, which mimics [anthrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthrax)." Two scientists commissioned by the Ministry of Defence stated that these trials posed no risk to the public. This was confirmed by Sue Ellison, a representative of the [Defence Science and Technology Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Science_and_Technology_Laboratory) at [Porton Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porton_Down) who said that the results from these trials "will save lives, should the country or our forces face an attack by chemical and biological weapons."

### Civil action

In February 2019, former soldier Inoke Momonakaya won £458,000 payout after a legal battle for the racial harassment and bullying he received while serving in the army.[^cite_note-11] In August 2019, *A Commons Defence Select Committee* report revealed that several female and [BAME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAME) military staff have raised concerns regarding discrimination, bullying and harassment.[^cite_note-12] In September 2019, two former British army soldiers Nkululeko Zulu and Hani Gue won a racial discrimination claim against the Ministry of Defence (MoD).[^cite_note-13] In November 2019, mixed race soldier Mark De Kretser sued MoD for £100k claiming he was subjected to "grindingly repetitive" racist taunts from colleagues.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Territorial Army cuts

In October 2009, the MOD was heavily criticised for withdrawing the bi-annual non-operational training £20m budget for the [Territorial Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Army_\(United_Kingdom\)) (TA), ending all non-operational training for six months until April 2010. The government eventually backed down and restored the funding. The TA provides a small percentage of the UK's operational troops. Its members train on weekly evenings and monthly weekends, as well as two-week exercises generally annually and occasionally bi-annually for troops doing other courses. The cuts would have meant a significant loss of personnel and would have had adverse effects on recruitment.[^cite_note-16]

### Overspending

In 2013, it was found that the Ministry of Defence had overspent on its equipment budget by £6.5bn on orders that could take up to 39 years to fulfil. The Ministry of Defence has been criticised in the past for poor management and financial control.[^cite_note-Sky_News-17] Specific examples of overspending include:

- Eight [Boeing Chinook HC3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Chinook_\(UK_variants\)#Chinook_HC3) were ordered in 1995 as dedicated [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) helicopters.

  [^cite_note-BBCChinook-18]

  &#x20;The aircraft were to cost £259 million and the forecast in-service date was November 1998.

  [^cite_note-BBCChinook-18]

  &#x20;However, although delivered in 2001, the Mk3 could not receive airworthiness certificates as it was not possible to certify the [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software), and would not enter service until 2017.

  [^cite_note-19]

  [^cite_note-20]

  &#x20;The procurement was described by [Edward Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Leigh), then Chairman of the [Public Accounts Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Accounts_Committee_\(United_Kingdom\)), as "one of the most incompetent procurements of all time" and the [National Audit Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Audit_Office_\(United_Kingdom\)) (NAO) issued a scathing report on the affair, stating that the whole programme was likely to cost £500 million.

  [^cite_note-21]

  [^cite_note-BBCChinook-18]

- In 2010, the [Nimrod MRA4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Nimrod_MRA4) maritime patrol aircraft procurement was cancelled after £3.4 billion had been spent on the programme. In addition there were termination costs which were not disclosed.

  [^cite_note-22]

  &#x20;In January 2011 it was reported by the *[Financial Times](136566.md)* that when the decision was taken to scrap the aircraft, "\[it] was still riddled with flaws".

  [^cite_note-23]

- Failure to manage stocks of supplies and spare parts, resulting in excess [inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventory#Defence_inventory) being held. The NAO raised concerns about inventory management from 1991 onwards, but a [Committee of MPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Accounts_Committee_\(United_Kingdom\)) argued in 2013 that "the root causes of the problem \[had] not been addressed". MoD officials accepted that the department had "much to do" but informed MPs that "it has already introduced its own [internal control measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_control) to prevent over-ordering of inventory, some of which are showing early signs of success". The Department told the MPs that it was "on track to reduce spending on inventory by £300 million in 2012–13, and that it \[planned] to reduce it by £500 million a year within the next three years".

  [^cite_note-24]

Following the 2025 [Strategic Defence Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_Review_\(2025\)), which suggested increased spending and large changes to the armed forces particularly toward [autonomous weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_weapon), the [Defence Investment Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Defence_Investment_Plan), expected in autumn 2025, was delayed amid warnings that there was a £28 billion funding gap over the next four years.[^cite_note-guardian-20260205-25][^cite_note-bbc-20260109-26]

Spending on renewing the [continuous at sea nuclear deterrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_at_Sea_Deterrent) and other nuclear submarine programmes through the [Defence Nuclear Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Nuclear_Enterprise) is projected to increase to between 20% and 25% of the MOD budget in the late 2020s, putting cost pressure on other spending.[^cite_note-pac-20260607-27]

### Hacking

In May 2024, the ministry's payroll system was reportedly targeted multiple times in a [cyberattack](37925700.md) in which personnel and their bank details were compromised. While initial reports attributed the [cyberattack to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_and_China), the Minister of Defence [Grant Shapps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Shapps) said it would take some time to conclude who was to blame.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

### Chiefs of the Defence Staff

The [Chief of the Defence Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff_\(United_Kingdom\)) (CDS) is the professional head of the British Armed Forces and the most senior uniformed military adviser to the [Secretary of State for Defence](302332.md) and the [Prime Minister](24150.md).

The CDS is supported by the Vice Chief of the Defence Staff (VCDS) who deputises and is responsible for the day-to-day running of the armed services aspect of the MOD through the Central Staff, working closely alongside the Permanent Secretary. They are joined by the professional heads of the three British armed services ([Royal Navy](26061.md), [British Army](4887.md) and [Royal Air Force](25679.md)) and the Commander of [Strategic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Command_\(United_Kingdom\)). All personnel sit at [OF-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranks_and_insignia_of_NATO) rank in the [NATO rank system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranks_and_insignia_of_NATO).[^cite_note-\:1-32]

Together the Chiefs of Staff form the [Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) with responsibility for providing advice on operational military matters and the preparation and conduct of military operations.

## Defence policy

See also: [Foreign policy of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_United_Kingdom)

The [Strategic Defence and Security Review 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_and_Security_Review_2015) included £178 billion investment in new equipment and capabilities.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] The review set a defence policy with four primary missions for the Armed Forces:[^cite_note-SDSR2015-38]

- Defend and contribute to the security and resilience of the UK and Overseas Territories.

- Provide the nuclear deterrent.

- Contribute to improved understanding of the world through strategic intelligence and the global defence network.

- Reinforce international security and the collective capacity of our allies, partners and multilateral institutions.

The review stated the Armed Forces will also contribute to the government's response to crises by being prepared to:[^cite_note-SDSR2015-38]

- Support humanitarian assistance and disaster response, and conduct rescue missions.

- Conduct strike operations.

- Conduct operations to restore peace and stability.

- Conduct major combat operations if required, including under NATO Article 5.

### Governance

Defence is governed and managed by several committees.

- The [Defence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom) provides the formal legal basis for the conduct of defence in the UK through a range of powers vested in it by statute and Letters Patent. It too is chaired by the Secretary of State, and its members are ministers, the senior officers and senior civilian officials.

  [^cite_note-\:0-39]

  [^cite_note-auto-40]

- The Defence Board is the main MOD corporate board chaired by the Secretary of State oversees the strategic direction and oversight of defence, supported by an Investment Approvals Committee, Audit Committee and People Committee. The board's membership comprises the Secretary of State, the Armed Forces Minister, the [Permanent Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Under-Secretary_of_State_for_Defence), the Chief and Vice Chief of the Defence Staff, the [Chief of Defence Materiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Defence_Materiel), Director General Finance and three non-executive board members.

  [^cite_note-\:0-39]

  [^cite_note-auto-40]

- Head Office and Corporate Services (HOCS), which is made up of the Head Office and a range of corporate support functions. It has two joint heads the Chief of the Defence Staff and the Permanent Secretary who are the combined TLB holders for this unit they are responsible for directing the other TLB holders.

### Contracting

Competitive procurement processes are used whenever possible,[^cite_note-47] and all new direct tender and contract opportunities valued over £10,000 are advertised on a system called the Defence Sourcing Portal. A separate internal policy generally operates in respect of low value purchasing below this threshold.[^cite_note-48]

DEFCON contract conditions are numbered defence [contract conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_term) are in contracts issued by the MOD (not to be confused with [DEFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEFCON) as used by the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md), which refers to a level of military "defence readiness condition").

Examples include:

- DEFCON 534: Subcontracting and [prompt payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_payment)

- DEFCON 620: a change control procedure

  [^cite_note-dc658-49]

- DEFCON 658 (cyber) applies to all suppliers down the [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain)

  [^cite_note-dc658-49]

- DEFCON 659 relates to security measures for disclosure of "Secret Matters" including within the supply chain, requiring a contractor to ensure that employees "engaged on any work in connection with the Contract have notice that the [Official Secrets Acts 1911–1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Secrets_Acts_1911_to_1989) apply to them and will continue so to apply after the completion or termination of the Contract", potentially also requiring employees to "sign a statement acknowledging that, both during the term of the Contract and after its completion or termination", they are bound by the Official Secrets Acts 1911–1989 (and where applicable by any other legislation).

  [^cite_note-50]

- DEFCON 705: the MOD's standard [IPR](14724.md) condition for fully funded research and technology contracts.

A full set of the DEFCONs can be accessed via the MoD's Defence Gateway (registration required).[^cite_note-51]

The government noted in 2013 that the MoD's third-party expenditure was characterised by "complex, high-value contracts". Defence purchasing contributes to government ambitions to make supply chains more accessible to [small and medium-sized enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium-sized_enterprises), but the government commented that it had yet to secure good insight into the supply chain role of SMEs.[^cite_note-52]

### Consultation

The **National Defence Industries Council** is a UK body through which the Ministry is able to consult strategically with its principal defence suppliers and, through the Council's sub-groups, to consult with bodies in specific industrial sectors. Membership consists of a range of companies and appointments are made "at the discretion of the Secretary of State".[^cite_note-53]

## Property portfolio

The Ministry of Defence is one of the United Kingdom's largest landowners, owning 227,300 [hectares](24919513.md) of land and foreshore (either freehold or leasehold) at April 2014, which was valued at "about £20 billion". The MOD also has "rights of access" to a further 222,000 hectares. In total, this is about 1.8% of the UK land mass. The total annual cost to support the defence estate is "in excess of £3.3 billion".[^cite_note-LHbulletin-54]

The defence estate consists of training areas and ranges (84.0%), [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) facilities (5.4%), [airfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airfield) (3.4%), [barracks](1033489.md) and camps (2.5%), storage and supply depots (1.6%), and other (3.0%).[^cite_note-LHbulletin-54] These are largely managed by the [Defence Infrastructure Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Infrastructure_Organisation).

### Main Building

Main article: [Ministry of Defence Main Building (United Kingdom)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_Main_Building_\(United_Kingdom\))



The MOD Main Building, Whitehall, London

The headquarters of the MOD are in [Whitehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehall) and is known as [MOD Main Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_Main_Building_\(United_Kingdom\)). This structure is [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) in style and was originally built between 1938 and 1959 to designs by [Vincent Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Harris) to house the [Air Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Ministry) and the [Board of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Trade). A major refurbishment of the building was completed under a [Private Finance Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_Finance_Initiative) contract by [Skanska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanska) in 2004.[^cite_note-55] The northern entrance in [Horse Guards Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_Guards_Avenue) is flanked by two monumental statues, *Earth* and *Water*, by [Charles Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Wheeler_\(sculptor\)). Opposite stands the [Gurkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) Monument, sculpted by [Philip Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Jackson_\(sculptor\)) and unveiled in 1997 by Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II). Within it is the Victoria Cross and George Cross Memorial, and nearby are memorials to the [Fleet Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm_Memorial) and [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force_Memorial) (to its east, facing the riverside).

[Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII)'s wine cellar at the [Palace of Whitehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Whitehall), built in 1514–1516 for [Cardinal Wolsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Wolsey), is in the basement of Main Building, and is used for entertainment. The entire vaulted brick structure of the cellar was encased in steel and concrete and relocated nine feet to the west and nearly 19 feet (5.8 m) deeper in 1949, when construction was resumed at the site after [World War II](32927.md). This was carried out without any significant damage to the structure.[^cite_note-56]
