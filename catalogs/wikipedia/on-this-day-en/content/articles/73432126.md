Presidential elections were held in [Cameroon](5447.md) on 12 October 2025.[^cite_note-1]

The presidential election resulted in a victory for the incumbent president [Paul Biya](392319.md) of the [Cameroon People's Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_People's_Democratic_Movement) who according to official published results received 54% of the vote. Opposition candidate [Issa Tchiroma](24368622.md) of the [Cameroon National Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_National_Salvation_Front) finished second with 35% of the vote.[^cite_note-2]

## Background

The [previous presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Cameroonian_presidential_election) on 7 October 2018 saw incumbent president [Paul Biya](392319.md), who has ruled since 1982, elected for another seven-year term following a 2008 constitutional amendment that removed term limits, allowing Biya to run again.[^cite_note-3] Biya's candidacy remained plausible, however, with his declaration of wanting to continue to serve the nation,[^cite_note-4] reaffirming during his 7 October campaign speech in Maroua that he will "never resign...until significant progress has been achieved in this area."[^cite_note-5] His candidacy for the 2025 presidential election caused controversy within the Cameroonian government,[^cite_note-6] while a petition was filed by opposition candidate [Akere Muna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akere_Muna) before the Constitutional Council seeking to disqualify Biya on grounds of the latter's advanced age, recurrent health absences, and presumed dependency on third parties.[^cite_note-7]

## Electoral system

The [President of Cameroon](380670.md) is elected by [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md); the candidate with the most votes is declared the winner with no requirement to achieve a majority.[^cite_note-8] Registration for voting ended on 31 August 2024, with more than seven million people estimated to have signed up.[^cite_note-29dec-9]

## Candidates

A total of 83 people registered their candidacy in the election,[^cite_note-xinhua-10] of which seven were women.[^cite_note-11] On 26 July, Elections Cameroon (Elecam) released a provisional list of 13 candidates, with [Hermine Patricia Tomaïno Ndam Njoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermine_Patricia_Toma%C3%AFno_Ndam_Njoya) as the only female candidate.[^cite_note-xinhua-10] Thirty-five appeals were subsequently made by rejected candidates before the Constitutional Council.[^cite_note-12] On 4 August, the Constitutional Council invalidated Hilaire Dzipan's candidacy, leaving 12 candidates on the race.

## Campaign

On 18 September 2025, Paul Biya's daughter, [Brenda Biya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Biya), published a video on social media calling on voters not to elect her father for president. She also accused her family of mistreating her.[^cite_note-31] Brenda subsequently deleted the video and issued an apology.[^cite_note-32]

Campaigning officially began on 27 September.[^cite_note-33]

## Aftermath

A week before the election, Minister of Territorial Administration [Paul Atanga Nji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Atanga_Nji) said that any unauthorized release of results would be deemed "high treason," saying that only the Constitutional Council can declare a winner.[^cite_note-38] Despite official results not being released yet, Issa Tchiroma declared himself the winner of the election in a speech on social media post on 14 October, and called on president Biya to concede.[^cite_note-39] [Grégoire Owona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A9goire_Owona), deputy secretary-general of Biya's RDPC, said that Tchiroma did not win and did not have the polling results.[^cite_note-40] Atanga also accused Tchiroma of plotting "a cleverly planned diabolical plan with his [occult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witchcraft_in_Africa#Cameroon) networks at home and abroad aimed at setting Cameroon ablaze".[^cite_note-41]

On 15 October, Tchiroma alleged that vote tampering had taken place, while protests broke out in several cities over allegations of electoral fraud, including at the Elecam headquarters in [Douala](19997263.md). The offices of the RDPC in [Dschang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dschang) was set on fire.[^cite_note-42] An armed vigil was established by Tchiroma's supporters outside his residence in [Garoua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garoua).[^cite_note-43] More than 800 were arrested nationwide, while conflict reports emerged as to the number of deaths, with authorities saying that 16 people were killed in the protests while opposition groups said the death toll was at 55. Data from United Nations sources suggest 48 dead, while [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) said that 2,000 people had been detained, many of whom had not been presented in court.[^cite_note-44]

On 19 October, Tchiroma released tallies showing him having won about 60% of the vote. The next day, the National Vote Counting Commission released provisional results showing Paul Biya leading with 53% of the vote, followed by Tchiroma at 35%.[^cite_note-45] The announcement of Biya as the provisional winner set off [protests in multiple cities](81442363.md).[^cite_note-46] A teacher died after she was reportedly shot by a police officer during protests in Garoua on 21 October.[^cite_note-47]

On 3 November, a three-day lockdown was launched by Tchiroma in protest against the election result, with Douala, [Maroua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroua) and Garoua being particularly affected. A partial shutdown also occurred in [Yaounde](57671.md).[^cite_note-48]

The [Episcopal Conference of Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Conference_of_Cameroon), which monitored the election, noted several irregularities during the election, including the relocation of polling stations and failure to update the electoral register, which contained the names of deceased persons.[^cite_note-protest-49]

The Constitutional Council announced the official results of the election on 27 October.[^cite_note-50]

On 6 November, Paul Biya was inaugurated for a new term as president.[^cite_note-51] Tchiroma fled to Nigeria then The Gambia, arriving Nov 7, 2025, where he was granted temporary humanitarian asylum by the Gambian government; he has continued to describe himself as "president-elect" from exile through 2026.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]
