**Nirmal Purja** ([Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language): निर्मल पुर्जा; 25 July 1983 – 30 July 2026), also known as **Nims** or **Nimsdai**, was a Nepali-British [mountaineer](20341.md). Before pursuing a career in mountaineering, he served in the [British Army](4887.md) with the [Brigade of Gurkhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_of_Gurkhas) and later in the [Special Boat Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boat_Service) (SBS), the special forces unit of the [Royal Navy](26061.md).

Purja climbed all 14 [eight-thousanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-thousander) – peaks above 8,000 metres or 26,000 feet – in a then-record time of six months and six days with the aid of [bottled oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottled_oxygen_\(climbing\)) between April and October 2019; the record stood until broken in 2023 by [Kristin Harila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristin_Harila) and [Tenjen Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenjen_Sherpa). Purja continues to hold the record for the fastest ascent of all eight-thousanders without supplementary oxygen at two years and 150 days.[^cite_note-guinnessworldrecords_623486-7] Purja was the first person to reach the summits of [Mount Everest](42179.md), [Lhotse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhotse), and [Makalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makalu) within 48 hours. In 2021, he and a team of nine other Nepalese climbers completed the first winter ascent of [K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K2). Considered one of the most accomplished climbers of his generation, Purja is widely regarded as a major figure in modern high-altitude mountaineering.[^cite_note-gq_20230119-8][^cite_note-theguardian_20260801_top-9][^cite_note-openthemagazine_20260801-10][^cite_note-11]

In July 2026, Purja and nine other climbers [died in an avalanche](83832946.md) on the Pakistani side of [Broad Peak](357055.md).

## Early life

Nirmal ("Nims")[^cite_note-nytimes_20211201-12] Purja was born on 25 July 1983[^cite_note-RedBull-13] in [Dana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana,_Nepal), a small village in Nepal's [Myagdi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myagdi_District)[^cite_note-RedBull2-14] near [Dhaulagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaulagiri), at 1,600 m above sea level.[^cite_note-NG1-15] He was the fourth of five children of Nanda Bahadur Purja and Purna Kumari.[^cite_note-thetimes_20260801-16] His father was a [Gurkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_regiments_\(India\)) soldier in the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), and his mother came from a farming background.[^cite_note-NG1-15] Because they were from different [Nepalese castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_system_in_Nepal), their marriage was frowned upon, and both were cut off financially and socially from their families.[^cite_note-NG1-15] Purja told *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic)*, "We came from a really poor family", and "As a kid, I remember I didn't even have flip-flops".[^cite_note-NG1-15] When he was four, his family moved lower down to the [Chitwan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitwan_District) near [Kathmandu](17168.md).[^cite_note-NG1-15]

Purja's three older brothers became Gurkha soldiers and they funded his attendance at an English-speaking boarding school.[^cite_note-NG1-15] During his schooling, he became proficient in [kick-boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kick-boxing).[^cite_note-NG1-15] He later attended [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University) in England, where he received a [postgraduate diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_diploma) in security management.[^cite_note-17]

## Military career (2003–2018)

Purja joined the Brigade of Gurkhas in 2003 and was accepted into the [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s [Special Boat Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boat_Service) (SBS) in 2009,[^cite_note-RedBull2-14] becoming the first Gurkha to join the elite British unit.[^cite_note-RedBull-13] He served in the SBS as a [cold-weather warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold-weather_warfare) specialist.[^cite_note-RedBull-13][^cite_note-th-18] Purja said that he was involved in all theatres of war in which Britain was engaged and that he was wounded when a sniper bullet struck the butt of his rifle, narrowly missing his neck.[^cite_note-NG1-15]

In 2018 he resigned from the SBS to focus full-time on high-altitude mountaineering.[^cite_note-NG1-15] He had become an important contributor to his family while serving in the military[^cite_note-BBC2021-19] and he was giving up his army pension, which he described as "a life-changing" amount of money.[^cite_note-NG1-15]

## Climbing career (2012–2026)

### Early Himalayan ascents

Purja made his first major Himalayan climb in 2012, reaching the summit of [Lobuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobuche) East with no previous mountaineering experience. On 18 May 2014, he made his first ascent of an [eight-thousander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-thousander) by summiting [Dhaulagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaulagiri) (8,167 metres) during a return trip of only 15 days.[^cite_note-Wanderlust-20] On 13 May 2016, he summited [Mount Everest](42179.md), his second eight-thousander.[^cite_note-Wanderlust-20]

On 15 May 2017,[^cite_note-Wanderlust-20] Purja led the Gurkha Expedition "G200E", which summited Everest with 13 Gurkhas to commemorate 200 years of Gurkha service in the British Army.[^cite_note-G200E-21] He climbed Mount Everest nine times: the third time on 27 May 2017,[^cite_note-22] the fourth on 22 May 2019,[^cite_note-23] the fifth on 31 May 2021,[^cite_note-24] the sixth on 15 May 2022,[^cite_note-NepalNews-25] the seventh 13 days later on 28 May 2022,[^cite_note-26] the eighth on 17 May 2023,[^cite_note-dailyecho_23552865-27] and the ninth on 27 May 2026.[^cite_note-Kuprianowicz-28]

### Project Possible 14/7



Purja in Kathmandu in 2019, after the final climb of Project Possible

With a plan to complete all 14 [eight-thousanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-thousander) in seven months, Purja summited the first mountain on 23 April 2019 and completed the first six-summit phase of his "Project Possible 14/7"[^cite_note-Project-4] on 24 May 2019: [Annapurna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapurna), [Dhaulagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaulagiri), [Kangchenjunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangchenjunga), [Mount Everest](42179.md), [Lhotse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhotse), and [Makalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makalu).[^cite_note-Project-4] He climbed with [Sherpas](224737.md) [Mingma Gyabu "David" Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingma_Gyabu_Sherpa), Lakpa Dendi (Zekson Son), Geljen Sherpa and Tensi Kasang, among other mountaineers. The last five summits were climbed in only 12 days. He broke his previous [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) by climbing Everest, Lhotse, and Makalu within 2 days and 30 minutes.[^cite_note-Wanderlust-20][^cite_note-THT-29][^cite_note-desnivel1-30][^cite_note-desnivel3-31][^cite_note-desnivel4-32][^cite_note-desnivel6-33]

Purja completed the second phase in July 2019, climbing [Nanga Parbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanga_Parbat) (8126 m, 3 July), [Gasherbrum I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasherbrum_I) (8080 m, 15 July), [Gasherbrum II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasherbrum_II) (8034 m, 18 July), [K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K2) (8611 metres, 24 July) and [Broad Peak](357055.md) (8047 m, 26 July), all in Pakistan.[^cite_note-THTBP-34][^cite_note-BP-35]

The third and final phase began in September 2019. He summitted [Cho Oyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cho_Oyu) (8188 m, [Tibet](31516.md)) on 23 September and [Manaslu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaslu) (8163 m, Nepal) on 27 September. On 1 October 2019, Chinese authorities granted Purja and his team a special permit to climb [Shishapangma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shishapangma) (8027 m, Tibet, China) in the autumn season, at the request of the Nepali government.[^cite_note-THT1-36] Purja left Nepal for Tibet on 18 October 2019, leading a five-member expedition,[^cite_note-THT2-37] and completed Project Possible 14/7 with a successful summit on 29 October using supplemental oxygen.[^cite_note-THTBMan-38][^cite_note-RaI-5]

Research published in 2022 noted that during Project 14/7, Purja had stopped at the rocky fore summit of Dhaulagiri and at the ridge point on Manaslu.[^cite_note-Alpinist-39]

In addition to the fastest ascent of the 14 tallest mountains with supplemental oxygen, Purja broke several further records: most 8,000 m mountains climbed in the spring season (six); most 8,000 m in the summer season (five); fastest summit of the three highest mountains in the world, (Everest, K2 and Kangchenjunga; fastest summit of the five highest mountains in the World, Everest, K2, Kangchenjunga, Lhotse and Makalu); fastest lower 8000ers, (Gasherbrum I, II, and Broad Peak); and fastest higher 8,000ers, with consecutive summits of Everest, Lhotse and Makalu in 48 hours (beats his own previous record of five days).[^cite_note-Project-4]

During Project Possible in May 2019, a photo taken by Purja of overcrowding on Everest went viral and was published in *[The New York Times](30680.md)*.[^cite_note-nytimes_20190918-40]

Project Possible was documented in the Netflix film *[14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_Peaks:_Nothing_Is_Impossible),* released on 29 November 2021.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-guinnessworldrecords_684255-42]

### First winter ascent of K2

Purja, along with nine other Nepali mountaineers, made history on 16 January 2021 as the first to climb [K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K2) in winter. His team – [Mingma David Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingma_Gyabu_Sherpa), Mingma Tenzi Sherpa, Geljen Sherpa, Pem Chiri Sherpa, Dawa Temba Sherpa, and himself – joined the team of [Mingma Gyalje Sherpa (Mingma G)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingma_Gyalje_Sherpa), Dawa Tenjin Sherpa and [Kilu Pemba Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilu_Pemba_Sherpa), and Sona Sherpa from [Seven Summits Treks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Summit_Treks). They successfully summited K2 at 16\:58 local [time in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Pakistan).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-\:0-44][^cite_note-\:1-45][^cite_note-\:2-46] This was the first successful K2 winter ascent after numerous attempts since 1987. Purja was the only team member to reach the summit without supplemental oxygen, becoming the first person to do so in winter.[^cite_note-SP-47][^cite_note-nimsdai_20210118-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-scmp_3118032-50][^cite_note-51]

After severe weather conditions hit the lower camps at the foot of K2[^cite_note-52] and some equipment was lost, Nepali mountaineers from the three teams decided to join efforts and climb the peak together.

### Further achievements

In May 2022, Purja set a new speed record for ascending Kangchenjunga, Mount Everest, and Lhotse consecutively without supplemental oxygen, with a time of 8 days 23 hours, and 10 minutes. He ascended Kangchenjunga on 7 May at 10\:50 am, Everest on 15 May at 8 am (his sixth ascent of Everest), and, after 26 hours, ascended Lhotse on 16 May at 10 am.[^cite_note-NepalNews-25]

On 4 October 2024, Purja broke two world records after summiting Shishapangma: the fastest time to summit all 14 peaks without supplemental oxygen (2 years and 5 months), and the fastest to summit all 14 peaks both with and without supplemental oxygen.[^cite_note-53]

On 21 October 2024, he became the first person to summit the 14 peaks and the [Seven Summits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Summits) both with and without supplemental oxygen.[^cite_note-54]

In 2025, Purja announced his latest challenge, the Hat-Trick Challenge, aiming to complete the 14 Peaks and the Seven Summits for the third time.[^cite_note-everestchronicle_20250521-55] He said he would self‑fund the project, with all donations and sponsorship money supporting the Nimsdai Foundation.[^cite_note-nagariknetwork_679358-56] The Foundation's mission is to support Big Mountain communities through initiatives including education, emergency aid, and environmental projects. Its most high‑profile project is the Big Mountain Clean Up,[^cite_note-homegrown_20220426-57] launched to remove waste from the high peaks.[^cite_note-58]

On 3 July 2025, Purja announced that he had summitted Nanga Parbat in Pakistan. This was his 50th successful ascent of an 8,000m peak and his 22nd without supplemental oxygen.[^cite_note-59] These ascents set two new records: the most successful summits by any climber and the most successful summits without supplemental oxygen.[^cite_note-dcnepal_20250709-60][^cite_note-61]

In May 2026, Purja broke his own world record for the fastest traverse of Everest and Lhotse without supplemental oxygen. He climbed from the summit of Everest to the summit of Lhotse in 13 hours, beating his previous record of 25 hours.[^cite_note-Kuprianowicz-28] A few days later, he summitted Makalu, marking his 56th ascent of an 8,000 m peak and further increasing his record for the highest number of 8,000 m summits.[^cite_note-everestchronicle_20260608-62]

## Personal life

Nirmal Purja was widely known as Nimsdai, with *dai* meaning "elder brother" in Nepali. Purja was of [Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magars) descent and was raised [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPurja202038-66][^cite_note-NYT-67][^cite_note-SP-47] His family had longstanding ties to the [Brigade of Gurkhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_of_Gurkhas). His father served in the [Gorkha Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_regiments_\(India\)) of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), while three of his older brothers served as [Gurkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) soldiers. His mother came from a farming background. Purja described becoming a Gurkha as his principal childhood ambition, following the example set by his father and brothers.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-climbing_20211125-69]

In 2006, he married Suchi Purja, the daughter of a Gurkha soldier, who worked in dentistry. The couple settled in [Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastleigh), near [Southampton](7920751.md), and had one daughter.[^cite_note-thetimes_20260801-16] Suchi appeared alongside her husband in connection with his mountaineering career, including in the documentary *[14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_Peaks:_Nothing_Is_Impossible)*, which portrayed the effect that his expeditions and ambitions had on their family life.

Purja's decision to leave the [Special Boat Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boat_Service) and pursue mountaineering full-time involved substantial personal and financial risks. By leaving the military in 2018, he gave up the prospect of receiving a full military pension, which he described as a life-changing amount of money. After struggling to obtain sufficient sponsorship for Project Possible, he remortgaged the family home to help finance the expedition.[^cite_note-thetimes_20260801-16]

Purja later said that when he flew from Heathrow to Kathmandu to begin the project, he had secured only about 15 per cent of the total funding required. He described the year preceding the expedition as being dominated by fundraising attempts rather than physical preparation and said that repeatedly being rejected by potential sponsors caused considerable stress.[^cite_note-climbing_20211125-69]

The documentary *14 Peaks* also depicted tensions within his family during Project Possible. His wife was concerned about the dangers he faced, his mother was experiencing serious health problems, and one of his older brothers opposed his decision to leave a secure military career to pursue the project. *The Guardian* described these circumstances, together with the pressure of sponsorship commitments and the second mortgage on his home, as an important part of the personal struggle behind the expedition.[^cite_note-theguardian_20211203_nirmal-70]

While serving in the armed forces, Purja studied at [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University), obtaining a postgraduate diploma in security management. In December 2023, the university awarded him an honorary doctorate in recognition of his military service and mountaineering achievements.[^cite_note-lboro_20231214-71]

## Controversies

### Sexual misconduct allegations

In May 2024, *The New York Times* reported on allegations of sexual misconduct against Purja levied by Finnish mountaineer [Lotta Hintsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotta_Hintsa) and Californian physician April Leonardo.[^cite_note-nytimes_20240531-72][^cite_note-adventuremagazine_20240608-73][^cite_note-\:3-74] In her December 2023 memoir, *Elämäni Vuoret 2* ("The Mountains of My Life"), Hintsa briefly described “an upsetting incident” during which an unnamed "very famous male climber…. kissed \[her] completely without warning" during a business discussion in Kathmandu, Nepal. Later, in an interview with *The New York Times*, she confirmed that “her experience was more disturbing than she had described in the book,” revealing that Purja had led her to the bedroom in his hotel suite, attempted to remove her clothes, touched her inappropriately, and masturbated next to her despite her repeated objections.[^cite_note-nytimes_20240531-72] In a separate incident that occurred in 2022, Leonardo alleged that Purja “showed up at her tent uninvited” and attempted to initiate sex with her several times after. Since the incidents took place outside their home countries, both Hintsa and Leonardo “felt powerless” and did not contact the law enforcement about their experience.[^cite_note-nytimes_20240531-72][^cite_note-adventuremagazine_20240608-73]

Purja “unequivocally denied allegations of wrongdoing” through his lawyer, and described the accounts as “defamatory and false.”[^cite_note-\:3-74][^cite_note-nytimes_20240531-72] In another statement, he announced that his team will be initiating legal proceedings against *The New York Times*.[^cite_note-75]

In light of the allegations, [Osprey Packs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osprey_Packs) withdrew as Purja’s sponsors. [Grivel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grivel_\(company\)) and [Scarpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarpa_\(company\)) suspended their marketing and promotional activities featuring the mountaineer.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

### *The Himalayan Times* blacklisting

In 2025, the Nepal Press Council blacklisted *[The Himalayan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Himalayan_Times)* and removed its government-funded advertising following an investigation into "imbalanced" articles about Purja and his companies, Skydive Nimsdai and Elite Exped.[^cite_note-78]

Across 2024, *The Himalayan Times* published 17 articles alleging wrongdoing by Purja and his businesses. In response, Purja and his companies filed a complaint with the Nepal Press Council, prompting an investigation.[^cite_note-79]

On 20 February 2025, the Council ruled that the publication had breached the Journalistic Code of Conduct, stating that the articles were "unilateral and imbalanced, aimed at creating hatred and discrimination, and potentially causing damage to individuals." As a result, the council blacklisted *The Himalayan Times* and withdrew government-funded advertising.[^cite_note-80]

On 12 March 2025, *The Himalayan Times* responded, alleging that the Nepal Press Council "has made an arbitrary decision to take 'unconstitutional action'" against them.[^cite_note-thehimalayantimes_20250312-81]

## Death

Main article: [2026 Broad Peak avalanche](83832946.md)

On 30 July 2026, Purja died while climbing [Broad Peak](357055.md) in Pakistan's [Karakoram range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_range).[^cite_note-telegraph_20260731-82][^cite_note-\:4-83][^cite_note-Prasain-84] His team of ten climbers – including American Mallory Geis, Chinese Wang Zhong, Nepali [Pur Bahadur Gurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pur_Bahadur_Gurung), Pakistani Sohail Sakhi and Omani [Nadhira Al Harthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadhira_Al_Harthy) – was hit by an avalanche at 9 am local time at around 6,600 m, between camps 2 and 3 on the west ridge of the mountain.[^cite_note-\:5-85][^cite_note-everesttoday_20260802-86] Purja's climbing company confirmed the death of all team members on 1 August. Purja's body was found on 2 August by a Nepali-Pakistani rescue team at around 5,700 m;[^cite_note-\:5-85][^cite_note-87] the Alpine Club of Pakistan announced the recovery of his remains on 5 August.[^cite_note-instagram_20260805-88]

### Tributes

[William, Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales), described Purja as "one of the world's greatest mountaineers", and said his thoughts were with his family and the families of the others killed.[^cite_note-standard_1292081-89] [Bear Grylls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Grylls) also paid tribute, describing Purja as a "remarkable and unique man" and referring to his "unstoppable spirit".[^cite_note-mirror_37503657-90] Nepal's Prime Minister [Balen Shah](70833999.md) said the history of the climber's courage and dedication "will always remain inspiring".[^cite_note-risingnepaldaily_20260801-91]
