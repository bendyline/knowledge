Parliamentary elections were held in [Egypt](8087628.md) for 17 governorates from 10 to 11 November 2025, while the remainder of the governorates held elections from 24 to 25 November 2025, with first-phase runoffs held from 3–4 December. Second-phase runoffs were held from 17 to 18 December. Annulments of 50 constituencies occurred because of electoral violations, with those elections being redone later in the month and in January 2026.

## Electoral system

The [Nation's Future Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation's_Future_Party) (NFP), the [Homeland Defenders Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Defenders_Party) (HDP), the [Republican People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_People's_Party_\(Egypt\)) (RPP), and the [Conference Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Congress_Party) spoke in favor of the [closed list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_list) system during a national dialogue held in 2023, while other parties, including the [Tagammu Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Progressive_Unionist_Rally_Party), the National Accord Party, the [Reform and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_Development_Party_\(Egypt\)), the [Al-Nour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nour_Party), and the [Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)), preferred a [proportional representation](23298.md) system.[^cite_note-1]

The [parliament](1913567.md) approved an [electoral law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_law) in May 2025 which created a mixed system; the NFP, the RPP, HDP, [Modern Egypt Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Egypt_Party), and the Coordination Committee of Parties’ Youth Leaders and Politicians were among the parties that supported it.[^cite_note-am25may-2]

The seats in parliament consist of 284 individual seats and 284 [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_list) [party list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_system) seats, with 28 additionally appointed by the president. The law marks out 143 [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) for individual seats and four constituencies for party lists. The party list constituencies include "Cairo and the Middle and South Delta" and "North, Middle and South Upper Egypt", which each represent 102 seats. The "Eastern Nile Delta" and "Alexandria and the Western Delta" both represent 40 seats.[^cite_note-ao5oct-3]

Party lists contain quotas, with each list required to have 51 women, three Christians, two candidates for "workers and farmers", two for "young people", and one each set aside for the "physically disabled" and [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates).[^cite_note-ao5oct-3]

## Dates

Voting for overseas residents was held from 7 to 8 November.[^cite_note-aj-4]

The first phase in Egypt began on 10 November 2025 and lasted until the next day; it included the [Giza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giza_Governorate), [Fayoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faiyum_Governorate), [Beni Suef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_Suef_Governorate), [Minya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minya_Governorate), [Assiut](468798.md), [New Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Valley_Governorate), [Sohag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohag_Governorate), [Qena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qena_Governorate), [Luxor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor_Governorate), [Aswan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan_Governorate), [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_Governorate), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Governorate), [Beheira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beheira_Governorate), and [Matrouh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrouh_Governorate) governorates.[^cite_note-5]

A second round of voting in Egypt began on 24 November and continues until 25 November in [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Governorate), [Dakahlia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakahlia_Governorate), [Damietta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damietta_Governorate), [Gharbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharbia_Governorate), [Ismailia](468782.md), [Kafr El Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_El_Sheikh_Governorate), [Monufia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monufia_Governorate), [North Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sinai), [Port Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Said_Governorate), [Qalyubiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalyubiyya_Governorate), [Sharqia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharqia_Governorate), [South Sinai](468812.md), and [Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Governorate) Governorates.[^cite_note-6] Around 5,606 polling stations were set up.[^cite_note-aj-4] Overseas voting for run off elections for the second phase took place beginning on 15 December[^cite_note-7] until 16 December.[^cite_note-8] Voting in the country began on 17 December[^cite_note-9] and lasted until 18 December.[^cite_note-10]

The election was the longest parliamentary election in Egyptian history, taking over three months because of various annulments of election results.[^cite_note-11]

## Parties

The [Egyptian Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Social_Democratic_Party), [Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)), and the [Reform and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_Development_Party_\(Egypt\)) agreed in February 2025 to join a proposed unified list, headed by the [Nation's Future Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation's_Future_Party).[^cite_note-12]

The parties that competed as part of the [National Unified List for Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unified_List_for_Egypt) included the Nation's Future Party, [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_Party_\(Egypt\)), the [Homeland Defenders Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Defenders_Party), the [Republican People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_People's_Party_\(Egypt\)), the [New Wafd Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Wafd_Party), the [Tagammu Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagammu_Party), the Egyptian Social Democratic Party, the Justice Party, the Reform and Development Party, the [Will of a Generation Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_of_a_Generation_Party), the Egyptian Freedom Party, the [Conference Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_Party), and the Coordination Committee for Parties’ Youth.[^cite_note-ao23oct-13]

The Egyptian Social Democratic Party, the Justice Party, and the Reform and Development Party (which were part of the [Civil Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Democratic_Movement)) formed the Democratic Path Alliance in May to contest individual seats ahead of the [2025 Egyptian Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Egyptian_Senate_election) and this election,[^cite_note-am25may-2] in addition to allying with the National Unified List.[^cite_note-14]

The [Reform and Renaissance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_Renaissance_Party) ran in the election.[^cite_note-z19nov-15]

The [Modern Egypt Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Egypt_Party) ran candidates.[^cite_note-ao10dec-16]

The [Constitution Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Party_\(Egypt\)) and the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Egypt\)) allied and ran for individual seats,[^cite_note-17] in an alliance known as the Free Path Alliance,[^cite_note-ao5oct-3] which is also translated as the Free Road Alliance.[^cite_note-18]

Various leftist parties who are part of the [Civil Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Democratic_Movement), including the [Socialist Popular Alliance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Popular_Alliance_Party), the [Egyptian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Egypt), the [Dignity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignity_Party_\(Egypt\)), and the [Bread and Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_and_Freedom_Party), formed the Social Justice Alliance ahead of the election.[^cite_note-z16oct-19] The alliance is also known as the People's Right coalition and also includes the [Egyptian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Communist_Party), the National Social Accord Party, the Hope Current, [Revolutionary Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Socialists), and the Democratic Front Party.[^cite_note-20]

Four coalitions ("Popular List, the Your Voice for Egypt List, the Call of Egypt List, and the Generation List") were disqualified from running.[^cite_note-21] The [Democratic Generation Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Generation_Party) responded that all necessary paperwork was submitted.[^cite_note-ao23oct-13]

Haitham al-Hariry, a former MP from [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) who is a member of the [Socialist Popular Alliance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Popular_Alliance_Party) (SPAP), as well as SPAP member Mohamed Abdel Halim, were disqualified from running, in addition to members of the [Al-Nour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nour_Party), after the [National Elections Authority](56255846.md) ruled that decrees by the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Egypt\)) exempting candidates were insufficient.[^cite_note-22]

## Conduct

Hanan Sharshar, a member of the [Homeland Defenders Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Defenders_Party), spoke of being pressured to pay 25 million [Egyptian pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pounds) in order to be a candidate for the National Unified List for Egypt,[^cite_note-23] which resulted in mass resignations in September.[^cite_note-24]

Candidate Nashwa al-Deeb withdrew during the first phase, which was marked by low voter turnout. According to Wael el-Sayed, who worked on behalf of Nagwa Othman, a member of the [Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)), campaign violations by the [Nation's Future Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation's_Future_Party) were the most noticeable, while a member of the [Egyptian Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Social_Democratic_Party) noted that it observed various electoral infractions.[^cite_note-25]

Zawia3 noted persistent vote buying.[^cite_note-26]

President [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](36703624.md) issued an "unprecedented" statement in mid-November calling for annulments if the outcome of a particular election was unclear.[^cite_note-27] On 18 November, Egypt annulled votes in 19 of the 70 constituencies following alleged "violations", including political ad violations and counting errors.[^cite_note-28] The [National Elections Authority](56255846.md) re-ran elections locally in those 19 constituencies, beginning on 3 December.[^cite_note-29] The repeated elections were spread out across seven governorates.[^cite_note-30] Egyptians located abroad began voting for the annulled 19 constituencies on 24 December.[^cite_note-31] The Higher Administrative Court ruled on 30 November that 29 additional districts during the first phase of the elections had to re-run their elections.[^cite_note-32] The results at four polling stations were invalidated during the second phase.[^cite_note-33]

Results for the first phase of the election were released on 18 November. The sole list to compete won in all of the constituencies in which it ran, by winning more than five percent of the vote.[^cite_note-36]

The National Elections Authority announced the results of the first round of the second phase in early December; the [Nation's Future Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation's_Future_Party) won 22 seats, the [Homeland Defenders Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Defenders_Party) won six seats, the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_Party_\(Egypt\)) won four seats, the [Republican People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_People's_Party_\(Egypt\)) won two seats, the [Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)) and the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Egypt\)) each won a seat and four independents also won seats.[^cite_note-ao4dec-37] Five opposition candidates (including the leader of the [Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)) and a member of the [Al-Nour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nour_Party)), won their second phase elections outright, while several (including a member of the [New Wafd Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Wafd_Party) and a member of the [Tagammu Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagammu_Party)) advanced to run offs.[^cite_note-38]

Runoff results for the second phase were expected be released on 25 December,[^cite_note-aj-4] though the annulments delayed their release until 10 January.[^cite_note-39]

## Aftermath

Sisi appointed 28 members to the parliament, including [Sameh Shoukry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sameh_Shoukry), on 11 January 2026.[^cite_note-40]
