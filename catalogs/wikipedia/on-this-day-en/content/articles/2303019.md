**Mary Carillo** (born March 15, 1957[^cite_note-WTABio-1]) is an American sportscaster and former professional [tennis](29773.md) player. She is an analyst for *[Tennis on NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_on_NBC)* and a reporter for [NBC Olympic broadcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Olympic_broadcasts).

## Career

### Tennis

Carillo played on the women's professional tennis circuit from 1977 to 1980. Her highest world rank was No. 33 in the [Women's Tennis Association Rankings](57768.md#WTA_rankings) from January through March 1980. She then retired, citing knee injuries.[^cite_note-5]

Carillo never won a major singles title, but did win the [1977 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Mixed_doubles) [mixed-doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_doubles_\(tennis\)) title with [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe). Carillo and McEnroe made it to the quarterfinals at [Wimbledon](91195.md) before being defeated, and later that year Carillo was a women's doubles quarterfinalist at the [US Open](82729.md).

### Sportscasting

#### Tennis coverage

Carillo began her television career working for [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network) from 1980 to 1987, [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) from 1981 to 1986 and [MSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Network) from 1981 to 1988. She then worked for [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) from 1988 to 1997 and again from 2003 to 2010. She also worked on [US Open](82729.md) coverage for [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports) from 1986 to 2014. In addition, Carillo worked as both a host and analyst on [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO)'s [Wimbledon](91195.md) coverage from 1996 to 1999, and on [Turner Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Sports)' Wimbledon coverage from 2000 to 2002. In May 2003, Carillo joined [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports) as an analyst on its [French Open](147724.md) and Wimbledon coverage, having made her debut as an analyst on NBC for the 1996 [Family Circle Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Circle_Cup) tennis event. She also does commentary on [The Tennis Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tennis_Channel) and appears regularly on [The Tennis Podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tennis_Podcast), hosted on Acast.

Carillo's candid and insightful commentary has earned her accolades throughout the industry, including the distinction of being called "the sport's top analyst" by *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)*.[^cite_note-nbc-6] She is known for her deep voice, quick wit and pointed sense of humor. Like her longtime friend and fellow [Douglaston, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglaston,_Queens), New York City native [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe), Carillo is known for her colorful turns of phrase, and is credited with coining "Big Babe Tennis" to describe the era in women's tennis dominated by large, powerful players such as [Lindsay Davenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Davenport), [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams) and [Venus Williams](167378.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-si-8] Carillo's unabashed and opinionated style of commentary has drawn criticism from several top players, including [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi), [Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams) and [Venus Williams](167378.md), and [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova).[^cite_note-maffei-9] Nevertheless, she was named Best Commentator by *[Tennis Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Magazine)* (1988–91), Best Commentator by *World Tennis* magazine (1986) and Broadcaster of the Year by the [Women's Tennis Association](57768.md) (1981 and 1985).

As a result of the [ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals)'s handling of domestic abuse allegations against [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md), Carillo stepped down from her presenting role at the [2021 Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Laver_Cup), in which Zverev played, saying she wanted no part in the "whitewashing of very serious allegations".[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

#### Olympic coverage

Carillo served as Olympic tennis analyst at both the [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Olympics) and [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Olympics) Summer Olympics and as the [skiing](28478.md) reporter for CBS's coverage at the [Albertville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Winter_Olympics), [Lillehammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Winter_Olympics) and [Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Olympics) Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-nbc-6]

During NBC's coverage of the [2002 Salt Lake City Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics) she covered [bobsled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobsled_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics), [luge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luge_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics) and [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics) competitions. Her comment that men's doubles luge is "like a bar bet gone bad" was recognized as "line of the year" in many sports television columns. In addition, Carillo's work co-hosting the 2002 Closing Ceremony alongside [Dan Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Hicks_\(sportscaster\)) earned her critical acclaim.

At the [2004 Athens Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Athens_Games), Carillo earned critical praise in her debut as a full-time Olympic host on [Bravo's coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bravo_\(US_TV_channel\)) in addition to anchoring [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network)'s live, Grand Slam-style coverage of the tennis gold medal finals. She delivered a lengthy, well-received commentary on badminton during this coverage.[^cite_note-13]

At the [2006 Winter Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Games) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), Carillo hosted *[Olympic Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Ice)*, a daily [figure skating](11152.md) show on the [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network). She co-hosted the daily figure-skating television program with [Scott Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Hamilton_\(figure_skater\)), [Dick Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Button), and [Jamie Salé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Sal%C3%A9) and [David Pelletier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pelletier).

Carillo served as late-night show host, closing ceremony host, and "Friend of [Bob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Costas)" for the [2008 Beijing Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Beijing_Games), her ninth [Olympic](22576.md) assignment and sixth with NBC. Her role focused on cultural commentary and "slice of life" pieces about China.[^cite_note-14] She repeated these duties—late-night host and human-interest reporter—for NBC's coverage of the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) in Vancouver, the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) in London, and the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics) in Sochi. She was also one of the torch bearers during the torch's tour through Canada.

Carillo was brought in to co-host the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md) opening ceremonies for NBC after *[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_\(American_TV_program\))* presenter [Savannah Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_Guthrie) stepped away in the wake of [her mother's disappearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Nancy_Guthrie).[^cite_note-15]

#### Other activities

Since 1997, Carillo has been a correspondent on [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO)'s *[Real Sports with Bryant Gumbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Sports_with_Bryant_Gumbel)*, winning a [Sports Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Emmy_Award) for her *Real Sports* feature on the [Hoyt Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Hoyt).

In 2009, 2013, and 2016, she co-hosted the 133rd, 137th and 140th [Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Kennel_Club_Dog_Show) broadcast on [USA Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Networks).

Carillo is a commentator for the *[Hallmark Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark_Channel)* special *Paw Star Game* premiering July 12, 2015. "At best, baby cats have the barest, most rudimentary grasp of the rules and regulations of American football and baseball", said Carillo. "And that's really okay with me. Frankly, watching kittens play any sport is going to be endearing and adorable."[^cite_note-Hollywood_Daily_Star_Paw_Star_Game-16] Carillo is also a commentator for Hallmark's *[Kitten Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitten_Bowl)*.[^cite_note-Hollywood_Daily_Star_Kitten_Bowl_2-17]

## Personal life

Carillo was born in New York City in the borough of [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn). She now splits her time between [Naples, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples,_Florida) and New York City's [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village). She was married for 15 years to tennis instructor Bill Bowden, with whom she has two children, Anthony (b. 1987) and Rachel (b. 1991).[^cite_note-18] They divorced in 1998.

## Legacy

In November 2025, Carillo was elected into the [International Tennis Hall of Fame](532503.md)'s class of 2026.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

- *Tennis My Way* (1984), for which she is second author to [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova)
