**Vilnius Čiurlionis International Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **VNO**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **EYVI**) ([Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language): *Tarptautinis Vilniaus Čiurlionio oro uostas*) is the [airport](1519705.md) of Vilnius, the capital of [Lithuania](17675.md). It is located 5.9 km (3.7 mi) south[^cite_note-AIP-4] of the city center. It is the largest of the three commercial airports in Lithuania by passenger traffic, with one runway and 5 million passengers a year.[^cite_note-ltou.lt-3] It is the second busiest airport in the Baltic states, after [Riga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_International_Airport),[17th-busiest airport in post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_the_former_Soviet_Union) as well as one of [Top 100 busiest airports in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe). Vilnius International Airport serves as a base for [airBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic), [Ryanair](262878.md), and [Wizz Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air). The airport is managed by [Joint Stock Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock_company) Lithuanian Airports under the [Ministry of Transport and Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_and_Communications_\(Lithuania\)).[^cite_note-5] It is the [96th busiest airport in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe). In honor of [Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikalojus_Konstantinas_%C4%8Ciurlionis), the airport was renamed to **Vilnius Čiurlionis International Airport** ([Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language): *Tarptautinis Vilniaus Čiurlionio oro uostas*) from 1 January 2025, to 31 December 2029.&#x20;

## History

### Early years



The opening of the Warsaw–Vilnius–Riga–Tallinn airline on 17 August 1932

The airport began operations in [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) on 17 August 1932[^cite_note-vno-opening-1] as [Wilno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilno)–Porubanek, Porubanek was the name of the neighbouring village which today is part of the Kirtimai district of Vilnius. Before [World War II](32927.md), it operated the then-domestic route between Wilno (Vilnius) and Warsaw as well as international route to Riga. Since 15 April 1939, it inaugurated a new route to [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas). The airport was used as a military airfield during the war. The airport resumed its activity as a civil airport as of 17 July 1944.[^cite_note-\:1-6]&#x20;

### Recent developments

Lithuanian Airlines (branded later as [FlyLAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlyLAL)) was established as the Lithuanian [flag carrier](465816.md) following independence in 1991 and inherited the Vilnius-based [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot) fleet of [Tupolev Tu-134](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-134), [Yakovlev Yak-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-40), [Yak-42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak-42) and [Antonov An-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-24), [An-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-26) aircraft, but rapidly replaced these Soviet-era aircraft types with modern [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) and [Boeing 757](151995.md) jets and [Saab 340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_340), [Saab 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_2000) turboprops. Operations were suspended effective 17 January 2009 as a result of growing financial difficulties. With the collapse of flyLAL, the airport lost its scheduled services to Amsterdam, Budapest, Istanbul, Madrid and [Tbilisi](42298.md). flyLAL used to operate to Dublin, Frankfurt, London, Milan and Paris in competition with [Aer Lingus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aer_Lingus), [airBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic) and [Lufthansa](97733.md).&#x20;

[AirBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic), the national airline of Latvia and under [Scandinavian Airlines](162868.md) part-ownership, opened up a second base at Vilnius in 2004 to complement its Riga operation and became the largest carrier at Vilnius, using [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) jets and [Fokker 50](1528661.md) turboprops. At one point, airBaltic operated to 19 destinations from Vilnius but, in 2009, the network covered only three destinations served by two aircraft based at Vilnius.&#x20;

Vilnius Airport is the main hub for Grand Cru Airlines and a base for [Wizz Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air). It used to be a main hub for Star1 Airlines until their end of operations in September 2010 and [Aurela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurela) until Aurela had lost its flight license. It was the hub for [Small Planet Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Planet_Airlines) and [Aviavilsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviavilsa) until both airlines folded. The airport was a secondary hub for [airBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic), [Estonian Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_Air) and [Skyways Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyways_Express) until they closed the bases in Vilnius.&#x20;

On 30 June 2013, [Air Lituanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Lituanica) also began its flights from the Vilnius Airport and established its base there serving several European cities. However, by 22 May 2015, the airline shut down all operations as well.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

The airport was closed for 35 days from 14 July 2017 to 17 August 2017 (inclusive) for runway reconstruction work, with all flights diverted to [Kaunas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas_Airport).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]&#x20;

In 2025, the airport was closed on several occasions due to helium-filled [weather balloons](154877.md) flying near the airport from Belarus being used to smuggle [black-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market) cigarettes into the European Union.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]&#x20;

## Terminal



Vilnius International Airport terminal building



Main entrance to the airport

The original terminal was built in 1934–1936 to a design by Józefa Mrówkowa-Ochmańska; Stanisław Połujan was the construction manager.[^cite_note-\:2-15] The three-story building was designed in modernist style, and had a restaurant with a terrace, a hotel for passengers, a newspaper kiosk, a customs post, a police station and a post office.[^cite_note-\:2-15] The building was criticized for its overly luxurious interior design and size, which was unsuitable for the small, nascent civilian air traffic in Vilnius.[^cite_note-\:2-15]&#x20;

The construction of the current airport building started in 1949 and completed in 1954.[^cite_note-\:1-6] It features a standard 1950s Soviet airport terminal design, originally intended for an airport with up to 20 aircraft movements per day. On the outside, it is decorated with sculptures of soldiers, workers and aviators, while inside walls and ceilings feature wreaths, bay leaves and stars, and until the early 1990s, the Soviet hammer and sickle, typical decor for Soviet public buildings of early post-war years.&#x20;

A new departure terminal, connected with the old building, was built in 1993.[^cite_note-16] Since then, the old building has been used as the arrival terminal only.[^cite_note-\:1-6]&#x20;

In November 2007, the new 1,000 m<sup>2</sup> (10,764 sq ft) terminal building was opened for operations which improved the capacity and facilities of the airport and complies with the requirements of the [Schengen agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_agreement). The passenger throughput of the terminal increased, passenger service quality was improved and more stringent aviation security measures were implemented. The new area of the renovated passenger terminal now reaches 3,462 m<sup>2</sup> (37,265 sq ft). It is equipped with 6 passenger boarding bridges, modern passenger [check-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check-in) equipment, new travel value and [duty-free shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty-free_shops) were opened as well as business lounge and VIP Lounge.[^cite_note-17]&#x20;

## New departure terminal

Construction of a new departure terminal at Vilnius Airport started in January 2023. After the completion of this 14,400 m<sup>2</sup> (155,000 sq ft) terminal, the total area of Vilnius Airport passenger terminals will increase by one third, and passenger throughput will double – from 1,200 passengers per hour to 2,400. Together with the construction of the new terminal, a redevelopment of road infrastructure is planned, including upgrades of engineering networks and a new transport scheme. The terminal was opened on 4 February 2025.[^cite_note-18] with projected cost of 50.2 million euros.[^cite_note-19]&#x20;

## Ground transportation



Vilnius airport railway station



The bus connecting the airport with Vilnius

### Train

Direct train services between [Vilnius Airport Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_Airport_Railway_Station) (referred to as "Oro uostas" in the schedules) and the central [station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_railway_station) of Vilnius were started in October 2008. Distance from the Airport to the Vilnius Central Railway Station (LTG Link) is 4.3 kilometres (2.7 mi), the journey takes 8 minutes.&#x20;

### Bus

The direct intercity express services operate from the Airport to Klaipėda, Palanga, Minsk and Daugavpils. Also, the Latvian company Flybus.lv operates service from Vilnius airport to Riga (via Panevėžys and Bauska).[^cite_note-\:0-101]&#x20;

### Public transportation

City's public buses operate from the airport. Tickets can be bought from: Trafi, M.Ticket. Also, the company Toks transports passengers from the bus station to Vilnius airport and back by [microbuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbus).[^cite_note-\:0-101]&#x20;

## Aviation services

Passenger handling, aircraft handling, into-plane fueling and de-icing/anti-icing services are handled by BGS and Litcargus.[^cite_note-102] \
&#x20;Pilot training is conducted at [Kyviškės airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyvi%C5%A1k%C4%97s_airfield), a non-commercial airport about 25 kilometers[^cite_note-103] from Vilnius Airport.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]&#x20;

## Incidents and accidents

- [Scandinavian Airlines Flight 2748](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Bombardier_Dash_8_landing_gear_accidents), operated with [Dash-8-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_Dash_8) (LN-RDS) with 48 passengers and 4 crew members, took off from [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) on 12 September 2007. It was heading to [Palanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palanga_International_Airport), Lithuania, but was diverted to Vilnius Airport (better suited for an emergency landing) when landing gear problems were discovered before landing. Upon touchdown, the right landing gear collapsed. All passengers and crew were evacuated safely. The local officials at the Vilnius International Airport noted that this was the most serious incident in recent years. This accident, along with the Aalborg accident just days earlier, caused all SAS Dash 8 Q400 planes to be grounded until the beginning of October of that year.

- On 23 May 2021, [Ryanair Flight 4978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryanair_Flight_4978), operated using a [Boeing 737-8AS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-8AS) with 171 passengers on board, traveling in Belarusian airspace en route from Athens to Vilnius, was intercepted by a Belarusian MiG-29 before it could reach Lithuanian airspace. It was then forced to land at [Minsk National Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk_National_Airport). Upon landing, the [Belarusian KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_KGB) arrested two of the passengers, opposition activist [Roman Protasevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Protasevich) and his girlfriend [Sofia Sapega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Sapega). The other passengers were allowed to reboard the plane to depart for Vilnius after seven hours.

- On 21 June 2023, the runway was closed after [Brussels Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airlines) Flight 2372, set to fly to [Brussels Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airport) could not take off as a landing gear tire popped while taxiing.

- On 3 February 2024, the airport was closed after [Avion Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avion_Express) Flight 8242, landing from [Milan Bergamo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Bergamo_Airport) slid off the runway upon touchdown.

  [^cite_note-107]

- On 25 November 2024, [Swiftair Flight 5960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiftair_Flight_5960) (operated by a [Boeing 737-476SF](15103676.md)) crashed on approach to the airport, killing one person and injuring three.
