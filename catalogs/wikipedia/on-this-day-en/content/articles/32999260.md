# Energy in Hungary



Hungary electricity generation by source in terawatt-hours

As of 2024, almost half of **energy in Hungary** is imported, with nuclear being the main domestic source.[^cite_note-1]

## Nuclear power

See also: [Nuclear energy in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_energy_in_Hungary)

Hungary had, in 2017, four operating nuclear power reactors, constructed between 1982 and 1987, at the [Paks Nuclear Power Plant](1835713.md).[^cite_note-3]

An agreement in 2014 with the EU and an agreement between Hungary and [Rosatom](1641369.md) may result in an additional two reactors being built for operation in 2030. The cost, estimated at €12.5bn, being funded mainly by Russia.[^cite_note-4]

## Oil

Hungary is reliant on oil from Russia for 46% of its needs in 2021, a decrease from 80% in 2013.[^cite_note-5]

An EU exemption to sanctions, following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022 allows Hungary to continue importing oil from Russia until December 2023.[^cite_note-6]

[MOL Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOL_Group) is an oil and gas group in Hungary.

## Gas

[Emfesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emfesz?action=edit\&redlink=1) is a natural gas distributor in Hungary. [Panrusgáz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panrusg%C3%A1z) imports natural gas from Russia mainly Gazprom.

The [Arad–Szeged pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arad%E2%80%93Szeged_pipeline) is a natural gas pipeline from Arad (Romania) to Szeged (Hungary).

[Nabucco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabucco_pipeline) and [South Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Stream) gas pipelines were intended to reach Hungary and further to other European countries. The [Nabucco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabucco_pipeline) gas pipeline was expected to pipe 31bn cubic metres of gas annually in a 3,300 km long pipeline constructed via Hungary, Turkey, Romania, Bulgaria and Austria. The [South Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Stream) gas pipeline was expected to pipe 63bn cu m of gas from southern Russia to Bulgaria under the Black Sea. The pipe was planned to run via Hungary to central and southern Europe.[^cite_note-7] These two were abandoned early in their design phases by a mix of disinterest, changing priorities and changes in geopolitical conditions in the larger Black Sea basin.

Hungary in 2022 is reliant on Russia for 80% of its natural gas and seeks to continue buying from Gazprom.[^cite_note-8] In October 2023 Bulgaria passed a law taxing Russian gas in transit to Hungary at 20 levs (10.22 euro) per MWh, roughly 20% of the purchase price of gas, the cost is probably payable by Gazprom. Hungary has complained about the tax.[^cite_note-9]

## Coal

The last coal electricity producer, the [Matra Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_Power_Plant) produced around 9% of the electricity needs of Hungary in 2020. It is served by two coal mines in [Visonta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visonta), and in [Bükkábrány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCkk%C3%A1br%C3%A1ny). The current generator is to shut down in 2025 to be replaced by a CCGT unit.&#x20;

## Renewable energy



Hungary renewable electricity production by source

Main article: [Renewable energy in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Hungary)

Hungary is a member of the [European Union](9317.md) and thus takes part in the EU strategy to increase its share of the [renewable energy](25784.md). The EU has adopted the 2009 Renewable Energy Directive, which included a 20% renewable energy target by 2020 for the EU.[^cite_note-EWEA2050-10] By 2030 wind should produce in average 26-35% of the EU's electricity and save Europe €56 billion a year in avoided fuel costs.[^cite_note-11]

The national authors of Hungary forecast is 14.7% renewables in gross energy consumption by 2020, exceeding their 13% binding target by 1.7 percentage points. Hungary is the EU country with the smallest forecast penetration of renewables of the electricity demand in 2020, namely only 11% (including [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) 6% and [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) 3%). The forecast includes 400 MW of new [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) capacity between 2010 and 2020. EWEA's 2009 forecast expects Hungary to reach 1.2 GW of installed wind capacity in this time.[^cite_note-EWEA2050-10] In the end of 2010 wind power capacity was 295 MW.[^cite_note-12]

### Wind power

Main article: [Wind power in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Hungary)

No new wind farms have been built since 2012, with a law in 2016 effectively banning wind farms in Hungary by requiring they be built further than 12km from any community. Capacity in 2022 is 330MW. In 2022 Hungary published a *Recovery and Resilience Plan* outlining a total of HUF 2,300 billion (ca. EUR 6 billion) for strategic development projects with the energy sector,[^cite_note-13] which may result in additional wind farms being built.

#### Solar power

Hungary had 4.8GW capacity for solar power in 2022, having grown from 26MW in 2016. The 2030 National Energy Strategy target is for 6GW capacity.[^cite_note-14] As of 2023, the installed solar power capacity was 5835 MW.[^cite_note-15] As of 2023, solar energy accounted for 18.4% of the country's total electricity production.[^cite_note-16]

## Carbon emissions

See also: [Global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming)

In 2007, emissions of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) totalled 53.9 million tonnes or around 5.4 tonnes per capita when the EU-27 average was 7.9 tonnes per capita.[^cite_note-SverigeE-17]
