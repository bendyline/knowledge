**Gotland** ([/ˈɡɒtlənd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); Swedish: [\[ˈɡɔ̌tːland\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e0/Sv-Gotland.ogg/Sv-Gotland.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e0/Sv-Gotland.ogg/Sv-Gotland.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>;[^cite_note-5] *Gutland* in [Gutnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutnish)),[^cite_note-6] also historically spelled **Gottland** or **Gothland** ([/ˈɡɒθlənd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)),[^cite_note-7] is [Sweden](5058739.md)'s largest island.[^cite_note-Renate-8][^cite_note-Sailing-9][^cite_note-Knowles-10][^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-11] It is also a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Sweden)/[county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Sweden) (Swedish län), [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Sweden), and [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dioceses,_deaneries_and_parishes_of_the_Church_of_Sweden). The province includes the islands of [Fårö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A5r%C3%B6) and [Gotska Sandön](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotska_Sand%C3%B6n) to the north, as well as the Karlsö Islands ([Lilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilla_Karls%C3%B6) and [Stora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stora_Karls%C3%B6)) to the west. The population is 61,023 (2024)[^cite_note-12] of which about 23,600 live in [Visby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby), the main town.[^cite_note-Gotlandisiffror-1]

Outside Visby, there are minor settlements and a mainly rural population. The island of Gotland and the other areas of the province of Gotland make up less than one percent of Sweden's total land area. The county formed by the archipelago is the second smallest by area and is the least populated in Sweden. In spite of the small size due to its narrow width, the driving distance between the furthermost points of the populated islands is about 170 kilometres (110 mi).[^cite_note-13]

Gotland is a [fully integrated part of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Sweden) with no particular autonomy, unlike several other offshore island groups in Europe. Historically, there was a linguistic difference between the archipelago and the mainland with [Gutnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutnish) being the native language. In recent centuries, [Swedish](26689.md) took over almost entirely and the island is virtually monolingually Swedish in modern times. The archipelago is a very popular domestic tourist destination for mainland Swedes, with the population rising to very high numbers during summers. Some of the reasons are the [sunny climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby#Climate) and the extensive shoreline bordering mild waters. During summer, Visby hosts the political event [Almedalen Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almedalen_Week), followed by the [Medieval Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Visby#Medieval_Week_in_Gotland), further boosting visitor numbers. In winter, Gotland usually remains surrounded by ice-free water and has mild weather.

Gotland has been inhabited since approximately 7200 BCE.[^cite_note-14] The island's main sources of income are agriculture, food processing, tourism, information technology services, design, and some heavy industry such as concrete production from locally mined [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone).[^cite_note-Fakta-15] From a military standpoint, it occupies a strategic location at the center of the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) and is home to the [Gotland Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotland_Regiment) which was re-established in 2018.

## Etymology

Further information: [Name of the Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_the_Goths)

The name of Gotland is closely related to that of the [Geats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geats) and [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths).[^cite_note-16]

## History

### Prehistoric time to Viking Age

The island has been occupied since [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory), as evidenced by archaeological sites such as the [Ajvide Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajvide_Settlement).[^cite_note-Outram-17] A comprehensive 2026 DNA study analyzing 56 individuals from Gotland found that during the Middle [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) the island was inhabited by two genetically distinct populations that lived in close proximity while maintaining very limited genetic admixture. Seal hunters associated with the [Pitted Ware culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitted_Ware_culture) showed strong genetic continuity with earlier [Scandinavian hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_hunter-gatherer) and shared closer genetic affinity with present-day [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts) and [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finns) populations.[^cite_note-Nature-2026-18]

In contrast, a contemporaneous [Funnelbeaker culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funnelbeaker_culture) farmer from Gotland exhibited predominantly [Anatolian Neolithic farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers) ancestry,[^cite_note-Nature-2026-18] consistent with the ancestry profile previously reported for the TRB farmer from [Gökhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6khem) parish in [Västergötland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4sterg%C3%B6tland) on the mainland, who clustered more closely with present-day Mediterranean populations.[^cite_note-19] The study also identified a high prevalence of early *[Yersinia pestis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yersinia_pestis)* strains in both populations. This genetic landscape was later transformed by the expansion of the [Corded Ware culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corded_Ware_culture), which introduced [Steppe pastoralist ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders) into the region.[^cite_note-Nature-2026-18]



Torsätra runestone ([U 614](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppland_Runic_Inscription_614)) raised in memory of one of the Swedish king's tribute collectors who fell ill and died during a trip to Gotland. [Swedish History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_History_Museum), Stockholm.

By the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age_Scandinavia), the island was inhabited by the [Gutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutes). According to the [Gutasaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutasaga), the island was first settled by [Þieluar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ej%C3%A1lfi_and_R%C3%B6skva), whose descendants became the Gutes. The saga also relates that one-third of the population emigrated to southern Europe, a tradition associated with the migration of the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths), whose name has the same origin as *Gutes*, the native name of the people of the island. It later tells that the Gutes voluntarily submitted to the king of Sweden and asserts that the submission was based on mutual agreement, and notes the duties and obligations of the Swedish King and Bishop in relationship to Gotland.[^cite_note-20] According to some historians, it is therefore an effort not only to write down the history of Gotland, but also to assert Gotland's independence from Sweden.[^cite_note-21]

It gives [Awair Strabain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awair_Strabain) as the name of the man who arranged the mutually beneficial agreement with the king of Sweden; the event would have taken place before the end of the ninth century, when [Wulfstan of Hedeby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wulfstan_of_Hedeby) reported that the island was subject to the Swedes:

> Then, after the [land of the Burgundians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornholm), we had on our left the lands that have been called from the earliest times [Blekingey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blekinge), and [Meore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6re), and [Eowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96land), and Gotland, all which territory is subject to the [Sweons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes_\(Germanic_tribe\)); and Weonodland was all the way on our right, as far as [Weissel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula)-mouth.[^cite_note-22]

The number of Arab [dirhams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirham) discovered on the island of Gotland alone is astoundingly high. In the various [hoards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoard) located around the island, there are more of these silver coins than at any other site in Western Eurasia. The total sum is almost as great as the number that has been unearthed in the entire Muslim world.[^cite_note-23] These coins moved north through trade between [Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rus'_people) merchants and the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), along the [Volga trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_trade_route), and the money made by Scandinavian merchants would help northern Europe, especially Viking Scandinavia and the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire), as major commercial centers for the next several centuries.[^cite_note-24]

The [Berezan' Runestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezan'_Runestone), discovered in 1905 in Ukraine, was made by a [Varangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangians) ([Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings)) trader named Grani in memory of his [business partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lag) Karl. It is assumed that they were from Gotland.[^cite_note-25]

#### Notable archaeological findings

Further information: [Spillings Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spillings_Hoard)



A part of the Spillings Hoard at [Gotland Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotland_Museum)

The [Mästermyr chest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4stermyr_chest), an important artefact from the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age), was found in Gotland.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

On 16 July 1999, the world's largest Viking silver treasure, the [Spillings Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spillings_Hoard), was found in a field at Spillings farm northwest of [Slite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slite).[^cite_note-28] The silver treasure was divided into two parts weighing a total of 67 kg (148 lb) (27 kg (60 lb) and 40 kg (88 lb)) and consisted mostly of coins, about 14,000, from foreign countries, mostly Islamic.[^cite_note-29] It also contained about 20 kg (44 lb) of bronze objects along with numerous everyday objects such as nails, glass beads, parts of tools, pottery, iron bands and clasps. The treasure was found by using a metal detector, and the finders fee, given to the farmer who owned the land, was over 2 million kronor (about US\$308,000).[^cite_note-30] The treasure was found almost by accident while filming a news report for [TV4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV4_\(Sweden\)) about illegal treasure hunting on Gotland.[^cite_note-31]

### Middle Ages



[Tofta Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofta_Church,_Gotland), one of the island's many [distinctive, well-preserved medieval churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_medieval_churches_on_Gotland)

Early on, Gotland became a commercial center, with the town of Visby the most important [Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) city in the [Baltic Sea](3335.md).[^cite_note-32] In late medieval times, the island had twenty district courts ([tings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thing_\(assembly\))), each represented by its elected judge at the island-ting, called *landsting*. New laws were decided at the landsting, which also took other decisions regarding the island as a whole.[^cite_note-Tacitus-33] The earliest recorded law for the island is [Gutalagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutalagen), which was written around 1220 CE and remained in use until 1645 when Sweden regained control of the island from Denmark.[^cite_note-34]

The city of Visby and rest of the island were governed separately, and [a civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Gotland_\(1288\)) caused by conflicts between the German merchants in Visby and the peasants they traded with in the countryside [had to be put down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gotland_\(1288\)) by King [Magnus III of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_III_of_Sweden) in 1288.[^cite_note-35] In 1361, [Valdemar Atterdag of Denmark invaded the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_Atterdag's_invasion_of_Gotland).[^cite_note-36] About 1,500 Gotlandic farmers were killed by the Danish invaders after massing for the [Battle of Mästerby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4sterby#Battle_of_M%C3%A4sterby).[^cite_note-Invasion-37]

In 1394, the [Victual Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victual_Brothers) occupied Gotland to set up a stronghold as a headquarters in Visby. Gotland became a [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) of the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights), awarded to them on the condition that they expel the piratical Victual Brothers from their fortified [sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate_haven).[^cite_note-Tacitus-33] In 1398, an invading army of Teutonic Knights [conquered the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Gotland_\(1398\)), destroying Visby and driving the Victual Brothers from Gotland. In 1409, Grand Master [Ulrich von Jungingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_von_Jungingen) of the Teutonic Knights guaranteed peace with the [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union) of [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), by selling Gotland to Queen [Margaret of Denmark, Norway and Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_I_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-Tacitus-33]

The authority of the landsting was successively eroded after the island was occupied by the Teutonic Order, then sold to [Eric of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_of_Pomerania) and after 1449 ruled by [Danish](76972.md) governors.[^cite_note-Tacitus-33] In late medieval times, the ting consisted of twelve representatives for the farmers, free-holders or tenants.

### Early modern period



Sockens on Gotland. The sockens are written in small text, the bolder names are the names of the municipalities existing 1950–1970.

Since the [Treaty of Brömsebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Treaty_of_Br%C3%B6msebro_\(1645\)) in 1645, the island has remained under Swedish rule.[^cite_note-Tacitus-33][^cite_note-38]

On 19 September 1806, [Gustav IV Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_IV_Adolf) offered the sovereignty of Gotland to the [Order of St. John of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller), who had been [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Malta) from [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitaller_Malta) in 1798, but the Order rejected the offer since it would have meant renouncing their claim to Malta. The Order never regained its territory, and eventually it reestablished itself in [Rome](25458.md) as the [Sovereign Military Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Military_Order_of_Malta).[^cite_note-39]

Administrative map of Gotland, 1805

On 22 April 1808, during the [Finnish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_War) between Sweden and Russia, a Russian army landed on the southeastern shores of Gotland near [Grötlingbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%B6tlingbo). Under command of [Nikolai Andreevich Bodisko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Bodisko) 1,800 Russians took the city of [Visby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby) without any combat or engagement, and occupied the island. A Swedish naval force rescue expedition was sent from [Karlskrona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlskrona) under the command of admiral [Rudolf Cederström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Cederstr%C3%B6m) with 2,000 men; the island was liberated and the Russians capitulated. Russian forces left the island on 18 May 1808.

## Administration

The traditional [provinces of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Sweden) serve no administrative or political purposes today, but are historical and cultural entities. In the case of Gotland, due to its insular position, the administrative [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Sweden) (*län*), [Gotland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotland_County), and the [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Sweden) (*kommun*), [Region Gotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_Gotland), both cover the same territory as the province. The [diocese of Visby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Visby) is also congruent with the province.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Gotland is traditionally divided into 92 [sockens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socken).[^cite_note-Gotlandisiffror-1] In January 2016, they were all reconstituted into [Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registration_districts_in_Sweden), administrative areas with the same borders as the former sockens.[^cite_note-43]

Gotland was granted its arms in about 1560.[^cite_note-44] The [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) is represented with a ducal coronet. ## Geography



Detailed map of Gotland



Topographic map of Gotland



The marine areas around Gotland, which lies between the numerals 7 and 8, which, along with No. 9, form the [Gotland Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotland_Basin)

Gotland is Sweden's largest island, and it is the largest island fully encompassed by the Baltic Sea (with Denmark's [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand) at the Baltic's edge).[^cite_note-Renate-8][^cite_note-Sailing-9][^cite_note-Knowles-10][^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-11] With its total area of 3,183.7 km<sup>2</sup> (1,229.2 sq mi) the island of Gotland and the other areas of the province of Gotland make up 0.8% of Sweden's total land area.[^cite_note-45] The province includes the small islands of Fårö and Gotska Sandön to the north, as well as the Karlsö Islands, (Lilla and Stora) to the west, which are even smaller. The island of Gotland has an area of 2,994 km<sup>2</sup> (1,156 sq mi), whereas the province has 3,183.7 km<sup>2</sup> (1,229.2 sq mi) \[3,151 km<sup>2</sup> (1,217 sq mi) of land excluding the lakes and rivers].[^cite_note-46] The population is 61,001 as of December 2021.[^cite_note-population-2] As of 2016, approximately 23,600 people (about 40% of residents) lived in Visby, which is the seat of the municipality and the capital of the county.[^cite_note-Gotlandisiffror-1]

Gotland is located about 90 km (56 mi) east of the Swedish mainland and about 130 km (81 mi) from the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), [Latvia](17514.md) being the nearest. Gotland is the name of the main island, but the adjacent islands are generally considered part of Gotland and the Gotlandic culture:

- [Furillen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furillen)

- [Fårö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A5r%C3%B6)

- [Gotska Sandön](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotska_Sand%C3%B6n), a [National park of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Sweden)

- The Karlsö Islands ([Stora Karlsö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stora_Karls%C3%B6) and [Lilla Karlsö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilla_Karls%C3%B6))

- [Laus holmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lau,_Gotland#Lau_Islets)

- Ytterholmen

- [Östergarnsholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96stergarnsholm)

There are several shallow lakes located near the shores of the island. The biggest is [Lake Bästeträsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_B%C3%A4stetr%C3%A4sk), located near [Fleringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleringe) in the northern part of Gotland. The [Hoburg Shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoburg_Shoal) [bird reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_reserve) is situated on the southern tip of the island.[^cite_note-mpa-47] The highest point of the island is [Lojsta Hed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lojsta#Lojsta_Moor) which stands 82 m (269 ft) above sea level. The average height of the island is 29 meters.[^cite_note-48]

Settlements besides Visby include:

- [Burgsvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgsvik)

- [Fårösund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A5r%C3%B6sund)

- [Hemse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemse)

- [Klintehamn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klintehamn)

- [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma,_Gotland)

- [Slite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slite)

- [Tofta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofta,_Gotland)

- [Vibble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibble)

Of these, Hemse is the largest settlement in southern Gotland and along with Roma the two largest inland villages. Burgsvik is the southernmost locality and Fårösund the northernmost. The island of Fårö is permanently settled, but with only a few hundred year-round residents and lacks a permanent fixed link to the main island. Residents are depending on an around the clock, free of charge, [car ferry](1457484.md) for transportation over a strait roughly 1.3 km (0.81 mi) wide, taking about eight minutes.[^cite_note-Fåröleden-49][^cite_note-50] Fårö may get connected to the main island with a [bridge](3397.md) in the future, but the project has had plenty of delays related to funding.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] At the closest point, the two islands are separated by less than 500 metres (1,600 ft), although that is at a distance from road connections.

Slite is the largest settlement on Gotland's sparsely populated east coast. The eastern coast of Gotland, including the adjacent marine waters and islets, has been designated an 150,000 ha [Important Bird Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area) (IBA) by [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International) because it supports a suite of [waterfowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfowl), [waders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wader) and [terns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tern).[^cite_note-bli-53]

### Climate



Gotland surrounded by the Baltic Sea showing the high levels of algal bloom in the waters (2019).

Gotland has a semi-continental variety of a [marine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Cfb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)). This results in larger seasonal differences than typical of marine climates in spite of it being surrounded by the Baltic Sea for large distances in all directions. This is due to strong continental winds travelling over the sea from surrounding great landmasses. Seasonal temperature variation is smaller in more isolated places on the island such as Hoburgen or Östergarnsholm, having warmer autumn and winter, but are cooler during spring and summer days.&#x20;

Seasonal lag is exceptionally strong in the weather station Östergarnsholm. As an example, December is warmer than March with temperature lows being similar to April. August is typically the warmest month, an unusual occurrence in Swedish sites. In the capital Visby, July and August temperatures tend to be quite similar.

Since winters usually remain just above freezing and [brackish water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackish_water) remaining liquid longer than freshwater, the sea remains ice-free all year round, except during rare extreme [cold waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_wave). The last time the whole passage from the mainland to Gotland froze was in 1987 when [icebreakers](483060.md) were used to maintain passenger and goods traffic to the island.[^cite_note-54]

### Geology

Main article: [Geology of Gotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Gotland)



[Rauks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauk) at Langhammars, Fårö



A Silurian [reef](240844.md) complex

Gotland is made up of a sequence of [sedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock) of a [Silurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silurian) age, dipping to the south-east. The main Silurian succession of [limestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and [shales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) comprises thirteen units spanning 200 to 500 m (660 to 1,640 ft) of stratigraphic thickness, being thickest in the south, and overlies a 75 to 125 m (246 to 410 ft) thick [Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician) sequence.[^cite_note-Laufeld1974-62]

It was deposited in a shallow, hot, and salty sea on the edge of an equatorial continent.[^cite_note-63] The water depth never exceeded 175 to 200 m (574 to 656 ft),[^cite_note-64] and became shallower over time as [bioherm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_knoll) detritus and terrestrial sediments filled the basin. Reef growth started in the [Llandovery Epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandovery_Epoch), when the sea was 50 to 100 m (160 to 330 ft), and reefs continued to dominate the sedimentary record.[^cite_note-Laufeld1974-62] Some sandstones are present in the youngest rocks towards the south of the island, which represent sand bars deposited very close to the shoreline.[^cite_note-Long1993-65]

The lime rocks have been weathered into characteristic [karstic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) [rock formations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rock_formations) known as [rauks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauk). Fossils, mainly of [crinoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crinoid), [rugose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugosa) [corals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral) and [brachiopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiopod), are abundant throughout the island; palæo-[sea-stacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_\(geology\)) are preserved in places.[^cite_note-66]

## Economy

The island's main sources of income are agriculture along with food processing, tourism, IT solutions, design and some heavy industry such as concrete production from locally mined [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone). Most of Gotland's economy is based on small scale production.[^cite_note-67] In 2012, there were over 7,500 registered companies on Gotland.[^cite_note-68] 1,500 of these had more than one employee.[^cite_note-Fakta-15] Gotland has the world's northernmost established vineyard and winery, located in [Hablingbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hablingbo).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

## Military

Main article: [Military on Gotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_on_Gotland)



A World War II era [pillbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillbox_\(military\)) located near Brucebo, Gotland County

Gotland occupies a strategic location in the Baltic sea from a defence viewpoint. In March 2015, the Swedish government decided to begin reestablishing a permanent military presence on Gotland, starting with an initial 150 troop garrison,[^cite_note-newgarrison-72] consisting primarily of elements from the [Swedish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Army). It has been reported that the bulk of this initial garrison will make up a new motorised rifle battalion,[^cite_note-riflebat-73] alternatively referred to in other reports as a "modular-structured rapid response Army battalion".&#x20;

A later report claimed that plans were at an advanced stage for a [support helicopter squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Air_Force#Helicopter_units) and an Air Force "fast response Gripen jet squadron" to also be based on the island to support the new garrison and further reinforce the defences.[^cite_note-defNewsA26-74] Prior to the disbandment of the original garrison, there had been a continuous Swedish military presence on Gotland in one form or another, for nearly 200 years.[^cite_note-75]

After the standing down of the original garrison, a battalion of the [Swedish Home Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Guard_\(Sweden\)) is based on Gotland for emergencies as part of the Eastern Military Region (*MR E*). The unit, *32\:a Gotlandsbataljonen* (the 32nd Gotland battalion), acts as a reserve component of the [Swedish Amphibious Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Amphibious_Corps).[^cite_note-76] Among the residual [war reserve stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reserve_stock) reported to be still in storage on Gotland in March 2015, were 14 tanks[^cite_note-77] ([Stridsvagn 122s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stridsvagn_122)) at the *Tofta skjutfält* (the [Tofta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofta,_Gotland) firing range),[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-novembertraining-79] but without any crews or dedicated maintenance personnel assigned to them.[^cite_note-tankwoes-80]



A 2005 photo of 4 IFVs ([Combat Vehicle 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_Vehicle_90)) during an exercise at *Tofta skjutfält*

Gotland currently has no local air defence capability.[^cite_note-Tjelvar1-81] Despite its importance as a naval base in the past,[^cite_note-82] as of 2004, there are no naval units based on Gotland.[^cite_note-Tjelvar1-81] The Tofta firing range, also known as the Tofta Tank firing range, is a military training ground 8 km (5.0 mi) south of Visby. Another less common name for the range is the Toftasjön firing range. Tracing its origins back to 1898, as of 2008 the range extended over 2,700 acres (11 km<sup>2</sup>). It was a major training and storage facility for the Gotland garrison during its existence, and was still occasionally used for training by elements of the [Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Armed_Forces) since the garrison was shut down in 2005.[^cite_note-novembertraining-79]

From the second half of 2014 onwards, there has been a marked increase in the use of the range, especially by armored units (mostly company sized),[^cite_note-novembertraining-79] as tensions in Northeastern Europe have escalated. At least one of the buildings on the range, the former tank [repair shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backshop), is currently owned by a private company ([Peab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peab)), with the military renting back the top floor for its own use.[^cite_note-tankwoes-80] When not used by the military, a number of cultural and sports events have been held at the range, one of the most notable being the [Gotland Grand National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotland_Grand_National), the world's largest [enduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enduro) race, from 1984 to 2023.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

During the late 2010s and early 2020s, Gotland has seen an increased focus on its strategic importance and an increase in military spending. The [Gotland Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotland_Regiment) was re-established in 2018, the first time since World War II that a new regiment has been established in Sweden. Following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and [Sweden's accession to NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden%E2%80%93NATO_relations#2022%E2%80%932024:_Accession_process_following_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), military readiness on Gotland increased, with the Swedish government spending 150 million euros to expand military infrastructure on the island.[^cite_note-Pfitzenmaier-85]

## Tourism



Norsta Aurar beach on [Fårö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A5r%C3%B6)

The first modern day tourists came to Gotland during the 19th century and were known as "bathers".[^cite_note-Kulturen-86] Gotland became very popular with socialites at the time through [Princess Eugenie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Eugenie_of_Sweden_and_Norway) who lived in [Västerhejde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4sterhejde), in the west part of the island from the 1860s.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-Turisthistoria-88]

When a new law ensuring two weeks vacation for all employees in Sweden was passed in 1938, camping became a popular pastime among the Swedes, and in 1955, Gotland was visited by 80,000 people.[^cite_note-Turisthistoria-88] In the 1970s mostly young people were attracted to Gotland. Since 2010 the island has become a more versatile vacation spot visited by people from all over the world, in all manner of ways.[^cite_note-Turisthistoria-88]

In 2001, it was the fifth largest tourist destination in Sweden based on the total number of guest nights.[^cite_note-89] Gotland is usually the part of Sweden which receives the most hours of sunlight during a year with Visby statistically the location with the most sunshine in Sweden.[^cite_note-90] In 2007 approximately 750,000 people visited Gotland.[^cite_note-Fakta-15]

In 1996, for the first time, ferries between Gotland and mainland Sweden carried more than 1 million passengers in a year. In 2007, the number of passengers exceeded 1.5 million.[^cite_note-91] In 2012, the ferries had 1,590,271 passengers and the airlines 327,255 passengers.[^cite_note-92] Even during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Sweden) tourism did not change much as Swedes chose to visit the island instead of travelling abroad.[^cite_note-93]

### Cruise ships and new pier



*[Serenade of the Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serenade_of_the_Seas)* at the pier in the cruise ship harbor, 2019

The main port of call on Gotland is Visby. The city is visited by a number of cruise ships every year.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] About 40 cruise lines frequent the Baltic sea with Visby as one of their destinations.[^cite_note-98] In 2005, 147 ships docked at Visby, in 2010 the number was 69.[^cite_note-Sweco-99] In 2014, 62 ships are scheduled to visit Visby.[^cite_note-Port-100] The decrease in visiting ships is due to the fact that the modern cruise ships are too large to enter Visby harbor.[^cite_note-Sweco-99]

Ships must anchor a fair distance from shore whereupon passengers are shuttled to shore in small boats, which is not possible during bad weather.[^cite_note-Krysskaj-101] In 2007, the first proposition for building a new pier at Visby harbor, large enough to serve the modern cruise ships, was made.[^cite_note-102] In 2011, the matter of the new pier was discussed in the [Riksdag](158651.md)[^cite_note-103] and in 2012 research and planning for the pier began.[^cite_note-Sweco-99]

In January 2014, a letter of intent for building a new cruise pier in Visby harbor was signed by [Region Gotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_Gotland) and Copenhagen Malmö Port (CMP). The pier was finished in 2018. The estimated cost is 250 million [crowns](16709.md) (about US\$38.52 million).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

### Air transport

The island is served by the only commercial airport, [Visby Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby_Airport) which offers connections to the Swedish mainland.[^cite_note-travel-106] With 467,857 passengers in 2018,[^cite_note-107] it is the 9th largest airport in Sweden.[^cite_note-108] Daily flights to [Stockholm Arlanda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Arlanda_Airport) is a common way to reach or depart from the island.[^cite_note-109]

## Culture



The eastern part of the Visby City Wall



*The lonely man on the cliff* - [Pelle Swedlund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelle_Swedlund) (1928). This painting portrays the [Högklint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6gklint), a giant and particular cliff near [Visby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby).

A number of stones with [grooves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grooves_\(archaeology\)) exist on Gotland. Archaeologists interpret these grooves as traces of an unknown industrial process in the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages). There are approximately 3,700 grinding grooves, of which about 750 occur in the solid limestone outcrop and the rest in other rock formations. The latter often consist of hard rocks such as granite or gneiss, but also soft rocks such as sandstone occur.[^cite_note-110] Grinding grooves are also found in Skåne, in southern Sweden and in Finland. Astronomer Göran Henriksson dates a number of these grinding grooves to the Stone Age, from c. 3300 BCE to c. 2000 BCE, based on astronomical alignments,[^cite_note-henriksson-111] although his methodology has been heavily criticized.[^cite_note-lindstrom_roslund-112]



A replica of a gold [torc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torc) from the [Havor Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havor_Hoard)



The [valknut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valknut) symbol has its most discovered examples on Gotland

The Medieval town of Visby has been entered as a site of the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World heritage](44940.md) programme. An impressive feature of Visby is the fortress wall that surrounds the old city, dating from the 13th century.[^cite_note-113]

Many of the residents still speak [Gutnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutnish) (Gutamål), the [autochthonous language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_language) on the islands. But most of them now speak [Gotlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotlandic) ([Swedish](26689.md): *gotländska*), a Gutnish-influenced Swedish dialect.[^cite_note-114] In the 13th century, a work containing the laws of the island, called "the Gotlandic law" (Gutalagen), was published in [Old Gutnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Gutnish), as well as the [Gutasaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutasaga).[^cite_note-115]

Gotland is noted for its 94 [Medieval churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_churches_on_Gotland),[^cite_note-116] most of which are restored and in active use. These churches exhibit two major styles of architecture: [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) and [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture). The older churches were constructed in the Romanesque style from 1150 to 1250. The newer churches were constructed in the Gothic architectural style that prevailed from about 1250–1400. The oldest painting inside one of the churches on Gotland stretches as far back in time as the 12th century.[^cite_note-117]

Traditional games of skill like [Kubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubb), [Pärk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A4rk), and [Varpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varpa) are played on Gotland. They are part of what has become called "Gutniska Lekar", and are performed preferably on the Midsummer's Eve celebration on the island, but also throughout the summer months. The games have widespread renown; some of them are played by people as far away as in the United States.[^cite_note-Stanga-118]

The knotwork design subsequently named the "[Valknut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valknut)" has the most attested historic instances on [picture stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picture_stone) in Gotland, which include being on both the [Stora Hammars I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stora_Hammars_stones) and the [Tängelgårda stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A4ngelg%C3%A5rda_stone).[^cite_note-119] Gotland also has a rich heritage of folklore, including myths about the [bysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bysen), *[Di sma undar jordi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di_sma_undar_jordi)*, Hoburgsgubben and the [Martebo lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martebo_lights).[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

Gotland gives its name to the traditional farmhouse ale [Gotlandsdricka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotlandsdricka), a turbid beer with much in common with Finnish [sahti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahti), and related beers from the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states).[^cite_note-122]

## In popular culture

*[The Long Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Ships)*, or *Red Orm* (original title: *Röde Orm*), a best-selling Swedish novel written by [Frans G. Bengtsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_G._Bengtsson), contains a vivid description of Gotland in the Viking Age. A section of the book is devoted to a Viking ship setting out to Russia, stopping on its way at Gotland and engaging a pilot from the island who plays an important part in their voyage. Gotlanders of the Viking era are depicted as city people, more sophisticated and cosmopolitan than other Scandinavians of their time, and proud of their knowledge and skills.

[Naomi Mitchison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Mitchison), in her autobiographic book "*You may well ask*", relates an experience during a walking tour in Sweden: "Over in Gotland I walked again, further than I would have if I had realized that the milestones were in [old Swedish miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_mile), so that my disappointing three-mile walk along the cold sea edge under the strange ancient fortifications was really fifteen English miles \[24 km]".[^cite_note-135]

The crime novels of [Mari Jungstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_Jungstedt), featuring Detective Superintendent Anders Knutas, are set on Gotland.

In the *[Battlefield Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlefield_Vietnam)* video game [modification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mod_\(video_gaming\)) *Invasion Gotland*, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armed_Forces) invades Gotland in 1977.

For the 1989 Studio Ghibli film, *[Kiki's Delivery Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiki's_Delivery_Service)*, by Hayao Miyazaki, he and other illustrators spent time in Gotland in preparation for animation.

## Astronomy



The night sky at the old gallows, Visby, Gotland

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of Gotland-related asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Gotland-related_asteroids).

A number of [asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) in the [main-belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_belt) are named after places on Gotland or Gotlanders, such as [10795 Babben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10795_Babben), [3250 Martebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3250_Martebo) and [7545 Smaklösa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7545_Smakl%C3%B6sa). Most of them have been named by Swedish [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) [Claes-Ingvar Lagerkvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claes-Ingvar_Lagerkvist), a summer resident on Gotland. All the Gotlandic names are vividly described in NASA's [JPL Small-Body Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPL_Small-Body_Database) in connection to each asteroid.[^cite_note-astro-136][^cite_note-sprak-137]
