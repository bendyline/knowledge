A **major non-NATO ally** (**MNNA**) is a designation given by the [United States government](195149.md) to countries that have strategic working relationships with the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md) while not being members of the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](21133.md) (NATO).&#x20;

While MNNA status does not automatically constitute a [mutual defense pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_defense_pact) with the [United States](3434750.md)—as would be the case through [NATO membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_NATO)—it does confer a variety of military and financial advantages that are otherwise unobtainable by non-NATO countries. The designation also denotes strong diplomatic and economic ties and is considered a symbol of mutual friendship.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-DeptState-2]

There are currently 22 major non-NATO allies across four continents: 12 in [Asia](689.md), 4 in [Africa](5334607.md), 4 in [South America](26769.md), and 2 in [Oceania](22621.md).

## History

MNNA status was first created in 1987,[^cite_note-USStatus-6] when [Congress](31756.md) added section 2350a — otherwise known as the [Sam Nunn Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Nunn) — to [Title 10 of the United States Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code).[^cite_note-31] It stipulated that cooperative research and development agreements could be enacted with non-NATO allies by the [secretary of defense](43998.md) with the concurrence of the [secretary of state](32293.md). The initial MNNAs were [Australia](4689264.md), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Japan](15573.md), and [South Korea](27019.md).[^cite_note-USStatus-6]

In 1996, major non-NATO allies received additional military and financial benefits when section 2321k was added to [Title 22 of the United States Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_22_of_the_United_States_Code) (also known as section 517 of the [Foreign Assistance Act of 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Assistance_Act)), which added MNNAs to many of the same exemptions from the [Arms Export Control Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_Export_Control_Act) that were enjoyed by NATO members. It also authorized the [President](24113.md) to designate a country as an MNNA thirty days after notifying Congress.[^cite_note-22USC-4] When enacted, the statute designated the initial five countries as major non-NATO allies and added [New Zealand](4913064.md) to the list.

### Israel

Further information: [Israel–United States military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_military_relations)

The [House of Representatives](19468510.md) passed the [United States–Israel Strategic Partnership Act of 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Israel_Strategic_Partnership_Act_of_2014) in December of that year.[^cite_note-32] This new category would have placed [Israel](9282173.md) one notch above the MNNA classification and would have added additional support for defense and energy infrastructure, in addition to strengthened cooperation through business and academics.[^cite_note-IsraelStrategicPartnership-33] The bill additionally called for the increasing of American [war reserve stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reserve_stock) in Israel to US\$1.8 billion.[^cite_note-IsraelStrategicPartnership-33] The bill did not reach a vote, and thus did not pass or become law.

### New Zealand

[U.S.–New Zealand strategic and military cooperation](12792048.md) suffered a setback after the breakdown of the [ANZUS Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS) in 1984, triggered by disagreements over [nuclear ship entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS#New_Zealand_bans_nuclear_material).[^cite_note-34] The designation of New Zealand as an MNNA in 1996 reflected the warming of bilateral relations between the two countries.[^cite_note-22USC-4] In June 2012, New Zealand signed a partnership arrangement with the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](21133.md) (NATO), further strengthening and consolidating relations with the United States.[^cite_note-35]

### Argentina

In 1998, President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) named [Argentina](18951905.md) as an MNNA for the "Argentine compromise and contribution to international peace and security" that was materialized in its participation in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) (Argentina was the only South American country to join the coalition's fight against [Iraq](26215470.md)), and for its continuing support for [United Nations peacekeeping missions](15652027.md).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Taiwan

When Congress enacted the Foreign Relations Authorization Act for FY 2003 on September 30, 2002, it required that [Taiwan](25734.md) be "treated as though it were designated a major non-NATO ally".[^cite_note-Kan2009-11] Despite some initial misgivings about Congress's perceived intrusion into the President's foreign affairs authority, the [Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) subsequently submitted a letter to Congress on August 29, 2003, designating [Taiwan](25734.md) as a *[de facto](43594.md)* MNNA.[^cite_note-Kan2009-11]

### Thailand and the Philippines

Around the same time as Taiwan's admission, invitations were sent to [Thailand](30128.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md), both of which accepted the offer.

[Singapore](27318.md), a long-term host of a [U.S. Air Force](32090.md) training squadron and a major [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) non-home port, was reportedly offered a similar arrangement as the Philippines and Thailand, but turned down the offer to allay concerns of its neighbors of strong Singaporean military ties with [Australia](4689264.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the United States, and [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-SingaporeUS-38][^cite_note-39] However, in October 2003, President George W. Bush and his Singaporean counterpart [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong) announced their intention to conclude a major bilateral Strategic Framework Agreement for a Closer Cooperation Partnership in Defence and Security (SFA), the details of which remained secret; it was signed on July 12, 2005.[^cite_note-SingaporeUS-38][^cite_note-40]

### Pakistan

See also: [Pakistan–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan%E2%80%93NATO_relations)

The designation of certain countries as MNNAs has not been without controversy, as has been the case with [Pakistan](23235.md), which was designated as an MNNA by President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) in 2004.[^cite_note-SAA-15][^cite_note-Riedel-41] In 2017, [Ted Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Poe) ([R](32070.md)–[TX](29810.md)) and [Rick Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Nolan) ([D](5043544.md)–[MN](19590.md)) from the [House of Representatives](19468510.md) introduced H.R. 3000; a bill to revoke Pakistan's position as an MNNA, citing [inadequate counterterrorism efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_state-sponsored_terrorism), the [harboring of Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_Osama_bin_Laden), and consistent Pakistani support for the [Taliban](30635.md).[^cite_note-42] The bill never received a vote, but in 2021, U.S. Representative [Andy Biggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Biggs) introduced H.R. 35 — another version of the legislation.

In 2017, [Joseph Dunford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Dunford), erstwhile chairman of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff), accused Pakistan's [Inter-Services Intelligence](2159021.md) of having ties to terrorist groups.[^cite_note-43] [Reuters](18998750.md) reported that "possible [Trump administration](52231341.md) responses being discussed include expanding [U.S. drone strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes_in_Pakistan) and perhaps eventually downgrading Pakistan's status as a major non-NATO ally."[^cite_note-44] In January 2023, Biggs introduced a bill to remove Pakistan's MNNA status.[^cite_note-45]

### Tunisia

In May 2015, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) declared his intention to designate [Tunisia](30188.md) as an MNNA while hosting his Tunisian counterpart [Beji Caid Essebsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beji_Caid_Essebsi) at the [White House](33057.md).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

### Brazil

In 2019, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) designated [Brazil](3383.md) as an MNNA[^cite_note-DeptState-2] for "Brazil's recent commitments to increase defense cooperation with the United States" after receiving a working visit from his Brazilian counterpart [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md).[^cite_note-48]



[NSA Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain) base is home to [US Naval Forces Central Command](9880750.md) and [US Fifth Fleet](418867.md).



Defense Secretary [Jim Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Mattis) meets with Egypt's President [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](36703624.md) during a meeting held at [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), April 5, 2017.

### Qatar

On January 31, 2022, President Joe Biden announced that [Qatar](8238258.md) would be designated as an MNNA, citing its assistance during the [United States' withdrawal from Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Afghanistan_\(2020%E2%80%932021\)) in 2021.[^cite_note-49] "This past year, our partnership with Qatar has been central to many of our most vital interests", Biden commented. "I'm notifying Congress that I will designate Qatar as a major non-NATO ally to reflect the importance of our relationship; I think it's long overdue". This relationship serves as a major upgrade for the relations between the U.S. and Qatar. It is the third country in the Gulf region to become a major non-NATO ally after Bahrain and Kuwait.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

On September 29, 2025, President Donald Trump signed an executive order guaranteeing U.S. support for the defense of Qatar if it receives an "armed attack," promising a U.S. military response to such aggression.[^cite_note-52] The order represents a commitment by Trump and his administration and is not a legally-binding treaty, which would have to be approved by at least two-thirds of the Senate in order for the president to ratify it.[^cite_note-53]

### Colombia

In March 2022, President [Joe Biden](145422.md) designated [Colombia](5222.md) as an MNNA, stating: "I've said for a long time Colombia is a keystone to our shared efforts to build a hemisphere as prosperous, secure and democratic ... Today I'm proud to announce that I intend to designate Colombia a major non-NATO ally because that's exactly what you are. A major, Major non-NATO ally."[^cite_note-ColombiaDesignation-19]

### Kenya

On May 23, 2024, President Joe Biden announced that [Kenya](188171.md) would be the 20th nation granted MNNA status, in recognition of its willingness to lead an [ intervention in Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Security_Support_Mission_in_Haiti),[^cite_note-54] which went into effect a month later on June 24.[^cite_note-KenyaDesignation-24]

### Saudi Arabia

Following his meeting with Crown Prince [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md), President Donald Trump announced on November 18, 2025 that [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) would be designated as an MNNA. Trump also stated he and Salman signed an "historic strategic defense agreement".[^cite_note-Axios_Saudi-55]

The designation, aimed at expanding bilateral military cooperation, accompanied [Riyadh](51554.md)'s pledge to increase its planned U.S. investments from US\$600 billion to \$1 trillion and Trump's confirmation that the U.S. will sell [F-35 fighter jets](11812.md) to the kingdom, making it the first [Middle Eastern](19323.md) state outside Israel to obtain the aircraft. Trump, noting the U.S. already counts 19 major non-NATO allies, said the strengthened partnership would advance shared security interests, while also remarking controversially on the 2018 killing of journalist [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi). The White House added that Saudi Arabia intends to purchase nearly 300 U.S. tanks.[^cite_note-Axios_Saudi-55]

### Peru

On December 12, 2025, President Donald Trump stated his intention to designate [Peru](170691.md) as an MNNA, as part of an outreach effort to Latin American partners while the administration escalated its campaign against Venezuela and alleged narco-terrorists.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

## Potential MNNAs

Some other countries have been proposed for granting MNNA status.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

### Ukraine, Georgia, and Moldova

See also: [Ukraine–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93NATO_relations), [Georgia–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia%E2%80%93NATO_relations), and [Moldova–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova%E2%80%93NATO_relations)

In 2014, after [Russia's annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) formally triggered the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), Ukraine's government requested MNNA status.[^cite_note-60] That year, a bill was introduced to Congress to grant MNNA status to [Georgia](48768.md), [Moldova](19260.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-61] Russia has been occupying parts of Georgia since the [Russo-Georgian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War) in 2008, and Russian troops have also stationed in Moldova's breakaway state of [Transnistria](376581.md) since the 1990s. In May 2019, a bill to designate Ukraine as an MNNA was introduced into the House of Representatives.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] As of 2023, these changes have not been made, despite support from the [U.S. Commission on Security and Cooperation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe) following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-64]

### United Arab Emirates and Oman

During a 2015 summit at [Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David) with members of the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md), the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Barack_Obama) considered designating Saudi Arabia, the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Oman](22316.md), and Qatar as MNNAs.[^cite_note-toosi-65] Qatar was later designated a MNNA by the [Biden administration](65732174.md) in 2022, followed by Saudi Arabia in 2025 by the [second Trump administration](78298108.md). In 2024, the United States recognized the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) as a major defense partner.[^cite_note-66]

### Iraq

Since 2008, [Iraq](7515928.md) and the United States have a strategic partnership that was formalized in their [Strategic Framework Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Iraq_Status_of_Forces_Agreement). It covers a range of areas, including security, economics, and culture. The United States has provided military and economic aid to Iraq since the overthrow of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) during the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md). Following the rise of the [Islamic State](9087364.md) in the mid-2010s, American troops were again deployed to Iraq in order to support the Iraqi military in their fight against the militant group. Although [most American troops withdrew from Iraq in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Iraq_\(2020%E2%80%932021\)), the United States has continued to provide military and economic assistance to the Iraqi government.[^cite_note-67]

### Mexico

After Colombia's nomination as an MNNA, some have proposed designating Mexico as well.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

### Ecuador

Law makers have proposed giving Ecuador the status of Major non-NATO ally.[^cite_note-70]

## Benefits

Countries designated as MNNAs are eligible for the following benefits:[^cite_note-DeptState-2]

- Entry into cooperative research and development projects with the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) (DoD) on a shared-cost basis;

- Participation in certain counterterrorism initiatives;

- Purchase of [depleted uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depleted_uranium) [anti-tank rounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare);

- Priority delivery of [military surplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_surplus) (ranging from [military rations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_rations) to [naval ships](1176720.md));

- Possession of [War Reserve Stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reserve_stock) of DoD-owned equipment that are kept outside of [American military bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_military_bases);

- Loans of equipment and materials for cooperative research and development projects and evaluations;

- Permission to use [American financing](22557286.md) for the purchase or lease of certain defense equipment;

- Reciprocal training;

- Expedited export processing of [space technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_technology);

- Permission for the country's corporations to bid on certain DoD contracts for the repair and maintenance of military equipment outside of the United States.

## Major partners

### India as a major defense partner

See also: [India–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_States_relations)

[India](14533.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) have significantly expanded defense and strategic cooperation since the early 21st century. In June 2016, the United States designated India a *Major Defense Partner*, a unique status intended to facilitate greater defense trade and technology sharing.[^cite_note-71] The designation was later codified into U.S. law through the [John S. McCain National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_S._McCain_National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2019).[^cite_note-72] This occurred less than a month after the House of Representatives passed the India Defense Technology and Partnership Act.[^cite_note-73] This allowed India to buy more advanced and sensitive technologies on par with those of the United States' closest allies and partners.[^cite_note-74]

India and the United States have also concluded several [foundational defense agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundational_agreement), including the Logistics Exchange Memorandum of Agreement (2016), the Communications Compatibility and Security Agreement (2018), the Basic Exchange and Cooperation Agreement (2020) which expanded interoperability and intelligence sharing between the two militaries,[^cite_note-75] and the pre-existing General Security of Military Information Agreement signed in 2002 that was further augmented by the Industrial Security Annex, signed in 2019,[^cite_note-76]

In June 2019, American lawmakers provided for enhancements to the strategic status of [India](14533.md), though this fell short of designating the country as an MNNA.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

Some analysts have described the level of cooperation between the two countries as comparable to that of certain U.S. Major Non-NATO Allies, although India continues to emphasize its policy of strategic autonomy and has not sought formal alliance status.[^cite_note-79]
