**Stockton** is a city in and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [San Joaquin County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_County,_California), United States.[^cite_note-NACo-13] It is the most populous city in the county, the [11th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_California) in California and the [60th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the U.S., with 320,804 residents at the 2020 census.[^cite_note-2020_Census_(City)-9] The city is located on the [San Joaquin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_River) in the northern [San Joaquin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley), within California's [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_\(California\)). It lies at the southeastern corner of a [large inland river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento%E2%80%93San_Joaquin_River_Delta) that separates it from other nearby cities, such as [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento) and the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area).

Stockton was founded by [Charles Maria Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Maria_Weber) during the [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) in 1849, after he acquired [Rancho Campo de los Franceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Campo_de_los_Franceses) to capitalize on its strategic location on the San Joaquin River. The city is named after famed [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) commodore [Robert F. Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Stockton),[^cite_note-14] and it was the first community in California to have a name not of Spanish or Native American origin. With the introduction of irrigation, railroad connections, and the opening of the [Port of Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Stockton) in 1933, the city developed as a logistics and commercial hub. Notable attractions in the city include the [Haggin Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haggin_Museum) and the [Gurdwara Sahib of Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Sahib_of_Stockton), the first [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) house of worship established in the U.S. The [University of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(United_States\)), chartered in 1851, is the oldest university in California and has been located in Stockton since 1923.

## History



[Carl David Maria Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_David_Maria_Weber) founded Stockton when he acquired and settled [Rancho Campo de los Franceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Campo_de_los_Franceses).

When Europeans first arrived in the Stockton area, it was settled by the Yatchicumne, a branch of the Northern Valley [Yokuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokuts_people). They built their villages on low mounds to keep their homes above regular floods. The Yatchicumne village of Pasasimas was located on a mound between Edison and Harrison Streets on what is now the [Stockton Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Channel) in downtown Stockton.[^cite_note-historycity-15]

The [Siskiyou Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siskiyou_Trail) began in the northern [San Joaquin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley). It was a centuries-old Native American footpath that led through the [Sacramento Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Valley) over the [Cascades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range) and into present-day [Oregon](26811621.md).[^cite_note-16]

The extensive network of waterways in and around Stockton was fished and navigated by [Miwok Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_and_Sierra_Miwok) for centuries. During the [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush), the [San Joaquin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_River) was navigable by ocean-going vessels, making Stockton a natural inland seaport and point of supply and departure for prospective gold-miners. From the mid-19th century onward, Stockton became the region's transportation hub, dealing mainly with agricultural products.

#### Mexican era

[Carl David Maria Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_David_Maria_Weber) was a German immigrant to the United States in 1836. He was born as Karl David Weber (February 18, 1814, in [Steinwenden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinwenden) – May 4, 1881, in Stockton) and then went by Charles in 1836 in the United States, first spending time in [New Orleans](53842.md) and then in [Texas](29810.md). He then came overland from Missouri to California with the [Bartleson-Bidwell Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartleson-Bidwell_Party) in 1841 and began to go by Carlos when he began working for [John Sutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sutter). In 1842, Weber settled in the [Pueblo of San José](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblo_of_San_Jos%C3%A9).

As an alien, Weber could not secure a land grant directly, so he formed a partnership with Guillermo (William) Gulnac. Born in New York, Gulnac had married a Mexican woman and sworn allegiance to Mexico, which then ruled California. He applied in Weber's place for [Rancho Campo de los Franceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Campo_de_los_Franceses), a land grant of 11 square [leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_\(unit\)) on the east side of the San Joaquin River.[^cite_note-17]

Gulnac and Weber dissolved their partnership in 1843. Gulnac's attempts to settle the Rancho Campo de los Franceses failed, and Weber acquired it in 1845. In 1846, Weber had induced a number of settlers to locate on the rancho when the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) broke out. Considered a Californio, Weber was offered the position of captain by Mexican general [José Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Castro), which he declined; he later, however, accepted the position of captain in the Cavalry of the United States. Captain Weber's decision to change sides lost him a great deal of the trust he had built up among his Mexican business partners. As a result, he moved to the grant in 1847 and sold his business in San Jose in 1849.

#### Gold Rush era

At the start of the [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) in 1848, Europeans and Americans started to arrive in the area of Weber's rancho on their way to the goldfields. When Weber decided to try his hand at gold mining in late 1848, he soon found selling supplies to [gold-seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) was more profitable.[^cite_note-18]

As the head of navigation on the San Joaquin River, the city grew rapidly as a miners' supply point during the Gold Rush. Weber built the first permanent residence in the [San Joaquin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley) on a piece of land now known as Weber Point.[^cite_note-historycity-15] During the Gold Rush, the location of what is now Stockton developed as a river port, the hub of roads to the gold settlements in the San Joaquin Valley and northern terminus of the [Stockton - Los Angeles Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_-_Los_Angeles_Road). During its early years, Stockton was known by several names, including "Weberville," "Fat City," "Mudville" and "California's Sunrise Seaport."[^cite_note-uopabout-2] In 1849, Weber laid out a town, which he named "Tuleburg," but he soon decided on "Stockton" in honor of [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) [Robert F. Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Stockton). Stockton was the first community in California to have a name that was neither Spanish nor [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) in origin.[^cite_note-facts-1] The first baptism performed in the [San Joaquin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley) occurred at the [First Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Christian_Church_\(Stockton,_California\)) in February 1852.[^cite_note-RecordNov1924pg39-19]

#### Chinese immigration

Thousands of Chinese came to Stockton from [Guangdong](65109.md) province of China during the 1850s due to a combination of political and economic unrest in China and the discovery of gold in California. After the gold rush, many worked for the railroads and land reclamation projects in the [Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento%E2%80%93San_Joaquin_River_Delta) and remained in Stockton. By 1880 Stockton was home to the third-largest Chinese community in California. Discriminatory laws, in particular the [Chinese Exclusion Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Exclusion_Act) of 1882, restricted immigration and prevented the Chinese from buying property.[^cite_note-20] The Lincoln Hotel, built in 1920 on South El Dorado Street by Wong Kee and his two brothers, was considered one of Stockton's finest hotels of the time.[^cite_note-21] Only after the [Magnuson Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Exclusion_Repeal_Act) was repealed in 1965 were American-born Chinese allowed to buy property and own buildings.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

#### Incorporation

The city was officially incorporated on July 23, 1850, by the county court, and the first city election was held on July 31, 1850. In 1851 the City of Stockton received its charter from the State of California. Early settlers included gold seekers from Asia, Africa, Australia, Europe, the Pacific Islands, Mexico and Canada. The historical population diversity is reflected in Stockton street names, architecture, numerous ethnic festivals and the faces and heritage of a majority of its citizens. In 1870 the Census Bureau reported Stockton's population as 87.6% white and 10.7% Asian. Many Chinese were immigrating to California as workers in these years, especially for the [Transcontinental Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_Railroad).[^cite_note-census-24]

[Benjamin Holt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Holt) settled in Stockton in 1883 and with his three brothers founded the Stockton Wheel Co., and later the [Holt Manufacturing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holt_Manufacturing_Company).

### 20th century

First [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) temple in the United States, built in Stockton in 1912

On Thanksgiving Day, November 24, 1904, Holt successfully tested the first workable [continuous track tread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_track) machine, plowing soggy [San Joaquin Valley Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_%E2%80%93_San_Joaquin_River_Delta) farmland.[^cite_note-laird-25] Company photographer Charles Clements was reported to have observed that the tractor crawled like a caterpillar, and Holt seized on the metaphor. "Caterpillar it is. That's the name for it."[^cite_note-lea-26]

On April 22, 1918, British Army Col. [Ernest Dunlop Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Dunlop_Swinton) visited Stockton while on a tour of the United States. The British and French armies were using many hundreds of Holt tractors to haul heavy guns and supplies during [World War I](4764461.md), and Swinton publicly thanked Holt and his workforce for their contribution to the war effort.[^cite_note-27] During 1914 and 1915, Swinton had advocated basing some sort of armored fighting vehicle on Holt's caterpillar tractors, but without success (although Britain did develop tanks, they came from a separate source and were not directly derived from Holt machines).[^cite_note-28] After the appearance of tanks on the battlefield, Holt built a prototype, the [gas–electric tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holt_gas%E2%80%93electric_tank), but it did not enter production.

On January 10, 1920, a major fire on Main Street threatened an entire city block. At about 2 a.m., a blaze was discovered in the basement of the Yost-Dohrmann store, which was gutted, and adjacent businesses were damaged by flames and water. Damage was estimated at \$150,000.[^cite_note-29]

By 1931, the [Stockton Electric Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Electric_Railroad) Co. operated 40 [streetcars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcar) over 28 miles (45 km) of track.[^cite_note-hwd-30]

Stockton is the site of the first [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) temple in the United States; [Gurdwara Sahib Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Sahib_Stockton) opened on October 24, 1912. It was founded by Baba Jawala Singh and Baba Wasakha Singh, successful Punjabi immigrants who farmed and owned 500 acres (202 ha) on the Holt River.[^cite_note-31]

In 1933, the [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Stockton) was modernized, and the Stockton Deepwater Channel, which improved water passage to [San Francisco Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay), was deepened and completed. This created commercial opportunities that fueled the city's growth. [Ruff and Ready Island Naval Supply Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruff_and_Ready_Island_Naval_Supply_Depot) was established, placing Stockton in a strategic position during the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-32] During the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) the town's canning industry became the battleground of a labor dispute resulting in the [Spinach Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Cannery_Strike_of_1937) of 1937.[^cite_note-33]



Partial view of the Stockton Assembly Center

During [World War II](32927.md), the Stockton Assembly Center was built on the San Joaquin County Fairgrounds, a few blocks from what was then the city center. One of 15 temporary detention sites run by the [Wartime Civilian Control Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Relocation_Authority), the center held some 4,200 [Japanese-Americans removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans) from their West Coast homes under [Executive Order 9066](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_9066), while they waited for transfer to more permanent and isolated camps in the interior of the country. The center opened on May 10, 1942, and operated until October 17, when the majority of its population was sent to [Rohwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohwer_War_Relocation_Center), Arkansas. The former incarceration site was named a California Historical Landmark in 1980, and in 1984 a marker was erected at the entrance to the fairgrounds.[^cite_note-34]

In 1979, the development of a residential area in Stockton at a burial ground of the tribe unearthed two hundred [Miwok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miwok) remains. In an attempt to prevent the further desecration of the burial grounds, a descendant of the people initiated a legal case which became *[Wana the Bear v. Community Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wana_the_Bear_v._Community_Construction)* (1982). The decision ultimately sided with the development company, which was heavily criticized by Native Americans as a display of [ethnocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocentrism).[^cite_note-\:0-35][^cite_note-\:1-36]

In September 1996, the [Base Realignment and Closure Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure) announced the final closure of Stockton's Naval Reserve Center on Rough and Ready Island. Formerly known as Ruff and Ready Island Naval Supply Depot, the island's facilities had served as a major communications outpost for submarine activities in the Pacific during the Cold War. The site is slowly being redeveloped as commercial property.[^cite_note-37]

### 21st century

In 2012, Stockton filed for what was then the largest municipal bankruptcy in U.S. history – which had multiple causes, including financial mismanagement in the 1990s, generous [fringe benefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_benefits) to unionized city employees,[^cite_note-38] and the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession). Stockton successfully exited bankruptcy in February 2015. It was named an [All-America City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-America_City_Award) in 1999, 2004, 2015, 2017, and 2018.

## Geography

Stockton is situated amidst the [farmland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land) of California's [San Joaquin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley), a subregion of the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Central_Valley). In and around Stockton are thousands of miles of [waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterways) that make up the [California Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Delta).

[Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_California) and [State Route 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_99), inland California's major north–south highways, pass through the city. [State Route 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_4) and the dredged [San Joaquin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_River) connect the city with the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) to its west, creating the [Stockton Deepwater Shipping Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Deepwater_Shipping_Channel). Stockton and [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California) are California's only [inland sea ports](1341936.md).

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 66.387 square miles (171.94 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 63.296 square miles (163.94 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 3.091 square miles (8.01 km<sup>2</sup>) (4.66%) is water.[^cite_note-CenPopGazetteer2025-7]

### Climate



Stockton in relation to the [Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento%E2%80%93San_Joaquin_River_Delta)

Stockton's climate lies right on the boundary of, and fluctuates between, [hot-summer Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) and [cool semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) (*BSk*). Stockton is characterized by very hot, arid summer and cool, wet winter. In an average year, nearly 95% of the 13.45 inches (341.6 mm) of precipitation falls from October through April.[^cite_note-NCEI_Summary_of_Monthly_Normals_-_1991-2020-39] Located in the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_\(California\)), the temperature range is much greater than in the nearby Bay Area. The degree of [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) is roughly twice as high in the summer as in the winter. [Tule fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tule_fog) blankets the area during some winter days. Stockton lies in the fertile heart of the California Mediterranean climate [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta), about equidistant from the Pacific Ocean and the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_\(U.S.\)). The intermediate climate between the coast and the Central Valley gives a similar climate to that of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz), Spain.[^cite_note-40]



Climate chart for Stockton

At the airport, the highest recorded temperature was 115 °F (46 °C) on July 23, 2006, and September 6, 2022, and the lowest was 16 °F (−9 °C) on January 11, 1949. There are an average of 88 afternoons annually with high temperatures of 90 °F (32.2 °C) or higher, and 19 afternoons of 100 °F (37.8 °C) or above; 19 mornings see low temperatures at or below freezing. The wettest "rain year" was from July 1982 to June 1983 with 27.89 in (708 mm) and the driest from July 1975 to June 1976 with 5.71 in (145 mm).[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Sacramento,_CA_(STO)-41] Note that regional difference of precipitation has been recorded in Stockton. The more northerly part of Stockton receives more precipitation than southern Stockton.

The most rainfall in one month was 8.22 in (209 mm) in February 1998 and the most rainfall in 24 hours was 3.01 in (76 mm) on January 21, 1967.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Sacramento,_CA_(STO)-41] There are an average of 56.5 days with measurable precipitation.[^cite_note-NCEI_Summary_of_Monthly_Normals_-_1991-2020-39] Only light amounts of snow have been recorded, and the only instance of measurable snowfall occurred on February 5, 1976, with 0.3 in (8 mm) measured.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Sacramento,_CA_(STO)-41]

A 2018 [federal](195149.md) study predicts that flooding of the [San Joaquin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_River) could possibly cause much of Stockton to become submerged beneath 10–12 feet of water, causing a [humanitarian disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_disaster) as costly and deadly as [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) if the [levees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levees) are not upgraded.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), there were 320,804 people, 97,736 households, and 71,960 families residing in the city.[^cite_note-57] The population density was 5,157.04 inhabitants per square mile (1,991.1/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 101,913 housing units at an average density of 1,638.29 per square mile (632.5/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the city was 23.52% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 12.62% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.73% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 21.99% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.83% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(U.S._Census\)), 25.54% from some other races and 13.76% from two or more races. [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) people of any race were 44.14% of the population.[^cite_note-58]

The census reported that 97.6% of the population lived in households, 1.7% lived in non-institutionalized group quarters, and 0.7% were institutionalized.

There were 97,736 households, out of which 42.4% included children under the age of 18, 43.5% were married-couple households, 8.4% were [cohabiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohabitation) couple households, 29.7% had a female householder with no partner present, and 18.4% had a male householder with no partner present. 20.6% of households were one person, and 8.9% were one person aged 65 or older. The average household size was 3.2. There were 71,960 [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_\(U.S._Census\)) (73.6% of all households).

The age distribution was 27.4% under the age of 18, 10.4% aged 18 to 24, 26.9% aged 25 to 44, 22.2% aged 45 to 64, and 13.1% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 33.5 years. For every 100 females, there were 96.3 males.

There were 101,913 housing units at an average density of 1,638.29 units per square mile (632.55 units/km<sup>2</sup>), of which 97,736 (95.9%) were occupied. Of these, 50.9% were owner-occupied, and 49.1% were occupied by renters.

In 2023, the US Census Bureau estimated that the median household income was \$76,851, and the per capita income was \$31,279. About 12.4% of families and 15.4% of the population were below the poverty line.[^cite_note-59]

Historically an agricultural community, Stockton's economy has since diversified into other industries, which include telecommunications and manufacturing.

Stockton's central location, relative to San Francisco and [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento), its proximity to the state and interstate freeway system, and its comparatively inexpensive land costs have prompted several companies to base their regional operations in the city.

### Shopping

The city of Stockton has one shopping mall, [Mershops Weberstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mershops_Weberstown). The city previously housed the [Sherwood Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_Mall), adjacent to Weberstown, but in 2022, it was converted into a shopping center now named Sherwood Place. It has the only Dillard's in the Northern California region at Mershops Weberstown.

### Real estate bubble



Stockton 2012 aerial view

The Stockton real estate market was disproportionately affected by the [2007 subprime mortgage financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_subprime_mortgage_financial_crisis), and the city led the United States in [foreclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure) for that year, with one of every 30 homes posted for foreclosure.[^cite_note-Christie-62] From September 2006 to September 2007, the value of a median-priced house in Stockton declined by 44%.[^cite_note-money.cnn.com-63]

Stockton's [Weston Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston_Ranch) neighborhood, a subdivision of modest tract homes built in the mid-1990s, had the worst foreclosure rate in the area according to [ACORN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Community_Organizations_for_Reform_Now), a now-defunct national advocacy group for low and moderate-income families.[^cite_note-64] Stockton found itself squarely at the center of the 2000s' speculative [housing bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_bubble). Real estate in Stockton more than tripled in value between 1998 and 2005, but when the bubble burst in 2007, the ensuing financial crisis made Stockton one of the hardest-hit cities in United States.[^cite_note-America's_Most_Dangerous_Cities-65]

Stockton housing prices fell 39% in the 2008 fiscal year, and the city had the country's highest [foreclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure) rate (9.5%) as well. Stockton also had an unemployment rate of 13.3% in 2008, one of the highest in the United States. Stockton was rated by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* in 2009 as America's fifth most dangerous city because of its [crime rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_rate).[^cite_note-America's_Most_Dangerous_Cities-65] In 2010, mainly due to the aforementioned factors, *Forbes* named it one of the three worst places to live in the United States.[^cite_note-66]

### City bankruptcy

Following the 2008 financial crisis, in June 2012 Stockton became the largest city in U.S. history to file for bankruptcy protection. It was surpassed by [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_bankruptcy) in July 2013. The city approved a plan to exit bankruptcy in October 2013,[^cite_note-Smith-67] and voters approved a sales tax on November 5, 2013, to help fund the exit.[^cite_note-Marcum-68]

The collapse in real estate valuations had a negative effect on the city's revenue base. On June 28, 2012, Stockton filed for [Chapter 9 bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_9,_Title_11,_United_States_Code).[^cite_note-69] On April 1, 2013, the [United States Bankruptcy Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bankruptcy_Court) Eastern District of California ruled that Stockton was eligible for bankruptcy protection.

The Stockton bankruptcy case lasted longer than two years and received nationwide attention. On October 4, 2013, Stockton City Council approved a bankruptcy exit plan by a 6–0 vote[^cite_note-Smith-67] to be filed with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of California, Sacramento. Voters approved a 3⁄4-cent sales tax on November 5, 2013, to help fund the bankruptcy exit.[^cite_note-Marcum-68]

On October 30, 2014, a [federal bankruptcy judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_bankruptcy_judge) approved the city's bankruptcy recovery plan, thus allowing the city to continue with the planned pension payments to retired workers.[^cite_note-70] The city exited from Chapter 9 bankruptcy on February 25, 2015.

### Experiment in Guaranteed Basic Income

As part of a privately funded experiment in [Universal Basic Income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Income) in 2019, the Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration (S.E.E.D.) conducted a pilot project that gave a \$500 stipend to 125 randomly selected residents for an 24-month period with "no strings attached."[^cite_note-71] It was made possible by the Economic Security Project,[^cite_note-72] an advocacy group chaired by Facebook co-founder [Chris Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hughes), which provided the first \$1 million for the program, and a dozen other Silicon Valley organizations and private donors who funded the rest of its \$3 million budget.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] The positive benefits of the program during the first year were described in an interim report published in March 2021.[^cite_note-75]

## Arts and culture

### Performing arts



The Fox California Theater

#### Music

- [Stockton Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Symphony) is the third-oldest professional orchestra in California (founded in 1926), after the [San Francisco Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Symphony) and the [Los Angeles Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Philharmonic).

  [^cite_note-77]

- [University of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(United_States\)) is known for its music conservatory and for being the home of the Brubeck Institute, named after [Dave Brubeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Brubeck), a [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(United_States\)) alumnus and jazz piano legend. The institute maintains an archive of Brubeck's work and offers a fellowship program for young musicians. The Brubeck Institute Jazz Quartet is composed of Pacific students and tours widely.

  [^cite_note-78]

- [San Joaquin Delta College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Delta_College) has a growing jazz program and is home to several official and unofficial jazz bands composed of [Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Delta_College) and Pacific students and faculty.

  [^cite_note-79]

- [Christian Life College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Life_College) offers associate and Bachelor of Arts degrees in Christian music.

Stockton hosts several live-music venues, including:

- [Stockton Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Arena), which is home to several sports teams, and has hosted nationally known entertainers such as [Gwen Stefani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Stefani), [Rob Zombie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Zombie), [Ozzy Osbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozzy_Osbourne), [Josh Groban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Groban), [Carrie Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood) and [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan).

- The annual Apollo Night talent show draws about 1,500 people to the [Stockton Memorial Civic Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Memorial_Civic_Auditorium) (1925) to watch performances by aspiring Northern California musicians.

#### Theatre

The Bob Hope Theatre in downtown Stockton, formerly known as the Fox California Theatre, built in 1930,[^cite_note-81] is one of several [movie palaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_palace) in the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_\(California\)). [Bob Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope) often came to Stockton to visit close friend and billionaire [tycoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycoon) [Alex Spanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Spanos), who donated much of the money to revitalize the theater after Hope's death. The [University of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(United_States\)) Faye Spanos Concert Hall often hosts public performances, as does the [Stockton Memorial Civic Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Memorial_Civic_Auditorium). The Warren Atherton Auditorium at the Delta Center for the Arts on the campus of the [San Joaquin Delta College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Delta_College) is a 1,456-seat theater with a 60-foot (18 m) proscenium and full grid system.[^cite_note-82] The Stockton Empire Theater is an [art deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) movie theater that has been revitalized as a venue for live music.

Founded in 1951, the Stockton Civic Theatre offers an annual series of musicals, comedies and dramas. It maintains a 300-seat theater in the Venetian Bridges neighborhood. The company also hosts the annual Willie awards for the local performing arts.

Other performing arts organizations and venues include the Stockton Opera[^cite_note-83] and others.

### Visual arts

#### Museums and galleries

Stockton is home to several museums:

- [Haggin Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haggin_Museum) — the private, non-profit fine arts and history museum was built in Victory Park in 1931. The museum displays 19th and 20th-century works of art and houses local historical exhibits. The Haggin Museum features collections and exhibits related to local Valley history and California history. The museum also displays fine art of late 19th and early 20th century artists such as [Jean Béraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_B%C3%A9raud), [Albert Bierstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bierstadt), [Rosa Bonheur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Bonheur), [William-Adolphe Bouguereau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William-Adolphe_Bouguereau), [Paul Gauguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gauguin), [Jean-Léon Gérôme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me), [Childe Hassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childe_Hassam), George Inness, Daniel Ridgway Knight, [Pierre-Auguste Renoir](24546.md), Jehan-Georges Vibert, and Jules Worms.

- The [San Joaquin County Historical Society and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_County_Historical_Society_and_Museum) operates an 18-acre (7.3 ha) museum facility at [Micke Grove Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micke_Grove_Regional_Park), two miles (3.2 km) north of the city. The museum houses exhibits dedicated to the founding of Stockton, San Joaquin County's legacy of innovation in agriculture and manufacturing, immigrant communities in Stockton and Lodi, and historic industries in San Joaquin County.

## Sports



[Adventist Health Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventist_Health_Arena), home of the [Stockton Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Kings)

The Stockton Ports Baseball Team play their home games at [Banner Island Ballpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banner_Island_Ballpark), a 5,000-seat facility built for the team in downtown Stockton. The Ports played their home games at Billy Hebert Field from 1953 to 2004. The Ports have been a single A team in Stockton since 1946 in the California Minor Leagues. Stockton has minor league baseball dating back to 1886.[^cite_note-96] The Ports have produced 244 Major League players including Gary Sheffield, Dan Plesac, Doug Jones, Pat Listach, and Stockton's own Dallas Braden among others.[^cite_note-97] The Ports have eleven championships and are currently the A class team for the Athletics. The Ports had the best win–loss percentage in all Minor League Baseball in the 1980s.[^cite_note-98]

A 10,000-seat arena, [Stockton Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Arena), located in Downtown Stockton, opened in December 2005 and is home to the [Stockton Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Kings) ([NBAGL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_G_League))

Stockton is home to the oldest NASCAR-certified race track West of the Mississippi. The Stockton 99 Speedway opened in 1947 and is a quarter-mile oval paved track with grandstands that can accommodate 5,000 spectators.

Stockton's designation for Little League Baseball is District 8, which has 12 leagues of teams within the city. Stockton also has several softball leagues including Stockton Girls Softball Association, and Port City Softball League, each having several hundred members.

Rowing Regatta featuring Junior, Collegiate and Master Level Rowing & Sculling Competition is organized by the [University of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(United_States\))[^cite_note-99] annually on the Stockton's Deep Water Channel. Teams from throughout Northern California compete in this Olympic sport which is also the oldest collegiate sport in the United States.

Stockton hosts a wide variety of sports events every year: from resident hockey, baseball and soccer games through basketball at the University of the Pacific and at the Stockton Arena; golf championships at two 18-hole courses and a Par 3 Executive Course; rowing, sailing and fishing on the Delta and the Stockton Channel; martial arts and cage fighting. There are four public golf courses open year-round, Van Buskirk, Swenson, and The Reserve at Spanos Park and Elkhorn Golf Course. Private courses include The Stockton Golf & Country Club, Oakmoore, and Brookside Golf & Country Club.

Stockton is one of a handful of cities that lays claim to being the inspiration for the 1888 poem "[Casey at the Bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_at_the_Bat)."[^cite_note-100] The [University of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(United_States\)) was the summer home of the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) Summer Training Camp from 1998 through 2002.

Stockton is also the base of [UFC](169660.md) fighters [Nick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Diaz) and [Nate Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Diaz). Nick is the former WEC and Strikeforce Welterweight champion,[^cite_note-101] while Nate is the winner of [The Ultimate Fighter 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ultimate_Fighter_5).[^cite_note-102] Both brothers are Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belts under [Cesar Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Gracie)[^cite_note-103] and operate a school in Stockton which teaches Brazilian jiu-jitsu to children and youth.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

## Parks and recreation



Pixie Woods

The City of Stockton has a small children's amusement park, Pixie Woods; the park opened in 1954 and has since welcomed more than one million visitors.[^cite_note-106]

Stockton also has the Children's Museum of Stockton, a children's museum located at 402 W. Weber Avenue with more than 40 exhibits.[^cite_note-107] The museum was inspired as a result of the [1989 Cleveland School Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_schoolyard_shooting) in Stockton, as the founding director was a teacher wounded in the school shooting.[^cite_note-108] The museum opened in March 1994, thanks to the help of the community of Stockton and the county of [San Joaquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_County,_California).

It also offers a large number of degree programs relative to its student population.[^cite_note-113] The men's Pacific Tigers basketball team has been in the NCAA Tournament nine times. The Tigers have played their home games at the Alex G. Spanos Center since 1982, prior to that playing at the Stockton Memorial Civic Auditorium since 1952. The campus has been used in the filming of a number of Hollywood films (see [below](#Popular_culture)), partly due to its likeness to East Coast [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) universities.[^cite_note-114]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Stockton is centrally located with access to:

- [Port of Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Stockton) — an international deep-water port

- [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) railroad system

- Intrastate and Interstate freeway systems

- [Stockton Metropolitan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Metropolitan_Airport)

#### Roads and railways



The [San Joaquin Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Street_station) [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) station in Stockton

Due to its location at the "crossroads" of the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Central_Valley) and a relatively extensive highway system, Stockton is easily accessible from virtually anywhere in California. [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_\(California\)) and [State Route 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_99), California's major north–south thoroughfares, pass through the city limits. The east–west highway [State Route 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_4) also passes through the city, providing access to the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) as well as the Sierra Nevada and its foothills. Stockton is the western terminus of [State Route 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_26) and [State Route 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_88), which extends to the Nevada border. In addition, Stockton is within an hour of [Interstate 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_\(California\)), [Interstate 205](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_205_\(California\)) and [Interstate 580](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_580_\(California\)).

Stockton is served by [San Joaquin Regional Transit District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Regional_Transit_District).[^cite_note-136]

Stockton is also connected to the rest of the nation through a network of railways. Stockton has two passenger rail stations. [Robert J. Cabral Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Cabral_Station), which provides service to Sacramento on [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)'s *[Gold Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Runner)* route, and also serves as the northern terminus of the [Altamont Corridor Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altamont_Corridor_Express) [commuter rail](6286.md) service to San Jose. [San Joaquin Street station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Street_station) provides service to Oakland via the *Gold Runner* route.

[Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific) and [BNSF Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF_Railway), the two largest railroad networks in North America both service Stockton and its port via connections with the [Stockton Terminal and Eastern Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Terminal_and_Eastern_Railroad) and [Central California Traction Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_California_Traction_Company), who provide local and interconnecting services between the various rail lines. The [Stockton Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Diamond) was the busiest interchange point in the state by 2020;[^cite_note-137] a [grade separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_separation) project to elevate the Union Pacific over the BNSF line is planned to be completed by 2026.

#### Air



Passenger terminal of [Stockton Metropolitan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Metropolitan_Airport)

Stockton is served by [Stockton Metropolitan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_Metropolitan_Airport), located on [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_County) land just south of city limits. The airport has been designated a [Foreign Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_trade_zones_of_the_United_States) and is mainly used by manufacturing and agricultural companies for shipping purposes. Since [airline deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_deregulation), passenger service has come and gone several times. Domestic service resumed on June 16, 2006, with service to [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley) by [Allegiant Air](1372018.md).[^cite_note-sjgov.org-138] The days of service and number of flights were expanded a few months later due to demand. Air service to [Phoenix](49121.md) began in September 2007.

On July 1, 2010, [Allegiant Air](1372018.md) implemented non-stop service to and from [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_California).[^cite_note-sjgov.org-138] In 2006 [Aeromexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeromexico) had plans to provide flights to and from [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara), Mexico, but the airport's plan to build a customs station at the airport was initially rejected by the customs service. However, the possibility of building this station is currently a continuing matter of negotiation between the airport and the customs service, and Aeromexico has indicated a continuing interest in eventually providing service. Ground transportation is available from Hertz, Enterprise, Yellow Cab and Aurora Limousine.

#### Seaport

The [Port of Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Stockton) is a fully operating seaport approximately 75 nautical miles (86 mi; 139 km) east of the Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco. Set on the [San Joaquin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_River), the port operates a 4,200-acre (17 km<sup>2</sup>)[^cite_note-Caltrans_Port_of_Stockton_Fact_Sheet-139] transportation center with berthing space for 17 [vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) up to 900 feet (270 m) in length.[^cite_note-140] As of 2014, the Port of Stockton had 136 tenants[^cite_note-141] and is served by BNSF & UP Railroads.[^cite_note-142] The port also includes 1.1 million square feet (100,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of dockside transit sheds and shipside rail track and 7.7 million square feet (720,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of warehousing.[^cite_note-143]

Adjacent to the port is [Rough and Ready Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruff_and_Ready_Island_Naval_Supply_Depot), which served as a [World War II](32927.md)–era naval supply base until it was decommissioned during the [Base Realignment and Closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure) process in 1995.

### Police department



Stockton Police Department seal

Cleveland Elementary School shooting

Main article: [Cleveland Elementary School shooting (Stockton)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Elementary_School_shooting_\(Stockton\))

On January 17, 1989, the Stockton Police Department received a threat against Cleveland Elementary School from an unknown person. Later that day, Patrick Purdy, who was later found to be mentally ill, opened fire on the school's playground with a semi-automatic rifle, killing five children, all Cambodian or Vietnamese refugees, and wounding 29 others, and a teacher, before taking his own life. The [Cleveland Elementary School shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Elementary_School_shooting_\(Stockton\)) received national news coverage and is sometimes referred to as the Cleveland School massacre.[^cite_note-144]

#### Budget crisis

The city cut its police force by more than 20% during the [2008 subprime mortgage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprime_mortgage_crisis), but voters approved a sales tax on November 5, 2013, that provided funds to hire an additional 120 police officers.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

#### Bank robbery

Main article: [2014 Stockton bank robbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Stockton_bank_robbery)

On July 16, 2014, officers responded to an armed bank robbery, which resulted in the four perpetrators taking three hostages and leading them on an hour-long high-speed pursuit. Over the course of the car chase, one suspect fired over 100 rounds from an [AK-47s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) at police, disabling 14 police vehicles, including the department's own [Lenco BearCat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenco_BearCat) armored personnel carrier. More than 30 officers shot over 600 rounds into the getaway vehicle. Two perpetrators were killed, two hostages were injured, one hostage was killed by police, and numerous vehicles and other property were damaged or destroyed by the nearly 1,000 rounds of ammunition fired by the robbers and police.[^cite_note-147] The department faced criticism with its handling of the incident in the aftermath.[^cite_note-148]

#### Crime



[Amtrak Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Police) cars at the Stockton – San Joaquin Street Station in Stockton, 2012

Stockton Police Chief Eric Jones credited 2013's drop in the murder rate to [Operation Ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ceasefire), a gun violence intervention strategy pioneered in Boston and implemented in Stockton in 2012,[^cite_note-149] combined with a federal gun and narcotics operation.[^cite_note-150]

### Fire department

The Stockton Fire Department was first rated as a Class 1 fire department by the [Insurance Services Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_Services_Office) in 1971. In 2005, all 13 of the city's stations met the [National Fire Protection Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fire_Protection_Association) standard of a 5-minute response time.[^cite_note-151] In 2009, it had 13 fire stations and over 275 career personnel.[^cite_note-152] Due in part to staffing levels that placed five staff on ladder companies and four staff on engines, it was one of only 57 departments among 44,000 to receive the Class 1 rating in 2010.[^cite_note-153]

The department maintained this rating until 2011, when during the city's Chapter 9 bankruptcy proceedings and following a Civil Grand Jury investigation, the city reduced staffing levels from 220 full-time staff to 177, and the 2011 budget from \$59 million to \$40 million. The department was cut by 30%.[^cite_note-154] The bankruptcy was due in part to a 1996 decision made by the city to provide firefighters with free health care after retirement, which they later expanded to all city employees. The benefit gradually grew into a \$417 million liability.[^cite_note-155]

As of 2016, the department consists of 12 firehouses that house 12 Engine Companies and three Truck Companies. In 2015 the Fire Department responded to over 40,000 emergency calls for service, including more than 300 working structure fires. The department is one of the busiest in the United States. The Stockton Fire Department is assisted on medical emergency calls by American Medical Response.[^cite_note-156]
