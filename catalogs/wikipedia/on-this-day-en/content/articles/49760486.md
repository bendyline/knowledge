[Protests](266137.md) against [Donald Trump](4848272.md) have occurred in the United States and internationally, mostly after [his entry into the 2016 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2016_presidential_campaign). Protests have expressed opposition to Trump's [campaign rhetoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetoric_of_Donald_Trump), [his ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism), his electoral win, [his first inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump), [his alleged history of sexual misconduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_sexual_misconduct_allegations), including a [2023 verdict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Jean_Carroll_v._Donald_J._Trump) in which he was held liable for [sexual abuse](7397019.md), and various presidential actions, including [his travel ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13769) in 2017 and aggressive [family separation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_separation_policy) in 2018.[^cite_note-\:29-13][^cite_note-\:30-14]

Some protests have taken the form of [walk-outs](2016860.md), business closures, and petitions as well as rallies, demonstrations, and marches. While most protests have been peaceful,[^cite_note-15] actionable conduct such as [vandalism](32676.md) and [assaults](1466.md) on Trump supporters has occurred.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Some protesters have been criminally charged with [rioting](146704.md).[^cite_note-\:1-18]

One of the largest organized protests against Trump was the day after his first inauguration; millions protested on January 21, 2017, during the [Women's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Women's_March), with each individual city's protest taken into consideration, making it what was then the largest single-day protest in the history of the United States.[^cite_note-\:10-19]

&#x20;Following Trump's victory in the [2024 presidential election](59568546.md), a new wave of protests was held in reaction to his [second presidency](78298108.md).[^cite_note-\:28-20] The [50501 movement](79143789.md) in coordination with [Indivisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_movement), the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) and several other organizations staged a series of protests known as the [Hands Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hands_Off_protests) and [No Kings protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50501_protests). In 2026, large [Hands off Greenland protests](82139265.md) against Trump took place in several countries in response to [Trump's desire to annex Greenland.](82044223.md)[^cite_note-21] According to data posted by the Crowd Counting Consortium at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), protests against Trump that were reported in official sources were 4 times as common during the first year of his second term compared to the first year of his first term.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## 2016 presidential campaign

Further information: [Timeline of protests against Donald Trump § 2016 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_protests_against_Donald_Trump#2016_presidential_campaign)

A number of protests against Trump's candidacy and [political positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_positions_of_Donald_Trump) occurred during his initial [presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_presidential_campaign,_2016), essentially at his [political rallies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rallies_for_the_2016_Donald_Trump_presidential_campaign).

### Political rallies

During his presidential campaign, activists organized demonstrations inside Trump's rallies, sometimes with calls to shut the rallies down;[^cite_note-CassidyProtestMovement-24][^cite_note-ShutItDown-25][^cite_note-26] protesters began to attend his rallies displaying signs and disrupting proceedings.[^cite_note-WP031216b-27][^cite_note-WSJEditorial-28] There were occasional incidents of verbal abuse and/or physical violence, either against protesters or against Trump supporters. While most of the incidents amounted to simple [heckling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler) against the candidate, a few people had to be stopped by [Secret Service](58221.md) agents. Large-scale disruption forced Trump to [cancel a rally in Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Donald_Trump_Chicago_rally_protest) on March 11, 2016, out of safety concerns.[^cite_note-ShutItDown-25] On June 18, 2016, [an attempt was made to assassinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Donald_Trump_Las_Vegas_rally_incident) Trump.[^cite_note-AP13September2016-29] Michael Steven Sanford, a British national and the perpetrator, was sentenced to one year in prison after he reached for a police officer's gun. He reportedly told a federal agent that he had driven from California to Las Vegas with a plan to kill Trump.[^cite_note-AP13December2016-30]

The protesters sometimes attempted to enter the venue or engage in activities outside the venue. Interactions with supporters of the candidate may occur before, during or after the event.[^cite_note-CHICAGOnumbers-2] At times, protesters attempted to rush the stage at Trump's rallies.[^cite_note-31] At times, anti-Trump protesters have turned violent and attacked Trump supporters and vice versa;[^cite_note-32] this violence has received [bipartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisanship) condemnation.[^cite_note-33] [MoveOn.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoveOn.org), [The People for Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_for_Bernie_Sanders), the [Muslim Students' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Students'_Association), [Assata's Daughters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assata's_Daughters), the Black Student Union, Fearless Undocumented Alliance and [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) were among the organizations who sponsored or promoted [the protests at the March 11 Chicago Trump rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Donald_Trump_Chicago_rally_protest).[^cite_note-CassidyProtestMovement-24][^cite_note-Participan-34][^cite_note-MoveOnConfirms-35][^cite_note-DisruptionsPlanned-36] There were reports of verbal and physical confrontations between Trump supporters and protesters at Trump's campaign events.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

### Hoaxes about protesters

Following a [June 2016 clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_protests_against_Donald_Trump#After_Trump_won_the_primaries) between protesters and Trump supporters in San Jose, California, a photo of Australian actress [Samara Weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_Weaving) appearing to be injured was widely circulated on social media.[^cite_note-Logan_N.-39][^cite_note-Stiles_J.-40][^cite_note-Silverman_C.-41][^cite_note-Norton_S.-42] The photo claimed to depict a Trump supporter attacked by [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States) protesters, but was actually Weaving in makeup for her role on the comedy-horror series *[Ash vs Evil Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_vs_Evil_Dead)*.[^cite_note-Logan_N.-39][^cite_note-Silverman_C.-41][^cite_note-Actor_Bruce_Campbell-43][^cite_note-Ash_vs_Evil_Dead_Actor-44] Weaving reacted negatively to the hoax, noting that she could not vote in the presidential election because she was not a U.S. citizen.[^cite_note-Logan_N.-39][^cite_note-Stiles_J.-40]

A similar hoax claiming to show a 15-year-old Trump supporter beaten by an anti-Trump mob in San Jose used an image of the actress Luisa Rubino from the telenovela *[La Rosa de Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rosa_de_Guadalupe)*.[^cite_note-Logan_N.-39][^cite_note-Rogers_T.-45][^cite_note-Teenager_Beaten-46] Rubino told an interviewer that in fact she did not support Trump "because I'm Mexican and I support the Latino community".[^cite_note-Logan_N.-39][^cite_note-Rogers_T.-45]

The [fact checking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact_checking) website [PolitiFact.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolitiFact.com) rated a separate story titled "Donald Trump Protester Speaks Out: 'I Was Paid \$3,500 To Protest Trump's Rally'" as "100 percent fabricated, as its author acknowledges".[^cite_note-47] [Paul Horner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Horner), a writer for a [fake news website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news_website), took credit for the article, and said he posted the deceitful ad himself.[^cite_note-caitlindewey-48]

### Trump's reactions

During the campaign, Trump was accused by some of creating aggressive undertones at his rallies.[^cite_note-Parker-49] Trump's Republican rivals blamed him for fostering a climate of violence, and escalating tension during events.[^cite_note-50] Initially, Trump did not condemn the acts of violence that occurred at many of his rallies, and indeed encouraged them in some cases.[^cite_note-Moyer-51][^cite_note-West-52]

In November 2015, Trump said of a protester in [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama), "Maybe he should have been roughed up, because it was absolutely disgusting what he was doing."[^cite_note-53] In December, the campaign urged attendees not to harm protesters, but rather to alert law enforcement officers of them by holding signs above their head and yelling, "Trump! Trump! Trump!"[^cite_note-54] Trump has been criticized for additional instances of fomenting an atmosphere conducive to violence through many of his comments. For example, Trump told a crowd in [Cedar Rapids, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Rapids,_Iowa), that he would pay their legal fees if they punched a protester.[^cite_note-55]

On February 23, 2016, when a protester was ejected from a rally in [Las Vegas](47737.md), Trump stated, "I love the old days – you know what they used to do to guys like that when they were in a place like this? They'd be carried out on a stretcher, folks", before adding, "I'd like to punch him in the face."[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### Security



Trump flanked by Secret Service agents before the election

Fairly early in the campaign the [United States Secret Service](58221.md) assumed primary responsibility for Trump's security. They were augmented by state and local law enforcement as needed. When a venue was rented by the campaign, the rally was a private event and the campaign might grant or deny entry to it with no reason given; the only stipulation was that exclusion solely on the basis of race was forbidden. Those who entered or remained inside such a venue without permission were technically guilty of or liable for [trespass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trespass_to_land).[^cite_note-NYT031316-59] Attendees or the press could be assigned or restricted to particular areas in the venue.[^cite_note-politico\:220407-60]

In March 2016, [Politico](9172097.md) reported that the Trump campaign hired plainclothes private security guards to preemptively remove potential protesters from rallies.[^cite_note-politico\:220407-60] That same month, a group calling itself the "Lion Guard" was formed to offer "additional security" at Trump rallies. The group was quickly condemned by mainstream political activists as a paramilitary fringe organization.

### Domestic



March against Trump in Saint Paul, Minnesota, on November 9, 2016

Following Trump's [election](1169845.md#Politics) to the presidency, students and other activists organized [larger protests in several major cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_protests_against_Donald_Trump#Post-election) across the United States, including [New York City](645042.md), [Boston](24437894.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Chicago](6886.md), [Portland](23503.md) and [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland). Tens of thousands of protesters participated,[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] with many chanting "Not my president!" to express their opposition to Trump's victory in the Electoral College (He lost the popular vote by a margin of 2.1 percent).[^cite_note-64] Protests were also held in Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, the Philippines, Australia and Israel with some continuing for several days, and more protests planned for the following weeks and months.[^cite_note-Guardian121116-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-\:5-67]

In November 2017, it was reported that as part of [Russian interference in the 2016 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2016_United_States_elections), the New York protest was actually organized by a "[troll farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troll_farm)", dedicated to fomenting discord in the U.S.[^cite_note-68] Thousands of Facebook users indicated they planned to attend a Trump protest on November 12, 2016, that was organized on a Facebook page for BlackMattersUS, a Russian-linked group, with ties to the [Kremlin](9787373.md). The organized rally took advantage of outrage among groups on the left following Trump's electoral college victory. An estimated 5,000 to 10,000 protesters convened at Manhattan's Union Square and then marched to Trump Tower on 5th Avenue.[^cite_note-69] CNN supported and provided coverage of the event. Protesters have held up a number of different signs and chanted various shouts including "Not my president" and "We don't accept the president-elect".[^cite_note-\:5-67][^cite_note-\:2-70] The movement organized on [Twitter](9988187.md) under the hashtags #Anti-Trump and #NotMyPresident.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-Reuters_2nd-72] Protesters after the election decided to demonstrate to show support for minorities, immigrants and other marginalized people in the United States.[^cite_note-\:18-73] Protesting also helped put a spotlight on the issues that were important to the demonstrators.[^cite_note-\:18-73] Some protesters had been part of other movements, such as [Occupy Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupy_Wall_Street), [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) and [Moral Mondays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_Mondays), but many people protesting Trump were new to demonstrating.[^cite_note-\:18-73]

After he won the election, the security around Trump and his family became noticeably more stringent. Sources reported that there were concerns about the ability to secure Trump's Manhattan residence due to its location and the large number of people who live there, as well as the number of people coming and going. Restrictions on private and commercial air traffic were imposed on airspace over Manhattan and other parts of the city until Inauguration Day.[^cite_note-NYPD,_Secret_Service_Upping_Security_at_Trump_Tower_for_President-Elect_Donald_Trump-74] Feminist icon [Camille Paglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Paglia) complained that [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer) "asserted absolutely no moral authority as the party spun out of control in a nationwide orgy of rage and spite" in the days after the election. Of the Democratic Party response to Trump's election, she called it "an abject failure of leadership that may be one of the most disgraceful episodes in the history of the modern Democratic party".[^cite_note-75]

## First presidency

Further information: [Timeline of protests against Donald Trump § First presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_protests_against_Donald_Trump#First_presidency)

*[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported in January 2018 that since Trump was inaugurated there was protests every day somewhere in the United States.[^cite_note-\:26-78] Protesters demonstrating after [Trump's first inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) have sought to "bring unprecedented disruption to his life as president", with protests following where Trump travels.[^cite_note-\:8-79] Protesters have sought to interrupt "people's business as usual" in order to force others to think about the impact of Trump's policies on the country, according to activist [Cat Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Brooks) in San Francisco.[^cite_note-\:4-80] A [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) professor at [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine), David Meyer, said that while it is not unusual to have protests after a new president, "What is unusual is the vigor, speed, size, and number of issues that they are challenging Trump on. To have a sustained \[protest], every weekend, every couple of days, and it's a different issue – I've never seen anything like this before."[^cite_note-81] Michael Heaney, an author and [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) professor, said in February 2017, that the protests were nowhere near the saturation rate and added, "If anything, it's just getting started."[^cite_note-\:20-82] The "first protest in space" was taken as a demonstration against Trump by the Autonomous Space Agency Network (ASAN) on April 12, 2017.[^cite_note-83]

Some protests have been "highly coordinated" by grassroots organizers, like the resistance campaign that was increasing in [California](5407.md).[^cite_note-\:4-80] Many of the protests have not only occurred in large, mostly Democratic cities, but have also taken place in smaller cities across the country.[^cite_note-\:7-84] In some of these cities, such as [Mason City, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_City,_Iowa), a majority voted for Trump.[^cite_note-\:7-84] Protests have also occurred worldwide, with international citizens objecting to the Trump administration.[^cite_note-\:9-85] Many of the protests have been organized via social media.[^cite_note-\:14-86] Many protesters have been calling the anti-Trump movement "[the resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_resistance)."[^cite_note-\:14-86] Women are leading many of the organization and volunteer efforts.[^cite_note-87] In addition, many participants have been first-time protesters.[^cite_note-\:20-82][^cite_note-pat-88] Protesters have become involved with organizing groups at a local level, such as [Indivisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_movement) and SwingLeft.[^cite_note-89] For many, their involvement in protest has become a part of their lives.[^cite_note-\:25-90] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported in January 2018 that the "overwhelming majority" of protests have been non-violent.[^cite_note-\:26-78]

Protesters have expressed concerns about the potential loss of rights for Muslims affected by [Executive Order 13769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13769), or the travel ban/Muslim ban/Muslim travel ban, and for a loss of [rights for LGBT people](3510770.md).[^cite_note-\:9-85] Other protesters have been against the proposed border wall between the United States and Mexico.[^cite_note-91] Musicians such as [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry), [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna), [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen), [Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez) and [Tom Morello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Morello), have voiced support for the protests.[^cite_note-92] Reasons for people protesting are diverse and many protesters care about multiple issues.[^cite_note-\:25-90] The number of people protesting against only Trump has diminished over time as many people are turning their attention to Congressional members.[^cite_note-93]

"White House Responds to Voter Outrage at Town Halls Across US" video report from [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America)

Protests are taking place at [town hall meetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_hall_meeting), where constituents are urging their senators and representatives to oppose some of the policies of the Trump administration or to investigate possible Trump ties to Russia.[^cite_note-94] Some GOP politicians have welcomed the protests, while others have avoided having town halls during the first [Congressional recess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recess_\(break\)) in 2017.[^cite_note-95] Representative [Leonard Lance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Lance) said he had never faced such a large town hall before after attending a recent one in February 2017.[^cite_note-\:20-82] Conservatives, like former [Milwaukee County Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_County_Sheriff) [David A. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Clarke_Jr.), have dismissed the protesters as "petulant children stomping their feet with these child-like nursery rhyme slogans."[^cite_note-96]

### Official reaction

Both Trump and his administration addressed the protests either officially or via social media. Trump's reaction to the Women's March via Twitter in January 2017 were contradictory with one tweet dismissing protesters and a later tweet praising protest as "a hallmark of our democracy."[^cite_note-97] Trump was also dismissive of a rally hosted by [Democrats](5043544.md) outside of the [Supreme Court](31737.md) against [Executive Order 13769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13769). Trump tweeted, "[Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi) and Fake Tears Chuck Schumer held a rally at the steps of the Supreme Court and mic did not work (a mess)-just like Dem party!"[^cite_note-98] On February 21, 2017, Trump tweeted that town hall protests were "planned out by liberal activists. Sad!"[^cite_note-99] [Sean Spicer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Spicer), on February 22, 2017, blamed recent town hall protests on "professional protesters."[^cite_note-100] This reaction is similar to the one from the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Barack_Obama) towards [Tea Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement) [protests in 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_against_Barack_Obama).[^cite_note-101]

After the [United Talent Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Talent_Agency) (UTA) hosted an anti-Trump rally called [United Voices](#United_Voices_Rally), instead of their normal Oscars party,[^cite_note-102] Trump went on Twitter and urged "the millions of people who voted to MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN" to have their own rally.[^cite_note-\:19-103] Trump supporters have attended rallies at official events, but none have created events in sizes "comparable in scale to those of his opponents."[^cite_note-\:19-103]

On February 28, 2017, Trump was interviewed on *[Fox & Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_&_Friends)* where he blamed former president Obama both for the protests and for the leaks in his administration. He claimed that Obama was involved in protest organization behind the scenes.[^cite_note-104] Trump claimed that Obama was behind the protests "because his people certainly are behind it."[^cite_note-105] There is no evidence linking a nonprofit group that advocates for similar positions to Obama, [Organizing for Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizing_for_Action), and the former president.[^cite_note-106]

### Rogue Twitter accounts

Following the election of Trump as the 45th president of the United States, a Resist movement began for those in opposition to his presidency.[^cite_note-Forbes_2017-107] Dozens of accounts purporting to be government agencies and departments took to Twitter to anonymously voice their opposition using accounts that are typically prefixed with either Rogue or Alt;[^cite_note-108] none of these accounts have come forward and identified themselves.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] Alice Stollmeyer, a digital advocacy strategist from Brussels with a background in science and communications, dubbed this Twitter resistance movement "#twistance".[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] It is thought that the staff of [Badlands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badlands_National_Park) were the first to create a "rogue account",[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] which was created in opposition to Trump's assertion that [climate change](5042951.md) was not real.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

### Presidential inauguration

Further information: [First inauguration of Donald Trump § Protests and demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump#Protests_and_demonstrations)



Human chain (many dressed in purple) on the Golden Gate Bridge to peacefully oppose the [inauguration of Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump)

Protesters at the inauguration of Donald Trump

A large number of protests were planned in connection with the first inauguration of Donald Trump as president on January 20, 2017.[^cite_note-118] Security preparation for Trump's inauguration gathered nearly 28,000 security personnel to participate in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-CNNinaugPROTEST-119] The vast majority of protesters, several thousand in all, were peaceful; however, many violent acts, such as property destruction, occurred.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-KeslinTaudeAvila-121][^cite_note-DwyerDomonokse-122] [DisruptJ20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DisruptJ20) protesters linked arms at security checkpoints and attempted to shut them down.[^cite_note-123] Some elements of the protesters were [black bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_bloc) groups and self-described [anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_the_United_States),[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] and engaged in sporadic acts of vandalism, [rioting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rioting), and violence.[^cite_note-DwyerDomonokse-122][^cite_note-LawlerTelegraph-126][^cite_note-127]

#### Washington, D.C.

On the eve of the inauguration, January 19, protesters gathered outside the [National Press Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Press_Building_\(Washington,_DC\)) in Washington D.C. where the [DeploraBall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeploraBall) was held. Several protesters threw debris at attendees, hitting one man in the head.[^cite_note-128] Police responded with teargas and pepper spray[^cite_note-129] scattering the crowd.



Limousine vandalized in Washington, D.C.

On the day of the Inauguration, January 20, a group of around 100 protesters smashed windows of businesses in downtown Washington and tipped over garbage cans.[^cite_note-130] The protesters also blocked entryways to the event and chained themselves to barricades, attempting with little success to prevent Trump supporters from gathering near the inaugural parade route.[^cite_note-CNNinaugPROTEST-119] Along the parade route, hundreds of demonstrators gathered at designated protest sites, waved signs and chanted anti-Trump slogans. Occasional clashes between police and demonstrators occurred,[^cite_note-131] with masked protesters throwing rocks and chunks of concrete at police.[^cite_note-132] Police in riot gear responded with tear gas, pepper spray, flash grenades, and other crowd dispersing tools. Violent protests continued late into the afternoon near Pennsylvania Avenue.[^cite_note-133]

A limousine was tagged with graffiti, its windows were shattered, and it was later set on fire.[^cite_note-LawlerTelegraph-126] The limo was owned by a Muslim immigrant.[^cite_note-134] The fire spread to a [Fox News](11121.md) crew SUV which was parked behind the limo.[^cite_note-135] A total of 230 people were arrested, and of those, 217 were charged at the federal level with felony rioting, which, if convicted, is punishable by up to 10 years in prison and a fine of up to \$250,000 (\~\$313,700 in 2024).[^cite_note-136] Six officers suffered minor injuries.[^cite_note-137]

On July 6, 2018, it was reported that 21 defendants pled guilty, including one to felony offenses, in connection to the riots and damages. From January to July 2018, about 169 individuals had the charges against them dismissed.[^cite_note-138]

#### California

On the morning of January 20, 2017, anti-Trump protesters blocked the headquarters of [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) in [San Francisco](49728.md) because the CEO of the company has been seen as a "collaborator" with Trump.[^cite_note-\:6-139] Around 16 people were arrested in the demonstration which created human chains to block the offices.[^cite_note-\:6-139] Other companies blocked Friday morning in San Francisco were the [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo) headquarters and [Caltrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltrain) tracks.[^cite_note-\:6-139] In [Los Angeles](18110.md), thousands turned out for a peaceful protest on January 19, despite the rain.[^cite_note-140] Demonstrators rallied outside of [Los Angeles City Hall](1221425.md).[^cite_note-141]

#### LaBeouf, Rönkkö & Turner

Artists [LaBeouf, Rönkkö & Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaBeouf,_R%C3%B6nkk%C3%B6_&_Turner) started [live-streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media) a planned four-year protest, titled [*HEWILLNOTDIVIDE.US*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaBeouf,_R%C3%B6nkk%C3%B6_&_Turner#HEWILLNOTDIVIDE.US,_2017_%E2%80%93_2021), at 9 a.m. on the morning of the inauguration on January 20.[^cite_note-\:12-142] Participants were invited to deliver the words "He will not divide us" into a camera mounted to a wall "as many times, and for as long as they wish", in what the artists described as "a show of resistance or insistence, opposition or optimism, guided by the spirit of each individual participant and the community."[^cite_note-BI-hwndu-143] The footage was broadcast on a 24/7 feed, which the artists announced would run for four years, or the duration of Trump's presidency.[^cite_note-\:12-142]

The initial host of the artwork, the [Museum of the Moving Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_Moving_Image_\(New_York_City\)) in New York, abandoned their involvement with the project after three weeks, citing public safety concerns.[^cite_note-\:11-144] The installation became especially contentious after people started yelling "[1488](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Words)" to the camera and because of increased "[loitering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loitering)" in the area around the museum,[^cite_note-\:13-145] with the museum receiving threats of violence.[^cite_note-\:12-142] The artists, meanwhile, said the museum had "bowed to political pressure" in ceasing their involvement with the project, adding that there had been no incidents of violence that they were aware of. More than a million people viewed the live-stream before it was shut down.[^cite_note-\:13-145] The exhibit relocated on February 18, 2017, to a wall outside the El Rey Theater in [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico).[^cite_note-146]

### Women's March

Main article: [2017 Women's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Women's_March)



Demonstrators on [Pennsylvania Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Avenue) participating in the Women's March on Washington

The [Women's March on Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Women's_March) was a January 21, 2017, protest in Washington, D.C., which attracted about 597,000 people to Independence Ave & Third St. to protest Donald Trump's first full day in office. Simultaneous protests drew large crowds across all 50 US states, and on six continents.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] There was an estimated 3.3 to 4.6 million people involved in the march across the country, making it the largest protest in United States history.[^cite_note-\:10-19][^cite_note-150]

Professor [Erica Chenoweth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erica_Chenoweth) contends that the Women's March shows signs of the beginning of a successful movement.[^cite_note-\:10-19] Rebecca Katz, a progressive strategist, also feels that the protests help successfully create a movement.[^cite_note-auto-151] Nelini Stamp, a director in the [Working Families Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Families_Party) has also seen the protests taking place after the inauguration as the creation of a "national protest movement."[^cite_note-\:17-152]

### February 4, 2017



San Francisco City Hall protest

On February 4, thousands of protesters marched on Trump's [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) resort in Palm Beach, Florida, where Trump was attending a fundraiser for the International Red Cross.[^cite_note-159] In New York City, thousands from the LGBTQ community gathered at the historic [Stonewall Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_Inn) in a show of solidarity with immigrant communities and those affected by Trump's travel ban.[^cite_note-160] Thousands of people in [San Francisco](49728.md) participated in a peaceful protest against Trump taking place outside [San Francisco City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_City_Hall).[^cite_note-161] Protests also took place in Los Angeles and Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-162] In Canada, thousands gathered outside the U.S. consulate in Toronto to protest against Islamophobia and Trump's ban.[^cite_note-163] Thousands of U.K. citizens also took to the streets in protest of the travel ban and [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May)'s invitation to Trump for a state visit.[^cite_note-164]

### Hollywood Walk of Fame

Trump's star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame), awarded to the host of *[The Apprentice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Apprentice_\(U.S._TV_series\))* in 2007, has been vandalized numerous times and destroyed twice – once in 2016 before the election, and once in 2018 when he was president.[^cite_note-smashed-279] Street performer Francisco Javier reported in April 2016 that people often showed disrespect to the star, sometimes defacing it with paint or letting their dogs relieve themselves on it. One person put a sticker over Trump's name.[^cite_note-280] After Trump's nomination as the Republican candidate in July 2016, the artist [Plastic Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_Jesus_\(artist\)) built a small wall with [barbed wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbed_wire) and "Keep Out" signs around the star, a reference to the promised wall intended to keep out illegal immigrants.[^cite_note-281]

In October 2016. James Lambert Otis of the [Otis Elevator Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otis_Elevator_Company) family attacked and destroyed the star with a sledge hammer. He said after his arrest he was supporting the [women who had accused Trump of sexual assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_sexual_misconduct_allegations), and that he would sell pieces of the star with the money going to those women.[^cite_note-282] In February 2017 Otis pleaded no contest to felony vandalism and was sentenced to three years probation, 20 days of community service, and \$4,400 (\~\$5,779 in 2025) in fines.[^cite_note-283]

On July 25, 2018 at 3\:33 A.M., police responded to a report of a man vandalizing Trump's star with a [pickaxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickaxe) that was hidden in a guitar case.[^cite_note-smashed-279] An hour later 24-year old Austin Clay turned himself in and was arrested for felony vandalism. Trump's star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame was left as rubble with the pickaxe left behind. [KNBC](1246824.md) found a witness who said he had asked the vandal, "What did Donald Trump do to you?" without receiving a response.[^cite_note-smashed-279] The star was replaced the next day.[^cite_note-284] Later a brawl broke out on the Hollywood Walk of Fame between a group of protesters and a group of Trump supporters, who were holding a [Make America Great Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_America_Great_Again) rally in support of Trump around his new replacement star.[^cite_note-285] In August, 2018, Trump supporters placed more than fifty laminated copies of the Trump star over blank squares at several locations, reportedly in response to a call for removal of Trump's star by a local city government.[^cite_note-286]

On October 21, 2018, a *[The New York Times](30680.md)* article reported on a [Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services) memo discussing plans to establish a definition of gender based on [sex assignment at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_assignment) across federal agencies – a policy which would prevent transgender people from changing the gender listed on their legal documents.[^cite_note-\:27-287] The memo argued in favor of a definition of gender "on a biological basis that is clear, grounded in science, objective and administrable" and the use of [genetic testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_testing) to determine sex in the event of disputes.[^cite_note-\:27-287] That night, several hundred people gathered in [Washington Square Park](745249.md) in opposition of the proposed policy.[^cite_note-288] Over the following days, thousands of protesters gathered in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md);[^cite_note-289] [San Diego](28504.md);[^cite_note-290] [Portland, Maine](23503.md);[^cite_note-291] [Minneapolis](6097240.md);[^cite_note-292] [Los Angeles](18110.md);[^cite_note-293] [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee);[^cite_note-294] [Boston](24437894.md);[^cite_note-295] and other cities across the country.

## Between presidencies

In 2024, Trump became the [president-elect of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States) again after winning the [2024 presidential election](59568546.md) against incumbent [Vice President](32759.md) [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris).

### Domestic

Protesters gathered in [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California) and [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California) to protest [both parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniparty).[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] An anti-Trump rally was held in downtown [Cleveland, Ohio](5951.md) on the night after the election for about an hour before protesters dispersed. Other protests were held that night in [Chicago, Illinois](6886.md); [New York City, New York](645042.md); and [Philadelphia, Pennsylvania](50585.md); with protesters outside the [Trump International Hotel and Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_International_Hotel_and_Tower_\(Chicago\)) in Chicago filmed chanting "Donald Trump, you [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) clown."[^cite_note-302][^cite_note-303]

The [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) (ACLU) issued a statement the day after Trump's election indicating that they would continue to fight against Trump's rhetoric and plans such as the mass deportation plans outlined in [Project 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_2025).[^cite_note-304] On January 18, 2025, two days before the inauguration, the Women's March held a protest rebranded as the [People's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_March) in [Washington DC](108956.md).[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306] A protest was also held in [Nashville](22018.md), [Tennessee](30395.md).[^cite_note-307] Additionally, Women's March protests were held in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) including [Manchester](20206.md), [Liverpool](18081.md), and [Plymouth](23508.md).[^cite_note-308][^cite_note-309]

### International

[Just Stop Oil](70538003.md) protesters sprayed orange paint on the [American Embassy in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_London) shortly after Trump was declared the winner on November 6.[^cite_note-310] The [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) issued a statement indicating that they were concerned about Trump's second term shortly after he was elected based on his prior rights-abusing record from his first term, and that "Independent institutions and civil society groups, including Human Rights Watch, will need to do all we can to hold him and his administration accountable for abuses."[^cite_note-311] On November 8, around 150 people held a rally known as "United against Fascism" in [Bern, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), following Trump's re-election.[^cite_note-312][^cite_note-313] On December 24, a protest was held at the [U.S. Embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Panama_City) in [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City) over Trump's threat to take back the [Panama Canal](24849.md). Protesters also referred to him as a "public enemy" of [Panama](22997.md).[^cite_note-314][^cite_note-315]

### People's March

Main article: [People's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_March)

On January 18, 2025, the [People's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_March) protests were held in Washington, D.C. in response to [Trump's second inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump), which was two days later.[^cite_note-316] The protest was attended by an estimated 50,000 people in Washington D.C., and other rallies were held throughout other cities in the United States.[^cite_note-317] Internationally, protests were also held in response to Trump's inauguration, including in Canada,[^cite_note-318] the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-319] and in France.[^cite_note-320]

### Day Without Immigrants 2025

Main article: [Day Without Immigrants (2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_Without_Immigrants_\(2025\))

The [Day Without Immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_Without_Immigrants_\(2025\)) was held across cities in the U.S. in February 2025. In Los Angeles protesters marched through [U.S. 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_101), temporarily blocking traffic.[^cite_note-\:28-20] The protesters carried banners with slogans such as ["Nobody is illegal"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_one_is_illegal) and other pro-immigration slogans. They also carried American and Mexican flags. Between the two protests there were around 1,600 people.[^cite_note-321] On February 5, 2025, thousands of protesters gathered at state capitols for the American Opposition[^cite_note-322][^cite_note-323] and [50501](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50501) protests to protest the policies and actions of the second Donald Trump administration, in the United States.[^cite_note-324] Coalitions and [pro-federal worker groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt_National_Park_Service) called for resistance and protest of actions taken by Trump through executive order and [Elon Musk](909036.md) through extra-governmental maneuvers.[^cite_note-325]

### Policy

On February 22, 2025, hundreds of protesters marched in West Hollywood, California, to demonstrate against policies of the Trump administration and Elon Musk's leadership of the so-called [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency) (DOGE). The protest, organized by Democracy Action Network (DAN) in coalition with Indivisible and the 50501 Movement, began at West Hollywood Park and proceeded along Santa Monica Boulevard to the Sal Guarriello Veterans' Memorial. Protesters expressed concerns about executive orders, federal spending cuts, and deregulation. The demonstration coincided with preparations for the annual Elton John AIDS Foundation Academy Awards Viewing Party.[^cite_note-326]

Protests against job cuts and reduced environmental regulation have also taken place in national parks, with more than a hundred events held on March 1, 2025. Many of these featured upside-down American flags as a distress signal, a symbol popularised by a flag hanging in Yosemite park a week earlier.[^cite_note-\:28-20] The concert [*Love Is Love*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Is_Love_\(concert\)) was held at the Kennedy Center in June 2025 to commemorate Pride Month and protest the president's changes to the organization. The [Fight the Trump Takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_the_Trump_Takeover?action=edit\&redlink=1) protest was held in August 2025.

### Hands Off protests

Main article: [Hands Off protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hands_Off_protests)

On April 5, 2025, what was then the largest nationwide protest of the second Trump administration took place under the banner "Hands Off." Organized by [Move On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoveOn), in coordination with partners including [Indivisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_movement), Women's March, [Public Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Citizen) and the new grassroots group [50501](79143789.md), the protests demonstrated opposition to the administration's policies ranging from the then just-announced [world-wide tariffs](78448911.md) to government cuts being made by the Department of Government Efficiency.[^cite_note-327] Marches and rallies took place in more than 1,300 locations, including in all 50 US states. Organizers estimated the total attendance to be over 3 million.[^cite_note-328]

### No Kings protests

Main article: [50501 protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50501_protests)



Protesters in New York City in the October 2025 No Kings protests

On June 14, 2025, the [June 2025 No Kings protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_No_Kings_protests) were held in response to the [United States Army 250th Anniversary Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_250th_Anniversary_Parade), and organizers of the protests estimated that over 5 million people participated in the protests.[^cite_note-329] These were followed by the [October 2025 No Kings protests](81279597.md) on October 18, 2025, which was reported by its organizers to have had over 7 million participants. An independent study estimated a lower number of protesters, between 5 million and 6.5 million, but concluded that either estimate would make the October protests the largest single-day protest in American history.[^cite_note-330]

A [third No Kings protest](82478314.md) was held on March 28, 2026, in response to recent [shootings by immigration agents](82067135.md) in the country, and the ongoing [war in Iran.](82533582.md) Organizers estimated at least 8 million participants took part in more than 3,300 events worldwide,[^cite_note-331] beating the previous record for [largest single-day protest in American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protests_and_demonstrations_in_the_United_States_by_size).

## Proposed legislation to curb protests

Protests in the mid-2010s, including those against the 2016 election of Donald Trump, have also led to some state legislatures creating various anti-protest bills that the [ACLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) stated were unconstitutional.[^cite_note-351] Many of these bills have been created by [Republican](32070.md) lawmakers.[^cite_note-352] By late February 2017, legislators in at least 18 states proposed legislation to curtail specific protest tactics, impose more severe penalties for protest tactics prohibited by existing law, or publicly discussed such proposals[^cite_note-FordTargetingProtesters-353] and by mid-May the number had risen to 20.[^cite_note-may10list-354] Some of the bills include high penalties, such as large fines and jail time, for blocking interstates or obstructing "economic activity."[^cite_note-\:21-355] A bill in [Minnesota](19590.md) would allow the police to sue demonstrators for the cost of policing protests.[^cite_note-\:21-355] The bill proposed in [Tennessee](30395.md) would allow anyone who injures a protester with their car "civil immunity" as long as the driver was not being reckless.[^cite_note-\:22-356] The proposed bill in [Indiana](21883857.md) would allow law enforcement to "use any means necessary" to clear people who block traffic.[^cite_note-357] Republican lawmaker [J. D. Mesnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._D._Mesnard) said these laws are "not about limiting people's rights."[^cite_note-\:23-358]

## Social media

In addition to in-person protests, there has also been Anti-Trump activism on social media. This activism has been represented by various hashtags. The following are several examples of hashtags that were used for Anti-Trump activism online. [VJayBombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VJayBombs) posted several videos of projected images in Los Angeles and Washington D.C. protesting decisions by the Trump administration.

### #Resist

Main article: [The Resistance (American political movement)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resistance_\(American_political_movement\))



[Protests against Executive Order 13769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_against_Executive_Order_13769) in Washington, D.C.

One of the most consistent hashtags that has been used in the Anti-Trump movement on social media is *#Resist*.[^cite_note-\:02-359] *#Resist* was first used on Twitter, Facebook and other platforms commencing immediately after the election in November. Though the exact origin of this hashtag is unknown, it spread to various social media platforms.[^cite_note-\:110-360]

Generally, this hashtag symbolizes solidarity against the [Trump administration](52231341.md). It is also used alongside other policy specific hashtags targeting marginalized groups such as minorities and women. Though its height of popularity occurred during the days following Trump's inauguration, it has resurfaced during times of political controversy and animosity.[^cite_note-\:24-361] For instance, there was a spike in usage throughout the week of Trump's response to the Unite the Right Charlottesville rally.[^cite_note-\:24-361] Moreover, in the three days following the announcement of the initial Muslim ban in late January, #Resist appeared in over 2.5 million tweets.[^cite_note-\:32-362] Several prominent celebrities have used the hashtag to show opposition to Trump, including [Shailene Woodley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shailene_Woodley), [Zendaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zendaya), [Sia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sia), [Rosie O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosie_O'Donnell), [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher), [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde), and [Sophia Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Bush).[^cite_note-\:32-362]

### #NotMyPresident

*#NotMyPresident* gained immediate popularity following Donald Trump's presidential election win on November 8, 2016.[^cite_note-\:42-363] Following Trump's win, *#NotMyPresident* immediately trended on Twitter and the hashtag was used in over 78,000 tweets.[^cite_note-364] Facebook was also used as an outlet for *#NotMyPresident*. On November 9, 2016, a Facebook event titled "Trump is Not My President" was created and received over 40,000 interactions.[^cite_note-365] The page was an example of social media activism transferring into real world protest, as a march in Union Square, New York, was created. *#NotMyPresident* was also used by social media influencers other political players. After the Charlottesville Riots, Democratic Senator [Brian Schatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Schatz) from Hawaii tweeted that Trump is "not my president."[^cite_note-\:52-366]

The hashtag movement *#NotMyPresident* was also met with criticism. Opponents of the movement believe that saying "#NotMyPresident" undermines America's democratic values as a whole due to its divisive tone.[^cite_note-367] Democratic North Dakota Senator [Heidi Heitkamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidi_Heitkamp) voiced her disapproval to the Anti-Trump movement on May 18, 2017, at the Greater North Dakota Chamber of Commerce. Heitkamp viewed the Anti-Trump movement as counterproductive, saying it results in a far more difficult path to draft legislation.[^cite_note-368]

### #StillWithHer

The hashtag *#ImWithHer* was first seen on Twitter, when Bill Clinton was watching his wife in the debate and decided to tweet his support: "What happens in Vegas ... is I watch @HillaryClinton prove she's the most qualified candidate for POTUS. #ImwithHer."[^cite_note-\:62-369] This tweet, posted in October 2015, gained more than 9,000 retweets and created a new campaign slogan for the Hillary 2016 campaign: "I'm With Her."[^cite_note-\:72-370] Many Twitter users use *#StillWithHer* hashtag to "express their messages of hope, sadness, and determination following the 2016 Election."[^cite_note-\:82-371]

### #LoveTrumpsHate

This hashtag plays on the double meaning of the word 'Trump.' It originated from Hillary Clinton's final campaign speech, in which she stated, 'love trumps hate.' Since then, the hashtag has been used by critics of Donald Trump to express opposition to his rhetoric and policies, which they view as harmful to certain minority groups.

## Impact

Protests after [Trump's first inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump's_first_inauguration) helped energize [progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) in the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) according to Ace Smith, a strategist for the party.[^cite_note-\:3-372] According to the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)*, "Protesters have quickened the outrage metabolism among members of Congress, encouraged disruptive tactics ... and mostly ended the argument within the congressional caucuses over whether Democrats should work with Trump on occasion rather than universally oppose him."[^cite_note-\:3-372] [Ben Wikler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Wikler), a director for [MoveOn.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoveOn.org), commented that it feels as if grassroots energy has exploded like a volcano.[^cite_note-\:8-79] Democrats in [Georgia](48830.md) have seen an increase in political activism, which the party would like to see continue.[^cite_note-373] In addition, [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_the_United_States) organizations saw a spike in membership,[^cite_note-374] and [town hall meetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_hall_meeting) had increased attendance.[^cite_note-\:17-152] Some Republicans avoided having town halls because of the large attendance rates.[^cite_note-\:20-82] Victoria Kaplan of MoveOn.org called this avoidance a sign that the protests are impactful.[^cite_note-\:20-82] Trump has become a common enemy for many different liberal and progressive groups who are now working together.[^cite_note-\:17-152] [Tom Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Perez), the head of the Democratic Party during Trump's first term in office, promised to bring more grassroots and anti-Trump action to the party.[^cite_note-375]

Groups on the left side of the political spectrum that have not always worked well together have started focusing less on their differences and more on a common enemy in Trump.[^cite_note-376] In addition, economic sectors that have not normally been politically active, like the tech industry, have seen a increase in activism.[^cite_note-377] Conservative districts have been confronting Republican congressmen about their voting records and their stances on the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act) (ACA).[^cite_note-auto-151] [Meetup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meetup_\(website\)), which had for 15 years allowed the creation of only non-partisan groups, has chosen to allow more than a thousand "#resist" groups which are free to join and run.[^cite_note-\:14-86] Protesters, many of whom are making their own signs, have raised sales in items such as [poster board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_board) and [markers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marker_pen). Between January 15 and 21, 2017, people in America spent \$4.1 million in poster boards.[^cite_note-378] [PepsiCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PepsiCo) released an ad in April 2017 which used the imagery of anti-Trump protests and [Kendall Jenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Jenner) to sell [Pepsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi).[^cite_note-379] The ad was criticized by *[Elle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_\(magazine\))* for appropriating imagery of the resistance against Trump and the policies of his administration.[^cite_note-380] The ad also evoked imagery from the [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) movement and was eventually pulled by PepsiCo.[^cite_note-381]
