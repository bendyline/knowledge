**Vale** (Portuguese pronunciation: [\[ˈvali\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)), formerly *Companhia Vale do Rio Doce* ([Doce River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doce_River) Valley Company),[^cite_note-3] is a Brazilian [multinational corporation](214491.md) engaged in metals and mining and one of the largest logistics operators in Brazil.[^cite_note-4] Vale is the largest producer of [iron ore](145352.md) and [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) in the world. It also produces manganese, [ferroalloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroalloy), copper, [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [potash](56509.md), [kaolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaolin), and [cobalt](24580536.md); as of 2014 the company operated nine [hydroelectricity](381399.md) plants, and a large network of railroads, ships, and ports used to transport its products.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

The company has had two catastrophic [tailings dam failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailings_dam_failure) in Brazil: [Mariana](48475234.md), in 2015, and [Brumadinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumadinho_dam_disaster), in 2019; the [Brumadinho dam disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumadinho_dam_disaster) caused the company to lose its license to operate eight tailings dams in [Minas Gerais](222651.md),[^cite_note-CIM-6][^cite_note-7] and its stock to drop nearly 25 percent in price.[^cite_note-WSJ-8]

## Current operations

Although the company's primary operations are in [Brazil](3383.md), Vale has operations in 30 countries, which are detailed below and on the company's website.[^cite_note-9]

### Ownership structure

The company is listed on the stock exchanges of [São Paulo](390875.md), [New York City](645042.md), [Jakarta](16275.md), [Paris](22989.md) and [Madrid](41188263.md).

### Mining business



Vale's **Carajás Iron Mine**, [Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par%C3%A1), 2009 [NASA](18426568.md) satellite photo

**[Iron ore](145352.md)**: Vale is the world's largest iron ore producer.[^cite_note-mining2016-10] Sales of iron ore fines and pellets represented 65% of total company revenues in 2014. In 2014, Vale sold 256 million [metric tonnes](31185.md) of iron ore fines and 44 million metric tonnes of iron ore pellets.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5] Vale's Mariana Hub was the 9th largest iron ore mining center in the world in 2014, with an output of 39 million metric tonnes.[^cite_note-11] Vale's Serra Sul / [S11D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S11D) is the largest mining reserve in the world. The company's iron ore mines are primarily in [Brazil](3383.md).[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

**[Nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel)**: Vale is the world's largest nickel producer.[^cite_note-mining2016-10] Sales of nickel represented 17% of total company revenues in 2014. In 2014, Vale sold 272,000 metric tonnes of nickel.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5] The company owns nickel mines in [Canada](5042916.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), [New Caledonia](21342.md) and [Brazil](3383.md). [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Inc) is known to buy the majority of their nickel from Vale.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5] In May 2022, Vale said that it had signed a long-term deal of supplying nickel for Tesla. The agreement involves supplying low-carbon Class 1 nickel which Tesla needs for its electric vehicles' batteries. In line with this, Vale is pushing to increase its exposure to the electric vehicle industry.[^cite_note-12]

**Fertilizer products, primarily [phosphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) and [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen)**: Sales of fertilizer products represented 6% of total company revenues in 2014. In 2014, Vale sold 9 million metric tonnes of fertilizer products.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

**[Copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper)**: Sales of copper concentrate represented 4% of total company revenues in 2014. In 2014, Vale sold 353,000 metric tonnes of copper. The company owns copper mines in [Brazil](3383.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Chile](5489.md) and [Zambia](34415.md).[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

**[Manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) and [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloys)**: Sales of manganese and alloys represented 1% of total company revenues in 2014. In 2014, Vale sold 2 million metric tonnes of manganese and alloys.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

**[Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal)**: Sales of coal represented 2% of total company revenues in 2014. In 2014, Vale sold 7.5 million metric tonnes of coal. The company owns coal mines in [Australia](4689264.md) and [Mozambique](19301.md).[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

### Logistics

#### Railroads

From 2000 to 2006, Vale invested more than \$1.3 billion on the acquisition of over 361 [locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive) and around 14,090 [freight cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_car#Freight_cars), those locomotives were primarily for [iron ore](145352.md) transportation, but some were for regular cargo.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5] Some of the locomotives purchased were secondhand for refurbishment but at least 55 of the locomotives acquired were new ones of the model [EMD SD70M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMD_SD70_series#SD70M_\(1992%E2%80%932004\)), each one costing about \$2 million.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

After those investments, Vale became the owner of over 800 locomotives and more than 35,000 freight cars.[^cite_note-20Fbusiness-5]

## History

Companhia Vale do Rio Doce (widely known as CVRD prior to 2007) (in English, "[Doce River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doce_River) Valley Company") was founded in [Itabira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itabira), [Minas Gerais](222651.md), by the Brazilian Federal Government on 1 June 1942.[^cite_note-history-29]

One year later the Vitória a Minas railroad was inaugurated.[^cite_note-history-29]

The 1950s marked Companhia Vale do Rio Doce's entry into the global iron ore market, after the company's mine-railroad-port complex was modernized and iron ore prices doubled. At first, sales were mostly to the United States, but exports to Europe increased over the course of the decade.[^cite_note-history3-30]

In 1966, the company inaugurated in [Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo) the [Port of Tubarão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tubar%C3%A3o), which was to become the most important port for CVRD and is still used to export [iron ore](145352.md) mined from the [Iron Quadrangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Quadrangle) in [Minas Gerais](222651.md).[^cite_note-history-29]

The company acquired a majority interest in the [Carajás Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraj%C3%A1s_Mine), with over 1.5 billion tonnes of iron ore in reserves, in 1970.[^cite_note-history5-31]

In 1974, Vale became the world's biggest exporter of iron ore, a title which it still holds today.[^cite_note-history5-31]

In 1982, Vale began to diversify after it started to produce [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md).[^cite_note-history5-31]

In the early-to-mid-1980s, profits increased considerably under the leadership of [Eliezer Batista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliezer_Batista), father of [Eike Batista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eike_Batista).[^cite_note-32]

In 1985, Vale started to explore the [Carajás Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraj%C3%A1s_Mine) in the state of [Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par%C3%A1) just after the 1,600 mm (5 ft 3 in) gauge Carajás railroad was opened.

In 1986, Ponta Madeira port terminal, which is still used to export iron ore mined at the [Carajás Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraj%C3%A1s_Mine), was inaugurated in the state of [Maranhão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranh%C3%A3o).

In March 2017, Vale SA chose a [commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_\(type_of_good\)) industry veteran, Fabio Schvartsman, as [chief executive officer](52234.md). Schvartsman was [CEO](52234.md) of [Klabin SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klabin), [Brazil](3383.md)'s largest paper and cardboard producer, for the previous six years.[^cite_note-33]

### Privatization in 1997

In May 1997, despite protests by Vale employees and some politicians, the [Brazilian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Government) auctioned a 41.73% interest in the company, which was sold for R\$3.34 billion (US\$3.13 billion). The largest interest purchased was a 16.3% stake purchased by Brazilian steel company [Companhia Siderúrgica Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companhia_Sider%C3%BArgica_Nacional).[^cite_note-34]

### Sale of wood pulp businesses

In 2001, Vale sold its Cenibra wood pulp business to [Aracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aracruz_Celulose) and [Votorantim Celulose e Papel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibria) jointly for US\$670.5 million to focus on mining and logistics.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In 2002, Vale sold 100,000 acres of land and the eucalyptus forests thereon related to its wood pulp business for R\$137 million.[^cite_note-37]

### Sale of steel businesses

In 2000, the company sold its stake in Açominas to [Gerdau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerdau) in exchange for preferred shares in [Gerdau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerdau).[^cite_note-38]

In 2001, Vale sold its stake in [Companhia Siderúrgica Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companhia_Sider%C3%BArgica_Nacional) for R\$520 million.[^cite_note-39]

In 2004, Vale sold its stake in CST to [Arcelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcelor) for US\$415.1 million.[^cite_note-40]

In 2006, Vale sold its interest in Siderar to [Ternium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternium) for US\$107.5 million.[^cite_note-41]

In 2006, the company sold 5,362,928 shares in [Usiminas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usiminas) for or R\$378.6 million.[^cite_note-42] In 2007, the company sold the majority of its stake in [Usiminas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usiminas).[^cite_note-43] In 2009, the company sold its remaining stake in [Usiminas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usiminas).[^cite_note-44]

### Sale of coal businesses

In 2014, Vale announced the sale of coal assets in [Mozambique](19301.md) to [Mitsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsui) in a \$950 million transaction.[^cite_note-45]

In 2015, Vale's Integra coal mine located in Australia's Hunter Valley in [New South Wales](21654.md) was sold to [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore) and [Bloomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_companies_of_Australia#Bloomfield_Collieries_Pty_Ltd) in a complex multi-party deal.[^cite_note-46]

### Acquisitions of Brazilian iron ore companies

In May 2000, the company acquired Sociomex, owner of the [Gongo Sôco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongo_S%C3%B4co) Mine, with proven reserves of approximately 75 million tonnes.[^cite_note-47]

In May 2000, the company also acquired a controlling interest in Samitri, one of the biggest pelletizing companies of Brazil.[^cite_note-48]

In April 2001, the company acquired Ferteco, then the third largest Brazilian producer of iron ore, with a production capacity of 15 million tonnes per year.[^cite_note-49]

In 2006, the company acquired Rio Verde Mineração for \$47 million.[^cite_note-50]

#### Acquisition of [Caemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caemi) and acquisition and partial disposition of MBR

On 1 April 2000, Vale offered to pay [Mitsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsui) US\$277 million for 50% of the common shares and US\$150 million for 40% of the preferred stock in [Caemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caemi). Caemi owned MBR, Brazil's second largest iron ore producer, mining over 60 million tonnes per year.[^cite_note-51]

The acquisition was approved by European regulators subject to conditions.[^cite_note-52]

In September 2003, Vale purchased an additional stake in Caemi from Mitsui for US\$426.4 million.[^cite_note-53]

On 23 January 2006, the company announced a [stock swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_swap) merger to acquire the interest Caemi that it did not already own.[^cite_note-54]

In 2007, increased its ownership in MBR by purchasing additional interests from [Mitsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsui) for US\$114.5 million.[^cite_note-55]

Also in 2007, Vale announced that it will lease the shares of MBR that it did not already own from their 7 Japanese shareholders for a 30-year period. The agreement required the company to pay a total of US\$60.5 million in 2007 and US\$48.1 million annually for a 30-year period and gave it total control of MBR.[^cite_note-56]

In 2015, Vale announced the sale of a 36.4% interest in MBR for R\$4 billion.[^cite_note-57]

### Diversification into [non-ferrous metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-ferrous_metals), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate)

In 2001, Vale increased its copper mining operations by purchasing the Sossego mine in Carajas, in northern Brazil, for \$48.5 million.[^cite_note-58]

In 2005, Vale acquired [Canico Resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canico_Resource), owner of a [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) mine in Brazil, after increasing its offer to \$865 million.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

In October 2006, Vale acquired Canadian-based nickel producer [Inco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Inco) for \$18.9 billion, including \$17.7 billion in cash and the assumption of \$1.2 billion in debt.[^cite_note-61] To gain approval from Canadian authorities, Vale promised to continue investments in Canada and not lay off people for 3 years after closing.[^cite_note-62]

In 2007, Vale made a major entry into coal mining by acquiring AMCI Holdings Australia for AU\$835 million.[^cite_note-63]

In 2010, Vale launched a public offer to acquire fertilizer and copper producer Paranapanema.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

In 2010, Vale acquired a controlling interest in Fosfertil via a series of transactions.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

In June 2011, Jason Chenier and Jordan Fram were killed at the 3,000 foot level of the [Frood Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frood_Mine) near [Sudbury, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudbury,_Ontario).[^cite_note-68]

In 2011, the company sold its aluminum business to Norsk Hydro in a US\$5.27 billion transaction.[^cite_note-69] Norsk Hydro thus became a truly integrated aluminium company. This gave Hydro the world's largest [alumina](141888.md) refinery and aimed to "secure raw materials for more than a hundred years of aluminum production". Hydro gained a large [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) supply, including control of the [Paragominas mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragominas_mine), one of the largest bauxite mines in the world. It also gained large [alumina](141888.md) refining and aluminum production capacity, including a 51 percent stake in the [Albras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albras) aluminum plant and 91 percent ownership of Alunorte, then the world's largest alumina refinery and now the largest outside China.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

In July 2012, Vale sold its ferro-manganese plants in [Europe](9239.md) to [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore) for \$160 million.[^cite_note-72]

In 2014, Vale sold its interest in Fosbrasil S.A., a manufacturer of phosphate-based products to [Israel Chemicals Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICL_Group_Ltd.) for \$52 million.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

In 2015, the company announced that it would sell a 25% interest in gold produced from its Salobo mine, located in Brazil, to [Silver Wheaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Wheaton) for US\$900 million.[^cite_note-75]

In 2015, the company entered into a [letter of intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_intent) to sell bauxite assets to [Norsk Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_Hydro).[^cite_note-76]

On 26 November 2015, Vale announced that it planned to reduce its [budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget) for [capital expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_expenditure) from US\$8 billion in 2015 to US\$6.2 billion in 2016, with further reductions to US\$4–5 billion by 2018.[^cite_note-77]

In May 2023 it was announced by [Eduardo Bartolomeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Bartolomeo) that [Mark Cutifani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Cutifani) would be appointed as Chair of the new [Vale Base Metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Base_Metals) (VBM) subsidiary in a spin-off event.[^cite_note-ft1-78] Vale was looking to divest from its tar baby,[^cite_note-bnnb1-79] as early as December 2022.[^cite_note-mt1-80] At the time VBM was a supplier to [Tesla](5533631.md) and [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors).[^cite_note-mt1-80] Reports were afoot that GM, [Mitsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsui), and the [Saudi Public Investment Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Public_Investment_Fund) were interested buyers of a 10% stake.[^cite_note-mt1-80] Former Tesla executive [Jerome Guillen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Guillen) would join the "energy transition board" of VBM along with Cutifani.[^cite_note-mcom1-81]

Vale spun out its metals business as a separate ringfenced entity headquartered in Toronto, with an independent board chaired by Cutifani. That process completed in July 2023. The unit was then one of the world’s largest producers of nickel, copper, and cobalt, and has operations across the globe. The parent company's chief executive stated that Cutifani could help the division explore a future “liquidity event”.[^cite_note-mcom1-81][^cite_note-nob1-82] In early 2023, the parent company earned 80% of its profits in its South American iron mines, and the balance from its Base Metals group.[^cite_note-ft1-78]

## Disasters and incidents



A village flooded in the [Mariana dam disaster](48475234.md) (2015). The dam was a property of [Samarco](48524738.md), a [joint venture](538782.md) between Vale and [BHP](200825.md).

### Bento Rodrigues dam collapse

Main article: [Mariana dam disaster](48475234.md)

On 5 November 2015, the [Mariana dam disaster](48475234.md) caused 19 deaths and massive environmental contamination when a [tailings dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailings_dam) collapsed at the [Samarco](48524738.md) mining site, co-owned by Vale and [BHP](200825.md). [Heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic_heavy_metal) contamination of the [Doce River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doce_River) caused water emergencies in many downstream cities which depend on the river for drinking water. Activities at the mine were suspended, and the companies agreed to pay compensation of R\$4.4 billion (US\$1.55 billion).[^cite_note-88]

An investigation into the disaster commissioned by BHP, Vale and Samarco found the collapse was due to a variety of construction and design flaws.[^cite_note-89] In June 2018, Samarco, Vale and BHP signed an agreement to drop a US\$7 billion lawsuit and allow two years for the companies to address the greater US\$55 billion suit seeking social, environmental and economic compensation.[^cite_note-90]

### Brumadinho dam disaster

Main article: [Brumadinho dam disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumadinho_dam_disaster)

On 25 January 2019, the [Brumadinho dam disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumadinho_dam_disaster) occurred at the Córrego do Feijão mine in the Brazilian state of Minas Gerais, releasing 3 billion gallons of mine waste,[^cite_note-NPR-91] in a wave of red iron ore which destroyed the mine's cafeteria, where many workers were present and died, and which flooded the town of [Brumadinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumadinho).[^cite_note-92] 270 were killed.[^cite_note-93] In 2020, prosecutors found that Vale had falsified safety reports for at least ten of its dams. They later charged sixteen officials of Vale including its CEO, with manslaughter.[^cite_note-94]

In 2021, Vale agreed to pay victims \$7 billion. Though substantially deadlier, the environmental devastation was not as severe as with Bento Rodrigues.

Following the Córrego do Feijão disaster, a Brazilian court ordered Vale to stop disposing of tailings at eight dams.[^cite_note-NPR-91]

### Tax dispute

In 2013, Vale S.A. agreed to pay the equivalent of \$9.5 billion to settle a tax dispute with the Brazilian authorities regarding its overseas profits from 2003 to 2012.[^cite_note-95]

### Totten Mine

On September 28, 2021, thirty-nine miners were trapped underground for 24 hours at the Totten Mine in Ontario after the entrance shaft became obstructed.[^cite_note-96]

## Criticism

In January 2012, Vale received the "people's choice" [Public Eye Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Eye_Award) as the corporation with the most "contempt for the environment and human rights" in the world. Vale received 25,000 votes, with the [Belo Monte Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belo_Monte_Dam) cited as a reason.[^cite_note-97]

During the interim between the two dam disasters of 2015 and 2019; Vale had denied owning an upstream tailings waste structure such as collapsed, causing the [Brumadinho dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumadinho_dam_disaster) to burst.[^cite_note-WSJ-8]

Following the 2019 disaster, [BBC News](1139893.md) reported that "Correspondents say the alarm system the company had installed to warn residents of any risk did not go off."[^cite_note-98]

In February 2021, an agreement was signed that required Vale to pay over \$7 billion in compensation to the families of the victims.[^cite_note-99] \$55 million was the settlement cost to the company for its role in making false certifications in regards to the 2019 disaster which the company reached with the U.S. [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) in late March 2023.[^cite_note-100]
