# Laser weapon

For science-fiction guns, see [Raygun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raygun). For sporting guns, see [Laser pistol (sport)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_pistol_\(sport\)).



The US-Israeli [Tactical High Energy Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_High_Energy_Laser) (THEL) was used to shoot down rockets and artillery shells before being canceled in 2005 as a result of "its bulkiness, high costs and poor anticipated results on the battlefield".

[^cite_note-NyTimes1-1]

A **laser weapon**[^cite_note-urlDirected_Energy-2] is a type of [directed-energy weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon) (DEW) that uses [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasers) to inflict damage. Laser weapons are of two types: low-power *laser dazzlers* that blind optical systems or human eyes, and high-power lasers that can physically damage or destroy targets, such as enemy aircraft, drones, and missiles.

One of the major issues with laser weapons is atmospheric [thermal blooming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_blooming), which is still largely unsolved. This issue is exacerbated when there is fog, smoke, dust, rain, snow, smog, foam, or purposely dispersed obscurant chemicals present. In essence, a laser generates a beam of light that requires clear air or a vacuum to operate.[^cite_note-3]



The [YAL-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_YAL-1), a modified [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747), owned by [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF). It was canceled in December 2011 and scrapped in September 2014.

YAL-1 live test

Low-power [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser) have the potential to serve as [non-lethal weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-lethal_weapons). They can cause temporary or permanent vision loss. The extent, nature, and duration of visual impairment depend on factors including the laser's power, wavelength(s), beam collimation and orientation, and duration of exposure. Lasers with a power output of less than one watt can cause permanent vision loss. The [Protocol on Blinding Laser Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_on_Blinding_Laser_Weapons) bans weapons designed to cause permanent blindness. Weapons designed to cause temporary blindness, known as [dazzlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzler_\(weapon\)), are in a separate category. Multiple incidents of [pilots exposed to lasers while flying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasers_and_aviation_safety) have been recorded.[^cite_note-4]

High-power laser weapons capable of damaging or destroying a target are experimental as of 2026. The use of laser-beam weaponry to destroy aerial targets has been under development for years. The [United States Navy](20518076.md) tested short-range (1 mile), 30-[kW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt) [Laser Weapon System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_Weapon_System) or LaWS for use against targets such as small [UAVs](58900.md), [rocket-propelled grenades](26286.md), and visible [motorboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorboat) or [helicopter](8286923.md) engines.[^cite_note-Luis_Martinez-5][^cite_note-6] A 60 kW system, [HELIOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance), was under development for destroyer-class ships as of 2020.[^cite_note-7] India's DRDO successfully tested a 30 kW DEW, designated Mk-II (A) DEW, in April 2025 which could disable drones at a range of 5 km.[^cite_note-DRDO-2025-8]

## Air defense systems

DEW for the destruction of incoming missiles are under development. One example is Boeing [Airborne Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_YAL-1), deployed inside a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) and designated as [YAL-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_YAL-1). This system was designed to eliminate short- and intermediate-range ballistic missiles during their boost phase.[^cite_note-9] It was canceled in 2012.

Another system was studied under the [Strategic Defense Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defense_Initiative) (SDI) and successor programs. This project aimed to employ ground-based or [space-based laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_laser) systems to destroy incoming [intercontinental ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBMs). However, various practical challenges, such as aiming a laser over a large distance through the atmosphere, complicated implementation. Optical scattering and [refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refraction) bent and distorted the beam, complicating aiming and reducing its efficiency.

A related concept was the [nuclear-pumped X-ray laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Excalibur), an orbiting [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb) surrounded by laser media in the form of glass rods. When a bomb detonated, the rods would be exposed to highly-energetic [gamma-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_rays) [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon), causing [spontaneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_emission) and [stimulated emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulated_emission) of [X-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray) photons within the rod atoms. This process would result in optical amplification of the X-ray photons, generating an X-ray beam that was little affected by atmospheric distortion and capable of destroying ICBMs in flight. However, the [X-ray laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_laser) became a single-use device, as it would destroy itself upon activation. Some initial tests were conducted with [underground nuclear testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_nuclear_testing), but the results were not promising.

### Iron Beam

Main article: [Iron Beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Beam)

Iron Beam is a laser-based [air defense system](1099196.md) which was unveiled at the [Singapore Airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Airshow) on 11 February 2014[^cite_note-10] by Israeli [defense contractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_contractor) [Rafael Advanced Defense Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems).[^cite_note-Reuters-11] The system is designed to destroy short-range rockets, artillery, and [mortar bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_bomb); it has a range of up to 7 km (4.3 mi), too close for the [Iron Dome](18342427.md) system to intercept projectiles effectively.[^cite_note-Reuters-11][^cite_note-12] In addition, the system could also intercept [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) (UAVs).[^cite_note-Defense_Update-13] Iron Beam will constitute the sixth element of Israel's integrated air defense system,[^cite_note-Reuters-11] in addition to [Arrow 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(Israeli_missile\)), [Arrow 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_3), [David's Sling](18975145.md), [Barak 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_8), and [Iron Dome](18342427.md).[^cite_note-14]

Iron Beam uses a [fiber laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_laser) to destroy an airborne target. Whether acting as a stand-alone system or with external cueing as part of an air-defense system, a threat is detected by a surveillance system and tracked by vehicle platforms in order to engage.[^cite_note-episkopos-15]

Iron Beam is expected to be operational by the end of 2025.[^cite_note-JN_end2025-16][^cite_note-BD_2-3years-17]

### Anti-drone systems



[USS *Preble* (DDG-88)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Preble_\(DDG-88\)) firing her [HELIOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance) laser system, 3 February 2025

In the 21st century, several countries have developed anti-drone laser systems to counter the increasing threat of small unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). These systems are designed to detect, track, and destroy drones using high-powered lasers, offering a cost-effective and flexible solution for airspace protection.

In the United States, Lockheed Martin demonstrated the capabilities of its ATHENA laser system in 2017, which uses a 30-kilowatt ALADIN laser to target and destroy UAVs.[^cite_note-droniv-18] Another American company, Raytheon, developed the High-Energy Laser Weapon System (HELWS) in 2019, which is capable of detecting and destroying drones at a distance of up to three kilometers.[^cite_note-droniv-18]

Turkey has also invested in the development of laser weapons, with companies like Roketsan producing the [ALKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALKA_\(weapon\)) system, which combines laser and electromagnetic weapons to incapacitate and destroy single or group targets.[^cite_note-droniv-18] Other Turkish companies, such as Aselsan and TUBITAK BILGEM, have also demonstrated laser systems capable of targeting small UAVs and explosive devices.[^cite_note-droniv-18]

Germany is another leader in the development of combat laser systems, with defense company Rheinmetall working on stationary and mobile versions of its High Energy Laser (HEL) system since the 2000s.[^cite_note-droniv-18] Rheinmetall's lasers are designed to protect against a variety of threats, including small and medium-sized UAVs, helicopters, missiles, mines, and artillery shells.[^cite_note-droniv-18]

Israel has also been actively developing laser weapons, with companies like Rafael Advanced Defense Systems demonstrating the compact Drone Dome system in 2020, which is designed to destroy UAVs and their swarms.[^cite_note-droniv-18] Another Israeli system, called Light Blade, was developed by OptiDefense to counter terrorist threats such as mini UAVs and explosive devices attached to balloons or kites.[^cite_note-droniv-18]

First announced in December 2024, on April 13 2025, the Ukrainian [Unmanned Systems Forces](76045069.md) released the first footage of a laser weapon system, called “Tryzub”, in use destroying a fibre optic [FPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_view_\(radio_control\)) drone. It is fitted into the back of a van and can be used against ground targets.[^cite_note-19]

On 16 May 2025, Ukraine revealed a small laser turret called SlimBeam, fitted to a [remote controlled weapon station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_controlled_weapon_station), capable of blinding optical sensors at 2 km and destroying drones at 800 meters. It can be remotely operated by a web-based system to reduce the risk to the operators of enemy fire. It could also be used for sabotage by targeting various locks or other objects.[^cite_note-20]

## Electrolaser

Main article: [Electrolaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolaser)

An electrolaser first [ionizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blooming_\(directed-energy_weapon\)) its target path, and then sends an electric current down the conducting track of [ionized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization) plasma, somewhat like [lightning](61344.md). It functions as a giant, high-energy, long-distance version of the [Taser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser) or [stun gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroshock_weapon).

## Pulsed energy projectile

Main article: [Pulsed energy projectile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsed_energy_projectile)

Pulsed Energy Projectile or PEP systems emit an infrared laser pulse which creates rapidly expanding [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_\(physics\)) at the target. The resulting sound, shock and electromagnetic waves stun the target and cause pain and temporary paralysis. The weapon is under development and is intended as a non-lethal weapon in crowd control, though it can also be used as a lethal weapon.

## Dazzler

A [dazzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzler_\(weapon\)) is a directed-energy weapon intended to temporarily blind or disorient its target with intense directed radiation. Targets can include sensors or human vision. Dazzlers emit [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) or invisible light against various electronic sensors, and [visible light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_light) against humans, when they are intended to cause no long-term damage to [eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye). The emitters are usually [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser), making what is termed a *laser dazzler*. Most of the contemporary systems are man-portable, and operate in either the red (a [laser diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_diode)) or green (a [diode-pumped solid-state laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diode-pumped_solid-state_laser), DPSS) areas of the [electromagnetic spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_spectrum).

Initially developed for military use, non-military products are becoming available for use in law enforcement and security.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]



PHASR Rifle

The [personnel halting and stimulation response rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personnel_halting_and_stimulation_response_rifle) (PHASR) is a prototype [non-lethal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-lethal_force) [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser) [dazzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzler_\(weapon\)) developed by the Air Force Research Laboratory's Directed Energy Directorate, U.S. [Department of Defense](7279897.md).[^cite_note-23] Its purpose is to temporarily disorient and blind a target. Blinding laser weapons have been tested in the past, but were banned under the 1995 [United Nations](31769.md) [Protocol on Blinding Laser Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_on_Blinding_Laser_Weapons), which the United States [acceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Convention_on_the_Law_of_Treaties) to on 21 January 2009.[^cite_note-24] The PHASR rifle, a low-intensity laser, is not prohibited under this regulation, as the blinding effect is intended to be temporary. It also uses a two-wavelength laser.[^cite_note-25] The PHASR was tested at [Kirtland Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirtland_Air_Force_Base), part of the [Air Force Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Research_Laboratory) [Directed Energy Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Research_Laboratory#Directorates) in [New Mexico](21649.md).

### Operational use

On 19 November 2025, [British Defence Secretary](302332.md) [John Healey](434625.md), stated that the Russian intelligence-gathering vessel *[Yantar](55295362.md)* had entered the United Kingdom’s wider waters north of Scotland during the previous weeks,[^cite_note-Newsweek-Russia-28][^cite_note-UKDefenceJournal-Russia-29] and was allegedly engaging in espionage and the mapping of UK's [undersea cables](79454.md).[^cite_note-Newsweek-Russia-28][^cite_note-UKDefenceJournal-Russia-29] In response, the UK deployed a Royal Navy [frigate](11464.md) and RAF [P-8 Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft](731169.md) to monitor and track the ship, during which *Yantar* reportedly directed dazzler system lasers at British pilots.[^cite_note-UKDefenceJournal-Russia-29][^cite_note-Newsweek-Russia-28] Healey described the Russian actions as "deeply dangerous", noting that this was the second visit of *Yantar* to UK waters in the same year, and warning that Britain was prepared to respond if the vessel attempted to travel further south.[^cite_note-Newsweek-Russia-28][^cite_note-UKDefenceJournal-Russia-29]

## Problems

See also: [Thermal blooming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_blooming)

Laser beams begin to cause [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_\(physics\)) breakdown in the atmosphere at energy densities of around one [megajoule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule) per cubic centimeter. This effect, called "blooming," causes the laser to defocus and disperse energy into the surrounding air. Blooming can be more severe if there is [fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fog), [smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke), [dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust), [rain](19009110.md), [snow](28191.md), [smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smog), or [foam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foam) in the air.

Techniques that may reduce these effects include:

- Spreading the beam across a large, curved mirror that focuses the power on the target, to keep energy density en route too low for blooming to happen. This requires a large, very precise, fragile mirror, mounted somewhat like a searchlight, requiring bulky machinery to slew the mirror to aim the laser.

- Using a [phased array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phased_array). For typical laser [wavelengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength), this method would require billions of [micrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometre)-size [antennae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_\(radio\)). There is currently no known way to implement these, though [carbon nanotubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_nanotube) have been proposed. Phased arrays could theoretically also perform [phase-conjugate amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_optics) (see below). Phased arrays do not require mirrors or lenses, and can be made flat and thus do not require a turret-like system (as in "spread beam") to be aimed, though range will suffer if the target is at extreme angles to the surface of the phased array.

  [^cite_note-106]

- Using a phase-conjugate laser system. This method employs a "finder" or "guide" laser illuminating the target. Any mirror-like ("specular") points on the target reflect light that is sensed by the weapon's primary amplifier. The weapon then amplifies inverted waves, in a positive feedback loop, destroying the target, with [shockwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_wave) as the specular regions evaporate. This avoids blooming because the waves from the target pass through the blooming, and therefore show the most conductive optical path; this automatically corrects for the distortions caused by blooming. Experimental systems using this method usually use special chemicals to form a "[phase-conjugate mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-conjugate_mirror)". In most systems, however, the mirror overheats dramatically at weapon-useful power levels.

- Using a very short pulse that finishes before blooming interferes, but this requires a very high power laser to concentrate large amounts of energy in that pulse which does not exist in a weaponized or easily weaponizable form.

  [^cite_note-107]

- Focusing multiple lasers of relatively low power on a single target. This is increasingly bulky as the total power of the system increases.

## Countermeasures

Essentially, a laser generates a beam of light which will be delayed or stopped by any [opaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opacity_\(optics\)) medium and perturbed by any [translucent or less than perfectly transparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_and_translucency) medium just like any other type of light. A simple, dense [smoke screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_screen) can and will often block a laser beam. Infrared or multi-spectrum[^cite_note-108] [smoke grenades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_grenades) or generators will also disturb or block [infrared laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_laser) beams. Any opaque case, cowling, bodywork, fuselage, hull, wall, shield or armor will absorb at least the "first impact" of a laser weapon, so the beam must be sustained to achieve penetration.

The Chinese [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) has invested in the development of specialized coatings that can deflect beams fired by U.S. military lasers. Laser light can be deflected, reflected, or absorbed by manipulating physical and chemical properties of materials. Artificial coatings can counter certain specific types of lasers, but a different type of laser may match the coating's [absorption spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_spectrum) enough to transfer damaging amounts of energy. The coatings are made of several different substances, including low-cost metals, [rare earths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths), [carbon fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fiber), silver, and diamonds that have been processed to fine sheens and tailored against specific laser weapons. China is developing anti-laser defenses because protection against them is considered far cheaper than creating competing laser weapons.[^cite_note-109]

Dielectric mirrors, inexpensive ablative coatings, thermal transport delay, and obscurants are also being studied as countermeasures.[^cite_note-110] In not a few operational situations, even simple, passive countermeasures like rapid rotation (which spreads the heat and does not allow a fixed targeting point except in strictly frontal engagements), higher acceleration (which increases the distance and changes the angle quickly), or agile maneuvering during the terminal attack phase (which hampers the ability to target a vulnerable point, forces a constant re-aiming or tracking with close to zero lag, and allows for some cooling) can defeat or help to defeat non-highly pulsed, high-energy laser weapons.[^cite_note-simple-111]

## In popular culture

Main article: [Weapons in science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_in_science_fiction)

[Arthur C. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_C._Clarke) envisaged [particle beam weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_beam_weapon) in his 1955 novel *[Earthlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthlight_\(novel\))*, in which energy would be delivered by high-velocity beams of matter.[^cite_note-112] After the invention of the laser in 1960, it briefly became the death ray of choice for science fiction writers.[^cite_note-Van_Riper_45-113] By the late 1960s and 1970s, as the laser's limits as a weapon became evident, the [ray gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_gun) began to be replaced by similar weapons with names that better reflected the destructive capabilities of the device, such as the *[blaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaster_\(Star_Wars\))* in *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars)* or *[phasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_in_Star_Trek)* in *[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek)*, which were originally lasers.
