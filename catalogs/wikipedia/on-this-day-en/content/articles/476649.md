**Trujillo** (Spanish: [\[tɾuˈxiʝo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_language): *Truhillu*; [Mochica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mochica_language): *Cɥimor*[^cite_note-11]) is a city in coastal northwestern [Peru](170691.md) and the capital of the [Department of La Libertad](714878.md). It is the third most populous city[^cite_note-12] and center of the [third most populous metropolitan area of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_of_Peru). It is located on the banks of the [Moche River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_River), near its mouth at the Pacific Ocean, in the [Moche Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_Valley). This was a site of the great prehistoric [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) and [Chimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimu) cultures[^cite_note-13] before the [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca) conquest and subsequent expansion.

The [Independence of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Trujillo) from Spain was proclaimed in the [Historic Centre of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Trujillo) on December 29, 1820, and the city was honored in 1822 by the [Congress of the Republic of Peru](1571007.md) with the title "Meritorious City and Faithful to the Fatherland",[^cite_note-RPP-14] for its role in the fight for Peruvian independence. Trujillo is the birthplace of Peru's [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary).

In 1823, Riva Agüero settled in Trujillo after being deposed, but his government lacked legal recognition, while the Congress in Lima continued to function and appointed Torre Tagle as the new president.[^cite_note-institutoramon-15] In 1824, to facilitate the campaign for independence, Trujillo was declared the provisional capital of Peru by Bolívar.[^cite_note-decreto1824-16] It was the scene of a [military revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Trujillo_uprising) in 1932. Trujillo is considered the "cradle of liberty and cradle of the judiciary in Peru".[^cite_note-PJ-4]

Trujillo is also known as the "City of Everlasting Spring",[^cite_note-17] is considered the "Capital of the [Marinera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinera)", a traditional dance in Peru, "Cradle of the [Peruvian Paso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_paso) horse",[^cite_note-senamhi.gob.pe-3] as well as the "Capital of Culture of Peru".[^cite_note-Ministerio_Público-1] It has sponsored numerous national and international cultural events, and has a lively arts community. Current festivals include the "[National Marinera Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Marinera_Festival)", the [Trujillo Spring Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Spring_Festival) and the [International Book Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Book_Festival),[^cite_note-Elportavoz-18] which is one of the most important cultural events in the country.[^cite_note-FLT-19]

Trujillo is close to two major [archeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeological) sites of pre-Columbian monuments: [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan), the largest adobe city in the ancient world, designated a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 1986; and the [temples of the Sun and Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_del_Sol) (the largest [adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe) pyramid in Peru).[^cite_note-destinos-20]

The city center contains many examples of colonial and religious architecture, often incorporating distinctive [wrought ironwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrought_iron). It includes residential areas, a central business district, and industrial supply distribution to the various districts. The [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Trujillo) has its seat here. [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is the predominant religion and 10 colonial churches are located within the old city wall, now encircled by [Avenida España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espa%C3%B1a_Avenue_\(Trujillo\)); additional churches in the towns of [Huamán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Huam%C3%A1n), [Huanchaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanchaco) and [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche,_Trujillo) are located within 15 kilometres (9.3 miles) of Trujillo's centre.

Since 2011, the city has been developing the pilot project *Trujillo: Sustainable City*, as part of the platform "Emerging and Sustainable Cities of the [Inter-American Development Bank](382592.md)", in cooperation with the IDB.[^cite_note-Trujillo:_Sustainable_city-BID-21] In 2012 Trujillo was selected by [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) to participate in a "Smarter Cities Challenge" project intended to improve public safety and transportation through technology.[^cite_note-rpp.com.pe-22][^cite_note-larepublica.pe-23]

## Identity

### Capital of culture

Trujillo is considered the "Capital of Culture of Peru"[^cite_note-Ministerio_Público-1] for the prominent writers associated with the city[^cite_note-citymark-24] such as [Cesar Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Vallejo) and [Víctor Raúl Haya de la Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Ra%C3%BAl_Haya_de_la_Torre), and because the city is a center for important cultural expressions as the [marinera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinera) dance, [Peruvian paso horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_paso), [caballitos de totora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballitos_de_totora), Trujillo's gastronomy, etc. The [North Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Group) was formed here, with [Eduardo González Viaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Via%C3%B1a) and [Gerardo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardo_Ch%C3%A1vez) as successors. The city presents important national festivals, such as [Marinera Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Marinera_Festival), [Spring Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Spring_Festival), and competitions for the paso horse and [caballito de totora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballito_de_totora).

### Symbols

#### Coat of arms

The [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Trujillo,_Peru?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the city was awarded on December 7, 1537, by Royal Decree issued by the King of Spain, [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). The shield consists of two columns rising from water, a king's crown on top surrounded by pearls and precious stones and two staffs that surround the letter K (for *Karolus*, [royal cypher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_cypher) of the King); and on the back is a black [griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin) facing right and supporting the shield.

#### Flag

The flag is the coat of arms on a white background; it is carried during the official ceremonies of the [Municipality of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Trujillo) in the main square of the city. Also it is flown every December 29 to commemorate the proclamation of independence of Trujillo in 1820.

#### Anthem

The anthem was written by [Ramiro Mendoza Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramiro_Mendoza_S%C3%A1nchez?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1974 with music composed by [Ramiro Herrera Orbegoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramiro_Herrera_Orbegoso?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1975.[^cite_note-25] The melody and lyrics were subsequently brought together at the Municipal Theater, accompanied by the traditional Band of Musicians of the 32nd Infantry Brigade and the Polyphonic Choir of what was then the [National Institute of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Culture). The city municipality primarily promotes the anthem through official civic ceremonies held in the city, where it is performed by musical bands. The anthem expresses a greeting to and recognition of the city through a celebration of its history.&#x20;

## History

The history of Trujillo has its beginning in ancient times, as the area at the mouth of the Moche River was long a center of successive pre-European cultures. They extended their domains along the northern coast of Peru.[^cite_note-26]

### Pre-Columbian era



Mural of the [Huaca de la Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_de_la_Luna) archeological site in the district of [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_District)



[Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) headdress, c. AD 400

The archaeological history of this region goes back to the early pre-[ceramic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic) period. For example, [Huaca Prieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Prieta) was occupied as early as 4700 BC.

Several ancient cultures developed in this area: the [Cupisnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupisnique), the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) and [Chimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chim%C3%BA_culture). Numerous [archaeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) and monumental remains attest to the high degree of complexity of these civilizations.

Among the Cupisnique culture sites are [Caballo Muerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballo_Muerto) and Huaca Prieta.

The Moche culture sites include [huacas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca): the [Temples of the Sun and Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_del_Sol)[^cite_note-isbn0-8160-2581-9-27] south of the city, the Huaca del Dragón (or Rainbow Huaca) and the Huaca Esmeralda to the north, and others.

The Chimu culture built its primary settlement at what is known as [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan), which was the capital, having an estimated 100,000 people at its peak. It is the largest pre-Columbian city built of [adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe) and has been designated a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md). Its remains are 5 km (3 miles) northwest of the current city center. The present Spanish–Peruvian city of Trujillo was founded in an ancestral territory populated by ancient indigenous civilizations. The Spanish founded new cities expressing their culture in what they called the "[Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru)".

#### Southern Moche civilization

Main article: [Moche (culture)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\))

The Southern Moche civilization flourished in northern Peru with its Huacas del Sol y de la Luna from about AD 100 to 800, during the Regional Development Epoch. The people likely had formed into a group of autonomous polities that shared a common elite culture, as seen in the rich iconography and monumental architecture that survive today. They are particularly noted for their elaborately painted [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic), [gold](12240.md) work, monumental constructions (huacas) and [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) systems.[^cite_note-Bawden-28]

Moche history is broadly divided into three periods – the emergence of the Moche culture in the Early Moche (AD 100–300), its expansion and florescence during the Middle Moche (300–600), and the urban nucleation and subsequent collapse in the Late Moche (500–750).[^cite_note-Bawden-28] Moche society was agriculture-based, and the cultural leaders invested in the construction of a network of [irrigation canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation_canal) for the diversion of river water to supply the crops. Their culture was sophisticated; and their artifacts fully express their lives, including scenes of hunting, fishing, fighting, sacrifice, elaborate ceremonies, and sexual acts.

#### Chimu Empire

Main article: [Chimu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimu_culture)



Nik An Palace of [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan)

The Chimu built and occupied a territory known as [Chimor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimor), with its capital at the city of [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan), a large adobe city in the [Valley of Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Moche) (around which present-day Trujillo city developed). The culture arose about 900 and flourished into the 14th century.

The Chimos were characterized by speaking the [Quingnam language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quingnam_language), one of the languages that is certain to have existed in the ancient province of Trujillo:

> \[A] cacique of what is today called **Trujillo**, called the Chimo \[...] was conquering the Yunga indians, and making the provinces of these plains from Paramonga to Paita and Tumbes tributaries \[...] and with his power he was introducing his native language, which is the one spoken today in the valleys of Trujillo. It was **[Quingnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quingnam_language)**, the language of this lord \[...]&#x20;

— [Antonio de la Calancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_la_Calancha) (1584-1654)[^cite_note-29]

The [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca) ruler [Tupac Inca Yupanqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac_Inca_Yupanqui) led a campaign which conquered the Chimu in around 1470.[^cite_note-30]

This was just 50 years before the arrival of the Spanish in the region. Consequently, Spanish chroniclers recorded accounts of Chimu culture from persons who had lived before the Inca conquest. Similarly, archaeological evidence suggest Chimor emerged from the remnants of Moche culture; early Chimu pottery had some resemblance to that of the Moche. Their ceramics are all-black, and their work in precious metals is very detailed and intricate. In the Late Chimu period, about 12,000 artisans lived and worked in Chan Chan alone.[^cite_note-Moseley,_M._E._1990-31] They engaged in fishing, agriculture, craft work, and trade. Artisans were forbidden to change their profession, and were grouped together in the citadel according to their area of specialization. Archeologists have noted a dramatic rise in the volume of Chimu craft production, which they attribute to artisans having been brought to [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan) from another area taken in conquest.[^cite_note-Moseley,_M._E._1990-31] As there is evidence of both metalwork (generally a male specialty) and [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) (a female art) in the same domestic dwelling, it is likely that both men and women were artisans.[^cite_note-Moseley,_M._E._1990-31] The men engaged in fishing, heavy agriculture (aided by irrigation and earthworks), and metallurgy. The women made ceramics and textiles (from spun and dyed [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [llama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama), [alpaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpaca), and [vicuña wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicu%C3%B1a_wool)). People used [reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_\(plant\)) fishing [canoes](99960.md), hunted, and traded using [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) coins.

In 2018 archaeologists uncovered the world's largest child-sacrifice site at Trujillo. *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic)* discovered the remains of over 140 children and the skeletons of llamas, apparently the result of ritual sacrifice.[^cite_note-32]

### Colonial era

#### Spanish foundation



Royal decree granting the coat of arms.

Main article: [Foundation of Trujillo, Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_Trujillo,_Peru)

Trujillo was one of the first cities in the Americas founded by the [Spanish conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquistadors).[^cite_note-Prescott-33]<sup>: 125 </sup> They arrived in an area that had been inhabited and developed for thousands of years by the [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples). According to historian [Napoleón Cieza Burga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napole%C3%B3n_Cieza_Burga?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) [Diego de Almagro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Almagro) founded the first settlement in November 1534,[^cite_note-laindustria.pe-5] calling it *Trujillo of New Castile* after [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo,_Spain), the home city of [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro). It was founded among four Chimu settlements: Huanchaco, Huamán, Moche and Mampuesto, to create an alliance against the [Incas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incas). On November 23, 1537, King [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of Spain gave the town the rank of 'city' and the [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) that remains a symbol for the city; it was the first city in Peru to receive a coat of arms from the king.[^cite_note-34] By 1544 Trujillo had around 300 homes and 1,000 inhabitants, and an economy booming from the cultivation of [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and other food crops and the raising of livestock.

The Spanish colonists welcomed a diverse array of religious orders from the time of its founding, and there was a boom in church construction in the city during the 16th and 17th centuries. In 1577 [Pope Gregory XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII) created the [Diocese of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Trujillo), and in 1616 construction work commenced on the [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Cathedral).

On February 14, 1619, Trujillo was struck by an [earthquake](10106.md), resulting in the near-total destruction of the city and the deaths of around 400 of its inhabitants. Rebuilding was slow. The people developed a devotion to [Saint Valentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Valentine), on whose day the earthquake hit. The [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) opened a [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) and school for the education and training of priests; they also served as [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionaries) to the indigenous peoples, as they introduced Christianity.

#### 17th century

##### The Wall of Trujillo

Main article: [Wall of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_of_Trujillo)



Map of Trujillo in 1786, by [Baltasar Jaime Martínez Compañón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltasar_Jaime_Mart%C3%ADnez_Compa%C3%B1%C3%B3n), showing the [City Walls of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_of_Trujillo)

Due to the proximity of the city to the sea (about 4 km \[2.5 miles] away) and the danger of attack by [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate) and privateers, the [Wall of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_of_Trujillo) was built for defense during the reign of Viceroy Melchor de Navarra and Rocafull[^cite_note-walloftrujillo-35] and the city mayors [Bartolome Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolome_Martinez) and [Fernando Ramirez Jarabeitia Orellana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Ramirez_Jarabeitia_Orellana?action=edit\&redlink=1). This wall was built by an Italian architect, Giuseppe Formento, who began construction on February 19, 1687. Formento based his design on that by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) for the Italian city of [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence). The wall was designed in an elliptical shape to save costs in its construction, and was completed in 1689. The wall reached a perimeter of 5.5 km (3.4 miles) and used more than 100,000 bricks. The defensive structure was composed of 15 bastions, 15 shades and 5 covered gates.

The [Huamán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huam%C3%A1n) Gate was oriented westward to the road to the village of the same name. The [Mansiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansiche,_La_Libertad,_Peru) Gate was located to the north, giving way to the highway. The Miraflores Gate opened to the east. The Sierra Gate was named after the road leading to this region. Lastly, the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) Gate gave access to people coming from the south. In 1942 the city developed a master plan; following the path of the ancient wall, it built [Avenida España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espa%C3%B1a_Avenue_\(Trujillo\)) to encircle the area now called the Historical Center of Trujillo.

In the latter half of the 17th century, severe [droughts](37335.md) and [pestilence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease) caused a major economic crisis for the city, which depended on agriculture. Trujillo regained prominence in the 18th century, in part due to the destruction of the city of [Saña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B1a,_Peru?action=edit\&redlink=1) by flooding in 1720. Trujillo also suffered from flooding in 1701, 1720, 1728 and 1814; and [earthquakes](10106.md) in 1725 and 1759.

By 1760 an estimated 9,200 people were living in the vicinity of the city. The foundation of the Municipality of Trujillo in 1779 coincided with a peak of prosperity for the city. Numerous undeveloped lots remained within the city walls but Trujillo was regarded as one of the most important cities in Northern Peru during the colonial era.

### Independence



Independence Street in front of Main Square, at right *El Libertador Hotel*



Architecture of [Paseo Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paseo_Pizarro), one of the busiest streets in the historic center of Trujillo. In the background is the historic [Plazuela El Recreo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazuela_El_Recreo), with tall trees forming the skyline in that part of the city.

Inspired by liberal ideas from members of its educational institutions, Trujillo became a principal centre of Peruvian republican sentiments. Led by the city mayor and [intendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendant) [José Bernardo de Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bernardo_de_Tagle), the [Intendancy of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendancy_of_Trujillo) declared its independence from Spain on December 29, 1820.

Between 1821 and 1825 the Trujillo region was the only stable and productive land within the nascent republic. In 1823, in response to the counterattack by royalist troops who took the city of Lima, the Congress, convened in Callao, decreed on June 21 to move the seat of government to Trujillo. However, just a few days later, on June 23, the Congress removed José de la Riva Agüero from the office of President of the Republic. Upon learning of these events, Riva Agüero embarked for Trujillo along with his ministers and part of the deputies. Once in the city, on June 26, he established his government seat, dissolved the Congress, and created a Senate of ten members. From there, he governed de facto until August 6, 1823.

On March 26, 1824, the Superior Court of the North (now the Superior Court of Justice of La Libertad), the first Superior Court of Justice in Peru, was founded. In the same year, the city welcomed the liberating army of Simón Bolívar, who assumed the government of the country and established in Trujillo the national accounts, the official gazette, and the general staff, on March 8, 1824. Later, by decree of March 26, 1824, Trujillo was designated as the provisional capital of the republic, while Lima was liberated.

The years following the revolution saw the growth in the economic influence of the city, compensating for a loss of political power to [Lima](85423.md) when it was designated as the capital, which instead suffered from the resulting political turmoil. The [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Moche) and [Chicama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicama_Valley) valleys emerged as new economic enclaves for the [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane) industry. Land was increasingly concentrated in large estates and a new "agricultural aristocracy" developed that was linked to and influenced national political power. The policy of free trade and openness to foreign investment attracted an influx of Europeans, principally from [Britain](31717.md) and [Germany](11867.md). By then, Trujillo had a population of 15,000 and began to grow beyond the city walls. New architectural styles were adopted, influenced by [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Romanticism) and [English Romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Romanticism).

During the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) against [Chile](5489.md) between 1879 and 1883, Trujillo contributed troops towards national defence. Although never a site of battle, Trujillo suffered from occupation by Chilean troops and their plundering of the surrounding countryside.

#### First Independent City of Peru

It is considered the "First Independent City of Peru" for three reasons: it proclaimed independence from Spain on December 24, 1820[^cite_note-RB-36] at the historical "Casa de la Emancipación" (House of Emancipation). Its leaders signed the declaration of independence at the Seminario de San Carlos y San Marcelo and proclaimed independence to an open council meeting in the Plaza de Armas, on December 29, 1820. Finally, on January 6, 1821, its leaders ratified the agreement and the proclamation of the independence of this city, as stated in the document called *Libro rojo* (the Red Book) of the Trujillo council.

Their actions gained independence for almost all of northern Peru, because the government of Trujillo city ruled what is now the regions of Tumbes, Piura, Lambayeque, La Libertad, Cajamarca, San Martín and Amazonas. Marquis of Torre Tagle said, "My people. From this time for the unanimous will of the people, Trujillo is free. I put our fate and that of people under the protection of Heaven! Long live the homeland! Long live independence!"[^cite_note-RB-36][^cite_note-37]

### Republican era



Architecture of the 18th and 19th centuries, Plaza de Armas

The Provisional Regulations given by General San Martín in 1821 created the Department of Trujillo, based on colonial administration. This status was acknowledged in the first [Constitution of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Peru) in 1823. Because of its size and economic wealth, the Department of Trujillo between 1821 and 1825 was the only stable and productive area that could organize and lead the nascent republic. The department encompassed nearly half the country. For the efforts of its people in the war of emancipation, San Martín gave it the title of "Meritorious City Loyal to the Homeland". The municipality of the city, then called a *[cabildo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabildo_\(council\))*, was given the rank of "honorable".

In 1823, after the creation of the Republic of Peru, the protectorate of José de San Martín was developed. Before royal troops took the city of Lima, the first President of Peru, Don [José de la Riva Agüero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_la_Riva_Ag%C3%BCero), together with Sánchez Carrión, named Trujillo as provisional capital of the country, which survived for a short period.

In 1824 the city received the liberation army of [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar). Taking over the government of the country, he established a temporary government of the country in Trujillo, on March 8, 1824.

In 1821 the Court of Appeals was created to replace the Royal Court. Its jurisdiction extended over the present departments of Cajamarca, Piura, Lambayeque, Amazonas (then known as the Chachapoyas), and Huamachuco (then known as Sánchez Carrión). On March 26, 1824 [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) established Trujillo's first Superior Court of Justice as the Northern Superior Court. It had been the first high court established in the Republic of Peru with the powers of the Supreme Court.

#### 19th century

By the end of the 19th century the five entrances had disappeared from the city, so it was that during this time the [Wall of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_of_Trujillo) was torn down and allowed the growth of the city. This urban expansion allowed the establishment of the neighborhoods of Chicago, La Unión and Pedro Muñiz. During the administration of Don Víctor Larco Herrera as mayor, the city began upgrading works such as the construction of City Hall, the arrangement and embellishment of the Plaza de Armas and the atrium of the cathedral. It also built the road to the resort of [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires,_Trujillo), which expanded the city urban planning perspective. Another project was the renovation of the Municipal Theater.

#### 20th century



Trujillo rail station in the early 20th century

In July 1932, Trujillo was once again at the centre of one of the most important episodes in the history of the Republic of Peru, the [1932 Trujillo uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Trujillo_uprising), which cost the lives of many citizens. Although this year came to be known as the "year of barbarism", it would also mark the political identity of the city during the second half of the 20th century.

The latter half of the 20th century saw the expansion of the city due to a combination of rural-to-urban migration and the consolidation of surrounding districts into the [Trujillo metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_metropolitan_area_\(Peru\)).

From 1980 Trujillo took on the aspect and behavior of a particularly dynamic [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), by which time the growth of the city and adjoining districts had produced a single metropolitan area, so in the 1980s the nascent Trujillo metropolitan area consisted of the integrated urban districts of [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_District), [El Porvenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Porvenir_District,_Trujillo) and [Florencia de Mora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florencia_de_Mora_District), with Víctor Larco Herrera and La Esperanza remaining discontinuous districts. In 1981 the city had 403,337 inhabitants. Also in the 1980s the Trujillo Industrial Park project was begun, located on the north side of the city, in the present La Esperanza district.

With the advent of the 1990s, the city of Trujillo was unified with the districts of [La Esperanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Esperanza_District,_Trujillo) and [Victor Larco Herrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Larco_Herrera,_Peru) and the spread of the city resulted in the districts of [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_District), [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_District), [Salaverry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaverry_District), and [Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laredo_District) becoming part of the metropolitan area. In the first half of the 1990s, after the emergence of the El Milagro area in [Huanchaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanchaco_District), it was joined to La Esperanza district, and increasing interdependence with the districts of Moche and Laredo, which was cemented Trujillo as a new [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis) of Peru, then with a population of 589,314 inhabitants.

#### 21st century

##### Modernization of the city



[2013 Bolivarian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Bolivarian_Games) opening ceremony, held in Trujillo

With the experience of the last two decades of the 20th century, when the city experienced an excessive disordered growth while increasing the costs for providing basic services to the population, the Provincial Municipality of Trujillo created the Trujillo Metropolitan Development Plan 2010, known as "Plandemetru", which was approved by city ordinance on November 30, 1995. Through this was planned the growth and development of the city up to 2010; later the lines of development of the metropolis were governed by the "Strategic Plan for Integral Development and Sustainable Trujillo till 2015" containing general guidelines for the development of the city by 2015.

##### Sustainable and smart city

In November 2010, Trujillo was the first city in Latin America and the [Caribbean](18956035.md) to be chosen by the [Inter-American Development Bank](382592.md) (IDB) to develop the pilot "Sustainable City" project as part of the platform "Emerging and Sustainable Cities of the Inter-American Development Bank".[^cite_note-Trujillo:_Sustainable_city-BID-21] This project includes a plan of action on [climate change](5042951.md), which will be held on emissions limits in Trujillo, and will review the list of investment projects with respect to climate sustainability. According to IDB representative Fidel Jaramillo, Trujillo was chosen as the first driver of progress in Latin America to develop a new initiative. Trujillo's plan is to conceive from other perspectives such as fiscal and financial sustainability, which is basic, but also from environmental sustainability and quality of life. The IDB is developing relevant projects in coordination with the Provincial Municipality of Trujillo.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In 2012 Trujillo began to develop, with the support of American technology corporation [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), the "Smart City" project, which will try to focus technologically on the two problems of public safety and transportation.[^cite_note-rpp.com.pe-22][^cite_note-larepublica.pe-23]

## Geography

### Location

Trujillo is located at an altitude of 34 metres (112 feet) on a coastal strip in the west of the province of Trujillo, in the old valley of [Chimor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimor) today known as the Moche or Santa Catalina Valley. Its main square is located at [8°6′3″S 79°1′34″W  /  8.10083°S 79.02611°W  / -8.10083; -79.02611](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Trujillo,_Peru\&params=8_6_3_S_79_1_34_W_) longitude at an altitude of 31.16 metres (102.23 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level) and lies 4.40 kilometres (2.73 miles) inland from the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), in a straight line along Avenido Larco.[^cite_note-40]

### Climate



Panoramic view of Trujillo

This city has a [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) (*BWh*, according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)) and it is known as *La Ciudad de la Eterna Primavera* (city of everlasting spring) because of its sunny and pleasant weather year-round. The *International Spring Festival* in early October attracts visitors from all over Peru and the world. The city is in an area of mild climate and low rainfall, with moderate temperatures ranging between 14 and 30 °C (57 and 86 °F) due to the [Humboldt Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Current).[^cite_note-41] Trujillo has a warm climate during the day and mild during the night due to the [sea breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_breeze). It has an average temperature of 18 °C (64 °F), and the extreme minimum and maximum temperatures fluctuate between 17 and 28 °C (63 and 82 °F) in winter and summer, respectively. Rains are light, sporadic and occur during the afternoon or evening. The [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) and their foothills are very close to the coast, and having a lower elevation relative to the mountains of central and southern Peru, the flow of moist air from the [Amazon region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_region), which converges with the sea breezes from the west, favors during the summer a higher frequency of light showers. According to the climate classification of [Thornthwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._W._Thornthwaite), city of Trujillo would correspond to an arid climate type with no rain during all seasons.[^cite_note-42]

The parts of the city closest to the sea experience [haze](998156.md) during the morning and usually the temperature is lower than in the central and upper parts of the city. However, during the phenomenon of [El Niño](317311.md) the climate varies, mainly the rainfall, with less intensity than in regions located north of the city, and the temperature can also be lifted.

### Hydrography

Trujillo is crossed by the [Moche River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_River) that passes to the south of the city; its waters have been used since ancient times by the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) and [Chimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimu) who inhabited this area, who took its waters to irrigate their fields; today the river is part of the [countryside of Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countryside_of_Moche) and its waters continue to be used for irrigation. The river empties into the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) right on the boundary between the districts of [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_District) and [Víctor Larco Herrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Larco_Herrera,_Peru).

### Physiography

Trujillo is set on a coastal plain of the [La Libertad Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Libertad_Region) and has a gentle topography[^cite_note-47] with hilly terrain, sitting on a [plateau](538005.md) of the [Trujillo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Province,_Peru). The low-lying areas of the city are very close to the Pacific Ocean and the higher elevations are close to the Andean foothills.

## Economy



According to a Study of Urban Buildings Market, between 2006 and May 2012 construction activities in Trujillo grew 500%.

[^cite_note-59]

In the 19th century, the city of Trujillo greatly expanded due to extensive irrigated agriculture, with high production and profits from the [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) industry. Today, [asparagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asparagus) and shoes are some of the main products of its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area). The irrigated lands of the Moche River Valley produce sugarcane, rice, and asparagus. Industries in the city include the sugar refineries, knitting mills, breweries and the shoe industry. Among the internationally known products of Trujillo, asparagus is exported to neighboring countries, Europe and the United States. The areas around Trujillo are among the largest exporters of white asparagus in the world. [Peru](170691.md) is the world's leading exporter of asparagus, followed by China and [Mexico](3966054.md).[^cite_note-60]

Trujillo is the most important economic center of northern Peru; it is an inland commercial and [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation) center for the surrounding farming areas. Its numerous shopping malls, supermarkets, department stores, and similar amenities make Trujillo a modern city.

In recent years another important economic sector in the city is the construction industry. According to statistical information provided by the "Institute of Construction and Development of the Peruvian Chamber of Construction" between 2006 and May 2012 construction activity in Trujillo grew by 500%.[^cite_note-61] The study reveals that in 2006 were built 92770 m2 and the first months of 2012 the figure rose to 437440 m2, mainly due to the increased amount of square meters per house, likewise states that the total built in so far this year, 84% is housing construction.

## Government

### Regional government



Trujillo City Hall

Trujillo is the capital of [La Libertad region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Libertad_region) as such is the seat of regional government and its technical organs of government line as are the regional offices of the scope of its jurisdiction, so the regional policies of this government are deployed from this city for use in its territory corresponding. The city also hosts various regional directorates of the [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)) that make up the country's public administration. Trujillo is also headquarters of the III-Territorial Police Directorate, as executing agency for the National Police of Peru.[^cite_note-63]

### Local government

The city, capital of La Libertad Region and the [Province of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Province,_Peru), is governed by the Provincial [Municipality of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Trujillo), which is governed by the provisions of the organic law of municipalities[^cite_note-64] and that it has jurisdiction throughout the province . There is limited authority to the city and there is no governing body of the city itself, in that sense, the municipalities of the metropolitan districts that make up the city have jurisdiction in matters relating to their own districts.

### Political system

The city is governed by a provincial mayor elected by popular vote every four years. The mayor is responsible for the municipal public administration and community, is the political representative of the municipality of the city and has political influences at the provincial level, so the guidelines of their policies are aimed primarily at the territorial level.

### Judicial system

Trujillo is home to the Superior Court of La Libertad, that was the first Superior Court of Justice established in the country during the government of [Simon Bolivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Bolivar) on March 26, 1824, under the name of Superior Court of North.[^cite_note-65] Is the governing body of the Judicial District of La Libertad. According to Peru's judicial system. The city has the highest judicial burden of the region La Libertad.

## Tourism



[Huanchaco Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanchaco), view of the sea with its typical [caballitos de totora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballitos_de_totora)



[Marinera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinera) dancers in Trujillo festival, celebrated in January of every year

Tourism is a major industry in Trujillo due to the city's proximity to important sites where the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) and [Chimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimu) civilizations evolved. These civilizations had highly skilled artisans, and many of their artifacts having been found during [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) digs in the city. Nearby ruins include the Chimu adobe city of Chan Chan, the world's largest city built from that material. It is sometimes called *Ciudad de la Luna* (City of the Moon) because the people worshipped the moon; or *de las Largas Murallas* (of the Long Walls). In size and complexity, it has been compared with [Teotihuacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teotihuacan) in Mexico, and the ancient cities of [Egypt](8087628.md). Other nearby ruins are the Moche ruins of *[Huaca del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_del_Sol)*, *[Huaca de la Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_de_la_Luna)*, *[Huaca del Dragón o Arco Iris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_del_Drag%C3%B3n)*, *[Huaca Esmeralda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Esmeralda)* and *[El Brujo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Brujo)*.

Trujillo aspires to be designated a [World Heritage Site](44940.md), because of the proximity of both cultures and its historical colonial city centre, whose historic *casonas* (mansions) attract many visitors. The mansions and manors of Trujillo are distinguished for their solemn and austere façades. Inside, their halls are overflowing with ornaments.

Trujillo's [wrought-iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrought-iron) window railings are a unique feature of the mansions. The House of Ganoza-Chopitea (*casa Ganoza*) has a polychromatic front in the [baroque style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_style), crowned by a [rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) frontispiece and two lions. It is the city's most representative example of *casonas* architecture. Another is the House of Mayorazgo, which was built in the early years of the city and holds one of [Peru](170691.md)'s greatest [numismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numismatic) collections. The revolutionary leader [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) lived in a house on the Plaza de Armas.

[Huanchaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanchaco) beach, a surfing destination, is located just north of Trujillo.

Trujillo's restaurants offer a wide variety of local food, such as *[shambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shambar)*, mostly served on Mondays; *[ceviche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceviche)*, *[sopa teologa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopa_teologa)* and *[cabrito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabrito)*.

- **Museo del Juguete** (Toy Museum)

Located a few blocks from the Plaza de Armas with its coffee bar is one of the most splendid of the city and unique in the country, owned by renowned painter Gerardo Chávez, here you can find toys to mid-20th-century.

- **Museo de Arte Moderno** (Museum of Modern Art)

Another museum belonging to the painter Gerardo Chávez, is located in the urbanization *Semirustica El Bosque*, the museum displays works of prominent artists, both national and foreign, and sculptures but also find a coffee bar and souvenir sales, is the first museum of modern art in Peru.

- **Museo Casa de la Emancipación** (Emancipation House Museum)

Is considered as a Civic Sanctuary of the city: here the Marquis Torre Tagle conceived the independence of Trujillo in 1820. Also here was Hosted the First Constitutional Congress and the Government Palace with Riva Agüero. Nowadays it hosts cultural exhibitions. It is located on the corner of *Jiron Gamarra with Jiron Pizarro* streets; is a traditional cultural center for excellence in Trujillo, here are art exhibitions and special ceremonies are performed in the central courtyard. With a well-restored house belonging to Banco Continental, is a must for all tourists seeking culture in Trujillo.

- **Museo Huacas de Moche** (Museum of Moche Temples)

Located at the foot of the Huaca de la Luna in the [Moche District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_District), this modern museum was opened in 2010 and it shows the recent archaeological discoveries of the Moche ceremonial religious center. Next to the Mochica monuments is a great touristic circuit for not stop visiting in Trujillo.

- **Chan Chan Museum**

The museum is located at the foot of [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan), the largest mud city in Latin America are shown in the most important findings found in the Chimu city as well as studies on political and religious division.

Trujillo city has many national and international festivals. Festivals and events occurring regularly include:

- **[Marinera Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Marinera_Festival)**

A festival of typical dance is very representative of the city, the national competition is organized by the [Club Libertad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertad_de_Trujillo) and takes place the last week of January, couples of dancers from different parts of the country and the world are prepared every year for contest the top of the different categories of competition that draws thousands of tourists every year. It also highlights the *marinera parade* also with the participation of [Peruvian paso horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Paso) and typical riders called chalanes through the main streets of the historic center.

- **[Trujillo Spring Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Spring_Festival)** (Spring International Festival)

Is considered by some as the most representative festival of the city that lives up to the nickname he carries. The festival is one of the most important in Peru and is done in early October of each year, by the Lions Club of La Libertad Region. The first festival was held in 1950, and has been held annually since. The flower festival has a rich and varied program of over a hundred activities to meet the tastes and interests of Trujillo people and thousands of domestic and foreign tourists. The activities are carried out for a month in which the city takes on a festive environment, thousands of domestic and foreign tourists arriving in the town for various events like the coronation of the Queen of Spring, competition horses step, the parade of foreign queens and Spring Corso through the main avenues of the city, where visitors revel in the maneuvers of the [Guaripolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaripola). The festival closes with the spring parade or *corso* and a private party organized by the Lions Club.

- **[San Jose Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Festival)**

Held in the resort of [Las Delicias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Delicias,_Trujillo) in the district of Moche on March 14, 15 and 16, is a feast day and it has been a tradition with a strong Spanish influence, which are enjoyed various activities for adults, youth and children, party hosts are Don Jose and Dona Josefa and Ms Maja, the event begins with the description of characters, activities, bars, flamenco dancing, etc. This festival is accompanied by a procession of the patron Saint Joseph, the fashion show, the bullfight, the parade of characters, and toromatch pamplonada in which involved several teams from other departments. Some houses are become in Spanish bars decorated with motifs like flags, grimaldas and posters.[^cite_note-72]

- **[Contest of Peruvian Paso Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_of_Paso_Horses_in_Trujillo)**

Trujillo is considered *cradle of [Peruvian paso horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Paso)* and in the city there are contests organized by the [Association of Breeders and Owners of Paso Horses in La Libertad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Breeders_and_Owners_of_Paso_Horses_in_La_Libertad?action=edit\&redlink=1), the best known and most important are The National Competition Paso Horses being done within the framework of the International Spring Festival made between September and October[^cite_note-73] and in the Festival and International Competition of Marinera in January.[^cite_note-74] Peruvian government has declared this kind of horses as Nation's cultural heritage.[^cite_note-75]

### Gastronomy



[Cebiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebiche), typical dish of Trujillo city, *National Cultural Heritage by the Peruvian government*



Shambar is a soup, typical dish of Trujillo city

Trujillo's [gastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomy) is based on fish, shellfish, seaweed, birds, and livestock.

Among the most representative dishes include:

- **[Cebiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebiche)**: Similar dishes have been eaten for at least 2000 years, including in the ancient [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) culture

  [^cite_note-86]

  &#x20;which had its capital south of the city of Trujillo. The dish is prepared using five basic ingredients: chunks of fish fillet with lemon, onion, salt and chili or chili Moche.

  [^cite_note-87]

  [^cite_note-88]

  &#x20;Ingredients are added to taste, with one result being mixed cebiche. Fish used include both freshwater and sea species, as well as other seafood such as shellfish and seaweed and sometimes vegetables. The dish is accompanied by products such as sweet potatoes, boiled corn, cassava, lettuce leaves, and roasted corn. This dish has been declared National Cultural Heritage by the Peruvian government.

  [^cite_note-89]

- **[Shambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shambar)**, soup made with beans also includes smoked ham. Served with roasted corn. In restaurants traditionally served on Mondays.

- **[Theologian soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theologian_soup?action=edit\&redlink=1)**: broth turkey and / or chicken with soaked bread, potatoes, milk and cheese, is traditionally prepared in the district of Moche.

- **Beans to the Trujillo**: black beans with sesame seeds and chili mirasol.

- **Pepián of turkey**: turkey stew with rice, ground corn, cilantro and chili.

- **Trujillo fish**: steamed fish with eggs and onion sauce.

- **Mollejitas to the sillao**: dish served with onion salad and boiled yucc.

  [^cite_note-90]

  [^cite_note-91]

- **[The alfajores of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfajor_de_Trujillo)**

In the city of Trujillo is typical the manufacturing and consumption of sweets and a series of traditional alfajores; formerly called Alfajor of Trujillo that has been manufactured by various candy stores being the best known Dulcería Castañeda, this candy store has become a traditional brand of alfajores in the city; since 1925 they have made alfajores and various giant named alfajor king kong formerly known as "Alfajor of Trujillo", "Dulcería Castañeda" currently has several locals. Its main products are their alfajores and which are requested as classics sweet souvenirs of the city of the everlasting spring.

### Maritime

The port town of Salaverry located at southwest Trujillo city is one of the most commercial ports of Peru. Salaverry port is located some 258 [nautical miles](21289.md) (478 kilometres; 297 miles) north of Callao. Its good linking with Trujillo (12 km (7.5 mi)) and the Panamerican Highway (8 km (5.0 mi)) makes the port of easy access by road to the shippers and receivers (mainly fishmeal, fertilizers, mineral concentrates, rice and sugar) located within its influence area. This port has great commercial activity due to agricultural exports. Another port town is [Puerto Chicama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Chicama) (Puerto Malabrigo) are used for maritime connection. with the world.

## Parks and green zones

- **The Mansiche Alameda**, located in the historic "old entrance of Mansiche" that was part of the ancient city wall, in the historic area of the monumental center of Trujillo. The place has a set of very old trees and is the venue for cultural presentations.

  [^cite_note-107]

- [Trujillo Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Botanical_Garden), located on America Sur Avenue near the Larco roundabout, just south of the Historic Center of Trujillo. It houses a large number of different plants of the coastal, Andes highlands and jungle regions. This botanical garden preserves species such as "pallar mochero" (a variety of Lima Bean), several cotton varieties, and many other plants. The garden also houses a great variety of birds that have taken residence, attracted by the floral diversity.

  [^cite_note-108]

- **[Swamps of Huanchaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamps_of_Huanchaco)**



Swamps of [Huanchaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanchaco) is an ecological [Chimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimu) [reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve), about 14 km (9 mi) northwest of the *historic center* of Trujillo city, [Peru](170691.md). From this ecological reserve the ancient [mochica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) extracted the raw material for the manufacture of the legendary [Caballitos de totora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballitos_de_totora) used since the time of the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) for fishing. It is seen very close the waves of the ocean and far away the mythic town of Huanchaco.

Also known as Wetlands of Huanchaco is an ecological Chimu reserve located in Huanchaco Beach, about 14 kilometres (8.7 miles) northwest of Trujillo city, Peru. From this ecological reserve the ancient mochica extracted the raw material for the manufacture of the ancient Caballitos de totora used since the time of the Moche for fishing. Currently Huanchaco fishermen still use their materials from this swamps to make their ships of work.
