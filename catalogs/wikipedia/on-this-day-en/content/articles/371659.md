**Ras**, from "**Rat sarcoma virus**", is a [family of related proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_family) that are expressed in all animal cell lineages and organs. All Ras protein family members belong to a class of protein called [small GTPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_GTPase), and are involved in transmitting signals within cells ([cellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_\(biology\)) [signal transduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_transduction)). Ras is the prototypical member of the [Ras superfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_superfamily) of proteins, which are all related in three-dimensional structure and regulate diverse cell behaviours.

When Ras is 'switched on' by incoming signals, it subsequently switches on other proteins, which ultimately turn on genes involved in [cell growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_growth), [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiation_\(cellular\)), and [survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis). Mutations in Ras genes can lead to the production of permanently activated Ras proteins, which can cause unintended and overactive signaling inside the cell, even in the absence of incoming signals.

Because these signals result in cell growth and division, overactive Ras signaling can ultimately lead to [cancer](105219.md).[^cite_note-pmid10394594-1] The three Ras genes in humans (*[HRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRAS)*, *[KRAS](9065739.md)*, and *[NRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroblastoma_RAS_viral_oncogene_homolog)*) are the most common [oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene) in human cancer; mutations that permanently activate Ras are found in 20–25% of all human tumors and up to 90% in certain types of cancer (e.g., [pancreatic cancer](363559.md)).[^cite_note-pmid12509763-2] For this reason, Ras inhibitors are being studied as a treatment for cancer and other diseases with Ras overexpression.

## History

The first two Ras genes, *[HRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRAS)* and *[KRAS](9065739.md)*, were identified[^cite_note-pmid12778136-3] from studies of two cancer-causing viruses of the *[Gammaretrovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammaretrovirus)* genus, the Harvey sarcoma virus and Kirsten sarcoma virus, by [Edward Scolnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Scolnick) and colleagues at the National Institutes of Health (NIH).[^cite_note-pmid6289320-4] These viruses were discovered originally in rats during the 1960s by Jennifer Harvey[^cite_note-pmid14243400-5] and [Werner H. Kirsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_H._Kirsten),[^cite_note-pmid5538357-6] respectively, hence the name Rat [sarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcoma).[^cite_note-pmid12778136-3] In 1982, activated and transforming human *ras* genes were discovered in human cancer cells by [Geoffrey M. Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_M._Cooper) at Harvard,[^cite_note-pmid6285471-7] [Mariano Barbacid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Barbacid) and [Stuart A. Aaronson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_A._Aaronson) at the NIH,[^cite_note-pmid6283384-8] [Robert Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Weinberg_\(biologist\)) at MIT,[^cite_note-pmid6283357-9] and [Michael Wigler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Wigler) at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory.[^cite_note-pmid7177195-10] A third *ras* gene was subsequently discovered by researchers in the group of [Robin Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Weiss) at the [Institute of Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Cancer_Research),[^cite_note-pmid6287287-11][^cite_note-pmid6304521-12] and [Michael Wigler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Wigler) at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory,[^cite_note-pmid6300838-13] named *[NRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroblastoma_RAS_viral_oncogene_homolog)*, for its initial identification in human neuroblastoma cells.

The three human *ras* genes encode extremely similar proteins made up of chains of 188 to 189 amino acids. Their gene symbols are HRAS, NRAS and KRAS, the latter of which produces the K-Ras4A and K-Ras4B isoforms from alternative [splicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_splicing).[^cite_note-14]

## Structure



[HRas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRAS) structure [PDB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_Data_Bank) 121p, ribbon showing strands in purple, helices in aqua, loops in gray. Also shown are the bound GTP analog and magnesium ion.

Ras contains six [beta strands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_strand) and five [alpha helices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_helices).[^cite_note-pmid11701921-15] It consists of two domains: a G domain of 166 [amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid) (about 20 kDa) that binds guanosine nucleotides, and a C-terminal membrane targeting region (CAAX-COOH, also known as [CAAX box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAAX_box)), which is lipid-modified by [farnesyl transferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnesyl_transferase), [RCE1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCE1), and [ICMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICMT)[^cite_note-16]

The G domain contains five G motifs that bind GDP/GTP directly. The G1 motif, or the P-loop, binds the beta phosphate of GDP and GTP. The G2 motif, also called Switch I or SW1, contains threonine35, which binds the terminal phosphate (γ-phosphate) of GTP and the divalent magnesium ion bound in the active site. The G3 motif, also called Switch II or SW2, has a DXXGQ motif. The D is aspartate57, which is specific for guanine versus adenine binding, and Q is glutamine61, the crucial residue that activates a catalytic water molecule for hydrolysis of GTP to GDP. The G4 motif contains a LVGNKxDL motif, and provides specific interaction to guanine. The G5 motif contains a SAK consensus sequence. The A is alanine146, which provides specificity for guanine rather than adenine.

The two switch motifs, G2 (SW1) and G3 (SW2), are the main parts of the protein that move when GTP is hydrolyzed into GDP. This conformational change by the two switch motifs is what mediates the basic functionality as a molecular switch protein. This GTP-bound state of Ras is the "on" state, and the GDP-bound state is the "off" state. The two switch motifs have a number of conformations when binding GTP or GDP or no nucleotide (when bound to SOS1, which releases the nucleotide).[^cite_note-RasCore-17]

Ras also binds a [magnesium ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium_in_biology) which helps to coordinate nucleotide binding.

## Function



Overview of signal transduction pathways involved in [apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis)

Ras proteins function as binary molecular switches that control intracellular signaling networks. Ras-regulated [signal pathways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_transduction) control such processes as actin [cytoskeletal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeletal) integrity, [cell proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_proliferation), [cell differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_differentiation), [cell adhesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_adhesion), [apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis), and [cell migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_migration). Ras and Ras-related proteins are often deregulated in cancers, leading to increased invasion and [metastasis](152509.md), and decreased apoptosis.

Ras activates several pathways, of which the [mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase cascade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAPK/ERK_pathway) has been well-studied. This cascade transmits signals downstream and results in the [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_\(genetics\)) of genes involved in cell growth and division.[^cite_note-Lodish-18] Another Ras-activated signaling pathway is the [PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PI3K/AKT/mTOR_pathway), which stimulates protein synthesis, cellular migration and growth, and inhibits apoptosis.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Activation and deactivation

Ras is a [guanosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanosine)-[nucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide)-binding protein. Specifically, it is a single-subunit [small GTPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_GTPase), which is related in structure to the G<sub>α</sub> subunit of [heterotrimeric G proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotrimeric_G_proteins) (large GTPases). G proteins function as binary signaling switches with "on" and "off" states. In the "off" state it is bound to the nucleotide [guanosine diphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanosine_diphosphate) (GDP), while in the "on" state, Ras is bound to [guanosine triphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanosine_triphosphate) (GTP), which has an extra [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) group as compared to GDP. This extra phosphate holds the two switch regions in a "loaded-spring" configuration (specifically the Thr-35 and Gly-60). When released, the switch regions relax which causes a [conformational change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformational_change) into the inactive state. Hence, activation and deactivation of Ras and other small G proteins are controlled by cycling between the active GTP-bound and inactive GDP-bound forms.

The process of exchanging the bound nucleotide is facilitated by [guanine nucleotide exchange factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanine_nucleotide_exchange_factor) (GEFs) and [GTPase activating proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTPase_activating_proteins) (GAPs). As per its classification, Ras has an intrinsic [GTPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTPase) activity, which means that the protein on its own will [hydrolyze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolyze) a bound GTP molecule into GDP. However this process is too slow for efficient function, and hence the GAP for Ras, RasGAP, may bind to and stabilize the catalytic machinery of Ras, supplying additional [catalytic residues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_residue) ("[arginine finger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arginine_finger)") such that a water molecule is optimally positioned for [nucleophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleophilic) attack on the gamma-phosphate of GTP. An inorganic phosphate is released and the Ras molecule is now bound to a GDP. Since the GDP-bound form is "off" or "inactive" for signaling, GTPase Activating Protein *inactivates* Ras by activating its GTPase activity. Thus, GAPs accelerate Ras *inactivation*.

GEFs catalyze a "push and pull" reaction which releases GDP from Ras. They insert close to the P-loop and magnesium [cation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cation) binding site and inhibit the interaction of these with the gamma phosphate [anion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anion). Acidic (negative) residues in switch II "pull" a lysine in the P-loop away from the GDP which "pushes" switch I away from the guanine. The contacts holding GDP in place are broken and it is released into the cytoplasm. Because intracellular GTP is abundant relative to GDP (approximately 10 fold more)[^cite_note-Lodish-18] GTP predominantly re-enters the nucleotide binding pocket of Ras and reloads the spring. Thus GEFs facilitate Ras *activation*.[^cite_note-pmid11701921-15] Well known GEFs include [Son of Sevenless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_of_Sevenless) (Sos) and [cdc25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cdc25) which include the [RasGEF domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RasGEF_domain).

The balance between GEF and GAP activity determines the guanine nucleotide status of Ras, thereby regulating Ras activity.

In the GTP-bound conformation, Ras has a high affinity for numerous [effectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effector_\(biology\)) which allow it to carry out its functions. These include [PI3K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PI3K). Other small GTPases may bind adaptors such as [arfaptin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arfaptin) or second messenger systems such as [adenylyl cyclase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenylyl_cyclase). The Ras binding domain is found in many effectors and invariably binds to one of the switch regions, because these change conformation between the active and inactive forms. However, they may also bind to the rest of the protein surface.

Other proteins exist that may change the activity of Ras family proteins. One example is GDI (GDP Disassociation Inhibitor). These function by slowing the exchange of GDP for GTP, thus prolonging the inactive state of Ras family members. Other proteins that augment this cycle may exist.

### Membrane attachment

Ras is attached to the [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane) owing to its [prenylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenylation) and [palmitoylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmitoylation) ([HRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRAS) and [NRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroblastoma_RAS_viral_oncogene_homolog)) or the combination of prenylation and a polybasic sequence adjacent to the prenylation site ([KRAS](9065739.md)). The C-terminal [CaaX box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CaaX_box) of Ras first gets farnesylated at its Cys residue in the cytosol, allowing Ras to loosely insert into the membrane of the [endoplasmatic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmatic_reticulum) and other cellular membranes. The Tripeptide (aaX) is then cleaved from the C-terminus by a specific prenyl-protein specific endoprotease and the new C-terminus is methylated by a [methyltransferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methyltransferase). KRas processing is completed at this stage. Dynamic electrostatic interactions between its positively charged basic sequence with negative charges at the inner leaflet of the [plasma membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_membrane) account for its predominant localization at the cell surface at steady-state. NRAS and HRAS are further processed on the surface of the [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus) by palmitoylation of one or two Cys residues, respectively, adjacent to the [CaaX box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CaaX_box). The proteins thereby become stably membrane anchored (lipid-rafts) and are transported to the plasma membrane on [vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicle_\(biology\)) of the [secretory pathway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretory_pathway). Depalmitoylation by [acyl-protein thioesterases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyl-protein_thioesterase) eventually releases the proteins from the membrane, allowing them to enter another cycle of palmitoylation and depalmitoylation.[^cite_note-Rocks-21] This cycle is believed to prevent the leakage of NRAS and HRAS to other membranes over time and to maintain their steady-state localization along the Golgi apparatus, secretory pathway, plasma membrane and inter-linked [endocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis) pathway.

## Ras in cancer

Mutations in the Ras family of [proto-oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-oncogene) (comprising H-Ras, N-Ras and K-Ras) are very common, being found in 20–30% of all human tumors.[^cite_note-bos-22] It is reasonable to speculate that a pharmacological approach that curtails Ras activity may represent a possible method to inhibit certain cancer types. Ras point mutations are the single most common abnormality of human proto-oncogenes.[^cite_note-24] [Ras inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_inhibitor?action=edit\&redlink=1) trans-farnesylthiosalicylic acid (FTS, [Salirasib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salirasib?action=edit\&redlink=1)) exhibits profound anti-oncogenic effects in many cancer cell lines.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

### Inappropriate activation

Inappropriate activation of the gene has been shown to play a key role in improper signal transduction, proliferation and malignant transformation.[^cite_note-Lodish-18]

Mutations in a number of different genes as well as RAS itself can have this effect. [Oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene) such as p210BCR-ABL or the growth receptor erbB are upstream of Ras, so if they are constitutively activated their signals will transduce through Ras.

The [tumour suppressor gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumour_suppressor_gene) [NF1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurofibromin_1) encodes a Ras-GAP – its mutation in [neurofibromatosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurofibromatosis) will mean that Ras is less likely to be inactivated. Ras can also be amplified, although this only occurs occasionally in tumours.

Finally, Ras oncogenes can be activated by point mutations so that the GTPase reaction can no longer be stimulated by GAP – this increases the half life of active Ras-GTP mutants.[^cite_note-Reuter-27]

### Constitutively active Ras

*Constitutively active Ras* (*Ras<sup>D</sup>*) is one which contains mutations that prevent GTP hydrolysis, thus locking Ras in a permanently 'On' state.

The most common mutations are found at residue G12 in the [P-loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-loop) and the catalytic residue Q61.

- The glycine to valine mutation at residue 12 (*Ras<sup>V12</sup>*)

  [^cite_note-28]

  &#x20;renders the GTPase domain of Ras insensitive to inactivation by GAP and thus stuck in the "on state". Ras requires a GAP for inactivation as it is a relatively poor catalyst on its own, as opposed to other G-domain-containing proteins such as the alpha subunit of heterotrimeric G proteins.

- *Residue 61*

  [^cite_note-29]

  &#x20;is responsible for stabilizing the transition state for GTP hydrolysis. Because enzyme catalysis in general is achieved by lowering the energy barrier between substrate and product, mutation of Q61 to K (Glutamine to Lysine) necessarily reduces the rate of intrinsic Ras GTP hydrolysis to physiologically meaningless levels.

See also "dominant negative" mutants such as S17N and D119N.

### Ras-targeted cancer treatments

[Reovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reovirus) was noted to be a potential cancer therapeutic when studies suggested it reproduces well in certain cancer cell lines. It replicates specifically in cells that have an activated Ras pathway (a cellular signaling pathway that is involved in cell growth and differentiation).[^cite_note-pmid19806501-30] Reovirus replicates in and eventually kills Ras-activated tumour cells and as cell death occurs, progeny virus particles are free to infect surrounding cancer cells. This cycle of infection, replication and cell death is believed to be repeated until all tumour cells carrying an activated Ras pathway are destroyed.

Another tumor-lysing virus that specifically targets tumor cells with an activated Ras pathway is a type II [herpes simplex virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herpes_simplex_virus) (HSV-2) based agent, designated FusOn-H2.[^cite_note-pmid16569513-31] Activating mutations of the Ras protein and upstream elements of the Ras protein may play a role in more than two-thirds of all human cancers, including most metastatic disease. [Reolysin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reolysin), a formulation of reovirus, and FusOn-H2 are currently in clinical trials or under development for the treatment of various cancers.[^cite_note-pmid19565924-32] In addition, a treatment based on siRNA anti-mutated [K-RAS](9065739.md) (G12D) called siG12D LODER is currently in clinical trials for the treatment of locally advanced pancreatic cancer (NCT01188785, NCT01676259).[^cite_note-33]

In [glioblastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glioblastoma) mouse models SHP2 levels were heightened in cancerous brain cells. Inhibiting [SHP2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PTPN11) in turn inhibited Ras dephosphorylation. This reduced tumor sizes and accompanying rise in survival rates.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

#### RAS inhibitors

Other strategies have attempted to manipulate the regulation of the above-mentioned localization of Ras. [Farnesyltransferase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnesyltransferase_inhibitor) have been developed to stop the [farnesylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnesylation) of Ras and therefore weaken its affinity to membranes.[^cite_note-pmid12509763-2] Other inhibitors are targeting the [palmitoylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmitoylation) cycle of Ras through inhibiting depalmitoylation by [acyl-protein thioesterases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyl-protein_thioesterase#Inhibition_of_acyl-protein_thioesterases), potentially leading to a destabilization of the Ras cycle.[^cite_note-36]

A 2024 study published in the *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* described a novel inhibitor-finding strategy for mutated Ras molecules.[^cite_note-37] The Ras mutations in the 12th residue position inhibit the binding of the regulatory GAP molecule to the mutated Ras, causing uncontrolled cell growth. The novel strategy proposes finding small glue molecules, which attach the mutated Ras to the GAP, prohibiting uncontrolled cell growth and restoring the normal function. For this goal a theoretical Ras-GAP conformation was designed with a several Å gap between the molecules, and a high-throughput in silico docking was performed for finding gluing agents. As a proof of concept, two novel molecules were described with satisfying biological activity.

&#x20;\
&#x20;In 2026, the novel RAS inhibitor [daraxonrasib](81322908.md) completed a phase 3 clinical trial (RASolute 302) to assess efficacy compared to standard-of-care chemotherapy.[^cite_note-38]

## In other species

In most of the cell types of most species, most *Ras* is the GDP type. This is true for *[Xenopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenopus)* [oocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oocyte) and [mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouse) [fibroblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibroblast).[^cite_note-Bollag-McCormick-1991-39]

### *Xenopus laevis*

As mentioned above most *X.* oocyte *Ras* is the GDP conjugate. Mammal *Ras* induces [meiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis) in *[X. laevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenopus_laevis)* oocytes almost certainly by potentiating [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin)-induced meiosis, but not [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone)-induced. [Protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_synthesis) does not seem to be a part of this step. Injection increases synthesis of [diacylglycerol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diacylglycerol) from [phosphatidylcholine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphatidylcholine). Some meiosis effects are [antagonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological_antagonist) by *[rap1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap1)* (and by a *Ras* modified to dock incorrectly). Both *rap1* and the modified *Ras* are co-antagonists with [p120Ras GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P120Ras_GAP) in this pathway.[^cite_note-Bollag-McCormick-1991-39]

### *Drosophila melanogaster*

Expressed in all tissues of *[Drosophila melanogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster)* but mostly in neural cells. Overexpression is somewhat lethal and, during development, produces eye and wing abnormalities. (This parallels - and may be the reason for - similar abnormalities due to mutated [receptor tyrosine kinases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_tyrosine_kinase).) The *D.* genes for *ras*es in mammals produce abnormalities.[^cite_note-Bollag-McCormick-1991-39]

### *Aplysia*

Most expression in *[Aplysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aplysia)* spp. is in neural cells.[^cite_note-Bollag-McCormick-1991-39]

### *Caenorhabditis elegans*

The gene in *[C. elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans)* is *let 60*. Also appears to play a role in receptor tyrosine kinase formation in this model. Overexpression yields a multivulval development due to its involvement in that region's normal development; overexpression in effector sites is lethal.[^cite_note-Bollag-McCormick-1991-39]

### *Dictyostelium discoideum*

Essential in *[Dictyostelium discoideum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictyostelium_discoideum)*. This is evidenced by severe developmental failure in deficient *ras* expression and by significant impairment of various life activities when artificially expressed, such as: increased concentration of [inositol phosphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inositol_phosphate); likely reduction of [cAMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_adenosine_monophosphate) binding to [chemotaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotaxis) receptors; and that is likely the reason [cGMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_guanosine_monophosphate) synthesis is impaired. [Adenylate cyclase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenylate_cyclase) activity is unaffected by *ras*.[^cite_note-Bollag-McCormick-1991-39]
