Seen as a rigid, [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_authoritarianism), [exclusivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_exclusivism), highly [centralized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralized), and [apocalyptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypticism) church,[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] the INC describes itself to be the [one true church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_true_church) and the [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restorationism) of the [original church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church) founded by Jesus, whereby all other Christian churches are [apostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Christianity).[^cite_note-sophia-38][^cite_note-Harper_1-39] According to INC [doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine), the official registration of the church with the Philippine government was on 13 July 1914, by Felix Manalo—who is upheld by members to be the [last messenger of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_prophet) in the [End Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschatology)[^cite_note-40]—was an act of [divine providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_providence) and the fulfillment of [biblical prophecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_prophecy) concerning the re-establishment of the original church of Jesus in the Far East[^cite_note-incmedia-organization-41][^cite_note-Palafox-42][^cite_note-44] concurrent with the coming of the [seventh seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_seals#Seventh_seal) marking the [end of days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dates_predicted_for_apocalyptic_events).[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-reformed-45]

By the time of Manalo's death in 1963, the INC had become a nationwide organization with 1,250 local chapels and 35 cathedrals.[^cite_note-Sanders-46] As his successor, Manalo's son, [Eraño Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_Manalo), led a campaign to grow and [internationalize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalization) the church until his death on 31 August 2009.[^cite_note-erdy_dead-47] His son, [Eduardo V. Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_V._Manalo), succeeded him as [Executive Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Minister_of_the_Iglesia_ni_Cristo).[^cite_note-eduardo-48] The [2020 Philippine census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Philippine_census) reported that 2.8 million were adherents of the INC, making it the fifth (5th) [largest denomination in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Philippines) behind the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines), [Evangelicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), and [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism).[^cite_note-PSA-PIF2020-49][^cite_note-51]

## History

During the [American colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)) of the Philippines, there were a variety of rural [anti-colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-colonialism) movements, often with religious undertones,[^cite_note-52] and [American Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Protestantism) [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) introduced several alternatives to the [Catholic Church](606848.md), the predominant church during the [Spanish colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521%E2%80%931898\)).[^cite_note-53]

### Felix Manalo

Main article: [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo)



Built in 1937, the former chapel of the congregation of Punta in Santa Ana, Manila is now an [INC museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_Museum_\(Santa_Ana,_Manila\)). Notable is the fence design featuring the letters "INK", the abbreviation of the church's original registered name *Iglesia ni Kristo*.

[^cite_note-54]

Felix Manalo, born on 10 May 1886, as Felix Ysagun to Mariano Ysagun y Villanueva and Bonifacia Manalo y Cruz in [Taguig, Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig,_Philippines),[^cite_note-55] was [baptized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) into the [Catholic Church](606848.md). Manalo's baptismal record, however, cannot be found, as records of his parish date back only to June 1886.[^cite_note-modesto-56] Manalo became dissatisfied with [Catholic theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_theology) in his teenage years. At the age of seven, Manalo was said to have attended classes under a certain "Maestro Cario" in Manila. However, his studies were interrupted by the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) in 1896, prompting him not to pursue further formal education, and turn to farming and [hatmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatmaking) instead.[^cite_note-modesto-56] According to the [National Historical Commission of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Commission_of_the_Philippines), the establishment of the [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church) (also called the [Aglipayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_Aglipay) Church), one of the enduring results of the Revolution, was his major turning point. Still, Manalo remained uninterested since its doctrines were mainly Catholic, although at the time he found himself aligned with *[colorumism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Philippine_folk_religions)*, a [syncretism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism) of Christian and [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animist) beliefs popular among Filipinos.[^cite_note-reed-57] In 1904, he joined the [Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Episcopal_Church),[^cite_note-senate-58] entered the Methodist [Florence Nicholson Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Theological_Seminary_\(Philippines\)), and became a [lay preacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_brother) for the Methodist Mission.[^cite_note-apologetics-59][^cite_note-61] He also explored various [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination), including the [Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism) (where he studied in the [Ellinwood Bible School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Theological_Seminary_\(Philippines\))), [Iglesia ni Cristo 1901 (Christian Mission)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church_\(Disciples_of_Christ\)), and finally the [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md) in 1911, where he served as a lay preacher.[^cite_note-63] After being antagonized by fellow [Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventist) for his *[colorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Philippine_folk_religions)* past, and being reprimanded for his [elopement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elopement) with his future wife (they were married in a different church), Manalo left [Adventism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventism) in 1913, and associated himself with [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist) and agnostic peers.[^cite_note-tuggy-18][^cite_note-introvigne-31][^cite_note-reed-57][^cite_note-nhi-64]



The INC Lokal ng Tondo, established on 6 November 1915, is one of the earliest congregations of the church. The [current structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_chapel,_Tondo) was completed in 1967.

In November 1913, Manalo secluded himself with religious literature and unused notebooks in a friend's house in [Pasay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasay), instructing everyone in the home not to disturb him. He emerged from seclusion three days later with his newfound doctrines.[^cite_note-introvigne-31][^cite_note-nhi-64][^cite_note-sophia-38] Manalo, together with his wife, went to Punta, [Santa Ana, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_Manila), in November 1913 and started preaching. He also returned to his hometown Taguig to [evangelize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism), where he was ridiculed and [stoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoning) at his evangelistic meetings with locals. He later baptized a few converts, including some of his persecutors, along the [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River) on 25 December 1913. They formed the first members of the church.[^cite_note-dec1913pasugo-15][^cite_note-inctribune-16] He eventually attracted more followers. Among those he invited to become ministers of his new church would be Bishop [Nicolas Zamora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Zamora) of [IEMELIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEMELIF), who was then leading an 11,000-member Methodist church (Zamora refused the offer).[^cite_note-65] Prompted by concerns that his evangelism and propagation efforts for the new church might be illegal,[^cite_note-tuggy-18] Manalo later registered his new church as the Iglesia ni Kristo[^cite_note-increg-11] (INK; lit. 'Church of Christ'; [Spanish](26825.md): *Iglesia de Cristo*) on 13 July 1914, at the [Bureau of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Finance_\(Philippines\)) as a [corporation sole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporation_sole), with himself as the first executive minister, Pedro Inocencio as bishop, Tomas de la Cruz as evangelist, and Serapio Dionisio as deacon.[^cite_note-nhi-64][^cite_note-senate-58][^cite_note-reed-57] Expansion followed as INC started building congregations in the provinces in 1916, with Pasig (then in [Rizal Province](232458.md)) having two locales established.[^cite_note-mb-66] On 25 December 1918, coinciding with the fifth (5th) anniversary celebration of the church, Manalo was [ordained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordained) as a minister by the following bishops and pastors: Alejandro Reyes ([IEMELIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEMELIF)), Victoriano Mariano ([IEMELIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEMELIF)), Gil Domingo ([Iglesia de los Cristianos Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unida_Church)), Guillermo Zarco ([Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines)), Emiliano Quijano ([Iglesia ni Cristo 1901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church_\(Disciples_of_Christ\)))[^cite_note-67], Nicolas Fajardo ([Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines)), Roque Bautista ([Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines)).[^cite_note-68] The first three ministers of the INC were [ordained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordained) thereafter, in May 1919, namely Justino Casanova (*pangulo ng lupon*), Federico Inocencio (*kagawad ng lupon*), and Teodoro Santiago (*kalihim ng lupon*).[^cite_note-sophia-38] Before leaving for the United States in August 1919, he visited the INC congregations and left the newly-ordained ministers in charge of the church until his return to the Philippines in 1921. Manalo went twice to the United States to study religion in Protestant-managed schools, first in 1919, and later in 1938.[^cite_note-tripod-19][^cite_note-nhi-64][^cite_note-70] Early church members were said to be mostly uneducated, illiterate, and coming from the lower socioeconomic classes, the ministers included.[^cite_note-modesto-56]



[Philippines Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_Free_Press) feature of the interview of Felix Manalo by [Teodoro Locsin, Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodoro_Locsin_Sr.) (11 February 1950), where Manalo claimed that the INC has 2 million members. In the said interview, Manalo also explained his support for Senator [Jose Avelino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Avelino), who lost his bid for the [1949 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Philippine_presidential_election). Framed photographs of Avelino and President [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) were visible in Manalo's office.



The INC Lokal ng Washington ([Sampaloc, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampaloc,_Manila)) is the first concrete chapel of the Iglesia ni Cristo. It was completed in 1948. The first members of this congregation mainly came from the INC Lokal ng Tayuman.

In response to the separation of congregations led by INC ministers Teofilo Ora, Januario Ponce, and Basilio Santiago, which schism severely divided the emerging church primarily in the provinces such as [Bulacan](252408.md) and [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), Manalo's title as the *Sugo* (transl. Messenger) was introduced to church doctrine in 1922, after interpreting that Manalo is the "angel from the East" mentioned in [Revelation 7\:2](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Revelation%207\:2\&version=nrsv). Ora and the ministers who went with him clashed with Manalo in terms of doctrine, such as the decreasing relevance of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) in the INC preaching (*paksa*), and how the church was run, particularly in light of reported abuses and immorality. Other changes by this time included the eventual rejection of [cassock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassock) (*sutana*) as clothing for ministers.[^cite_note-zabala-71] Manalo's authority over the church was so pervasive that outsiders termed the church as the *Iglesia ni Manalo* (transl. Church of Manalo) and its adherents "Manalistas" for their "fanatical" obedience to the church administration.[^cite_note-reed-57] By 1924, INC had about 3,000 to 5,000 adherents in 43 or 45 congregations in Manila and six nearby [provinces](165100.md).[^cite_note-reed-57] Church growth, however, came with more troubles. In 1928, INC Minister Nicolas Perez of Bulacan led another significant split across church ranks. Perez protested against abuses of the church, the changing doctrine on [vices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vices) and jewelry, and the controlling nature of the church administration that discourages [open-mindedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-mindedness) and [critical thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thinking) among its members.[^cite_note-zabala-71][^cite_note-73] According to Teodoro Santiago, the third INC Minister to be ordained in the church, Manalo began to openly reject the [deity of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christology) around the year 1932, but still maintained that Jesus is Lord (*Panginoong Hesukristo*).[^cite_note-tuggy-18] By 1936, INC had 85,000 members. This figure grew to 200,000 by 1954.[^cite_note-mb-66] A [Cebu](160278.md) congregation was built in 1937—the first to be established outside of [Luzon](69375.md), and the first in the [Visayas](72418.md). By 1938, Manalo threatened the INC brethren that he would leave the church and start anew, prompting a circular issued by Teodoro Santiago to submit their respective explanations (*salaysay*) to keep the church united.[^cite_note-74] During [World War II](32927.md), Manalo was offered by the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines) to lead the all-Filipino [Evangelical Church of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines) (福音教会). His refusal led to Japanese suspicion and surveillance, to the point that Manalo acceded to the Japanese demand to have Prudencio Vasquez, division minister of Nueva Ecija and later of Bicol, as the [Executive Minister of the Iglesia ni Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Minister_of_the_Iglesia_ni_Cristo). This was formalized through a circular issued on 29 June 1942.[^cite_note-pasugo-75] Manalo resumed being the Executive Minister after the war. The first [mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_\(Christianity\)) to [Mindanao](20546.md) was commissioned in 1946. On 15 March 1948, the church was formally reconstituted through amendments to its Articles of Incorporation as the Iglesia Ni Cristo (INC), with a twofold name (Iglesia Ni Cristo and Church of Christ) used in English-speaking countries.[^cite_note-reed-57] Meanwhile, its first concrete chapel was built in [Sampaloc, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampaloc,_Manila) also in 1948.[^cite_note-reed-57][^cite_note-nhi2-76] Adherents fleeing for the provinces away from Manila, where the [Japanese forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_Army) were concentrated during [World War II](32927.md), were used for evangelization.[^cite_note-reed-57] By 1950, Manalo himself claimed that the INC had 2 million members.[^cite_note-modesto-56][^cite_note-77] The 1960 [Census in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_the_Philippines), however, showed adherents totaling to 270,104. By 1955, the overall educational attainment of INC members had also improved, with an estimated 35 percent of its total membership being considered literate. This, however, is lower than the national literacy rate of 75 percent in the same year.[^cite_note-modesto-56] As Manalo's health began to fail in the 1950s, his son [Eraño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_G._Manalo) began taking leadership of the church. Manalo died on 12 April 1963.[^cite_note-mb-66][^cite_note-nhi2-76]



Grand Evangelical Rally (Malaking Pamamahayag) of the Iglesia Ni Cristo aimed to attract new members.



Completed in 1954, the Iglesia Ni Cristo (INC) Lokal ng Cubao in [Quezon City](284223.md) is the first major chapel designed by Filipino Architect [Carlos A. Santos-Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Santos-Viola) for the INC. It is believed to have inspired future designs of INC chapels all over the [Philippines](23440.md).

### International expansion

Main article: [Eraño Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_Manalo)



The [INC Lokal ng Palanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_chapel,_Makati), formerly known as the INC Lokal ng Makati, was formed as a congregation in 1955 by members from INC locales in Pasay and Manila. After being destroyed by fire, the current structure was rehabilitated and rededicated in 2011, becoming one of the first edifices in the [Philippines](23440.md) to exhibit the modern architectural design of INC chapels.



The [INC Lokal ng Bago Bantay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_chapel,_Bago_Bantay), completed on 4 December 1964, is currently the only INC chapel located along [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA), a major thoroughfare in the [Philippines](23440.md).

While [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) during his lifetime was convinced on keeping the INC as exclusively [Filipino](2298105.md),[^cite_note-truthfully-78] a reinterpretation and change in doctrine regarding [Isaiah 43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_43)\:5 prompted the INC to expand to the Western world, or what INC doctrine called the "Far West" (*Malayong Kanluran*). However, it must also be noted that this region, geographically speaking, would have been farther east of the [Philippines](23440.md) instead of west.[^cite_note-79] Still, the global expansion pushed through. It would be on 27 July 1968, when [Eraño Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_Manalo) officiated the inaugural worship service of the church in [Ewa Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewa_Beach), [Honolulu](13887.md), Hawaii—the first mission of the INC outside the Philippines. The following month, INC established the San Francisco congregation.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] INC reached Europe through the United Kingdom in 1971 and Canada in 1973. INC established its first congregation in South Africa in 1978.[^cite_note-82] INC established congregations in [Guantánamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guant%C3%A1namo_Bay), [Cuba](5042481.md), in 1990; [Rome](25458.md), Italy, on 27 July 1994; [Jerusalem, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem,_Israel), on 31 March 1996; and [Athens, Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens,_Greece), on 10 May 1997.[^cite_note-83] In 1998, INC established 543 congregations and missions in 74 countries outside the Philippines.[^cite_note-reed-57]

During the 1970s INC boasted of having around 2,500 congregations.[^cite_note-tuggy-18] INC started operating a radio station in 1969; its [first television program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Iglesia_ni_Cristo) aired in 1983.[^cite_note-mb-66] The Ministerial Institute of Development, renamed as "Iglesia ni Cristo (Church of Christ) School for Ministers", was founded in 1974 in [Quiapo, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiapo,_Manila), and moved to [Quezon City](284223.md) in 1978. In 1971, the INC Central Office building was built in Quezon City. In 1984, the 7,000-seat [Central Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Temple) was added to the complex. The Tabernacle, a multipurpose tent-like building that can accommodate up to 4,000 people, was finished in 1989. The complex also includes the [New Era University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Era_University), a tertiary education institution run and managed by the INC.[^cite_note-reed-57] Eraño G. Manalo died on 31 August 2009.[^cite_note-erdy_dead-47] His son, [Eduardo V. Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_V._Manalo), succeeded him as executive minister upon his death.[^cite_note-eduardo-48]

## Beliefs and core values



The [INC Central Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INC_Central_Temple) (*Templo Central*) in [Quezon City](284223.md) was completed in 1984 and is the only INC building to date that is called a *temple*. According to architect [Carlos A. Santos-Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Santos-Viola), in designing INC edifices, he had to create a style that could not "be mistaken for any other sect except Iglesia \[ni Cristo]." He also related how [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) considered [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) to be the "most religious type of architecture" for its verticality, which was interpreted as "pointing towards Heaven."

[^cite_note-pundasyon-97]

Iglesia ni Cristo believes that it is the [one true church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_true_church) established by [Jesus of Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_of_Nazareth) in the [first century CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_century_CE). It posits that its registration in the Philippines—approximately two millennia after his [death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus)—is the fulfillment of a prophecy that Jesus's church would re-emerge in the Far East (*Malayong Silangan*) via [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo).[^cite_note-incmedia-organization-41] Because of the similarities it shares with the Christian movement known as [Restorationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restorationism), INC's doctrines have been described as Restorationist.[^cite_note-annharper-98]



The Iglesia Ni Cristo (INC) *Lokal ng Tayuman* (lit. 'Locality of Tayuman') is the second-oldest congregation of the church, established after the congregation at Punta in [Santa Ana, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_Manila). The chapel is located along Apitong Street in Santa Cruz, Manila. [Manila City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_City_Council) Resolution No. 431, series of 2015, urged the [National Historical Commission of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Commission_of_the_Philippines) to recognize the locale as a historic site.

### Bible

The Iglesia ni Cristo believes that the [Christian Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Bible) is the only sacred text [inspired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_inspiration) by [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Christianity). Therefore, it is the sole basis of all the group's beliefs and practices. The INC uses the [Protestant Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Bible), which includes 66 canonized texts. Members are discouraged from independent [Bible reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_study_\(Christianity\)),[^cite_note-gabriel-100] for only INC ministers have the authority (*may karapatan*) to interpret and teach scripture.[^cite_note-incmedia-corevalues-101][^cite_note-eranof-102] However, Manalo was even stricter regarding who could preach and teach in the movement. According to him, only the [Executive Minister of the Iglesia ni Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Minister_of_the_Iglesia_ni_Cristo) can truthfully teach on the Bible.[^cite_note-truthfully-78]

### God the Father, Jesus Christ and the Holy Spirit

The Iglesia ni Cristo believes that [God the Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father) is the [creator deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity) and the only true God. The INC regards the traditional, mainstream Christian doctrine of the [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity) as heresy,[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-reed-57] adopting a version of [unitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism). The church holds that its position was attested by Jesus and the [12 apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_apostles) in the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament).[^cite_note-sophia-38][^cite_note-incmedia-corevalues-101][^cite_note-pasugo_november_1968-103]

> Christ and the Apostles are united in teaching how many and who is the real God. Similar to other true Christians, according to Apostle Paul, there is only one God, the Father—not the Son and more so not the Holy Spirit. The Apostles also did not teach that there is one God who has three personas who are also Gods.… It \[Trinity] is not found in the Holy Scriptures or the Bible, and if \[Catholic] priests ever use the Bible to prove this teaching of theirs, all are based only on suppositions and presumptions.

— trans. from *Pasugo* (November 1968)[^cite_note-pasugo_november_1968-103]

The church believes that Jesus is the [Son of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_of_God_\(Christianity\)),[^cite_note-incmedia-corevalues-101] the [mediator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingly_office_of_Christ) between God the Father and humanity,[^cite_note-reed-57] and a creation of God the Father. God [sanctified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctification) him to be [without sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeccability), and bestowed upon him the titles "[Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord)" and "[Son of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_of_God)". The church sees Jesus as God's highest creation and that he was a human, denying the [divinity of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity_of_Jesus).[^cite_note-apologetics-59] Adherents profess belief in the theory of [substitutionary atonement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitutionary_atonement) as well as in the [redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redemption_\(theology\)) of humankind. Jesus is believed to have been "foreordained before the foundation of the world" and sent by God "to deal with sin". Members believe they "are saved by [Christ's blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_of_Christ)", who died because of his "self-sacrificing love".[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-pasugo_august_1969-104]

INC believes that the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit) is the power of God, not a deity, having been sent by God the Father and Jesus to guide God's people.[^cite_note-105] According to INC doctrine, there are [seven gifts of the Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_gifts_of_the_Holy_Spirit) (or seven Holy Spirits), which is based on the church's interpretation of [Revelation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revelation_5)\:6.[^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-106]

The Iglesia ni Cristo's [doxology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doxology), however, continues to use a [trinitarian formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinitarian_formula), which the INC has officially rejected. The INC's doxology was derived from bishop [Thomas Ken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Ken)'s 1674 work "*[Praise God, from whom all blessings flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_100th)*."[^cite_note-gabriel-100]

### One true church



The Iglesia ni Cristo flag is believed to be inspired by the [Flag of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy), the tricolor template of green, white, and red of which was first used in banners of [Napoleonic Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic) (1796). As for the INC, the colors were intended to represent faith, hope, and love, while the seven-branched candelabrum, the [Menorah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menorah_\(Temple\)), of [Judaism](15624.md) represents the church in the Christian Bible.

The Iglesia ni Cristo believes that it is the [one true church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_true_church) (*bayang banal*) and founded by Jesus,[^cite_note-incmedia-corevalues-101] having been restored by Felix Manalo in the [last days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dates_predicted_for_apocalyptic_events) (*mga huling araw*). It believes that the first-century church—or the fourth-century church—[apostasized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Christianity),[^cite_note-catholic-107] instead following [false teachings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy).[^cite_note-sophia-38][^cite_note-Harper_1-39] The INC argues that this apostate church became the [Catholic Church](606848.md). The INC has posited that no true church can experience schisms, splits, or separations.[^cite_note-108] Its establishment is hailed as the harbinger of the [End of Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschatology).[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-reed-57][^cite_note-apologetics-59]



The official INC seal, which features the church's version of the [Agnus Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnus_Dei), a symbol representing [Jesus](1095706.md), whereby the banner and the [cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_cross) bore by the [Lamb of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_of_God) are replaced by the [weighing scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighing_scale) and the [Square and Compasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_and_Compasses) of [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry). The [dove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_symbolism) represents the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit). Notably, [God the Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father) is not represented on the official INC logo. Also featured in the seal is the tricolor shield of the INC. Like the church flag, it is believed to be inspired by the [Flag of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy); green, white, and red were first used in banners of [Napoleonic Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic) (1796).

[^cite_note-109]

The church believes that the Iglesia ni Cristo is the fulfillment of the Hebrew Biblical [Isaiah 43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_43)\:5, with the "far east" (*Malayong Silangan*) referring to the [Philippines](23440.md), where the Church of Christ would allegedly be founded.[^cite_note-sophia-38][^cite_note-reformed-45][^cite_note-apologetics-59][^cite_note-reed-57][^cite_note-catholic-107] According to the official INC publication *Pasugo*, however, there is no explicit mention of the [Philippines](23440.md) in the Bible. The "far east" may include the nations of Asia in general.[^cite_note-110] According to [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo), the INC was established for the "other sheep" ([John 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_10)\:16) and "those who are far off" ([Acts 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_2)\:38–39), which Manalo interpreted to be strictly about [Filipinos](2298105.md) only.[^cite_note-truthfully-78] Thus, internationalization of the INC never occurred during the lifetime of Manalo, keeping the church exclusively Filipino. INC teaches that its members constitute the "[elect of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predestination)". [God's grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sola_gratia) and [faith in Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sola_fide) are insufficient to save humans; instead, only INC membership brings about one's salvation, and there is no [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_salvation) outside the Iglesia ni Cristo.[^cite_note-111] Marriages and relationships, in general, between members and non-members are prohibited by the church.[^cite_note-reed-57][^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-pcij-112][^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-Harper_1-39] However, those outside the church prior to the foundation of the INC in 1914 will be judged and saved according to the law that is [written in their hearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience).[^cite_note-113] Aligned with this, Manalo claimed that Filipino nationalist [José Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal) was holy (*banal*), despite never being a member of the Iglesia ni Cristo (Rizal died in 1896), and preached that they owed their religious freedom from Rizal.[^cite_note-114] As members of the same church, members are not allowed to sue each other in court.[^cite_note-115] The Iglesia ni Cristo is also particular about the official name of the true church, which they say is "Church of Christ or Iglesia ni Cristo (in Tagalog)", although historically the church has changed its name from the originally registered *Iglesia ni Kristo* (INK). The two passages often cited by INC to support this, although none of them referred to the church as a [proper noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_noun), are [Romans 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romans_16)\:16 "Greet one another with a holy kiss. The churches of Christ greet you",[^cite_note-116] and the [George Lamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lamsa) translation of [Acts 20\:28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_20\:28): "Take heed therefore ... to feed the church of Christ which he has purchased with his blood."[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Despite the aforementioned verses being the alleged basis for their church name, INC members in general do not practice the [holy kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_kiss).[^cite_note-eranof-102]

### Felix Manalo as the founder



Completed on 27 July 1962, the [Iglesia Ni Cristo (INC) Lokal ng San Francisco del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_chapel,_San_Francisco_del_Monte), also known locally as the *Lokal ng Frisco*, is one of the major chapels designed by Architect [Carlos A. Santos-Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Santos-Viola) for the INC. It was also one of the last chapels dedicated during the lifetime of INC founder [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo), later serving as the site of his funeral in 1963. Prior to the construction of the current structure of the [INC Lokal ng Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_chapel,_Tondo) (1967) and the [INC Lokal ng Templo Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INC_Central_Temple) (1984), the INC Lokal ng Frisco was the largest INC chapel in terms of seating capacity at 3,200.

According to the INC, although admittedly Manalo's name cannot be found in the Bible (neither in the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament) nor the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament)),[^cite_note-imptname-9] the church believes that Manalo is the "angel from the East" mentioned in [Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation) 7\:1–3 who started preaching about the restored church during the [End Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschatology). The verse reads:

*I saw four angels standing at the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow on the earth, on the sea, or on any tree. Then I saw another angel ascending from the east, having the seal of the living God. And he cried with a loud voice to the four angels to whom it was granted to harm the earth and the sea, saying, "Do not harm the earth, the sea, or the trees till we have sealed the servants of our God on their foreheads."*

[^cite_note-119]

This is the start of the period, according to INC, being referred to in the Bible as the ends of the earth (cf Is 41\:9–10; 43\:5–6), the time when the end of the world is near, even at the very doors (cf. Mt. 24\:3, 33), which began with the outbreak of a war of global proportions (cf. Mt. 24\:6–7)[^cite_note-Harper_1-39][^cite_note-120] Manalo is from the Philippines, which they say is in the "center" of the Far East.[^cite_note-121] The ‘four winds’ in Revelation 7\:1–3, they say refers to [World War I](4764461.md) and the four angels are the four leaders known as [The Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Four_\(World_War_I\)) ([Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson), [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George), [Georges Clemenceau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Clemenceau), and [Vittorio Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Orlando))[^cite_note-122] who they say worked on the prevention of the winds of war.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Still, INC theology considers [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) as its founder, the first of God's four messengers of the [church era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispensationalism), with Manalo's task being to reestablish the church in the Far East (*Malayong Silangan*).[^cite_note-125]

Manalo is also portrayed as the fulfillment of several passages in [Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah) and other [books of the Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books_of_the_Bible), including the one Isaiah 46\:11 called the "bird of prey" (*ibong mandaragit*).[^cite_note-sophia-38][^cite_note-reed-57]

As the one who sent by God to restore the INC, Manalo became the first executive minister and spiritual leader of the church.[^cite_note-reformed-45] As such, he taught that what is written in the Bible was the ultimate authority in all aspects of the church, and effectively as a messenger of God, Manalo is "the foremost [Biblical authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_authority) for all humanity and the divinely designated leader of a reestablished [Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ) in the modern world."[^cite_note-reed-57]

The INC celebrates the birth anniversary of Felix Manalo every May.[^cite_note-gabriel-100]

### Martin Luther as the predecessor

INC members believe the doctrine that there are four messengers of God in the [church era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispensationalism), namely [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ), [Paul the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle), [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther), and [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo), with Manalo being the "Last Messenger."[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-126] Also according to the INC, German [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) and theologian [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther) was the second angel mentioned in Revelation 14\:8,[^cite_note-127] which reads as follows.

*And another angel followed, saying,*&#x20;

*"Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she has made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication."*

[^cite_note-128]

Therefore, as Luther was also ordained to be God's messenger by challenging the [Catholic Church](606848.md) and becoming a key figure of the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), he was considered by the church as a predecessor to Manalo, who was also accorded the recognition of being an angel.[^cite_note-129]

### Baptism

The church believes that baptism is done by [immersion baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersion_baptism) or [Believer's baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Believer's_baptism) by adults in water, and that it is necessary that people be baptized in the Iglesia ni Cristo to become disciples of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ).[^cite_note-incmedia-corevalues-101] While Jesus was baptized according to the Gospels, [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo), as the church founder, was himself not baptized in the fold of the INC. Church doctrine justified this by comparing Manalo's standing with that of [Paul the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle) and [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) (that is, Manalo as *Ang Tanging Sugo na may Dalawang Pagkahalal sa Karapatan*).[^cite_note-130] The church rejects [infant baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_baptism). Newborn children of members are instead dedicated to God through a congregational prayer, led by an ordained minister of the INC.[^cite_note-eranof-102]

On 7 September 2019, the INC set the [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) for most people baptized in a single event through its "Grand Baptism" at the [Philippine Arena](32817630.md) with 18,272 newly baptized members. Of this number, 94 were non-Filipinos (foreigners).[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

### Expulsion

Members who are not living in accordance with the [doctrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine) taught in the INC are reported (*ulat*) and admonished, usually by requiring the violating member to explain (*salaysay*). Those who continue in violation of INC doctrines after being admonished are [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication) or [excommunicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication) from the INC (*tiwalag*), thus [losing salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Christianity). Former members have reportedly experienced [shunning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunning) and [ostracism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostracism) from the faithful INC members. As such, the church does not believe in the [perseverance of the saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseverance_of_the_saints). Certain violations, such as eating blood,[^cite_note-133] having too long of an absence from church services,[^cite_note-134] drinking [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drink), [eloping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eloping), or having a [relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_\(love\)) (including marriage) with a non-member, may result in mandatory expulsion.[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-pcij-112][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

### Eschatology and resurrection

See also: [Christian eschatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_eschatology)

INC believes that a person is composed of a body ("vehicle"), [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul) ("individual"), and spirit ("life" or fuel). Members believe that when a person dies, [their body and soul both die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mortalism), they will become ineligible for [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation), and go into the grave where both will remain until the [Second Coming of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Coming_of_Christ), whereas the spirit will go back to God. The church does not believe there will be a [millennial reign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism) of Christ. Upon Christ's return, all dead servants of God, from the time of the patriarchs up to the last days, would be [resurrected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_the_dead) to join living faithful and loyal INC members. They will be rewarded by living in the Holy City or [New Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jerusalem), together with [God the Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father), and Jesus Christ. At the right time chosen by God, a second resurrection would occur, and non-INC members will experience [second death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_death) which is the [Lake of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_Fire) *(Dagát-dagatang Apóy*).[^cite_note-Harper_2-33][^cite_note-137]

The church believes that God set a [day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Judgment) where he will judge all people. They believe that this day is also the Second Coming of Jesus Christ.[^cite_note-incmedia-corevalues-101]

### LGBTQ rights

The church believes that God created [gender binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_binary).[^cite_note-138] According to INC doctrine, same-sex relationships are prohibited, and being involved in such relationships is sinful.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

### Membership in organizations and labor unions

The INC does not believe members should join organizations or labor unions which run counter to their doctrines and teachings.[^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-Ando1969-141]<sup>: 336 </sup> The policy comes from an interpretation of the [Second Epistle to the Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_the_Corinthians) where members of the church should not join any organization with non-members. Joining social gatherings with non-INC members such as [proms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prom) and [parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party) are also prohibited by the INC.[^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-142] The legality of a closed shop firing INC members based on their religious convictions reached the [Supreme Court](1335534.md) in 1974. The court ruled in favour of the INC member, holding that the provision of the law exempting those with religious objections to union membership from closed shop agreements is constitutional.[^cite_note-143]

### Christmas and other holidays

The INC does not celebrate Christmas, Easter, [Passover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover), [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween), [Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine's_Day), and other holidays the church considers as "pagan" of origin, although it allows the celebration of other occasions also of "pagan" origin such as [birthdays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthdays).[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] In lieu of these holidays, the INC celebrates [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving) (*pasasalamat*) during its anniversary celebration in July, and at the end of the year in December.[^cite_note-147] Despite having "pagan" origins, the church also celebrates the New Year.[^cite_note-148] Bringing offerings (*lagak*) to the church during [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving) events are commanded. The church teaches that the greater the offering (*abuloy* or *handog*), the greater is the faith.[^cite_note-zabala-71][^cite_note-eranof-102]

Without rejecting the celebration of Christmas, however, [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) preached that the meaning of Christmas is [peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace) and the [good news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel) that Jesus was born to bring [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation).[^cite_note-149] This concept of adopting Christmas in INC doctrine was carried over by other INC writers such as Emiliano Agustin, who wrote "*Sa Paskong marilag tanggapin sa puso, Ang Kapayapaang mahigit sa ginto*" (Accept this majestic Christmas in your hearts, this [Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace) which is greater than gold),[^cite_note-150] and Conrado Salonga, who wrote "*Tila kailan lang ang dating kahapon, Pasko na naman, narito na ngayon, Kaya naman kahit munting paghahandog, ay aking nilayon*" (It seemed like yesterday, it's already Christmas today, so I aim to give, even a little offering).[^cite_note-151] According to a 1969 article of General Evangelist Bienvenido Santiago, Sr., "*Hindi kami tumututol na ipagdiwang ang kapanganakan ni Kristo.*" (We are not against the celebration of the [birth of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus)).[^cite_note-gabriel-100] In a 1980s radio interview with [Manolo Favis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolo_Favis), INC Minister Oni Santiago related how during the lifetime of [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo), the INC still celebrated Christmas. Santiago observed that it was only a recent phenomenon (that is, reckoning from the time of the interview) that Christmas was prohibited and was replaced by the INC [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving) because, according to him, offerings decreased when members used up their resources for buying [Christmas gifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_gift) instead of giving them to the church. During [World War II](32927.md), the INC also observed Easter, which it had done so with [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical) churches in the Philippines.[^cite_note-152]

## Practices

### Worship and prayer



A missionary gathering inside the [Philippine Arena](32817630.md).

The church conducts mandatory regular worship services (*samba*) twice during the week, usually scheduled on Thursdays and Sundays (there are also Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday schedules depending on the locale).[^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-kavanagh-153][^cite_note-154] The INC also practices [sex segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_segregation) during worship and mandates its own strict [dress code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_code) or proper attire which members should be allowed to wear.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] The church itself describes the INC prescribed clothing as conservative and "holy array."[^cite_note-157] According to INC Minister Igmidio Zabala, midweek services were introduced in the church by 1922.[^cite_note-zabala-71] While Zabala credits the development of midweek worship services to [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) himself, Teodoro Santiago attributed this to INC Minister Justino Casanova, who during Manalo's trip to the United States (1919–1921) began presiding worship services during Wednesdays. Upon returning to the Philippines, it was Manalo's decision to settle with the Thursday schedule.[^cite_note-tuggy-18]

These services are conducted in the local languages (providing sign language interpreters and translators in some congregations). It consists of singing of hymns, prayers, studies of the bible, collection of voluntary offerings (*abuloy* or *handog*, while the building fund is called *tanging handugan*), and benediction.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] Both [God the Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father) and [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) are worshiped, despite church doctrine denying the [divinity of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity_of_Christ).[^cite_note-bereans-160] The ministers of every congregation in a given worship service use the same [sermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermon) outline prepared by the executive minister. [Deacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) (*diyakono*) and [Deaconesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaconess) (*diyakonesa*) guide worshipers to their seats and collect voluntary offerings.[^cite_note-Harper_1-39] The singing of hymns is led by the locale's choir. The first [hymnbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymnbook), termed *Ang Himnario ng Iglesia ni Cristo*, which consists of over 300 songs, was published in 1937. To date, the church has over 500 hymns. INC Head Deacon Melanio Gabriel, however, claimed that the INC "copied" its hymns and songs from existing [Christian music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_music) produced by other churches and denominations.[^cite_note-gabriel-100] Children's worship services ([Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Pagsamba ng Kabataan*, or *PNK*) are held every weekend. They use similar lessons as the standard worship services taught using the [Socratic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socratic_method) (question and answer).[^cite_note-sophia-38] [Church attendance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_attendance) is strictly monitored through attendance forms and [QR codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QR_code) provided by the INC. [Gadgets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadget) and electronic devices such as [cellphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phones) and [smartwatches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartwatch) are banned from being used during worship services, or even brought inside church premises. As such, church members are required to surrender all their gadgets and electronic devices prior to entering and participating in worship services. Photography, videography, and other recording activities inside the church are also prohibited. Church members are mandated to attend their respective locales or congregations where they are enrolled or registered. If any member will be moving for whatever reason, a transfer to the new locale or congregation must be filed with the church administration. Transfers are valid for 30 days.[^cite_note-eranof-102] The church teaches that willfully forsaking the worship service is a grievous sin, usually prompting congregational action such as visits (*dalaw*),[^cite_note-161] thus members are expected to attend the congregational worship services twice a week without fail.[^cite_note-Ando1969-141]<sup>: 334–345 </sup>



INC worship services still proceed as scheduled despite rains and flooding. This photo was taken at the wake of [Tropical Storm Ondoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ketsana).

The church encourages its members to take up a [ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_ministry) (*tungkulin*), attend weekly committee meetings (*pulong*), and make prayer a part of everyday life (*panata*). Thus, prayer before various activities, such as taking meals, travelling, and going to sleep, are commonly practiced.[^cite_note-162] Prayers recited in [rote repetition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rote_learning) are not observed.[^cite_note-163] Instead, INC members believe that prayers are meant to make known their requests to God. Also according to INC doctrine, prayers are answered by God if they are done in faith and in obedience to God's commands.[^cite_note-eranof-102] The [Executive Minister of the Iglesia ni Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Minister_of_the_Iglesia_ni_Cristo) is usually a theme in prayers of INC members.[^cite_note-164]

Besides repetitive prayers, the INC also rejects [prayer for the dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayer_for_the_dead). Following this, INC members are prohibited from celebrating [All Saints' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints'_Day) (1 November) and [All Souls' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls'_Day) (2 November), in addition to the holidays they are banned to celebrate. According to INC doctrine, remembering the departed is not allowed because they have already [lost consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_sleep), and will not be granted [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation) until they experience [resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection) and receive eternal life.[^cite_note-165] Regardless of the person being a member of the INC or not, death separates the person from the [one true church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_true_church) and does not enjoy the same benefits as the living.[^cite_note-166] Therefore, the dead are ineligible to be saved.[^cite_note-eranof-102][^cite_note-167]

### Evangelism



Cover of the August 1939 issue of the *Pasugo* featuring an artist's depiction of the "angel from the East", and a photograph of INC Minister Mariano Castro, who later served as a religious mentor to [Eraño Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_Manalo).

INC members have the responsibility to share their faith to others (who according to INC doctrine are "slaves to corruption"), and are mandated to "[bear fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_of_the_Holy_Spirit)" by bringing in new prospects (*akay*), particularly during evangelical missions (stylized as *EVangelical Mission*, with the letters EVM capitalized), to learn INC doctrines through Bible studies and worship services.[^cite_note-eranof-102]

Since February 1939, the church has been publishing *Pasugo*[^cite_note-Harper_2-33] (officially translated as *God's Message*) in both Tagalog and English.[^cite_note-catholic-107] Filipino was the only language used since its inception in 1939 until 1953. Currently, about two-thirds of its pages are devoted to the English-speaking population.[^cite_note-168] Felix Manalo wrote its first editorial where he stated the publication's purpose, including the propagation of the faith.[^cite_note-sophia-38] Issues contain articles which detail INC doctrines and refute doctrines which it considers as heresy, such as the [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity).[^cite_note-Harper_1-39][^cite_note-apologetics-59] It also features information on church history, educational programs and missionary achievements, including lists and photographs of newly dedicated chapels. In 2001, it had a monthly circulation of 235,000 copies.[^cite_note-reed-57] For the year 2009, there were more than four million copies of *Pasugo* distributed worldwide.[^cite_note-169] According to [Similarweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similarweb), the official website of *Pasugo* has 7,381 monthly visits as of 2025.[^cite_note-170]

In the [Philippines](23440.md), through the [Christian Era Broadcasting Service International Incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Era_Broadcasting_Service) (CEBSI Incorporated), INC broadcasts programs that discuss Bible teachings over the radio and television. These programs are aired by about 60 other radio stations all over the Philippines (i.e. [INC Radio- DZEM 954kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DZEM)) and several more in the US and Australia. [INCTV 48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DZCE-TV), which is carried on all cable providers in the Philippines and some channels in the US Direct TV ch 2068, telecast the INC's religious programs. These programs can also be seen in the Internet via one of the organisation's news website.[^cite_note-171]

Although the INC claims that membership numbers does not necessarily make a religion true, and appeal to popularity should not be counted as a binding justification for acceptance,[^cite_note-172] the church nonetheless holds regular religious gatherings called evangelical missions (stylized as *EVangelical Mission*, with the letters EVM capitalized) which aim to attract more followers.[^cite_note-173] On 28 February 2012, INC held its largest Philippine-wide evangelical missions simultaneously on 19 sites across the country.[^cite_note-174] In Manila site alone, more than 600,000 people attended the event.[^cite_note-600k-175] On 13 April 2013, INC launched *Lingap-Pamamahayag* under its project *Kabayan Ko, Kapatid Ko* (English: My Countrymen, My Brethren), which incorporates outreach missions to its evangelical missions.[^cite_note-Kabayan-176] On 26 September 2015, INC held its first worldwide evangelical mission at the Philippine Arena as the main venue and in 2,125 sites throughout the world through video conferencing. It was officiated by INC executive minister, Eduardo Manalo.[^cite_note-177]

### Outreach

On 19 November 1981, INC launched the *Lingap sa Mamamayan* ("Aid To Humanity") Program. The program aims to provide relief goods, health care, and other services to the needy, especially those who are afflicted by calamities and disasters. It also provides seminars for disaster preparedness, first aid, and family planning. Other humanitarian activities such as blood donation and community clean up drives were also conducted in different parts of the world where the Iglesia ni Cristo is established.[^cite_note-179]

Felix Y. Manalo (FYM) Foundation, the INC's arm in executing the *Lingap sa Mamamayan* and other related programs, was formally registered in the Philippines on 4 February 2011, and in the United States on 17 May 2012. The institution is also recognized in Japan, South Korea, Spain, and Russia.[^cite_note-180]

INC also established the UNLAD International, Inc.[^cite_note-181] Aimed to broaden the reach of the program for livelihood and to help more people, UNLAD was registered to the Philippine government on 17 March 2013.[^cite_note-182]

On 7 July 2012, the INC Lingap sa Mamamayan was conducted in the slums of Parola in [Tondo, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo,_Manila) and was awarded three [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) for breaking records in the most people involved in a dental health check; the most blood pressure readings taken in 8 hours; and the most [blood glucose level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_sugar_level) tests in 8 hours.[^cite_note-183] On 29 April 2016, four more Guinness world records were broken by the INC. These records are the largest collection of clothes for recycle/donation, the most shoes donated to charity in 24 hours, the most medical ultrasound examinations in eight hours, and the most medical risk assessment in eight hours which was also held in Tondo, Manila.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

On 15 February 2014, INC received another two Guinness world records when they conducted a worldwide charity walk simultaneously on 135 different sites scattered in 29 countries. INC holds the records for the largest charity walk on a single venue when 175,509 members of the church finished the 1.6 km walk in Manila; and for the largest charity walk in 24 hours (multiple venues) when a total of 519,521 participants finished the charity walk in different parts of the world. The proceeds were used for the housing and livelihood projects of super [Typhoon Haiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Haiyan) survivors.[^cite_note-186] INC also broke the same records on 6 May 2018, for its Worldwide Walk to Fight Poverty with 283,171 people in single venue, and 773,136 people in multiple venues for its African missions and outreach.[^cite_note-187]

On 22 February 2014, INC conducted another Lingap sa Mamamayan at its first resettlement project in Barrio Maligaya in [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija). Coinciding with the barrio's 49th anniversary, INC bagged another world record after setting the record for the most hunger relief packs distributed within eight hours. A total of 302,311 hunger relief packages were given.[^cite_note-188]

Administration and ministerial work are delegated into [ecclesiastical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiology) districts (termed *divisions* until 1990) which are led by District Supervising Ministers (O1, formerly *division ministers* or *ministrong regular*) and Assistant District Supervising Ministers (O2, formerly *regular evangelist*). They are assisted by [deacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) (*diyakono*), [deaconesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaconess) (*diyakonesa*), secretaries (*kalihim*), treasurers (*ingat-yaman*), and [workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laity) (*manggagawa*).[^cite_note-increg-11][^cite_note-reed-57] Ecclesiastical districts usually comprise 30 congregations (referred to as locales) on average, if excluding house churches, group worship services (GWS), and church extensions.[^cite_note-incdirectory-4] All locales were directly managed by Felix Y. Manalo until 1924 when the first ecclesiastical district was organized in [Pampanga](255602.md).[^cite_note-sophia-38]

Members are also organized into *Christian Family Organizations* (CFOs), which cater to groups depending on their respective life stages. *Buklod* (formerly *Buklod ng Gintong Layunin*) is for married members. *KADIWA* (Kabataang May Diwang Wagas, formerly *Kapisanang Maligayang Pagtatagumpay*) is for single members 18 years old and above. *Binhi* is for baptized members aged 12 to 17. *Pagsamba ng Kabataan* (PNK) is for members' children aged 4 to 11. There are also CFOs aimed for different settings, such as the Society of Communicators and Networkers International (SCAN).[^cite_note-203] Platforms critical of the church such as the [satirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satirical) page *2ph4u* (also known as *2philippines4u* in other social media channels) reportedly received serious attention from the SCAN for alleged "[defamation](28661.md) and dissemination of harmful falsehoods" concerning the INC. On Facebook alone, *2ph4u* has over 83,000 followers.[^cite_note-204]

## Architecture













Not all INC chapels bear the trademark design of INC churches in the Philippines, such as the INC congregations in [Bonn](3295.md), [Belfast](5046.md), [Queensland](59051.md), [Glen Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Eden), Washington, D.C., and [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee).

Iglesia ni Cristo church buildings (*kapilya*) primarily serve as places of worship but are also used for other religious functions. These are described by *Culture and Customs of the Philippines*, a book published by [Greenwood Publishing Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_Publishing_Group), as structures "which employ exterior [neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo_Gothic) vertical support columns with tall narrow windows between, interlocking [trapezoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezoid), and [rosette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosette_\(design\)) [motifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motif_\(visual_arts\)), as well as tower and [spires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spire)." According to Architect [Carlos A. Santos-Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Santos-Viola), in designing INC edifices, he had to create a style that "cannot be mistaken for any other sect except Iglesia." He also related how Manalo considered the [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) as the "most religious type of architecture" for its verticality, which was interpreted as "pointing towards heaven."[^cite_note-pundasyon-97] There are multiple entrances leading to the main [sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary), where males and females sit on either side of the [aisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisle) facing a dais where sermons are made. The [choir loft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir_loft) is located behind the dais, and in larger churches, a [baptistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptistry) with pools for [immersion baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersion_baptism) is located at the back of the church.[^cite_note-culture_and_customs-205] Meanwhile, Fernando Nakpil-Zialcita, an [anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologist) from [Ateneo de Manila University](330339.md),[^cite_note-admu-206] said that INC churches can be uniquely identified for "its exuberant use of fanciful forms and ornaments \[and a] brilliant white facade whose silhouette is a cusped Gothic arch or a flattened [Saracenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracenic_architecture) (Muslim) arch."[^cite_note-reed-57] The distinctive spires represent "the reaching out of the faithful to God."[^cite_note-sophia-38] Prominent architects, such as [Juan Nakpil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Nakpil) (a [National Artist of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines) for architecture) and [Carlos A. Santos-Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Santos-Viola), had been involved in designing INC churches while the Engineering and Construction Department of INC, established in 1971 and headed by Felix Manalo's youngest son [Bienvenido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bienvenido_Manalo), oversees the uniformity in design of church buildings.[^cite_note-culture_and_customs-205][^cite_note-PHA-207]



The [INC Central Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INC_Central_Temple), the largest single congregation of the Iglesia ni Cristo, can accommodate up to 7,000 people.

The first chapel was built on Gabriela Street in Tondo, Manila in 1918, fashioned out of *sawali* (woven leaf panels), [nipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nypa_fruticans) and wood, typified the style and materials of the early chapels. After [World War II](32927.md), INC began to build concrete chapels, the first of these in Washington (Maceda), [Sampaloc, Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampaloc,_Manila) completed in 1948. Next came the chapel and former official residence of the executive minister in San Juan, [Rizal](232458.md) (now [San Juan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Metro_Manila), part of [Metropolitan Manila](72340.md)), designed by Juan Nakpil.[^cite_note-208] The [Central Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INC_Central_Temple), which opened on 27 July 1984, can accommodate up to 7,000 persons, and was designed by [Carlos A. Santos-Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Santos-Viola).[^cite_note-209] It features [octagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagon_house) spires, "fine [latticework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latticework)" and ribbed windows. Recent buildings are variations on the designs of the Central Temple. These are designed to accommodate 250 to 1,000 persons while larger churches in [Metro Manila](72340.md) and provincial capitals can accommodate up to 3,000 persons.[^cite_note-reed-57]

The church regards "decent" and "well-constructed" chapels and houses of worship as the only fitting places to conduct worship services.[^cite_note-eranof-102] INC churches outside the Philippines which were acquired from different religions were converted and underwent intensive renovations to meet the standard of INC worship services.[^cite_note-210]

## Political influence in the Philippines

See also: [Iglesia ni Cristo and Philippine elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_and_Philippine_elections), [2015 Iglesia ni Cristo leadership controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Iglesia_ni_Cristo_leadership_controversy), [2015 Iglesia ni Cristo protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Iglesia_ni_Cristo_protests), [National Rally for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rally_for_Peace), and [Rally for Transparency and a Better Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_Transparency_and_a_Better_Democracy)



President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) meets with INC Executive Minister [Eduardo V. Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_V._Manalo) in December 2018

The Iglesia ni Cristo is close to [fundamentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_fundamentalism) style and supports conservative politicians.[^cite_note-introvigne-31] Since the days of [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) at the helm, the church is known for its practice of [bloc voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloc_voting) during elections.[^cite_note-zabala-71][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213] This so-called "[Unity Vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_bloc)" is justified by the INC as one of their "[great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\))" (*dakilang kapangyarihan*), explained as such in their official publication *Pasugo*: "*Ang katangiang ito sa pagkakaisa ng Iglesia Ni Kristo ay isang lakas na hindi matutularan ng ibat-ibang relihiyon sa Pilipinas, bagama't iyan ang sa ngayo'y pinagsisikapang magawa ng Iglesia Katolika at iba pang mga relihiyon.*" (This characteristic of unity of the Iglesia ni Kristo is a strength that could not be emulated by the different religions in the Philippines, despite this being the current aim of the [Catholic Church](606848.md) and other religions).[^cite_note-214]

During the 2016 presidential election, INC communities in [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur), [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), [Rizal](232458.md), [Dasmariñas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasmari%C3%B1as), and [Quezon City](284223.md) delivered 98% to 100% of the total votes to the endorsed candidates.[^cite_note-215] The church supported the candidacy of [Benigno "Noynoy" Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) during the 2010 presidential elections,[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] and also voted for [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) in the 2016 presidential elections.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219] In the 2022 elections, Iglesia ni Cristo endorsed the candidacies of [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) and [Sara Duterte](42504861.md) for president and vice president respectively.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] In the 2025 senatorial elections, the church supported the senatorial candidacy of INC member [Rodante Marcoleta](68240498.md).[^cite_note-222]

In the [1935 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Philippine_presidential_election), Senate President [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) reportedly cultivated the support of the INC, to the point of calling [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) a bishop (whereas there is no such title in the INC hierarchy).[^cite_note-mangahas-223][^cite_note-224] While still a faithful member of the [Catholic Church](606848.md)[^cite_note-226], Quezon's courage to stand up to the predominant religious institution in the Philippines earned him points to Manalo and his church,[^cite_note-227] which as of 1936 had reported having 85,000 members across 500 congregations, all of which were in Luzon at the time.[^cite_note-kavanagh-153]



President [Ramon Magsaysay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Magsaysay) with [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo) of the INC holding a cigar. Manalo initially supported Magsaysay, then criticized the president for the latter's perceived [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism).

In the [1961 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Philippine_presidential_election), the INC opposed the campaign of [Diosdado Macapagal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Macapagal), who would eventually win the polls and make him the first Filipino vice president to defeat an incumbent president. At this time, the INC is seen by the media as "the [bogey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogeyman) of Philippine politics."[^cite_note-228] For the vice presidency, the INC supported the Independent candidate [Sergio Osmeña, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a,_Jr.), who lost to Macapagal's running mate [Emmanuel Pelaez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Pelaez), in a three-way race that saw Osmeña taking second place with nearly 2.2 million votes. According to Filipino journalist and publisher [Max Soliven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Soliven), more moderate estimates of the bloc voting power of the "militant and politically-minded" INC at the time totaled to 250,000 votes, contrary to [Felix Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Manalo)'s claim of having 850,000 members.[^cite_note-229]

In the [1965 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Philippine_presidential_election), support from [Eraño Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_Manalo) and the INC was one of the attributed reasons behind the victory of [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) over the incumbent Macapagal.[^cite_note-230]



President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) and some Philippine senators (namely [Sergio Osmeña, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a,_Jr.), [Arturo Tolentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Tolentino), [Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino), and [Jose Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Roy)) during a birthday celebration of Executive Minister [Eraño Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era%C3%B1o_Manalo).

In the [1969 Philippine vice presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_presidential_election), the INC supported the losing bid of [Genaro Magsaysay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genaro_Magsaysay), running mate of presidential candidate [Sergio Osmeña, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a,_Jr.), in a split ticket (that is, Marcos-Magsaysay)[^cite_note-232]. Incumbent Vice President [Fernando Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Lopez), the first Filipino to be elected to the vice presidency thrice and later Chairman of [ABS-CBN Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN_Corporation), believed he has "debunked Iglesia political power with the help of responsible voters." In victory, Lopez remarked that he would rather lose the election than "surrender" to the INC. Lopez (with 62.7 percent vote share) also polled higher than his running mate, President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) (with 62.2 percent vote share).[^cite_note-233]

On 23 September 1972, when President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) announced placing [Martial law in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_the_Philippines), [MetroCom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary_Metropolitan_Command) troops led by General [Fidel Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Ramos) stormed the [INC Central Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INC_Central_Temple) to close down the church's media arm [Eagle Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Broadcasting_Corporation). However, having no orders from the Executive Minister to allow government forces to enter in their compound, the INC guards armed with [Thompson submachine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_submachine_gun) opened fire to defend the place, and killed one [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) in the process.[^cite_note-234] By the time Defense Minister [Juan Ponce Enrile](3322486.md) arrived in the area, MetroCom troops have already retaliated to the church defiance and killed at least 12 INC guards. After discussions with the church administration, government forces were successful in shutting down the INC media. Enrile called the battle as "the only violent incident" of the day, while Media Czar [Primitivo Mijares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitivo_Mijares) dubbed it as the "only armed resistance to the military efforts to enforce martial law."[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236]

In the [1986 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Philippine_presidential_election), while the church administration supported the reelection bid of President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), Filipino investigative journalist Malou Mangahas wrote that the decision "came close to seriously dividing its flock" as errant members voted for the opposition candidate [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino). This prompted a [house-to-house campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Door-to-door) by INC ministers to determine who voted against the church administration's choice (*tagubilin*). However, instead of being used as grounds for expulsion (*tiwalag*), erring members were instead compelled to write their explanations (*salaysay*).[^cite_note-mangahas-223]

A rally against inflation, particularly the rising prices of petroleum, was scheduled by the INC on 22 July 1991. Days before the event, the rally was called off. While the church administration claimed there was "no political agenda" in the planned rally, later recollections of the aborted rally would postulate that the government of President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) was allegedly "threatened" by the prospect of having 2 million INC members joining the demonstrations. According to the 1990 [Philippine census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_census), adherents of the INC at the time were around 1.4 million.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]

From 25 April to 1 May 2001, the INC formed the backbone of protesters who joined [EDSA III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_III), which supported the cause of Former President [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada).[^cite_note-mangahas-223][^cite_note-240][^cite_note-EDSA_INC-241]

### 2015 Iglesia ni Cristo leadership controversy

A dispute between senior members of the INC in the Philippines occurred in July 2015. It was reported that the INC had [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication) some of its ministers, along with high-profile members Felix Nathaniel "Angel" Manalo and [Cristina "Tenny" Villanueva Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenny_Manalo), the brother and mother of current INC Executive Minister [Eduardo Manalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Manalo), respectively.[^cite_note-supporters-282]

The INC administration released a statement claiming that Eduardo had agreed to the expulsion of his brother and mother from the INC, as decided upon by its overall leadership.[^cite_note-supporters-282] Angel and Tenny had reportedly been illegally detained at the Iglesia's Central Office Complex in [Tandang Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandang_Sora), [Quezon City](284223.md), and that at least ten ministers of the Church were missing and alleged to have been abducted.[^cite_note-coverup-283]

Former INC ministers Roel Rosal and Isaias Samson, Jr., claimed that the *Sanggunián* (the highest administrative council of the INC) had unlawfully abducted and detained ministers, along with members of the Manalo family, to cover up corruption surrounding the chief auditor, Glicerio "Jun" Santos, Jr.[^cite_note-Inquirer6-284] On 24 July 2015, the INC, represented by Glicerio B. Santos IV, head counsel and son of Santos, Jr.,[^cite_note-285] filed a libel complaint against Samson.[^cite_note-286] Detained INC Evangelical Worker Lowell Menorca stated that he was forcibly detained by the INC administration, and was kidnapped at gunpoint by police officers in the employ of INC leaders and was forced to deny his captivity under duress.[^cite_note-287] Menorca later fled to Canada and filed for refugee status, which was granted in 2018, with the [Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Refugee_Board_of_Canada) stating: "When the panel considers the links between the INC and the law enforcement authorities in the Philippines, the general climate of impunity that pervades Philippines law enforcement, particularly with respect to the issue of [extrajudicial killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing), and the level of corruption that exists in the Philippines government and law enforcement apparatus, the panel is satisfied Menorca would be unable to avail himself of state protection, from the risks that he fears in that country..."[^cite_note-288]

### Political endorsements

The Iglesia ni Cristo is known for its political endorsements every election season in the Philippines. The church has previously endorsed the presidential candidacies of the dictator [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), who was ousted by the first People Power revolution and escaped to the United States amid corruption scandals,[^cite_note-289][^cite_note-290] [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada), who was ousted by the second People Power revolution and arrested for plunder,[^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292] [Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal-Arroyo), who was arrested for plunder,[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294] [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md), who was arrested for [crimes against humanity](61099.md) by the [International Criminal Court](14880.md),[^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296] and [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md).[^cite_note-297] It has endorsed the vice presidential candidacy of [Sara Duterte](42504861.md), who has been impeached twice.[^cite_note-298][^cite_note-299] The church has previously endorsed the re-election bids of senators [Jinggoy Estrada](2395334.md), who has been arrested thrice for plunder,[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] [Bong Revilla](8452762.md), who has been arrested twice for plunder,[^cite_note-302][^cite_note-303] and fellow INC member [Rodante Marcoleta](68240498.md), who has been arrested for plunder.[^cite_note-304][^cite_note-305] It has also endorsed the mayorship of [Alice Guo](76882460.md) (Guo Hua Ping), a Chinese spy and convicted human trafficker linked to the controversial [Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators](61693885.md) (POGOs) scandal.[^cite_note-306][^cite_note-307][^cite_note-308][^cite_note-309][^cite_note-310]

## Felix Y. Manalo Foundation

The Felix Y. Manalo (FYM) Foundation is the humanitarian outreach arm of the Iglesia ni Cristo (INC) church.[^cite_note-311] It manages INC's international aid programs providing free medical and dental clinics, educational assistance, disaster relief and other essential goods to needy communities.[^cite_note-312]

In August 2022, the FYM Foundation, in partnership with Iglesia ni Cristo congregations in Canada, distributed 2,000 grocery bags and donated funds to local charities during a *Care for Humanity* event in Red Deer.[^cite_note-313] In 2022, FYM Foundation received the 2021 Presidential Award for Filipino Individuals and Organizations Overseas (PAFIOO) under the "Banaag" category.[^cite_note-314]

## Reception from other religions and denominations

See also: [Religion in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Philippines)



Concentration of Iglesia Ni Cristo members in the Philippines according to the 2020 Philippine Census per barangay level. Barangay New Era in [Quezon City](284223.md) where the [INC Central Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INC_Central_Temple) is located has a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) majority (53.2 percent of the barangay population adhere to [Islam](6037917.md), compared to 32.9 percent adhering to INC). Meanwhile, the barangay where the church's second oldest congregation [Iglesia ni Cristo chapel, Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo_chapel,_Tondo) is located in [Manila](184334.md) has a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) majority (95.1 percent adhering to [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism), compared to 2.1 percent adhering to INC).

[Hukbalahap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukbalahap) leader and [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism) [Christian socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_socialist) [Luis Taruc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Taruc) wrote in his book *Born of the People* (1949 and 1953) that during [World War II](32927.md), Filipino landlords and the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines) used members of the INC as [civilian guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_the_Empire_of_Japan), with signs labeled as *I am Iglesia* seen as guarantee of protection.[^cite_note-315]

Joseph Kavanagh of the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits), a teacher at the [San Jose Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Seminary), observed in 1955 that many of the doctrinal attacks of the INC, particularly against the [Catholic Church](606848.md), were unoriginal and had been answered before. He also noted that Manalo's [anti-Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Catholicism) essentially makes him a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant), and the church doctrines were more of a "potpourri of borrowings from several different creeds, the fruit, undoubtedly, of Mr. Manalo's spiritual wanderings."[^cite_note-kavanagh-153]

Since 1980, there have been [conflicts between the INC and the Members Church of God International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflicts_between_Iglesia_ni_Cristo_and_Members_Church_of_God_International) (MCGI), when MCGI Overall Servant [Eliseo Soriano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliseo_Soriano) started his radio program *[Ang Dating Daan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Dating_Daan)* (ADD). Through his program, he discussed biblical issues and "exposed" what he believes to be wrong doctrines of other religious groups, including those of INC. In 2001, after 20 years of reticence, the INC launched its own program, *[Ang Tamang Daan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Tamang_Daan)*, then hosted by INC Ministers Maximo Bularan, Michael Sandoval, and Ramil Parba, as a direct response for the first time to *[Ang Dating Daan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Dating_Daan)*, featuring video footages and recordings of ADD hosts (besides Soriano himself, this roster included Josel Mallari and Willy Santiago) as issues were tackled and their format likened to a "[running debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputation)." Over time, the animosity between the two groups has intensified and the relationship has been severely strained.[^cite_note-316][^cite_note-317][^cite_note-318]

In 1983, [Christian Reformed Church in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Reformed_Church_in_North_America) minister Johan D. Tangelder called the INC a [pseudo-Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoreligion) or quasi-Christian sect. He also noted the "[regimented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regimen) lifestyle" and the "authoritarian organization" of the INC.[^cite_note-319]

[Karl Keating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Keating), the founder of [Catholic Answers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Answers), said in 1990 that the INC engages in [anti-Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Catholicism) and [anti-Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Protestantism) in its *God's Message* magazine. Keating views the church as being built on a set of anti-Catholic doctrines, and that their lessons, as well as their *God's Message* magazine are dedicated more to debunking Catholic and Protestant beliefs and doctrines than to explaining their own positions.[^cite_note-Keating-320]

In 1992, the [Movie and Television Review and Classification Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_and_Television_Review_and_Classification_Board) (MTRCB) gave an [X rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_rating) to the church's television program of the same name, *[Ang Iglesia ni Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Iglesia_ni_Cristo)*, for "criticizing different religions, based on their own interpretation of the Bible."[^cite_note-321] The MTRCB also recommended for the program to "delve on explaining their own faith and beliefs and avoid attacks on other faith." Banking on the principle of [freedom of religion](46006.md), the INC appealed the case to the [Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeals_of_the_Philippines) in 1995, which affirmed the MTRCB's actions, and to the [Supreme Court](1335534.md) in 1996, which reversed the appellate court's decision.[^cite_note-322] The principle applied in this particular case was also cited in a 2005 case between the MTRCB and Filipino media company [ABS-CBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN).[^cite_note-323]

Let Us Reason Ministries, an online [apologetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apologetics) research group, has challenged the Iglesia ni Cristo's doctrines that one can only receive [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_\(Christianity\)) if they are a member of the INC, and for saying that the INC has the sole authority from God to interpret and preach the Bible, while other religions do not.[^cite_note-LetUsReason2-324] They also say that the Iglesia ni Cristo fallaciously misinterprets Biblical passages to suit their doctrines.[^cite_note-LetUsReason3-325]

Ibn Majah, a former INC member who converted to [Islam](6037917.md), observed in 2006 that the [monotheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheistic) view of the INC "served as a bridge for him to embrace Islam." He also compared his conversion to Islam as "much better" than his experience with the INC. He also commended the [Muslim commitment to prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah), which he professed he has not found in the INC.[^cite_note-326]

In his Annual Report in 2016, [Adventist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventist) Executive Secretary G. T. Ng referred to the INC as a possible source of lessons for the [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md) on how to nurture their members. In particular, Ng remarked how the INC considers membership care seriously. Ng also said, "When members come to church, they report their presence. After the service is over, [elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_\(Christianity\)) and [deacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) take note of the names of the absentees and visit each one in the afternoon."[^cite_note-327]

[James White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_White_\(theologian\)), of [Alpha and Omega ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_and_Omega_ministries), challenged the theology of the Iglesia ni Cristo in a public debate. White and Jose Ventilacion of the Iglesia ni Cristo met for a debate on 21 April 2017, in [Rapid City, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_City,_South_Dakota). White also dispelled later rumors that he was converted into an INC member, saying that it was the INC being in "damage control mode."[^cite_note-SDAApologetics-328][^cite_note-329]

Dr. Yuchen Ma of [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University) classified the INC as a [new religious movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_religious_movement) (NRM), but has emphasized that although the INC claims to be the [one true church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_true_church), their doctrines and practices were clearly influenced by both Philippine Catholic and American Protestant traditions. A unique distinction of the INC was the Manalo claim of being the "[last messenger of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_prophet)." However, Ma also noted that despite having this doctrine, the INC "has experienced two changes in religious leadership within the Manalo family."[^cite_note-yuchen-32]

Christian platform Apologetics Index pointed out "unbiblical beliefs" of the INC, including opposition to the Trinity, inconsistency with how a creature (which Jesus is claimed to be by the INC) can be worshipped, [good works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_works) and obedience to the church administration will bring salvation, the fulfillment of prophecy through Manalo, and [soul sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mortalism).[^cite_note-330]

Kyle Butt, [D.Min.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Min.) of Apologetics Press commended the growth, the beautiful buildings, and the zealousness for evangelism of the INC. However, it was also observed that the church "veered from Christ’s teachings." He also noted inconsistencies with the church doctrine, particularly with how the Bible is interpreted, and how Jesus can be worshipped despite not being divine for INC members.[^cite_note-331]

A number of former INC members who have left or have been expelled from the church have since formed groups and communities.[^cite_note-332] One of the significant online communities supporting former members would be the [Subreddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subreddit) ex-Iglesia Ni Cristo (r/exIglesiaNiCristo) which has over 50,000 members (or "[apostates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostates)" as the group calls themselves), more than 3,000 contributions a week, and 1.7 million views a month (on average). While mainly composed of former members, as their name implies, their community which began in 2016 is also open to people of various religious backgrounds, including current INC members. Among the more known INC members who left the church are actresses [Kathryn Bernardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathryn_Bernardo), [Yasmien Kurdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasmien_Kurdi), and [Janice de Belen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janice_de_Belen).[^cite_note-333][^cite_note-334][^cite_note-335][^cite_note-336]

[Christ's Commission Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_Commission_Fellowship) (CCF) member Yvette Espiritu shared in a 2016 testimony how she left the INC despite her family having positions in the church, and how her life changed with CCF as she "experienced a [loving God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnibenevolence)." Espiritu also professed that she has never opened the Bible as an INC member since it was discouraged by the church. According to her, the INC cultivated fear in members like her by instilling the concept of an angry and [transactional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactionalism) God who punishes disobedience.[^cite_note-337][^cite_note-338]

In 2019, [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) apologist Edward K. Watson criticized the "juvenile level" and "outright lying" of INC doctrine, which taught that [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) and the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit) are not considered God, although the church itself was supposed to be called the "[Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ)", and the "effortless" switching of [Bible translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_translations) to support their ideology. He also labeled INC members as "mindless zombies" since the church dictated who to vote, imposed public [shunning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunning) and humiliation of disciplined or former members, and discouraged its members from conducting independent Bible study. Despite the INC harping the term "*magsuri*" or analyze/research during its evangelism campaigns, Watson shared how bullying and intimidation became the tactic for INC ministers and members against those who do try to analyze or research their beliefs and values. Notwithstanding the controlling nature of the church and its violation of basic human rights, Watson observed that the INC is particularly successful among Filipinos (but a failure among non-Filipinos) because "it satisfied the Filipino need for cultural pride and their desire for social harmony." According to him, INC members like hearing that they are "special and better than the rest." However, he also noted that financial stability is the top concern of all INC families, making it difficult for them to leave the church, especially those who rely on church subsidies for daily living and economic security, such as INC ministers themselves.[^cite_note-339]

In 2021, The [Bereans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereans) Apologetics Research Ministry outright observed that "the *Iglesia Ni Cristo* is not a Christian church but a religious organization that continues to attack Christianity’s fundamentals, most notably the divinity of the Lord Jesus Christ and [salvation doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_in_Christianity)." It also regarded the church as a "cult" confused with the meaning of religion which presented the following contradiction: while it rejects the [deity of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christology), the INC official publication *Pasugo* contained frequent quotations from [Trinitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinitarian) theologians and scholars. To witness to INC members, The [Bereans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereans) Apologetics Research Ministry suggested various approaches such as using the context of the Bible verses being cited to avoid misquotations, providing questions that require critical thinking, and avoiding trivial or peripheral beliefs which do not contribute to learning the core values of Christianity.[^cite_note-340][^cite_note-341]

A book-length Catholic treatment of INC history and teachings is: Elesterio, Fernando, *The Iglesia Ni Kristo: Its Christology and Ecclesiology*, Quezon City, Philippines: Cardinal Bea Studies, [Cardinal Bea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Bea) Institute, Loyola School of Theology, [Ateneo de Manila University](330339.md), 1976.
