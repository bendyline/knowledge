The **territorial authorities of New Zealand** are the country's [municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) local government entities, existing alongside the country's [regional councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_New_Zealand). There are 67 such authorities, including 12 city councils, 53 district councils and 2 [*sui generis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_generis) councils ([Auckland Council](22312574.md) and [Chatham Islands Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands_Council)). Five territorial authorities[^cite_note-1] also have the powers of regional councils, and these are called **unitary authorities**.

Territorial authorities are not subservient to regional councils, and several of them have jurisdictions overlapping multiple regional council areas. Territorial authorities are responsible for the administration of local matters and resources. These include roads, building consents, water supply and sanitation, amongst other responsibilities. Non-unitary territorial authorities are not responsible for broader regional environmental management or public transport, as those are the responsibilities of the regional councils.

## History

In 1840 the [Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Office) was given the authority to divide New Zealand into counties, [hundreds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_\(county_division\)), towns, townships, and [parishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parish). These divisions were to be of set sizes (similar to North American divisions, many of which are rectangular in shape) but this was rarely implemented.[^cite_note-2]

### 1989 local government reforms

For many decades until the [local government reforms of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_New_Zealand_local_government_reforms), a borough with more than 20,000 people could be proclaimed a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_in_New_Zealand). The boundaries of councils tended to follow the edge of the built-up area, so little distinction was made between the urban area and the local government area.

New Zealand's local government structural arrangements were significantly reformed by the [Local Government Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Commission_\(New_Zealand\)) in 1989 when [approximately 700 councils and special purpose bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_former_territorial_authorities_in_New_Zealand) were amalgamated to create 87 new local authorities. Regional councils were reduced in number from 20 to 13, territorial authorities (city/district councils) from 200 to 75, and special purpose bodies from over 400 to 7.[^cite_note-3] The new district and city councils were generally much larger and most covered substantial areas of both urban and rural land. Many places that once had a city council were now being administered by a district council.

As a result, the term "city" began to take on two meanings.

*City* also came to be used in a less formal sense to describe major urban areas independent of local body boundaries. This informal usage is jealously guarded. Gisborne, for example, adamantly described itself as the first city in the world to see the new millennium. Gisborne is administered by a district council, but its status as a city is not generally disputed.

Under current law, an urban area has to be at least 50,000 residents before it can be officially proclaimed as a city.[^cite_note-4]

### Changes since 1989

Since the 1989 reorganisations, there have been few major reorganisations or status changes in local government. Incomplete list:

- 1991: [Invercargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invercargill,_New_Zealand) re-proclaimed a city.

- 1992: [Nelson-Marlborough Regional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson-Marlborough_Regional_Council) abolished by a Local Government Amendment Act. Of its territorial authorities, the [Kaikōura District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaik%C5%8Dura_District) was transferred to the Canterbury Region, and [Nelson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_City_Council) and [Tasman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_District) and [Marlborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlborough_District) districts became [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities).

- 1995: The [Chatham Islands County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands) was dissolved and reconstituted by an [Act of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament#New_Zealand) as the "Chatham Islands Territory", with powers similar to those of territorial authorities and some functions similar to those of a regional council.

  [^cite_note-5]

- 2004: [Tauranga](31495916.md) became a city again on 1 March.

- 2006: The [Banks Peninsula District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banks_Peninsula_District) merged into [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch) as a result of a 2005 referendum.

- 2010: [Auckland Council](22312574.md), a unitary authority, replaced seven local councils and the regional council.

Reports on completed reorganisation proposals since 1999 are available on the Local Government Commission's site (link below).

### 2007–2009 Royal Commission on Auckland Governance

On 26 March 2009, the [Royal Commission on Auckland Governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_Auckland_Governance) recommended the Rodney, North Shore, Waitakere, Auckland City, Manukau, Papakura and Franklin territorial councils and the Auckland Regional Council be abolished and the entire Auckland region to be amalgamated into one "supercity".[^cite_note-6] The area would consist of one city council (with statutory provision for three [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) councillors), four urban local councils, and two rural local councils:

- Rodney local council would lose Orewa, Dairy Flat, and Whangaparaoa but retain the remainder of the current Rodney District. The split areas as well as the current North Shore City would form a Waitemata local council.

- Waitakere local council would consist of the current Waitakere City as well as the Avondale area.

- Tamaki Makaurau would consist of the current Auckland City and Otahuhu (excluding [CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_CBD))

- Manukau local council would consist of the urban parts of the current Manukau City and of the Papakura District.

- Hunua local council would consist of the entire Franklin District, much of which is currently in the Waikato Region, along with rural areas of the current Papakura District and Manukau City.

- The entire Papakura District would be dissolved between urban and rural councils.

The National-led Government responded within about a week. Its plan, which went to a Select Committee, accepted the proposal for supercity and many community boards, but rejected proposals for local councils and, initially, no [separate seats for Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_wards_and_constituencies).

Public reaction to the Royal Commission report was mixed, especially in regards to the Government's amended proposal. Auckland Mayor [John Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Banks_\(New_Zealand_politician\)) supported the amended merger plans.[^cite_note-7]

Criticism of the amended proposal came largely from residents in Manukau, Waitakere and North Shore Cities.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In addition, Māori Affairs Minister [Pita Sharples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pita_Sharples) spoke against the exclusion of the Māori seats, as recommended by the Royal Commission.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Opposition Leader [Phil Goff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Goff) called for a referendum on the issue.[^cite_note-17]

### Creation of Auckland Council

[Auckland Council](22312574.md) was created on 1 November 2010—a unitary authority that is classed as both a region and a territorial authority. It incorporated the recommendations of the Royal Commission and was established via legislation.[^cite_note-18] Auckland Council is uniquely divided into "local boards" representing the lowest tier of local government.[^cite_note-19]

The Commission received 23 responses, 19 of which made specific proposals for change. In December 2017 the Local Government Commission determined its preferred option for local government reorganisation on the West Coast to be the transfer of district plan preparation from the Buller, Grey and Westland district councils to the West Coast Regional Council.

## Governance

### Mayors

Further information: [Mayors in New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_in_New_Zealand)

The directly elected leaders of territorial authorities are called [mayors](101146.md). They chair local council meetings and have limited [executive powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_powers), including the ability to appoint a deputy mayor, establish committees, and select chairpersons for said committees. Their constitutional role, as laid out in the Local Government Act 2002, is to provide leadership to their councillors and citizens of their districts, and to guide the direction of council plans and policies.

### Youth councils

Under the terms of the [Local Government Act 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_2002), district councils have to represent the interests of their future communities and consider the views of people affected by their decisions. To fulfill that requirement and give young people a say in the decision-making process, many councils have a youth council. [^cite_note-Renews_Apr_2024-36] In late December 2023, the [Ashburton District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashburton_District_Council) scrapped their youth council, stating they could engage better with younger people online and describing the current youth council as "a youth club where they ate pizza."[^cite_note-37] In early January 2024, the [Gore District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gore_District_Council) opted to restructure its youth council and ruled out dismantling it.[^cite_note-38] In April 2024, the [Whanganui District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whanganui_District_Council) proposed scrapping its youth council by June 2024 as part of budget saving measures.[^cite_note-Renews_Apr_2024-36]

## Community and local boards

Main articles: [Community boards in New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_boards_in_New_Zealand) and [Local boards of Auckland Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_boards_of_Auckland_Council)

| Community and local boards |  |
| --- | --- |
| Community boards  Local boards |  |
| Location | [New Zealand](4913064.md) |
| Number | 111 community boards21 local boards (as of 2025) |

Any territorial authority can set up subdivisions of itself to represent specific communities, known as community or local boards. These were first established as part of the [1989 local government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_New_Zealand_local_government_reforms), and are currently defined by the [Local Government Act 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_2002).

Community boards are sub-district, unincorporated [local government bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_New_Zealand) that may be established for any contiguous area in a territorial authority district.[^cite_note-guide-41] They are intended to represent and advocate for specific communities within a council catchment area. As of 2025, there are around 110 community boards across New Zealand.[^cite_note-42]

Local boards have greater powers than community boards. As of 2025, [Auckland Council](22312574.md) is the only territorial authority in New Zealand with local boards,[^cite_note-LGNZ-43] which were established by the Local Government (Auckland Council) Act 2009.

Auckland Council has 21 local boards covering the entire city which provide governance at the local level and are responsible for libraries and other community facilities, local parks and events, and have the power to develop local by-laws or propose local targeted rates.[^cite_note-LGNZ-43] Each local board has 5 to 9 elected members, with 149 local board members across all boards.[^cite_note-44]
