The **National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration** (**NOAA** [/ˈnoʊ.ə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*NOH-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a United States scientific and [regulatory agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_agency) tasked with [forecasting weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_forecasting), monitoring oceanic and atmospheric conditions, [charting the seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography), conducting [deep-sea exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep-sea_exploration), and managing fishing and protection of marine mammals and endangered species in the US [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone). The agency is part of the [United States Department of Commerce](70243.md) and is headquartered in [Silver Spring, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Spring,_Maryland).

## History

### Founding

NOAA traces its history back to multiple agencies,[^cite_note-history-8] some of which are among the earliest in the federal government:[^cite_note-lib.noaa.gov-9]

- [United States Coast and Geodetic Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_and_Geodetic_Survey), formed in 1807

- [Weather Bureau of the United States](286989.md), formed in 1870

- [Bureau of Commercial Fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_Commission), formed in 1871 (research fleet only)

- [Coast and Geodetic Survey Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Commissioned_Officer_Corps#Coast_and_Geodetic_Survey_Corps), formed in 1917

### 20th century

The most direct predecessor of NOAA was the [Environmental Science Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Science_Services_Administration) (ESSA), into which several existing scientific agencies such as the [United States Coast and Geodetic Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_and_Geodetic_Survey), the [Weather Bureau](286989.md), and the [uniformed Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Commissioned_Corps) were absorbed in 1965.[^cite_note-lib.noaa.gov-9]

NOAA was established within the Department of Commerce via the Reorganization Plan No. 4,[^cite_note-lib.noaa.gov-9] and formed on October 3, 1970, after U.S. President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) proposed creating a new agency to serve a national need for "better protection of life and property from natural hazards... for a better understanding of the total environment... \[and] for exploration and development leading to the intelligent use of our marine resources".[^cite_note-10]

NOAA is a part of the Department of Commerce rather than the [Department of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior), because of a feud between President Nixon and his interior secretary, [Wally Hickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Hickel), over the Nixon Administration's [Vietnam War](32611.md) policy. Nixon did not like Hickel's letter urging Nixon to listen to the Vietnam War demonstrators,[^cite_note-11] and punished Hickel by not putting NOAA in the Interior Department.[^cite_note-12]

### 21st century

In 2007, NOAA celebrated 200 years of service in its role as successor to the U.S. [Survey of the Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_and_Geodetic_Survey).[^cite_note-13]

In 2021, NOAA had 11,833 civilian employees.[^cite_note-BestPlacesToWork.org_Agency_Report-6] Its research and operations are further supported by 321 [uniformed service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformed_services_of_the_United_States) members, who made up the [NOAA Commissioned Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Commissioned_Corps).[^cite_note-14]

In 2024, [Project 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_2025) proposed to get rid of the [Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Oceanic_and_Atmospheric_Research), which would "dismantle" NOAA's research division.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Smith-2024-16]

[NOAA has experienced numerous changes under the second presidency of Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_under_the_second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump). On February 27, 2025, several hundred NOAA staffers, mainly probationary, were laid off after staffers from the [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency) (DOGE) entered the headquarters of NOAA.[^cite_note-17] By early March, 1,300 NOAA staff members (roughly 10% of the total workforce) were laid off.[^cite_note-18]

In June, 2025, the Department of Defense announced it would no longer provide critical weather data to scientists and forecasters, including to NOAA staff.[^cite_note-19]

## Organizational structure

### Silver Spring Campus

Since 1993, NOAA's administrative headquarters has been located at the Silver Spring Metro Center office complex in downtown [Silver Spring, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Spring,_Maryland).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The consolidated 1.2 million sq ft (110,000 m<sup>2</sup>), four-building campus was constructed in 1993 and is home to over 40 NOAA sub-agencies and offices, including the [National Weather Service](286989.md).

### Administrator

Main article: [Under Secretary of Commerce for Oceans and Atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Commerce_for_Oceans_and_Atmosphere)

[Neil Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Jacobs) was confirmed as Under Secretary for Oceans and Atmosphere and NOAA Administrator on Oct 7th, 2025 and he took over shortly thereafter.[^cite_note-22] Prior to that, NOAA Chief of Staff Laura Grimm had been the Acting Under Secretary since March 31, 2025 and prior to that [Nancy Hann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Hann) had held this role following the change of administrations on January 20, 2025 when [Rick Spinrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Spinrad) resigned.[^cite_note-23]

[Neil Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Jacobs), then Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Environmental Observation and Prediction, served as acting [Under Secretary of Commerce for Oceans and Atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Commerce_for_Oceans_and_Atmosphere) at the US Department of Commerce and as NOAA's interim administrator from February 25, 2019, to January 20, 2021 during the first Trump Administration.[^cite_note-24] Jacobs succeeded [Timothy Gallaudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Gallaudet), who succeeded Benjamin Friedman. The three served in series as NOAA's interim administrator throughout the [first Trump Administration](52231341.md).[^cite_note-25]

### Independent agency proposal

NOAA was created by an executive order in 1970 and has never been established in law, despite its critical role. In January 2023, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported that Congressman [Frank Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lucas_\(Oklahoma_politician\)), the new chair of the House Science, Space and Technology Committee, had released draft legislation to make NOAA an independent agency, rather than it being part of the Commerce Department. Lucas' push was in response to Republican leaders who had signaled plans to slash funding for agencies and programs that continued to receive annual appropriations, but had not been reauthorized by Congress. "It's been made quite clear in the Republican conference that my friends don't want to fund programs that are not properly authorized," said Lucas. "NOAA is very important, so we need to get it authorized."[^cite_note-26]

### Services

NOAA works toward its mission through six major line offices: the [National Environmental Satellite, Data, and Information Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environmental_Satellite,_Data,_and_Information_Service) (NESDIS), the [National Marine Fisheries Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Fisheries_Service) (NMFS), the [National Ocean Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ocean_Service) (NOS), the [National Weather Service](286989.md) (NWS), the [Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Oceanic_and_Atmospheric_Research) (OAR) and the [Office of Marine and Aviation Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Marine_and_Aviation_Operations) (OMAO).[^cite_note-NOAA-27] NOAA has more than a dozen staff offices, including the Office of the Federal Coordinator for Meteorology, the [NOAA Central Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Central_Library), the Office of Program Planning and Integration (PPI).[^cite_note-NOAA-27]

#### National Weather Service

Main article: [National Weather Service](286989.md)

The [National Weather Service](286989.md) (NWS) is tasked with providing "weather, hydrologic and climate forecasts and warnings for the United States, its territories, adjacent waters and ocean areas, for the protection of life and property and the enhancement of the national economy", according to NOAA.[^cite_note-28] This is done through a collection of national and regional centers, 13 river forecast centers (RFCs), and more than 120 local weather forecast offices (WFOs).[^cite_note-29] They are charged with issuing weather and river [forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_forecasting), [advisories, watches, and warnings](2241482.md) on a daily basis. They issue more than 734,000 weather and 850,000 river forecasts, and more than 45,000 severe weather warnings annually. NOAA data is also relevant to the issues of [climate change](5042951.md) and [ozone depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_depletion).[^cite_note-30]

The NWS operates [NEXRAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXRAD), a nationwide network of Doppler [weather radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar) which can detect [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) and their velocities. Many of their products are broadcast on [NOAA Weather Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Weather_Radio), a network of radio [transmitters](61164.md) that broadcasts weather forecasts, severe weather statements, watches and warnings 24 hours a day.[^cite_note-31]

#### National Ocean Service

Main article: [National Ocean Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ocean_Service)

The [National Ocean Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ocean_Service) (NOS) focuses on ensuring that ocean and coastal areas are safe, healthy, and productive. NOS scientists, natural resource managers, and specialists serve America by ensuring safe and efficient marine transportation, promoting innovative solutions to protect coastal communities, and conserving marine and coastal places.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

The National Ocean Service is composed of eight program offices: the Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services,[^cite_note-34] the Office for Coastal Management,[^cite_note-35] the National Centers for Coastal Ocean Science,[^cite_note-36] the [Office of Coast Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Coast_Survey),[^cite_note-37] the Office of [National Geodetic Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geodetic_Survey),[^cite_note-38] the Office of [National Marine Sanctuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Sanctuaries),[^cite_note-39] the Office of Ocean and Coastal Resource Management,[^cite_note-40] and the [Office of Response and Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Response_and_Restoration).[^cite_note-41]

There are two NOS programs, the [Mussel Watch Contaminant Monitoring Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussel_Watch_Program) and the NOAA Integrated Ocean Observing System (IOOS). There are two staff offices, the International Program Office and the Management and Budget Office.

#### National Environmental Satellite, Data, and Information Service

Main article: [National Environmental Satellite, Data, and Information Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environmental_Satellite,_Data,_and_Information_Service)



A NOAA engineer at work

The [National Environmental Satellite, Data, and Information Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environmental_Satellite,_Data,_and_Information_Service) (NESDIS) was created by NOAA to operate and manage the US environmental satellite programs, and manage NWS data and those of other government agencies and departments.[^cite_note-42] NESDIS's [National Centers for Environmental Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centers_for_Environmental_Information) (NCEI) archives data collected by the NOAA, [United States Navy](20518076.md), [United States Air Force](32090.md), the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md), and meteorological services around the world. It comprises the Center for Weather and Climate, previously NOAA's [National Climatic Data Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Climatic_Data_Center), the National Coastal Data Development Center (NCDDC), the [National Oceanographic Data Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Oceanographic_Data_Center) (NODC), and the [National Geophysical Data Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geophysical_Data_Center) (NGDC)).

In 1960, [TIROS-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIROS-1), NASA's first owned and operated geostationary satellite, was launched. Since 1966, NESDIS has managed polar orbiting satellites (POES). Since 1974, it has operated [geosynchronous satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geosynchronous_satellites) (GOES). In 1979, NOAA's first [polar-orbiting environmental satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_Operational_Environmental_Satellites) was launched. Current operational satellites include [NOAA-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA-15), [NOAA-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA-18), [NOAA-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA-19), [GOES 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES_13), [GOES 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES_14), [GOES 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES_15), [Jason-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason-2), [DSCOVR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSCOVR) and [SWO-F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Weather_Follow_On-Lagrange_1). In 1983, NOAA assumed operational responsibility for the [Landsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsat_program) satellite system.[^cite_note-43]

Since May 1998, NESDIS has operated the [Defense Meteorological Satellite Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Meteorological_Satellite_Program) (DMSP) satellites on behalf of the [Air Force Weather Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/557th_Weather_Wing).[^cite_note-44]

New generations of satellites are developed to succeed the current polar orbiting and geosynchronous satellites, the [Joint Polar Satellite System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Polar_Satellite_System), and [GOES-R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES-R), which launched in November 2016.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

NESDIS runs the Office of Projects, Planning, and Analysis (OPPA) formerly the Office of Systems Development,[^cite_note-47] the Office of Satellite Ground Systems (formerly the Office of Satellite Operations)[^cite_note-48] the Office of Satellite and Project Operations,[^cite_note-49] the Center for Satellite Applications and Research (STAR)],[^cite_note-50] the Joint Polar Satellite System Program Office[^cite_note-51] the [GOES-R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostationary_Operational_Environmental_Satellite) Program Office, the International & Interagency Affairs Office, the [Office of Space Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Space_Commerce)[^cite_note-52] and the Office of System Architecture and Advanced Planning.

#### National Marine Fisheries Service

Main article: [National Marine Fisheries Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Fisheries_Service)

The [National Marine Fisheries Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Fisheries_Service) (NMFS), also known as NOAA Fisheries, was initiated in 1871 with a primary goal of the research, protection, management, and restoration of commercial and recreational [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries) and their habitat, and protected species. The NMFS operates twelve headquarters offices, five regional offices, six fisheries science centers, and more than 20 laboratories throughout the United States and U.S. territories, which are the sites of research and management of marine resources. The NMFS operates the [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Fisheries Office of Law Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Oceanic_and_Atmospheric_Administration_Fisheries_Office_of_Law_Enforcement) in [Silver Spring, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Spring,_Maryland), which is the primary site of marine resource law enforcement.

#### Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research

Main article: [Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Oceanic_and_Atmospheric_Research)

NOAA's research, conducted through the Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research (OAR), is the driving force behind NOAA environmental products and services that protect life and property and promote economic growth. Research, conducted in OAR laboratories and by extramural programs, focuses on enhancing our understanding of environmental phenomena such as tornadoes, hurricanes, climate variability, solar flares, changes in the ozone, [air pollution transport and dispersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_dispersion_modeling),[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] [El Niño](317311.md)/[La Niña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ni%C3%B1a) events, fisheries productivity, ocean currents, deep sea thermal vents, and coastal ecosystem health. NOAA research also develops innovative technologies and observing systems.

The NOAA Research network consists of seven internal research laboratories, extramural research at 30 [Sea Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Grant_Colleges) university and research programs, six undersea research centers, a research grants program through the Climate Program Office, and 13 cooperative institutes with academia. NOAA and its academic partners are used by scientists, engineers, technicians, and graduate students.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The [Air Resources Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Resources_Laboratory) (ARL) is one of the laboratories in the Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research. It studies processes and develops models relating to climate and air quality, including the transport, dispersion, transformation and removal of [pollutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollutants) from the ambient atmosphere. The emphasis of the ARL's work is on data interpretation, technology development and transfer. The specific goal of ARL research is to improve and eventually to institutionalize prediction of trends, dispersion of [air pollutant plumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution_dispersion_terminology), [air quality](10934212.md), atmospheric deposition, and related variables.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The [Atlantic Oceanographic and Meteorological Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Oceanographic_and_Meteorological_Laboratory) (AOML), is part of NOAA's Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research, located in [Miami](53846.md), [Florida](18933066.md). AOML's research spans hurricanes, coastal ecosystems, oceans, and human health, climate studies, global carbon systems, and ocean observations. AOML's organizational structure consists of an Office of the Director and three scientific research divisions, Physical Oceanography, Ocean Chemistry and Ecosystems, and Hurricane Research. The Office of the Director oversees the Laboratory's scientific programs, as well as its financial, administrative, computer, outreach/education, and facility management services.[^cite_note-aoml.noaa.gov-59]

Research programs are augmented by the Cooperative Institute for Marine and Atmospheric Studies (CIMAS), a joint enterprise with the University of Miami's Rosenstiel School of Marine and Atmospheric Science. CIMAS enables AOML and university scientists to collaborate on research areas of mutual interest and facilitates the participation of students and visiting scientists. AOML is a member of a unique community of marine research and educational institutions located on Virginia Key in Miami, Florida.[^cite_note-aoml.noaa.gov-59]

In 1977, the [Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Marine_Environmental_Laboratory) (PMEL) deployed the first successful moored equatorial current meter – the beginning of the [Tropical Atmosphere Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Atmosphere_Ocean_project), TAO, array. In 1984, the [Tropical Ocean-Global Atmosphere program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Ocean-Global_Atmosphere_program) (TOGA) program began.

The [Arctic Report Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Report_Card) is the annual update charts of the ongoing impact of changing conditions on the environment and community by NOAA. In 2019, it was compiled by 81 scientists from 12 nations.[^cite_note-60]

## Hurricane Dorian forecast

Main articles: [Hurricane Dorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian) and [Hurricane Dorian-Alabama controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian-Alabama_controversy)

[Hurricane Dorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian) was an extremely powerful and destructive tropical cyclone that devastated the northwestern Bahamas and caused significant damage to the Southeastern United States and Atlantic Canada in September 2019. By September 1, NOAA had issued a statement saying that the "current forecast path of Dorian does not include Alabama". However, on that date, President Donald Trump tweeted that Alabama, among other states, "will most likely be hit (much) harder than anticipated".[^cite_note-Feldscher-68][^cite_note-Embury-69][^cite_note-guardian-7sep2019-70]

Shortly thereafter, the [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama) office of the National Weather Service issued a tweet that appeared to contradict Trump, saying that Alabama "will NOT see any impacts from Dorian". On September 6, NOAA published a statement from an unidentified spokesperson supporting Trump's September 1 claim. The statement also labelled the Birmingham, Alabama branch of the National Weather Service's contradiction of Trump as incorrect.[^cite_note-Feldscher-68][^cite_note-Embury-69][^cite_note-guardian-7sep2019-70] *The New York Times* reported that the NOAA September 6 statement was prompted by a threat from U.S. Commerce Secretary [Wilbur Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbur_Ross) to fire high-level NOAA staff unless they supported Trump's claim. The Department of Commerce described this report as "false".[^cite_note-BBCsack-71][^cite_note-72]

Meanwhile, *The Washington Post* reported that NOAA had twice ordered National Weather Service employees not to provide "any opinion" on Hurricane Dorian and to "only stick with official National Hurricane Center forecasts". The first order came after Trump's September 1 comments and the Birmingham, Alabama National Weather Service's contradiction of Trump. The second order came on September 4 after Trump displayed an August 29 map that was altered with a black marker to show that Hurricane Dorian may hit Alabama.[^cite_note-Freedman-73]

On September 9, speaking at an Alabama National Weather Service (NWS) meeting the Director of the National Weather Service gave a speech supporting Birmingham NWS and said the team "stopped public panic" and "ensured public safety". He said that when Birmingham issued their instructions they were not aware that the calls they were receiving were a result of Trump's tweet. The acting [chief scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Chief_Scientist) and assistant administrator for the ocean and atmospheric research said he is "pursuing the potential violations" of the agency's scientific integrity policy.[^cite_note-74]

## Flag



Flag of NOAA

The NOAA flag is a modification of the flag of one of its predecessor organizations, the [United States Coast and Geodetic Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_and_Geodetic_Survey). The Coast and Geodetic Survey's flag, authorized in 1899 and in use until 1970, was blue, with a white circle centered in it and a red triangle centered within the circle. It symbolized the use of [triangulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulation) in [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying), and was flown by ships of the Survey.[^cite_note-Pan-75][^cite_note-76]

When NOAA was established in 1970 and the Coast and Geodetic Survey's assets became a part of NOAA, NOAA based its own flag on that of the Coast and Geodetic Survey. The NOAA flag is, in essence, the Coast and Geodetic Survey flag, with the NOAA logo—a circle divided by the silhouette of a seabird into an upper dark blue and a lower light blue section, but with the "NOAA" legend omitted—centered within the red triangle. NOAA ships in [commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_commissioning) display the NOAA flag; those with only one [mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_\(sailing\)) fly it immediately beneath the ship's [commissioning pennant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioning_pennant) or the personal flag of a civilian official or [flag officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_officer) if one is aboard the ship, while multimasted vessels fly it at the masthead of the forwardmost mast.[^cite_note-77] NOAA ships fly the same [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(flag\)) as [United States Navy](20518076.md) ships but fly the NOAA flag as a distinguishing mark to differentiate themselves from Navy ships.
