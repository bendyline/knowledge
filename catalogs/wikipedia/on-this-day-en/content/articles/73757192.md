# Political violence in the United States

&#x20;  In the context of the [history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States), [political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence) is defined as [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence) which the state or a [non-state actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-state_actor) perpetrates in order to achieve political goals. Political violence has occurred throughout American history—from the [assassinations of four presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_presidential_assassination_attempts_and_plots) to [civil unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_incidents_of_civil_unrest_in_the_United_States) and [terrorist attacks](18563588.md). Experts report that incidents have increased significantly since 2016, reaching levels not seen since the 1970s. 21st-century data indicates that the highest incidence of deadly political violence in the United States has come from [right-wing extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_extremists), with [left-wing extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_extremists) and [Islamists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamists) being the other main ideological categories. Contributing factors toward political violence include dehumanizing [political rhetoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rhetoric) and [partisan division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics), [authoritarian personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarian_personality) traits, hostile [sexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexist) beliefs, [online radicalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_radicalization), and a sense of threat or increased fear.

Although widely discussed, realized acts of political violence in the United States are rare and constitute about 1% of all [hate crimes](13547.md).[^cite_note-Westwood,_Grimmer,_Tyler_&_Nall_2022-1]

## Definition

[Reuters](18998750.md) defined political violence thusly: "Incidents linked to an election or a partisan political dispute, or premeditated acts driven by an identifiable ideology. Random hate crimes and violence involving police such as officer-involved killings or aggressive crowd-control tactics at protests were not included."[^cite_note-Parker_&_Eisler_2023-2] The violence-prevention organization Over Zero defines it thusly: "Physical harm or intimidation that affects who benefits from or can participate fully in political, economic, or sociocultural life."[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3]

## Chronology

### 18th century



A 1902 illustration of the [Burr–Hamilton duel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr%E2%80%93Hamilton_duel)

Politically motivated violence was common during the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). In the late 1760s, [Sons of Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Liberty) militants would often attack suspected British loyalists, [tarring and feathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarring_and_feathering) them, a practice brought from Europe which resulted in lacerations and scarring.[^cite_note-Valsania-2025-4] Hangings, raids, and executions became more frequent in the 1770s.[^cite_note-Valsania-2025-4]

[Henry Brockholst Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Brockholst_Livingston), who had survived an assassination attempt in 1785, killed a man in a 1798 gun duel. Livingston's reputation was unharmed by the incident and he later became a [Supreme Court](31737.md) justice.[^cite_note-Valsania-2025-4] [Another duel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwinnett%E2%80%93McIntosh_duel) took place between former Georgia governor and [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) signatory [Button Gwinnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Button_Gwinnett) and his opponent [Lachlan McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachlan_McIntosh) in 1777; Gwinnett died after the shooting.[^cite_note-5] A number of [other pistol duels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_duels_in_the_United_States) took place in the 18th century.[^cite_note-Valsania-2025-4]

### 19th century



A depiction of Lincoln's assassination

Violence continued after the Revolution. In 1804, vice president [Aaron Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burr) killed [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States) [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) in [a gun duel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr%E2%80%93Hamilton_duel).[^cite_note-\:8-6] The [Know Nothing party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_Nothing) was responsible for a number of attacks against Catholics and immigrants in the early 1800s. In 1844, [Joseph Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Smith), the founder of [Mormonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism) and the [Latter-Day Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter-Day_Saint) movement, announced his [candidacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Smith_1844_presidential_campaign) for the presidency of the United States. He became the first assassinated presidential candidate in U.S. history when he was [shot and killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Joseph_Smith) by a mob on June 27.[^cite_note-7]

The topic of slavery also ignited several episodes of violence near Kansas during the 1850s.[^cite_note-8] The lead-up to the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) saw regional violence like the conflict over the expansion of slavery known as [Bleeding Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding_Kansas) and local protests against the [Fugitive Slave Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugitive_Slave_Act_of_1850) including the [Christiana Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiana_Riot) and the assemblies organized by the [Boston Vigilance Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Vigilance_Committee), as well as incidents at the [U.S. Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Capitol) such as the [caning of Charles Sumner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caning_of_Charles_Sumner) and the [1858 Congressional brawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1858_Congressional_brawl).[^cite_note-9]

In 1856, abolitionist [John Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_\(abolitionist\)) with like-minded settlers [killed five pro-slavery settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottawatomie_massacre) north of [Pottawatomie Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottawatomie_Creek) in Franklin County, Kansas.[^cite_note-10] In 1859, Brown led [a raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown's_raid_on_Harpers_Ferry) on the [United States arsenal at Harpers Ferry, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpers_Ferry_Armory) in an effort to initiate a [slave revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_revolt#North_America) in [Southern states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antebellum_South). It has been called the "dress rehearsal" for the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War).[^cite_note-Horwitz-11]<sup>: 5 </sup>

President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) was [assassinated in 1865](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln), and he had suffered two assassination attempts before that. The first president to be assassinated, Lincoln was shot by [John Wilkes Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilkes_Booth) while watching a theatre production.[^cite_note-\:9-12] One motive attributed to his killing was his support for Black rights.[^cite_note-\:9-12] The [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era) was also a period of considerable violence, including multiple [Freedmen massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedmen_massacres) and assassinations of federal appointees and locally elected officials.[^cite_note-13] In 1881, president [James A. Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Garfield) was [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_James_A._Garfield) six months after taking office.[^cite_note-\:10-14][^cite_note-\:9-12]

### 20th century

#### 1900–1920

President [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley) was [assassinated in 1901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_William_McKinley) by anarchist activist [Leon Czolgosz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Czolgosz), who was opposed to "the Republican form of government".[^cite_note-Firstpost-2025-15][^cite_note-\:10-14] Eleven years later, former president [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) survived [a gunshot in 1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Theodore_Roosevelt) while campaigning to return to office.[^cite_note-\:9-12] President-elect [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) survived a [shooting during a rally in 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Franklin_D._Roosevelt), but Chicago mayor [Anton Cermak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Cermak) was killed.[^cite_note-Firstpost-2025-15]



The building where the bomb intended for Rockefeller exploded, 1914

Bomb attacks were relatively common in the early 20th century. A [terrorist bomb exploded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_Avenue_explosion) prematurely inside an apartment of the [Anarchist Black Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_Black_Cross) in 1914. The bomb was intended to kill business magnate [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller), who had been blamed for the [Ludlow Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludlow_Massacre).[^cite_note-16] Another bomb set up by labor leaders [exploded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparedness_Day_bombing) in San Francisco during a [Preparedness Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparedness_Day) parade in 1916, killing 10 people.[^cite_note-17] The [Sacramento Governor's Mansion was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Sacramento_Governor's_Mansion_bombing) in 1917 during an attempt to assassinate governor [William Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Stephens_\(American_politician\)).[^cite_note-18] About 30 bombs were sent to political leaders in the country, including U.S. [attorney general](44002.md) [A. Mitchell Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Mitchell_Palmer), in 1919.[^cite_note-19]

Tensions over housing and labor following [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_in_World_War_I) led to the [Red Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Summer) of 1919, which involved [racial terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_conflict) and riots across the United States.[^cite_note-\:2-20] The death of a Black child at a [segregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States) swimming area, combined with labor tensions between White and Black workers, [led to riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Summer#Chicago_riots:_July_27%E2%80%93August_12) that killed thirty-eight people.[^cite_note-\:2-20] Black workers negotiating for better pay in [Elaine, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine,_Arkansas), led to [one of the most violent riots in U.S. history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_massacre).[^cite_note-\:2-20]

Political radicals detonated a [bomb in Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_bombing) in 1920, killing 38 people.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In 1921, the Black community of [Tulsa, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa,_Oklahoma), known for their wealth and prosperity, [was destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_race_massacre) by a violent White mob.[^cite_note-\:2-20] In 1932, the [Bonus Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonus_Army) was cleared. In 1935, former Louisiana Governor and senator [Huey Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huey_Long) was assassinated during a legislative session. Long had been considering a presidential bid at the time. In 1937, the [Ponce massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponce_massacre) was perpetrated.

#### 1940–1960

Following [World War II](32927.md), Black veterans were attacked by White Americans who now had more competition for jobs and housing.[^cite_note-\:2-20] President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) was the target of [an assassination attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Harry_S._Truman) in 1950. He escaped uninjured, but two [White House police officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Police_Force) were killed, in addition to one of the assailants.[^cite_note-\:9-12] In 1951, civil rights activists [Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_T._Moore) and [Harriette Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriette_Moore) were [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murders_of_Harry_and_Harriette_Moore). On March 1, 1954, four [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans) nationalists perpetrated the [1954 United States Capitol shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_United_States_Capitol_shooting), seeking to promote [Puerto Rican independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_movement_in_Puerto_Rico) from the United States. Five members of [Congress](31756.md) were injured.[^cite_note-k656-23]

#### 1960–1980

The 1960s have been called a "time of great upheaval and discord and division", which saw several major assassinations.[^cite_note-\:11-24][^cite_note-\:32-25] Civil rights leader [Medgar Evers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medgar_Evers) was killed in 1963, hours after [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) gave a televised [civil rights address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Report_to_the_American_People_on_Civil_Rights).[^cite_note-Karen_Bates_trials-26][^cite_note-\:14-27] Rushed to a hospital, he was initially refused entry due to being Black.[^cite_note-Karen_Bates_trials-26][^cite_note-\:14-27] Later that year, [President Kennedy was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F_Kennedy) by [Lee Harvey Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Harvey_Oswald); Oswald was killed two days later while being transported.[^cite_note-\:9-12] Activist [Malcolm X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_X) was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Malcolm_X) in 1965 by [Thomas Hagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hagan). Civil rights activist [Martin Luther King Jr.](20076.md) and Senator [Robert F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy) were both assassinated in 1968.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShahidullah200864-28] [King's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Martin_Luther_King_Jr.) led to [riots in more than 100 cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_assassination_riots),[^cite_note-\:2-20] and historian [Mark K. Updegrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_K._Updegrove) stated that his death hindered the [civil rights movement](49001.md) since King was such an effective leader.[^cite_note-\:11-24] Robert F. Kennedy was seeking the presidential nomination of the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) when [he was killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Robert_F._Kennedy), and had recently won the California primary.[^cite_note-\:9-12] [A large-scale riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Democratic_National_Convention_riots) at the [Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Convention) in Chicago in 1968 involved 10,000 [protesters against the war in Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_United_States_involvement_in_the_Vietnam_War) and 3,000 police officers, and was broadcast on live television.[^cite_note-\:2-20] In 1968, protesters were shot in the [Orangeburg Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orangeburg_Massacre). Two years later, protesters were shot in the [Kent State shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_shootings).

In 1969, [Black Panther Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party) deputy chairman [Fred Hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hampton) was drugged, then shot and killed in his bed during a predawn raid at his Chicago apartment by a tactical unit of the [Cook County State's Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_County_State's_Attorney)'s Office, who received aid from the [Chicago Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Police_Department) and the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) leading up to the attack. Law enforcement sprayed more than 100 gunshots throughout the apartment; the occupants fired once. During the raid, Panther [Mark Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Clark_\(activist\)) was also killed and several others were seriously wounded. Presidential candidate [George Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wallace) was shot at a campaign stop in [Maryland](18858.md), in 1972 while seeking the Democratic presidential nomination, which left him [paraplegic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraplegic).[^cite_note-\:9-12] President [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) was the target of two assassination attempts within a matter of weeks in 1975, and escaped both incidents uninjured.[^cite_note-\:9-12] In 1976, [Orlando Letelier was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Orlando_Letelier).&#x20;

During the 1960s and 1970s, many extremists joined left-wing groups, often spurred by the civil rights movement or the [Vietnam War](32611.md). "[Underground movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_movement)" radicals associated with groups like the [Weather Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Underground), the [New World Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_Liberation_Front), [Chicano Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicano_Liberation_Front), and the [Symbionese Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbionese_Liberation_Army) claimed responsibility for hundreds of small-scale bombings in the 1970s.[^cite_note-\:16-29] In an eighteen-month period between 1971 and 1972, the FBI recorded 2,500 bombings in the U.S.—nearly five per day.[^cite_note-\:16-29] They were usually detonated with prior warning or at night, resulting in few casualties and limited public outcry.[^cite_note-\:16-29][^cite_note-Parker_&_Eisler_2023-2] The bombings were usually intended to destroy property such as government buildings, with the goal of affecting decision-making rather than taking lives.[^cite_note-Parker_&_Eisler_2023-2] The deadliest such attack in the 1970s, targeting a Wall Street restaurant in 1975, killed just four people.[^cite_note-\:16-29] Public acceptance of the bombings was so pervasive that when a small bomb was detonated in a [Bronx](3338.md) cinema, the audience refused to leave so they could keep watching the film.[^cite_note-\:16-29]

#### 1980–2000



Aftermath of the bombing in Oklahoma, 1995

A 1986 [Central Missouri State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Missouri_State_University) comparative analysis between left-wing and right-wing extremist groups commissioned by the [Department of Justice](52563.md) found that both left-wing and right-wing extremist groups pose dangers to the U.S., but right-wing groups were assessed as having greater potential impact due to their alignment with traditional American values.[^cite_note-30] By the 1990s, more extremists were joining right-wing organizations, including militias and [white supremacist](33178.md) groups.[^cite_note-31]

President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) was [shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Ronald_Reagan) in 1981 after giving a speech in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-Boyette-32] His press secretary, [James Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brady), was shot in the attack, leaving him paralyzed.[^cite_note-\:9-12] An [assassination attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Bill_Clinton) of President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) was carried out in 1994, which involved gunfire towards the [White House](33057.md); the perpetrator was sentenced to 40 years in prison.[^cite_note-Boyette-32] In 1984, writer and journalist [Henry Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Liu) was assassinated. Another example of political violence was [1985 MOVE bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_MOVE_bombing) by the [Philadelphia Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Police_Department) during an armed battle with [MOVE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOVE_\(Philadelphia_organization\)), a [Black liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_liberation) organization. A [bomb in Oklahoma City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City_bombing) killed 168 individuals in 1995, which the FBI described as the country's worst act of domestic terrorism.[^cite_note-Parker_&_Eisler_2023-2] The bomb was set up by [Timothy McVeigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_McVeigh), who held antigovernment views.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Tennessee Democratic state senator [Tommy Burks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Burks) was assassinated by a Republican rival in 1998, when he was running for reelection.[^cite_note-35]

### 21st century

#### 2000–2020



Aftermath of the 2017 Charlottesville car attack

President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) was the target of [multiple failed assassination plots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_incidents_involving_Barack_Obama) during his presidency.[^cite_note-\:4-36][^cite_note-\:5-37][^cite_note-\:6-38] In 2009, physician [George Tiller was murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Tiller). In 2011, Congressional representative [Gabby Giffords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabby_Giffords) was shot in the head in [an attempted assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Tucson_shooting).[^cite_note-Boyette-32][^cite_note-\:3-39][^cite_note-\:13-40] The number of threats against members of Congress increased tenfold in the five years after 2016, falling again after 2020.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2023-41] Several Republican Congressmen were targets of a [mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_baseball_shooting) during a practice session for the annual [Congressional Baseball Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Baseball_Game) in Alexandria, Virginia, in June 2017, with then House Majority Whip [Steve Scalise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Scalise) being critically injured.[^cite_note-42] In 2016, [Micah Xavier Johnson shot police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_shooting_of_Dallas_police_officers). A [car attack during a Charlottesville protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville_car_attack) in 2017 killed one person and injured 35 in August 2017.[^cite_note-43] [Pipe bombs were planted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_mail_bombing_attempts) near the homes of Barack Obama and [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in 2018.[^cite_note-44] A paper by the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies), published in 2023, reported that three times more violent political acts and plots had been carried out since 2016 than in the last 25 years.[^cite_note-\:7-45]

#### 2020–present



Supporters of then-President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) attempted to stop the counting of [electoral votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_votes) on January 6, 2021, during the [January 6 Capitol attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_Capitol_attack).

There were about 300 acts of political violence in the United States, from the [January 6 attack](66307490.md) to the 2024 election.[^cite_note-Parker_10212024-46][^cite_note-\:7-45] According to the research, that was the largest surge since the 1970s.[^cite_note-\:7-45] Political [violence during the 2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence_in_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election) was also at its highest since the 1970s, and most recent violence came from right-wing assailants.[^cite_note-Parker_10212024-46][^cite_note-Parker_&_Eisler_2023-2]



A [pipe bomb](233935.md) was placed near the [DNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Convention) building during the January 6 attacks. Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) was driven 20 feet near one of them.

[^cite_note-47]

As of 2023, political violence comes "overwhelmingly from [the right](56522.md)", according to the [Global Terrorism Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Terrorism_Database), FBI statistics, and other research.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3][^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2023-41][^cite_note-\:1-48] The [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) found that all of the 61 political killings in the U.S. from 2022 through 2024 were committed by right-wing extremists.[^cite_note-Riccardi_2025-49] A Princeton University study reported that the number of cases involving harassment and threats against local public officials had increased 74% in 2024 compared with 2022, totaling 600 cases.[^cite_note-PBS_News-2025-50] Serious threats against federal judges doubled from 2021 to 2023, according to the [U.S. Marshals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marshals_Service).[^cite_note-\:32-25]

In one notable event, then [Republican presumptive nominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries) and former president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) was [shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Donald_Trump_in_Pennsylvania) during a [rally](6787314.md) in [Butler, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler,_Pennsylvania), in July 2024. The bullet narrowly missed his temple, hitting his ear before killing an audience member behind Trump and injuring two others.[^cite_note-51] Later that year, there was a [second attempt](77879114.md) on Trump's life in Florida.[^cite_note-\:22-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-\:32-25]

The early 2020s saw [protesters cleared in D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_photo_op_at_St._John's_Church#Clearing_Lafayette_Square_and_St._John's), [a plot to kidnap the Democratic governor of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot), [Gretchen Whitmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretchen_Whitmer), in October 2020,[^cite_note-Boyette-32] the [January 6 Capitol attack](66307490.md) in 2021,[^cite_note-m3532-54][^cite_note-Leingang-20252-55][^cite_note-Bidgood-20252-56] the [hammer attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Paul_Pelosi) on Democratic Representative [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi)'s husband [Paul Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pelosi) in 2022,[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-\:32-25] and the [attempted assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Kavanaugh_assassination_plot) of conservative Supreme Court justice [Brett Kavanaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Kavanaugh) in 2022 due to his position on the overturning of *[Roe v. Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_v._Wade)*.[^cite_note-\:32-25][^cite_note-Boyette-32][^cite_note-\:15-58] In December 2024, [UnitedHealthcare](1845551.md) CEO [Brian Thompson was killed](78525568.md) in an allegedly politically motivated attack. [^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In 2024, [a pro-Israeli group attacked protesters at UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UCLA_protests#Counter-protester_attack). In January 2025, [several Minnesota legislators and their families were killed or had attempts on their lives](80209838.md).[^cite_note-61] In September 2025, [right-wing](56522.md) political activist [Charlie Kirk](53245776.md) was [assassinated during a public debate](81040139.md) in [Orem, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orem,_Utah).[^cite_note-62] Following this, historian [Mark K. Updegrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_K._Updegrove) warned that political violence and rhetoric in the 2020s mirrored that of the 1960s.[^cite_note-\:11-24][^cite_note-Boyette-32]

In April 2026, a [Kimberly-Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberly-Clark) warehouse was [set on fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kimberly-Clark_Distribution_Center_fire). The arson was reportedly committed over labor grievances and pay grievances, in reportedly an [anti-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalist) sentiment. No deaths or injuries have been documented, but the property damage is estimated at \$600 million, and the warehouse was considered a total loss.[^cite_note-63]

#### Threats against local officials

Several U.S. cities reported increased threats against [local officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_United_States) in 2024 and 2025, often connected to [municipal policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_government) decisions. In Utah, the mayor of [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City) received repeated violent threats related to the adoption of new [city flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Salt_Lake_City) designs and other policy positions. A [Farmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmington,_Utah) man was arrested after police said he attempted to enter the [Salt Lake City–County Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City_and_County_Building) and posted messages online encouraging violence toward the mayor and her family.[^cite_note-64] He later pleaded guilty to misdemeanor [stalking](25272420.md) in December 2025 and was subsequently sentenced to jail.[^cite_note-65]

In 2025, there was an [arson attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pennsylvania_Governor's_Residence_arson) on Democratic Pennsylvania governor [Josh Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Shapiro)'s residence in April,[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] a [fatal shooting of two Israeli embassy staffers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Capital_Jewish_Museum_shooting) in Washington, D.C. in May,[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] and in June, a [anti-Zionist fire attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Boulder_fire_attack) in [Colorado](5399.md)[^cite_note-70] and [shootings of two Democratic Minnesota legislators and their spouses](80209838.md).[^cite_note-71] [Solomon Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Pe%C3%B1a) was convicted of hiring men to shoot at various Democratic politicians in response to his loss in the [2022 New Mexico House of Representatives election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_New_Mexico_House_of_Representatives_election).[^cite_note-72] Mike Jensen of the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), which has tracked political violence since the 1970s, said that 2025 experienced 150 politically motivated attacks in the first six months, almost twice as many as there were at the same point in 2024.[^cite_note-73]

## Contemporary landscape

Data shows that political violence had "skyrocketed" in the years prior to 2021, with threats against election administrators reaching unprecedented levels in 2020.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] Speaking to [Reuters](18998750.md) in 2023, [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) criminologist [Gary LaFree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_LaFree) said political violence was at the worst level since the 1970s.[^cite_note-Schneid-74] Although widely discussed, realized acts of political violence in the United States are rare and constitute about 1% of all hate crimes.[^cite_note-Westwood,_Grimmer,_Tyler_&_Nall_2022-1]

The primary trend is the "ungrouping" of political violence as people self-radicalize via the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet), with most political violence committed by people who do not belong to any formal organization.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] Journalist Stefan Grobe writes that extremist ideologies spread on [social media](5897742.md), where "a crude language of memes, slang and jokes blurs the line between posturing and provoking violence, normalising radical ideologies and activities".[^cite_note-Grobe_2025-75] Since 2016, there has been a large increase in attacks on politicians and government employees.[^cite_note-The_Economist_2025-76] [START](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Consortium_for_the_Study_of_Terrorism_and_Responses_to_Terrorism) researcher Michael Jensen recorded 150 politically motivated attacks in the first half of 2025, almost twice the number in the same period in 2024.[^cite_note-Boyette-32]

Studies of political violence in the United States have typically listed [right-wing extremist](221220.md), [left-wing extremist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_extremist) and [Islamist](15012.md) as the main ideological categories.[^cite_note-The_Economist_2025-76][^cite_note-CATO-77][^cite_note-Jasko-78][^cite_note-Duran_2021-79][^cite_note-l728-80] There is no federal database of political violence in the United States, and studies have produced varied tallies depending on how violence and ideology is defined. Perpetrators of political violence, who may be mentally ill, often defy a clear ideological classification.[^cite_note-The_Economist_2025-76]

### Right-wing extremism

Further information: [Anti-abortion violence § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion_violence#United_States), [Domestic terrorism § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism#United_States), [Domestic terrorism in the United States § Right-wing extremist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism_in_the_United_States#Right-wing_extremist), [Far-right politics § United States](221220.md#United_States), [Far-right subcultures § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_subcultures#United_States), [Radical right (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_\(United_States\)), [Right-wing politics § United States](56522.md#United_States), [Right-wing populism § United States](8354382.md#United_States), [Right-wing terrorism § United States](5210960.md#United_States), and [Terrorism in the United States § Right-wing and anti-government extremism](18563588.md#Right-wing_and_anti-government_extremism)



According to a September 2025 [Cato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute) review, when the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) are excluded, 63% of politically motivated extremist killings in the U.S. since 1975 have been caused by right-wing perpetrators, with Islamists causing 23% and left-wing perpetrators 10%. When September 11 is included, Islamists account for 87% of killings (not shown in chart).

[^cite_note-CATO-77]

[^cite_note-Schneid-74]



According to a 2021 *Criminology, Criminal Justice, Law & Society* study, between 1990–2020, victims of far-right ideologically motivated homicides in the U.S. substantially outnumbered victims of far-left perpetrators. Far-right homicides have also occurred more often than [jihadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadi) violence inspired by [Islamic extremism](8734632.md) (not shown in chart).

[^cite_note-Duran_2021-79]

Multiple studies from 1975 onward have found greater rates of right-wing political violence in the United States, as well as causing more deaths,[^cite_note-Jasko-78][^cite_note-81] compared to that of other ideological groups.[^cite_note-The_Economist_2025-76][^cite_note-Schneid-74][^cite_note-Somin_2025-82] [Bruce Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Hoffman) stated this is partially due to the prevalence of militia organizations, and that the extreme left were more likely to target property rather than people.[^cite_note-Riccardi_2025-49] Perpetrators of [far-right](221220.md) planned violence are older and more established than typical [domestic terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism_in_the_United_States)—they frequently have jobs, are married, or have children.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] Political violence has been greatest "in suburbs where Asian American and Hispanic American immigration has been growing fastest, particularly in heavily Democratic metropoles surrounded by Republican-dominated rural areas."[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3]

As of 2021, the right-wing subgroups most prone to violence included white [evangelical Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), who believe in the [QAnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QAnon) conspiracy alleging widespread [pedophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedophilia) among Democratic politicians and celebrities; and [Republicans](32070.md) who "feel threatened by either women or minorities".[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3]



Trump regularly uses social media to criticize his political opponents. Democratic officials have criticized the posts as encouraging political violence.

[^cite_note-83]

President Trump has been identified as a key figure in increasing political violence in the U.S.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Reuters reported that political violence increased greatly following the election of Trump in 2016, noting his use of "[incendiary rhetoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetoric_of_Donald_Trump)",[^cite_note-Parker_10212024-46] which has been described as a form of [stochastic terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_terrorism).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] For instance, in November 2025, Trump made multiple social media posts calling Democrats traitors who should be charged with [sedition](324091.md), [punishable by death](5902.md), and shared a social media post calling for Democrats to be hanged.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

According to the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League), all 61 political killings between 2022 and 2024 were committed by right-wing extremists.[^cite_note-Riccardi_2025-49] [Counterterrorism](83392.md) experts described Trump's previous [pardon of the January 6 attackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardon_of_the_January_6_attackers) as having created a permission structure for them to commit political violence.[^cite_note-91] A week after the [assassination of Charlie Kirk](81040139.md) in September 2025, the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) removed a 2024 study,[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] titled "What NIJ Research Tells Us About Domestic Terrorism",[^cite_note-95] which showed that white supremacist and far-right violence "continues to outpace all other types of terrorism and domestic violent extremism" in the United States,[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] in contrast to statements made by the Trump administration.[^cite_note-98] Right-wing political violence in the United States sharply declined in the months after the second election of Trump.[^cite_note-j189-99]

### Left-wing extremism

Main articles: [Left-wing populism § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_populism#United_States), and [Left-wing terrorism § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_terrorism#United_States)

In modern United States history, a minority of acts of political violence have been committed by [left-wing extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics). Left-wing political violence has increased since 2016 when [Donald Trump](4848272.md) was elected president, and according to one study which was conducted by the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies), by September 2025, it eclipsed right-wing political violence for the first time in more than 30 years.[^cite_note-j189-99][^cite_note-w034-100] The report noted five left-wing terrorist attacks in the first half of 2025 compared to one act of right-wing terrorism, and described left-wing violence as having risen from "very low levels".[^cite_note-j189-99] A 2021 *Criminology, Criminal Justice, Law & Society* study covering 1990–2020 also found a "gradual linear increase" in far-left homicide incidents, with 40.5% of those studied occurring during Donald Trump's first term of 2016–2020 (compared to 11.0% of far-right incidents), and 2017 being the only year where far-left homicide incidents outnumbered far-right ones.[^cite_note-Duran_2021-79] Left-wing terrorism was most prominent in the United States in the 1960s and 1970s with groups such as the [Weather Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Underground) and [Symbionese Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbionese_Liberation_Army), before declining in the 1980s and 1990s as right-wing and [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) terrorism rose.[^cite_note-LeftAtlantic-101]

Left-wing political violence in the United States is typically directed at specific individuals rather than groups, and it has most commonly occurred at government or law enforcement facilities.[^cite_note-j189-99] A 2025 *[Terrorism and Political Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_and_Political_Violence)* study found that self-proclaimed [antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifa_\(United_States\)) supporters are more likely to support violence toward [outgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group_and_out-group) compared to people who did not support a fringe group, similar to [QAnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QAnon) supporters on the right.[^cite_note-j176-102] Antifa supporters in the United States have primarily committed violence in the context of ideological clashes at protests.[^cite_note-l037-103][^cite_note-f683-104]

### Islamist extremism

Main article: [Islam in the United States § Jihadist extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_United_States#Jihadist_extremism)

See also: [Islamic extremism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_extremism_in_the_United_States)

According to the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies), between 1994 and 2024, 140 violent attacks were either conducted or planned by Islamist extremists ([jihadists](7826589.md)) in the United States. Since 2001, the number of attacks by jihadists has peaked twice: during the first years of [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s presidency (2009–2013) and during the peak of the rule of the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (2014–2019).[^cite_note-h623-105] The number of Islamist terrorist attacks declined significantly after the Islamic State's defeat and loss of territory in 2019, but individual incidents continue to occur. Most acts of jihadist violence in the United States have been perpetrated by U.S. citizens, and foreign nationals have also committed some notable attacks.[^cite_note-h623-105][^cite_note-h958-106] The 2001 [September 11 attacks](5058690.md)—by far the deadliest single instance of politically motivated terrorism in modern United States history—has been attributed to Islamist extremism.[^cite_note-CATO-77]

### Contributing factors

The strongest predictors of whether someone justifies or commits political violence include aggressive or authoritarian personality traits, poor self-control, and hostile sexist beliefs.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2023-41] Political and social enablers of political violence include dehumanizing and denigrating rhetoric from political leaders and their normalization of violence.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2023-41] Also contributing to political violence is a sense of threat or increased fear (caused by things like [pandemics](24255.md) or rises in crime).[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2023-41] A 2021 analysis found that, across party lines, the people most likely to support political violence were those who held hostility towards women.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3]

Another risk factor for election violence is [partisan division based on identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics).[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] Americans have shifted from mixed, cross-cutting identities to aligned partisan packages where multiple identities (urban/rural, race, religion, gender) tend to be highly correlated with one's political party.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] This makes political attacks feel personal—threatening one's entire identity rather than just policy preferences—intensifying political disputes.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] The 2020s' patterns of political violence resemble the 19th century, where one's race, ethnicity, religion, and immigration status is associated with their partisan identity.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] Despite efforts to reduce [affective polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarization)—how much members of the opposing party like each other—there is little evidence that it contributes to political violence.[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2023-41]

### Support for political violence



A graffiti in New York asks "was [Luigi](78560779.md) justified?"

In the 21st century, public support for political violence increased throughout the latter half of the 2010s.[^cite_note-107] Endorsements of violence "suddenly leapt" in October 2020. By February 2021, more than two-thirds of [Republicans](32070.md) and half of [Democrats](5043544.md) saw the other party as "downright evil", and over half of Republicans agreed that "if elected leaders will not protect America, the people must do it themselves even if it requires taking violent action".[^cite_note-Kleinfeld_2021-3] Leading up to 2022, scientific research reported that between 8% to 40% of Americans supported political violence.[^cite_note-Westwood,_Grimmer,_Tyler_&_Nall_2022-1] A September 2025 poll by [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) found that 20% of Americans between 18–29 years old believed political violence was sometimes justified, higher than any other age group.[^cite_note-u262-108]

A 2022 academic paper stated that U.S. surveys have overestimated the rate of support for political violence due to methodological flaws.[^cite_note-109] According to [G. Elliott Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Elliott_Morris), polling tends to exaggerate approval of political violence. Asking more precise questions, less than 5% condone violent felonies, with little difference between parties.[^cite_note-110] Furthermore, individuals tend to significantly overestimate approval of violence within the other party, and are less likely to support it themselves when correctly informed.[^cite_note-111] According to political analyst [Bruce Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Hoffman), Americans have become more prone to accept previously stigmatized political speech. He stated that the suspect who allegedly killed [UnitedHealthcare](1845551.md) CEO [Brian Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Thompson_\(businessman\)) became a "[folk hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_hero)" and had a musical in his honor sell out in theaters.[^cite_note-PBS_News-2025-50]
