The **Pakistan Army** or **Pak Army** ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): پاک فوج, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Urdu): *Pāk Fauj*, pronounced [\[ˈpaːk fɔːdʒ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)) is the [land service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare) and the largest component of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md). The [president of Pakistan](419460.md) is the [supreme commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the army. As of the [2025 reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-seventh_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan), the Pakistan Army is commanded by the Chief of Defence Forces (CDF), a position held concurrently by the [Chief of Army Staff (COAS)](7790064.md), who is typically a four-star general. The Army was officially established in August 1947 after the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India).[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4] According to statistics provided by the [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) (IISS) in 2025, the Pakistan Army has approximately 560,000 [active duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_duty) personnel, supported by the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(Pakistan\)) and the [Civil Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Armed_Forces). In accordance with the [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan), Pakistani citizens can voluntarily enlist in military service as early as age 17, but cannot be deployed for combat until age 18.

The primary objective and constitutional mission of the Pakistan Army is to ensure the national security and national unity of [Pakistan](23235.md) by defending it against external aggression or the threat of war. It can also be requisitioned by the [Pakistani federal government](1897833.md) to respond to internal threats within its borders.[^cite_note-5] During national or international calamities or emergencies, it conducts humanitarian rescue operations at home and [is an active participant in peacekeeping missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping_missions_involving_Pakistan) mandated by the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN). Notably, it played a major role in rescuing trapped American soldiers who had requested the assistance of a [quick reaction force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_reaction_force) during [Operation Gothic Serpent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gothic_Serpent) in [Somalia](27358.md). Pakistan Army troops also had a relatively strong presence as part of a UN and [NATO](21133.md) coalition during the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) and the larger [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars).<sup>: 70 </sup>[^cite_note-Bloomsbury_Publishing_USA,_Harper-6]

The Pakistan Army, a major component of the Pakistani military alongside the [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md) and [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md), is a [volunteer force](9354649.md) that saw extensive combat during [three major wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts) with [India](14533.md), several [border skirmishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan%E2%80%93Pakistan_skirmishes) with [Afghanistan](737.md) at the [Durand Line](203570.md), and [a long-running insurgency](4591548.md) in the [Balochistan region](8431743.md) that it has been combatting alongside [Iranian security forces](14305018.md) since 1948.[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Fair,_2014-7][^cite_note-8]<sup>: 31 </sup> Since the 1960s, elements of the army have repeatedly been deployed in an advisory capacity in the [Arab states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) during the [Arab–Israeli wars](7960202.md), and to aid the United States-led coalition against [Iraq](26215470.md) during the [First Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). Other notable military operations during the [global war on terrorism](13425800.md) in the 21st century have included: [*Zarb-e-Azb*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zarb-e-Azb), [*Black Thunderstorm*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Black_Thunderstorm), and [*Rah-e-Nijat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rah-e-Nijat).[^cite_note-9]

In violation of its constitutional mandate, it has repeatedly overthrown elected civilian governments, overreaching its protected constitutional mandate to "act in the aid of civilian federal governments when called upon to do so".[^cite_note-10] The army has been involved in enforcing [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan) against the federal government with the claim of restoring law and order in the country by dismissing the [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan) and [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan) on multiple occasions in past decades—while maintaining a wider commercial, foreign and political interest in the country. This has led to allegations that it has acted as a [state within a state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_state).[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspapers,_Javed-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The Pakistan Army is operationally and geographically divided into [various corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Corps_of_the_Pakistan_Army).[^cite_note-15] The Pakistani constitution mandates the role of the [president of Pakistan](419460.md) as the civilian [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the Pakistani military.[^cite_note-16] The Pakistan Army is commanded by the [Chief of Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)), also known as ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): سپہ سالار; [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Urdu) *Sipah Salaar*) who is by statute (although typically) a [four-star general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-star_general) and a senior member of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) appointed by the [prime minister](461896.md) and subsequently affirmed by the president.[^cite_note-News_International,_2016-17] The current Chief of the Army Staff is Field Marshal [Asim Munir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asim_Munir_\(general\)), who was appointed on 29 November 2022 and promoted to [field marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal) in May 2025.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Mission

Main article: [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan)

Its existence and constitutional role are protected by the [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan), where its role is to serve as the [land-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_forces) uniform service branch of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md). The Constitution of Pakistan establishes the principal [land warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Pakistan) uniform branch in the Pakistan Armed Forces as its states:

> The Armed Forces shall, under the directions of the Federal Government, defend Pakistan against external aggression or threat of war, and, subject to law, act in aid of civil power when called upon to do so.

— Constitution of Pakistan[^cite_note-20]

### Division of British Indian Army and the first war with India (1947–1952)

Main article: [India–Pakistan war of 1947–1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_war_of_1947%E2%80%931948)



The 6th [Frontier Force Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Force_Regiment) of the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) in the fronts of [World War II](32927.md) in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic) in [1943–44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_in_Italy)



Members of the newly formed Pakistani Security Guard standing at attention during parade review for Pakistan's Leader Jinnah

The Pakistan Army came into its modern birth from the division of the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) that ceased to exist as a result of the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) that resulted in the [creation of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_of_Pakistan) on 14 August 1947.<sup>: 1–2 </sup>[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4] Before even the partition took place, there were plans ahead of dividing the British Indian Army into different parts based on the religious and ethnic influence on the areas of India.<sup>: 1–2 </sup>[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4]

On 30 June 1947, the [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Department_\(United_Kingdom\)) of the [British administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) in India began planning the dividing of the \~400,000 men strong British Indian Army, but that only began few weeks before the partition of India that resulted in violent [religious violence in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_violence_in_India).<sup>: 1–2 </sup>[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4] The Armed Forces Reconstitution Committee (AFRC) under the chairmanship of British [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal_\(United_Kingdom\)) Sir [Claude Auchinleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Auchinleck) had devised the formula to divide the military assets between India and Pakistan with ratio of 2\:1, respectively.<sup>: conts. </sup>[^cite_note-Notion_Press,_Col._Chandar,_2018-21]



The Map of Kashmir, showing the tri-national control from China, Pakistan, and India, [ca. 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_in_Pakistan)

A major division of the army was overseen by Sir [Chandulal Madhavlal Trivedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandulal_Madhavlal_Trivedi), an Indian [civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Civil_Service_\(British_India\)) who was influential in making sure that \~260,000 men would be transferred into forming the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) whilst the remaining balance going to Pakistan after the [independence act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Independence_Act_1947) was enacted by the United Kingdom on the night of 14/15 August 1947.<sup>: 2–3 </sup>[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4]

Command and control at all levels of the new army was extremely difficult, as Pakistan had received six armoured, eight [artillery](2508.md) and eight [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) regiments compared to the twelve armoured, forty artillery and twenty-one infantry regiments that went to India.<sup>: 155–156 </sup>[^cite_note-A\&C_Black,_Roy-22] In total, the size of the new army was about \~150,000 men strong.<sup>: 155–156 </sup>[^cite_note-A\&C_Black,_Roy-22] To fill the vacancy in the command positions of the new army, around 13,500<sup>: 2 </sup>[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4] [military officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_ranks) from the [British Army](4887.md) had to be employed in the Pakistan Army, which was quite a large number, under the command of [Lieutenant-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-general_\(United_Kingdom\)) [Frank Messervy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Messervy), the first [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Chief_\(Pakistan_Army\)) of the Pakistan Army.<sup>: 70 </sup>[^cite_note-Vij_Books_India_Pvt_Ltd,_Khanna-23]

Eminent fears of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India)'s seizing the control over the state of [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)), the October 1947.<sup>: conts. </sup>[^cite_note-I.B.Tauris,_V._Schofield,_2003.-24] Attempting to maintain his control over the [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)), [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh) deployed his troops to check on the tribal advances but his troops failed to halt the advancing tribes towards the valley.<sup>: 40 </sup>[^cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press,_Mahapatra,_2017-25] Eventually, [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh) appealed to [Louis Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mountbatten,_1st_Earl_Mountbatten_of_Burma), the [Governor-General of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Government_ministries_of_India) terms which eventually led to the deployment of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\))– this agreement, however, was contested by Pakistan since the agreement did not include the [consent](51582.md) of the [Kashmiri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_people).<sup>: 40 </sup>[^cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press,_Mahapatra,_2017-25] Sporadic fighting between militia and Indian Army broke out, and units of the Pakistan Army under [Maj-Gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major-General) [Akbar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Khan_\(Pakistani_general\)), eventually joined the militia in their fight against the Indian Army.<sup>: 40 </sup>[^cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press,_Mahapatra,_2017-25]

Although, it was [Lieutenant-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-General_\(United_Kingdom\)) Sir [Frank Messervy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Messervy) who opposed the tribal invasion in a cabinet meeting with [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan) in 1947, later leaving the command of the army in 1947,<sup>: 447 </sup>[^cite_note-26] in a view of that British officers in the [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) and Pakistan Army would be fighting with each other in the war front.<sup>: 417 </sup>[^cite_note-PublicAffairs,_Hiro,_2015-27] It was Lt-Gen. #### Reorganization under the United States Army (1952–1958)

After the incident involving Gracey's disobedience, there was a strong belief that a native commander of the Pakistan army should be appointed, which resulted in the [Government of Pakistan](1897833.md) rejecting the [British](31717.md) [Army Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Board)'s replacement of Gen. Gracey upon his replacement, in 1951.<sup>: 34 </sup>[^cite_note-31] Eventually, [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan) approved the promotion paper of Maj-Gen. [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(President_of_Pakistan\)), whose name was not included in the promotion list was elevated to the promotion that resulted in a lobbying provided by Iskandar Mirza, the [Defense Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Secretary_of_Pakistan) in [Ali Khan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Khan_administration).[^cite_note-paksoldiers.com-33] A tradition of appointment based on favoritism and qualification that is still in practice by the civilian [Prime Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Pakistan) in Pakistan.[^cite_note-paksoldiers.com-33] Ayub was promoted to the acting rank of full general to command the army as his predecessors [Frank Messervy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Messervy) and [Douglas Gracey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Gracey) were performing the duty of commander-in-chief of the Pakistan Army in the acting rank of general, the neighboring country India's first commanders-in-chief were same in this context.

The department of the army under General [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(President_of_Pakistan\)) steered the army's needs towards heavy focus and dependence towards the imported hardware acquired from the United States, in spite of acquiring it from the domestic industry, under the [Military Assistance Advisory Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Assistance_Advisory_Group) attached to Pakistan in 1954–56.<sup>: 36 </sup>[^cite_note-Bloomsbury_Publishing,_Higgins,_2016-30] In 1953, the 6th Infantry Division was raised and disbanded the 6th Division in 1956 followed by the disbandment of the 9th Infantry Division as the American assistance was available only for one armored and six infantry divisions.<sup>: 36 </sup>[^cite_note-Bloomsbury_Publishing,_Higgins,_2016-30] During this time, an army [combat brigade team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_combat_team) was readily made available by Gen. In 1956, the 1st Armored Division in Multan was established, followed by the [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group) in [Cherat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherat) under the supervision of the [U.S Army's Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces).<sup>: 55 </sup>[^cite_note-Major_Nasir_Uddin_pp55-29]<sup>: 133 </sup>[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Nawaz,_2008-37] Under [Gen. Pakistan's army had been able to withstand Indian pressure, but a continuation of the fighting would only have led to further losses and ultimate defeat for Pakistan. Most Pakistanis, schooled in the belief of their own martial prowess, refused to accept the possibility of their country's military defeat by "Hindu India" and were, instead, quick to blame their failure to attain their military aims on what they considered to be the ineptitude of Ayub Khan and his government.[^cite_note-64]

According to neural claims Pakistan suffered from 1,500 Soldiers killed and 4,300 wounded[^cite_note-\:2-65][^cite_note-\:9-66] and India suffered from 3,712 Soldiers killed and 7,638 wounded.[^cite_note-\:2-65][^cite_note-\:9-66] Pakistan lost 200-250 tanks,[^cite_note-Tucker-2021-67][^cite_note-\:1-68] and India lost 200-300 tanks.[^cite_note-Tucker-2021-67][^cite_note-\:1-68]

However, most neutral assessments agree that India had the [upper hand over Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965#Assessment_of_losses) when ceasefire was declared,[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-USLib-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] but the propaganda in Pakistan about the war continued in favor of Pakistan Army.[^cite_note-74] The war was not rationally analysed in Pakistan with most of the blame being heaped on the leadership and little importance given to intelligence failures that persisted until the debacle of the [third war with India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971) in 1971.[^cite_note-75] The Indian Army's action was restricted to [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region) of both sides with Indian Army mainly in fertile Sialkot, Lahore and Kashmir sectors,[^cite_note-Arif2001-76][^cite_note-77] while Pakistani land gains were primarily in southern deserts opposite [Sindh](28972.md) and in the [Chumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumb) sector near Kashmir in the north.[^cite_note-Arif2001-76]

With the United States' arms embargo on Pakistan over the issue of the war, the army instead turned to the Soviet Union and China for hardware acquisition, and correctly assessed that a lack of infantry played a major role in the failure of Pakistani armour to translate its convincing material and technical superiority into a major operational or strategic success against the Indian Army.[^cite_note-Defence_Journal,_Maj._AH_Amin-78] Ultimately, the army's high command established the 9th, 16th, and 17th infantry divisions in 1966–68.[^cite_note-Defence_Journal,_Maj._AH_Amin-78] In 1966, the [IV Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IV_Corps_\(Pakistan\)) was formed and its headquarter was established, and permanently stationed in Lahore, [Punjab](24751.md) in [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-Vij_Books_India_Pvt_Ltd,_Alam,_2012-79]

The army remained involved in the nation's civic affairs, and ultimately imposed the second martial law in 1969 when the [writ of the constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1962) was abrogated by then-army commander, Gen. [Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan), who took control of the nation's civic affairs after the resignation of President Ayub Khan, resulted in a massive labor strikes instigated by the [Pakistan Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Peoples_Party) in [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) and [Awami League](404861.md) in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan).[^cite_note-Martinus_Nijhoff_Publishers,_Omar,_2002-80]

In a lawsuit settled by the [Supreme Court of Pakistan](843857.md), the legality of the martial law was deemed questionable as the Supreme Court settled the suit by retroactively invalidated the martial law that suspended the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1962) and notably ruled that Yahya Khan's assumption of power was "illegal usurpation".<sup>: 59–60 </sup>[^cite_note-Martinus_Nijhoff_Publishers-49] In light of the Supreme Court's judgement, the army held the publicly televised conference when President Yahya Khan announced to hold the [nationwide general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Pakistani_general_election) in 1969–70.<sup>: 59–60 </sup>[^cite_note-Martinus_Nijhoff_Publishers-49]

#### Suppression, civil conflict in East Pakistan and Indian invasion (1969–1971)

Main articles: [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September), [India and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_and_state-sponsored_terrorism), [1971 East Pakistan genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_East_Pakistan_genocide), [1971 East Pakistan Intellectuals massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_East_Pakistan_Intellectuals_massacre), [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight), [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War), and [India–Pakistan war of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_war_of_1971)

In 1969, [President](419460.md) [Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan) decided to make administrative changes in the army by appointing the Gen. [Abdul Hamid Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Khan_\(general\)) as the [Army Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Chief_of_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) (ACOS) of the Pakistan Army, who centralized the chain of command in Rawalpindi in a headquarters known as "High Command".<sup>: 32 </sup>[^cite_note-81] From 1967 to 1969, a series of major [military exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces_Eastern_Command#Operation_X-Sundarbans-1) was conducted by infantry units on East Pakistan's border with India.<sup>: 114–119 </sup>[^cite_note-82] In 1970, the Pakistan army's military mission in [Jordan](7515964.md) was reportedly involved in [tackling and curbing down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September) the Palestinian infiltration in Jordan.[^cite_note-83] In June 1971, the enlistment in the army had allowed the [Army GHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHQ_\(Pakistan_Army\)) in Rawalpindi to raise and established the 18th infantry division, stationed in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_Pakistan), [Sindh](28972.md), for the defence of 900 kilometres (560 mi) from [Rahimyar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahimyar_Khan) to [Rann of Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rann_of_Kutch), and restationed the 23rd infantry division for defending the [Chhamb-Dewa Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhamb,_Hamirpur).[^cite_note-Defence_Journal,_Maj._AH_Amin-78]

In 1971, the [II Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_Corps_\(Pakistan\)) was established and headquartered in Multan, driven towards defending the mass incursion from the Indian Army.[^cite_note-Vij_Books_India_Pvt_Ltd,_Alam,_2012-79] In December 1971, the 33rd infantry division was established from the army reserves of the [II Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_Corps_\(Pakistan\)), followed by raising the 37th Infantry Division.[^cite_note-Defence_Journal,_Maj._AH_Amin-78] Pakistan Army reportedly helped the [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md) towards establishing its amphibious branch, the [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines), whose [battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) was airlifted to [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) along with the 9th Infantry Division.[^cite_note-Defence_Journal,_Maj._AH_Amin-78]

The intervention in East Pakistan further grew when the [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight) resulted in the overtaking of the government buildings, communication centers, and restricting the politicians opposed to military rule.<sup>: 263 </sup>[^cite_note-84] Within a month, Pakistani national security strategists realized their failure of implementing the plan which had not anticipated civil resistance in East, and the real nature of Indian strategy behind their support of the resistance.<sup>: 2–3 </sup>[^cite_note-85]

The Yahya administration is widely accused of permitting the army to commit the [war crimes against the civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_East_Pakistan_genocide) in East and curbing civil liberties and human rights in Pakistan. The [Eastern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan_Military_Command) under [Lt-Gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-General) [A. A. K. Niazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._A._K._Niazi), who had [area responsibility](9452512.md) of the defending the Eastern Front and had the responsibility to protect, was leveled with accusations of escalating the political violence in the East by the serving military officers, politicians, and journalists in Pakistan.[^cite_note-Geo_news_tv-86][^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Kiessling-87] Since the [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Pakistani_general_election) in 1970, the army had detained several key politicians, journalists, peace activists, student unionists, and other members of civil society while curbing the freedoms of movement and speech in Pakistan.<sup>: 112 </sup>[^cite_note-88] In East Pakistan, the unified [Eastern Military Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Military_Command_of_Pakistan) under Lt-Gen. A.A.K. Niazi, began its engagement with the [armed militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini) that had support from [India](14533.md) in April 1971, and eventually fought against the Indian Army in December 1971.<sup>: 596 </sup>[^cite_note-ABC-CLIO,_Heo_and_Jr.-89]<sup>: 596 </sup> The army, together with marines, launched ground offensives on both fronts but the Indian Army successfully held its ground and initiated well-coordinated ground operations on both fronts, initially capturing 15,010 square kilometres (5,795 sq mi)<sup>: 239 </sup>[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Nawaz,_2008-37] of Pakistan's territory; this land gained by India in [Azad Kashmir](2745.md), [Punjab](24751.md) and [Sindh](28972.md) sectors.<sup>: 239 </sup>[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Nawaz,_2008-37]

Responding to the ultimatum issued on 16 December 1971 by the Indian Army in East, Lt-Gen. Niazi agreed to concede defeat and move towards signing the [documented surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Instrument_of_Surrender) with the Indian Army which effectively and unilaterally ended the [armed resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) and led the creation of [Bangladesh](3454.md), only after India's official engagement that lasted 13 days.[^cite_note-pakdefeat1-90] It was reported that the [Eastern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Eastern_Command) had surrendered \~93,000–97,000 uniform personnel to Indian Army– the largest surrender in a war by any country after the [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-pakdefeat2-91] Casualties inflicted to army's I Corps, II Corps, and Marines did not sit well with President Yahya Khan who turned over control of the civic government to [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto) through an executive decree.[^cite_note-pakdefeat3-92]

Commenting on the defeat, the military observer in the Pakistan Army, Major A.H. Amin, reported that the war strategists in the army had not seriously considered a full-fledged invasion from India until December 1971, because it was presumed that the Indian military would not risk intervention by China or the United States, and the high command failed to realize that the Chinese would be unable to intervene during the winter months of November to December, due to snowbound Himalayan passes, and the Americans had not made any real effort to persuade India against attacking East Pakistan.[^cite_note-93]

### 21st century: War performances

#### Religious insurgency and War on Terror (2001–present)

Responding to the [terror attacks](5058690.md) in [New York](645042.md) in the United States, the army joined the [combat actions in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_invasion_of_Afghanistan) with the United States and simultaneously engage in [military standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001-2002_India-Pakistan_standoff) with [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) in 2001–2002. In 2004–2006, the military observers from the army were deployed to guide the [Sri Lankan army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_army) to end the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_civil_war) with the [Tamil fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTTE_Insurgency).[^cite_note-123]

To overcome the governance crises in 2004–2007, the [Musharraf administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) appointed several army officers in the civilian institutions with some receiving extensions while others were deployed from their combat service– thus affecting the fighting capabilities and weakening the army.<sup>: 37 </sup>[^cite_note-Outlook_Publishing,_Mir-124] Under Gen. Musharraf's leadership, the army's capabilities fighting the [fanatic Talibans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Taleban) and [Afghan Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs_in_Pakistan) fighters in Pakistan further weakened and suffered [serious setbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Swat) in gaining control of the [tribal belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FATA) that fell under the control of the [Afghan Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs_in_Pakistan) and [Uzbek fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_of_Uzbekistan).<sup>: 37 </sup>[^cite_note-Outlook_Publishing,_Mir-124] From 2006 to 2009, the army fought the series of [bloody battles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_in_Pakistan_2006%E2%80%9309) with the fanatic [Afghan Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs_in_Pakistan) and other [foreign fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Mujahideen)[^cite_note-Outlook_Publishing,_Mir-124] most notably the [army action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Lal_Masjid) against a [Islamist](15012.md) [private militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_army) at the [Red Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Masjid,_Islamabad) in [Islamabad](51153.md).[^cite_note-BBC05-125] and with the controversial assassination of [Baloch politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Bugti) in 2006, the army had to engage in battles with the [Baloch separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_separatist) fighting for the Balochistan's autonomy.<sup>: 37 </sup>[^cite_note-Outlook_Publishing,_Mir-124]

In April 2007, the major reorganization of the commands of the army was taken place under Gen. [Ahsan Saleem Hayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahsan_Saleem_Hayat), the [vice army chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) under Gen. Musharraf, established the Southern, Central, and the Northern Commands. With Gen. Musharraf's resignation and Gen. [Ashfaq Parvez Kayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashfaq_Parvez_Kayani) becoming the [army chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)), the army realigned itself to review its combat policies and withdrew officers in civilian institutions to focus on its primary constitutional mission to [protect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility_to_protect) and [responsible](9452512.md) in 2009–14.<sup>: 37 </sup>[^cite_note-Outlook_Publishing,_Mir-124][^cite_note-Masood-126] In 2012, there was a serious accident involving the entire battalion from the [Northern Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Light_Infantry) when the [avalanche struck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Siachen_Glacier_avalanche) the battalion base in [Siachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siachen_Glacier), entrapping 135 soldiers and including several army officers.[^cite_note-aljazeera1-127]

In 2013–2016, the [homegrown far-right guerrilla war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_North-West_Pakistan) with the [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Taleban), [Afghan Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs_in_Pakistan), and the [Central Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Central_Asia) fighters took the [decisive turn in favor of the army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zarb-e-Azb) under [Sharif administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Sharif_ministry), eventually gaining the control of the entire country and established the writ of the constitution in the affected lawless regions.[^cite_note-128] As of its current deployment as of 2019, the army [remained engage in border fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_border_skirmishes_\(2016%E2%80%932018\)) with the Indian Army while [deploying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces_deployments_in_Saudi_Arabia) its combat strike brigade teams in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) in a response of [Saudi intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi-led_intervention_in_Yemen).[^cite_note-129]

## Personnel

### Commissioned officers

Main article: [Army ranks and insignia of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ranks_and_insignia_of_Pakistan)

The [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer) [army ranks and insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ranks_and_insignia_of_Pakistan) authorized in the Pakistan Army are modified and patterned on the [British Army](4887.md)'s [officer ranks and insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_officer_rank_insignia) system.[^cite_note-Ranks_and_insignia-134] There are several paths of becoming the commissioned officer in the army including the admission and required graduation from the [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy) in [Kakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakul_Military_Academy).  To become an officer in the army, the academic four-year [college degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_degree) is required for the candidates to become officers in the army, and therefore they are designated by insignia unique to their staff community.

Selection to the officer candidates is highly competitive with \~320–700 individuals are allowed to enter in the [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy) annually, with a small number of already [graduated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_student) physicians, specialists, [veterinaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary_physician) and the engineers from the [civilian universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_in_Pakistan) are directly recruited in the administrative staff corps such as [Medical Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Medical_Corps), Veterinary Corps, [Engineering Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_Engineers,_Pakistan_Army), Dental Corps and these graduated individuals are the heart of the administrative corps.<sup>: 293 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] The product of a highly competitive selection process, members of the staff corps have completed twelve years of education in their respected fields (such as attending the schools and universities), and has to spend two years at the Pakistan Military Academy, with their time divided about equally between [military training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_training) and academic work to bring them up to a baccalaureate education level, which includes [English-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-language) skills.<sup>: 293 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] The Department of Army also offers employment to civilians in financial management, accountancy, engineering, construction, and administration, and has currently employed 6,500 civilians.[^cite_note-Infrastructures_Development-136]

The military officers in the Pakistani military seek retirement between the ages of forty-two and sixty, depending on their ranks, and often seeks employment in the [federal government](1897833.md) or the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_sector_of_Pakistan) where the pay scales are higher as well as the opportunity for gain considerably greater.<sup>: 294 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]

### Warrant officers

The Pakistan Army uniquely uses the [junior commissioned officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_commissioned_officer) (JCO) ranks, equivalent of the [Warrant officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer_\(United_States\)) or the [Limited duty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_duty_officer) in the [United States military](32212.md), inherited from the former [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) introduced by the [British Army](4887.md) in [India](14533.md) between the enlisted and officer ranks. The JCOs are single-track specialists with their subject of expertise in their particular part of the job and initially appointed (NS1) after risen from their enlisted ranks, receiving the promotion (SM3) from the commanding officer.

The usage of the [junior commissioned officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_commissioned_officer) is the continuation of the former [Viceroy's commissioned officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy's_commissioned_officer) rank, and the JCO ranking system benefited the army since there was a large gap existed between the officers and the enlisted personnel at the time of the establishment of the new army in 1947. Over the several years, the JCOs rank system has outlived its usefulness because the educational level of the enlisted personnel has risen and the army has more comfortably adopted the [U.S. Army](32087.md)'s ranking platform than the British.[^cite_note-Hamid_Hussain,_Defence_Journal_of_Pakistan-36] Promotion to the [JCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_commissioned_officer) ranks remains a powerful and influential incentive for that enlisted personnel desire not to attend the accredited four-year college.

### Enlisted personnel

Main article: [Quota system in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_in_Pakistan)

The recruiting and enlistment in the army is nationwide but the army's recruiting command maintains an ethnic balance, with those who turned away are encourage to join the either the [Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines) or the [Air Force](377433.md).<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] Most enlisted personnel had come from the [poor and rural families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_village_life) with many had only rudimentary literacy skills in the [past](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pakistan), but with the increase in the [affordable education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Pakistan) have risen to the [matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation_in_Pakistan) level (12th Grade).<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] In the past, the army recruits had to re-educate the illiterate personnel while processing them gradually through a paternalistically run regimental training center, teaching the official language, [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), if necessary, and given a period of elementary education before their military training actually starts.<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]

In the thirty-six-week training period, they develop an attachment to the regiment they will remain with through much of their careers and begin to develop a sense of being a Pakistani rather than primarily a member of a tribe or a village.<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] Enlisted personnel usually serve for eighteen to twenty years, before retiring or gaining a commission, during which they participate in regular military training cycles and have the opportunity to take academic courses to help them advance.<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]

The [noncommissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) (or enlists) wear respective regimental color chevrons on the right sleeve.<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] Center point of the uppermost chevron must remain 10 cm from the point of the shoulder.<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] The Company/battalion appointments wear the appointments badges on the right wrist.<sup>: 292 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] Pay scales and incentives are greater and attractive upon enlistment including the allocation of land, free housing, and financial aid to attend the colleges and universities.<sup>: 294 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135] Retirement age for the enlisted personnel varies and depends on the enlisted ranks that they have attained during their services.<sup>: 294 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]

### Recruitment and training



The [passing out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) (graduation) of cadets from the [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy) in [Kakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakul) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_in_Pakistan). The education and military training last for two years before cadets become officers.

[^cite_note-Pakistan_Military_Academy_-_Cadets_Training-137]

Prior to August 1947, the [British Army](4887.md)'s recruiting administration had recruited the enlists from the districts of the [Jhelum](766672.md), [Rawalpindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District), and [Campbellpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock_District) that dominated the recruitment flows.[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4] From 1947 to 1971, the Pakistan Army was predominantly favored to recruit from [Punjab](24751.md) and was popular in the country as the "Punjabi Army" because of heavy recruiting interests coming from the [rural and poor families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_village_life) of villages in Punjab as well as being the most populous province of Pakistan.<sup>: 149 </sup>[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

Even as of today, the Pakistan Army's recruiters struggle to enlist citizens and their selfless commitment to the military from the [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation_in_Pakistan) (i.e. [Karachi](17123.md) and [Peshawar](66230.md)) where the preference of the [college education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_education) is quite popular (especially attending [post-graduate](153981.md) schools in the United States and the [English-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations)) as well as working in the settled [private industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Pakistan) for lucrative salaries and benefits, while the military enlistment still comes from the most rural and remote areas of Pakistan, where commitment to the military is much greater than in the [metropolitan cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation_in_Pakistan).<sup>: 31 </sup>[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Fair,_2014-7]

After 1971, the [Bhutto administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto#President_of_Pakistan) introduced the [Quota system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_in_Pakistan) and drastically reduced the officers and enlists from Punjab and gave strong preference to residents in [Sindh](28972.md), [Balochistan](4687.md), and [Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), and such policy continue to exists to maintain an ethnic balance in the army.<sup>: 163 </sup>[^cite_note-140] Those who are turned away are strongly encourage to join the [Marines Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines_Corps) or the [Air Force](377433.md).[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4]

In 1991, the department of the army drastically reduced the size of personnel from Punjab, downsizing the army personnel to 63%, and issues acceptable medical waivers interested enlists while encouraging citizens of [Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) and [Sindh](28972.md). This decision has given a fair chance to every citizen of Pakistan to be part of the Pakistan Army as each district possesses a fixed percentage of seats in all branches of the Army, as per census records. By 2003–05, the department of army continued its policy by drastically downsizing the personnel from Punjab to 43–70%.[^cite_note-141]

The Department of Army has relaxed its recruitment and medical standards in [Sindh](28972.md) and [Balochistan](4687.md) where the height requirement of 5 feet 4 inches is considered acceptable even with the enlists educational level at eighth grade is acceptable for the waiver; since the army recruiters take responsibility of providing education to 12th grade to the interested enlists from Balochistan and Sindh.<sup>: 31 </sup>[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Fair,_2014-7] In Punjab and [Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) where the recruitment is popular, the height requirement remains to be at 5 feet 6 inches with minimum education of 10th grade.[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Fair,_2014-7]

The army cadets undergo training in [Kakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakol,_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) at the [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy) where basic training takes place. Such training usually lasts for two years until the cadets are able to meet their graduation requirements from the academy.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Military_Academy_-_Cadets_Training-137] All the cadets have to attend and be trained at the PMA regardless of attending the military schools and colleges in other parts of the country.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Military_Academy_-_Cadets_Training-137]

Duration wise, it is one of the longest military training period in the country, and the training continues for two years until the cadet is being able [pass out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) from the academy, before selecting the college to start the career of their choice in the military.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Military_Academy_-_Cadets_Training-137]

### Women in the Pakistan Army

Main article: [Women in the Pakistan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Pakistan_Armed_Forces)



[MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO)'s Pakistani Female Engagement Team in [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md)

Women have been part of the Pakistan Army since 1947, and from the army's inception to 2018 approximately 4,000 women served the army though most women were doctors and civilians.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Today,_desk,_2019-142] In the years of 1947, '48 and '49, women were inducted into the Women's Guard Section of the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_National_Guard) and trained in medical work, welfare, and clerical positions (this was later disbanded).[^cite_note-143] Pakistan Army has a separate cadet course for women which is known as 'Lady Cadet Course', female cadets are trained in [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy).[^cite_note-144] After induction, women army officers go through a six-month military training at the Pakistan Military Academy which is not like their male counterparts. The comprehensive training includes military education and development of physical efficiency skills.[^cite_note-Women_dress-145]

Pakistan is the only Muslim-majority nation which appoints women to [general officer](148537.md) ranks, such as [Major-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major-General) [Shahida Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahida_Malik), the first woman army officer and military physician by profession who was promoted to a two-star rank.[^cite_note-146] In July 2013, the Army trained female paratrooper officers for the first time.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] In 2020, [Nigar Johar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigar_Johar) became the first female [Lieutenant General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_general_\(Pakistan\)) in the army, she was from the [Pakistan Army Medical Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Medical_Corps).[^cite_note-150]

### Religious minorities in Pakistan Army

The Army recruits from all religions in Pakistan including [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Pakistan), [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Pakistan), [Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism), [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Pakistan) who have held command-level positions.[^cite_note-Geo_News,_Ode,_2017-151] Religious services are provided by the Chaplain Corps for Muslims, Hindus, Christians, Sikhs, and Zoroastrians.[^cite_note-Vij_Books_India_Pvt_Ltd,_Alam,_2012-79]

In 1993, Major-General [Julian Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Peter) was the first Christian to be appointed at the command position while [Hercharn Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercharn_Singh) became the first Sikh to be commissioned in the army. Between 1947 and 2000, a policy of restricting [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Pakistan) prior enlisting in the Pakistan Army was in practice until the policy was reversed by the federal government.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_Dev,_2015-152] In 2006, army recruiters began recruiting Hindus into the army and people of all faith or no faith can be promoted to any rank or commanding position in the army.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] Pakistani Hindus have served in the Pakistan Armed Forces, with milestones including Captain Danish as the first Hindu officer commissioned in 2006,[^cite_note-155] Major Dr Kelash Kumar and Major Dr Aneel Kumar as the first Hindu officers promoted to Lieutenant Colonel in 2022,[^cite_note-156] and Dev Anand as the first Hindu pilot officer in the Pakistan Air Force in 2023.[^cite_note-157] Three notable Pakistani Hindu martyrs include Heman Das Kohli, killed in the line of duty in 2023,[^cite_note-158] Lance Naik Lal Chand Rabari, killed in the line of duty in 2017[^cite_note-159] and [Ashok Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashok_Kumar_\(soldier\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), posthumously awarded the Tamgha‑e‑Shujaat for bravery in Waziristan in 2013.[^cite_note-160]

## Uniforms

Main article: [Khaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaki)

From 1947 to 1971, the [army service uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Service_Uniform) of the Pakistan Army closely resembled to the [army uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_uniform) of the [British Army](4887.md), but the uniform changed in preference of [Sherwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwani). The army service uniform consists of a light khaki (tan) coloured long-sleeved shirt with matching trousers. It is accompanied by beret or peaked cap depending on the regiment the soldier belongs to. Organizational headwear, sashes, belts, spats, medals, lanyards, and other achievements are added for ceremonial duties. Unlike other countries in [South Asia](21566765.md), Pakistan army [officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer) uniforms don't include a [aiguillette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiguillette), rather it is used mostly by [aid-de-camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aide-de-camp). Senior officers wear a greenish-khaki stand-and-fall collared tunic, called the [Sherwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwani), with two front pockets, cap of a synthetic material, trousers with two pockets.<sup>: 222 </sup>[^cite_note-164]

In the 1970s, the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Pakistan\)) introduced the first camouflage pattern in the [army combat uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Combat_Uniform), resembling the British-styled [DPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disruptive_Pattern_Material) but this was changed in 1990 in favor of adopting the [U.S. Woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Woodland) which continued until 2010.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Army-_Camopedia-165] In winter front such as in the [Siachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siachen) and near the [Wakhan Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakhan_Corridor), the Pakistan Army personnel wears the heavy winter all white military gear.[^cite_note-166]

As of 2011, the camouflage pattern of the brown and black [BDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Dress_Uniform) was issued and is worn by the officers and the army troops in their times of deployments. The Pakistan Army has introduced arid camouflage patterns in uniform and resized qualification badges which are now service ribbons and no longer worn along with the ranks are now embroidered and are on the chest. ## Components and structure

### Army components and branches

Since the commencement of its organization in 1947, the army's functionality is broadly maintained in two main branches: Combat Arms and Administrative Services.<sup>: 46 </sup>[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_Cheema,_2002-35]<sup>: 570 </sup>[^cite_note-Area_Handbook,_US_Govt._1971-167] From 1947 to 1971, the Pakistan Army had the responsibility of maintaining the British-built [forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_forts_in_Pakistan) till the new and modern garrisons were built after 1971 and performing non-combat duties such as engineering and construction.[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing,_Inc.,_Coughley-4]

Currently, the Army's combat services consist of active-duty personnel and reservists that operate as members of the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(Pakistan\)) and the paramilitary [Civil Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Armed_Forces).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-132] The latter includes the [Frontier Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Corps) and the [Pakistan Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Rangers), which often perform military police duties for the provincial governments in Pakistan to help control and manage the law and control situation.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-132]

The two main branches of the army, Combat Arms and Administrative Services, also consist of several branches and functional areas that include the [army officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ranks_and_insignia_of_Pakistan), [junior commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_commissioned_officer) (or [warrant officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer_\(United_States\))), and the enlisted personnel who are classified from their branches in their uniforms and berets.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-132] In Pakistan Army, the careers are not restricted to military officials but are extended to civilian personnel and contractors who can progress in administrative branches of the army.[^cite_note-pakistanarmy.gov.pk-168]

### Command structure

Main article: [Structure of the Pakistan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_the_Pakistan_Army)



The command and control structure of the six tactical operational commands in the Pakistan Army

The reorganization of the position standing army in 2008, the Pakistan Army now operates six tactical commands, each commanded by the [GOC-in-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Officer_Commanding-in-Chief), with a holding [three-star rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-star_rank): [Lieutenant-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-General).[^cite_note-Vij_Books_India_Pvt_Ltd,_Alam-97] Each of the six tactical commands directly reports to the office of [Chief of Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)), operating directly at the [Army GHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHQ_\(Pakistan_Army\)).[^cite_note-Vij_Books_India_Pvt_Ltd,_Alam-97] Each command consists of two or more [Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps)– an army field formation responsible for [zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusion_zone) within a command theater.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-132]

There are nine active Corps in the Pakistan Army, composing of mixed infantry, mechanized, armored, artillery divisions, while the [Air Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Air_Defence_Corps), [Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Aviation_Corps), and the [Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Aviation_Corps) and [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Special_Forces) are organized and maintained in the separate level of their commands.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-132]

Established and organized in March 2000, the [Army Strategic Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Strategic_Forces_Command_\(Pakistan\)) is exercise its authority for responsible training in [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_safety), weapons deployments, and activation of the [atomic missile systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_missile_research_and_development_program).[^cite_note-169]

### Infantry branch

Main article: [Infantry Branch (Pakistan Army)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Branch_\(Pakistan_Army\))

Since its establishment in 1947, the Pakistan Army has traditionally followed the [British](31730.md) [regimental system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regimental_system) and culture, and currently there are six organized [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) regiments.[^cite_note-Infantry,_Army_ISPR-171]

In the infantry branch, there are originally six regiments are in fact the administrative military organization that are not combat field formation, and the size of the regiments are vary as their rotation and deployments including [assisting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Aid_to_the_Civil_Authorities) the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_Pakistan) in [civic administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Administrative_Service).[^cite_note-172]

In each of original six regiments, there are multiple battalions that are associated together to form an infantry regiment and such battalions do not fight together as one formation as they are all deployed over various formations in shape of being part of the [brigade combat team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_combat_team) (under a [Brigadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_\(United_Kingdom\))), [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)), or a being part of much larger [corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps).[^cite_note-Independent_Pakistan-173]

After the independence from the [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) in 1947, the Pakistan Army begin to follow the [U.S. Army](32087.md)'s standing formation of their [Infantry Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Branch_\(United_States\)), having the infantry battalion serving for a time period under a different command zone before being deployed to another command zone, usually in another sector or terrain when its tenure is over.[^cite_note-Independent_Pakistan-173]

The Pakistan Army has a division dedicated towards conducting the [unconventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_warfare) and [asymmetric warfare](46863.md) operations, established with the guidance provided by the [United States Army](32087.md) in 1956.[^cite_note-Random_House,_Bennett-175] This competitive special operation force is known as the [Special Services Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Services_Group) (Army SSG, distinguishing the [Navy SSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_\(Navy\))), and is assembled in eight battalions, commanded by the [Lieutenant-Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-Colonel), with addition of three [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_\(military_unit\)) commanded by the [Major](201920.md) or a [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(army\)), depending on the availability.[^cite_note-discovermilitary,_Army_SSG-176]

## Military philosophy

Since the 1960s, officers of the Pakistan Armed Forces have produced a significant body of autobiographical and analytical writings, continuing a tradition established by officers of the colonial Indian Army. Studies of this literature note that by 2011 more than 60 military authors had published nearly 100 titles, ranging from personal memoirs to accounts of military operations and political events. Some of these works became widely circulated and were reprinted multiple times, while others were self-published and distributed through local book markets. Scholars observe that these writings form an important part of the historiography of Pakistan and its military, as they provide firsthand perspectives on major events such as the Indo-Pakistani wars and the political role of the armed forces.[^cite_note-177]

### Combat doctrine (1947–2007)

Main article: [Exercise Zarb-e-Momin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Zarb-e-Momin)



The [U.S.-Pakistan military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan-United_States_military_relations): The group photo of the [United States Army](32087.md) and the Pakistan Army after coordinating the [joint operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Pakistan_floods) in [2010.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_in_Pakistan)

In 1947, the Pakistan Army's war strategists developed a combat doctrine which was called "[The Riposte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riposte)", which featured a strategy of "offensive-defense".<sup>: 310 </sup>[^cite_note-SAGE_Publications_India,_Rajain,_2005-178][^cite_note-179] In 1989, the first and official implementation of this strategy was refined and featured in the major military exercise, [Exercise Zab-e-Momin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Zarb%E2%80%93e%E2%80%93Momin), organized under [Lt-Gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-General) [Hamid Gul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Gul)[^cite_note-Army_ISPR,_Ex._Zarb-e-Momin-180]– this combat doctrine was fully focused in engaging towards its primary adversary, [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army).<sup>: 310 </sup>[^cite_note-SAGE_Publications_India,_Rajain,_2005-178]

In 1989–99, the [JS HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_HQ_\(Pakistan\)), working with the Army GHQ to identify several key factors considering the large conventional attacks from the better equipped and numerically advantage adversary, the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), derived the combat doctrine to assess the vulnerability of Pakistan where its vast majority of [population centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Pakistan) as well as political and military targets lies closer to the [international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_border) with [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-Defense_Journal,_Beg,_1999-181]



The Pakistan Army's special forces soldiers in a drill conducting jointly with the Russian special forces in 2016

The national security strategists explored the controversial idea of [strategic depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_depth) in form of fomenting friendly foreign relations with [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Iran](14653.md) while India substantially enhancing its offensive capabilities designed in its doctrine, the [Cold Start Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Start_\(military_doctrine\)).[^cite_note-Defense_Journal,_Beg,_1999-181] Due to the numerical advantage of [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) over its smaller adversary, the Pakistan Army, the Pakistani national security analysts noted that any counterattack on advancing Indian Army would be very tricky and miscalculated – the ideal response of countering the attacks from the Indian ground forces would be operationalizing the [battle-ranged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_ballistic_missile) [Hatf-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatf-I)A/[Hatf-IB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatf-IB) missiles.[^cite_note-Defense_Journal,_Beg,_1999-181] In times of national emergency, the Pakistan Army Reserves, supported by the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(Pakistan\)) and [Civil Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Armed_Forces), would likely be deployed to reinforce defensive positions and fortifications.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182] However, after the orders are authorized the Corps in both nation's will take between 24 and 72 hours to completely mobilize their combat assets. Therefore, both nation's armies will be evenly matched in the first 24 hours since the Pakistani units have to travel a shorter distance to their forward positions.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182]

Pakistan's military doctrine emphasizes a proactive defense, also referred to as "offensive-defense". This strategy prioritizes seizing the initiative in a conflict and launching limited counteroffensives to preempt potential enemy advances.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182] Proponents of Pakistan's "offensive-defense" doctrine argue that it offers several advantages. One key benefit is the potential to disrupt an enemy's offensive plans, forcing them to shift focus from their initial attack to defending their own territory. This could place Pakistan in a more favorable position by dictating the terms of engagement on the battlefield.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182] The strategic calculations by Pakistan Army's war strategists hope that the Pakistan Army's soldiers would keep the Indian Army engaged in fighting on the Indian territory, therefore the collateral damage being suffered by the Indian Army will be higher.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182] Pakistani planners also estimate that since Indian forces will not be able to reach their maximum strength near the border for another 48–72 hours, Pakistan might have parity or numerical superiority against India.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182] An important aspect in "offensive-defense" doctrine was to seize sizable Indian territory which gives Pakistan an issue to negotiate with India in the aftermath of a possible ceasefire brought about by the international pressure after 3–4 weeks of fighting.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182]

Due to fortification of [LoC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control) in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) and difficult terrains in Northern Punjab, the Army created the [Pakistan Army Reserves](#Combat_maneuvering_organizations) in the 1990s that is concentrated in the desert terrain of [Sindh](28972.md)-[Rajasthan](26291.md) sector, The Army Reserve South of the [Pakistan Army Reserves](#Combat_maneuvering_organizations) is grouped in several powerful field-level corps and designed to provide defensive maneuvers in case of war with the Indian Army.[^cite_note-Indian_Defence_Review,_Singh,_2011-182]

### Threat Matrix (2010–present)

Main articles: [Threat Matrix (database)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threat_Matrix_\(database\)), [New Concept of War Fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Concept_of_War_Fighting), [Exercise Azm-e-Nau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Azm-e-Nau), [Exercise High Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_High_Mark), and [Exercise Sea Spark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Sea_Spark)



[Urban warfare](319027.md) near [Afghanistan](737.md): Pakistan Army infantry troops engage in door-to-door [clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seek_and_destroy) during [N. Waziristan offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zarb-e-Azb) in 2016.

After the failure of the "Offensive-defense" in 1999, the national security institutions engaged in [critical thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thinking) to evaluate new doctrine that would provide a comprehensive [grand strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_strategy) against the infiltrating enemy forces, and development began 2010–11 for the new combat doctrine.[^cite_note-Express_Tribune,_2013-183] In 2013, the new combat doctrine, the [Threat Matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threat_Matrix_\(database\)), was unveiled by the [ISPR](7718433.md), that was the first time in its history that the army's national security analysts realized that Pakistan faces a real threat from within, a threat that is concentrated in areas along western borders.[^cite_note-Express_Tribune,_2013-183] The Threat Matrix doctrine analyze the military's comprehensive operational priorities and goes beyond in comprehensively describing both existential and non-existential threats to the country.[^cite_note-Express_Tribune,_2013-183]

Based on that strategy in 2013, the Pakistani military organized a four-tier joint military exercise, code-named: [Exercise Azm-e-Nau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Azm-e-Nau), in which the aim was to update the military's "readiness strategy for dealing with the complex security threat environment."[^cite_note-dawn,_Exercise_Azm-e-Nau-184] The objective of such exercises is to assess tactics, procedures, and techniques, and explore joint operations strategies involving all three branches of the military: the Army, [Air Force](377433.md), and [Navy](1183789.md).[^cite_note-dawn,_Exercise_Azm-e-Nau-184] In successive years, the Pakistani military combined all the branch-level exercises into [joint warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_warfare) exercises, in which all four branches now participate, regardless of the terrain, platforms, and control of command.[^cite_note-dawn,_Exercise_Azm-e-Nau-184]

## Education and training

### Schooling, teachings, and institutions



The Pakistan Army Music band's [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conducting) saluting after the performance in [Russia](25391.md)

The Pakistan Army offers wide range of extensive and lucrative careers in the military to young high school graduates and the college degree holders upon enlistment, and Pakistan Army operates the large number of training schools in all over the country.[^cite_note-Introduction_of_training_in_the_Pakistan_Army-185] The overall directions and management of the [army training schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Public_Schools_&_Colleges_System) are supervised and controlled by the policies devised by the [Education Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Education_Corps), and philosophy on instructions in army schools involves in modern education with combat training.[^cite_note-186]

At the time of its establishment of the Pakistan Army in 1947, the [Command and Staff College in Quetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_Staff_College_in_Quetta) was inherited to Pakistan, and is the oldest college established during the [colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire) in [1905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_in_India).[^cite_note-187] The [British officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_ranks) in the Pakistan Army had to established the wide range of schools to provide education and to train the army personnel in order to raise the dedicated and professional army.[^cite_note-188] The wide range of military officers in the Pakistani military were sent to attend the staff colleges in the United States, [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Australia](4689264.md), and [Canada](5042916.md) who were trained and excelled in courses in armory, infantry, artillery, and ordnance in 1950–1961.<sup>: 293 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]

The United States eventually took over the overall training programs in the Pakistan Army under the [International Military Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Education_and_Training) (IMET) but the U.S. coordination with Pakistan varied along with the vicissitudes of the [military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan%E2%80%93United_States_military_relations) between [two countries](7144235.md).<sup>: 12 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Ahrari,_2001-189] In the 1980s, the army had sent \~200 army officers abroad annually, two-thirds actually decided to attend schooling in the United States but the cessation of the United States' aid to Pakistan led the suspension of the [IMET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Education_and_Training), leading Pakistani military officers to choose the schooling in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).<sup>: 294 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]

After the [terrorist attacks](5058690.md) in the United States in 2001, the [IMET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Education_and_Training) cooperation was again activated with army officers begin attending the schooling in the United States but the training program was again suspended in 2018 by the [Trump administration](52231341.md), leveling accusations on [supporting armed Jihadi groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegations_of_support_system_in_Pakistan_for_Osama_bin_Laden) in [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-Reuters,_Ali_and_Stewart,_2018-190]

The Pakistan Army's training schools are not restricted to the department of the army only but inter-services officers and personnel have been trained and educated as part of the interdepartmental cooperation.[^cite_note-Introduction_of_training_in_the_Pakistan_Army-185] The Pakistan Army takes responsibility of providing the military training and education to [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines) at their School of Infantry and Tactics, and military officers in other branches have attended and qualified [psc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psc_\(military\)) from the [Command and Staff College in Quetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_Staff_College_in_Quetta).[^cite_note-Introduction_of_training_in_the_Pakistan_Army-185] Officers holding the ranks of [captains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(army\)), [majors](201920.md), [lieutenants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_\(naval\)) and [lieutenant-commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-Commander) in marines are usually invited to attend the courses at the Command and Staff College in Quetta to be qualified as [psc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psc_\(military\)).<sup>: 9 </sup>[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-44]

Established in 1971, the [National Defense University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) (NDU) in [Islamabad](51153.md) is the senior and higher education learning institution that provides the advance [critical thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thinking) level and research-based [strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_strategy) level education to the senior military officers in the Pakistani military.[^cite_note-National_Defence_University-191] The [NDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) in Islamabad is a significant institution of higher learning in understanding the institutional norms of military tutelage in Pakistan because it constitutes the "highest learning platform where the military leadership comes together for common instruction", according to thesis written by Pakistani author Aqil Shah.<sup>: 8 </sup>[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-44] Without securing their [graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) from their master's program, no officer in the Pakistani military can be promoted as general in the army or air force, or admiral in the navy as it is a prerequisite for their promotion to become a senior member at the [Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee).<sup>: 8–9 </sup>[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-44]

Besides, the platform provided at the [NDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) in Islamabad represents a radical shift from the emphasis on operational and staff functions and the level of ranks are imposed as a qualification to attend the [master's program](170346.md) at the [NDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad), usually [brigadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_\(United_Kingdom\)), [air commodores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Commodore), and [commodores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)), are invited to given admission in a broad range of strategic, political, social, and economic factors as these factors affect the country's national security.<sup>: 8–9 </sup>[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-44] In this sense, the [NDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) becomes the critical thinking institution as its constitutes active-duty senior military officers corps' baptism into a shared ideological framework about the military's appropriate role, status, and behavior in relation to state and society, and shared values affect how these officers perceive and respond to civilian governmental decisions, policies, and political crises.<sup>: 9–10 </sup>[^cite_note-192] Admissions to the army's military engineering colleges and [NDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) is not restricted to military officials but the civilians can also attend and graduate from the NDU, allowing the civilians to explore the broader aspects of national security.<sup>: 8–9 </sup>[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-44]



The [M60 AVLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M60_AVLB), the engineering vehicle currently inventory in Pakistan Army

Established in 1991, the [National University of Sciences and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Sciences_and_Technology,_Pakistan) (NUST) has now absorbed and amalgamated the existing military colleges of engineering, signals, aeronautical, medical and is a counterpart institution in [science and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_engineering) to that of the [National Defense University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) (NDU) in [Islamabad](51153.md).[^cite_note-193]

The foreign military officials and students, including from the United States, have attended the Command and Staff College in Quetta and the [National Defense University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad) (NDU) in [Islamabad](51153.md) but the American instructors and observers have penned critical analysis by reporting the curriculum offered by the Command and Staff College in Quetta to be narrow focus and failure to encourage speculative thinking or to give adequate attention to less glamorous subjects, such as logistics.<sup>: 293 </sup>[^cite_note-DIANE_Publishing,_Blood,_1996-135]<sup>: 518 </sup>[^cite_note-194]

## Civil engineering and construction

Main articles: [Civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) and [Karakoram Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_Highway)

Since the 1970s, the Pakistan Army's engineering formations have been involved in [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) of the important landmarks in the country, [hydroelectricity](381399.md), [power generation](9540.md), [dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dams_in_Pakistan), and [national freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-Infrastructures_Development-136]

The Pakistan Army has built major civil engineering landmarks in the country, including the [Karakoram Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_Highway), [Skardu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skardu_Airport), and the [national security sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_test_site) in [Kahuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahuta).[^cite_note-Infrastructures_Development-136] The [Frontier Works Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Works_Organization) of the army has built several infrastructure projects with the [Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) all over the country, and has built the communications lines in Northern Pakistan through its [Special Communications Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Communications_Organization) (SCO).[^cite_note-Infrastructures_Development-136]

The Corps of Engineers are the major civil engineering contractor and engineering consultant employed by the [federal government](1897833.md), advising on construction management and improving the efficiency of construction measures in times of natural calamities.[^cite_note-Corps_of_Engineers-195]

The Pakistan Army's landmark civil engineering projects included the [Lyari Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyari_Expressway) in Karachi, [Makran Coastal Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makran_Coastal_Highway) in [Balochistan](4687.md) and the [Khanpur Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanpur_Dam) in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md).[^cite_note-Corps_of_Engineers-195] Besides their infrastructure projects in Pakistan, the Pakistan Army has built several infrastructures projects in other parts of the world as part of their deployment in the United Nations peacekeeping missions.[^cite_note-Corps_of_Engineers-195]

## Involvement in Pakistani society

See also: [International response to the 2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_response_to_the_2005_Kashmir_earthquake) and [2009 refugee crisis in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_refugee_crisis_in_Pakistan)



The Pakistan Army soldiers distributing the military rations to the affectees of the national calamities. The Army often involves in the civil society to relief activities and national-building to the local population of affected areas.



The [RVF Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Corps_of_Remount_Veterinary_and_Farms) moving animals and livestock to a safer location after the flood warning issues by the [NDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Disaster_Management_Authority_\(Pakistan\)) in 2017

The Pakistan Army has played an integral part in the civil society of Pakistan, almost since its inception.[^cite_note-Taylor_and_Francis-e-Library-198] In 1996, General Jehangir Karamat described Pakistan armed forces' relations with the society:

> In my opinion, if we have to repeat of past events then we must understand that Military leaders can pressure only up to a point. Beyond that their own position starts getting undermined because the military is after all is a mirror image of the civil society from which it is drawn.

— General Jehangir Karamat on civil society–military relations[^cite_note-Taylor_and_Francis-e-Library-198]

In times of national calamities and natural disasters, including the devastating [earthquake in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake) or the [great floods in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Pakistan_floods), the army engineering corps, medical, logistical personnel, and other armed forces services have played a major role in [area rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehabilitation_counseling) and reconstruction of cities and towns while distributing the relief goods and [military rations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_rations) to the affected civilians.[^cite_note-Disaster_operations_by_Army-199] Since 1948, the army has been involved in providing power generation to affected areas, building dams, and construction of towns and cities, and conducting rescue operations for evacuations of general public and animals from endangerment.[^cite_note-Disaster_operations_by_Army-199]

To coordinate and manage the proper relief operations, reconstructions, and rehabilitation, the [federal government](1897833.md) [appoints the active-duty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Aid_to_the_Civil_Authorities), as an external billets appointments, to lead federal agencies such as the [NDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Disaster_Management_Authority_\(Pakistan\)).[^cite_note-200] Besides relief activities in the country, the Pakistan Army has also engaged in other parts of the world such as coordinating and leading the relief efforts in [Indonesia](14579.md), [Bangladesh](3454.md), and [Sri Lanka](26750.md) after these countries were affected by the [underwater earthquake that resulted in tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake) in 2004.[^cite_note-201]

[Stephen P. Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_P._Cohen) in his article, Pakistan: Army, Society and Security writes:

> There are armies which guard their nation's borders, there are armies which are concerned with protecting their own position in society, and there are armies which defend a cause or an idea. The Pakistan Army does all three.

— Stephen P. Cohen[^cite_note-202]

## Corporate and business activities

See also: [Fauji Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauji_Foundation), [Army Welfare Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Welfare_Trust), and [Defence Housing Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Housing_Authority)

The Pakistan Army's business interests, managed through foundations like the Fauji Foundation, have been a subject of international scrutiny. News agencies and investigations by financial regulators allege the military controls a vast network of business enterprises and conglomerates, with estimates suggesting a total revenue of US\$20 billion in 2007–2008.[^cite_note-aljazeera,_Abdullahi,_2008-203] The [Defence Housing Authority (DHA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Housing_Authority) and [Askari Real Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Welfare_Trust) are prominent examples of the Pakistan Army's large-scale real estate ventures. However, critics point out that none of the 46 housing schemes directly built by the armed forces cater to ordinary soldiers, civilian officers or other army personnel.[^cite_note-204]

The [Fauji Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauji_Foundation) (lit. 'Soldier Foundation') is a Pakistani conglomerate publicly traded on the [Pakistan Stock Exchange](49066780.md) (PSX). It manufactures and sells processed meat, and operates stud and dairy farms primarily for the military's use. The foundation also has subsidiaries that contribute to the civilian economy through various enterprises, including bakeries, security services and banking. Its factories produce a wide range of goods, including sugar and fertilizer.[^cite_note-aljazeera,_Abdullahi,_2008-203]

### Nishan-e-Haider

Main article: [Nishan-e-Haider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishan-e-Haider)

- Nishan-e-Haider, (

  [Nishan-e-Haider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishan-e-Haider)\
  (lit. 'Order of Lion')\
  Nine out of ten Army personnel have been posthumously honoured

In [military awards hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Awards), the [Nishan-e-Haidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishan-e-Haider) (lit. 'Order of Lion Urdu نشان حیدر') is the highest and most prestigious honour awarded posthumously for bravery and actions of valor in event of war.<sup>: 220 </sup>[^cite_note-206] The honour is a namesake of [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali) and the recipients receiving this honorary title as a sign of respect: *Shaheed* meaning *martyr*.<sup>: 4 </sup>[^cite_note-207]

Since 1947–2019, there has been ten Pakistani military officers and personnel who have honoured with this prestigious medal— out of which, nine have been officers and soldiers in the Pakistan Army, bestowed to those who engaged in wars with India.[^cite_note-208]

### Recipient of the foreign awards

The Pakistan Army has received foreign awards for its services to other nations. Notably, two army pilots from the [Aviation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Aviation_Corps) were honored for a daring rescue operation. Lt-Col. Rashiduhlla Beg and Lt-Col. Khalid Amir were awarded the Golden Order for Services by the Slovenian President in the capital, Ljubljana, for rescuing Slovenian mountaineer Tomaz Humar, who was stranded on the 8,125-meter (26,657 ft) peak Nanga Parbat.[^cite_note-209]

In addition, there are numbers of the army general officers have been honoured multiple times with the United States's [Legion of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Merit) for cooperation and strengthening bilateral ties with the United States 1980s–2015.<sup>: 261 </sup>[^cite_note-210] In 2010, the Pakistan Army was awarded with a [gold medal](526488.md) at the [Exercise Cambrian Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Cambrian_Patrol) held in Wales in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]
