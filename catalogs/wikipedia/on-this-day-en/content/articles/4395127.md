**Aeroenlaces Nacionales, S.A. de C.V.**, trading as **Viva** (formerly **Viva Aerobus**), is a [major Mexican low-cost airline](11898233.md) headquartered at [Monterrey International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_International_Airport), in Apodaca, Nuevo Leon, [Mexico](3966054.md). Measured by passenger numbers, it is Mexico's third-largest airline and eleventh-largest airline in North America, offering more than 160 routes in more than 50 destinations serving [Mexico](3966054.md), the [United States](3434750.md), Central and South America.[^cite_note-6]

Viva is fully owned by the largest bus company group in Mexico, IAMSA, and was co-founded by and invested in by Irelandia Aviation.[^cite_note-irelandia-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-irelandia-vivaaerobus-stake-9]

Viva operates mostly within a combination of [point-to-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-to-point_transit) system with direct flights between middle-size airports and a [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoke%E2%80%93hub_distribution_paradigm) system. [Monterrey International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_International_Airport) serves as its largest hub in terms of passengers carried and the number of departures. [Cancun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn_International_Airport), [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Hidalgo_y_Costilla_Guadalajara_International_Airport), [Mexico City-Benito Juarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City_International_Airport), [Los Cabos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Cabos_International_Airport), [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida_International_Airport), [Mexico City-Felipe Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_%C3%81ngeles_International_Airport) and [Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_International_Airport) serve as operating bases.

## History

The airline, launched as Viva Aerobus, commenced operations on 30 November 2006, with its hub at [Monterrey International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_International_Airport), in [Monterrey](40455.md), Mexico, and with an initial investment of \$50 million and two [Boeing 737-300](15103676.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-irelandia-vivaaerobus-stake-9] Viva Aerobus was co-owned by [Ryanair](262878.md)'s Irelandia Aviation and the Mexican bus company IAMSA.[^cite_note-it-vivaaerobus-ipo-10] Ryanair joined with Alexander Maurice Mason of Kite Investments to establish "RyanMex" to facilitate the Irish family's investment in the Mexican airline. IAMSA has showed interest in developing a new stage of transportation after its successful bus market in Mexico, and Irelandia has been actively investigating the market opportunity, leading to the companies' partnership to form a Mexican low-cost carrier.[^cite_note-irelandia-7] Irelandia held a 49% of shares in the airline, while IAMSA had the remaining majority stake.[^cite_note-it-vivaaerobus-ipo-10]

The airline initially connected Monterrey to a number of Mexican domestic locations, and in July 2007, publicly confirmed its intention to open its first base outside of Mexico and first US destination in Austin, Texas (although it ultimately pulled out of Austin in 2009).[^cite_note-11]

Viva Aerobus fares were intended to undercut traditional Mexican carriers by up to 50%, in a change of the industry that started with the arrival of the country's second generation of low-cost airlines ([Avolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avolar), [MexicanaClick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MexicanaClick), [Interjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interjet), [Volaris](3717400.md)) and the privatization of [Mexicana de Aviación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicana_de_Aviaci%C3%B3n_\(1921%E2%80%932010\)), one of the two top national airlines. The airline started operations at [Monterrey International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_International_Airport) in November 2006 with two aircraft and one nonstop service connecting [Monterrey](40455.md) to [Tijuana](101739.md), under the leadership of Mike Szucs as its CEO. A year after its foundation, in October 2007, Viva Aerobus had already transported one million passengers. Later, Donald Rogers became Viva Aerobus CEO in May 2009, the month when [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara) became the airline's second base. In June 2010, the airline's leadership was assigned to Juan Carlos Zuazua, current CEO. Expansion continued and the airline established [Mexico City](18987.md), [Cancun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancun), [Tijuana](101739.md) and [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Yucat%C3%A1n) as its new bases in 2011, 2013, 2015 and 2022, respectively.[^cite_note-12]

On 5 November 2007, the airline received approval from the [US Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Transportation) to operate to [Austin-Bergstrom International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin-Bergstrom_International_Airport), initially serving the Mexican destinations of [Cancún](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn), Monterrey, [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara) and [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Guanajuato). Flights to the [South Terminal Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Terminal_Austin) began on 1 May 2008.

On 16 May 2009, Viva Aerobus stated it would cease passenger operations at Austin-Bergstrom International Airport on 31 May 2009. The airline blamed the pullout in part on an outbreak of [swine flu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H1N1_2009), which caused an unprecedented decrease in demand for service.[^cite_note-13] The company began to fly between Monterrey and Las Vegas in the summer of 2009.

In November 2009, the airline announced it had applied to commence operations between [Hermosillo](147209.md) and [Las Vegas](47737.md) beginning in March 2010.

In April 2010, the airline finally began serving Mexico's capital with two flights, one from Monterrey and the other from Guadalajara. It also started serving Houston's [George Bush Intercontinental Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bush_Intercontinental_Airport). However, the airline planned to serve [Houston Hobby Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Hobby_Airport) on completion of that airport's new terminal in 2015.[^cite_note-14]

In 2010, the airline opened a focus city at [Mexico City International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City_International_Airport) on 1 October after Mexicana ceased operations. Viva Aerobus switched Mexico City from focus city into a hub on 1 April 2011, beginning more destinations from the airport.

On 26 July 2011, the airline received approval to fly to [Chicago-Midway International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago-Midway_International_Airport).[^cite_note-15] On 15 August the same year, Viva Aerobus announced it would begin round-trip service between San Antonio (SAT) and Monterrey (MTY), Mexico starting that November.[^cite_note-16] On 13 April 2012, the airline ceased flights to Chicago Midway. On 7 December 2019, the airline resumed flying from Monterrey to Chicago, but this time via [O'Hare International Airport](248449.md). In addition, the airline flew to [Morelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morelia_International_Airport), [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara_International_Airport), [Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Baj%C3%ADo_International_Airport) and [Zacatecas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Calera_Airport) from Chicago-O'Hare for the 2019-2020 [winter season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_season).[^cite_note-17]

In October 2013, Viva Aerobus has signed an agreement to order 52 [Airbus A320](164933.md) aircraft, consisting of 40 [A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320neo) and 12 [A320ceo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320ceo) aircraft, for \$5.1 billion, making it the largest order by a Latin American carrier.[^cite_note-it-vivaaerobus-ipo-10][^cite_note-18]

On 15 May 2014, Viva Aerobus received and started using Airbus A320s. It continued using Boeing 737s until the end of 2016, when it began operating an all-Airbus fleet.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

In October 2014, Viva Aerobus began nonstop flights from Ciudad Juárez (Chihuahua State) to Mexico City, León, and Hermosillo. In 2015, the airline launched flights to [Dallas–Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth_International_Airport) from several Mexican cities.[^cite_note-21] The service was terminated in October 2015.[^cite_note-22]

As of 8 December 2016, IAMSA has acquired a 100% stake of the company after Irelandia Aviation sold its 49% stake to the company following regulatory approval.[^cite_note-irelandia-vivaaerobus-stake-9][^cite_note-23]

On 17 December 2017, Viva Aerobus began non-stop service to [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md) from Guadalajara International Airport.

In 2018, Viva Aerobus began non-stop service to [Las Vegas](47737.md) and [New York City](645042.md) from Mexico City International Airport.[^cite_note-24]

Viva Aerobus announced the launch of its cargo subsidiary, Viva Cargo, in January 2020.[^cite_note-25]

In December 2021, Viva Aerobus has partnered with [Allegiant Air](1372018.md) to form an alliance and expand low-budget services between the United States and Mexico. Subject to regulatory approval, the alliance is planned to launch in 2023.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In early August 2022, Viva Aerobus announced a [codeshare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codeshare) agreement with [Iberia](98613.md) to better connect passengers going between Mexico and Spain.

In September 2024, Viva Aerobus and [Air Canada](145623.md) announced an interline agreement.[^cite_note-29]

In October 2024, the airline underwent a major rebranding as simply "Viva", and introduced a new "Flex-Sí-bilidad" policy for a more relaxed experience for passengers, which include improved ticket, name, and flight changes. Alongside, a new logo and livery was introduced.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

In December 2025, Viva and [Volaris](3717400.md) have signed an agreement to merge and form a new airline group, effectively creating Mexico's largest domestic carrier. The deal would allow the airlines to continue operating as separate brands.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Headquarters

The corporate headquarters is in Terminal C of [Monterrey International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_International_Airport) in [Apodaca, Nuevo Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apodaca,_Nuevo_Leon).[^cite_note-51] It occupies space in the terminal's cargo zone.[^cite_note-52]

### Sponsorships

To promote physical activity and healthy recreation, Viva has been the official sponsor of the [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_F.C.), [Tigres UANL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_UANL) and [América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) Liga MX soccer teams; as well as Los Tomateros de Culiacan and Los Sultanes de Monterrey Mexican baseball teams. Internationally, it is also a sponsor of the [NBA](22093.md) team the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md) and the [Las Vegas Raiders](22312.md) of the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### Fleet development



Viva [Airbus A321neo](32104573.md) in its former livery version

In its beginnings, Viva Aerobus operated with a fleet integrated by [Boeing 737-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) aircraft. In October 2016, the airline took ownership of the first [PW1100-JM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_PW1000G)-powered [Airbus A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo).[^cite_note-Nensel-2016-73] This was part of an order placed with Airbus in October 2013 for 52 [Airbus A320 family](164933.md) aircraft, including 12 A320ceos, at the time the largest number of aircraft ordered by a Mexican airline directly from Airbus.[^cite_note-Nensel-2016-73][^cite_note-Airbus-2020-74][^cite_note-Airbus-2018-75] The first A320ceo had been delivered in May 2015.[^cite_note-Nensel-2016-73] At the time of receiving the first A320ceo, the carrier operated a fleet of six leased A320s and [Boeing 737-300](15103676.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-76]

Viva Aerobus amended an existing order with Airbus in July 2018 to include the [Airbus A321neo](32104573.md), of which 41 were included in the new orderbook.[^cite_note-Airbus-2018-75] The carrier took delivery of the first of these aircraft in June 2020.[^cite_note-Airbus-2020-74] In July 2023, the airline signed a memorandum of understanding with Airbus for an additional 90 240-seater Airbus A321neo aircraft.[^cite_note-77]

## Accidents

- 18 March 2021: Flight 4343, operated by an [Airbus A320-232](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320-200), was a scheduled flight from Puerto Vallarta-Gustavo D. Ordaz Airport to Monterrey-General Mariano Escobedo International Airport. While turning 180 degrees to line up for takeoff from runway 22, the nose [landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear) collapsed. All 133 people on board were evacuated via the emergency slides safely. However, the crash resulted in the aircraft being [written off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_loss).
