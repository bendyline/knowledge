# Snuff film

"Snuff movie" redirects here. For the 2005 film, see [*Snuff-Movie*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff-Movie). For the 1976 film, see [*Snuff* (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_\(film\)).

&#x20;A **snuff film**, **snuff movie**, or **snuff video** is a type of film, oftentimes defined as being produced for profit or financial gain, that shows scenes of actual [homicide](123451.md) committed or orchestrated by the filmmakers.

The concept of snuff films became known to the general public during the 1970s, when an [urban legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_legend) alleged that a clandestine industry was producing such films for profit. The rumor was amplified in 1976 by the release of a film called *[Snuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_\(film\))*, which capitalized on the legend through a disingenuous marketing campaign. However, that film, like others on the topic, relied on [special effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_effects) to simulate [murder](20171.md). The existence of a snuff movie industry has never been proven: [horror film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film) magazine *[Fangoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangoria)* later described the very concept as a "myth" and "a [scare tactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearmongering), dreamt up by the media to terrify the public."[^cite_note-Fangoria-1]

In 2025, [*Vice*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_\(magazine\)) published an article documenting what they described as the first actual snuff video, known as *[The Vietnamese Butcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Xu%C3%A2n_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1t)*, depicting the [killing of a willing participant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensual_homicide). Video clips and images from this murder have been sold since February 2025 on the [dark web](40712897.md) and through [Telegram](41501305.md) channels.

## Definitions

A snuff film is a movie in a purported genre of films in which a person is actually [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murdered), though some variations of the definition may include films that show people dying by [suicide](16831059.md). According to existing definitions, snuff films can be [pornographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornographic_film) and are made for financial gain but are supposedly "circulated amongst a jaded few for the purpose of entertainment".[^cite_note-snopesApril2021-2] The *[Collins English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_English_Dictionary)* defines a "snuff movie" as "a pornographic film in which an unsuspecting actress or actor is murdered at the climax of the film";[^cite_note-Collins-3] the *[Cambridge Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Advanced_Learner's_Dictionary)* defines it more broadly as "a violent film that shows a real murder".[^cite_note-4]

*[Fangoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangoria)* defined snuff movies as "films in which a person is killed on camera. The death is premeditated, with the purpose of being filmed in order to make money. Often times, there is a sexual aspect to the murder, either on film (as in, a porn scene that ends horribly) or that the final project is used for sexual gratification." Films featuring deaths that are authentic but accidental "are not considered snuff because the deaths were not planned. Other death on video, such as [terrorists beheading victims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beheading_video), are done to fulfill an ideology, not to earn money."[^cite_note-Fangoria-1]

Some filmed records of executions and deaths in war exist, but in those cases the death was not specifically staged for financial gain or entertainment.[^cite_note-snopesApril2021-2] There have been a number of "amateur-made" snuff films available on the Internet. However, such videos are produced by the murderers to make an impact on an audience or for their own satisfaction, and not for financial profit. Some [specialized websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_site) show videos of actual killings for profit, as their [shock value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_value) will attract an audience; but these websites are not operated by the perpetrators of the murders.[^cite_note-whowatchesthisstuff-5]

According to *[Snopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snopes)*, the idea of an actual snuff film "industry" clandestinely producing such "entertainment" for monetary gain is preposterous because "capturing a murder on film would be foolhardy at best. Only the most deranged would consider preserving for a jury a perfect video record of a crime they could go to the executioner for. Even if the murderer stays completely out of the camera's way, too much of who the killer is, how the murder was carried out, and where it took place would be part of such a film, and these details would quickly lead police to the right door. Though someone whose mania has caused them to lose touch with reality might skip over this point, those who are supposedly in the business for the money would be all too aware of this. It doesn't make sense to flirt with the electric chair for the profits derived from a video."[^cite_note-snopesApril2021-2]

## History of the concept

### Origins of the urban legend

The noun *snuff* originally meant the part of a candle wick that has already burned; the verb *snuff* meant to cut this off, and by extension to extinguish or kill.[^cite_note-6] The word has been used in this sense in English slang for hundreds of years. It was defined in 1874 as a "term very common among the lower orders of London, meaning to die from disease or accident".[^cite_note-7]

Film studies professor Boaz Hagin argues that the concept of films showing actual murders originated decades earlier than is commonly believed, at least as early as 1907. That year, Polish-French writer [Guillaume Apollinaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Apollinaire) published the short story *A Good Film* about [newsreel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsreel) photojournalists who stage and film a murder due to public fascination with crime news; in the story, the public believes the murder is real but police determine that the crime was faked.[^cite_note-8] Hagin also proposes that the film *[Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_\(1976_film\))* (1976) contains an explicit (fictional) snuff film depiction when television news executives orchestrate the on-air murder of a news anchor to boost ratings.

Stuntman [Ormer Locklear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormer_Locklear) and his copilot died in an airplane crash that was included in the final print of the 1920 film *[The Skywayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Skywayman)*. The deaths were used to promote the film.[^cite_note-9]

According to film critic [Geoffrey O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_O'Brien), "whether or not commercially distributed 'snuff' movies actually exist, the possibility of such movies is implicit in the stock [B-movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-movie) motif of the mad artist killing his models, as in *[A Bucket of Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bucket_of_Blood)* (1959), *[Color Me Blood Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_Me_Blood_Red)* (1965), or *Decoy for Terror* (1967) also known as *Playgirl Killer*."[^cite_note-10] Likewise, the protagonist of *[Peeping Tom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peeping_Tom_\(1960_film\))* (1960) films the murders he commits, though he does so as part of his mania and not for financial gain: a 1979 article in *[The New York Times](30680.md)* described the character's activity as making "private 'snuff' films".[^cite_note-11]

The first known use of the term *snuff movie* is in a 1971 book by [Ed Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sanders), *The Family: The Story of Charles Manson's Dune Buggy Attack Battalion*. This book included the interview of an anonymous one-time member of [Charles Manson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Manson)'s "[Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manson_Family)", who claimed that the group once made such a film in [California](5407.md), by recording the murder of a woman. However, the interviewee later added that he had not watched the film himself and had just heard rumors of its existence. In later editions of the book, Sanders clarified that no films depicting real murders or murder victims had been found.[^cite_note-snopesApril2021-2][^cite_note-12]

During the first half of the 1970s, [urban legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_legend) started to allege that snuff films were being produced in [South America](26769.md) for commercial gain, and circulated clandestinely in the United States.[^cite_note-csicop.org-13][^cite_note-Cashing-14]

### *Snuff* controversy (1976)

Main article: [Snuff (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_\(film\))

The idea of movies showing actual murders for profit became more widely known in 1976 with the release of the [exploitation film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation_film) *Snuff*.[^cite_note-csicop.org-13][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] This low-budget [horror film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film), loosely based on the [Manson murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate%E2%80%93LaBianca_murders) and originally titled *Slaughter*, was shot in [Argentina](18951905.md) by [Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Findlay_\(filmmaker\)) and [Roberta Findlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberta_Findlay). The film's distribution rights were bought by Allan Shackleton, who eventually found the picture unfit for release and shelved it. Several years later, Shackleton read about snuff films being imported from South America and decided to cash in on the rumor as an attempt to recoup his investment in *Slaughter*.[^cite_note-csicop.org-13][^cite_note-Cashing-14][^cite_note-Press-17]

Shackleton retitled *Slaughter* to *Snuff* and released it with a new ending that purported to depict an actual murder committed on a film set.[^cite_note-Cashing-14] *Snuff'*&#x73; promotional material suggested, without stating outright, that the film featured the real murder of a woman, which amounted to [false advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_advertising).[^cite_note-poison-18][^cite_note-Leonard-19] The slogan read: "The film that could only be made in South America... where life is CHEAP".[^cite_note-20] Shackleton put out false newspaper clippings that reported a citizens group's crusading against the film,[^cite_note-csicop.org-13] and hired people to act as protesters to picket screenings.[^cite_note-csicop.org-13]

Shackleton's efforts succeeded in generating a [media frenzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_frenzy) about the film: real [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism) and citizens groups eventually started protesting the movie and picketing theaters.[^cite_note-Leonard-19][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-varrev-22] As a result, New York District Attorney [Robert M. Morgenthau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Morgenthau) investigated the picture, establishing that it was a [hoax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoax).[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-23][^cite_note-24] The controversy nevertheless made the film financially profitable.[^cite_note-csicop.org-13][^cite_note-25]

### Rumors related to serial killers and other controversies

In subsequent years, more urban legends emerged about snuff movies. Notably, multiple [serial killers](42626.md) were rumored to have produced snuff films: however, no such videos were proven to exist. [Henry Lee Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lee_Lucas) and his accomplice [Ottis Toole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottis_Toole) claimed to have filmed their crimes, but both men were "pathological liars" and the purported films were never found.[^cite_note-Fangoria-1] [Charles Ng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ng) and [Leonard Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Lake) videotaped their interactions with some of their future victims, but not the murders. [Lawrence Bittaker and Roy Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Bittaker_and_Roy_Norris) made an audio recording of their encounter with one victim, though not of her death. Likewise, [Paul Bernardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bernardo) and [Karla Homolka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karla_Homolka) made videos of Bernardo sexually abusing two victims, but did not film the murders. In all those cases, the recordings were not intended for public consumption and were used as evidence during the murderers' trials.[^cite_note-snopesApril2021-2]

Over the years, [several films were suspected of being "snuff movies"](#False_snuff_films), though none of these accusations turned out to be true. A similar controversy concerned the filming of the video for the 1989 song "[Down in It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_in_It)" by [Nine Inch Nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails), in which [Trent Reznor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Reznor) acted in a scene which ended with the implication that Reznor's character had fallen off a building and died. To film the scene, a camera was tied to a balloon with ropes. Minutes after filming started, the ropes snapped and the balloons and camera flew away, eventually landing on a farmer's field in [Michigan](18859.md). The farmer later handed it to the [FBI](11127.md), who began investigating whether the footage was a snuff film portraying a person committing suicide.[^cite_note-Convulsion_Nine_Inch_Nails_1991-26][^cite_note-27] The FBI identified Reznor and the investigation ended when it was confirmed that Reznor was alive and the footage was not related to crime.[^cite_note-Convulsion_Nine_Inch_Nails_1991-26][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

Around 2018, a [conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory) called "Frazzledrip" and related to [Pizzagate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzagate_conspiracy_theory) and [QAnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QAnon), purported the existence of a snuff video where [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) and her aide [Huma Abedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huma_Abedin) murdered a young girl as part of a [Satanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanism) ritual.[^cite_note-snopes-frazzle-2018-30][^cite_note-31]

### Internet age

The advent of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet), by allowing anyone to broadcast self-made videos to an international audience, also changed the means of production of films that may be categorized as "snuff". There have been several cases of murders being filmed by their perpetrators and later finding their way online. These include videos made by Mexican [cartels](197015.md) or [jihadist](7826589.md) groups, at least one of the videos shot by the [Dnepropetrovsk maniacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnepropetrovsk_maniacs) in mid-2000s [Ukraine](31750.md), the [video shot by Luka Magnotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Jun_Lin) from [Montreal](7954681.md) in 2012, the video shot by [Vester Lee Flanagan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murders_of_Alison_Parker_and_Adam_Ward) in 2015, as well as cases of [livestreamed murders](52973276.md), including videos made by [mass shooters](31883778.md).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

Author [Steve Lillebuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Lillebuen), who wrote a book on the Magnotta case, commented that [social media](5897742.md) had created a new trend in crime where killers who crave an audience can become "online broadcasters" by showing their crimes to the world.[^cite_note-steve-36][^cite_note-steve2-37]

*Fangoria* commented that Magnotta's 2012 video, which showed him mutilating the corpse of his victim, was the closest thing in existence to an actual snuff movie, especially as Magnotta had done some crude editing and used a [song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Faith_\(song\)) as a soundtrack, which amounted to minimal production values. However, it did not show the murder itself and was originally published to attract attention and not for monetary gain.[^cite_note-Fangoria-1] The charges of which Magnotta was found guilty included "publishing [obscene](22036467.md#Canada) materials".[^cite_note-38] In 2016, the owner of [Bestgore.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bestgore.com), the website that originally hosted Magnotta's video, pleaded guilty to an obscenity charge and was sentenced to a six-month conditional sentence, half of which was served under house arrest.[^cite_note-39]

In 2025, it was reported members of the Russian mercenary [Wagner Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) were sharing graphic videos of war crimes via [Telegram](41501305.md), sometimes even behind [paywalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall), prompting calls for an [ICC](14880.md) investigation. A confidential legal brief submitted to the International Criminal Court (ICC) by legal experts at [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley) asserts Wagner has been distributing highly graphic videos—involving mutilation, torture, and even scenes implying cannibalism—on Telegram channels tied to their network.[^cite_note-Pronczuk-40] These videos were shared explicitly to terrorize civilians and dehumanize victims in [Mali](19127.md), [Burkina Faso](3470.md), and [Niger](21373.md).[^cite_note-Skopeliti-41] The Telegram channel White Uncles in Africa, believed to be managed by current or former Wagner operatives, has reposted such content. Experts argue that even sharing these videos may itself be a war crime, constituting a violation of human dignity under the [Rome Statute](68762.md).[^cite_note-Skopeliti-41]

On July 7, 2025, the Israeli state arms manufacturer and exporter [Rafael Advanced Defense Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems) made a social media posting on X that promoted their [Spike Firefly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Firefly) miniature [loitering munition](53703349.md) using a video of an attack on a lone man who was walking out in the open in the middle of a street.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

### *The Vietnamese Butcher*

Main article: [Killing of Nguyễn Xuân Đạt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Xu%C3%A2n_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1t)

In late July 2025, graphic video and screenshot compilations of what became colloquially known as *"The Vietnamese Butcher"* began circulating on [Telegram](41501305.md) and other encrypted messaging apps. It was eventually leaked to the public through [shock sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_sites) and other platforms with an extended version, bringing into the light what is described by *[Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_\(magazine\))* as potentially the first authentic snuff film.[^cite_note-u406-45]

The video itself involved a willing male participant, who was depicted masturbating before being beheaded with a meat cleaver by a man wearing a plastic [Guy Fawkes mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Fawkes_mask). A montage shows body parts stacked up, intestines separated, followed by images of some kind of food containing an unidentified meat.[^cite_note-u406-45] 11 video clips and 98 images of the event were made available on the [dark web](40712897.md) and on Telegram to buy as a pack since at least February 2025. Videos and images of the film were also shared around various social media apps and shock sites. *Vice'*&#x73; report suggested that the victim may have been aware of the intention to monetize the film, and also encouraged it.[^cite_note-u406-45]

The victim was eventually identified as Nguyễn Xuân Đạt (10 March 1989 – 25 January 2025),[^cite_note-46] a Vietnamese man who had multiple Facebook accounts expressing his [sexual desire of being beheaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autassassinophilia).[^cite_note-u406-45] Internet users tried to find information on the killer.[^cite_note-u406-45] Other similar footage, with images of Asian men being graphically murdered, later surfaced on [Baidu Tieba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidu_Tieba), a Chinese messaging app often used for criminal activities.[^cite_note-u406-45]

In late November 2025, Internet users traced the leaked information to a suspect, eventually leading to a police investigation at an alleged crime scene inside a [market surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_surveillance) workplace in [Lạng Sơn province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%E1%BA%A1ng_S%C6%A1n_province).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On 3 December 2025, the [Ministry of Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_\(Vietnam\))[^cite_note-49] and Lạng Sơn provincial police released information that the suspect had been identified as a 57-year-old civil service worker named Đoàn Văn Sáng, and arrested under the charge of murder.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

## In fiction

See also: [Category\:Films about snuff films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Films_about_snuff_films)

Since the concept became familiar to the general public, snuff films being made for profit or entertainment have been used as a core plot element or at least mentioned in numerous works of fiction, including the 1979 films *[Hardcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_\(1979_film\))* and *[Bloodline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodline_\(1979_film\))*, and [Bret Easton Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bret_Easton_Ellis)'s 1985 novel *[Less than Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Less_than_Zero_\(novel\))*. The 1991 novel *The Brave* by [Gregory Mcdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Mcdonald) and its [1997 film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brave_\(film\)) tell the story of a destitute man who agrees to "star" as the victim in a snuff film so he can provide for his family. The novel *A Dance at the Slaughterhouse* (1991) by [Lawrence Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Block) shows private investigator [Matthew Scudder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Scudder) discovering what appears to be a snuff film and looking into its origins.&#x20;

The making or discovery of one or several snuff films is the premise of various [horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film), [thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_film) or [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_film) films, such as *[Last House on Dead End Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_House_on_Dead_End_Street)* (1977), *[Videodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videodrome)* (1983), *[Special Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Effects_\(film\))* (1984), *[Strange Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Days_\(film\))* (1995), *[Tesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesis)* (1996), *[8mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8mm_\(film\))* (1999), *[Vacancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacancy_\(film\))* (2007), *[Snuff 102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_102)* (2007), *[A Serbian Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Serbian_Film)* (2010), *[Sinister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinister_\(film\))* (2012), *[The Counselor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Counselor_\(film\))* (2013), *[Luther: The Fallen Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther:_The_Fallen_Sun)* (2023), and the episode "[The Devil of Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_of_Christmas)" (2016) in the [black comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy) series *[Inside No. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_No._9)*. The 2003 video game *[Manhunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhunt_\(video_game\))* sees the main character being forced to participate in a series of snuff films to guarantee his freedom. The 2005 video game *[Grand Theft Auto: Liberty City Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto:_Liberty_City_Stories)* features a mission titled "Snuff", where the main character kills a few gangsters while unknowingly being filmed for a snuff movie by a third party, which may be a reference to *[Manhunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhunt_\(video_game\))*. Also, pretend snuff porn is sometimes filmed as a [fetish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_fetishism).

Several horror films such as *[Cannibal Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannibal_Holocaust)* (1980) and *[August Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Underground)* (2001) have depicted "snuff movie" situations, coupled with [found footage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Found_footage_\(film_technique\)) aesthetics used as a narrative device. Though some of these films have generated controversy as to their nature and content, none were, nor have officially purported to be, actual snuff movies.

### False snuff films

#### *Faces of Death*

Main article: [Faces of Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faces_of_Death)

The 1978 [mondo film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondo_film) *Faces of Death*, which spawned several sequels, is one of the films most commonly associated with the "snuff movie" concept, even though it was not produced by murderers nor clandestinely distributed. Purporting to be an educational film about [death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death), it mixed [footage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footage) of actual deadly accidents, suicides, autopsies, or executions, with "outright fake scenes" obtained with the help of special effects.[^cite_note-snopesApril2021-2]

#### The *Guinea Pig* films

Main article: [Guinea Pig (film series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_Pig_\(film_series\))

The first two films in the Japanese *Guinea Pig* horror film series, *[Guinea Pig: Devil's Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_Pig:_Devil's_Experiment)* and *[Guinea Pig 2: Flower of Flesh and Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_Pig_2:_Flower_of_Flesh_and_Blood)* (both released in 1985) are designed to look like snuff films; the video is grainy and unsteady, as if recorded by amateurs, and extensive [practical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_effects) and [special effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_effects) are used to imitate such features as internal organs and graphic wounds. It was later alleged that *Flower of Flesh and Blood* had been an inspiration for Japanese serial killer [Tsutomu Miyazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutomu_Miyazaki), who murdered several preschool girls in the late 1980s. However, Miyazaki apparently owned a copy of another film in the series and not of that specific one.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-vice-54]

In 1991, actor [Charlie Sheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Sheen) became convinced that *Flower of Flesh and Blood* depicted an actual homicide and contacted the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI). The FBI initiated an investigation but closed it after the series' producers released a "making of" film demonstrating the special effects used to simulate the murders.[^cite_note-55]

#### *Cannibal Holocaust*

Main article: [Cannibal Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannibal_Holocaust)

The Italian horror film *Cannibal Holocaust* (1980), which depicts various atrocities recorded and in some cases committed by a fictional documentary crew, caused director [Ruggero Deodato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruggero_Deodato) and the producers to be charged for obscenity in Italy.[^cite_note-56] During the film's release in France, a magazine suggested that some of the deaths depicted in the film were real.[^cite_note-57] While rumors later alleged that the director was at some point formally charged with murder, there is no evidence that this was the case.[^cite_note-58] Deodato said that he was actually suspected by the [Italian judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Italy), but was able to clear himself by asking cast members to appear in court.[^cite_note-In_the_Jungle-59]

Other than graphic gore, the film contains several scenes of sexual violence and the genuine deaths of six animals onscreen and one off screen, issues which find *Cannibal Holocaust* in the midst of controversy to this day. It has also been claimed that *Cannibal Holocaust* is banned in over 50 countries,[^cite_note-60] although this has never been verified. In 2006, *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* magazine named *Cannibal Holocaust* as the 20th most controversial film of all time.[^cite_note-61]

#### *August Underground* trilogy

Main articles: [August Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Underground), [August Underground's Mordum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Underground's_Mordum), and [August Underground's Penance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Underground's_Penance)

This trilogy of horror films (2001, 2003 and 2007), which depict graphic tortures and murders, is shot as if it were amateur footage made by a serial killer and his accomplices. In 2005, director and lead actor Fred Vogel, who was traveling with copies of the first two films to attend a horror film festival in Canada, was arrested by Canadian customs pending charges of transporting obscene materials into the country. The charges were eventually dropped after Vogel had spent ten hours in custody.[^cite_note-bzfilminterview-62]
