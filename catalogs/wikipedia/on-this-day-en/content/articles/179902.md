**Thrissur** ([Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam_language): തൃശൂർ, pronounced [\[triˈʃ(ː)uːr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Malayalam) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bb/Thrissur.ogg/Thrissur.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bb/Thrissur.ogg/Thrissur.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), [formerly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaming_of_cities_in_India) **Trichur**, also known by its historical name **Thrissivaperur**, is a city and the headquarters of the [Thrissur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_district) in [Kerala](4349459.md),[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] India. It is the [third largest urban agglomeration in Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_populous_urban_agglomerations_in_Kerala) after [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi) and [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode), and the [21st largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_million-plus_urban_agglomerations_in_India) in India.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Thrissur is classified as a [Tier-2 city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_Indian_cities) by the [Government of India](553883.md). The city is built around a 65-acre (26 ha) hillock called [Thekkinkadu Maidanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thekkinkadu_Maidan) which seats the [Vadakkumnathan temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_Temple). It is located 284 kilometres (176 mi) north-west of the state's capital city, [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram). Thrissur was once the capital of the [Kingdom of Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cochin),[^cite_note-11] and was a point of contact for the [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), [Greeks](42056.md), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_people), [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) and [English](18803164.md).

Thrissur is known as City of Celebrations and also called the cultural capital of [Kerala](4349459.md) because of its cultural, spiritual and religious leanings throughout history.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Thrissur-13] The city centre contains the [Kerala Sangeetha Nadaka Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Sangeetha_Nadaka_Academy), [Kerala Lalithakala Akademi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Lalita_Kala_Akademi) and [Kerala Sahitya Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Sahitya_Academy).[^cite_note-14] The city hosts the [Thrissur Pooram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Pooram), one of the largest temple festival in the state.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The festival is held at the Thekkinkadu Maidan of [Vadakumnathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_Temple) temple in April or May in the Malayalam month 'medam'.[^cite_note-Thrissur-13]

The city has historically been a centre of Hindu scholarship, and [Christianity](5211.md), [Islam](6037917.md) and [Judaism](15624.md) are believed to have entered the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) through Thrissur and its surrounding areas. Thrissur has a large number of well-known temples including the [Vadakkumnathan temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_Temple), [Thiruvambadi Sri Krishna Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvambadi_Sri_Krishna_Temple), and [Paramekkavu Bagavathi temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramekkavu_Bagavathi_Temple). There are three major Catholic churches, the [St. Antony's Syro-Malabar Catholic Forane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Antony's_Syro-Malabar_Church,_Ollur), [Our Lady of Lourdes Syro-Malabar Catholic Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes_Metropolitan_Cathedral) and [Our Lady of Dolours Syro-Malabar Catholic Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Dolours,_Thrissur), the largest Christian church in India.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Thrissur is home to the [Academy of Sharia and Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sharia_and_Advanced_Studies) an Islamic institute that is unusual in that it teaches [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) and aspart of the Sanskrit syllabus it includes study of several key Hindu texts.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The city is the headquarters of four major [scheduled banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Banks_\(India\)), [South Indian Bank Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Indian_Bank_Ltd), [CSB Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSB_Bank), [Dhanalakshmi Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanalakshmi_Bank) and [ESAF Small Finance Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESAF_Small_Finance_Bank)[^cite_note-25] as well as several [chit funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chit_fund).[^cite_note-The_Indian_Express-26] The city is also known for its silks and gold jewellery industry.

## Etymology

The name Thrissur ([Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam): തൃശ്ശൂർ) is a shortened form of the Malayalam word *Thirusshivaperoor* (meaning: City of the Sacred [Lord Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Shiva)).[^cite_note-31] The name owes itself to the most prominent feature of the city, which is the [Vadakkumnathan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkumnathan_Temple), which has Shiva as its presiding deity.[^cite_note-32] Thrissur was officially known by its anglicized name Trichur until 1990 when the government decided to replace it with its real Malayalam name.[^cite_note-33] Thrissur was also known as "Vrishabhadripuram" (Kailasam of the South) in ancient days.[^cite_note-34]

## History

Main article: [History of Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Thrissur)

The presence of numerous prehistoric [megaliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith) and [dolmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmen) indicates that Thrissur has been inhabited from at least 1000 BCE to 500 CE.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-The_Hindu-36]



&#x20;Image of the main entrance of [Vadakkunnathan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_Temple) seen from [Swaraj Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_Round) from Illustrated Guide to the South Indian Railway

The [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) had naval influence in many parts of [Kerala](4349459.md) in the 16th century, including Thrissur. At the beginning of the 17th century, the Portuguese naval power was reduced and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) became the main naval power. With the help of the Dutch, the royal family of the [Kingdom of Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cochin) recaptured Thrissur from the [Zamorin of Calicut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamorin_of_Calicut) in 1710.[^cite_note-Brief_History-37]

Thrissur rose to importance after Maharaja [Sakthan Thampuran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakthan_Thampuran) ascended the throne of the Kingdom of Cochin (1769–1805) and made Thrissur his capital. The Maharaja made the city into a major financial and commercial hub of [South India](678583.md), by inviting [Syrian Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians) families and [Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmins) from adjoining areas.[^cite_note-Brief_History-37]

&#x20;During 1750–60 [Hyder Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyder_Ali), the Sultan of the powerful [Kingdom of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore), conquered Thrissur, making it a tributary of Mysore. In 1786, the son of [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan) of Mysore led another invasion of Thrissur but retreated after the Srirangapattanam war.[^cite_note-38] In the meantime, [Rama Varma X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_Varma_X), the successor of Sakthan Thampuran signed a treaty with the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company), transforming the state of [Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochin) into a British Protectorate.



The first known map of Thrissur City with [Vadakkunnathan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_Temple) prepared by [John Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gould) in 1816

The [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) gained momentum after a Committee was formed at the 1919 meeting of the [Indian National Congress](149333.md). The civil disobedience movement attracted many people in Thrissur in subsequent years, and [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) visited the city in 1927 and 1934 to promote it.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

[R. K. Shanmukham Chetty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._K._Shanmukham_Chetty), the controversial [Diwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divan) of Cochin Kingdom from 1935 to 1941, developed the city by constructing [Thrissur Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Town_Hall) and [Ramanilayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanilayam), buildings which remain important in Kerala politics. Other important civic buildings and infrastructure constructed around this time include the [Municipal Corporation Building of Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Corporation_Building,_Thrissur) and the [Swaraj Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_Round).[^cite_note-A_Krishnan_2009_p.35-38-41][^cite_note-42]

In 1947, when India gained [independence from colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Independence_Act_1947), Thrissur was part of the Kingdom of Cochin. [Thrissur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_district) was formed on 1 July 1949, with the headquarters at the city of Thrissur.

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Thrissur)



The [Thrissur-Ponnani Kole Wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Kole_Wetlands) is one of largest, highly productive and threatened wetlands in Kerala. It acts as natural drainage for City of Thrissur.

Thrissur is the headquarters of [Thrissur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_district), in the center of the Indian state of Kerala. The city is 75 km north-east of [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi,_India), 133 km south-west of [Coimbatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbatore), 124 km south-east of [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode) and 151 km north of [Changanacherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changanacherry).[^cite_note-\:0-43] The city is located in a hillock called [Thekkinkadu Maidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thekkinkadu_Maidan) which is the second highest point in the city after the [Vilangan Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilangan_Hills). The city has an average altitude of 2.83 meters above sea level.[^cite_note-\:0-43]

From the hillock, the city gradually flattens into the [Thrissur-Ponnani Kole Wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur-Ponnani_Kole_Wetlands), which act as natural drainage for the city. The water from the wetlands flows via rivers into the [Laccadive Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laccadive_Sea), keeping Thrissur city safe from the major flooding that affects most other cities in Kerala.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

The city is located in the midland region of Kerala, with an extended part of the Palakkad plains.[^cite_note-Jose-48] The city geologically is composed of Archaean gneisses and crystalline schists. Major parts of the city are covered by [Archaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archean) rocks.[^cite_note-49] The city lies near the center of the Indian tectonic plate (the Indian Plate) and is subject to comparatively little [seismic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic) or [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic) activity.



Ponds and canals in [Vadakkechira, Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkechira,_Thrissur) prevent flooding during monsoon season.

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), the City of Thrissur features a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) (*Am*). Summer lasts from March to May which is the hottest time of the year. Summer months are uncomfortable due to higher levels of heat and humidity. Daytime temperatures can rise up to 36 to 38 °C (96.8 to 100.4 °F) coupled with excessive humidity. Summer is followed by the [southwest monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-west_monsoon) from June to September. October and November form the post-monsoon or retreating monsoon season. Winter from December through February is cooler, and windy, due to winds from the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats). Winter months are generally dry and less humid compared to other months of the year. Morning temperatures are usually cool and daytime temperatures hover around 30 °C or 86 °F.[^cite_note-Climate-50]

The city is drenched in the monsoonal season by heavy showers. The average annual rainfall is approximately 3,100 millimetres or 122 inches. The South-west monsoon generally sets in during the last week of May. After July the rainfall decreases. On average, there are 124 rainy days in a year. The maximum average temperature of the city in the summer season is 36 °C or 96.8 °F while the minimum temperature recorded is 27 °C or 80.6 °F. The winter season records a maximum average of 31 °C or 87.8 °F and a minimum average of 20 °C or 68 °F.[^cite_note-Climate-50]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Thrissur)

Thrissur is home to many Malayali entrepreneurs[^cite_note-The_Economic_Times-52] and is a major financial and commercial hub of Kerala.[^cite_note-South_Indian_Bank_sees_sunshine_in_slowdown-53] Historians say that King Sakthan Thampuran invited [Syrian Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians) families and [Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) to settle in Thrissur city from their business centers in adjoining areas. Soon, Thrissur became a flourishing centre of internal trade in Kerala. Thrissur is one of the major manufacturing centers of plain gold and [rolled gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolled_gold) jewellery in South India; up to 70% of Kerala's jewellery is manufactured in Thrissur. There are around 3,000 gold ornaments manufacturing units in the city and 40,000-odd artisans and others work in these units.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The industry provides direct and indirect employment to 200,000 people in Thrissur.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] The artisans based in these units craft nearly 85 percent of one tonne gold which is used per day in Kerala. About 90 tonnes of gold was being used annually in Kerala for manufacturing of ornaments daily.



The Town Hall in Thrissur

According to the [Reserve Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Bank_of_India), Thrissur has been regarded as a banking town since the 1930s, when 58 banks were headquartered in the city. In the present, the city remains a significant center for banking and finance, with the headquarters of major banks like [South Indian Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Indian_Bank), [CSB Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSB_Bank), [Dhanlaxmi Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanlaxmi_Bank), and other financial institutions like [Manappuram Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manappuram_Finance), [Kerala State Financial Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_State_Financial_Enterprises) and [ESAF Small Finance Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESAF_Small_Finance_Bank).[^cite_note-The_Hindu_3-58][^cite_note-About_Us-59][^cite_note-60] Many [chit funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chit_fund), a type of Indian savings and credit system, are located in Thrissur. In 2010, an estimated 3,000 chit fund companies were located in Thrissur, employing approximately 35,000 people.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]



[South Indian Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Indian_Bank) headquarters in Thrissur City

The city is a hub for the [Ayurvedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda) drug-manufacturing industry. Out of the 850 ayurvedic drug-manufacturing companies in Kerala, about 150, including some of the largest in the state like [Oushadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oushadhi), [Vaidyaratnam Oushadhasala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaidyaratnam_Oushadhasala), [KP Namboodiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KP_Namboodiris), are located in and around the city.[^cite_note-63] [Thrissur Ayurveda Cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Ayurveda_Cluster), another initiative by a group of Ayurvedic manufacturers of Thrissur, has developed a cluster in KINFRA Park in [Koratty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koratty) in Thrissur District.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

Retail businesses in Thrissur include [Kalyan Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyan_Group), [Jos Alukkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos_Alukkas), [Joy Alukkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Alukkas) and Josco Group.[^cite_note-68] [InfoPark Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infopark,_Thrissur), the fourth technology park in Kerala, is situated in Thrissur District.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Tourism has also contributed heavily to the economy of Thrissur. ### Municipal governance

Thrissur has functioned as a municipality since 1921 under the Cochin Municipal Regulations.[^cite_note-Malayala_Manorama-73][^cite_note-74] In 1932, the new corporation building was constructed, and in 1972, several village councils or [panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj) were added to the municipality.[^cite_note-Malayala_Manorama-73] On 1 October 2000, the municipal town was upgraded to a [municipal corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation_\(India\)) and took over the towns and villages of [Ayyanthole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyanthole), Koorkkenchery, [Nadathara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadathara), Vilvattom (part), [Ollur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollur) and [Ollukkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollukkara).

The city is administered by the [Thrissur Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Municipal_Corporation), headed by a mayor and comprising three legislative assemblies Thrissur, Ollur and [Cherpu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherpu).[^cite_note-75] For administrative purposes, the city is divided into 56 wards, from which the members of the corporation council are elected for five years.[^cite_note-76]

It is the second-largest city corporation in the state of Kerala in India. The corporation, headquartered in the city of Thrissur proper, directly controls power, water supply and solid waste management system in the city.[^cite_note-Malayala_Manorama-73][^cite_note-77] The [Thrissur Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Municipal_Corporation) and Town and Country Planning Department (TCPD) are the agencies that prepare development plan for the city.[^cite_note-adb.org-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

State agencies provide several essential public services in the city. The [Kerala Water Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Water_Authority) provides water supply; the [Kerala Fire and Rescue Services Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Fire_and_Rescue_Services) provides fire protection and emergency response; the [Kerala Police](52132287.md) is responsible for law enforcement and public safety; and the [Kerala State Electricity Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_State_Electricity_Board) provides electricity distribution. Public transport is operated mainly by the [Kerala State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) (KSRTC), along with private bus operators.

### Police services

The [Thrissur City Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_City_Police) is responsible for policing and law enforcement in the city. It operates under the [Kerala Police](52132287.md) and is headed by a [Commissioner of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner_of_Police_\(India\)) (CP). The Thrissur City Police has jurisdiction over an area of 570.79 km², covering Thrissur city and the municipalities of [Guruvayoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guruvayur), [Chelakkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelakkara), [Wadakkanchery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadakkancherry,_Thrissur), [Kunnamkulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunnamkulam) and [Chavakkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavakkad).[^cite_note-81]

The city is divided into four sub divisions, Thrissur, [Kunnamkulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunnamkulam), [Ollur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollur) and [Guruvayur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guruvayur). It also operates 24 police stations, including a woman police station, a cyber crime station and a traffic police station.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]The city is the headquarters of Thrissur City Police and Thrissur Rural Police.&#x20;

The Thrissur City Police Commissionerate operates out of the erst-while District Armed Police headquarters at [Ramavarmapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramavarmapuram). The Thrissur Rural Police has its headquarters at the District Collectorate complex at Ayyanthole. The city is also the headquarters of the [Deputy Inspector General of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Inspector_General_of_Police), Thrissur Range, which looks after the law and order of [Thrissur District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_district), [Palakkad District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad_District) and [Malappuram District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malappuram_district). All the [Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Police) of these three districts come under his jurisdiction. The city also contains the Kerala Police Academy,[^cite_note-85] [Central Prison, Viyyur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Prison,_Viyyur),[^cite_note-86] [Police Dog Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Dog_Training_Centre) and [Excise Academy and Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excise_Academy_and_Research_Centre). [Indian Reserve Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_and_Union_Territory_Police_Forces#Indian_Reserve_Battalion), the new commando unit of [Kerala Police](52132287.md) is headquartered in [Ramavarmapuram.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramavarmapuram)[^cite_note-87] [Border Security Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Security_Force) (148 battalion) have its first center in Kerala in Thrissur only.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

### Festivals



[Kerala Sahitya Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Sahitya_Academy) building in Thrissur

Thrissur is known as the Cultural Capital of Kerala, the city enjoys a thriving cultural tradition dating back to centuries. [Thrissur Pooram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Pooram), the largest [pooram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pooram) in Kerala, is sometimes referred to as 'the pooram of all poorams'. It is celebrated every year in the month of [Medam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medam) (mid-April to mid-may) as per the [Malayalam calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam_calendar). For thirty-six hours, the city plays host to a large gathering of people and elephants.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

[Puli Kali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puli_Kali), also known as *Kavakali*, is another festival, which attracts thousands of people to the city. It is performed by trained artists to entertain people on the occasion of [Onam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onam), an annual harvest festival, celebrated mainly in Kerala.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] [Buon Natale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buon_Natale) is the cultural festival conducted at [Swaraj Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_Round) with [Christmas](6237.md) celebration. [Buon Natale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buon_Natale) procession entered the [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) in 2014 for having the most number of people dressed up as [Santa Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Claus). Other important festivals celebrated in the city include [Christmas](6237.md), [Onam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onam), [Diwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali), [Easter](9325.md), [Eid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Fitr) and [Vishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishu).

Elephants play a major part in many of the city's festivals.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] *Aanayoottu* (feeding of elephants), held in [Vadakkunnathan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_Temple) in the City annually, is the world's largest elephant feeding ceremony. The ceremony is conducted on the first day of the Malayalam month of Karkidakam.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

In 1952 when Current Books set its first shop in Thrissur by former education Minister Professor [Joseph Mundassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mundassery), it become the abode of writer's like [O. V. Vijayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._V._Vijayan), [Kovilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovilan), [V. K. N.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._K._N.), [Uroob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uroob), [Edasseri Govindan Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edasseri_Govindan_Nair), [M. T. Sankara Pillai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._G._Sankara_Pillai) and [Sarah Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Joseph_\(author\)). The area was later known as *Current Moola* ("Current Corner").[^cite_note-108] The building that housed the Current Books bookshop was demolished in 2011.[^cite_note-109]

Thrissur is home to prominent [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam_literature) literary figures like [Kovilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovilan), [Kunhunni Mash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunhunni_Mash), [Sukumar Azhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukumar_Azhikode), [K. ### Religious buildings



One of the four [Vadakumnathan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakumnathan_Temple) Gates, inside the [Swaraj Maidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_Maidan)

The [Vadakkunnathan temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakkunnathan_temple), believed to have been founded by the legendary saint [Parasurama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasurama), is an example of the Kerala style of architecture and houses several sacred shrines and murals delineating graphically, various episodes from the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata). [Thiruvambadi Sri Krishna Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvambadi_Sri_Krishna_Temple), one of the largest [Sree Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) temples in Kerala and [Paramekkavu Bagavathi Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramekkavu_Bagavathi_Temple) which is one of the largest [Bagavathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagavathi) temples in Kerala is also situated in the city. Aykunnu Pandavagiri Devi Temple is situated at Venginisseri village, that around 9 km from city center. [Sree Guruvayurappan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guruvayur_Temple) is located in [Guruvayur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guruvayur) (28 km from Thrissur); it is also referred to as [Bhuloka Vaikunta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaikuntha) which means "Holy Abode of Vishnu on Earth".

Asia's tallest church, the [Our Lady of Dolours Syro-Malabar Catholic Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Dolours_Syro-Malabar_Catholic_Basilica) (Puthan Pally), [Our Lady of Lourdes Syro-Malabar Catholic Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes_Syro-Malabar_Catholic_Metropolitan_Cathedral) which has an underground shrine, [Saint Antony's Syro-Malabar Church, Ollur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Antony's_Syro-Malabar_Church,_Ollur) which has been called as *Chinna Roma* (Small Rome) are masterpieces of architecture and indigenous paintings. [Saint Euphrasia's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrasia_Eluvathingal) tomb and museum also situated in the city. [St. Thomas Syro-Malabar Church, Palayur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thomas_Syro-Malabar_Church,_Palayur) (28 km from Thrissur) is the first church in India, and [Thomas the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle) performed the first baptism in India here.

The Chettiyangadi Hanafi Mosque in Thrissur City is one of the oldest mosque in Thrissur. [Cheraman Juma Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheraman_Juma_Mosque) in [Kodungallur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodungallur), (40 km from Thrissur) is the first mosque in India.

### Cuisine

The cuisine of Thrissur is linked to its history, geography, demography and culture. Rice is the staple food. *Achappam*, *Kuzhalappam*, *Vatteppam*, *Unniyappam* and *Pazham Pori* are common snacks. *Vellayappam*, a kind of [rice hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopper_\(food\)) is another dish which is special to the city, they have a food street named *vellayappam angadi*.

## Education



Government Engineering College, Thrissur

Thrissur, which has been a centre of learning from ancient times, is developing as a modern education hub.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Schools in city are either run publicly by the Kerala Government or privately, some with financial aid from the Government. [Church Mission Society School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Mission_Society_Higher_Secondary_School,_Thrissur) started in 1845 is the oldest school and [St. Thomas College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thomas_College,_Thrissur) is the first college in Thrissur. On 2 September 2022, Thrissur joined the [UNESCO](21786641.md) Global Network of Learning Cities (GNLC).[^cite_note-UNESCO-GNLC-date-119]

Football is the most popular sport in the city, and there are two football stadiums in the city, [Thrissur Municipal Corporation Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Municipal_Corporation_Stadium) and Thope Stadium.The largest and highest temporary stadium in the world as per [Guinness Book of World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) was built in the Thrissur Corporation Ground in 1990 for the occasion of [Federation Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_Cup_\(India\)) football tournament, which was designed by Er. TJ Antony. International ballers and former Indian captains C. V. Pappachan, [I. M. Vijayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._M._Vijayan) and [Jo Paul Ancheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Paul_Ancheri), and the Under 17 World Cup player [K. P. Rahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahul_K._P.) belongs to Thrissur. The [N.I. David Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N.I._David_Memorial_Trophy), an annual inter-club football tournament is held in Thrissur every year. The football championship was started in 1996 by the Superintendent of Police, Thrissur.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] The city has a floodlit stadium, known as [Thrissur Municipal Corporation Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Municipal_Corporation_Stadium).[^cite_note-Sports-125] It also has two indoor stadiums, [V.K.N. Menon Indoor Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V.K.N._Menon_Indoor_Stadium) and a [SAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Authority_of_India) maintained [Thrissur Aquatic Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Aquatic_Complex) with international facilities. Thrissur has contributed many national and international bodybuilding stars to [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-Sports-125] Chess prodigy [Nihal Sarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihal_Sarin) is from Thrissur.

## Media

The first [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam) newspaper which published from Thrissur was *Lokamanyan* in 1920. Then came *[Deenabandhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deenabandhu)* edited by [V. R. Krishnan Ezhuthachan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._R._Krishnan_Ezhuthachan). *Ezhuthachan* started publishing as a weekly in 1941 from Thrissur. It was one of the first periodicals that supported the national movement. As soon as the [Quit India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quit_India) movement was started, its editor and staff were sent to jail and publications were banned.[^cite_note-Vidyanidhi.org-126] Later *Lokamanyan* (1920); *Kerala Chintamani* (1905); *Kerala Kesari* (1924); *Mahatma* (1930); *Gomathy* (1930) and *Navajeevan* of [Joseph Mundassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mundassery) was also published from Thrissur.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] *The Express* started in 1944 from Thrissur with K Krishanan as the editor is known in Central Kerala for its nationalist and socialist views.[^cite_note-Vidyanidhi.org-126] Major Malayalam newspapers published in Thrissur include *[Malayala Manorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayala_Manorama)*, *[Mathrubhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathrubhumi)*, *[Madhyamam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhyamam)*, *[Deepika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepika_\(newspaper\))*, *[Kerala Kaumudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Kaumudi)*, *[Deshabhimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deshabhimani)*, *[Mangalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangalam_Publications)*, *Veekshanam*, *Metro Vaartha* and *[Janayugom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janayugom)*. A number of evening papers, like *[General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_\(Newspaper\))* in Malayalam and *[City Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Journal_\(Thrissur\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)* in English, are also published from the city. Newspapers in other regional languages like [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) and [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language) are also available in city.

The first cinema hall in Kerala, with a manually operated film projector, was opened in Thrissur by [Jose Kattookkaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Kattookkaran) in 1907. In 1913, the first electrically operated film projector was established in city again by [Jose Kattookkaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Kattookkaran) and was called the *Jose Electrical Bioscope* now known as [Jos Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos_Theatre).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

A film festival, known as the [ViBGYOR Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ViBGYOR_Film_Festival), used to be held in the city every year until 2019. It was an international short and documentary film festival. Telecom services are provided by various players like [Airtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharti_Airtel), [Vodafone Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Idea), [Jio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jio), and the state-owned [BSNL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSNL).[^cite_note-133]

Private FM radio stations in the Thrissur are [Club FM 104.8 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94.3_Club_FM)MHz, [Radio Mango 91.9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Mango_91.9)  MHz,[^cite_note-Radio_Mango_Thrissur-134] [Red FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_FM_\(India\)) 91.1 MHz.[^cite_note-RED_FM_Thrissur-135] [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) has an AM (630 kHz) and an FM (101.1 MHz) station for the city.[^cite_note-136] The transmitter of the [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) (630 kHz) was commissioned on 4 November 1956. The station started independent broadcasting in 1974.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] Thrissur has a [Doordarshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doordarshan) studio with a low power transmitter located near the studio.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

### Road

The city is connected to the [North-South Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_and_East%E2%80%93West_Corridor) [National Highway (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_\(India\)) via the four-lane [National Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_47_\(India\)) 544, previously NH 47.[^cite_note-142] The highway traverses through the entire length and breadth of the city from different points and provides access to the nearby cities such of [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi), [Palakkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad) and [Coimbatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbatore). [NH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_47_\(India\)) 544 provides two main exit points at [Mannuthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannuthy) and [Thalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalore) which is bypass to the Thrissur city.

The city is largely dependent on private buses, taxis and [auto rickshaws](593765.md) for public transport.

State-owned [Kerala State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) (KSRTC) runs inter-state, inter-district and city services. Thrissur has three bus stations, the [Shaktan Thampuran Private Bus Stand, Thrissur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaktan_Thampuran_Private_Bus_Stand) in [Sakthan Thampuran Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakthan_Thampuran_Nagar), Vadakke Stand (Northern Bus Stand) and the [Thrissur KSRTC Bus Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_KSRTC_bus_station) near the [Thrissur railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_railway_station). [State Highway (SH 69)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Highway_69_\(Kerala\)) Thrissur-[Kuttippuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuttippuram) Road, [SH 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Highway_22_\(Kerala\)) [Kodungallur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodungallur) – [Shornur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shornur) Road, [SH 75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Highway_75_\(Kerala\)) Thrissur – Kanjani – [Vadanappally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadanappally) Road are the three state highways which connect city with its suburbs and municipalities.

### Railway



Platform of [Thrissur Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Railway_Station)

The [Southern Railway zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Railway_zone) of the [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways) operates the main rail transport system in Thrissur. There are four railway stations in Thrissur city. [Thrissur railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_railway_station), one of the five NSG-2 category railway stations in [Trivandrum Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivandrum_Division) of [Southern Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Railway_zone), provides trains to three directions and lies on the busy [Shoranur–Cochin Harbour section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoranur%E2%80%93Cochin_Harbour_section). It has a satellite station, [Punkunnam railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punkunnam_railway_station)[^cite_note-143] and two minor stations, [Ollur railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollur_railway_station) and [Mulankunnathukavu railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulankunnathukavu_railway_station). Thrissur railway station also connects to the temple town of [Guruvayur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guruvayur) by [Guruvayur–Thrissur spur line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guruvayur%E2%80%93Thrissur_spur_line). In addition, Southern Railway is running a suburban railway system connecting Thrissur to [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi) and [Palakkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad) using Mainline Electrical Multiple Unit services ([MEMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEMU)).[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]
