**Muzaffarabad**[^cite_note-7] is the capital and largest city of [Azad Kashmir](2745.md), a [Pakistani](23235.md)-administered [administrative territory](446017.md) in the disputed [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) region.[^cite_note-tertiary-kashmir-1]

The city is located in [Muzaffarabad District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarabad_District), near the confluence of the [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River) and [Neelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_River) rivers. The district is bounded by the Pakistani province of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) in the west, the [Kupwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupwara_district) and [Baramulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baramulla_district) districts of [Indian](14533.md)-administered [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) in the east, and the [Neelum District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neelum_District) in the north.

## History

Muzaffarabad was founded in 1646 by [Sultan Muzaffar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Muzaffar_Khan), chief of the [Bomba tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_\(tribe\))[^cite_note-8] who ruled [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir).[^cite_note-9] Khan also constructed the [Red Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Fort,_Muzaffarabad) that same year for the purpose of warding off incursions from the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire).

### Sikh Empire

In 1827, [Raja Zabardast Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Zabardast_Khan?action=edit\&redlink=1), who had succeeded his father Hassan Ali Khan as the [Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja) of Muzaffarabad, led a [guerrilla campaign](12720.md) against the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire), targeting their garrisons in [Handwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handwara), [Baramulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baramulla), and the [Hazara region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_region). His leadership and strategic strikes disrupted Sikh control in the area.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Gathering a sizable force, Zabardast Khan declared independence and planned an invasion of the [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley). In response, [Diwan Kirpa Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwan_Kirpa_Ram) led a large Khalsa army to suppress the rebellion. Between [Baramulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baramulla) and Muzaffarabad, across a span of nearly 77 miles, Zabardast Khan's forces—supported by the local Muslim population—launched persistent attacks on the advancing Sikh army, employing guerrilla tactics from caves, rocks, and forests. The Sikh forces suffered heavy casualties, and Diwan Kirpa Ram's army faced serious defeats and significant losses.[^cite_note-12]

### Dogra era

On 28 May 1849, [James Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Abbott_\(Indian_Army_officer\)), at the time boundary commissioner, wrote that "intelligence received from Cashmere that a [Jumboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) Force of 4,000 men is about to march to Moozuffurabad, where there are already 3,000. This report may be a feint of the Maharaja to overcome the hill tribes, who, though quite peaceful at present, have been much opprest \[sic] and are ready enough to rise when opportunity offers."[^cite_note-Abbott-13]

Abbott also wrote that it is "highly desirable therefore that this report, which has greatly alarmed them and may drive them to desperation, be contradicted; and I have accordingly addrest the Maharaja disclaiming belief in such a rumour, and assuring him that any movement of troops in this direction at this moment will not have a friendly aspect. The assembly of any force upon the frontier were an encouragement to the insurgents in [Mooltan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Multan_\(1848%E2%80%931849\)) and to others who are disposed to join them."[^cite_note-Abbott-13]

The following day on 29 May Abbott wrote:&#x20;

> I had not understood yesterday that another Jumboo force was said to be about to march from Cashmere upon Kurnao, a district between the Cashmere river and the Kishengunga. This Force is rated at 6,000. The mountaineers anxiously enquire of me whether they may not defend themselves from this invasion. I have declined putting any restraint upon their measures of self defence, assuring them, however, that any rising, excepting to resist invasion, would subject them to the displeasure of the British Government. If they can act in concert, they ought to be able to destroy this force, large as it is, their country being strong and the whole population bearing arms.
>
> If these military movements are really contemplated, they are highly objectionable at this season, and of a most suspicious character. The information is the best I can command, and agrees perfectly with the manifest apprehension of the Jumboo Moonshee in my camp. The Maharaja would plead his right to move his troops wheresoever he pleases within his own kingdom, but he is perfectly well aware of the sensation they will create in the Punjaub.[^cite_note-Abbott-13]

### Indo-Pakistani war of 1947

Main article: [Battle of Muzaffarabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Muzaffarabad)

The Battle of Muzaffarabad occurred on 22 October 1947 between Pakistani-backed Pashtun tribesmen, pro-Pakistani Kashmiri rebels, and the [Jammu and Kashmir State Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Rifles) in the town of Muzaffarabad. The battle resulted in a rapid defeat of the Jammu and Kashmir State Forces, leading to the capture of Muzaffarabad by the tribesmen.

### 2005 earthquake

Main article: [2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake)

The city was near the [epicenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicenter) of the [2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake), which had a [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) of 7.6 [M<sub>w</sub>](6207163.md#Mw). The earthquake destroyed about 50 percent of the buildings in the city (including most government buildings) and is estimated to have killed up to 80,000 people in the Pakistani-controlled areas. As of 8 October 2005, the [Pakistani government](1897833.md)'s official death toll was 87,350, while other estimates have put the death toll at over 100,000.[^cite_note-14]

Muzaffarabad's public transportation system primarily relies on buses, rickshaws, and small pickup trucks for intracity travel. Following the devastating [2005 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake) that severely damaged infrastructure, including roads, the city government, with [international aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_response_to_the_2005_Kashmir_earthquake), rebuilt roads, bridges, and other essential infrastructure. However, public transportation remains underdeveloped. Notably, Muzaffarabad lacks a railway system and a functional airport. The nearest railway station is located in the [Rawalpindi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District) of [Pakistani Punjab](24751.md).[^cite_note-18]

## Education

Muzaffarabad is the educational hub of [Azad Kashmir](2745.md), hosting several private and public institutions. The education sector in the city is administered by the [Government of Azad Jammu & Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Azad_Kashmir). The region has a literacy rate of approximately 77.5% which is significantly higher than the national average of Pakistan.[^cite_note-19] Following the [2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake), which destroyed a significant portion of the city's educational infrastructure, major reconstruction projects were launched to rebuild schools and university campuses. According to the Earthquake Rehabilitation and Reconstruction Authority (ERRA), 7,608 post-earthquake reconstruction projects were planned under three implementation channels: sponsor-funded, donor-funded, and [Government of Pakistan](1897833.md)–funded.[^cite_note-\:0-20] While sponsor- and donor-funded projects were completed, 1,730 Government of Pakistan–funded projects, including 1,112 in education, remained incomplete by 2010 due to funding shortfalls.[^cite_note-\:0-20]

### Higher Education

The primary institution of higher learning in the city is [the University of Azad Jammu & Kashmir (UAJK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Azad_Jammu_&_Kashmir). Established in 1980, it is a multi-campus public university, offering undergraduate and postgraduate programs across a range of disciplines.[^cite_note-\:1-21] The main campus is located in Muzaffarabad,[^cite_note-\:1-21] with additional facilities at the King Abdullah Campus in Chatter Class, which was constructed after the 2005 earthquake as part of the reconstruction of the region's higher education infrastructure.[^cite_note-22]

The King Abdullah Campus was inaugurated in 2023 following a grant of approximately US\$90 million from the [Saudi Fund for Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Fund_for_Development).[^cite_note-\:2-23] The campus includes around 15 academic departments, administrative blocks, a library, auditorium, and student accommodation facilities. Around 10,000 students are expected to benefit from the facility.[^cite_note-\:2-23]
