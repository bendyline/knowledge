The **IAI Heron** (**Machatz-1**) is a [medium-altitude long-endurance unmanned aerial vehicle](9309406.md) (MALE UAV) developed by the Malat (UAV) division of [Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries). It is capable of Medium Altitude Long Endurance (MALE) operations of up to 52 hours' duration at up to 10.5 km (35,000 ft). It has demonstrated 52 hours of continuous flight, but the effective operational maximal flight duration is less, according to payload and flight profile. An advanced version, the Heron TP, is also known as the [IAI Eitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Eitan).

On 11 September 2005, it was announced that the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) purchased US\$50 million worth of Heron systems.[^cite_note-1]

## Design and development

The Heron navigates using an internal [GPS navigation device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS_navigation_device), and either a pre-programmed flight profile (in which case the system is fully autonomous from takeoff to landing), manual override from a [ground control station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAV_ground_control_station), or a combination of both. It can autonomously return to base and land in case of lost communication with the ground station. The system has fully automatic launch and recovery (ALR) and all-weather capabilities.

The Heron can carry an array of sensors, including [thermographic camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermographic_camera) (infrared) and visible-light [airborne ground surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_ground_surveillance), intelligence systems ([COMINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMINT) and [ELINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELINT)) and various [radar](25676.md) systems, totaling up to 250 kg (550 lb). The Heron is also capable of [target acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_acquisition) and artillery adjustment.

The payload sensors communicate with the ground control station in real time, using either direct [line of sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_propagation) [data link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_link), or via an airborne/satellite relay. Like the navigation system, the payload can also be used in either a fully pre-programmed autonomous mode, or manual real-time remote operation, or a combination of both.

### Super Heron

At the February 2014 [Singapore Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Air_Show), IAI unveiled the Super Heron refinement of the Heron UAS. The Super Heron has a 200-horsepower [diesel engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine)[^cite_note-2] that increases its rate of climb and performance. Its range is 250 km (160 mi) line-of-sight and 1,000 km (620 mi) by satellite control. Endurance is 45 hours at a maximum altitude of 30,000 ft (9,100 m). Cruising speed is 60 to 80 kn (110 to 150 km/h; 69 to 92 mph) and top speed over 150 kn (280 km/h; 170 mph).[^cite_note-3]

## Operational history

The Heron saw significant use during Operation [Cast Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast_Lead) in [Gaza](12047.md) of (2008–2009). During the deployment, each brigade combat team was assigned a UAV squadron for close support. This was the first Israeli operation in which UAVs, helicopters, and fighter jets were allocated to ground forces directly without IAF central command authorizing sorties. Air-support controller teams operated alongside brigade commanders at the front emphasizing the brigade commander's utilization of direct air assets.[^cite_note-DefenceNews_2009-03-08-4] A high degree of situational awareness was achieved by maintaining at least a dozen UAVs in flight over Gaza at all times. Aerial surveillance was provided by Heron and [Hermes 450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit_Hermes_450) UAVs and Apache attack helicopters. Along with coordination between the air force and ground troops, Israeli ground forces were able to utilize cooperation with the [Israel Security Agency](311752.md) by having operatives attached to the forward units. This inter-service coordination allowed for a higher level of tactical awareness and the ability to strike time-critical targets.[^cite_note-Aviation_Week_2009-05-11-5] During the Gaza war, a super Heron UAV was reported to have been shot down, making it the first time one has been lost in combat against Palestinian militants, with PIJ claiming responsibility for the downing.[^cite_note-6] Other countries operating the Heron include [Singapore](27318.md), [India](14533.md) and [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-7] [France](5843419.md) operates a derivative of Heron named **Eagle** or **Harfang**.[^cite_note-8] In 2008, Canada announced a plan to lease a Heron for use in Afghanistan, starting in 2009.[^cite_note-9] In mid-2009, Australia leased two Herons as part of a multimillion-dollar lease to operate the vehicles in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-10] In early July 2013, the Heron reached 15,000 flight hours over [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-11] Australia concluded its use of the Heron in support of [Operation Slipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Slipper) in Afghanistan on 30 November 2014, after it had accumulated 27,000 flight hours.[^cite_note-12] Royal Australian Air Force retired two Herons in June 2017.[^cite_note-RAAF_Heron_retire-13]

Beginning in 2021, [Malta](19137.md)-based Herons have been used by [Frontex](3072700.md), the European Border and Coast Guard Agency, to monitor migrant activity in the southern [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). This activity drew controversy when it was announced that the data gathered would be shared with countries including [Libya](17633.md) and [Tunisia](30188.md). The concern was that the data could be used to force irregular migrants back to their point of departure, denying them the opportunity to seek asylum in other countries.[^cite_note-14]

### India

The [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md) initially purchased 12 Heron drones along with [Searcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Searcher) drones from IAI in 2002. The purchase was as a result of the report from the Kargil Review Committee. However, the satisfactory performance of the drone during the multiple [search and rescue](172599.md) missions after [2004 tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_tsunami) led to planning of an additional order of 50 Heron UAVs. Though the contract negotiations were over by 2004 end, the contract signing was delayed to 2005 due to [Lok Sabha elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha_elections_2004). The contract was worth \$220 million.[^cite_note-ptiviarediff20051124-15][^cite_note-Bikhchandani-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The drone costs ₹80 crore (equivalent to ₹85 crore or US\$8.8 million in 2026) per unit.[^cite_note-timesofindia20141126-19][^cite_note-financialexpress20240425-20] As of 2009, the Indian Navy operated four Heron UAVs along with eight Searcher II drones. The UAVs formed the innermost layer, up to 200 nmi (370 km; 230 mi), of the three-tier maritime surveillance grid.[^cite_note-21] The Navy planned to acquire two additional Heron UAVs with three ground control stations and two ship control stations at a cost of [₹](490783.md)386 [crore](121420.md) (equivalent to ₹11 billion or US\$110 million in 2026).[^cite_note-TOI090105-22]

In case of the Indian Army, the drones were initially deployed by the [Regiment of Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment_of_Artillery_\(India\)) for [target acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_acquisition) roles to assist artillery units. However, after the [2020 China-India standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_China%E2%80%93India_skirmishes), the Heron units of the [Eastern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Command_\(India\)) were moved to [Army Aviation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Aviation_Corps_\(India\)) by August 2021 and the routine surveillance sorties per day were doubled. This move was done to allow the drones to take part in wide range of operations.[^cite_note-Philip-23] Post reorganisation, the Heron UAVs were placed under the Army Aviation Brigade at Missamari, [Assam](186162.md) which includes three squadrons. Each squadron has one Remotely Piloted Aircraft (RPA) flights equipped with Herons. Three flights included a total of 14 UAVs of the Eastern Command.[^cite_note-thehindu20211017-24]

#### Additional purchase

On 14 July 2020, reports indicated that the Indian Air Force intended to purchase additional units of Heron Mk1 UAVs to complement the existing fleet. The quantity was not revealed.[^cite_note-theprint20200714-25][^cite_note-economictimes20200714-26]

On 18 September 2025, the [Asian News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_News_International) reported that the Indian Armed Forces plan to acquire additional Heron drones following its success in [Operation Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sindoor). The Army is also expected to equip the drones with air-launched [Spike NLOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_\(missile\)#Spike_NLOS) anti-tank missiles.[^cite_note-27]

#### Project Cheetah

Project Cheetah was a programme of the Indian Armed Forces to upgrade and weaponise their existing fleet of around 90 Heron UAVs.[^cite_note-theprint20200714-25][^cite_note-economictimes20200714-26]

The project gain fast-track approval from the Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) on 14 July 2021. Around 90 drones were to be upgraded with [satellite communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_communication) (SATCOM), advanced reconnaissance equipment and ability to fire [laser-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb), air-launched [anti-tank guided missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_guided_missiles) and other [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition). The programme will cost around ₹3,500 crore (equivalent to ₹43 billion or US\$450 million in 2026). The cost negotiation was completed around 2021-22. After this, the proposal was sent to the [Cabinet Committee on Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security) for final approval.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

On 19 September 2022, it was reported that the upgrades would be carried out by manufacturing units of Indian firms with assistance from Israel. In the current variant, the equipment of the UAVs includes [thermographic camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermographic_camera), airborne ground surveillance, visible light, radar systems, etc. The project was reportedVs were expected to undergo upgrades in two phases. In the first phase, "in the last stage of decision-making".[^cite_note-30]

The UAVs were to be upgraded in two phases. In the first phase, the SATCOM and sensor suite was upgradation would be carried out. SATCOM capability would enhance the range to unlimited of the UAVs which is restricted to the within visual range of 200–250 km (120–160 mi). However, in 2023, it was reported that India has dropped plans to arm the Heron drones in the Armed Forces and the other upgrades would be carried out within India. The report stated that the airspace that India has to fight in is heavily contested and is not very suitable for drone warfare.[^cite_note-31]

#### Heron Mk2

It was reported in May 2021 that Indian Army was negotiating with IAI to lease 4 units of [Heron TP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron_TP) UAVs under the emergency procurement powers of the Armed Forces. The drones would be leased for three years and 2 of the drones would arrive by August. The advanced variants of the drone will have automatic taxi-takeoff and landing (ATOL) and [SATCOM](45207.md) systems.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] However, after contract signing, it was corrected that the drones would not be leased but purchased and the variant is Heron Mk2.[^cite_note-Bikhchandani-16][^cite_note-Philip-23] A total of 10 Heron Mk2 have been ordered including 4 units for Army and 6 units for the Air Force.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

Two of the UAVs were inducted by the Indian Army in November 2022 and deployed along the [Line of Actual Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Actual_Control) (LAC) at Leh, [Ladakh](303611.md).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-thehindu20231108-37] The rest of the 2 units were inducted in [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India) by September-end the same year.[^cite_note-thehindu20220915-38][^cite_note-39]

In August 2023, Indian Air Force inducted 4 Heron Mk2. The drone squadron, nicknamed the "Wardens of the North", was deployed in the northern sector under the commissioning Commanding Officer [Wing Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_commander) Pankaj Rana.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-timesofindia20230813-41] In November 2023, the Indian Air Force placed an order for two more Heron Mk2 UAVs in addition to the current four by using emergency procurement powers.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Further orders for Mk2 were placed by all the three services of the Indian Armed Forces, including the [Indian Navy](604522.md), following [Operation Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sindoor).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

#### Crashes

As of 2024, India has lost 12 Heron Mk1 UAVs since it was inducted into service in 2002. The crashes are attributed to several reasons like human errors, engine failures as well as loss of contact with Ground Control Stations (GCS).

- In November 2005, it was reported that after induction of Heron UAVs in 2002 at least four UAVs were lost within 2 years. The first instance of the crash occurred in 2003 on a forward airbase in [Rajasthan](26291.md). While one of the crashes was attributed to human error, the others were due to technical issues and included the death of an IAF handler. Two drones of [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) and [Air Force](403355.md) each were lost.

## Heron variants

- [Turkey](11125639.md) operates a special variant of the Heron, which utilizes Turkish-designed and manufactured electro-optical subsystems. For example, the Turkish Herons use the ASELFLIR-300T airborne thermal Imaging and targeting system designed and manufactured by [ASELSAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASELSAN) of Turkey. The Turkish Herons also have stronger engines in order to compensate for the added payload created by the heavier ASELFLIR-300T. This is the same [FLIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FLIR) system currently used in the [TAI/AgustaWestland T129](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI/AgustaWestland_T129) attack helicopter
