**Filipinos** ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Mga Pilipino*)[^cite_note-51] are citizens or people identified and related with the [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country) or [sovereign state](23604120.md) of the [Philippines](23440.md). Filipinos come from various [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), all typically speaking [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English), or other [Philippine languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_languages). Despite formerly being subject to [Spanish administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Philippines), less than 1% of Filipinos are fluent in [Spanish](26825.md).[^cite_note-52] Currently, there are more than 185 [ethnolinguistic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Philippines) in the Philippines each with its own [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Philippines), identity, culture, tradition, and history.&#x20;

## Names

See also: [Names of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_Philippines)

The name *Filipino*, as a [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym), was derived from the term *las Islas Filipinas* 'the Philippine Islands',[^cite_note-53] the name given to the archipelago in 1543 by the [Spanish](963152.md) explorer and [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Preachers) priest [Ruy López de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_L%C3%B3pez_de_Villalobos), in honor of [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain).[^cite_note-Scott1994-54] During the [Spanish period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521%E2%80%931898\)), natives of the Philippine islands were usually known in the Philippines itself by the generic terms ***indio*** ("Indian (native of the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies))") or *indigena* 'indigenous',[^cite_note-Cruz-55] while the generic term *chino* ("Chinese"),[^cite_note-auto5-56][^cite_note-57] short for "*indio chino"* was used in [Spanish America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_America) to differentiate from the [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) *indios* of the Spanish territories in the [Americas](29833.md) and the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies). The term *Filipino* was sometimes added by Spanish writers to distinguish the *indio chino* native of the Philippine archipelago from the *indio* of the Spanish territories in the Americas.[^cite_note-auto5-56] [^cite_note-58][^cite_note-Scott1994-54] The term *Indio Filipino* appears as a term of self-identification beginning in the 18th century.[^cite_note-Scott1994-54]&#x20;

In 1955, [Agnes Newton Keith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnes_Newton_Keith) wrote that a 19th century edict prohibited the use of the word "Filipino" to refer to indios. This reflected popular belief, although no such edict has been found.[^cite_note-Scott1994-54] The idea that the term *Filipino* was not used to refer to *indios* until the 19th century has also been mentioned by historians such as Salah Jubair[^cite_note-59] and [Renato Constantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Constantino).[^cite_note-Aguilar2005-60] However, in a 1994 publication the historian [William Henry Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Scott_\(historian\)) identified instances in Spanish writing where "Filipino" did refer to "indio" natives.[^cite_note-Larousse-61] Instances of such usage include the *Relación de las Islas Filipinas* (1604) of [Pedro Chirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Chirino), in which he wrote chapters entitled "Of the civilities, terms of courtesy, and good breeding among the Filipinos" (Chapter XVI), "Of the Letters of the Filipinos" (Chapter XVII), "Concerning the false heathen religion, idolatries, and superstitions of the Filipinos" (Chapter XXI), "Of marriages, dowries, and divorces among the Filipinos" (Chapter XXX),[^cite_note-62] while also using the term "Filipino" to refer unequivocally to the non-Spaniard natives of the archipelago like in the following sentence:&#x20;

> The first and last concern of the Filipinos in cases of sickness was, as we have stated, to offer some sacrifice to their *anitos* or *diwatas*, which were their gods.[^cite_note-63]

— [Pedro Chirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Chirino), Relación de las Islas Filipinas

In the *Crónicas* (1738) of Juan Francisco de San Antonio, the author devoted a chapter to "The Letters, languages and politeness of the Philippinos", while Francisco Antolín argued in 1789 that "the ancient wealth of the Philippinos is much like that which the Igorots have at present".[^cite_note-Scott1994-54] These examples prompted the historian [William Henry Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Scott_\(historian\)) to conclude that during the Spanish period:&#x20;

> \[...]the people of the Philippines were called Filipinos when they were practicing their own culture—or, to put it another way, before they became *indios*.[^cite_note-Scott1994-54]

— [William Henry Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Scott_\(historian\)), Barangay- Sixteenth Century Philippine Culture and Society

While the Philippine-born Spaniards during the 19th century began to be called *españoles Filipinos*, logically contracted to just *Filipino*, to distinguish them from the Spaniards born in Spain, they themselves resented the term, preferring to identify themselves as *"hijo/s del país"* ("sons of the country").[^cite_note-Scott1994-54]&#x20;

In the latter half of the 19th century, *[ilustrados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilustrado)*, an educated class of *[mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)* (both [Spanish mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos) and [Sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley) [Chinese mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino), especially Chinese mestizos) and *indios* arose whose writings are credited with building [Philippine nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_nationalism). These writings are also credited with transforming the term ***Filipino*** to one which refers to everyone born in the Philippines,[^cite_note-Kramer-64][^cite_note-65] especially during the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) and [American Colonial Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)) and the term shifting from a geographic designation to a national one as a [citizenship nationality by law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law).[^cite_note-Kramer-64][^cite_note-Aguilar2005-60] Historian [Ambeth Ocampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambeth_Ocampo) has suggested that the first documented use of the word *Filipino* to refer to Indios was the [Spanish-language](26825.md) poem *[A la juventud filipina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_la_juventud_filipina)*, published in 1879 by [José Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal).[^cite_note-Ocampo1995-66] Writer and publisher [Nick Joaquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Joaquin) has asserted that [Luis Rodríguez Varela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Varela) was the first to describe himself as *Filipino* in print.[^cite_note-67] Apolinario Mabini (1896) used the term *Filipino* to refer to all inhabitants of the Philippines. Father Jose Burgos earlier called all natives of the archipelago as *Filipinos*.[^cite_note-68] In [Wenceslao Retaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslao_Retana)'s *Diccionario de filipinismos*, he defined *Filipinos* as follows,[^cite_note-\:0-69]&#x20;

> *todos los nacidos en Filipinas sin distincion de origen ni de raza.*\
> &#x20;All those born in the Philippines without distinction of origin or race.

— [Wenceslao E. Retaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslao_Retana), Diccionario De Filipinismos: Con La Revisión De Lo Que Al Respecto Lleva Publicado La Real Academia Española

[American authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Government_of_the_Philippine_Islands) during the [American colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)) also started to colloquially use the term *Filipino* to refer to the native inhabitants of the archipelago,[^cite_note-Blair19152-70] but despite this, it became the official term for all [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship) of the sovereign independent [Republic of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Philippines), including non-native inhabitants of the country as per the [Philippine nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law).[^cite_note-Scott1994-54] However, the term has been rejected as an identification in some instances by minorities who did not come under Spanish control, such as the [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_people) and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines) [Moros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people).[^cite_note-Scott1994-54][^cite_note-Aguilar2005-60]&#x20;

The lack of the letter "*F*" in the 1940–1987 standardized [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) alphabet ([Abakada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abakada_alphabet)) caused the letter "*P*" to be substituted for "*F*", though the alphabets or writing scripts of some non-Tagalog ethnic groups included the letter "F". Upon official adoption of the modern, 28-letter [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) alphabet in 1987, the term *Filipino* was preferred over *Pilipino*. Locally, some still use "Filipino" to refer to the people and "Pilipino" to refer to the language, but in international use "Filipino" is the usual form for both.&#x20;

A number of Filipinos refer to themselves colloquially as "*[Pinoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinoy)*" (feminine: "*Pinay*"), which is a slang word formed by taking the last four letters of "*Filipino*" and adding the [diminutive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminutive) [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affix) "*-y*". Or the non-gender or gender fluid form Pinxy (seldom used in the country but used amongst [Filipino-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Americans) communities).&#x20;

In 2020, the neologism *Filipinx* appeared; a demonym applied only to those of Filipino heritage in the diaspora and specifically referring to and coined by [Filipino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Americans) imitating *[Latinx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinx)*, itself a recently coined gender-inclusive alternative to *Latino* or *Latina*. An online dictionary made an entry of the term, applying it to all Filipinos within the Philippines or in the diaspora.[^cite_note-71] In actual practice, however, the term is unknown among and not applied to Filipinos living in the Philippines, and *Filipino* itself is already treated as gender-neutral. The dictionary entry resulted in confusion, backlash and ridicule from Filipinos residing in the Philippines who never identified themselves with the foreign term.[^cite_note-esquiremag.ph-72][^cite_note-opinion.inquirer.net-73]&#x20;

Native Filipinos were also called **Manilamen** (or **Manila men**) by English-speaking regions or Tagalas by Spanish-speakers during the colonial era. They were mostly sailors and pearl-divers and established communities in various ports around the world.[^cite_note-Welch2014-74][^cite_note-Aguilar-75] One of the notable settlements of Manilamen is the community of [Saint Malo, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Malo,_Louisiana), founded at around 1763 to 1765 by escaped slaves and deserters from the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy).[^cite_note-Bishops2001-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] There were also significant numbers of Manilamen in [Northern Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Australia) and the [Torres Strait Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islands) in the late 1800s who were employed in the [pearl hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_hunting) industries.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-Ruiz-Wall-81]&#x20;

In [Mexico](3966054.md) (especially in the Mexican states of [Guerrero](195816.md) and [Colima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colima)), [Filipino immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_immigration_to_Mexico) arriving to [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) during the 16th and 17th centuries via the [Manila galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon) were called *chino*, which led to the confusion of early Filipino immigrants with that of the much later [Chinese immigrants to Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_immigration_to_Mexico) from the 1880s to the 1940s. A genetic study in 2018 has also revealed that around one-third of the population of Guerrero have 10% Filipino ancestry.[^cite_note-Wade-82][^cite_note-83]&#x20;

### Prehistory

Main article: [Prehistory of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_the_Philippines)

See also: [Negrito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito), [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), and [Models of migration to the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Models_of_migration_to_the_Philippines)







**Left to right**: \[**1**] Five teeth attributed to *[Homo luzonensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_luzonensis)*; \[**2**] CCH1, a third metatarsal bone (MT3) discovered on May 5, 2007, in [Callao Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_Cave), belonging to *[Homo luzonensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_luzonensis)*, a species of [archaic human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_human); \[**3**] [Callao Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_Cave) .

The oldest [archaic human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_humans) remains in the Philippines are the "[Callao Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_Man)" specimens discovered in 2007 in the [Callao Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_Cave) in [Northern Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Luzon). They were dated in 2010 through [uranium-series dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium-thorium_dating) to the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene), c. 67,000 years old. The remains were initially identified as modern human, but after the discovery of more specimens in 2019, they have been reclassified as being members of a new species – *[Homo luzonensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_luzonensis)*.[^cite_note-Détroit-84][^cite_note-85]&#x20;

The oldest indisputable modern human (*[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)*) remains in the [Philippines](23440.md) are the "[Tabon Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabon_Man)" fossils discovered in the [Tabon Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabon_Caves) in the 1960s by [Robert B. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bradford_Fox), an [anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologist) from the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Philippines). These were dated to the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic), at around 26,000 to 24,000 years ago. The Tabon Cave complex also indicates that the caves were inhabited by humans continuously from at least 47,000 ± 11,000 years ago to around 9,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Scott1984pp14-15-86][^cite_note-UNESCO-87] The caves were also later used as a burial site by unrelated [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and [Metal Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Ages) cultures in the area.[^cite_note-Détroit2-88]&#x20;



Migration of the sea-faring [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) and their [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages).



The Negritos are descendants of one of the earliest groups of modern humans to reach the [Philippines](23440.md)

The Tabon Cave remains (along with the [Niah Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niah_Cave) remains of [Borneo](4518.md) and the [Tam Pa Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tam_Pa_Ling) remains of [Laos](17752.md)) are part of the "First [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland) People", the earliest branch of [anatomically modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_humans) to reach [Island Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Southeast_Asia) at the time of lowered sea levels of [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland), with only one 3 km sea crossing.[^cite_note-Migrations_to_Philippines_over_50,000_yrs_(via_genome-sequence_data)-89] They entered the Philippines from Borneo via [Palawan](240395.md) at around 50,000 to 40,000 years ago. Their descendants are collectively known as the [Negrito people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito_people), although they are highly genetically divergent from each other. Philippine Negritos show a high degree of [Denisovan Admixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovan_Admixture), similar to [Papuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people_of_New_Guinea) and [Indigenous Australians](12598742.md), in contrast to Malaysian and Andamanese Negritos (the [Orang Asli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Asli)). This indicates that Philippine Negritos, Papuans, and Indigenous Australians share a common ancestor that admixed with [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovans) at around 44,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Jinam-90] Negritos include ethnic groups like the [Aeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeta_people) (including the Agta, Arta, Dumagat, etc.) of Luzon, the [Ati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ati_people) of [Western Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Visayas), the [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_people_\(Philippines\)) of [Palawan](240395.md), and the [Mamanwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamanwa_people) of [Mindanao](20546.md). Today they comprise just 0.03% of the total Philippine population.[^cite_note-State2794-91]&#x20;

After the Negritos, were two early Paleolithic migrations from East Asian (basal [Austric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austric), an ethnic group which includes [Austroasiatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_language_family)) people, they entered the Philippines at around 15,000 and 12,000 years ago, respectively. Like the Negritos, they entered the Philippines during the lowered sea levels during the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_ice_age), when the only water crossings required were less than 3 km wide (such as the [Sibutu strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibutu_Passage)).[^cite_note-Migrations_to_Philippines_over_50,000_yrs_(via_genome-sequence_data)-89] They retain partial genetic signals among the [Manobo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo_people) and the [Sama-Bajau people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau_people) of [Mindanao](20546.md).&#x20;

The last wave of prehistoric migrations to reach the Philippines was the [Austronesian expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_expansion) which started in the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) at around 4,500 to 3,500 years ago, when a branch of [Austronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesians) from [Taiwan](25734.md) (the ancestral [Malayo-Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian)-speakers) migrated to the [Batanes Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batanes_Islands) and [Luzon](69375.md). They spread quickly throughout the rest of the islands of the Philippines and became the dominant ethnolinguistic group. They admixed with the earlier settlers, resulting in the modern Filipinos – which though predominantly genetically Austronesian still show varying genetic admixture with Negritos (and vice versa for Negrito ethnic groups which show significant Austronesian admixture).[^cite_note-Lipson2014-92][^cite_note-Spriggs-93] Austronesians possessed advanced sailing technologies and colonized the Philippines via sea-borne migration, in contrast to earlier groups.[^cite_note-mijares2006-94][^cite_note-ANU-95]&#x20;



Austronesian trade networks

Austronesians from the Philippines also later settled [Guam](11974.md) and the other islands of [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia), and parts of [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia). From there, they colonized the rest of [Austronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesia), which in modern times include [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia), coastal [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea), [Island Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Melanesia), [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia), and [Madagascar](18964.md), in addition to Maritime Southeast Asia and Taiwan.[^cite_note-ANU-95][^cite_note-Melton-96]&#x20;

The connections between the various [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) have also been known since the [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_colonization_of_the_Philippines) due to shared [material culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_culture) and linguistic similarities of various peoples of the islands of the [Indo-Pacific](330456.md), leading to the designation of Austronesians as the "[Malay race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_race)" (or the "[Brown race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_race)") during the age of [scientific racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism) by [Johann Friedrich Blumenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_Blumenbach).[^cite_note-Blust2013-97][^cite_note-Bhopal-98][^cite_note-Ross-99] Due to the [colonial American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_colonial_period_of_the_Philippines) education system in the early 20th century, the term "Malay race" is still used incorrectly in the Philippines to refer to the Austronesian peoples, leading to [confusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayness) with the non-indigenous [Melayu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_people).[^cite_note-Acabado-100][^cite_note-lasco-101][^cite_note-Palatino-102][^cite_note-Curaming-103]&#x20;

### Archaic epoch (to 1565)

Since at least the 3rd century, various ethnic groups established several communities. These were formed by the assimilation of various native Philippine kingdoms.[^cite_note-State2794-91] [South Asian](21566765.md) and [East Asian](19605700.md) people together with the people of the Indonesian archipelago and the Malay Peninsula, traded with Filipinos and introduced [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Buddhism](3267529.md) to the native tribes of the Philippines. Most of these people stayed in the Philippines where they were slowly absorbed into local societies.&#x20;

Many of the *[barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay)* (tribal municipalities) were, to a varying extent, under the *de jure* jurisprudence of one of several neighboring empires, among them the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya), [Javanese](69336.md) [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Empire), [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) [Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola), [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa) and [Khmer empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire), although *de facto* had established their own independent system of rule. Trading links with [Sumatra](28679.md), [Borneo](4518.md), [Java](69336.md), [Cambodia](334751.md), [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula), [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina), [China](5405.md), [Japan](15573.md), [India](14533.md) and [Arabia](47858.md). A [thalassocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy) had thus emerged based on international trade.&#x20;

Even scattered barangays, through the development of inter-island and international trade, became more culturally homogeneous by the 4th century. [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus)-[Buddhist](3267529.md) culture and religion flourished among the noblemen in this era.&#x20;

In the period between the 7th to the beginning of the 15th centuries, numerous prosperous centers of trade had emerged, including the Kingdom of [Namayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan) which flourished alongside [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay),[^cite_note-City_Government_of_Pasay-104][^cite_note-105] [Cebu](160278.md), [Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo),[^cite_note-106] [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan), the Kingdom of [Sanfotsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanfotsi) situated in [Pangasinan](1691800.md), the Kingdom of Luzon now known as [Pampanga](255602.md) which specialized in trade with most of what is now known as Southeast Asia and with China, Japan and the [Kingdom of Ryukyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ryukyu) in [Okinawa](22477.md).&#x20;

From the 9th century onwards, a large number of [Arab](2185.md) traders from the Middle East settled in the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago) and intermarried with the local [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), Bruneian, Malaysian, Indonesian and [Luzon](69375.md) and [Visayas](72418.md) indigenous populations.[^cite_note-Arab-Malays-107]&#x20;

In the years leading up to 1000 AD, there were already several maritime societies existing in the islands but there was no unifying political [state](23604120.md) encompassing the entire Philippine archipelago. Instead, the region was dotted by numerous semi-autonomous *[barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangays)* (settlements ranging in size from villages to city-states) under the sovereignty of competing [thalassocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy) ruled by [datus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu), [rajahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah) or [sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan)[^cite_note-108] or by upland agricultural societies ruled by "petty plutocrats". Nations such as the Wangdoms of [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caboloan) and [Ma-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma-i) as well as Ma-i's subordinates, the [Barangay states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precolonial_barangay) of [Pulilu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulilu) and [Sandao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandao); the Kingdoms of [Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Maynila), [Namayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan), and [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tondo); the [Kedatuans of Madja-as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madja-as), [Dapitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapitan_Kingdom), and [Cainta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta_\(historical_polity\)); the Rajahnates of [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Cebu), [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Butuan) and [Sanmalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanmalan); and the Sultanates of [Buayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Buayan), [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao), [Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_Lanao) and [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sultanate); existed alongside the highland societies of the [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao_people) and [Mangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangyan).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Some of these regions were part of the Malayan empires of [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya), [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Empire).[^cite_note-end-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]&#x20;

#### Historic caste systems

**[Datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu)** – The [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) *[maginoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maginoo)*, the [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language) *ginu* and the [Visayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_languages) *tumao* were the nobility social class among various cultures of the pre-colonial Philippines. Among the Visayans, the tumao were further distinguished from the immediate royal families or a ruling class.&#x20;

**[Timawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timawa)** – The timawa class were free commoners of Luzon and the Visayas who could own their own land and who did not have to pay a regular tribute to a maginoo, though they would, from time to time, be obliged to work on a datu's land and help in community projects and events. They were free to change their allegiance to another datu if they married into another community or if they decided to move.&#x20;

**[Maharlika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika)** – Members of the Tagalog warrior class known as maharlika had the same rights and responsibilities as the timawa, but in times of war they were bound to serve their datu in battle. They had to arm themselves at their own expense, but they did get to keep the loot they took. Although they were partly related to the nobility, the maharlikas were technically less free than the timawas because they could not leave a datu's service without first hosting a large public feast and paying the datu between 6 and 18 pesos in gold – a large sum in those days.&#x20;

**[Alipin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alipin)** – Commonly described as "servant" or "slave". However, this is inaccurate. The concept of the alipin relied on a complex system of obligation and repayment through labor in ancient Philippine society, rather than on the actual purchase of a person as in Western and Islamic [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery). Members of the alipin class who owned their own houses were more accurately equivalent to medieval European [serfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serf) and commoners.&#x20;

By the 15th century, Arab and Indian missionaries and traders from Malaysia and Indonesia brought Islam to the Philippines, where it both replaced and was practiced together with indigenous religions. Before that, indigenous tribes of the Philippines practiced a mixture of [Animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Buddhism](3267529.md). Native villages, called *barangays* were populated by locals called Timawa (Middle Class/freemen) and Alipin (servants and slaves). They were ruled by [Rajahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah), [Datus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu) and [Sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan), a class called [Maginoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maginoo) (royals) and defended by the [Maharlika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika) (Lesser nobles, royal warriors and aristocrats).[^cite_note-State2794-91] These Royals and Nobles are descended from native Filipinos with varying degrees of [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples) and [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples), which is evident in today's DNA analysis among South East Asian Royals. This tradition continued among the Spanish and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) traders who also intermarried with the local populations.[^cite_note-116]&#x20;

### Spanish period (1521–1898)

Depiction of Filipino celebration

The first census in the Philippines was in 1591, based on tributes collected. The tributes counted the total founding population of the Spanish Philippines as 667,612 people.[^cite_note-117]<sup>: 177 </sup>[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] 20,000 were Chinese migrant traders,[^cite_note-120] at different times: around 15,600 individuals were Latino soldier-colonists who were cumulatively sent from Peru and Mexico and they were shipped to the Philippines annually,[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-Convicts_or_Conquistadores-122] 3,000 were Japanese residents,[^cite_note-123] and 600 were pure Spaniards from Europe.[^cite_note-124] There was a large but unknown number of [South Asian Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipinos), as the majority of the slaves imported into the archipelago were from [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) and Southern India,[^cite_note-125] adding [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_language) speaking South Indians and [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language) speaking [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis) into the ethnic mix.&#x20;

The Philippines was governed by the Spaniards. The arrival of Portuguese explorer [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan) ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): Fernão de Magalhães) in 1521 began a period of European immigration. During the Spanish period, the Philippines was part of the [Viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy) of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), which was governed and administered from [Mexico City](18987.md). Early Spanish settlers were mostly explorers, soldiers, government officials and religious [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friars_in_Spanish_Philippines) born in Spain and Mexico. Most Spaniards who settled were of [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basques) ancestry,[^cite_note-126] but there were also settlers of [Andalusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusians), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans), and [Moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) descent.[^cite_note-127] The *Peninsulares* (governors born in Spain), mostly of [Castilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilians) ancestry, settled in the islands to govern their territory. Most settlers married the daughters of [rajahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah), [datus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu), and [sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) to reinforce the alliances of the islands. The *Ginoo* and *Maharlika* castes (royals and nobles) in the Philippines prior to the arrival of the Spaniards formed the privileged *[Principalía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal%C3%ADa)* (nobility) during the early Spanish period.&#x20;

The arrival of the Spaniards to the Philippines, especially through the commencement of the [Manila-Acapulco galleon trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon) that connected the [Philippines](23440.md) through [Manila](184334.md) to [Acapulco](1446.md) in [Mexico](3966054.md), attracted new waves of immigrants from [China](5405.md), as Manila was already previously connected to the [Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road), as shown in the [Selden Map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selden_Map), from [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou) and [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou) in [Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Fujian) to [Manila](184334.md), maritime trade flourished during the Spanish period, especially as Manila was connected to the ports of [Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnan_region), such as [Yuegang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuegang) (the old port of [Haicheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haicheng,_Fujian) in [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou), [Fujian](88585.md)).[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] The Spaniards recruited thousands of Chinese migrant workers from "*Chinchew*" ([Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou)), "*Chiõ Chiu*" ([Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou)), "*Canton*" ([Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou)), and [Macau](19068.md) called *[sangleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley)* (from [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Hokkien) [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [生理](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%94%9F%E7%90%86); [Pe̍h-ōe-jī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%CC%8Dh-%C5%8De-j%C4%AB): *Sng-lí*; lit. 'business') to build the colonial infrastructure in the islands. Many Chinese immigrants converted to Christianity, intermarried with the locals, and adopted Hispanized names and customs and became assimilated, although the children of unions between Filipinos and Chinese that became assimilated continued to be designated in official records as *[mestizos de sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley)*. The Chinese mestizos were largely confined to the [Binondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binondo) area until the 19th century. However, they eventually spread all over the islands and became traders, landowners and moneylenders. Today, their descendants still comprise a significant part of the [Philippine population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Philippines) especially its [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie),[^cite_note-Chirot-130] who during the late [Spanish Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1565%E2%80%931898\)) in the late 19th century, produced a major part of the *[ilustrado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilustrado)* [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia) of the late [Spanish Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines), that were very influential with the creation of [Filipino nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_nationalism) and the sparking of the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) as part of the foundation of the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) and subsequent sovereign independent [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-Formation_of_Filipino_Nationality-131][^cite_note-Reid-132] Today, the bulk of the families in the [list of the political families in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_families_in_the_Philippines) have such family background. Meanwhile, the Spanish-era Sangley's pure ethnic Chinese descendants of which, replenished by later migrants in the 20th century, that preserved at least some of their [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino#Society_and_culture), integrated together with mainstream [Filipino culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Philippines), are now in the form of the modern [Chinese Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) community, who currently play a leading role in the Philippine business sector and contribute a significant share of the [Philippine economy](23445.md) today,[^cite_note-auto1-133][^cite_note-auto-134][^cite_note-auto2b-135][^cite_note-auto3-136][^cite_note-auto4-137] where most in the current list of the Philippines' richest each year comprise [Taipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai-pan) [billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire) of Chinese Filipino background, mostly of [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Hokkien) descent, where most still trace their roots back to mostly [Jinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinjiang,_Fujian) or [Nan'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan%27an,_Fujian) within [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou) or sometimes [Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen) (Amoy) or [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou), all within [Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Fujian), the Philippines' historical trade partner with [Mainland China](55079.md).&#x20;

In the 16th and 17th centuries, thousands of [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) traders also migrated to the Philippines and assimilated into the local population.[^cite_note-Leupp2003p52-53-138] Many were assimilated throughout the centuries, especially through the tumultuous period of [World War II](32927.md). Today, there is a small growing [Nikkei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkeijin) community of [Japanese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines) in [Davao](296162.md) with roots to the old [Little Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japantown) in Mintal or Calinan in [Davao City](489401.md) during the [American colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)), where many had roots starting out in [Abaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abac%C3%A1) plantations or from workers of the Benguet Road ([Kennon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennon_Road)) to [Baguio](162480.md).&#x20;

[British forces occupied Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Manila) between 1762 and 1764 as a part of the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War). However, the only part of the Philippines which the British held was the [Spanish capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros) of [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manila) and the principal naval port of [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite), both of which are located by the [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay). The war was ended by the [Treaty of Paris (1763)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)). At the end of the war the treaty signatories were not aware that Manila had been taken by the British and was being administered as a British colony. Consequently, no specific provision was made for the Philippines. Instead they fell under the general provision that all other lands not otherwise provided for be returned to the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire).[^cite_note-139] Many [Indian](7564733.md) [Sepoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy) troops and their British captains mutinied and were left in Manila and some parts of the [Ilocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos) and [Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan). The [Indian Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipino) in Manila settled at [Cainta, Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta,_Rizal) and the ones in the north settled in [Isabela](271106.md). Most were assimilated into the local population. Even before the British invasion, there were already also a large but unknown number of [Indian Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipinos) as majority of the slaves imported into the archipelago were from [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) or Southern [India](14533.md),[^cite_note-140] adding [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_language) speaking South Indians and [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language) speaking [Bangladeshis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Bangladesh) into the ethnic mix.&#x20;

*[Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) [Sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley) y [Chino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino)* ([Sangley Chinese or Chinese Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino)), c. 1841 *[Tipos del País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipos_del_Pa%C3%ADs)*, watercolor by [Justiniano Asuncion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justiniano_Asuncion)

A total of 110 [Manila-Acapulco galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon) set sail between 1565 and 1815, during the Philippines trade with Mexico. Until 1593, three or more ships would set sail annually from each port bringing with them the riches of the archipelago to Spain. European *criollos*, *mestizos* and Portuguese, French and Mexican descent from the Americas, mostly from Latin America came in contact with the Filipinos. [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people), [Indian](7564733.md) and [Cambodian](5430.md) Christians who fled from religious persecutions and killing fields also settled in the Philippines during the 17th until the 19th centuries. The Mexicans especially were a major source of military migration to the Philippines and during the Spanish period they were referred to as guachinangos[^cite_note-Intercolonial-141][^cite_note-142] and they readily intermarried and mixed with native Filipinos. Bernal, the author of the book "Mexico en Filipinas" contends, that they were middlemen, the guachinangos in contrast to the Spanish and criollos, known as Castila, that had positions in power and were isolated, the guachinangos in the meantime, had interacted with the natives of the Philippines, while in contrast, the exchanges between Castila and native were negligent. Following Bernal, these two groups—native Filipinos and the Castila—had been two "mutually unfamiliar castes" that had "no real contact." Between them, he clarifies however, were the Chinese traders and the guachinangos (Mexicans).[^cite_note-Intercolonial-141] In the 1600s, Spain deployed thousands of Mexican and Peruvian soldiers across the many cities and presidios of the Philippines.[^cite_note-Mexicans-143]&#x20;

With the inauguration of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1867, Spain opened the Philippines for international trade. European investors of British, Dutch, German, Portuguese, Russian, Italian, and French nationality were among those who settled in the islands as business increased. More Spaniards and Chinese arrived during the next century. Many of these migrants intermarried with local *mestizos* and assimilated with the indigenous population.&#x20;

El Mestiso by Justiniano Asuncion

In the 1860s to 1890s, in the urban areas of the Philippines, especially at Manila, according to burial statistics, as much as 3.3% of the population were pure European Spaniards and the pure Chinese were as high as 9.9%. The Spanish Filipino and Chinese Filipino Mestizo populations also fluctuated. Eventually, many families belonging to the non-native categories from centuries ago beyond the late 19th century diminished because their descendants intermarried enough and were assimilated into and chose to self-identify as Filipinos while forgetting their ancestor's roots[^cite_note-154] since during the Philippine Revolution to modern times, the term "Filipino" was expanded to include everyone born in the Philippines coming from any race, as per the [Philippine nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] That would explain the abrupt drop of otherwise high Chinese, Spanish and mestizo, percentages across the country by the time of the first American census in 1903.[^cite_note-157] By the 20th century, the remaining ethnic Spaniards and ethnic Chinese, replenished by further Chinese migrants in the 20th century, now later came to compose the modern [Spanish Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) community and [Chinese Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) community respectively, where families of such background contribute a significant share of the [Philippine economy](23445.md) today,[^cite_note-auto1-133][^cite_note-auto-134][^cite_note-auto2-2][^cite_note-auto3-136][^cite_note-auto4-137] where most in the current list of the Philippines' richest each year comprise [billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire) of either [Chinese Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) background or the old elite families of [Spanish Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) background.&#x20;

### Late modern



Filipina women in [Filipiniana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Clara_gown) dress, (Manila, 1899).

After the defeat of Spain during the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) in 1898, Filipino general, [Emilio Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Aguinaldo) declared [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Declaration_of_Independence) on June 12 while General [Wesley Merritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Merritt) became the first American [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines#United_States_Military_Government_\(1898%E2%80%931901\)) of the Philippines. On December 10, 1898, the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1898\)) formally ended the war, with Spain ceding the Philippines and other territories to the [United States](3434750.md) in exchange for \$20 million.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]&#x20;



A native Filipina with [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino), [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_in_the_Philippines) / [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines) settlers in the Philippines, 1900

The [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War) resulted in the deaths of at least 200,000 Filipino civilians.[^cite_note-160] Some estimates for total civilian dead reach up to 1,000,000.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] After the Philippine–American War, the United States civil governance was established in 1901, with [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft) as the first American [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines#United_States_Colonial_Government_\(1901-1935\)).[^cite_note-163] A number of Americans settled in the islands and thousands of interracial marriages between Americans and Filipinos have taken place since then. Owing to the strategic location of the Philippines, as many as 21 bases and 100,000 military personnel were stationed there since the United States first colonized the islands in 1898. These bases were decommissioned in 1992 after the end of the [Cold War](325329.md), but left behind thousands of [Amerasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerasian) children.[^cite_note-findarticles.com-164] The country gained [independence](62450.md) from the United States in 1946. The [Pearl S. Buck International Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Hills_Farm) estimates there are 52,000 Amerasians scattered throughout the Philippines. However, according to the center of Amerasian Research, there might be as many as 250,000 [Amerasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerasians) scattered across the cities of [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Clark_City), [Angeles City](842728.md), [Manila](184334.md), and [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo).[^cite_note-Military_Filipino_Amerasians_-_2_sources-165] In addition, numerous Filipino men enlisted in the US Navy and made careers in it, often settling with their families in the United States. Some of their second- or third-generation families returned to the country.&#x20;

[Philippine nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law) is currently based upon the principle of *[jus sanguinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jus_sanguinis)* and, therefore, descent from a parent who is a citizen of the Republic of the Philippines is the primary method of acquiring national citizenship. Birth in the Philippines to foreign parents does not in itself confer Philippine citizenship, although RA9139, the Administrative Naturalization Law of 2000, does provide a path for administrative naturalization of certain aliens born in the Philippines. Since many of the above historical groups came to the Philippines before its establishment as an independent state, many have also gained citizenship before the founding of either the [First Philippines Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) or [Third Republic of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1946%E2%80%931965\)). For example, many [Cold-War-era](325329.md) [Chinese migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) who had relatives in the Philippines attain [Filipino citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law) for their children through [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) with [Chinese Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) families that trace back to either the late [Spanish Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1565%E2%80%931898\)) or [American Colonial Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)). Likewise, many other modern expatriates from various countries, such as the [US](3434750.md), often come to the [Philippines](23440.md) to marry with a [Filipino citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law), ensuring their future children attain [Filipino citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law) and their Filipino spouses ensure [property ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ownership).&#x20;

During the [Spanish period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1565%E2%80%931898\)), [Spaniards](963152.md) from [Spain](26667.md) and [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America) mainly referred to [Spaniards born in the Philippines (Spanish Filipinos)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) in [Spanish](26825.md): *"**[Filipino/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/filipino)**" (m) or "[Filipina/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/filipina)" (f)*[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-Abella-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-\:1-170] in relation to those born in Hispanic America called in [Spanish](26825.md): *"[Americano/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/americano)" (m) / "[Americana/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/americana)" (f) or "[Criollo/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/criollo)"*, whereas the Spaniards born in the Philippines themselves called the Spaniards from Spain as "*[Peninsular/es](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/peninsular#Spanish)*" with themselves also referred to as "***[Insular/es](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/insular#Spanish)***".[^cite_note-\:1-170] Meanwhile, the caste system hierarchy and taxation system during the Spanish Times dictated that those of mixed descent were known as "*[**Mestizo**/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mestizo#Spanish)*" (*m*) / "*[Mestiza/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mestiza#Spanish)*" (*f*), specifically those of mixed [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) and [native Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Philippines) descent were known as "*[Mestizo/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) de [Español](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino)*" ([Spanish Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)), whereas those of mixed [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) and [native Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Philippines) descent were known as "*[Mestizo/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) de [Sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley)*" ([Chinese Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)) and the mix of all of the above or a mix of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) were known as "*[Tornatrás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torna_atr%C3%A1s)*". Meanwhile, the [ethnic Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) migrants ([Chinese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino)) were historically referred to as "***[Sangley/es](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley)***" (from [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Hokkien) [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [生理](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%94%9F%E7%90%86); [Pe̍h-ōe-jī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%CC%8Dh-%C5%8De-j%C4%AB): *Sng-lí*; lit. 'business'), while the [natives of the Philippine islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Philippines) were usually known by the generic term "***[Indio/s](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/indio#Spanish)***"[^cite_note-\:1-170] (lit. "[Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies), native of the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies)").&#x20;

Filipinos of mixed ethnic origins are still referred today as *[mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_mestizo)*. However, in common popular parlance, mestizos usually refer to Filipinos mixed with Spanish or any other European ancestry. Filipinos mixed with any other foreign ethnicities are named depending on the non-Filipino part. Historically though, it was the *[Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) de [Sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley)* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)) that numbered the most among *[mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)*,[^cite_note-Formation_of_Filipino_Nationality-131] though the *[Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) de [Español](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino)* ([Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)) carried more [social prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation) due to the [caste system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste) hierarchy that usually elevated [Spanish](963152.md) blood and [Christianized natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) to the peak, while most descendants of the *[Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) de [Sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley)* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)), despite assuming many of the important roles in the economic, social and political life of the nation, would readily assimilate into the fabric of Philippine society. Despite regional, linguistic, and cultural diversity, Filipinos are commonly linked by shared historical experiences, civic traditions, and national institutions that contribute to a collective sense of identity.[^cite_note-171]&#x20;

[Marcelo Azcárraga Palmero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Azc%C3%A1rraga_Palmero), the only Spanish prime minister of mestizo (Filipino) descent

People classified as 'blancos' (whites) were the insulares or "Filipinos" (a person born in the Philippines of pure Spanish descent), peninsulares (a person born in Spain of pure Spanish descent), Español mestizos (a person born in the Philippines of mixed Austronesian and Spanish ancestry) and tornatrás (a person born in the Philippines of mixed Austronesian, Chinese and Spanish ancestry). [Manila](184334.md) was racially segregated, with blancos living in the walled city of [Intramuros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros), un-Christianized sangleys in Parían, Christianized sangleys and mestizos de sangley in Binondo and the rest of the 7,000 islands for the indios, with the exception of Cebu and several other Spanish posts. Only mestizos de sangley were allowed to enter Intramuros to work for whites (including mestizos de español) as servants and various occupations needed for the colony. Indio were native Austronesians, but as a legal classification, Indio were those who embraced Roman Catholicism and Austronesians who lived in proximity to the Spanish colonies.&#x20;

People who lived outside [Manila](184334.md), [Cebu](285868.md) and the major Spanish posts were classified as such: 'Naturales' were Catholic Austronesians of the lowland and coastal towns. The un-Catholic Negritos and [Austronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesians) who lived in the towns were classified as 'salvajes' (savages) or 'infieles' (the unfaithful). 'Remontados' (Spanish for 'situated in the mountains') and 'tulisanes' (bandits) were [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people) Austronesians and Negritos who refused to live in towns and took to the hills, all of whom were considered to live outside the social order as Catholicism was a driving force in Spanish everyday life, as well as determining social class in the territory. People of pure Spanish descent living in the [Philippines](23440.md) who were born in Spanish America were classified as 'americanos'. Mestizos and africanos born in Spanish America living in the Philippines kept their legal classification as such and usually came as indentured servants to the 'americanos'. The Philippine-born children of 'americanos' were classified as 'Ins'. The Philippine-born children of mestizos and Africanos from Spanish America were classified based on patrilineal descent.&#x20;



A *[mestiza de sangley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestiza_de_sangley)* woman in a photograph by Francisco Van Camp, c. 1875

The term *negrito* was coined by the Spaniards based on their appearance. The word 'negrito' would be misinterpreted and used by future European scholars as an ethnoracial term in and of itself. Both Christianized negritos who lived in the archipelago and un-Christianized negritos who lived in tribes outside were classified as 'negritos'. Christianized negritos who lived in Manila were not allowed to enter Intramuros and lived in areas designated for indios.&#x20;

A person of mixed [Negrito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito) and [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) ancestry were classified based on patrilineal descent; the father's ancestry determined a child's legal classification. If the father was 'negrito' and the mother was 'India' (Austronesian), the child was classified as 'negrito'. If the father was 'indio' and the mother was 'negrita', the child was classified as 'indio'. Persons of Negrito descent were viewed as being outside the social order as they usually lived in tribes outside and resisted conversion to [Christianity](5211.md).&#x20;

This legal system of racial classification based on patrilineal descent had no parallel anywhere in the Spanish-ruled territories in the Americas. In general, a son born of a sangley male and an indio or mestizo de sangley female was classified as mestizo de sangley; all subsequent male descendants were mestizos de sangley regardless of whether they married an India or a mestiza de sangley. A daughter born in such a manner, however, acquired the legal classification of her husband, i.e., she became an India if she married an indio but remained a mestiza de sangley if she married a mestizo de sangley or a sangley. In this way, a chino mestizo male descendant of a paternal sangley ancestor never lost his legal status as a mestizo de sangley no matter how little percentage of Chinese blood he had in his veins or how many generations had passed since his first Chinese ancestor; he was thus a mestizo de sangley in perpetuity.&#x20;

However, a 'mestiza de sangley' who married a blanco ('Filipino', 'mestizo de español', 'peninsular' or 'americano') kept her status as 'mestiza de sangley'. But her children were classified as tornatrás. An 'India' who married a blanco also kept her status as India, but her children were classified as mestizo de español. A mestiza de español who married another blanco would keep her status as mestiza, but her status will never change from mestiza de español if she married a mestizo de español, Filipino or peninsular. In contrast, a mestizo (de sangley or español) man's status stayed the same regardless of whom he married. If a mestizo (de sangley or español) married a filipina (woman of pure Spanish descent), she would lose her status as a 'filipina' and would acquire the legal status of her husband and become a mestiza de español or sangley. If a 'filipina' married an 'indio', her legal status would change to 'India', despite being of pure Spanish descent.&#x20;

The *[de facto](43594.md)* social stratification system based on class that continues to this day in the country had its beginnings in the Spanish area with a discriminating caste system.[^cite_note-172]&#x20;

The Insulares, who already saw their distinct identity from the peninsulares adopted the term *Filipino* to refer to themselves. And among these Insulares Luis Rodriguez y Varela was the first to use it.[^cite_note-Jett2017-173] The use of the term was later adopted by the Spanish and Chinese [mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) or those born of mixed Chinese-indio or Spanish-indio descent. Late in the 19th century, [José Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal) popularized the use of the term *Filipino* to refer to all those born in the Philippines, including the Indios.[^cite_note-Owen_2014_https\://books.google.com/books?id=cippAwAAQBAJ\&dq=indio+filipino+rizal\&pg=PA275_275-174] When ordered to sign the notification of his death sentence, which described him as a Chinese mestizo, Rizal refused. He went to his death saying that he was *indio puro*.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-Owen_2014_https\://books.google.com/books?id=cippAwAAQBAJ\&dq=indio+filipino+rizal\&pg=PA275_275-174]&#x20;

After the Philippines' independence from Spain in 1898 and the word Filipino "officially" became a nationality that includes the entire population of the Philippines regardless of racial ancestry, as per the [Philippine nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law) and as described by [Wenceslao Retana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslao_Retana)'s *Diccionario de filipinismos*, where he defined ***Filipinos*** as follows,[^cite_note-\:0-69]&#x20;

> *todos los nacidos en Filipinas sin distincion de origen ni de raza.*\
> &#x20;All those born in the Philippines without distinction of origin or race.

— [Wenceslao E. Retaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslao_Retana), Diccionario De Filipinismos: Con La Revisión De Lo Que Al Respecto Lleva Publicado La Real Academia Española

Migration of the [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) and their [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages).

[^cite_note-177]

The aboriginal settlers of the Philippines were primarily [Negrito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito) groups. Negritos today comprise a small minority of the nation's overall population, and received significant geneflow from Austronesian groups, as well as an even earlier "Basal-East Asian" group, while the modern Austronesian-speaking majority population does not, or only marginally show evidence for admixture, and cluster closely with other [East/Southeast Asian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_people).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarena_et_al2021-179] There were also immigrations from [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages), [Papuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papuan_languages), and [South Asian](21566765.md) peoples.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarena_et_al2021-179]&#x20;

The majority population of Filipinos are [Austronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people), a linguistic and genetic group whose historical ties lay in [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia) and southern East Asia, but through ancient migrations can be found as indigenous peoples stretching as far east as the [Pacific Islands](341111.md) and as far west as [Madagascar](18964.md) off the coast of Africa.[^cite_note-cavalli-sforza-180][^cite_note-Capelli2001-181] The current predominant theory on Austronesian expansion holds that Austronesians settled the Philippine islands through successive southward and eastward seaborne migrations from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) [Austronesian populations of Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_aborigines).[^cite_note-nzsr-182]&#x20;

Other hypotheses have also been put forward based on linguistic, archeological, and genetic studies. These include an origin from mainland [southern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_southern_China) (linking them to the [Liangzhu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangzhu_culture) and the [Tapengkeng culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapengkeng_culture), later displaced or assimilated by the expansion of speakers of [Sino-Tibetan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages));[^cite_note-ko-183][^cite_note-ho-184] an *in situ* origin from the [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland) continental shelf prior to the [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) at the end of the [last glacial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period) (c. 10,000 BC);[^cite_note-Mark_Donohue_and_Tim_Denham-185][^cite_note-leeds-186] or a combination of the two (the [Nusantao Maritime Trading and Communication Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantao_Maritime_Trading_and_Communication_Network) hypothesis) which advocates [cultural diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_diffusion) rather than a series of linear migrations.[^cite_note-solheim-187]&#x20;

### Genetics

Main article: [Genetic studies on Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_studies_on_Filipinos)

According to a genetic study done by the [Kaiser Permanente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Permanente) (KP) Research Program on Genes, Environment, and Health (RPGEH), most self-identified Filipinos sampled, have "modest" amounts of European ancestry consistent with older admixture.[^cite_note-Kaiser-188]&#x20;

### Dental morphology

[Dental morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentition) provides clues to prehistoric migration patterns of the Philippines, with Sinodont dental patterns occurring in East Asia, Central Asia, North Asia, and the Americas. Sundadont patterns occur in Southeast Asia as well as the bulk of Oceania.[^cite_note-Hinke_et_al_p.1903-189] Filipinos exhibit [Sundadonty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinodonty_and_Sundadonty),[^cite_note-Hinke_et_al_p.1903-189][^cite_note-190] and are regarded as having a more generalised dental morphology and having a longer ancestry than its offspring, Sinodonty.&#x20;

### Historic reports



Filipino members of [Knights of Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_Columbus).

Published in 1849, The *Catalogo Alfabetico de Apellidos* contains 141 pages of surnames with both Spanish and Hispanicized indigenous roots.&#x20;

Authored by Spanish Governor-General Narciso Claveria y Zaldua and Domingo Abella, the catalog was created in response to the Decree of November 21, 1849, which gave every Filipino a surname from the book. The decree in the Philippines was created to fulfill a Spanish colonial decree that sought to address colonial subjects who did not have a last name. This explains why most Filipinos share the same surnames as many Hispanics today, without having Spanish ancestry.&#x20;

Augustinian Friar, Joaquín Martínez de Zúñiga, in the 1800s, measured varying ratios of Spanish-Mestizos as percentages of the populations of the various provinces, with ranges such as: 19.5% of the population of Tondo (The most populous province), to Pampanga (13.7%), Cavite (13%) and Bulacan (10.8%) to as low as 5% in Cebu, and non-existent in the isolated provinces.[^cite_note-Estadismo1-144][^cite_note-Estadismo2-145] Overall the whole Philippines, even including the provinces with no Spanish settlement, as summed up, the average percentage of Spanish Filipino tributes amount to 5% of the total population.[^cite_note-Estadismo1-144][^cite_note-Estadismo2-145]&#x20;

The book, "Intercolonial Intimacies Relinking Latin/o America to the Philippines, 1898–1964 By Paula C. Park" citing "Forzados y reclutas: los criollos novohispanos en Asia (1756-1808)" gave the number of later Mexican soldier-immigrants to the Philippines, pegging the number at 35,000 immigrants in the 1700s,[^cite_note-Intercolonial-141] in a Philippine population which was only around 1.5 Million,[^cite_note-191] thus the Latin Americans, mainly Mexicans, formed 2.33% of the population.[^cite_note-192]&#x20;

In relation to this, a population survey conducted by German ethnographer [Fedor Jagor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedor_Jagor) concluded that 1/3rd of Luzon which holds half of the Philippines' population had varying degrees of Spanish and Mexican ancestry.[^cite_note-193]&#x20;

Meanwhile, according to older records held by the [Senate of the Philippines](299521.md), there were approximately 1.35 million [ethnic (or pure)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) within the Philippine population, while Filipinos with any [Chinese descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley) comprised 22.8 million people (20% of the population).[^cite_note-senate.gov.ph-194]&#x20;

### Current immigration

Main article: [Immigration to the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_Philippines)

Recent studies during 2015, record around 220,000 to 600,000 American citizens living in the country.[^cite_note-195] This increased to 750,000 Americans living in the Philippines by year 2025, forming 0.75% of the population.[^cite_note-ManilaBulletin-196] There are also 250,000 [Amerasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerasian) across [Angeles City](842728.md), Manila, [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Clark_City) and [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo), forming 0.25% of the population.[^cite_note-Military_Filipino_Amerasians_-_2_sources-165] Together, the total percentage of individuals who possess full or partial [American](19792942.md) descent form 1% of the total demographics of the Philippines.&#x20;

## Languages

Main articles: [Languages of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Philippines) and [Philippine languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_languages)



The indigenous (native) Philippine languages spoken around the country that have the largest number of speakers in a particular region with Tagalog being the largest. Note that on regions marked with black diamonds, the language with the most speakers denotes a minority of the population.

[Austronesian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages) have been spoken in the Philippines for thousands of years. According to a 2014 study by Mark Donohue of the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) and Tim Denham of [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University), there is no linguistic evidence for an orderly north-to-south dispersal of the Austronesian languages from Taiwan through the Philippines and into Island Southeast Asia (ISEA).[^cite_note-Mark_Donohue_and_Tim_Denham-185] Many adopted words from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) and Tamil were incorporated during the strong wave of [Indian (Hindu-Buddhist) cultural influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_Philippines) starting from the 5th century BC, in common with its Southeast Asian neighbors. Chinese languages were also commonly spoken among the traders of the archipelago. However, with the advent of Islam, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) soon came to supplant Sanskrit and Tamil as holy languages. Starting in the second half of the 16th century, Spanish was the official language of the country for the more than three centuries that the islands were governed through [Mexico City](18987.md) on behalf of the Spanish Empire. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, Spanish was the preferred language among *[Ilustrados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilustrados)* and educated Filipinos in general. Significant disagreements exist, however, on the extent [Spanish](26825.md) use beyond that. It has been argued that the Philippines were less hispanized than [Canaries](5717.md) and [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), with Spanish only being adopted by the ruling class involved in civil and judicial administration and culture. Spanish was the language of only approximately ten percent of the Philippine population when Spanish rule ended in 1898.[^cite_note-197] As a lingua franca or creole language of Filipinos, major languages of the country like [Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano), [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language), [Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikol_languages), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Waray-Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray-Waray_language), and [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilokano_language) assimilated many different words and expressions from Castilian Spanish.&#x20;

[Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano) is the only Spanish-based [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) in Asia. Its vocabulary is 90 percent Spanish, and the remaining 10 percent is a mixture of predominantly [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), and some English. Chavacano is considered by the [Instituto Cervantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Cervantes) to be a Spanish-based language.[^cite_note-198]&#x20;

In sharp contrast, another view is that the ratio of the population which spoke Spanish as their *mother tongue* in the last decade of Spanish rule was 10% or 14%.[^cite_note-GGR-199] An additional 60% is said to have spoken Spanish as a second language until World War II, but this is also disputed as to whether this percentage spoke "kitchen Spanish", which was used as marketplace lingua compared to those who were actual fluent Spanish speakers.[^cite_note-GGR-199]&#x20;

In 1863 a Spanish decree introduced [universal education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_education), creating free public schooling in Spanish, yet it was never implemented, even before the advent of American annexation.[^cite_note-200] It was also the language of the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution), and the 1899 [Malolos Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Constitution) proclaimed it as the "official language" of the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic), albeit a temporary official language. Spanish continued to be the predominant [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) used in the islands by the elite class before and during the American colonial regime. Following the American occupation of the Philippines and the imposition of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English), the overall use of Spanish declined gradually, especially after the 1940s.&#x20;

According to *[Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue)*, there are about 180 languages spoken in the Philippines.[^cite_note-201] The [1987 Constitution of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Constitution_of_the_Philippines) imposed the [Filipino language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language)[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-Gonzalez-203] as the [national language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language) and designates it, along with the [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English), as one of the [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language). [Regional languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_languages) are designated as [auxiliary official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_language). The constitution also provides that Spanish and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) shall be promoted on a voluntary and optional basis.[^cite_note-204]&#x20;

Other Philippine languages in the country with at least 1,000,000 native and indigenous speakers include [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_language), [Central Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bikol_language), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language), [Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano) (Spanish-based creole), [Albay Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albay_Bikol_language), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language), [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language), [Kinaray-a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinaray-a_language), [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language), [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language), [Masbateño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate%C3%B1o_language), [Aklanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aklan_language) and [Ibanag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibanag_language). The 28-letter modern [Filipino alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_alphabet), adopted in 1987, is the official writing system. In addition, each ethnicity's language has their own writing scripts and set of alphabets, many of which are no longer used.[^cite_note-205]&#x20;

However, there has been a resurgence of these ancient scripts, and initiatives to push the government for standardization. The most prominent script, Baybayin, is a writing system native to the Philippines, with the word 'baybay' meaning "to spell" in Tagalog (Bielenberg, 2018). Due to Spanish colonization, this script was replaced with the Latin alphabet which became the standard of the Philippines. In recent times, there has been a large interest in revitalizing Baybayin, with scholars spreading awareness and education online, and artists interpreting this script into their work.[^cite_note-206]&#x20;

## Religion

Main article: [Religion in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Philippines)



Devotees flock to the [Basilica Minore del Santo Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_Minore_del_Santo_Ni%C3%B1o) during the novena Masses.

According to then [National Statistics Office (NSO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority) as of 2010, over 92% of the population were [Christians](8276776.md), with 80.6% professing [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-PSA-2015PSY_2-207] The latter was introduced by the Spanish beginning in 1521, and during their more than 330-year [colonization of the islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Philippines), they managed to convert a vast majority of Filipinos, resulting in the Philippines becoming the largest predominantly Catholic country in Asia. There are also large groups of [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_Philippines) denominations, which either grew or were founded following the [disestablishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_state) of the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines) during the [American Colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Colonial_Period_\(Philippines\)). The homegrown [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) is currently the single largest church whose headquarters is in the Philippines, followed by [United Church of Christ in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines). The [Iglesia Filipina Independiente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_Filipina_Independiente) (also known as the Aglipayan Church) was an earlier development, and is a [national church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_church) directly resulting from the [1898 Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution). Other Christian groups such as the [Victory Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Christian_Fellowship),[^cite_note-208] [Eddie Villanueva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Villanueva)-founded and led [Jesus Is Lord Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Is_Lord_Church), [Jesus Miracle Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Miracle_Crusade), [Mormonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_in_the_Philippines), [Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Orthodox_Church), and the [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah%27s_Witnesses) have a visible presence in the country.&#x20;

The second largest religion in the country is [Islam](6037917.md), estimated in 2014 to account for 5% to 8% of the population. [^cite_note-US-StateDept_irf_Report-209] [Islam in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines) is mostly concentrated in southwestern [Mindanao](20546.md) and the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago) which, though part of the Philippines, are very close to the neighboring [Islamic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world) of [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Indonesia](14579.md). The Muslims call themselves *Moros*, a [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Philippines) word that refers to the [Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) (albeit the two groups have little cultural connection other than Islam).&#x20;

Historically, ancient Filipinos held animist religions that were influenced by [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_Philippines) and [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Philippines), which were brought by traders from neighbouring Asian states. These [indigenous Philippine folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Philippine_folk_religions) continue to be present among the populace, with some communities, such as the [Aeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeta), [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot), and [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad), having some strong adherents and some who mix beliefs originating from the indigenous religions with beliefs from Christianity or Islam.[^cite_note-hislop-210][^cite_note-211] There are temples also for Sikhism, also located in the provinces and in the cities, sometimes located near Hindu temples.[^cite_note-212]&#x20;

As of 2013, religious groups together constituting less than five percent of the population included [Sikhism](27964.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Seventh-day Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventists), [United Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ), [United Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodists), the [Episcopal Church in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_in_the_Philippines), [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God), [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (Mormons), and Philippine (Southern) [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention); and the following domestically established churches: [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) (Church of Christ), [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church) (Aglipayan), [Members Church of God International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_Church_of_God_International), [Jesus Is Lord Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Is_Lord_Church), and [The Kingdom of Jesus Christ, the Name Above Every Name](47225546.md). In addition, there are [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad), who are indigenous peoples of various animistic and syncretic religions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBureau_of_Democracy,_Human_Rights,_and_Labor2014-213]&#x20;

## Diaspora

Main article: [Overseas Filipinos](2257094.md)

Further information: [Filipinos in the New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_the_New_York_metropolitan_area) and [Filipinos in Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_Hawaii)



Spectators at the annual [Philippine Independence Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independence_Day_Parade) on [Madison Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Avenue) in [Manhattan](45470.md), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_the_New_York_metropolitan_area)

There are currently more than 10 million Filipinos who live overseas. Filipinos form a minority ethnic group in the Americas, Europe, Oceania,[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics-215] the Middle East, and other regions of the world.&#x20;

There are an estimated four million [Americans of Filipino ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_American) in the United States, and more than 300,000 American citizens in the Philippines.[^cite_note-BNP2007-216] According to the U.S. Census Bureau, immigrants from the Philippines made up the second largest group after Mexico that sought [family reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_reunification).[^cite_note-217]&#x20;

Filipinos make up over a third of the entire population of the [Northern Marianas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Marianas_Islands), an American territory in the [North Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_Ocean), and a large proportion of the populations of [Guam](11974.md), Palau, the [British Indian Ocean Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Ocean_Territory), and [Sabah](28678.md).[^cite_note-Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics-215]&#x20;
