**Mashhad** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): مشهد [\[mæʃˈhæd\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/46/Fa-ir-mashhad\_%281%29.ogg/Fa-ir-mashhad\_%281%29.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/46/Fa-ir-mashhad_%281%29.ogg/Fa-ir-mashhad_%281%29.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), historically also known as **Mashad** ([/məˈʃæd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*mə-SHAD*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-britannica-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] **Meshhed**, or **Meshed**,[^cite_note-16] is the [second-most-populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Iranian_cities_by_population) in Iran, located in the north-east of the country[^cite_note-Simigh-17] about 740 kilometres (460 miles) east of [Tehran](57654.md).[^cite_note-18] In the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Mashhad_County\)) of [Mashhad County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_County), it serves as the capital of [Razavi Khorasan](1289401.md) province, the county, and the district.[^cite_note-Khorasan_Province_Structure-19] It has a population of about 3,400,000 (2016 census), which includes the areas of Mashhad Taman and [Torqabeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torqabeh).[^cite_note-20]

Mashhad has been governed by different ethnic groups. A small village, by the 9th century it was known as [Sanabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanabad_\(Mashhad\)) and was located—along with [Tus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tus,_Iran) and other villages—on the ancient [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) connecting them with [Merv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merv) to the east. It outgrew surrounding villages and became known by its current name, meaning "place of [martyrdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrdom)", in reference to the [Imam Reza shrine](14030191.md), where the eighth [Shia Imam](26961.md), [Ali al-Rida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_al-Rida), is buried. The Abbasid caliph [Harun al-Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid) is also buried within the same shrine. The shrine is the most [pilgrimaged](23763.md) site in [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiaism),[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] visited by nearly 30 million each year.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

Mashhad became associated with [Ferdowsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdowsi), the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Persian-language_poets_and_authors) poet and author of the *[Shahnameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahnameh)*, who was born in Tus (with many institutions in Mashhad named after him). Mashhad enjoyed relative prosperity in the [Mongol period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire), and continued to grow. Between 1736 and 1796, Mashhad became the capital of [Afsharid Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_Iran) which was ruled by the [Afsharid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty) founded by [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah), [whose tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Nader_Shah) is located in the city. In the modern era, Mashhad continued to expand and became the hometown of some of the most significant literary figures and artists of modern Iran, such as the poet [Mehdi Akhavan-Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Akhavan-Sales), and the traditional Iranian singer and composer [Mohammad-Reza Shajarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad-Reza_Shajarian). On 30 October 2009 (the anniversary of Imam Reza's martyrdom), Iran's then-President [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) declared Mashhad to be "The spiritual capital of Iran."[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The city was also named the Capital of [Islamic Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_culture) by the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) in 2017.[^cite_note-29]

## History

### Etymology and early history

Ancient Greek sources mention the passage and residence of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in this land, which was called "[Susia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susia)" ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Σούσια), in 330 BC.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] The map of [Tabula Peutingeriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabula_Peutingeriana), which dates back to the early [Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_era), names this city on the west of [Merv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merv), Alexandria, instead of Susia.[^cite_note-34] [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder), says there is a city in the middle of [Parthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia), near [Arsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asaak) and [Nisiaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisa,_Turkmenistan), called "Alexandropolis" after its founder.[^cite_note-35] Many Muslim historians, from the 10th to the 16th century AD, attribute the founding of "[Sanabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanabad_\(Mashhad\))" (the old name of the city) to Alexander.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Also in the [Shia](26961.md) [hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith) sources, which the narrators connect to the 7th to 9th centuries AD, there are quotations that [Imam Ridha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Ridha) and [Harun al-Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid) are buried in a city founded by [Dhu al-Qarnayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhu_al-Qarnayn). [^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

The older name of Mashhad is Sanabad (سناباد *Sanâbâd*). It was eventually renamed to Mashhad during the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran). The name Mashhad comes from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), meaning a [Mazar (mausoleum)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar_\(mausoleum\)).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] It is also known as the place where [Ali ar-Ridha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ar-Ridha) ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), Imam Reza), the [eighth Imam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Imam) of Shia Muslims, died (according to the Shias, was martyred). Reza's shrine was placed there.[^cite_note-45]

The ancient [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) city of [Patigrabanâ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patigrabana), mentioned in the [Behistun inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_inscription) (520 BC) of the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid) Emperor [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great), may have been located at the Mashhad.[^cite_note-46]

At the beginning of the 9th century (3rd century AH), Mashhad was a small city called Sanabad, which was situated 24 kilometres (15 miles) away from [Tus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tus,_Iran). There was a summer palace of [Humayd ibn Qahtaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayd_ibn_Qahtaba), the governor of [Khurasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khurasan). In 808, when [Harun al-Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid), [Abbasid caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_caliph), was passing through to quell the insurrection of [Rafi ibn al-Layth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafi_ibn_al-Layth) in [Transoxania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxania), he became ill and died. He was buried under the palace of Humayd ibn Qahtaba. Thus the Dar al-Imarah was known as the Mausoleum of Haruniyyeh. In 818, Ali al-Ridha was martyred by [al-Ma'mun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ma'mun) and was buried beside the grave of Harun.[^cite_note-47] Although Mashhad owns the cultural heritage of Tus (including its figures like [Nizam al-Mulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_al-Mulk), [Al-Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghazali), [Ahmad Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Ghazali), [Ferdowsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdowsi), [Asadi Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asadi_Tusi), and [Shaykh Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaykh_Tusi)), earlier [Arab](2185.md) geographers have correctly identified Mashhad and Tus as two separate cities that are now located about 19 kilometres (12 miles) from each other.[^cite_note-48]

### Mongolian invasion: Ilkhanids

Although some believe that after this event, the city was called Mashhad al-Ridha (the place of martyrdom of al-Ridha), it seems that Mashhad, as a place-name, first appears in al-Maqdisi, i.e., in the last third of the 10th century. About the middle of the 14th century, the traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) uses the expression "town of Mashhad al-Rida". Towards the end of the Middle Ages, the name Nuqan, which is still found on coins in the first half of the 14th century under the [Il-Khanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-Khanids), seems to have been gradually replaced by al-Mashhad or Mashhad.[^cite_note-49]



[Terken Khatun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terken_Khatun_\(wife_of_Ala_ad-Din_Tekish\)), Empress of the Khwarazmian Empire, known as "the Queen of the Turks", held captive by Mongol army

[Shias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shias) began to make [pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziarat) to his grave. By the end of the 9th century, a dome was built above the grave, and many other buildings and [bazaars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazaars) sprang up around it. Over the course of more than a millennium, it has been destroyed and rebuilt several times.[^cite_note-50] In 1161, however, the [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) seized the city, but they spared the sacred area their pillaging. Mashad al-Ridha was not considered a "great" city until [Mongol raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_raids) in 1220, which caused the destruction of many large cities in Khurasan but leaving Mashhad relatively intact in the hands of Mongolian commanders because of the cemetery of Ali Al-Rezza and Harun al-Rashid (the latter was stolen).[^cite_note-51] Thus the survivors of the massacres migrated to Mashhad.[^cite_note-Zabeth_1999_pp._14-15-52] When the [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) visited the town in 1333, he reported that it was a large town with abundant fruit trees, streams and mills. A great dome of elegant construction surmounts the noble mausoleum, the walls being decorated with colored tiles.[^cite_note-Imam_Reza-9] The most well-known dish cooked in Mashhad, "sholeh Mashhadi" (شله مشهدی) or "Sholeh", dates back to the era of the Mongolian invasion when it is thought to be cooked with any food available (the main ingredients are meat, grains and abundant spices) and be a Mongolian word.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

### Timurid Empire



The map of the Persian Empire in 1747 at the time of Afsharid Dynasty. The name of Mashhad is seen belong Tous.

It seems that the importance of Sanabad-Mashhad continually increased with the growing fame of its sanctuary and the decline of Tus, which received its death-blow in 1389 from [Miran Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miran_Shah), a son of [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur). When the Kartid noble who governed the place rebelled and attempted to make himself independent, Miran Shah was sent against him by his father. Tus was stormed after a siege of several months, sacked and left a heap of ruins; 10,000 inhabitants were massacred. Those who escaped the holocaust settled in the shelter of the 'Alid sanctuary. Tus was henceforth abandoned and Mashhad took its place as the capital of the district.

Later on, during the reign of the [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty) [Shahrukh Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrukh_Mirza), Mashhad became one of the main cities of the realm. In 1418, his wife [Goharshad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goharshad) funded the construction of an outstanding mosque beside the shrine, which is known as the [Goharshad Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goharshad_Mosque).[^cite_note-Zabeth_1999_pp._14-15-52] The mosque remains relatively intact to this date, its great size an indicator to the status the city held in the 15th century.

### Safavid Empire

[Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I), founder of the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty), conquered Mashhad after the death of [Husayn Bayqarah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_Bayqarah) and the decline of the Timurid dynasty. He was later captured by the [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks) during the reign of [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abbas_I). In the 16th century the town suffered considerably from the repeated raids of the Özbegs (Uzbeks). In 1507, it was taken by the troops of the Shaybani or Shabani Khan. After two decades, Shah Tahmasp I succeeded in repelling the enemy from the town again in 1528. But in 1544, the Özbegs again succeeded in entering the town and plundering and murdering there. The year 1589 was a disastrous one for Mashhad. The Shaybanid '[Abd al-Mu'min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Mu'min) after a four months' siege forced the town to surrender. Shah Abbas I, who lived in Mashhad from 1585 until his official ascent of the throne in Qazwin in 1587, was not able to retake Mashhad from the Özbegs until 1598. Mashhad was retaken by the Shah Abbas after a long and hard struggle, defeating the Uzbeks in a great battle near [Herat](14128.md) as well as managing to drive them beyond the [Oxus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxus_River). [Abbas the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_the_Great) wanted to encourage Iranians to go to Mashhad for pilgrimage. He is said to have walked from [Isfahan](223149.md) to Mashhad. During the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) era, Mashhad gained even more religious recognition, becoming the most important city of Greater Khorasan, as several [madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah) and other structures were built beside the [Imam Reza shrine](14030191.md). Besides its religious significance, Mashhad has played an important political role as well. The Safavid dynasty has been criticized in a book (Red Shi'sm vs. Black Shi'ism) on the perceived dual aspects of the Shi'a religion throughout history) as a period in which although the dynasty didn't form the idea of Black Shi'ism, but this idea was formed after the defeat of Shah Ismail against the Ottoman leader Sultan Yavuz Selim. Black Shi'ism is a product of the post-Safavid period.

### Qajar Empire



Mashhad in 1858

Some believe that Mashhad was ruled by [Shahrukh Afshar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrukh_Afshar) and remained the capital of the [Afsharid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty) during [Zand dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zand_dynasty)[^cite_note-55] until [Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan_Qajar) conquered the then larger region of [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) in 1796.[^cite_note-Ghani2001-56]

#### 1912 Imam Reza shrine bombardment

In 1911 Yusuf Khan of Herat was declared independent in Mashhad as Muhammad Ali Shah and brought together a large group of [reactionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactionaries) opposed to the revolution, and keep stirring for some time. This gave Russia the excuse to intervene and 29 March 1912 bombed the city; this bombing killed several people and pilgrims; action against a Muslim shrine caused a great shock to all [Islamic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_countries). On 29 March 1912, the sanctuary of Imam Reza was bombed by the Russian artillery fire, causing some damage, including to the golden dome, resulting in a widespread and persisting resentment in the Shiite Muslim world as well as [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India). This bombing was orchestrated by Prince Aristid Mikhailovich Dabizha (a [Moldovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovans) who was the Russian Consul in Mashhad) and [General Radko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radko_Dimitriev) (a [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_people) who was commander of the [Russian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Cossacks) in the city).[^cite_note-Kazemzadeh2013-57] Yusuf Khan ended up captured by the Persians and was executed.

### Pahlavi dynasty

#### Modernization under Reza Shah



Aerial view of imam Reza shrine, 1976

The modern development of the city accelerated under Reza Shah (1925-1941). Shah Reza Hospital (currently Imam Reza Hospital, affiliated with the [Basij](2892082.md) organization) was founded in 1934; the sugar factory of Abkuh in 1935; and the [Mashhad University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_University_of_Medical_Sciences) in 1939. The city's first power station was installed in 1936, and in 1939, the first urban transport service began with two buses. In this year the first population census was performed, with a result of 76,471 inhabitants.[^cite_note-mums-58]

#### 1935 Goharshad Mosque rebellion

Main article: [Imam Reza shrine rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Reza_shrine_rebellion)

In 1935, a backlash against the modernizing, anti-religious policies of [Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah) erupted in the Mashhad shrine. Responding to a cleric who denounced the Shah's heretical innovations, corruption, and heavy consumer taxes, many bazaars and villagers took refuge in the shrine, chanted slogans such as "The Shah is a new Yazid." For four days local police and army refused to violate the shrine and the standoff was ended when troops from Azerbaijan arrived and broke into the shrine,[^cite_note-59] killing dozens and injuring hundreds, and marking a final rupture between Shi'ite clergy and the Shah.[^cite_note-60] According to some Mashhadi historians, the Goharshad Mosque uprising, which took place in 1935, is an uprising against Reza Shah's decree banning all veils (headscarf and chador) on 8 January 1936.[^cite_note-61]

#### 1941–1979 reforms



[Comprehensive planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_planning) of Mashhad in 1974

Mashhad experienced population growth after the [Anglo-Soviet invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran) of Iran in 1941 because of relative insecurity in rural areas, the 1948 drought, and the establishment of Mashhad University in 1949. At the same time, public transport vehicles increased to 77 buses and 200 taxis and the railway link with the capital, Tehran, was established in 1957. The 1956 census reflected a population of 241,989 people. The increase in population continued in the following years thanks to the increase in Iranian oil revenues, the decline of the feudal social model, the agrarian reform of 1963, the founding of the city's airport, the creation of new factories and the development of the health system. In 1966, the population reached 409,616 inhabitants, and 667,770 in 1976. The extension of the city was expanded from 16 to 33 square kilometres (170,000,000 to 360,000,000 square feet).

During [World War II](32927.md), Polish refugee children were admitted in Mashhad in March 1942 (see also *[Iran–Poland relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Poland_relations)*).[^cite_note-jw-62] After receiving food and medical care at a local hospital, the children were further evacuated to India.[^cite_note-jw-62]



Mashhad Statue Sq. 1960s

In 1965 an important urban renewal development project for the surroundings of the shrine of Imam Reza was proposed by the Iranian architect and urban designer [Dariush Borbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dariush_Borbor) to replace the dilapidated slum conditions which surrounded the historic monuments. The project was officially approved in 1968. In 1977 the surrounding areas were demolished to make way for the implementation of this project. To relocate the demolished businesses, a new bazaar was designed and constructed in Meydan-e Ab square (in Persian, "میدان آب")[^cite_note-mums-58] by Dariush Borbor. After the revolution, the urban renewal project was abandoned.

#### 1994 Imam Reza shrine bombing

On 20 June 1994, a bomb exploded in a prayer hall of the [shrine of the Imam Reza](14030191.md).[^cite_note-63] The bomb that killed at least 25 people on 20 June in Mashhad exploded on [Ashura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Ashura).[^cite_note-64] The [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_people) terrorist [Ramzi Yousef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramzi_Yousef), a [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) Muslim turned [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabi), one of the main perpetrators of the 1993 World Trade Center bombing, was found to be behind the plot.[^cite_note-65]

#### 2026 Iran massacres

On 22 January 2026, during the [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md), 33-year-old Ali Rahbar was reportedly executed in Mashhad, becoming one of the first protesters to be executed.[^cite_note-66] Iran's judiciary denied his execution having taken place, stating that such a person had not even been detained.[^cite_note-67]

## Geography

The city is located at 36.20º North latitude and 59.35º East longitude, in the valley of the [Kashafrud River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashafrud_River) near [Turkmenistan](198149.md), between the two mountain ranges of [Binalood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binalud_Mountains) and [Hezar Masjed Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezar_Masjed_Mountains). The city benefits from the proximity of the mountains, having cool winters, pleasant springs, and mild summers. It is only about 250 km (160 mi) from [Ashgabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashgabat), Turkmenistan.

The city is the administrative center of [Mashhad County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_County) (or the *[shahrestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Iran)* of Mashhad) as well as the somewhat smaller Central District (*[Bakhsh-e Markazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhsh_\(administrative_division\))*) of Mashhad. The city itself, excluding parts of the surrounding *Bakhsh* and *Shahrestan*, is divided into 13 smaller administrative units, with a total population of more than 3 million.[^cite_note-amar.org.ir-68]

### Climate

Mashhad features a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSk*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *BShk*) with very hot summers, cold winters and [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate)-like dry summer precipitation pattern. The city only sees about 250 millimetres (9.8 inches) of precipitation per year, some of which occasionally falls in the form of [snow](28191.md). Mashhad has wetter and drier periods with the bulk of the annual precipitation falling between the months of December and May. Summers are typically hot and dry, with average high temperatures exceeding 33 °C or 91.4 °F for three months. Winters are typically cool to cold and somewhat damper, with overnight lows routinely dropping below freezing. Mashhad enjoys on average just above 2900 hours of sunshine per year.

Snow cover had been observed in 21.1 days annually, with only 3.8 days in which the snow depth exceeds 0.10 metres or 4 inches.[^cite_note-ncei-69]

The highest recorded temperature was 43.8 °C or 110.8 °F on 6 July 1998 and the lowest recorded temperature was −28 °C or −18.4 °F on 3 February 1972.

### Ethnic groups

The vast majority of Mashhadi people are ethnic [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), who form the majority of the city's population. Other [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) include [Kurdish](17068.md) and [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmens) people who have emigrated recently to the city from the [North Khorasan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Khorasan_province). There is also a significant community of non-Arabic speakers of [Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs_in_Khorasan) descent who have retained a distinct Arabian culture, cuisine and religious practices.

There are also over 20 million pilgrims who visit the city every year.[^cite_note-Imam_Reza-9]

### Religion

See also: [Imam Reza shrine](14030191.md)

Today, the holy shrine and its museum hold extensive cultural and artistic treasuries of Iran, in particular manuscript books and paintings. Several [theological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theological) schools are associated with the shrine of the [Eighth Imam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Imam).

The second-largest holy city in the world, Mashhad attracts more than 20 million tourists and pilgrims every year, many of whom come to pay homage to the [Imam Reza shrine](14030191.md) (the eighth [Shi'ite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'ite) Imam). It has been a magnet for travellers since medieval times.[^cite_note-Imam_Reza-9] Thus, even as those who complete the pilgrimage to Mecca receive the title of *Haji*, those who make the pilgrimage to Mashhad—and especially to the Imam Reza shrine—are known as *Mashtee*, a term employed also of its inhabitants. As an important problem, the duration when new passengers stay in Mashhad has been considerably reduced to 2 days and they prefer to finish their trip immediately after doing pilgrimage and shopping in the markets.[^cite_note-83] There are about 3000–5000 unauthorized residential units in Mashhad,[^cite_note-84] which, as a unique statistic worldwide, has caused various problems in the city.

Although mainly inhabited by Muslims, there were in the past some religious minorities in Mashhad. They were mainly Jews, who were forcibly [converted to Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converted_to_Islam) in 1839 after the Allahdad [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) took place for [Mashhadi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhadi_Jews) in 1839.[^cite_note-85] They became known as Jadid al-Islam ("Newcomers in Islam"). On the outside, they adapted to the Islamic way of life, but often secretly kept their faith and traditions.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-Glazebrook_2007_189-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

## Economy



[Turquoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turquoise), one of the products of Mashhad

Mashhad is Iran's second largest automobile production hub. The city's economy is based mainly on dry fruits, salted nuts, saffron, Iranian sweets like gaz and sohaan, and precious stones. According to old writings and documents, the oldest existing carpet attributed to the city belongs to the reign of Shah Abbas ([Abbas I of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_I_of_Persia)). Also, there is a type of carpet, classified as Mashhad Turkbâf, which, as its name suggests, is woven by hand with Turkish knots by craftsmen who emigrated from [Tabriz](439791.md) to Mashhad in the nineteenth century. Among other major industries in the city are the nutrition, clothing, leather, textiles, chemical, steel, metallic, and non-metallic mineral industries, construction materials factories, & the handicraft industry.

With more than 55% of all the hotels in Iran, Mashhad is the hub of tourism in the country. Religious shrines are the most powerful attractions for foreign travelers; 20 to 30 million pilgrims from Iran and more than 2 million pilgrims and tourists from elsewhere around the world came to Mashhad every year as of 2015.[^cite_note-91]

Unemployment, poverty, drug addiction, and theft are the most important social problems of the city.[^cite_note-92]

The [divorce rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce_rate) in Mashhad had increased by 35 percent by 2014.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Khorasan and Mashhad ranked second in violence across the country in 2013.[^cite_note-95]

### Astan Quds Razavi

At the same time, the city has kept its character as a goal of pilgrimage, dominated by the strength of the economic and political authority of the Astan Quds Razavi, the administration of the Shrine waqf, probably the most important in the Muslim world and the largest active [bonyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonyad) in Iran.[^cite_note-Andrew_Higgins-96] The Astan Quds Razavi is a major player in the economy of the city of Mashhad.[^cite_note-Bellaiguep.15-97] The land occupied by the shrine has grown fourfold since 1979 according to the head of the foundation's international relations department. The Shrine of Imam Reza is vaster than Vatican City.[^cite_note-Andrew_Higgins-96] The foundation owns most of the real estate in Mashhad and rents out shop space to bazaaris and hoteliers.[^cite_note-Bellaiguep.15-97] The main resource of the institution is endowments, estimated to have annual revenue of \$210 billion.[^cite_note-Hoover-98] [Ahmad Marvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Marvi) is the current Custodian of Astan Quds Razavi.

The city's International Exhibition Center is the second most active exhibition center after Tehran, which due to proximity to Central Asian countries hosts dozens of international exhibitions each year. Companies such as Smart-innovators in Mashhad are pioneers in electrical and computer technology.

## Language

The language mainly spoken in Mashhad is [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) with a variating Mashhadi accent, which can at times, prove itself as a sort of dialect.[^cite_note-103] The Mashhadi Persian dialect is somewhat different from the standard Persian dialect in some of its tones and stresses.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

## Culture

### Religious seminaries



[Tomb of Ferdowsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Ferdowsi) in [Tous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tous,_Iran)

Long a center of secular and religious learning, Mashhad has been a center for the [Islamic arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_art) and sciences, as well as piety and pilgrimage. Mashhad was an educational centre, with a considerable number of Islamic schools (madrasas, the majority of them, however, dating from the later Safavid period. Mashhad Hawza (Persian: حوزه علمیه مشهد) is one of the largest seminaries of traditional Islamic school of higher learning in Mashhad, which was headed by [Abbas Vaez-Tabasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Vaez-Tabasi) (who was Chairman of the Astan Quds Razavi board from 1979) after the revolution, and in which Iranian politician and clerics such as [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), [Ahmad Alamolhoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Alamolhoda), [Abolghasem Khazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolghasem_Khazali), [Mohammad Reyshahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reyshahri), [Morteza Motahhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morteza_Motahhari), [Abbas Vaez-Tabasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Vaez-Tabasi), and Madmoud Halabi (the founder of [Hojjatieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hojjatieh) and Mohammad Hadi Abd-e Khodaee learned Islamic studies). The number of seminary schools in Mashhad is now thirty nine and there are an estimated 2,300 seminarians in the city.[^cite_note-106]

The [Ferdowsi University of Mashhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdowsi_University_of_Mashhad), named after the Iranian poet, is located here. The [Madrassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrassa) of [Ayatollah Al-Khoei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ayatollah_Abul-Qassim_Khoei), originally built in the seventeenth century, is the city's traditional centre for religious learning. The Razavi University of Islamic Sciences, founded in 1984, stands at the centre of town, within the shrine complex.



[Tomb of Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Nader_Shah)

Mashhad is also home to one of the oldest libraries of the Middle-East called the [Central Library of Astan Quds Razavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Library_of_Astan_Quds_Razavi) with a history of over six centuries. There are some six million historical documents in the foundation's central library. A museum is also home to over 70,000 manuscripts from various historical eras.



Central Museum of great Khorasan, Mashhad, next to [Koohsangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koohsangi)

The [Astan Quds Razavi Central Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astan_Quds_Razavi_Central_Museum), which is part of the Astan-e Quds Razavi Complex, contains [Islamic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_art) and historical artifacts. In 1976, a new edifice was designed and constructed by the Iranian architect [Dariush Borbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dariush_Borbor) to house the museum and the manuscripts.

In 1569 (977 H), 'Imad al-Din Mas'ud Shirazi, a physician at the Mashhad hospital, wrote the earliest Islamic treatise on [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis), one influenced by European medical thought. Kashmar rug is a type of [Persian rug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_rug) indigenous to this region.

During recent years, Mashhad has been a clerical base to monitor the affairs and decisions of state. In 2015, Mashhad's clerics publicly criticized the performance of concert in Mashhad, which led to the order of cancellation of concerts in the city by [Ali Jannati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Jannati), the [minister of culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_culture), and then his resignation on 19 October 2016.

### Newspapers

There are three influential newspapers in Mashhad, Khorasan (خراسان), Qods (قدس) and Shahrara (شهرآرا), which have been considered "conservative newspapers". They are three Mashhad-based daily published by and representing the views of their current and old owners: [Foundation of Martyrs and Veterans Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_Martyrs_and_Veterans_Affairs), [Astan Quds Razavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astan_Quds_Razavi) and Mashhad Municipality, respectively.[^cite_note-bbcm-107]

### Capital of Islamic culture

The [Islamic Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Educational,_Scientific_and_Cultural_Organization) named Mashhad 2017's "cultural capital of the Muslim world" in Asia.[^cite_note-108] Several international events, especially entrepreneurs networking event entitled Entrepreneurs Show 2017, was organized by CODE International in collaboration with Ferdowsi University of Mashhad, Khorasan Science and Technology Park, and city district government of Mashhad.[^cite_note-109]

### Airport



Domestic Flights Terminal of Mashhad International Airport

Mashhad is served by the [Mashhad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_International_Airport), which handles domestic flights to Iranian cities and international flights, mostly to neighbouring [Arab countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world). The airbase serves jointly as a civilian airport and a military airbase.[^cite_note-110] During the [June 2025 Israeli strikes on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_Israeli_strikes_on_Iran), it was reported on 15 June that the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) bombed an [aerial refueling plane](237949.md) at the airport.[^cite_note-111]

The airport is the country's second-busiest after [Tehran Mehrabad Airport](2025041.md) and above Tehran's [Imam Khomeini International Airport](716542.md).[^cite_note-112]

As of 2015 it was connected to 57 destinations and had frequent flights to 30 cities within Iran and 27 destinations in the Central Asia, the Middle East, East Asia and Europe.[^cite_note-113]

### Rail



Mashhad railway station



Locating for Mashhad railway station construction in 1954

[Mashhad railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_railway_station) has [Local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_\(train\)), [Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional), [InterRegio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterRegio), and [InterRegio-Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterRegio-Express) services. The station is owned by [IRI Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Railways) and has daily services from most parts of the country, plus two suburban services. The building was designed by [Heydar Ghiai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Ghiai). Mashhad is connected to three major rail lines: [Tehran](57654.md)-Mashhad, Mashhad-[Bafq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafq) (running south), and Mashhad-[Sarakhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarakhs) at the border with [Turkmenistan](198149.md). Some freight trains continue from Sarakhs towards [Uzbekistan](31853.md) and to [Kazakhstan](16642.md), but have to change bogies because of the difference in [Rail gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_gauge). Cargo and passenger rail services are provided or operated by [RAJA Rail Transportation Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Railways),[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] Joopar Co.,[^cite_note-116] and Fadak Trains Co.[^cite_note-117] A new service from [Nakhchivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhchivan_\(city\)), [Azerbaijan](746.md), to Mashhad, [Iran](14653.md), was launched in December 2016.[^cite_note-118]

In April 2025, Iran and Turkmenistan agreed to start a cross-border passenger train linking Mashhard and Merv, Turkmenistan's capital, with the goal of strengthening economic ties.[^cite_note-119]

### Railway & Subway

Main article: [Mashhad Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_Light_Rail)

Mashhad Urban Railway Corporation (MURCO) is constructing [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_Light_Rail) and [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_Urban_Railway) system for the city of Mashhad which includes four lines with 84.5 kilometres (52.5 miles) length. Mashhad Urban Railway Operation Company (MUROC)[^cite_note-metro.mashhad.ir-120] is responsible for the operation of the lines. The LRT line has been operational since 21 February 2011 with 19.5 kilometres (12.1 miles) length and 22 stations[^cite_note-metro1-121] and is connected to [Mashhad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_International_Airport) from early 2016. The total length of line 1 is 24 kilometers and has 24 stations. the current headway in peak hours is 4.5 minutes.

## Government and politics

### Astan Quds

Astan Quds which controls the shrine- the tourism driver- is a wealthy tax exempt religious/political organization. It is recommended to reduce poverty in city a Bazaar be opened by poor people in a courtyard.[^cite_note-126]

### Members of Parliament

Mashhad's current members of parliament are described as politicians with fundamentalist conservative tendencies, who are mostly the members of [Front of Islamic Revolution Stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_of_Islamic_Revolution_Stability), an Iranian principlist political group. They were elected to the Parliament on 26 February 2016.

### Members of Assembly of Experts

[Mahmoud Hashemi Shahroudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Hashemi_Shahroudi) and [Ahmad Alamolhoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Alamolhoda) are two members of the Iranian Assembly of Experts from Mashhad. Hashemi Shahroudi is currently First Vice-chairman of the [Iranian Assembly of Experts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Assembly_of_Experts).[^cite_note-127] He was the Head of Iran's Judiciary from 1999 until 2009 who upon accepting his position, appointed [Saeed Mortazavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Mortazavi), a well known fundamentalist and controversial figure during President Mahmud Ahmadinejad's reelection, prosecutor general of Iran.[^cite_note-128] He was supported by Mashhad's reformists as the candidate of the Fifth Assembly on 26 February 2016.

### City Council and mayor

Main article: [Islamic City Council of Mashhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_City_Council_of_Mashhad)

In 2013, an Iranian principlist political group, [Front of Islamic Revolution Stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_of_Islamic_Revolution_Stability) (which is partly made up of former ministers of [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) and [Mohammad Taghi Mesbah Yazdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Taghi_Mesbah_Yazdi)),[^cite_note-129] gained a landslide victory in Mashhad City Council,[^cite_note-Khabar_Online2-130] which on 23 September 2013, elected Seyed [Sowlat Mortazavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sowlat_Mortazavi) as mayor, who was former governor of the province of [South Khorasan](1289358.md) and the city of Birjand.[^cite_note-131] The municipality's budget amounted to 9600 billion Toman in 2015.[^cite_note-132]
