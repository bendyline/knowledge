# Self-coup



Cavalry in the streets of [Paris](22989.md), after President [Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) seized dictatorial power in the [1851 French coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_French_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

A **self-coup**, also called an **autocoup** (from [Spanish](26825.md)&#x20;*&#x20;_[autogolpe](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/autogolpe#Spanish)_*) or **coup from the top**, is a form of [coup d'état](25914154.md) in which a political leader uses illegal means to stay in office or to vastly increase their power.[^cite_note-\:18-1] The leader may dissolve or render powerless the national [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) and unlawfully assume [extraordinary powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_powers). Other measures may include annulling the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution), suspending [civil courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_courts), and having the [head of government](37570.md) assume [dictatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator) powers.[^cite_note-UY72-2][^cite_note-atlantic-2020-12-07-3]

From 1946 to the beginning of 2021, an estimated 148 self-coup attempts took place, 110 in [autocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracies) and 38 in [democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracies).[^cite_note-Nakamura-4]
