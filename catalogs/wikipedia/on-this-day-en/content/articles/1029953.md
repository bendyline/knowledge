The **Tunisia national football team** represents [Tunisia](30188.md) in men's international [association football](10568.md) competitions, and is controlled by the [Tunisian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Football_Federation) (TFF). The team competes under the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF) on the continental level, and is affiliated with [FIFA](11049.md) for global competitions. Additionally, the team is a member of the [Union of North African Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAF) (UNAF) and the [Union of Arab Football Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Arab_Football_Associations) (UAFA). The team is colloquially known as *Eagles of [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage)* by fans and the media, with the [bald eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle) serving as its symbol.[^cite_note-3] Their home kit is primarily white and their away kit is red, which is a reference to the [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Tunisia) of the country. The team has qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) seven times and the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) 22 times, and has appeared in four editions of [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) and participated once in the [FIFA Confederation Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup). Since 2001, Tunisia's home stadium is the [Hammadi Agrebi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammadi_Agrebi_Stadium) in [Radès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rad%C3%A8s), Tunis.

Tunisia's first match after independence was against [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team), which they won 4–2. The team qualified for the FIFA World Cup for the first time in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) as the only representative of the continent, and became the first African and Arab team to win a World Cup match by beating [Mexico](808402.md) [3–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_2#Tunisia_vs_Mexico) in the first game. Tunisia holds the most consecutive appearances in the Africa Cup of Nations, with 17, qualifying for every tournament between [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [2025](57061770.md). By winning the [2004 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations), they qualified for the [2005 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) and was eliminated in the group stage. The team qualified twice for the [African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Nations_Championship), winning the title in their first appearance in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_African_Nations_Championship), while also competing in the [FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Arab_Cup) three times, winning the title in the inaugural edition in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Arab_Cup) and finishing second in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Arab_Cup). [Radhi Jaïdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radhi_Ja%C3%AFdi), with 105 international matches, holds the record for the number of matches played for Tunisia while [Issam Jemâa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issam_Jem%C3%A2a), with [36 goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_goals_scored_by_Issam_Jem%C3%A2a), is their all-time highest scorer. Their highest-ever [FIFA Men's World Ranking](719095.md) was 14th in April and May 2018, while their lowest rank was 65th in July 2010.[^cite_note-4]

On 13 October 2025, Tunisia became the first team in history to qualify for the World Cup without conceding a goal, during the [2026 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_Group_H) campaign.

## History

Main article: [History of the Tunisia national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Tunisia_national_football_team)

### 1928–1956: French protectorate of Tunisia and beginning



The Tunisian football team in 1939.

Before independence, an unofficial team was formed in 1928, comprising the best Tunisian players from the [Tunisian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Ligue_Professionnelle_1). The team's first match was on 11 March 1928, against the [France national football B team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_football_B_team); Tunisia lost 8–2.[^cite_note-5] Their next friendlies, against the same team on 23 March 1930 and 26 March 1933, also resulted in heavy defeats: 0–5 and 1–6 respectively.

Tunisia had to wait until 1932 for their first match win: a 1–0 victory over [French Algeria](292341.md).[^cite_note-6] Most of the matches that Tunisia played in the 30s and 40s were against French teams, whether it was French Algeria, the [French military team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Sports_Council) or the France B team, in addition to a match against the [France national team](250197.md) in 1941. Most of these matches were played at the [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_El_Menzah#History) in [Tunis](57659.md).

### 1956–1962: Post independence, First international participation

Tunisia gained [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_independence) from France on 20 March 1956. The [Tunisian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Football_Federation) was founded on 29 March 1957 and the Tunisian team played a match with Austrian club [FC Admira Wacker Mödling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Admira_Wacker_M%C3%B6dling) on 30 December of the same year, winning 4–1. Tunisia became affiliated with [FIFA](11049.md) and the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) in 1960. The independent Tunisia played their first match against [Algeria](1029528.md) on 1 June 1957, in the midst of the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War), in which Tunisia lost 2–1. They played their first official match at the [1957 Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Arab_Games) where they won against [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) 4–3 after scoring the first Tunisian goal in an official competition by [Farzit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahim_Ben_Miled?action=edit\&redlink=1). They won against [Iraq](1039652.md) 4–2 and [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) 4–2 before losing in the final against [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team) 3–1. In 1960, Yugoslavian [Milan Kristić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Kristi%C4%87) became the first foreign manager. The national team qualified for the [1960 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics), their first international event after beating [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_national_football_team), [Morocco](1024865.md) and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team). In the Olympic Games, the team suffered three defeats: against [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 6–1, [Argentina](454699.md) 2–1, and [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) 3–1. [Frane Matošić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frane_Mato%C5%A1i%C4%87) was appointed as the second Yugoslav coach of Tunisia after Kristić led Tunisia to qualify for the Olympics.

### 1962–1978: Golden generation, First World Cup participation



Tunisia at the [1978 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) against Egypt.

In 1962, Tunisia entered the [African Cup of Nations qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) for the first time. The team qualified for the tournament after beating [Morocco](1024865.md) and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), and went on to finish third after beating [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team) in the third-place match. The team won the [1963 Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Arab_Cup), after winning against Syria, [Jordan](1090065.md), Lebanon, and [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team). Tunisia also qualified for the [1963 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and [CAF](715265.md) decided that Tunisia would host the [1965 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), making the final after beating [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia_national_football_team) 4–0 in the opening match in [Stade Chedly Zouiten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Chedly_Zouiten),[^cite_note-7] losing 3–2 to [Ghana](1029620.md) in extra-time of the final.[^cite_note-8] Despite this early success, Tunisia did not enter the Cup of Nations again until 1976, and did not qualify until 1978. In 1973, the team entered the [Palestine Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Palestine_Cup_of_Nations) and won in dominant fashion, winning all six of their matches overcoming Syria, [Egypt](1022953.md), [Palestine](1302455.md), [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_national_football_team) and Iraq, scoring 19 goals. In February 1975, after a short stint with Hungarian manager [André Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Nagy), [Abdelmajid Chetali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelmajid_Chetali) was hired. Tunisia qualified for their [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) debut in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup). Tunisia defeated Mexico 3–1,[^cite_note-9] but were defeated by [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 1–0,[^cite_note-10] and drew scoreless against defending champion [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_national_football_team).

### 1978–1994: Decline and Missing six editions of the AFCON

After their inaugural World Cup, Tunisia experienced a decline. Between 1980 and 1992, the team managed to qualify for only two tournaments: the [1982 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations), and the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics). In both, they were knocked out in the first round. They, however, reached the last round of the [1986 World Cup qualifiers](157231.md) by beating Nigeria before being defeated by [Algeria](1029528.md). Former [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) manager [Jean Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Vincent) was hired but the team was unable to qualify for the [1988 African Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in Morocco after a defeat against Algeria.

The team experienced poor results in the [African Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_All-Africa_Games) with defeats against Cameroon, [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_national_football_team) and [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_football_team). [Taoufik Ben Othman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoufik_Ben_Othman), assistant manager in 1978,[^cite_note-11] improved Tunisia's results, as they qualified for the Olympic Games after surpassing [Morocco](1024865.md) and [Egypt](1022953.md) in the qualifiers. However, he was removed days before the start of the competition after poor results in the [1988 Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Arab_Cup) and failure to win in their matches against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team),[^cite_note-12] Lebanon,[^cite_note-13] Egypt[^cite_note-14] and Iraq,[^cite_note-15] as well as friendlies against [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_national_football_team), [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_national_football_team) and East Germany. Polish manager [Antoni Piechniczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Piechniczek) was temporarily appointed and supervised the team in the first round of 1990 World Cup qualifiers and also in the finals of the Olympic Games; in the latter, Tunisia tied [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team) 0–0[^cite_note-16] and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) 2–2[^cite_note-17] and lost to [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_national_football_team) 4–1.[^cite_note-18] [Mokhtar Tlili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokhtar_Tlili) was appointed manager, however, he still missed the [African Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in 1990 after a defeat to Senegal. Piechniczek returned but still failed to qualify for the 1990 World Cup. Despite missing the [1992 African Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), the federation renewed confidence in him because of the respectable performance he had given in the qualifiers; an early exit from the World Cup qualifiers for [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) contributed to his dismissal after a draw with Morocco. [Youssef Zouaoui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youssef_Zouaoui) replaced him; Tunisia hosted the 1994 African Cup of Nations replacing original hosts [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire), but finished at the bottom of the group, after a 2–0 loss to Mali[^cite_note-19] and a draw with Zaire.[^cite_note-20]

### 1994–2002: Beginning of Resurgence, 1996 AFCON runners-up

[Henryk Kasperczak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Kasperczak) became the new manager after Tunisia hosted the 1994 African Cup of Nations. Tunisia qualified for the [1996 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_African_Cup_of_Nations) and finished second in their group, putting them through to the quarter-finals. Tunisia went on to beat [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals and [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) in the semi-finals,[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] to reach their first major final in 31 years, but lost to host country [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) 2–0.[^cite_note-23] Tunisia reached the quarter-finals of the [1998 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_African_Cup_of_Nations) in the lead of the group with a win over [DR Congo](1030243.md),[^cite_note-24] [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_national_football_team) and a defeat from [Ghana](1029620.md),[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] where they were eliminated in a penalty shootout by host country [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team).[^cite_note-27] The team also qualified for [that year's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) after a 20-year absence: they again failed to advance from the group stages, losing 2–0 to [England](9904.md) and 1–0 to [Colombia](680175.md),[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] and drawing 1–1 with [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team).[^cite_note-30] Kasperczak was sacked and replaced with [Francesco Scoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Scoglio), who guided the team to the [2000 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_African_Cup_of_Nations), where they finished in fourth place after losing to [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-31] The following year, Scoglio departed to rejoin [Genoa CFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_CFC), sparking a period of severe instability. [Eckhard Krautzun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckhard_Krautzun) initially took over and guided the team to a second [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) appearance, but then resigned, citing interference from the [Tunisian FA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Football_Federation) with his coaching. [Henri Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Michel) replaced him, but was sacked when Tunisia crashed out of the [2002 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations) without scoring a single goal after scoreless draws with [Senegal](1017436.md) and Zambia and a defeat from Egypt. Finally, [Ammar Souayah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammar_Souayah) took over in time for the 2002 World Cup; the team drew in friendlies with Norway and South Korea and were defeated by Denmark and Slovenia. In the finals, Tunisia exited the tournament in the group stage, drawing 1–1 with [Belgium](679716.md), losing 2–0 to [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) and co-hosts [Japan](993546.md),[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] prompting a search for a new manager.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### 2002–2008: Roger Lemerre era, 2004 AFCON champions



Tunisia against [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in a friendly game at the [7 November Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammadi_Agrebi_Stadium) in 2009.

In September 2002, the [Tunisian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Football_Federation) announced that it was finalizing a contract with former France manager [Roger Lemerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lemerre). Tunisia hosted the [2004 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations), winning the group.[^cite_note-38] They defeated Senegal in the quarter-finals,[^cite_note-39] and Nigeria in the semi-finals. Tunisia built a 1–0 lead after four minutes with [Mehdi Nafti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Nafti)'s concentration pushed by [Francileudo Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francileudo_Santos), before Morocco levelled. Tunisia restored their lead,[^cite_note-40] giving them their first [African Cup of Nations](250680.md) title. They also won the CAF's African National Team of the Year award. Lemerre became the first manager to win two different continental tournaments, having previously won [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000) with France. As a result, Tunisia qualified for the [2005 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in Germany, playing the hosts, [Argentina](454699.md) and [Australia](4792249.md). The only points they would win was a victory over Australia.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Before their 2006 World Cup appearance, Lemerre took the Tunisians to a training camp in Switzerland, where they played international friendlies against Swiss clubs. Tunisia would only record one draw in Germany, against Saudi Arabia, losing against [Spain](609825.md) and [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team).[^cite_note-44] [Hatem Trabelsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatem_Trabelsi) announced his retirement from international football after eight years,[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] and Lemerre led Tunisia to the 2008 African Cup of Nations. Tunisia won their 2008 AFCON group after a draw in the opening match against Senegal 2–2, a 3–1 victory over South Africa, and a goalless draw against Angola. They then lost against [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) 3–2 in extra time.[^cite_note-47]

### 2008–2014: Disappointments and missing the World Cup



Tunisia against Ivory Coast at the [2013 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in South Africa.

Portuguese [Humberto Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberto_Coelho) was appointed as the new manager on 3 June 2008. Coelho would fail to qualify for the 2010 World Cup. [Faouzi Benzarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faouzi_Benzarti) was appointed as the new manager, and was also sacked after Tunisia were eliminated from the group stage in the [2010 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). In June 2010, [Bertrand Marchand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Marchand) was appointed manager for a two-year contract. After a series of horrendous results, Tunisia fell to 65th in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings), the worst in its history. [Sami Trabelsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_Trabelsi) was appointed, and the team qualified for the [2012 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), where they were eliminated in the quarter-finals after a defeat by [Ghana](1029620.md).

In the [2013 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Tunisia snatched a late winner against [Algeria](1029528.md), before a defeat by [Ivory Coast](1039856.md), 3–0. The last match ended with a 1–1 draw against [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_national_football_team). In February 2013, [Nabil Maâloul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabil_Ma%C3%A2loul) replaced Sami Trabelsi; in their first two [2014 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) matches, Tunisia beat Sierra Leone 2–1 and clinched a 2–2 draw in [Freetown](56661.md). On 16 June, during the fifth round of the group stage, Tunisia tied 1–1 against Equatorial Guinea. A 2–0 loss to Cape Verde on 7 September all but eliminated Tunisia; however, Tunisia advanced after FIFA disqualified Cape Verde for cheating. They would then be knocked out by Cameroon.

### 2014–2022: Renaissance and two World Cup participations



Tunisia against Belgium at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia.



Tunisia against Mali at the [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in Cameroon. The match stopped at the 85th minute.



Tunisia against Denmark at the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar.

Belgian manager [Georges Leekens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Leekens) was appointed in early 2014; early results included a 1–1 draw against [Colombia](680175.md) and a 1–0 win over [South Korea](1018627.md),[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] both in friendly matches. Under Leekens, the team climbed from 49th to 22nd in the FIFA rankings. Tunisia qualified for the [2015 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_African_Cup_of_Nations), and topped their group for the first time since 2008, winning against [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) and drawing with [Cape Verde](1205575.md) and [DR Congo](1030243.md).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] They were eliminated in the quarter-finals after a defeat to host [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team).[^cite_note-53] In July 2015, [Henryk Kasperczak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Kasperczak) returned as manager after 17 years. He managed to qualify the team for the [2017 African Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and reached the quarter-finals of the competition after beating Algeria and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team),[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] before losing against [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team).[^cite_note-56]

On 27 April 2017, [Nabil Maâloul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabil_Ma%C3%A2loul) returned as manager despite the disapproval of the Tunisian supporters following the failure at the 2014 World Cup qualifiers, but this time he qualified Tunisia for the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia. Tunisia's qualification for the World Cup and its results in friendlies against [Iran](272865.md) and [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team),[^cite_note-57] led to its rise to 14th place in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings), their best ever. Before the World Cup, Tunisia drew with [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) and [Portugal](576328.md),[^cite_note-58] in addition to a narrow defeat against Spain 1–0.[^cite_note-59] Despite this, in the World Cup, Tunisia were once again eliminated from the group stage. In the first match,[^cite_note-60] [England](9904.md) won 2–1.[^cite_note-61] [Belgium](679716.md) defeated the North Africans 5–2,[^cite_note-62] and in Tunisia's last game against [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team),[^cite_note-63] the Arab nation won 2–1.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Tunisia qualified for the [2019 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) with new manager [Alain Giresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Giresse); the new manager would only record three ties, against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team), and [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania_national_football_team) to qualify for the round 16. They eventually would win against [Ghana](1029620.md), and [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_national_football_team) 3–0 in the quarter-finals, to qualify for the semi-finals for the first time in 15 years, losing to [Senegal](1017436.md) 1–0 in extra time. In September 2021, the national team began its [2022 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) campaign with three consecutive victories against Equatorial Guinea, Zambia,[^cite_note-66] and Mauritania; they would then draw Mauritania 0–0 and lose against Equatorial Guinea 1–0, to advance for the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_third_round) on the top of [the group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_second_round#Group_B).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

Qatar hosted the [2021 FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Arab_Cup); Tunisia started with a 5–1 win against Mauritania.[^cite_note-69] They then suffered an unexpected defeat to [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team),[^cite_note-70] before winning against the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team).[^cite_note-71] In the quarter-finals Tunisia won against [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) 2–1, and scored a 95th minute winner against Egypt in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-72] Tunisia faced [Algeria](1029528.md) in the final, losing 2–0.[^cite_note-73] The [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) was postponed to early 2022; in the group stage, Tunisia began with a 1–0 defeat against [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team), with Zambian referee [Janny Sikazwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janny_Sikazwe) ending the match in the 85th minute.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] In the second match, the team achieved a 4–0 victory over Mauritania,[^cite_note-76] and lost against [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia_national_football_team) in the last group match. Tunisia defeated [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) in the round of 16, and were eliminated by Burkina Faso.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

In March, Tunisia qualified for their sixth World Cup, the [2022 tournament](17742072.md) in Qatar, the first hosted by an Arab nation, after beating Mali 1–0 on aggregate, taking revenge for the earlier loss in the AFCON.[^cite_note-79] They then defeated [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) and [Japan](993546.md) to win the [2022 Kirin Cup Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kirin_Cup_Soccer) title for the first time,[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] and [Ferjani Sassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferjani_Sassi) was named the best player of the tournament while his compatriot [Issam Jebali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issam_Jebali) finished as the top scorer with two goals.[^cite_note-82] Tunisia played two pre-World Cup friendlies in France in September 2022, defeating Comoros 1−0 in [Croissy-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croissy-sur-Seine)[^cite_note-83] and losing 5−1 to Brazil at the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes) in Paris.[^cite_note-84] With the exception of Brazil, Tunisia's relatively good forms increased confidence on the side to break the knockout stage taboo as Tunisia found themselves grouped with world champions [France](250197.md), European dark horse [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and Asian minnows [Australia](4792249.md).[^cite_note-85] In [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D), Tunisia drew Euro 2020 semi-finalists Denmark 0−0 in a rather decent display by the African side.[^cite_note-86] But a 0−1 loss against Australia followed, severely hampering Tunisia's odds to progress. A 1−0 victory over France courtesy of a goal from [Wahbi Khazri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahbi_Khazri) was not enough to seal Tunisia's place in the last 16 as Australia's [Mathew Leckie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_Leckie) goal against Denmark meant the Asian representative placed second.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

### 2023–present: Difficulties and decline

After the World Cup, the federation rejected Kadri's resignation and extended his contract until 2024.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] The team topped their [2023 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) group after defeating [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) twice, [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana_national_football_team) 3–0, and a loss against [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team) 0–1. The team's performances began to decline, especially after the [Tunisian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Football_Federation) president, Wadie Jary, was imprisoned after accusations of financial corruption were proven.[^cite_note-91] On 17 November 2023, the team started [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification](60000294.md) with two victories against [São Tomé and Príncipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Tom%C3%A9_and_Pr%C3%ADncipe_national_football_team) 4–0 and [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team) 1–0.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] At the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Tunisia was drawn alongside [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team), [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) and [South Africa](1018598.md).[^cite_note-94]

The team endured a poor performance at the tournament, as they lost to Namibia 0–1, before drawing their latter group matches against Mali 1–1 and South Africa 0–0, being eliminated from the group stage. Kadri was dismissed and [Montasser Louhichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montasser_Louhichi) was appointed in his place temporarily. In March, the team participated in the Egyptian Capital Cup, a friendly tournament affiliated with the [2024 FIFA Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIFA_Series#Egypt_\(ACUD_Cup\)).[^cite_note-95] The team played in the semi-final against [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) and, after a 0–0 draw, lost on penalties 4–5.[^cite_note-96] In the third-place match, the team faced [New Zealand](1023035.md); the match also ended 0–0, which ended with a 4–2 penalty shootout victory and a third-place finish for Tunisia.[^cite_note-97] Louhichi continued to lead the team in the third and fourth rounds of the World Cup qualifiers.[^cite_note-98] On 1 July, [Faouzi Benzarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faouzi_Benzarti) was appointed coach for the fourth time.[^cite_note-99] Under Benzarti's leadership, the team started [2025 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) with two wins against [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_national_football_team) 1–0 and [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia_national_football_team) 2–1.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Tunisia then suffered a defeat at home against [Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_national_football_team) 0–1 and drew them away 1–1,[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] prompting Benzati's resignation before being replaced by [Kais Yaâkoubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kais_Ya%C3%A2koubi), who led Tunisia to the AFCON.

On February 10, 2025, [Sami Trabelsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_Trabelsi) returned to the team as coach twelve years after his departure, leading the team to qualifying for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] On October 13, 2025, Tunisia became the first team in history to qualify for the World Cup without conceding a goal, during the [2026 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_Group_H) campaign.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] On November 18, at [Stade Pierre-Mauroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Pierre-Mauroy) in [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), Tunisia achieved a historic 1–1 draw against [Brazil](149286.md) in front of 40,000 spectators.[^cite_note-108] The team entered the [2025 FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Arab_Cup) in Qatar missing key players due to their European clubs refusing to release them and conflicting schedule to the also upcoming [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md), resulting in their elimination from the group stage after a loss, a draw, and a win.[^cite_note-109] On June 15, 2026, Tunisia lost 5-1 to Sweden in their first group match of the 2026 FIFA World Cup,[^cite_note-110] and subsequently lost 4-0 to Japan in their second group game, eliminating them from the tournament.[^cite_note-111]

## Home stadium

From 1956, the year of independence, until 1967, the 18,000-seat [Chedly Zouiten Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chedly_Zouiten_Stadium) was the Tunisian National Stadium.[^cite_note-worldstadiums.com2-112] The stadium hosted matches of the [1965 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations), including the final between Tunisia and [Ghana](1029620.md), and matches from the [1994 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_African_Cup_of_Nations). In 1967, the [El Menzah Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Menzah_Stadium), with a capacity of 39,858, was opened; it was built to host the [1967 Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Mediterranean_Games). Tunisia's first match at the stadium was played on 8 September 1967 against [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team). On 11 December 1977, the stadium witnessed Tunisia qualifying for the World Cup for the first time in its history. It was completely renovated for the 1994 African Cup of Nations, and later hosted some matches for the [2004 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations). Tunisia's last match at El Menzah was on 4 January 2017 against [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team),[^cite_note-113] and the stadium has been undergoing renovations since 2022.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

In 2001, [Hammadi Agrebi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammadi_Agrebi_Stadium) (opened as 7 November Stadium) was inaugurated as Tunisia's national stadium ahead of the [2001 Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Mediterranean_Games). Located in the sports city of Radès in [Radès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rad%C3%A8s), in the southern suburb of the city [Tunis](57659.md), has an all-seater capacity of 60,000 as the largest stadium in the country. Tunisia's first match at the stadium was on 21 August 2002 against [France](250197.md). In 2004, the stadium hosted Tunisia's six matches during the 2004 African Cup of Nations, which the team won for the first time after defeating [Morocco](1024865.md) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_final). The stadium is considered the team's current stronghold, as they haven't played any matches away from it since 2017.

The Tunisians often host their matches at the [Mustapha Ben Jannet Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustapha_Ben_Jannet_Stadium) in [Monastir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastir,_Tunisia) which has a capacity of 20,000. In addition, there are many other venues that host games, such as the [Sousse Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sousse_Olympic_Stadium), which hosted a friendly match between Tunisia and [Switzerland](681862.md) on 14 November 2012 and also hosted a match in the [2012 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification).[^cite_note-116] On 15 November 2016, The team played their first match at the [Gabès Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gab%C3%A8s_Municipal_Stadium) against [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania_national_football_team).[^cite_note-117]

## Rivalries

Main articles: [Algeria–Tunisia football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Tunisia_football_rivalry) and [Egypt–Tunisia football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Tunisia_football_rivalry)



Tunisia against [Algeria](1029528.md) at the [2013 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in South Africa.

Tunisia's main football rivals are its neighbours Algeria, Morocco, and Egypt, with which it shares close cultural and political relations. Tunisia have played 45 games against [Algeria](1029528.md). After the [independence of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War), a friendly match took place at the [Stade Chedly Zouiten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Chedly_Zouiten).[^cite_note-118] The teams also met three times in the [qualifying phase of the World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) and [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)). The overall record slightly favours the Algerians with 16 wins, 14 draws and 14 losses. Algeria and Tunisia have played three times in official competitions: twice in the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md), in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations),[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] which Tunisia won both times, and once in the [FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Arab_Cup) in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Arab_Cup), which Algeria won.[^cite_note-121] Egypt and Tunisia have met 35 times in both official and friendly matches. Tunisia have won 16 matches against Egypt's 11, while 8 matches have ended in a draw.[^cite_note-122] Tunisia and [Morocco](1024865.md) have played 50 games since their independence from France in 1956.[^cite_note-123] Tunisia first played Morocco in [1962 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) on 30 October 1960 in [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca).[^cite_note-124] Many matches were played in [World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) as they met in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), [1970](157227.md), [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), [1990](157232.md), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) qualifying.[^cite_note-125] They also met four times in the African Cup of Nations. Two of them ended in a draw in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_African_Cup_of_Nations) and the other two matches ended up as a Tunisian win in the [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) AFCONs.[^cite_note-126]

## Honours

Main article: [Tunisia national football team honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia_national_football_team_honours)
