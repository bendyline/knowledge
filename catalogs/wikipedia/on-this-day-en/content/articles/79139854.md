[Local elections in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_elections_in_the_United_Kingdom) were held on 7 May 2026 for 5,066 English councillors[^cite_note-\:2-1] for 136 [English local authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_local_authorities)[^cite_note-auto3-3] ([all 32 London borough councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_London_local_elections), 32 [metropolitan boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_borough), 18 [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England), 6 [county councils](28319096.md), 48 [district councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district)) and six [directly elected mayors in England](598892.md). Most of these seats in England were last up for election in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_Kingdom_local_elections). Some of these elections were [postponed from 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_Kingdom_local_elections#Elections_delayed_to_2026).[^cite_note-\:22-7][^cite_note-\:32-8]

No local elections were held in the rest of the United Kingdom, other than two by-elections in Wales. The [2026 Scottish Parliament election](65934215.md) and [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md) were held the same day.[^cite_note-starmersparty-9] The results were a success for both [Reform UK](59757202.md) and the [Green Party](79787.md) (which had the largest increase in vote share), although Reform's vote share dropped since the previous election and the Greens underperformed [opinion polls](#Opinion_polls) while Labour overperformed prior seat projections, and the vote projections showed a slight recovery for the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)). The [Labour Party](19279158.md) continued their collapse in vote share, with little change for the [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)).The elections resulted in Reform UK gaining 1,453 councillors, while the Green Party won 587 seats, its highest number of councillors at the time.[^cite_note-10]

Polling expert [John Curtice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Curtice) noted that these results further confirmed the fragmentation of the United Kingdom's politics, as none of the political parties had the significant backing of the public; according to [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News) and the [BBC](19344654.md), [Reform UK](59757202.md) won 26% to 27% of the vote, while most of the other major parties hovered around 20% to 16% of the vote. Reform UK did well in areas with high [Brexit](41688778.md) support. [Labour](19279158.md) lost 1,498 councillors and lost control of 38 councils, triggering [an internal leadership crisis](83157483.md) that culminated in [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) announcing his resignation as [Prime Minister](24150.md).

## Background

The [English Devolution White Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Devolution_Bill#White_Paper) on 16 December 2024 set out the [Labour government](77280613.md)'s plans for [local government reorganisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England), involving the remaining [two-tier counties of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_two-tier_counties_of_England) being abolished with elections to new [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England). Some of the elections scheduled for May 2025 were delayed by a year to allow reorganisation to take place.[^cite_note-\:22-7][^cite_note-AutoZP-1-11] At least 13 of the 21 county councils asked the government to delay their elections.[^cite_note-AutoZP-2-12] On 5 February 2025, the government announced that elections to nine councils (seven county councils and two unitary authorities) would not take place in 2025 to allow restructuring to take place, with elections to reformed or newly created replacement authorities taking place in 2026.[^cite_note-\:32-8]

By November 2025, it had been announced that [Surrey County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey_County_Council) and the districts included in it would be replaced by new unitary authorities, but the government said that other initially-scheduled 2025 elections would take place in the existing local government structure unless there was "strong justification otherwise", with the process of creating new unitary authorities delayed.[^cite_note-AutoZP-3-13][^cite_note-AutoZP-4-14] Four new combined authority mayoral elections — [Greater Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Greater_Essex_mayoral_election), [Hampshire and the Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Hampshire_and_the_Solent_mayoral_election), [Norfolk and Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Norfolk_and_Suffolk_mayoral_election), and [Sussex and Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Sussex_and_Brighton_mayoral_election) — were delayed to 2028, having been originally scheduled for 2026.[^cite_note-independent_2877661-15]

In December 2025, the [Labour](19279158.md) government invited 63 councils to raise capacity concerns with ongoing [local government reorganisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England) and request a postponement of their 2026 local elections, after also postponing 6 combined authority mayoral elections that were scheduled to occur on the same day. This move prompted criticism from the [Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Commission_\(United_Kingdom\)), which questioned the credibility of the reasoning given and said that it caused "unprecedented" uncertainty. The Commission stated that "There is a clear conflict of interest in asking existing Councils to decide how long it will be before they are answerable to voters". Opposition parties also criticised the decision, accusing Labour of denying people the right to vote.[^cite_note-AutoZP-5-16] By February 2026, the government confirmed that 30 of the 63 council elections had been postponed.[^cite_note-AutoZP-6-17][^cite_note-AutoZP-7-18][^cite_note-AutoZP-8-19] However, following a legal challenge by [Reform UK](59757202.md), which had made major gains in [the previous local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_Kingdom_local_elections), the government withdrew on 16 February 2026 its plans to delay elections, after receiving legal advice that the delay could be unlawful; all scheduled elections for 2026, along with the delayed elections from 2025, were now to take place.[^cite_note-AutoZP-9-20]

This is the second set of local elections during the premiership of Prime Minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md), and it occurred during a time of Reform UK and the Greens rising sharply in opinion polls. It is the first set of local elections to take place after [Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski) became leader of the [Green Party](79787.md).[^cite_note-\:3-21]

In England, Labour were defending more than 2,500 seats, the Conservatives over 1,300 and the Liberal Democrats just under 700.[^cite_note-AutoZP-10-22]

Over 25,000 candidates were nominated to stand in the elections. Reform UK, Labour, the Conservatives and the Green party all stood candidates in over 95% of wards, while the Liberal Democrats had candidates in 86%. The only other party with candidates in more than 2% of wards was the [Trade Unionist and Socialist Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_Unionist_and_Socialist_Coalition), with 9%.[^cite_note-AutoZP-11-23]

### Labour

On 20 March 2026,[Sharon Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Graham) (the General Secretary of [Unite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unite_the_Union)) said in a speech to refuse workers at a protest near a waste depot in [Tyseley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyseley), Birmingham that Labour would be "decimated" in the upcoming local elections and should "hang their heads in shame" over its handling of the [Birmingham bin strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_bin_strike).[^cite_note-AutoZP-12-24]

On 30 March 2026, Keir Starmer launched Labour's local election campaign at [City of Wolverhampton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Wolverhampton_College), West Midlands. According to ITV, [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Wolverhampton_Council) was at the time one of Labour's safest councils in the region and Keir Starmer denied [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Birmingham_City_Council_election) was a "lost cause".[^cite_note-AutoZP-13-25] The BBC said Labour was going into the elections with consistently low [poll ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_next_United_Kingdom_general_election).[^cite_note-AutoZP-14-26]

### Conservatives

On 19 March 2026, [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch) launched the Conservative campaign for the local elections at an event at [Sinfonia Smith Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinfonia_Smith_Square) in [Westminster](37432.md), London.[^cite_note-bbc_7959959-27][^cite_note-AutoZP-15-28]

On 6 May 2026, Kemi Badenoch declined to rule out local pacts with Reform in an interview with [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News), suggesting she would be "happy" to see Conservative and Reform councillors collaborating to deliver right wing policies at a local level. On 7 May 2026, she rowed back on this in an interview with the [The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_\(United_Kingdom\)), saying "We're not doing deals with Reform. I don't want to see us helping Reform... they're not serious."[^cite_note-AutoZP-16-29]

### Liberal Democrats

On 24 March 2026, [Ed Davey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Davey) launched the [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) campaign for the local elections at an event in Lovelace Lodge, in [East Horsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Horsley), Surrey.[^cite_note-AutoZP-17-30][^cite_note-getsurrey_33656656-31]

### Reform UK

On 1 January 2026, [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) announced he wanted to go "double or quits" by planning to spend more than £5 million over the following four months in the run-up to the local elections, saying he wanted to spend "every single penny in the bank account" on a mass direct mail and social media campaign. He called the year's set of local elections the "single most important event" before the [next general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_UK_general_election). In August 2025, Reform UK received a £9 million donation from [Christopher Harborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Harborne), a British–Thai billionaire businessman.[^cite_note-timesandstar_25734041-32]

On 10 March 2026, Farage launched Reform's local election campaign at a live-streamed event with 1,500 supporters at GG's restaurant, in [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Isle_of_Wight), Isle of Wight. This was the first in a planned nationwide series of rallies.[^cite_note-AutoZP-18-33]

### Green Party

Following the election of [Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski) as [Green Party](79787.md) leader, the party rose sharply in popularity polls.[^cite_note-\:0-34][^cite_note-\:1-35]

The Green Party launched its local elections campaign on 9 April 2026.[^cite_note-Green_campaign-36] The party stated its desire to build and maintain council housing and social homes, pointing to Green-led councils in Lewes and Mid Suffolk, which built hundreds of new council homes in previous years.[^cite_note-Green_campaign-36][^cite_note-Green_campaign,_BBC-37] It also vowed to put pressure on the Labour Party to introduce rent controls and abolish the leasehold system, "freeing five million people from the financial burden of service charges".[^cite_note-Green_campaign,_BBC-37]

### Restore Britain

On 1 April 2026, [Rupert Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Lowe) announced [Restore Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restore_Britain) would not stand candidates for the local elections except in the [Great Yarmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Yarmouth) area of the [2026 Norfolk County Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Norfolk_County_Council_election) through [Great Yarmouth First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Yarmouth_First), its local affiliate party, stating the election would serve as a testing ground for future Restore Britain candidacies.[^cite_note-AutoZP-20-38]

### Independents

Dissatisfied over the Labour government's [position on the Gaza war](76740255.md), a large number of [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_United_Kingdom) candidates stood as independents, campaigning exclusively on [pro-Gaza sentiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-39] Many among them were endorsed by the [Your Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_Party_\(UK\))[^cite_note-40] and [The Muslim Vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Muslim_Vote).[^cite_note-41] In Birmingham, significant controversy erupted over the candidature of Shahid Butt, a [British Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pakistanis) who had been convicted in a plot of bombing the [British consulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom%E2%80%93Yemen_relations), an [Anglican Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Yemen) and a Swiss-owned hotel in [Yemen](350939.md) in 1999,[^cite_note-42] who campaigned on an explicitly [new antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_antisemitism) platform.[^cite_note-43]

## Results



Candidates and party agents watching the count for the 7 May 2026 Camden Council elections in Highgate ward

### Overview

With all 136 councils being declared, Reform UK took 1,453 councillor positions, an increase of 1,451 seats. Labour took 1,068 seats, a decrease of 1,496 seats. The Tories took 801 seats, a decrease of 563 seats. The Liberal Democrats took 844 seats, an increase of 155. The Green Party took 587 seats, an increase of 411. Independent-orientated candidates won 212 seats, up 34 seats. The Residents' Association won 36 seats, a decrease of 31.[^cite_note-\:5-44]

Labour lost control of 38 councils, Reform UK gained 14 councils, Conservatives lost 6 councils, the Liberal Democrats gained 1 council, Green gained 5 councils, and 23 councils switched to being in a state of no overall control by a political party.[^cite_note-\:5-44]

### Metropolitan boroughs

There are thirty-six metropolitan boroughs, which are single-tier local authorities. Thirty-two of them had an election in 2026 ([Doncaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Doncaster), [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_City_Council), [Wirral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirral_Council) and [Rotherham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotherham_Metropolitan_Borough_Council) do not). Of these, [Birmingham City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_Council) and [St Helens Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Helens_Council) hold their elections on a four-year cycle from 2022, so were due to hold an election in 2026. In 2025 [Barnsley Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnsley_Council) held a public consultation regarding the permanent adoption of the whole council election cycle, with the result that Barnsley will hold its elections on a four-year cycle starting from 2026.[^cite_note-moderngov_125359-47]

The remaining twenty-nine councils generally elect a third of their councillors every year for three years with no election in each fourth year, on the same timetable which included elections in 2026. Thirteen of these metropolitan borough councils had all of their councillors up for election in 2026 rather than the usual one-third, following ward boundary changes from their [LGBCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBCE) electoral review. All thirteen will likely be reverting to thirds in 2027, 2028 and 2030.

### Other unitary authorities

Most of these [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) elect councillors in thirds, with councillors elected in 2022 up for reelection in 2026. Swindon and Milton Keynes elect councillors by thirds, but had all seats up in 2026 due to new ward boundaries. Thurrock and Isle of Wight both had all-up elections delayed from 2025. East Surrey and West Surrey are both newly-created councils with all councillors to be elected.

## Analysis

### Fragmentation

[Psephologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psephology) [John Curtice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Curtice) said the results "confirmed the fragmentation of our politics" and that the projection was that Reform UK was ahead of the rest of parties and that each received similar levels of support; he noted that the results were "bleak" for Labour and the political parties do not have the "support of a substantial section of the public".[^cite_note-AutoZP-31-70] The [Projected National Share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projected_National_Share) was calculated to show the support of the parties nationwide.[^cite_note-AutoZP-32-71]

According to [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News)'s 2026 National Equivalent Vote, Reform UK won 27% of the vote, the Conservatives won 20%, Labour won 15%, the Greens and Liberal Democrats each won 14%, and [independents](2172378.md) won 10%. Sky News added that these results were an improvement for the Conservatives from the previous year's local elections, while Reform "slid backwards slightly".[^cite_note-\:6-72] If applied to the House of Commons, it was projected that Reform UK would have 284 seats, Labour 110, Conservatives 96, Liberal Democrats 80, SNP 36, Plaid Cymru 13, Green 13, and others 18.[^cite_note-\:6-72] The BBC's Projected National Vote, a similar projection, put party support at Reform with 26%, Greens 18%, Labour 17%, Conservatives 17% and Liberal Democrats 16%.[^cite_note-pnv-4]

### Performances

Reform UK performed significantly well in areas where the vote for [Brexit](41688778.md) was high; in areas where Brexit reached over 55% support, Reform UK won 47% of the vote, while they won 36% in areas between 45–55%, and 19% in areas where support for Brexit dipped below 45%.[^cite_note-\:6-72] Reform took many seats from Labour in their [heartlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wall_\(British_politics\)).[^cite_note-AutoZP-33-73] In the case of the Conservatives, they lost the most support in areas that strongly supported them in elections beforehand, losing 12% in areas that gave them 35% of the vote share.[^cite_note-\:6-72]

The Conservatives performed better than expected, even when suffering extensive losses, which led to [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch) claiming that the Conservatives are "coming back".[^cite_note-\:7-74] The BBC reported that there was no pressure to challenge Badenoch within leadership, even with these losses, and she also said that "good strategy takes time."[^cite_note-AutoZP-34-75] [Tony Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Travers), a local government expert at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics), said that the "Tories have done surprisingly well" in places such [Bexley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bexley_London_Borough_Council_elections), [Westminster](37432.md), and [Wandsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandsworth).[^cite_note-\:7-74] Political analyst [Peter Kellner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kellner) also noted the Conservatives had a loss rate of 68% in [2025's elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_Kingdom_local_elections), compared to 44% in 2026's elections.[^cite_note-\:7-74] Political commentor Henry Hill pushed back against these interpretations, saying the Conservative Party is still going backward and "its councillor base is being eviscerated."[^cite_note-\:7-74] The Conservative Party saw its fifth consecutive election where it lost seats.&#x20;

The Labour Party suffered large losses in the councils that they controlled.[^cite_note-thenorthernecho_26088274-76] In [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_London_Built-up_Area), Labour performed particularly poorly.[^cite_note-AutoZP-35-77] Labour lost control of [Lambeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambeth_London_Borough_Council) and [Lewisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewisham_London_Borough_Council_elections) to the [Green Party of England and Wales](79787.md).[^cite_note-AutoZP-36-78] In [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England), Labour lost seats to [Reform UK](59757202.md).[^cite_note-AutoZP-37-79]

A record number of 466 Muslim candidates were elected to local government positions during these elections. Although most of them came from the Labour Party (199), the party in overall performed poorly in areas like [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), [Blackburn with Darwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_with_Darwen), [Tower Hamlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Tower_Hamlets), [Newham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Newham) and [Redbrige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Redbridge) that house large Muslim populations,[^cite_note-80] where Muslim independents and the Green Party significantly eroded Labour's Muslim support base due to their [singular-minded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-issue_politics) and [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_during_the_Gaza_war) campaign on the Gaza war ([with many Green Party candidates making antisemitic statements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_Green_Party_of_England_and_Wales#2026_allegations_and_responses) in the backdrop of [widespread antisemitic violence in London](83012228.md)),[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] in addition to [Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamism_in_the_United_Kingdom) policy platforms like funding reconstruction in Gaza, [boycotting](43473050.md) Israeli technology, restricting the display of the [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack) in Muslim-majority areas (as a fallback from a [controversy in the previous year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Raise_the_Colours)), criminalising [depiction of Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depictions_of_Muhammad) & [desecration of the Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_desecration) ‐ a phenomenon described by [Policy Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policy_Exchange) as 'Islamopopulism'.[^cite_note-83]

## Aftermath

See also: [2026 State Opening of Parliament](81870023.md)

### Labour government

Main article: [2026 Labour Party leadership crisis](83157483.md)

Asked whether he would resign, Keir Starmer called the election results "tough" but said, "I'm not going to walk away from those challenges and plunge the country into chaos".[^cite_note-AutoZP-40-86] On 9 May 2026, Labour MP and former government minister [Catherine West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_West) gave an interview in which she called on a cabinet minister to challenge Starmer for the leadership, in default of which she said that she would run against him.[^cite_note-AutoZP-41-87]

Over the weekend following the election results, approximately 30 Labour MPs publicly called for a change within the leadership or a timetable for Starmer to resign as Prime Minister.[^cite_note-AutoZP-42-88] Many other Labour MPs have also called on Starmer to resign.[^cite_note-AutoZP-43-89]

On 11 May 2026, [Catherine West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_West) pulled back from her challenge.[^cite_note-AutoZP-44-90] Joe Morris, a ministerial aide to Wes Streeting, said that "regrettably, it is now clear that the prime minister no longer has the trust or confidence of the public". By that night, six Labour MPs had quit or been replaced as ministerial aides, and 72 MPs had publicly urged Starmer to either resign immediately set a timetable for his departure.[^cite_note-AutoZP-45-91][^cite_note-AutoZP-46-92]

On 14 May, the Health Secretary [Wes Streeting](17169295.md) resigned from the Cabinet saying he had 'lost confidence' in Starmer's leadership. Later on the same day, Labour MP [Josh Simons](77295092.md) announced his resignation as Member of Parliament, triggering the [2026 Makerfield by-election](83181171.md) so that the Mayor of Greater Manchester [Andy Burnham](414818.md) can return to parliament and stand for the Labour leadership.[^cite_note-AutoZP-47-93]

### Opposition parties

#### Reform

Nigel Farage hailed Reform UK's results during this election as a "historic shift in British politics", also noting that this was a "complete reshaping of British politics in every way." Reform UK's home affairs spokesperson, [Zia Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Yusuf), said that if these results were "replicated in the general election, Kemi Badenoch would lose her seat," referring to the current Tory leader.[^cite_note-AutoZP-48-94]

#### Green

[Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski), the leader of the Green Party, said that "two-party politics is not just dying, it is dead and it is buried," adding that he believes that Starmer "needs to go." He also said that "it's very clear that the new politics is the Green Party versus Reform."[^cite_note-AutoZP-49-95]
