**Balendra Shah**[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] (born 27 April 1990) is a Nepali politician, structural engineer, and rapper who has served as the [prime minister of Nepal](2888818.md) since 2026. At age 36, he is the [world's youngest serving state leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_leaders_by_age#Youngest_serving_state_leaders). He assumed office following his party's [landslide victory](41053306.md) in the [2026 general election](69672716.md).[^cite_note-4] Shah is the [member of parliament](2707214.md) for [Jhapa 5](66153447.md) and was the [mayor of Kathmandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Kathmandu) from 2022 to 2026, the first [independent politician](2172378.md) to hold that office.

Shah, a structural engineer and rapper, entered politics in 2022 as mayor of Kathmandu. He emerged as a prominent political figure during the [2025 Gen Z protests](81013780.md) and in January 2026, he resigned as Mayor of Kathmandu and joined the [Rastriya Swatantra Party](72312845.md) (RSP) to contest the [2026 general election](69672716.md) as the party's prime ministerial candidate.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] He defeated former prime minister [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md) in [Jhapa 5](66153447.md) ending the latter's long-term dominance in the constituency.[^cite_note-7]

Under [Shah's premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Balen_Shah), the RSP government has initiated a "*100-Point Charter of Governance"*, restructured the administration, and prioritized service delivery and anti-corruption. Despite criticism regarding his refusal to address the public, Shah remains a popular figure with over three-fourths of participants in an OnlineKhabar survey rating his government's performance over the first 100 days positively.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Early life and education

Shah was born on 27 April 1990 in the Naradevi area of [Kathmandu](17168.md) to a [Maithil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithils) family from [Ekdara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekdara_Rural_Municipality), [Mahottari District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahottari_District).[^cite_note-AlJazeera-2026-10] He is the youngest of three children born to Dhruva Devi Shah, a [housewife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housewife) from [Dhanusha District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanusha_District), and Dr. Ram Narayan Shah, an [Ayurvedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda) physician who worked at the Naradevi Ayurvedic Hospital.[^cite_note-MyRepublica-2026-11] Shah's family prioritized education and discipline, while his father encouraged his interest in creative activities like music.[^cite_note-AlJazeera-2026-10] His elder sister, Sujata Shah Sejekan, is a painter, while his elder brother, Kaushal Shah, is a [chartered accountant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_accountant).[^cite_note-MyRepublica-2026-11]

Shah attended the Jaljala Secondary School and Alliance Academy, where he completed his primary and secondary education, respectively.[^cite_note-MyRepublica-2026-11] He later attended [V.S. Niketan Higher Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V.S._Niketan_Higher_Secondary_School) and completed his intermediate science studies.[^cite_note-MyRepublica-2026-11] It was during this time that he developed an interest in [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop_music) and began writing his own songs in Nepali and Maithili.[^cite_note-AlJazeera-2026-10] Shah went on to study [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) at Himalayan WhiteHouse International College, pursuing a [Bachelor of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Engineering) (BE) degree.[^cite_note-KathmanduPost-2022-12] He later moved to [Bengaluru](44275267.md), [India](14533.md) where he attended Nitte Meenakshi Institute of Technology (NITTE), affiliated with [Visvesvaraya Technological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visvesvaraya_Technological_University). He received a [Master of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Technology) (M.Tech) in [structural engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_engineering) in 2018.[^cite_note-MyRepublica-2026-11]

After returning to Nepal, Shah worked as a structural engineer and was involved in infrastructure projects in the [Kathmandu Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathmandu_Valley).[^cite_note-KathmanduPost-2022-12] He later became a PhD research fellow at [Kathmandu University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathmandu_University) where his research was based on the conservation of traditional [Newa architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_architecture) and [sustainable tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_tourism). His doctoral studies were suspended when he began pursuing politics full-time.[^cite_note-AlJazeera-2026-10]

## Music career

Shah entered Nepal's underground [Nephop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephop) scene in the early 2010s. His first single, "Sadak Balak" (Street Kid), was recorded in 2012 from lyrics he had written while in school. The track addressed urban social issues in Kathmandu and gained attention within Nepal's underground hip-hop scene.[^cite_note-\:6-13]

In 2013 he participated in *Raw Barz*, a YouTube battle-rap series featuring emerging Nepali artists. His participation increased his visibility within the Nepali hip-hop scene. Over the following years he released songs addressing political corruption, social inequality, and urban life.[^cite_note-Wiki-14]

Shah frequently incorporated political and social themes into his music and online commentary.[^cite_note-Republica-15]

In 2024 he wrote, composed, and performed "Nepal Haseko…" for the film *Laaj Sharanam*.[^cite_note-KathmanduPost-16]

## Mayor of Kathmandu (2022–2026)

Main article: [2022 Kathmandu municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kathmandu_municipal_election)



Shah in 2024

Shah had been discussing the idea of entering local politics since 2020. On 17 December 2021, he used his Facebook page to announce that he would contest the upcoming mayoral election as an independent.[^cite_note-\:3-17] The move was treated by many as a curiosity—an entertainer dabbling in politics—but his campaign quickly gathered momentum. His core message revolved around waste management, traffic reform, transparent public services, anti-corruption measures, and the protection of Kathmandu's built heritage.[^cite_note-\:1-18][^cite_note-\:4-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-auto-22]

His styling was deliberately distinct from career politicians. He wore dark sunglasses, carried a wooden walking stick (*lauro*), and moved through the capital's narrow alleys on foot, stopping to talk to shopkeepers and street vendors. Young volunteers, many of them first-time voters, ran much of his ground campaign, and his rallies blended street theatre with hip-hop performances.[^cite_note-auto-22][^cite_note-BBCvictory-23]

On 26 May 2022, he won the election with 61,767 votes (38.6%), defeating [Nepali Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_Congress) candidate Sirjana Shrestha and former mayor [Keshav Sthapit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshav_Sthapit) by a margin of more than 23,000 votes.[^cite_note-BBCvictory-23][^cite_note-\:7-24][^cite_note-\:8-25] He was sworn in on 30 May 2022 and immediately began a series of measures that defined his tenure.[^cite_note-26]

His rise drew comparisons with the late [Ujwal Thapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjwal_Thapa), the founder of [Bibeksheel Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibeksheel_Nepali), who had spent years trying to break the hold of traditional parties. Several veterans of the Bibeksheel movement joined Shah's mayoral campaign, and his election was widely framed as a delayed harvest of seeds Thapa had planted before his death from [COVID-19](63030231.md) in June 2021.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Infrastructure and public services

Within days of taking office Shah reached an agreement with the [Ministry of Urban Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Urban_Development_\(Nepal\)) to restart garbage collection, which had been stalled for weeks. By August 2022 private companies were contracted to handle solid waste disposal following a four-point pact with local residents' committees.[^cite_note-29]

His administration installed tactile paving along major footpaths to assist visually impaired pedestrians, and constructed *falcha*-style bus shelters modelled on traditional Newari rest houses. New bus routes were introduced, stops were upgraded, and a smart ticketing system was piloted on several corridors.[^cite_note-30]

### Transparency

Shah made the proceedings of Kathmandu's municipal council publicly accessible for the first time by introducing live telecasts of meetings. An online building permit system was rolled out and digital signatures were introduced to cut paperwork and reduce opportunities for bribe-taking.[^cite_note-31]

### Education and healthcare

Shah launched the "Textbook-Free Friday" programme in community schools, setting aside one day a week for technical skills and extracurricular activities. He also instructed private schools to offer scholarships to at least 10% of their students, enforcing a long-ignored legal provision.[^cite_note-32] In healthcare, Rs 90 million was allocated to equip modern operating theatres at [Kanti Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanti_Children's_Hospital), upgrade outpatient facilities at [Tribhuvan University Teaching Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_University_Teaching_Hospital), and improve coronary care at Manmohan Transplant Centre.[^cite_note-33]

### Cultural heritage

His office expanded support for the Living [Kumari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumari_\(goddess\)), Bhairav, and Ganesh festivals. Cooperation agreements were signed with [Tribhuvan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_University) to modernise municipal services and conserve the city's traditional stone water spouts known as *[hiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhunge_dhara)*.[^cite_note-34]

### Demolitions and evictions

Shortly after taking office, Shah launched a demolition campaign against structures he said had encroached on public land. Bulldozers were sent to commercial buildings including the Alfa Beta Complex in Baneshwor, the RB Complex in Khichapokhari, and the Suraj Arcade on New Road.[^cite_note-35] Businesses were given 35 days' notice before enforcement began. The initial wave of demolitions was supported by some residents who had long resented the encroachments, but the campaign later drew criticism for its implementation and the absence of follow-up rebuilding.[^cite_note-36]

The drive also targeted the buried [Tukucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukucha_Khola) (Ikshumati) River. Part of the Jai Nepal Cinema Hall was torn down to uncover a section of the waterway, but a stay order from the Patan High Court halted further work. The court later ruled that while the river deserved protection, private property rights had to be respected as well. By early 2026 the exposed section remained visible but no additional conservation had been carried out.[^cite_note-37]

In November 2022, attempts to evict squatters from the Bagmati riverbanks turned violent when municipal police clashed with residents, leaving 21 people injured.[^cite_note-38] Squatters and activists demanded alternative accommodation before any relocation.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Shah's crackdown on street vendors also attracted international criticism. [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) accused the city of using disproportionate force, citing videos that showed municipal police chasing and beating vendors.[^cite_note-41] Small protests erupted, led by activists who argued that nearly half of Nepal's economy relied on informal businesses and that the city offered no safety net for those displaced.[^cite_note-42] In September 2023 the activist known as Iih staged a 199‑hour vigil outside city hall; the protest ended with a commitment from the city to explore temporary permits and return confiscated goods.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

### Comment inciting violence against Singha Durbar

In September 2023, Shah posted on Facebook that he would "set Singha Durbar on fire" if municipal vehicles were stopped again, after his wife Sabina Kafle was halted at a police checkpoint while travelling in a car with government plates. The post drew criticism from political commentators and members of the public.[^cite_note-45] An aide initially claimed Kafle had been rushing to hospital in labour, but it later emerged that she had already given birth a week earlier.[^cite_note-46] The incident resurfaced in September 2025 when Gen Z protesters did set fire to Singha Durbar; Shah denied any connection to his earlier statement and urged the public to avoid rumours.[^cite_note-47]

### Map of Greater Nepal and ban on Indian films

In June 2023, Shah placed a map of "[Greater Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Nepal)" in his office—a map showing territories controlled by Nepal before the 1816 [Treaty of Sugauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Sugauli) that are now part of India. The gesture was widely interpreted as a response to the display of an "[Akhand Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhand_Bharat)" map in India's new Parliament.[^cite_note-48]

The same month, he banned the screening of all Indian films in Kathmandu after the Bollywood release *[Adipurush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adipurush)* included dialogue stating that "Sita is a daughter of India"—a line that generated controversy in Nepal, who regard Sita as having been born in Janakpur. The Patan High Court ordered the ban lifted; Shah refused to comply and publicly labelled the judiciary and central government "Indian slaves", though he later complied with the court order.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Offensive posts and the 2025 Gen Z protests

In November 2025 a late‑night Facebook post by Shah used an profanity directed at India, China, the United States, and all major Nepali political parties. Though the post was deleted within minutes, screenshots circulated widely and drew criticism from political commentators and social media users.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

During the [2025 Nepalese Gen Z protests](81013780.md) in September 2025, the student wing of the Nepali Congress filed a police complaint accusing Shah of inciting violence and withholding fire engines from burning government buildings. He rejected the allegations, insisting that city crews worked "around the clock" to protect public infrastructure.[^cite_note-54]

## Prime Minister of Nepal

### 2026 general election

Main article: [2026 Nepalese general election](69672716.md)



Balen Shah and [Rabi Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Lamichhane) during Kathmandu Valley election rally held by [RSP](72312845.md) for [2026 General Election](69672716.md).

On 28 December 2025, Shah formally joined the [Rastriya Swatantra Party](72312845.md) (RSP), stepping down as Mayor of Kathmandu on 18 January 2026 to contest the [2026 general election](69672716.md). He was immediately projected as the party's prime‑ministerial candidate.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] His arrival significantly increased public attention toward the RSP, which had been struggling under the weight of founder [Rabi Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Lamichhane)'s legal troubles.[^cite_note-57] Political observers noted that the presence of widely trusted figures such as Shah and [Kul Man Ghising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kul_Man_Ghising) had given the party a renewed political momentum.[^cite_note-58]

Shah chose to contest [Jhapa 5](66153447.md), the stronghold of four‑time former prime minister [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md), who had held the seat almost continuously since 2008. The decision was seen as politically significant, a deliberate attempt to prove that the old guard could be beaten on its own turf. His rallies drew large crowds of young supporters, many of whom had never participated in a formal campaign before.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] When votes were counted on 5 March 2026, the RSP led in more than 90 constituencies, and in Jhapa‑5 Shah defeated Oli by a margin of 49,614 votes—68,348 to 18,734—one of the largest reported victory margins in a Nepali parliamentary election.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] On 7 March he received his victory certificate and formally became a member of the [House of Representatives](2707214.md).[^cite_note-khabarhub2026-63]

### Appointment

Main article: [Balen Shah cabinet](82600702.md)

As the parliamentary leader of the largest party, Shah was appointed Prime Minister by President [Ram Chandra Paudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Chandra_Paudel) on 27 March 2026, and later announced members of his cabinet.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] At 35 years old he became the youngest prime minister in the history of the Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal.

## Tenure as Prime Minister (2026–present)



Balen Shah with Secretary-General of the United Nations [António Guterres](471806.md) in 2026

### 100-point reform agenda

On 27 March, Shah's first day as prime minister, the government launched a 100-point reform agenda under a Delivery-Based Governance model to modernize Nepal's state institutions.[^cite_note-66] The plan requires every ministry to operate under measurable targets, deadlines, and public performance monitoring. Major measures include reducing [federal ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ministries_of_Nepal) from 22 to 17, banning party-affiliated unions in the civil service and education sector, establishing an Asset Investigation Commission to audit the wealth of senior public officials from 2048 BS onward, strengthening anti-money-laundering controls, creating a paperless, integrated government system with home delivery of passports, licenses, and national ID cards.[^cite_note-67] The agenda also removes partisan student wings from schools, abolishes internal exams up to Grade 5, enforces a requirement that hospitals provide 10% of beds free of charge to disadvantaged patients, among many other reforms.[^cite_note-68]

### Fortnightly salary system

On 20 April 2026, the government announced that all civil servants, police, and military personnel would be paid every 15 days instead of monthly.[^cite_note-69] Finance Minister [Dr Swarnim Wagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarnim_Wagle) said the change was intended to improve cash flow and economic liquidity, and that bi‑weekly payment cycles were common in developed countries.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Nepal became the first South Asian country to adopt such a pay cycle for government staff.[^cite_note-72]

### Enforcement of tariff on Indian goods

From 19 April 2026, Nepal began stricter enforcement of tariff rules on goods worth over NPR 100 brought in from India. The move was aimed at curbing small-scale cross‑border shopping that bypassed formal tax channels and at boosting domestic trade.[^cite_note-73] Border markets on the Indian side reported a steep drop in customers, while Nepali traders generally welcomed the policy. Residents in border areas, however, complained about the sudden rise in the cost of daily essentials.[^cite_note-74]

### Squatter settlement

On 23 April 2026, Balen Shah's government decided to begin the first phase of squatter settlement clearance in Thapathali, Manohara, Sinamangal and Gairigaun in Kathmandu with the help of local government and security force.[^cite_note-75] The move was aimed at re-settling the illegal settlements residing alongside the riverbanks of Kathmandu to new houses and shelters in various places in the valley. This decision was highly criticised and objected to by human rights organizations, activists, and opposition parties citing that there are not enough places built yet that can hold all the people and that the government is making decisions irrationally without any proper plans.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

### Re-structuring of Ministries

On 13 May 2026, the government decided to reduce the number of federal ministries from 22 to 18, with the main objective of maintaining administrative reform and frugality, reducing unnecessary current expenditure, and making the work performance more effective and swift.[^cite_note-78] The decision to merge some of the ministries faced criticism from former bureaucrats and senior officials about rationale behind the mergers, renaming and redistribution of portfolios.[^cite_note-79]

## Personal life

Shah married Sabina Kafle, a public health professional, in 2018. The couple have one daughter and live in the Gairigaun neighbourhood of Tinkune, Kathmandu.[^cite_note-\:269228-80]

## Recognition

*[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine included Shah in its "[The 100 Most Influential People of 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100)", citing his independent victory as a challenge to Nepal's established party system.[^cite_note-82] He was again listed in the *Time* 100 for 2026, recognition of his political rise and his influence on political change following the [2025 Gen‑Z protests](81013780.md).[^cite_note-83]

**Notable records and distinctions attributed to Shah include:**

- The youngest serving state leader in the world from 27 March 2026 to present.

- The youngest serving Prime Minister of the Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal.

- The first independent candidate to win the mayorship of Kathmandu.
