**Alexus Gregory Grynkewich** (born October 15, 1971) is an American [general](2965165.md) who has served as the commander of [United States European Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_European_Command) and the [Supreme Allied Commander Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Allied_Commander_Europe) since 2025.

Grynkewich graduated from the [United States Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy) in 1993. During his career in the [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force), Grynkewich was an [F-16 Fighting Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcon) and [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-22_Raptor) pilot, and has over 2,300 flight hours. His senior posts have included commander of the [53rd Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Wing) and deputy commander for operations of the [Operation Inherent Resolve Combined Joint Task Force](44618350.md), which was heavily involved in the [war against the Islamic State](43517689.md).

Grynkewich was the director of operations of the [U.S. Central Command](338482.md) from 2020 to 2022, and the commander of the [Ninth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Air_Force) from 2022 to 2024. After the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) in Israel, he oversaw airstrikes in response to the [targeting of U.S. forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_US_bases_during_the_Middle_Eastern_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)) by Iranian [proxy groups](36658251.md), and the interception of the [April 2024 Iranian strikes against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2024_Iranian_strikes_against_Israel). Grynkewich was then the director for operations of the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff) from 2024 to 2025, during which time he was involved in the [U.S. support to Ukraine](70376708.md).

## Early life and education

Alexus Gregory Grynkewich[^cite_note-1] was born on October 15, 1971[^cite_note-Wikidata-2] and is an [American of Belarusian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Americans). His great-grandfather Elias (Ilya) Grynkevitch immigrated to the United States in 1899 from [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk), then located in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). His father, Gregory Wayne Grynkewich (1949–2012), was a scientist and inventor.[^cite_note-3] His mother, Linda Craine Grynkewich (born 1950), is a scientist, educator, and writer.[^cite_note-4]

Grynkewich graduated from the [Georgia Military College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Military_College) Preparatory School in 1989,[^cite_note-GMC-5] and was commissioned as a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) from the [United States Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy) in 1993 after graduating with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in [military history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history). In 1994 Grynkewich also obtained a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree in history from the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia). From September 1994 to August 1996 he underwent pilot training at the [Vance Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vance_Air_Force_Base) in [Oklahoma](22489.md) and the [Luke Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Air_Force_Base) in [Arizona](21883824.md). In addition, his later education included the [Squadron Officer School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_Officer_School) in 1997, the [Air Command and Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Command_and_Staff_College) in 2003, the [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School) and the [Air War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_War_College) in 2006, and the [Joint Advanced Warfighting School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Forces_Staff_College) at [National Defense University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_University_\(United_States\)) in 2010, among other courses.[^cite_note-AFBio-6]

## Military career

In September 1996 he became an F-16 pilot and the squadron chief of training for [18th Fighter Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Fighter_Interceptor_Squadron) at [Eielson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eielson_Air_Force_Base), [Alaska](624.md). He held that role until July 1999, and in the following month Grynkewich was appointed as an F-16 instructor pilot, flight commander, and evaluator in the [421st Fighter Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/421st_Fighter_Squadron) at [Hill Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Air_Force_Base), [Utah](31716.md) until December 2001. From January 2002 to January 2003 he was an F-16C instructor pilot and the chief of weapons for [80th Fighter Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80th_Fighter_Squadron) at [Kunsan Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsan_Air_Base) in [South Korea](27019.md). Starting in February 2003 Grynkewich became an operation test and evaluation instructor pilot for the F-16C and the F-22A at the [422d Test and Evaluation Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/422d_Test_and_Evaluation_Squadron), then chief of F-22A evaluation for the [53rd Test and Evaluation Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Test_and_Evaluation_Group), and director of operations at the [59th Test and Evaluation Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/59th_Test_and_Evaluation_Squadron). During his time as a pilot, Grynkewich achieved the rating of command pilot and has over 2,300 flight hours.[^cite_note-AFBio-6]

In September 2005 he began studies at the Naval Postgraduate School, and following graduation, from January to December 2007 he worked at [Air Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Combat_Command) in several roles. Grynkewich was then the commander of the [49th Operations Support Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_Operations_Group) from January 2008 to June 2009. After that he attended the Joint Advanced Warfighting School, where he received a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree. Between July 2010 and May 2012 Grynkewich worked at the Headquarters, [U.S. European Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._European_Command), in [Germany](11867.md), including as the Chief of the Plans Division. From June 2012 he was vice commander of the [57th Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Wing) at [Nellis Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellis_Air_Force_Base), [Nevada](21216.md), and from May 2013 he was commander of the [53rd Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Wing) at [Eglin Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglin_Air_Force_Base), [Florida](18933066.md).[^cite_note-AFBio-6][^cite_note-7] In June 2015 Grynkewich was appointed to Headquarters, U.S. Air Force, at [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), serving as the Chief of the Strategic Planning Integration Division. In that role he was responsible for working on the Air Force's 30-year Resource Allocation Plan.[^cite_note-GMC-5][^cite_note-AFBio-6] In June 2016 he became Deputy Director for Operations, Operations Team Three, at the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff), and then the Deputy Director, Global Operations, at [Operations (J3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_\(J3\)), Joint Staff.[^cite_note-AFBio-6]

### General officer



Grynkewich speaking at the Air Forces Central change of command ceremony, [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md), July 2022

Between April 2019 and May 2020 he served as Deputy Commander for Operations, [Combined Joint Task Force – Operation Inherent Resolve](44618350.md), and as Commander, [9th Air Expeditionary Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Air_Expeditionary_Group).[^cite_note-AFBio-6] In this role he took part in the [U.S. campaign](44120613.md) against the [Islamic State](9087364.md) in [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Syria](7515849.md),[^cite_note-CENTCOM-8] working closely with the [Iraqi security forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_security_forces) and the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md).[^cite_note-9]

In June 2020 Grynkewich became the director of operations at the [United States Central Command](338482.md), until June 2022, and in the following month he became commander, [Ninth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Air_Force) and U.S. Air Forces Central.[^cite_note-AFBio-6][^cite_note-10] As commander of Air Forces Central, Grynkewich oversaw all U.S. air operations in the Middle East, including strikes on [Iranian](14653.md) [proxy groups](36658251.md) to protect [U.S. forces from attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_US_bases_during_the_Middle_Eastern_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), and helping intercept the [April 2024 Iranian strikes against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2024_Iranian_strikes_against_Israel), the latter happening just days before he turned over command to his successor.[^cite_note-11]

In January 2024, Grynkewich was nominated for assignment as director for operations of the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] He completed his assignment at Central Command in April 2024 and took up the post of director for operations at [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) in May.[^cite_note-AFBio-6] During his tenure, he worked on the [U.S. support to Ukraine](70376708.md) during the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md).[^cite_note-14]

### EUCOM and NATO commander

In June 2025, Grynkewich was nominated for promotion to general and assignment as the commander of [United States European Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_European_Command) and [Supreme Allied Commander Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Allied_Commander_Europe).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] He is the first officer in recent history appointed to that position from a three-star role instead of already being a four-star general.[^cite_note-17] Grynkewich took command of EUCOM in a ceremony on July 1, 2025,[^cite_note-18] and became the Supreme Allied Commander Europe on July 4, succeeding Army General [Christopher G. Cavoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_G._Cavoli).[^cite_note-19]

In February 2026 Grynkewich participated in negotiations in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) involving American, Russian, and Ukrainian officials, during which it was agreed to restore military-to-military communication between the U.S. and Russia, which had been suspended since late 2021.[^cite_note-20] Before that, in his role as commander of NATO forces, Grynkewich also had the authority to be in contact with the Russian Chief of the General Staff, [Valery Gerasimov](37587470.md), for deescalation purposes.[^cite_note-21]
