In March 2025, the [United States](3434750.md) launched a large campaign of air and naval strikes against [Houthi](17240332.md) targets in [Yemen](350939.md). Codenamed **Operation Rough Rider**,[^cite_note-NYTimes20250404-14] it was the first large-scale US military operation in the [Middle East](19323.md) during President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [second term](78298108.md).[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] The strikes began on March 15, targeting radar systems, air defenses, and ballistic and drone launch sites used by the Houthis to [attack](75207766.md) commercial ships and naval vessels in the [Red Sea](26191.md) and [Gulf of Aden](58716.md).[^cite_note-NYTimes20250315-16] On 30 April 2025, the [United Kingdom](31717.md) joined the United States in conducting strikes on Houthi targets.[^cite_note-17]

The Houthi group began [targeting international shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_attacks_on_commercial_vessels) in October 2023, after [Israel invaded the Gaza Strip](74998519.md) in response to the [October 7 Hamas attacks](74998317.md). Claiming solidarity with Palestinians and aiming to pressure [Israel](9282173.md) into agreeing to a ceasefire and lifting its [blockade of Gaza](75013719.md),[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] the Houthis launched missiles and drones at vessels traveling near Yemen, and also fired [ballistic missiles](38025.md) at Israeli cities, killing at least one civilian in [Tel Aviv](31453.md).[^cite_note-France24-20240721-18][^cite_note-TheGuardian20240719-19] In response, the United States, the United Kingdom, and a multinational coalition began [Operation Prosperity Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian), combining naval escorts with episodic airstrikes on Houthi military and civilian infrastructure.[^cite_note-APNews20250315-20]

By mid-March 2025, the Houthis had attacked more than 190 ships, sinking two, seizing another, and killing at least four seafarers.[^cite_note-BBC20250315-21] On March 18, Trump warned [Iran](14653.md)—longtime [backers of the Houthis](78962952.md)—that further attacks would be considered acts of aggression, despite no direct involvement.[^cite_note-BBC20250318-22]

On May 6, President Donald Trump declared the strikes to be over, "effective immediately," as a result of a [ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States%E2%80%93Houthi_ceasefire) between the US and the Houthis, brokered by [Oman](22316.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250506-23] The Houthis asserted that the ceasefire did not in "any way, shape, or form" preclude attacking Israel, which had just [begun bombing Yemen](79886126.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250507-24]

The [Houthis](17240332.md) are a [Shia Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islamism) military and political group located in [Yemen](350939.md),[^cite_note-NYTimes20250315-16] backed by [Iran](14653.md),[^cite_note-Naar-2024-25]\[^cite_note-[www.cfr.org-26](http://www.cfr.org-26/)][^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] and widely considered part of the Iranian-led [Axis of Resistance](36658251.md).[^cite_note-Naar-2024-25][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Economist20250116-28] Emerging in the 1990s, they played a major role in [Yemen's civil war](46215853.md), opposing the [Presidential Leadership Council (SLC)](70488867.md). The group took control over northern Yemen, including the capital, [Sanaa](18691665.md), since 2014,[^cite_note-NYTimes20250315-16]

The Saudi-led coalition, backed by the United States, has fought against the Houthi Movement for over a decade. Since the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) in October 2023, they have continued to target commercial shipping in the Red Sea using missiles, drones, and explosive-laden boats, forcing many shipping companies to reroute vessels around southern Africa at higher costs, in an attempt to force a ceasefire in the Gaza War and an end to the [Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip](75013719.md).[^cite_note-NYTimes20250315-16][^cite_note-CNN20250315-29] Since November 2023, they have conducted over 190 attacks on shipping,[^cite_note-BBC20250315-21] sunk two vessels, seized another, and killed at least four seafarers, disrupting global trade.[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* based in the UK reported that Red Sea cargo shipments had decreased by 70% in volume as a result of these strikes. It alleged that the Houthis had turned their strikes into a business model by requesting [Protection racket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket) payments from shipowners in exchange for safe passage, generating substantial revenue through [blackmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackmarket) transactions and a form of maritime extortion.[^cite_note-Economist20250116-28]

The Houthis also conducted ballistic missile and drone attacks on Israeli cities, including [Eilat](66162.md) and [Tel Aviv](31453.md), where [a drone strike killed one Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Houthi_drone_attack_on_Israel).[^cite_note-France24-20240721-18][^cite_note-TheGuardian20240719-19] In response, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Israel, with support from a multi­national coalition,[^cite_note-30] launched [attacks in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian) to disrupt their capabilities.[^cite_note-APNews20250315-20][^cite_note-BBC20250315-21]

While other members of Iran's Axis of Resistance—including [Hamas](13913.md), [Hezbollah](13919.md), and [Syria](57134800.md)—suffered major setbacks, the Houthis have used Yemen's geography to continue to fight against the Presidential Leadership Council.[^cite_note-Economist20250116-28][^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] Following a [cease­fire in Gaza-Israel in January 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Gaza_war_ceasefire), they halted their attacks, but tensions resurfaced when they warned to resume strikes on targets in Israel if Israel disrupted humanitarian aid to Gaza again, which it blocked on 2 March. Following the aid block, the Houthis set a four-day deadline for border crossings to be reopened and aid to be allowed. The Houthis announced a resumption in attacks on 11 March after the demands were not met.[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250317b-31] The Houthis also fired a missile at a US Air Force [F-16](11642.md) and shot down a US [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md) drone. President Trump redesignated the Houthis as a [foreign terrorist organization](421835.md) in January 2025.[^cite_note-32]

In February 2025, [CBS News](359551.md) reported that President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) had rolled back constraints on American commanders, allowing them to authorize airstrikes and special operations raids outside conventional battlefields. This broadened the range of people who could be targeted, with the Houthis being among the first under the new rule.[^cite_note-33]

Weapons analysts have suggested that the Houthis may have acquired advanced drone technology, potentially increasing their operational range. Many of the militia's weapons are either manufactured in underground facilities or smuggled from Iran,[^cite_note-NYTimes20250315-16] their primary backer. Iran's enrichment of uranium to near weapons-grade levels remains a major international concern. Meanwhile, economic hardship in Iran has fueled domestic unrest.[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15]

## Prelude

Following Trump's election, Gen. [Michael Kurilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kurilla), commander of the [United States Central Command](338482.md), proposed an 8–10 month bombing campaign based on [Israel's operation against Hezbollah](75005425.md), where the US would strike Houthi air defenses before conducting targeted assassinations against senior Houthi officials. Saudi officials who supported Kurilla's proposal provided a list of 12 prominent Houthi leaders, stating that their assassinations would "cripple" the group. In early March, Trump partially approved Kurilla's plan, giving it 30 days to exhibit results in a campaign named "Operation Rough Rider" by [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md).[^cite_note-NYTimes20250512-34]

On 13 March 2025, National Security Adviser [Michael Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Waltz) created a [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_\(software\)) group chat called "Houthi PC small group", for [Trump administration](78298108.md) officials to coordinate the attacks. The group included accounts that seemed to correspond to [Marco Rubio](5502549.md), [JD Vance](53396477.md), [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md), [Scott Bessent](38532044.md), [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md), [John Ratcliffe](42699041.md), [Steve Witkoff](40901053.md), [Susie Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Wiles), [Joe Kent](68079795.md), and [Stephen Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Miller_\(political_advisor\)). Waltz also accidentally added the [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)*, [Jeffrey Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Goldberg). This incident became known as [Signalgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signalgate).

On the morning of 14 March, Vance messaged the group asking for the attacks to be delayed by one month, saying: "I am not sure the president is aware how inconsistent this is with his message on [Europe](9239.md) right now" and that the attacks "risk that we see a moderate to severe spike in [oil prices](5137675.md)". But Hegseth convinced Vance to continue with the attack, saying that the attacks were "not about the Houthis. I see it as two things: 1) Restoring [Freedom of Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_navigation), a core national interest; and 2) Reestablish [deterrence](735661.md), which Biden cratered." Miller messaged at 9\:35 a.m. [ET](496028.md) that Trump had given approval for the attacks.[^cite_note-TheAtlantic20250324b-35][^cite_note-guard-24mar2025-36]

Trump authorized the attack plan, which had been in development for several weeks, with the final order issued on Saturday, 15 March. Some members of Congress received briefings from the White House the same day.[^cite_note-CNN20250315-29] At 11\:44 a.m. ET, Hegseth messaged the group operational details of the attacks, including information about targets, weapons used, and attack sequencing.[^cite_note-37]

## Strikes

### First week (15–21 March 2025)

#### 15 March

CCTV footage showing the effect of US strikes in Sanaa

[^cite_note-38]

At least 40 air raids took place across Yemen, mainly in the capital of [Sanaa](18691665.md) and in [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate).

The eight strikes in Sanaa included one on a residential area that killed 15 people and injured nine others;[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250316a-39] one on the [Sanaa International Airport](4202141.md), which houses a major military facility, with images showing black smoke billowing over the area;[^cite_note-APNews20250315-20] and four in the Geraf neighborhood of Shaoub district in northern Sanaa.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-APNews20250315-20] [Al-Masirah](49486469.md) and other local media reported airstrikes in Sanaa at 1\:30 p.m. [ET](496028.md).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Twelve strikes hit Saada, including one on a power station in [Dahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahyan) that caused a power outage.[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250316a-39] Military installations in [Taiz](545270.md) were also struck.[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15][^cite_note-43] In [Kahza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahza?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Ibb Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibb_Governorate), Houthi media reported that 15 people were killed after American attacks hit two residential buildings.[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250316a-39] Eight strikes took place in [Al Bayda Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bayda_Governorate), and further attacks hit [Dhamar Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamar_Governorate), [Hajjah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjah_Governorate), and [Marib Governorate](3604391.md).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-AlJazeera20250316a-39]

The strikes, which US officials described as the largest military attack of Trump's second term, were carried out by fighter jets from the [*Nimitz*-class](181160.md) [aircraft carrier](2219.md) [USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman), stationed in the northern [Red Sea](26191.md), and [United States Air Force](32090.md) attack planes and armed drones launched from regional bases.[^cite_note-NYTimes20250315-16] The [BBC](19344654.md) reported that the UK did not participate in the airstrikes but assisted in [refueling](237949.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250317b-31]

After the initial strikes, Trump claimed on Truth Social that the Houthis were waging a campaign of "piracy, violence, and terrorism against American, and other, ships, aircraft, and drones",[^cite_note-CNN20250315-29] and vowed to use "overwhelming lethal force" until Houthi attacks on shipping cease. "No terrorist force will stop American commercial and naval vessels from freely sailing the Waterways of the World."[^cite_note-APNews20250315-20] He added, "Your time is up, and your attacks must stop, starting today. If they don't, hell will rain down upon you like nothing you have ever seen before."[^cite_note-CNN20250315-29] He also warned Iran to end its support for the Houthis, promising to hold the country "fully accountable" for the actions of the militant group.[^cite_note-APNews20250315-20][^cite_note-CNN20250315-29]

Hegseth posted on [X](9988187.md) that "Houthi attacks on American ships & aircraft (and our troops!) will not be tolerated; and Iran, their benefactor, is on notice", adding that "freedom of navigation will be restored".[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15]

The Houthis described the attacks as a "war crime" and vowed retaliation.[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] According to the Houthi-run health ministry, at least 53 people were killed, including five children and two women, and 98 others were wounded.[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250316b-45] The [United Nations Children's Fund](19698110.md) (UNICEF) independently determined that at least 2 children were killed in the attack.[^cite_note-UNICEFYemen-46]

Waltz claimed that the attacks killed several Houthi leaders and indicated that the US would consider hitting Iranian targets in Yemen.[^cite_note-47]

In the Signal chat, Hegseth said that the strikes had killed the Houthis' "top missile guy".[^cite_note-TheAtlantic20250324b-35][^cite_note-48]

#### 16 March

Airstrikes hit military headquarters, weapon storage facilities, and detection equipment used to locate shipping.[^cite_note-AlArabiya20250318-49] Al-Masirah reported two US strikes in [Al Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah).[^cite_note-50]

Saudi news outlet [AlHadath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlHadath) reported that the security chief of Houthi leader [Abdul-Malik al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Malik_al-Houthi) was killed in Saada.[^cite_note-Ynetnews20250325-51]

The Houthis claimed that they targeted the aircraft carrier [USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman) using 18 ballistic and cruise missiles and a drone. A US official said that a US aircraft shot down 11 drones launched by Houthis towards USS *Harry S. Truman* and a missile fired by Houthis failed in flight and splashed down into the sea. Later, the Houthis said that they fired missiles and drones towards USS *Harry S. Truman* and its several warships for the second time.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

[Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) reported that US airstrikes in [Al Jawf Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jawf_Governorate) killed three Houthi officials, including Commander Zain al Abidin al Mahtouri in [al Hazm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hazm,_Yemen).[^cite_note-ISW19Mar2025-55]

#### 17 March

Two early-morning air raids in the port city of [Al Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah) hit a cotton ginning factory and the command cabin of the hijacked Israel-linked cargo ship *[Galaxy Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_Leader),* Houthi-aligned [Saba News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_News_Agency) reported.[^cite_note-56] Saba also said strikes had damaged an under-construction cancer facility in Saada.[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250317a-57]

Another strike killed "Abu Taha" Zain al-Abidin Ali Abdullah al-Muhturi, a senior official in the Houthi internal security department, in the headquarters of the government complex in the city of Al-Hazm, the center of [Al Jawf Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jawf_Governorate), northeast of Sanaa.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

Other strikes hit the Al-Habashi Iron Factory in the [Bajil district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajil_district) of [Al Hudaydah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah_Governorate)[^cite_note-61] and sites south of the [presidential compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Yemen) in Sanaa.[^cite_note-JPost20250317-62][^cite_note-63]

Lieutenant General [Alex Grynkewich](66715028.md), US [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff) Director for Operations, said that more than 30 targets in Yemen had been struck since the bombing campaign began, killing dozens of Houthi militants.[^cite_note-AlArabiya20250318-49][^cite_note-64]

Trump declared in a post that more Houthi attacks would be viewed as direct actions, or *[casus belli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casus_belli)*, by Iran that would incur severe consequences.[^cite_note-BBC20250318-22]

On the night of March 17/18, the United States launched a wave of strikes targeting Houthi and [IRGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC) targets, reportedly inflicting casualties on both.[^cite_note-65]

#### 18 March

[Agence France-Presse](471981.md) was told by witnesses that three strikes were conducted in Saada in the late night.[^cite_note-AlArabiya20250319-66]

#### 19 March

Houthi media reported that at least 10 strikes were conducted by the US in Sanaa and Al Hudaydah.[^cite_note-AlArabiya20250319-66] Strikes also took place in [As Safra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Safra_district), Saada, which is reportedly one of the most militarized Houthi strongholds, containing training bases and weapon storage facilities. According to [Reuters](18998750.md), [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench) and [bunkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunker) that were built to house Houthi leaders following former president [Joe Biden](145422.md)'s airstrikes in Yemen were also struck, forcing the group's leadership into hiding.[^cite_note-67] US strikes on Sanaa injured seven women and two children in a residential neighborhood.[^cite_note-68] Al-Masirah also reported strikes in Al-Sawadiya, southeast of Sanaa.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] ISW reported airstrikes on the Houthi's Communications Ministry and Postal Authority in Sanaa.[^cite_note-ISW19Mar2025-55]

Pro-Houthi media reported that funerals were held in Sanaa for 16 slain militants.[^cite_note-Ynetnews20250325-51]

#### 20 March

Al-Masirah reported that at least four US strikes hit the [Al Mina district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mina_district) of [al-Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hudaydah),[^cite_note-71] but later Houthi aligned media reported a total of five strikes in Al Hudaydah Governorate, including one hitting a cotton processing plant in [Zabid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabid),[^cite_note-72] the cotton processing plant had allegedly been converted into a mines and explosive devices factory.[^cite_note-73] US also conducted an airstrike near Safraa.[^cite_note-ISW21Mar2025-74]

Yemen's Houthi-run health ministry said that a US airstrike hit a wedding hall which was under construction in [Sanaa](18691665.md), injuring nine people, including women and children.[^cite_note-75]

#### 21 March

Yemeni media reported that US fighter jets conducted six air strikes in [At Tuhayta district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_Tuhayta_district)[^cite_note-76] including strikes reportedly targeting a Houthi military base in al Fazah-al Tuhayta.[^cite_note-ISW21Mar2025-74] ISW reported a total of seven airstrikes.[^cite_note-ISW21Mar2025-74]

### Second week (22–26 March 2025)

On 22 March, AlHadath reported that a prior US airstrike on a naval base in Al Hudaydah wounded eight people, including [Mansour al-Saadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_al-Saadi?action=edit\&redlink=1), the commander of the Houthi naval forces.[^cite_note-Ynetnews20250325-51]

[Saba News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_News_Agency) reported that the US conducted three strikes on [Hodeida International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodeida_International_Airport).[^cite_note-77] Three Houthi commanders were killed in airstrikes in [Majzar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majzar?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Marib Governorate](3604391.md).[^cite_note-ISW24Mar2025-78] A Houthi military base and ammunition depot was targeted in a US airstrike in [Al Jawf Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jawf_Governorate).[^cite_note-ISW24Mar2025-78]

On 23 March, the Houthi-controlled Health Ministry reported that one person was killed and 13 others, including three children, were injured in a US strike on a residential building in the Aser area of western Sanaa. Strikes were also reported in Saada, Al Hudaydah, and Marib.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] The strikes in western Sanaa reportedly targeted a Houthi ammunition depot.[^cite_note-ISW24Mar2025-78] [AlHadath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlHadath) reported that the person killed was a senior Houthi official.[^cite_note-81] [Saba News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_News_Agency) reported a US attack on the [Port of Salif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Salif).[^cite_note-82] Saba news agency also reported US bombings in [Sahar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahar_district) and [Kitaf wa Al Boqe'e district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitaf_wa_Al_Boqe'e_district). It also reported that the US conducted five air attacks in [Marib Governorate](3604391.md).[^cite_note-83]

According to [Michael Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Waltz), the attacks killed key Houthi leaders, including their "head missileer", and also targeted their headquarters, communication nodes, arms factories, and [unmanned surface vehicle](7808557.md) production facilities.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

On 24 March, Saudi media outlet [Al Hadath](6315937.md) reported that a high-level Houthi official was killed in overnight US airstrikes in Sanaa.[^cite_note-87]

On 25 March, [Saba News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_News_Agency) reported that US air strikes in the outskirts of [Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada) injured at least two people.[^cite_note-88] Saba News Agency reported that a US strike hit a cancer hospital in [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate) for the second time, destroying it.[^cite_note-89] ISW reported a total of 12 airstrikes in [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate).[^cite_note-90]

On 26 March, ISW reported that the US conducted at least 14 airstrikes against Houthi infrastructure and leadership.[^cite_note-91]

The Houthi-linked [Al-Masirah](49486469.md) reported two US strikes in [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate).[^cite_note-92] Al-Masirah reported US strikes in the [Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada) area, Al Salem district in Saada Governorate and [Harf Sufyan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harf_Sufyan_district).[^cite_note-93] Al-Masirah also reported more US strikes in [Sanaa](18691665.md).[^cite_note-94]

The Yemen Data Project reported that at least 25 civilians, including four children, were killed and at least 28 civilians were wounded in the airstrike campaign so far.[^cite_note-95]

### Third week (27 March – 2 April)

## Ceasefire

Main article: [2025 United States–Houthi ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States%E2%80%93Houthi_ceasefire)

On 6 May 2025, US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) declared an end to the strikes on Yemen, stating that they were over, "effective immediately," as a result of a ceasefire between the US and the Houthis, brokered by [Oman](22316.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250506-23] The Houthis agreed to halt attacks on vessels in the Red Sea but emphasized that the ceasefire did not in "any way, shape, or form" apply to Israel, which had just launched [its own strikes on Yemen](79886126.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250507-24] While Trump framed the truce as the Houthis having "capitulated" and not "want\[ing] to fight anymore," while also having shown "a lot of bravery," the Houthis claimed that it was in fact the US that "backed down."[^cite_note-258] According to reports, Iran played a role in persuading the Houthis to reach a truce with the United States to help build "momentum" for the [2025 Iran–United States negotiations](79685207.md).[^cite_note-259] According to Israeli officials, Israel was not given "advance notice" of the US-Houthi ceasefire. Israeli media described the ceasefire as "very bad news for Israel" and "doubly surpris\[ing]".[^cite_note-260] Following the ceasefire deal, Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) asserted that "Israel will defend itself by itself."[^cite_note-261]

## Foreign involvement

Before the campaign, the UK had not officially acknowledged launching airstrikes in Yemen since May 2024.[^cite_note-DeclassifiedUK20250318-262] All previous British airstrikes were conducted during the tenure of UK prime minister [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak) and US president [Joe Biden](145422.md).[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-APNews20250430-235] At the start of the operation, the UK did not directly participate in the attacks but had provided support for US aircraft through "routine allied [air-to-air refuelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-air_refuelling)."[^cite_note-BBC20250315-21][^cite_note-264] *[Declassified UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declassified_UK)* published an investigation on 18 March which showed that 53 people, including women and children, had died from US fighter jets aided by British aerial refuelling.[^cite_note-DeclassifiedUK20250318-262][^cite_note-265] The UK conducted its first operation during the campaign, which was also its operation under US president Donald Trump, on 29 April. The British defense secretary stated that the airstrikes were "in the interests of our national and economic security" and were to "prevent further attacks against UK and International shipping."[^cite_note-266]

A Saudi official denied reports claiming Saudi Arabia was providing logistical support for US attacks on Yemen. Speaking to *[Al Arabiya](321645.md)*, the official dismissed the allegations as "misleading" and stated that the kingdom was not supplying oil for the military operations.[^cite_note-267] The dismissal came after reports that the UK assisted in refueling US fighter jets over Saudi Arabian airspace, while the Houthis vowed to take action "against any country that cooperates with the American enemy".[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250317b-31][^cite_note-268]

*[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that Israel provided intelligence from an individual in Yemen about a senior Houthi figure who would later be targeted in the March 15 airstrike, which was discussed in a group chat on Signal.[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270]

On 28 March, *[The New Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Arab)* reported that Houthi politburo member Mohammed Al-Farah accused UAE-allied separatist groups, including the [Southern Transitional Council](54251566.md), of providing intelligence to the US military for their airstrikes, calling them "traitors" and stating that "the UAE's mercenaries stand out more than anyone else in their vileness and depravity." He threatened that if the separatist groups continued supplying intelligence to the US, his group would not only consider striking [Mokha](2082636.md) or [Shabwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabwa) but could also strike Abu Dhabi and Dubai directly in response. The country was [previously targeted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Abu_Dhabi_attack) by the Houthis in January 2022 in response to its involvement in the [Yemeni Civil War](46215853.md).[^cite_note-271]

## Reactions

### Yemen

The Houthi political bureau called the attacks a [war crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_war_crimes).[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] Deputy head of the Houthi media office [Nasruddin Amer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasruddin_Amer?action=edit\&redlink=1) said that "Sanaa will remain Gaza's shield and support and will not abandon it no matter the challenges."[^cite_note-272] Another senior Houthi member told *[Al Arabiya](321645.md)* said that the attacks violated Yemen's sovereignty and would be met with a "painful and deterrent" response.[^cite_note-273] On 18 March, the pro-Houthi [Yemeni Armed Forces](34252.md) stated that "the US aggression will not deter the steadfast and struggling Yemen from fulfilling its religious, moral, and humanitarian duties toward the Palestinian people", and reiterated the Houthis' demand for Israel to lift the [blockade against the Gaza Strip](75013719.md).[^cite_note-274]

The UN-recognized [Presidential Leadership Council](70488867.md) urged the international community to strengthen cooperation and adopt a comprehensive strategy to counter the Houthi militias and block their funding. The PLC stated that US strikes signaled a shift in the international approach to the Houthis, emphasizing that their threat now extends beyond Yemen and its neighbors, endangering global security and stability.[^cite_note-275]

On 17 March, tens of thousands of protesters marched in support of the Houthis in [Sanaa](18691665.md), [Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada), [Dhamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamar,_Yemen), [Al Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah), and ['Amran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Amran).[^cite_note-276]

#### Houthi military response

On 16 March, the Houthis stated that they fired 18 ballistic and cruise missiles and a drone at [USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman) and [its carrier strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_8).[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250316b-45] A US official claimed that 11 drones were intercepted by [F-16](11642.md) and [F-18](45998.md) fighter jets, while a Houthi missile malfunctioned and landed in the water.[^cite_note-277] On the morning of 17 March, the Houthis claimed another strike on USS *Harry S. Truman* and surrounding warships, consisting of 18 missiles and a drone.[^cite_note-AlJazeera20250317a-57] On the morning of 18 March, the Houthis claimed a third attack on the aircraft carrier and surrounding warships, which US general [Alexus Grynkewich](66715028.md) said was hard to confirm since their attacks "were missing their targets by over 100 miles".[^cite_note-JPost20250317-62][^cite_note-278] A fourth attack on the aircraft carrier, consisting of [cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missiles) and drones, was claimed by the Houthis on 19 March.[^cite_note-279]

According to a May 2025 report from *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, several American F-16s and an F-35 fighter jet were nearly struck by Houthi air defenses.[^cite_note-NYTimes20250512-34]

### Iran and Axis of Resistance

Following the attacks, commander-in-chief of the [IRGC](1285472.md) [Hossein Salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Salami) stated that the Houthis acted independently from Iran, and warned that "Iran will respond decisively and destructively if they take their threats into action", referring to Trump's threats against the country. The [Iranian Ministry of Foreign Affairs](1166971.md) called the attacks a violation of the [Charter of the United Nations](88805.md) and [international law](8195726.md). Foreign minister [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md) said that America had no authority over Iran's foreign policy.[^cite_note-Reuters20250315-15] The Supreme Leader of Iran, [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), condemned the US strikes on Yemen as a "crime that must be stopped".[^cite_note-280]

In a letter to the UN Secretary-General [António Guterres](471806.md) and the Security Council, Iran's permanent representative [Amir Saeed Iravani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Saeed_Iravani) stated that Trump and other US officials have made "reckless and provocative statements" containing "baseless accusations" and threats of force against Tehran. He dismissed claims made by Trump regarding [Iranian support to the Houthis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Houthi_relations), accusing the US president of attempting to "unlawfully justify acts of aggression and war crimes against Yemen". He warned that any aggression against Iran would have severe consequences, for which the US would be fully accountable, and urged the Security Council to take serious note of inflammatory statements and ensure compliance with the UN Charter.[^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283]

Lebanese militant group [Hezbollah](13919.md) issued a statement condemning the attacks.[^cite_note-284] Iraq's [Harakat Hezbollah al-Nujaba](48027962.md) also condemned the attacks and vowed to respond.[^cite_note-285]

Iran described the strikes on the Ras Issa fuel port on April 17 as "barbaric" while Hamas called them "blatant aggression."[^cite_note-TheGuardian20250418-286]

### International

[United Nations](31769.md) secretary-general [António Guterres](471806.md) called for a cessation of military activities in the region and warned that an escalation could destabilize Yemen and worsen the nation's humanitarian situation.[^cite_note-287]

[Russian](25391.md) foreign minister [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) called for an end to the strikes and urged for diplomatic talks to find a peaceful solution during a call with his US counterpart [Marco Rubio](5502549.md).[^cite_note-288]

[Chinese](5405.md) foreign ministry spokesperson [Mao Ning](71679066.md) called for dialogue and a deescalation, while affirming China's position against a military escalation in the region.[^cite_note-289]

[North Korean](21255.md) Ambassador to Egypt, Ma Dong-hee, who is also accredited to Yemen, condemns the attacks as a US threat to regional and global order.[^cite_note-290]

## Military plan leak

Main article: [United States government group chat leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government_group_chat_leaks)





Screenshots from the leaked chat showing [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) discussing plans for the attacks

The addition of *Atlantic* editor-in-chief Jeffrey Goldberg to Waltz's Signal chat group, and the subsequent revelation that Hegseth had sent attack plans in an unsecure platform, drew widespread criticism.



A screenshot of the leaked chat showing [Michael Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Waltz) and [JD Vance](53396477.md) discussing the result of the attacks

Some national security experts suggested that the coordination of the attacks over Signal likely violated the [Espionage Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espionage_Act_of_1917) and the [Federal Records Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Records_Act).[^cite_note-TheAtlantic20250324a-291][^cite_note-292] When questioned by reporters on 24 March, the day Goldberg's article about the leak was published, Hegseth responded, "Nobody was texting war plans, and that's all I have to say about that."[^cite_note-reu-24mar2025-293] Hegseth also described Goldberg as "deceitful and highly discredited".[^cite_note-guard2-24mar2025-294]

### Reactions of US politicians

On March 24, 2025, House minority leader [Hakeem Jeffries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakeem_Jeffries) called for a Congressional investigation to be opened to understand how this operational security breach occurred. Jeffries called the incident "reckless, irresponsible and dangerous".[^cite_note-APNews20250324-295]

Following confirmation by a spokesperson for the [National Security Council](44026.md) that the leaked group chat was real,[^cite_note-guard-24mar2025-36] [White House Press Secretary](284101.md) [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md) stated, "As President Trump said, the attacks on the Houthis have been highly successful and effective, President Trump continues to have the utmost confidence in his national security team, including National Security Advisor Mike Waltz".[^cite_note-APNews20250324-295]

Chairman of the [Senate Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services), Republican Senator [Roger Wicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Wicker) responded to the leak stating, "We're very concerned about it and we'll be looking into it on a [bipartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisanship) basis."[^cite_note-APNews20250324-295]

## Analysis

The expert think tank [Atlantic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Council) expressed doubt about the practicality of United States war aims. It stated that it will be challenging to track down Houthi leader [Abdul-Malik al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Malik_al-Houthi) due to "limited intelligence on the ground in Yemen," adding that this is the same reason behind the US's "difficulty assessing the success of its operations" [throughout its Red Sea operations in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian).[^cite_note-TheGuardian20250418-286]

*The New York Times* concluded that America had failed to establish air superiority over Ansar Allah while burning through ammunition reserves.[^cite_note-296]

*The Hill* opinionated that the Houthis will become stronger.[^cite_note-297]

An article in [Responsible Statecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsible_Statecraft) claimed there is hypocrisy in Trump policies for paying for war that Europe mostly benefits from.[^cite_note-298] Europe is mostly affected by the shipping lane disruption, while the US paid the cost of the bombings.

An article by [Middle East Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Monitor), a Qatar-funded pro-Hamas lobbying group, claims that the US failed to meet its strategic goals.[^cite_note-MiddleEastMonitor20250518-299]

## Aftermath

The bombing campaign had difficulty destroying the Houthis' underground arsenal of missiles, drones and launchers.[^cite_note-NYTimes20250404-14][^cite_note-MiddleEastMonitor20250518-299] The cost of the bombing campaign has been estimated to cost billions.[^cite_note-300]

After the ceasefire with the US was declared the Houthis fired ballistic missiles at Israel.[^cite_note-301][^cite_note-302]

In July 2025, the Houthis resumed attacks on commercial shipping, sinking two Liberian-flagged vessels.[^cite_note-303] In response, the EU launched [Operation Aspides](76128469.md) to provide limited protection to vessels using the Red Sea.
