**Death sentence with reprieve** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 死刑缓期执行, [abbr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation): 死缓) is a [criminal punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_punishment) of the criminal [law of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China). It is a two-year [suspended sentence](711640.md) where the [execution](5902.md) is only carried out if the convicted commits further crimes during the suspension period. The convicted remains incarcerated during the entire suspension period. After that period, the sentence is automatically reduced to [life imprisonment](148681.md), or to a 25-year prison sentence based on meritorious behavior.[^cite_note-\:0-1] The reprieve is integrated into the sentence, unlike a [pardon](288593.md) which occurs after the sentence.[^cite_note-Trevaskes-2] The punishment is found in chapter 5 (death penalty), sections 48, 50 and 51.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

Chinese courts hand down this form of sentencing more often than actual death sentences.[^cite_note-Trevaskes-2] The sentence emphasizes the severity of the crime and the mercy of the court, and comes from traditional Chinese jurisprudence.[^cite_note-Scobell-3] According to researchers, the post-2007 death penalty reforms resulted in a larger proportion of death sentences becoming suspended.[^cite_note-Miao-4] Based on the 2024 sentencing of [Yang Hengjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Hengjun) to death with reprieve for espionage charges, Ryan Mitchell, a law professor at the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong), stated that death with reprieve was used in recent years to reduce the rate of executions without abolishing capital punishment. He also stated that such sentences were typically reserved for serious crimes with potentially serious social consequences, and that they were rarely commuted to fixed-term imprisonment.[^cite_note-5]

The 2015 criminal law amendment allowed sentences to restrict commutation to only life imprisonment *without* the possibility of parole for convictions of bribery or "plundering the public treasury".[^cite_note-6] This was the case with [Bai Enpei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_Enpei) and [Fu Zhenghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_Zhenghua).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]
