**Hubei**[^cite_note-7] is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_China) in [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China). It has the [seventh-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) among Chinese provinces, the second-largest within Central China, and the third-largest among inland provinces. Its provincial capital at [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan) serves as a major political, cultural, and economic hub for the region.

Hubei is associated with the historical [state of E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_E) that existed during the [Western Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Zhou_dynasty) (c. 1045 – 771 BC). Its name means 'north of the lake', referring to [Dongting Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongting_Lake).[^cite_note-8] It borders [Henan](166408.md) to the north, [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui) and [Jiangxi](173817.md) to the east, [Hunan](166409.md) to the south, and [Chongqing](171752.md) and [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) to the west. The high-profile [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) is located at [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang) in the west of the province.

The Hubei region was home to sophisticated [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) cultures.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] By the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) (770–476 BC), the territory of today's Hubei formed part of the powerful [State of Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_\(state\)). Chu, nominally a tributary state of the [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty), was itself an extension of the Chinese civilization that had emerged some centuries before in the north; but Chu also represented a culturally unique blend of northern and southern culture, and it developed into a powerful state that controlled much of the middle and lower [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), with its power extending northwards into the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain).[^cite_note-11]



Detail of an embroidered silk gauze ritual garment from a 4th-century BC, [Zhou era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty) tomb at Mashan, [Jiangling County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangling_County), Hubei

During the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period) (475–221 BC) Chu became the major adversary of the upstart [State of Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) to the northwest (in present-day [Guanzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanzhong), [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) province), which began to assert itself by outward expansionism. As wars between Qin and Chu ensued, Chu lost more and more land: first its dominance over the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin), then (in 278 BC) its heartland, which corresponds to modern Hubei.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] In 223 BC Qin chased down the remnants of the Chu regime, which had fled eastwards [during Qin's wars of uniting China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin's_wars_of_unification).[^cite_note-14]

Qin founded the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty) in 221 BC, the first unified dynasty in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_proper). The Qin dynasty was succeeded in 206 BC by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), which established the province ([*zhou*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_\(political_division\))) of [Jingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingzhou_province) in today's Hubei and [Hunan](166409.md). The Qin and Han played an active role in the extension of farmland in Hubei, maintaining a system of river dikes to protect farms from summer floods.[^cite_note-15] Towards the end of the Eastern Han dynasty in the beginning of the 3rd century, Jingzhou was ruled by regional warlord [Liu Biao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Biao). After his death in 208, Liu Biao's realm was surrendered by [his successors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Cong_\(Han_dynasty\)) to [Cao Cao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_Cao), a powerful warlord who had conquered nearly all of north China; but in the [Battle of Red Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Red_Cliffs) (208 or 209), warlords [Liu Bei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Bei) and [Sun Quan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Quan) drove Cao Cao out of Jingzhou. Liu Bei then took control of Jingzhou and appointed Guan Yu as administrator of Xiangyang (in modern [Xiangyang, Hubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangyang,_Hubei)) to guard Jing province; he went on to conquer Yizhou (the Sichuan Basin), but lost Jingzhou to Sun Quan; for the next few decades Jingzhou was controlled by the [Wu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu), ruled by Sun Quan and his successors.[^cite_note-16]



Three Gorges area

The incursion of northern nomadic peoples into the region at the beginning of the 4th century ([Five Barbarians' rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_and_rebellion_of_the_Five_Barbarians) and [Disaster of Yongjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_of_Yongjia) ([永嘉之乱](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B0%B8%E5%98%89%E4%B9%8B%E4%B9%B1))) began nearly three centuries of division into a nomad-ruled (but increasingly Sinicized) north and a [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese)-ruled south. Hubei, to the south, remained under southern rule for this entire period, until the unification of China by the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) in 589. In 617 the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) replaced Sui, and later on the Tang dynasty placed present-day Hubei under the jurisdiction of several [circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(political_division\)): [Jiangnanxi Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnanxi_Circuit) in the south; [Shannandong Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannandong_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1) (山南东道) in the west, and [Huainan Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huainan_Circuit) in the east. After the Tang dynasty disintegrated in the early 10th century, Hubei came under the control of several regional regimes: [Jingnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingnan) in the center, [Yang Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)) and its successor [Southern Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tang) to the east, the [Five Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties) to the north and Shu to Shizhou (施州, in modern [Enshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshi_City), [Enshi Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshi_Tujia_and_Miao_Autonomous_Prefecture)).[^cite_note-17]

The [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) reunified the region in 982 and placed most of Hubei into [Jinghubei Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinghubei_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1), a longer version of Hubei's current name. [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) conquered the region in 1279, and under [their rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) the province of [Huguang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguang) was established, covering Hubei, Hunan, and parts of [Guangdong](65109.md) and [Guangxi](47836950.md).

The [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) (1368–1644) drove out the Mongols in 1368. Their version of Huguang province was smaller, and corresponded almost entirely to the modern provinces of Hubei and Hunan combined. Hubei lay geographically outside the centers of the Ming power. During the last years of the Ming, today's Hubei was ravaged several times by the rebel armies of [Zhang Xianzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Xianzhong) and [Li Zicheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zicheng). The [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu) [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) which took control of much of the region in 1644, soon split Huguang into the modern provinces of Hubei and Hunan. The Qing dynasty, however, continued to maintain a [Viceroy of Huguang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_Huguang), one of the most well-known viceroys being [Zhang Zhidong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Zhidong) (in office between 1889 and 1907), whose modernizing reforms made Hubei (especially [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan)) into a prosperous center of commerce and industry. The [Huangshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangshi)/[Daye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daye,_Hubei) area, south-east of Wuhan, became an important center of mining and metallurgy.[^cite_note-18]

In 1911, the [Wuchang Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_Uprising) took place in modern-day Wuhan. The uprising started the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution), which overthrew the Qing dynasty and established the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)). In 1927 Wuhan became the seat of a government established by left-wing elements of the [Kuomintang](16903.md), led by [Wang Jingwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jingwei); this government later merged into [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek)'s government in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing). During [World War II](32927.md) the eastern parts of Hubei were conquered and occupied by [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), while the western parts remained under Chinese control.

During the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) in the 1960s, Wuhan saw fighting between rival [Red Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Guards_\(China\)) factions. In July 1967, civil strife struck the city in the [Wuhan Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Incident) ("July 20th Incident"), an armed conflict between two hostile groups who were fighting for control over the city at the height of the Cultural Revolution.[^cite_note-19]

As the fears of a nuclear war increased during the time of [Sino-Soviet border conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_border_conflict) in the late 1960s, the [Xianning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianning) prefecture of Hubei was chosen as the site of [Project 131](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Project_131), an underground military-command headquarters.[^cite_note-20]



Yellow Crane Tower

The province—and Wuhan in particular—suffered severely from the [1954 Yangtze River Floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Yangtze_River_Floods). Large-scale dam construction followed, with the [Gezhouba Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezhouba_Dam) on the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) near [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang) started in 1970 and completed in 1988; the construction of the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam), further upstream, began in 1993. In the following years, authorities resettled millions of people from western Hubei to make way for the construction of the dam. A number of smaller dams have been constructed on the Yangtze's tributaries as well.

The [Xianning Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianning_Nuclear_Power_Plant) is planned in Dafanzhen, Tongshan County, Xianning, to host at least four 1,250-megawatt (MW) AP1000 pressurized-water reactors. Work on the site began in 2010; plans envisaged that the first reactor would start construction in 2011 and go online in 2015. However, construction of the first phase had yet to start as of 2018.

On 1 December 2019, the first case of [COVID-19](63030231.md) in the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) was identified in the city of [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan). In January 2020, the [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) virus was officially identified, leading local and federal governments to implement massive [quarantine](25237.md) zones across Hubei province, especially in the capital [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan) (the epicenter of the outbreak). Authorities partially or fully locked down 15 cities, directly affecting 57 million people. Following severe outbreaks in numerous other countries, including in different areas of the world, the [World Health Organization declared the COVID-19 a pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization's_response_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic) in March 2020. However, after more than eight weeks, the lockdown on most cities in the province was lifted.

The [Jianghan Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianghan_Plain) takes up most of central and southern Hubei, while the west and the peripheries are more [mountainous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain), with ranges such as the [Wudang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wudang_Mountains), the [Jing Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing_Mountains), the [Daba Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_Mountains), and the [Wu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Mountains) (in rough north-to-south order). The [Dabie Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabie_Mountains) lie to the northeast of the Jianghan Plain, on the border with [Henan](166408.md) and [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui); the Tongbai Mountains lie to the north on the border with [Henan](166408.md); to the southeast, the [Mufu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mufu_Mountains) form the border with [Jiangxi](173817.md). The highest peak in Hubei is [Shennong Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shennong_Peak?action=edit\&redlink=1), found in the [Daba Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_Mountains) of the [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) area of [Shennongjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shennongjia); it has an altitude of 3105 m.[^cite_note-21]



[Qing River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_River) in [Changyang Tujia Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changyang_Tujia_Autonomous_County), southwestern Hubei

The two major rivers of Hubei are the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) and its left tributary, the [Han River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshui_River); they lend their names to the [Jianghan Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianghan_Plain) – Jiang representing the Yangtze and han representing the Han River. The Yangtze River enters Hubei from the west via the [Three Gorges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges); the eastern half of the [Three Gorges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges) ([Xiling Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiling_Gorge) and part of [Wu Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Gorge)) lie in western Hubei, while the western half is in neighbouring [Chongqing](171752.md). The Han River enters the province from the northwest. After crossing most of the province, the two great rivers meet at the center of Wuhan, the provincial capital.

Among the notable tributaries of the Yangtze within the province are the [Shen Nong Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shen_Nong_Stream) (a small northern tributary, severely affected by the Three Gorges Dam project); the [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_River), a major waterway of southwestern Hubei; the [Huangbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangbo_River) near [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang); and the [Fushui River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushui_River) in the southeast.

Thousands of lakes dot the landscape of Hubei's Jianghan Plain, giving Hubei the name of "Province of Lakes"; the largest of these lakes are [Liangzi Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangzi_Lake) and [Hong Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Lake). The numerous hydrodams have created a number of large reservoirs, the largest of which is the [Danjiangkou Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danjiangkou_Reservoir) on the Han River, on the border between Hubei and [Henan](166408.md).

Hubei has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa* or *Cwa* under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), with four distinct seasons. Winters are cool to cold, with average temperatures of 1 to 6 °C (34 to 43 °F) in January, while summers are hot and humid, with average temperatures of 24 to 30 °C (75 to 86 °F) in July; punishing temperatures of 40 °C (104 °F) or above are widely associated with Wuhan, the provincial capital. The mountainous districts of western Hubei, in particular [Shennongjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shennongjia), with their cooler summers, attract numerous visitors from Wuhan and other lowland cities.

Besides the capital [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), other important cities are [Jingmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingmen); [Shiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiyan), a center of automotive industry and the gateway to the [Wudang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wudang_Mountains); [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang), the main base for the gigantic hydroelectric projects of southwestern Hubei; and [Shashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_District).

## Politics

Further information: [Governor of Hubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Hubei) and [Party Secretary of Hubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Hubei)

Like all [governing institutions in mainland China](23239.md), Hubei has a parallel party-government system,[^cite_note-PoliticalSystem2-32] in which the [CCP Hubei Provincial Committee Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Hubei) outranks the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Hubei).[^cite_note-33] The [CCP Hubei Provincial Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubei_Provincial_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) acts as the top policy-formulation body, and has control over the Hubei Provincial People's Government.

## Economy



The [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) on the Yangtze River

Hubei is often called the "Land of Fish and Rice" (鱼米之乡). Important agricultural products in Hubei include [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea), while industries include [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles), metallurgy, machinery, power generation, textiles, foodstuffs and high-tech commodities.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-34]

[Mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) resources that can be found in Hubei in significant quantities include [borax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borax), [hongshiite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongshiite?action=edit\&redlink=1), [wollastonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollastonite), [garnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet), [marlstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlstone), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), [rutile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutile), [rock salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_salt), [gold](12240.md) amalgam, [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) and [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium). The province's recoverable reserves of [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) stand at 548 million tons, which is modest compared to other Chinese provinces. Hubei is well known for its mines of fine turquoise and green faustite.

Hubei was a major recipient of China's investment in industrial capacity during the [Third Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Front_\(China\)) campaign.[^cite_note-\:6-35]<sup>: 298 </sup>



Tea plantations on the western slopes of the [Muyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muyu,_Hubei) Valley

Since completion in 2012, the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) in western Hubei provides plentiful [hydroelectricity](381399.md), with an average annual power production of 95 Twh. Existing hydroelectric stations include [Gezhouba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezhouba_Dam), [Danjiangkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danjiangkou), [Geheyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geheyan), [Hanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanjiang_Dam?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Duhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duhe), [Huanglongtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanglongtan?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Bailianhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailianhe?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Lushui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lushui) and [Fushui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushui).

Hubei is the [7th-largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) of China, the second largest in the [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China) region after [Henan](166408.md), the third largest in the [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) region after [Guangdong](65109.md) and Henan and the third largest among inland provinces after [Henan](166408.md) and [Sichuan](65185.md). As of 2021, Hubei's [nominal GDP](12594.md#Nominal_GDP_and_adjustments_to_GDP) was US\$787 billion (CNY 5 trillion). Its GDP (nominal) per capita exceeded US\$13,000, making it the [richest landlocked province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita), the richest province in the [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China) region, and 2nd richest province in [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) region after [Guangdong](65109.md).[^cite_note-\:0-36]

## Culture



[Hubei Provincial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubei_Provincial_Museum)



Hubei Museum of Art



Hubei Provincial Library

People in Hubei speak [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) dialects; most of these dialects are classified as [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin) dialects, a group that also encompasses the Mandarin dialects of most of southwestern China.

Perhaps the most celebrated element of [Hubei cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubei_cuisine) is the [Wuchang bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_bream), a freshwater [bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bream) that is commonly steamed.

Types of traditional [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera) popular in Hubei include [Hanju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_opera) and [Chuju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuju_\(Chinese_opera\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (楚剧; *Chǔ Jù*).

The [Shennongjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shennongjia) area is the alleged home of the *[Yeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeren)*, a wild undiscovered [hominid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae) that lives in the forested hills.

The people of Hubei are given the uncomplimentary nickname "[Nine-headed Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-headed_Bird)" by other Chinese, from a [mythological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mythology) creature said to be very aggressive and hard to kill. *"In the sky live nine-headed birds. On the earth live Hubei people."* (天上九头鸟，地上湖北佬; *Tiānshàng jiǔ tóu niǎo, dìshàng Húběi lǎo*)

[Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan) is one of the major culture centers in China.

Hubei is thought to be the province that originated the card game of *[dou dizhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dou_dizhu)*.

## Education

As of 2022, Hubei hosts 130 institutions of higher education, ranking sixth together with [Hunan](166409.md) (130) among all Chinese provinces after [Jiangsu](166412.md) (168), [Guangdong](65109.md) (160), [Henan](166408.md) (156), [Shandong](105032.md) (153), and [Sichuan](65185.md) (134).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-\:1-58] The [Huazhong University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huazhong_University_of_Science_and_Technology)(HUST), [Wuhan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_University) and many other institutions in Wuhan make it a hub of higher education and research in China. Wuhan is the city that has the largest college student population in the world (1.3 million) studying in its 89 universities.

## Transportation



Boats on the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan)

Prior to the construction of China's national railway network, the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) and [Hanshui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshui) Rivers had been the main transportation arteries of Hubei for many centuries, and still continue to play an important transport role.

Historically, Hubei's overland transport network was hampered by the lack of bridges across the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), which divides the province into northern and southern regions. The first bridge across the Yangtze in Hubei, the [Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Yangtze_River_Bridge) was completed in 1957, followed by the Zhicheng Bridge in 1971. As of October 2014, Hubei had [23 bridges and tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels) across the Yangtze River, including nine bridges and three tunnels in Wuhan.

### Rail

The [railway from Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingguang_railway) reached Wuhan in 1905, and was later extended to Guangzhou, becoming the first north-to-south railway mainline to cross China. A number of other lines crossed the province later on, including the [Jiaozuo–Liuzhou railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozuo%E2%80%93Liuzhou_railway) and [Beijing–Kowloon railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Kowloon_railway), respectively, in the western and eastern part of the province.

The first decade of the 21st century has seen a large amount of new railway construction in Hubei. The [Wuhan–Guangzhou high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Guangzhou_high-speed_railway), roughly parallel to the original Wuhan-Guangzhou line, opened in late 2009, it was subsequently extended to the north, to Beijing becoming the [Beijing–Guangzhou high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Guangzhou_high-speed_railway). An east-west high-speed corridor connecting major cities along the Yangtze, the [Shanghai–Wuhan–Chengdu passenger railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Wuhan%E2%80%93Chengdu_passenger_railway) was gradually opened between 2008 and 2012, the [Wuhan–Yichang railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Yichang_railway) section of it opening in 2012.[^cite_note-59] The [Wuhan–Xiaogan intercity railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Xiaogan_intercity_railway) was opened in December 2016 and it was extended when the [Wuhan–Shiyan high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Shiyan_high-speed_railway) opened in November 2019.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-IRG-61]

### Air

Hubei's main airport is [Wuhan Tianhe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Tianhe_International_Airport). [Yichang Sanxia Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang_Sanxia_Airport) serves the Three Gorges region. There are also passenger airports [in Xiangyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangyang_Airport), [Enshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshi_Airport), and [Jingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingzhou) ([Shashi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Airport), named after the city's [Shashi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_District)).

## Tourism

The province's best-known natural attraction (shared with the adjacent [Chongqing](171752.md) municipality) is the scenic area of the [Three Gorges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges) of the Yangtze. Located in the far west of the province, the gorges can be conveniently visited by one of the numerous tourist boats (or regular passenger boats) that travel up the Yangtze from [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang) through the [Three Gorges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges) and into the neighboring [Chongqing](171752.md) municipality.

The [mountains of western Hubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_Mountains), in particular in [Shennongjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shennongjia) District, offer a welcome respite from Wuhan's and Yichang's summer heat, as well as skiing opportunities in winter. The tourist facilities in that area concentrate around [Muyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muyu,_Hubei) in the southern part of [Shennongjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shennongjia), the gateway to Shennongjia National Nature Reserve (神农架国家自然保护区). Closer to the provincial capital, Wuhan, is the [Mount Jiugong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Jiugong?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*Jiugongshan*) national park, in [Tongshan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongshan_County,_Hubei) near the border with [Jiangxi](173817.md).

A particular important site of both natural and cultural significance is [Mount Wudang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Wudang) (*Wudangshan*) in the northwest of the province. Originally created early in the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), its building complex has been listed by [UNESCO](21786641.md) since 1994 as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).

The province also has historical sites connected with China's more recent history, such as the [Wuchang Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_Uprising) Memorial in Wuhan, [Project 131](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Project_131) site (a Cultural-Revolution-era underground military command center) in [Xianning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianning), and the National Mining Park (国家矿山公园) in [Huangshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangshi).[^cite_note-63]

## Sister State/Twinning

Following a July 1979 State of Ohio Trade Mission to China, Hubei and [Ohio](22199.md) formed a sister province-state relationship.[^cite_note-\:05-64]<sup>: 111–113 </sup> The pairing was based on the fact that both Hubei and Ohio are located in national heartlands, are large industrial areas and transportation hubs, and have significant agricultural sectors.[^cite_note-\:05-64]<sup>: 113 </sup>

In 2005, Hubei province signed a twinning agreement with [Telemark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemark) county of Norway, and a "Norway-Hubei Week" was held in 2007.
