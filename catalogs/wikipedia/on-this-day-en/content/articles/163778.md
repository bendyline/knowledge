# Automotive industry

The **automotive industry** comprises a wide range of [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company) and [organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation) involved in the [design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design), [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_development), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), [marketing](59252.md), [selling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selling), [repairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maintenance), and [modification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custom_car) of [motor vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] It is one of the world's largest [industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(economics\)) by [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue) with global automotive market at \~\$2.75 trillion in 2025.[^cite_note-3] Companies involved in the assembly and marketing of automotive vehicles are heavily dependent on other firms in their [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain).[^cite_note-4]

The word *automotive* comes from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) *autos* (self), and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *motivus* (of [motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion)), referring to any form of self-powered vehicle. This term, as proposed by [Elmer Sperry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Ambrose_Sperry)[^cite_note-STS_of_US-5] (1860–1930), first came into use to describe automobiles in 1898.[^cite_note-6]

The automotive industry began in the 1860s, with hundreds of manufacturers pioneering the [horseless carriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_Era_car). Early car manufacturing involved manual assembly by a human worker. The process evolved from engineers working on a stationary car to a conveyor belt system where the car passed through multiple stations of more specialised engineers. In the 1960s, robotic equipment was introduced, and most cars are now mainly assembled by automated machinery.[^cite_note-7]

For many decades, the [United States](23576520.md) led the world in total automobile production, with the "[Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(automobile_manufacturers\))" companies, [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), [Ford](30433662.md), and [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler), being the world's three largest auto manufacturers for a time, and G.M. and Ford remaining the two largest until the mid-2000s. In 1929, before the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), the world had 32,028,500 automobiles in use, of which the U.S. automobile enterprises produced more than 90%. At that time, the U.S. had one car per 4.87 persons.[^cite_note-8] After 1945, the U.S. produced around three-quarters of the world's auto production. In 1980, the U.S. was overtaken by [Japan](30864860.md) and then became a world leader again in 1994. Japan narrowly passed the U.S. in production during 2006 and 2007, and in 2008 also [China](8518766.md), which in 2009 took the top spot (from Japan) with 13.8 million units, although the U.S. surpassed Japan in 2011, to become the second-largest automobile industry. In 2024, China produced more than 31 million vehicles in a year, after breaking 30 million in 2023, reaching 29 million for the first time in 2017 and 28 million the year before. In 2024, China produced the most passenger cars in the world, with Japan, India, Germany, and South Korea trailing. This was achieved by Chinese car companies signing joint ventures with foreign manufacturers.[^cite_note-9] From 1970 (140 models) to 1998 (260 models) to 2012 (684 models), the number of automobile models in the U.S. has grown nearly twofold.[^cite_note-10] As of 2026, China releases 542 new models ([road legal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street-legal_vehicle), production cars) every six months.[^cite_note-11]

During the 2020s, European automakers faced increasing competition from Chinese manufacturers, particularly in the [electric vehicle](279350.md) market, while adapting to the [EU's planned phase-out of new fossil-fuel vehicles by 2035](55503807.md). Chinese brands such as [BYD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Company) expanded their presence in Europe, while European manufacturers faced the costs of transitioning to electric vehicles. [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group) and other major European manufacturers responded with restructuring measures amid weak profitability and growing competition.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## Safety

Main article: [Automobile safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_safety)

See also: [2009–2011 Toyota vehicle recalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%932011_Toyota_vehicle_recalls), [General Motors ignition switch recalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_ignition_switch_recalls), and [Firestone and Ford tire controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firestone_and_Ford_tire_controversy)



A 2010 [Hyundai Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Tucson) used for a [crash test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_test) by the [Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_Institute_for_Highway_Safety)

Safety is a state that implies being protected from any risk, danger, damage, or cause of injury. In the automotive industry, safety means that users, operators, or [manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturers) do not face any risk or danger coming from the motor vehicle or its spare parts. Safety for the automobiles themselves implies that there is no risk of damage.

Safety in the automotive industry is particularly important and therefore highly regulated. [Automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles) and other [motor vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicles) have to comply with a certain number of regulations, whether local or international, in order to be accepted on the market. The standard [ISO 26262](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_26262), is considered one of the best practice frameworks for achieving automotive [functional safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_safety).[^cite_note-14]

In case of safety issues, danger, [product defect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_defect),[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] or faulty procedure during the manufacturing of the motor vehicle, the maker can request to return either a batch or the entire production run. This procedure is called [product recall](892586.md). Product recalls happen in every industry and can be production-related or stem from raw materials.

Product and operation tests and inspections at different stages of the [value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_chain) are made to avoid these product recalls by ensuring end-user security and safety and compliance with the automotive industry requirements. However, the automotive industry is still particularly concerned about product recalls, which cause considerable financial consequences.

## Economy

See also: [Automotive industry by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_by_country)



An advertisement for the [Pontiac 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac_6), c. 1928

In 2007, there were about 806 million cars and light trucks on the road, consuming over 980 billion litres (980,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of [petrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrol) and [diesel fuel](81761.md) yearly.[^cite_note-17] The automobile is a primary mode of [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) for many developed economies. The Detroit branch of [Boston Consulting Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Consulting_Group) predicted that, by 2014, one-third of world demand would be in the four [BRIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRIC_\(economics_term\)) markets (Brazil, Russia, India, and China). Meanwhile, in developed countries, the automotive industry has slowed.[^cite_note-twnside1-18] It is also expected that this trend will continue, especially as the younger generations of people (in highly urbanised countries) no longer want to own a car, and prefer other modes of transport.[^cite_note-19] Other potentially powerful automotive markets are [Iran](14653.md) and [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-20] Emerging automobile markets already buy more cars than established markets. The industry's expansion has also led to specialised academic programs in automotive marketing and dealership management; for example, [Northwood University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_University) in Michigan hosts the annual Northwood University International Auto Show, which is the largest student-run outdoor automotive exhibition in North America.[^cite_note-21]

According to a J.D. Power study, emerging markets accounted for 51 percent of the global [light-vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_commercial_vehicle) sales in 2010. The study, performed in 2010 expected this trend to accelerate.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] However, more recent reports (2012) confirmed the opposite; namely that the automotive industry was slowing down even in BRIC countries.[^cite_note-twnside1-18] In the United States, vehicle sales peaked in 2000, at 17.8 million units.[^cite_note-24]

In July 2021, the European Commission released its "[Fit for 55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal)" legislation package,[^cite_note-25] which contains important guidelines for the future of the automotive industry; all new cars on the European market must be [zero-emission vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-emissions_vehicle) from 2035.[^cite_note-26]

The governments of 24 developed countries and a group of major car manufacturers including [GM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), [Ford](30433662.md), [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo), [BYD Auto](1461760.md), [Jaguar Land Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Land_Rover) and [Mercedes-Benz](18564164.md) committed to "work towards all sales of new cars and vans being zero emission globally by 2040, and by no later than 2035 in leading markets".[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Major car manufacturing nations like the United States, Germany, China, Japan and South Korea, as well as [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen), [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota), [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot), [Honda](13729.md), [Nissan](21240.md) and [Hyundai](42257.md), did not pledge.[^cite_note-29]

## Environmental impacts



Trucks' share of US vehicles produced, has tripled since 1975. Though vehicle fuel efficiency has increased within each category, the overall trend toward less efficient types of vehicles has offset some of the benefits of greater fuel economy and reduction of carbon dioxide emissions.

[^cite_note-EPA_AutomotiveTrends_202212-30]

&#x20;Without the shift towards SUVs, energy use per unit distance could have fallen 30% more than it did from 2010 to 2022.

[^cite_note-GlobalFuelEfficInit_202311-31]

The global automotive industry is a major consumer of water. Some estimates surpass 180,000 L (39,000 imp gal) of water per car manufactured, depending on whether tyre production is included. Production processes that use a significant volume of water include surface treatment, painting, coating, washing, cooling, air-conditioning, and boilers, not counting component manufacturing. Paintshop operations consume especially large amounts of water because equipment running on water-based products must also be cleaned with water.[^cite_note-32]

In 2022, Tesla's [Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_Berlin-Brandenburg) ran into legal challenges due to droughts and falling groundwater levels in the region. Brandenburg's Economy Minister Joerg Steinbach said that while water supply was sufficient during the first stage, more would be needed once Tesla expands the site. The factory would nearly double the water consumption in the Gruenheide area, with 1.4 million cubic meters being contracted from local authorities per year — enough for a city of around 40,000 people. Steinbach said that the authorities would like to drill for more water there and outsource any additional supply if necessary.[^cite_note-33]

Road transport is a major source of [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md), with passenger cars and vans accounting for more than 60% of road transport emissions globally. Automakers have responded to increasingly stringent emissions standards and environmental policies by improving fuel efficiency and developing hybrid and [electric vehicles](279350.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] However, the industry has also faced controversy over emissions testing, most notably the [Dieselgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieselgate) scandal that began in 2015 when [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen) was found to have used software to manipulate emissions tests; subsequent investigations and legal proceedings also implicated other manufacturers.[^cite_note-36]

The expansion of electric vehicles has also increased demand for battery minerals such as [lithium](17561.md), [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) and [cobalt](24580536.md), whose extraction can cause local environmental damage, including [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), habitat destruction and water pollution. In the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), a major source of cobalt, mining has contributed to environmental degradation, while nickel mining has been associated with deforestation and pollution in countries such as [Indonesia](14579.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]
