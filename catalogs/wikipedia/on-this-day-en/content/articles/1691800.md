**Pangasinan**, officially the **Province of Pangasinan** ([Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language): *Luyag/Probinsia na Pangasinan*, [\[paŋɡasiˈnan\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA);[^cite_note-4] [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Probinsia ti Pangasinan*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Lalawigan ng Pangasinan*), is a coastal [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region) of [Luzon](69375.md). Its capital is [Lingayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen,_Pangasinan) while [San Carlos City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos,_Pangasinan) is the most populous. Pangasinan is in the western part of [Luzon](69375.md) along the [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf) and the [South China Sea](74209.md). The province covers a total land area of 5,451.01 square kilometres (2,104.65 sq mi).[^cite_note-nscblist-1] According to the 2020 census, it has a population of 3,188,540.[^cite_note-5] The official number of registered voters in Pangasinan is 2,156,306.[^cite_note-6] The western portion of the province is part of the homeland of the [Sambal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people), while the central and eastern portions are the homeland of the [Pangasinan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people). Due to ethnic migration, the [Ilocano people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) settled in the province.

The name "Pangasinan" refers to the province, its people and the spoken language. Indigenous Pangasinan speakers are estimated to number at least 2 million. The Pangasinan language is official in the province and is one of the officially recognized regional languages in the Philippines. Several ethnic groups enrich the cultural fabric of the province. Almost all of the people are Pangasinans and the rest are descendants of the Bolinao and Ilocano who settled the eastern and western parts of the province.[^cite_note-7] Pangasinan is spoken as a second language by many ethnic minorities in Pangasinan. The minority ethnic groups are the [Bolinao-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolinao_language) [Zambals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambal_people), and [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people).

Popular tourist attractions in Pangasinan include the [Hundred Islands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Islands_National_Park) in [Alaminos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaminos,_Pangasinan) and the white-sand beaches of [Bolinao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolinao,_Pangasinan) and [Dasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasol,_Pangasinan). [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan) is known for its Bangus Festival ("Milkfish Festival"). Pangasinan is also known for its mangoes and ceramic oven-baked *[Puto Calasiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puto_Calasiao)* (native rice cake). Pangasinan occupies a [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic) [geo-political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geo-political) position in the central plain of Luzon. Pangasinan has been described as the gateway to northern Luzon.

## Etymology

The name *Pangasinan* means "place of salt" or "place of salt-making"; it is derived from the prefix *pang-*, meaning "for", the root word *asin*, meaning "salt", and suffix *-an*, signifying "location". The [Spanish form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_orthography) of the province's name, *Pangasinán*, remains predominant, albeit without [diacritics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diacritics) and so does its pronunciation: [\[paŋɡasiˈnan\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish). The province is a major producer of salt in the Philippines. Its major products include [bagoong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagoong) ("shrimp-paste") and alamang ("salted-[krill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krill)").

## History

### Early history

Main articles: [Pangasinan (historical region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_\(historical_region\)) and [Caboloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caboloan)



A map of Pangasinan (yellow), Caboloan (brown) and surrounding settlements during the 15th-16th centuries.

Pangasinan, like the rest of the Philippines, was settled by [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) by sea during the [Austronesian expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_expansion). They established settlements along the [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf) and was part of the [ancient Austronesian trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_maritime_trade_network) to [India](14533.md), [China](5405.md), and [Japan](15573.md), since at least the 8th century. The primary industry along the coastal areas was salt-making, which is the origin of the name "Pangasinan" ("place of salt-making"). The interior lands were called "[Caboloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caboloan)" ("place of bolo bamboos"), referring to the abundance of bolo bamboo (*Gigantochloa levis*).[^cite_note-officialhistory-8]

Pangasinan is identified with "[Feng-jia-shi-lan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_\(historical_region\))" which appears in [Ming Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Dynasty) Chinese records. They are believed to have sent emissaries to China with symbolic "tributes" from 1403 to 1405, which was [required to establish trade relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tributary_states_of_China#Ming).[^cite_note-Scott-9]

In the 16th century, Pangasinan was called the "Port of Japan" by the Spanish. The locals wore native apparel typical of other maritime Southeast Asian ethnic groups in addition to Japanese and Chinese silks. Even common people were clad in Chinese and Japanese cotton garments. They blackened their teeth and were disgusted by the white teeth of foreigners which were likened to that of animals. They used porcelain jars typical of Japanese and Chinese households. Japanese-style gunpowder weapons were encountered in naval battles in the area.[^cite_note-10] In exchange for these goods, traders from all over Asia would come to trade primarily for gold and slaves, but also deerskins, [civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civet) and other local products. Other than a notably more extensive trade network with Japan and China they were culturally similar to other Luzon groups to the south.

Pangasinans were also described as a warlike people who were known for their resistance to Spanish conquest. Bishop Domingo Salazar described Pangasinans as the fiercest and cruelest in the land. They were untouched by Christianity but like Christians they used vintage wine in small quantities for sacramental practices. The church bragged that they, not the Spanish military, won the northern part of the Philippines for Spain. The church was strict with adulterers; the punishment was death for both parties. Pangasinans were known to take defeated [Sambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people), ([Aeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeta)) and [Negrito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito) warriors to sell as slaves to Chinese traders.[^cite_note-11]

### Spanish colonial era

On April 27, 1565, the Spanish [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi) arrived in the Philippine islands with about 500 soldiers and established a Spanish settlement. On May 24, 1570, the Spanish forces defeated [Rajah Sulayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Sulayman) and other rulers of Manila and later declared [Manila](184334.md) the capital of the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies). After securing Manila, the Spanish forces conquered the rest of the island of Luzon, including Pangasinan.

#### Provincia de Pangasinán

In 1571, the Spanish conquest of Pangasinan began with an expedition by the Spanish [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) [Martín de Goiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Goiti), who came from the Spanish settlement in Manila through [Pampanga](255602.md). About a year later another Spanish conquistador, [Juan de Salcedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Salcedo), sailed to [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf) and landed at the mouth of the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River). [Limahong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limahong), a Chinese pirate, fled to Pangasinan after his fleet was driven away from Manila in 1574. Limahong failed to establish a colony in Pangasinan, as an army led by de Salcedo chased him out of Pangasinan after a seven-month siege.

What is known today as the Province of Pangasinan dates back to an administrative and judicial district as early as 1580. Its capital was Lingayen, but its territorial boundaries were first delineated in 1611. Lingayen has remained the capital of the province except for a brief period during the revolutionary era when [San Carlos City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos,_Pangasinan) served as temporary administrative headquarters and during the slightly longer Japanese occupation, when [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan) was the capital.[^cite_note-History_of_Pangasinan-12]

The province of Pangasinan was formerly classified as an *alcaldía mayor de término* or first-class civil province during the Spanish regime. Its territorial jurisdiction once included most of the province of [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) and portions of what are now the Provinces of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) and La Union.[^cite_note-History_of_Pangasinan-12] By the end of the 1700s, Pangasinan had 19,836 native families and 719 [Spanish Filipino families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos).[^cite_note-Estadismo1-13]<sup>: 539 </sup>[^cite_note-Estadismo2-14]<sup>: 31, 54, 113 </sup>

### Rebellion against the Spanish rule

#### Malong liberation

Andres Malong, a native chief of the town of Binalatongan (now named San Carlos City), liberated the province from Spanish rule in December 1660. The people of Pangasinan proclaimed Andres Malong *Ari na Pangasinan* ("King of Pangasinan"). Pangasinan armies attempted to liberate the neighboring provinces of Pampanga and Ilocos, but were repelled by a Spanish-led coalition of loyalist tribal warriors and mercenaries. In February 1661, the newly independent Kingdom of Pangasinan fell to the [Captaincy General of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines).

#### Palaris Liberation

On November 3, 1762, the people of Pangasinan proclaimed independence from Spain after a rebellion led by [Juan de la Cruz Palaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_la_Cruz_Palaris). The Pangasinan Revolt was sparked by news that Manila had fallen to the British on October 6, 1762. The [Traité de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)) ended the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War) among Britain, France, and Spain on March 1, 1763. On January 16, 1765, Juan de la Cruz Palaris was captured and Pangasinan was again subjugated by the Spanish.

### Philippine Revolution

Main article: [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution)

The [Katipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan), a nationalist secret society, was founded on July 7, 1892, with the aim of uniting the peoples of the Philippines in the fight for independence and religious freedom. The [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) began on August 23, 1896, and was led by the *Supremo*, [Andrés Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Bonifacio). On November 18, 1897, a Katipunan council was formed in Western Pangasinan with Presidente Generalisimo Roman Manalang and General Mauro Ortiz.

General [Emilio Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Aguinaldo) proclaimed Philippine independence on June 12, 1898. [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan), the major commercial center of Pangasinan, was surrounded by Katipunan forces on July 18, 1898. The Battle of Dagupan lasted from July 18 to 23 of that year with the surrender of 1,500 Spanish soldiers under Commander Federico J. Ceballos and Governor Joaquin de Orengochea. It was fought by local Katipuneros under the command of General [Francisco Makabulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Makabulos) and the last remnants of the once mighty Spanish Army under General Ceballos. Three local heroes fought in the five-day battle, Don Daniel Maramba of Santa Barbara, Don Vicente Prado of San Jacinto and Don Juan Quezada of Dagupan. Their armies amassed in Dagupan making a last stand at the brick-walled Catholic Church.



Grave of Don Daniel B. Maramba ([Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara,_Pangasinan))

Maramba led the liberation of the town of Santa Barbara on March 7, 1898, following a signal for simultaneous attack from Makabulos. Hearing that Santa Barbara fell to rebel forces, the Spanish in Dagupan attempted to retake the town but were repelled by Maramba's forces. After the setback, the Spanish decided to concentrate their forces in Lingayen in order to protect the provincial capital. This allowed Maramba to expand his operations to include Malasiqui, Urdaneta and Mapandan which he defeated in succession. He then defeated the town of Mangaldan before proceeding to the last Spanish garrison in Dagupan. On March 7, 1898, rebels under the command of Prado and Quesada attacked convents in the province of Zambales which now constitute western Pangasinan.

Attacked and brought under Filipino control were Alaminos, Agno, Anda, Alos, Bani, Balincaguin, Bolinao, Dasol, Eguia and Potot. The revolt then spread to Labrador, Sual, Salasa and many other towns in the west. The towns of Sual, Labrador, Lingayen, Salasa and Bayambang were occupied first by the forces of Prado and Quesada before they attacked Dagupan.

On April 17, 1898, General Makabulos appointed Prado as Politico-Military Governor of Pangasinan, with Quesada as his second-in-command. In May 1898, General Emilio Aguinaldo returned from his exile in Hong Kong following the signing of the Pact of Biac-na-Bato in December 1897. Aguinaldo's return renewed the flames of the revolution. On June 3, 1898, General Makabulos entered Tarlac.

So successful were the [Katipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan) in their many pitched battles against Spanish forces that on June 30, 1898, Spanish authorities decided to evacuate all forces to Dagupan for a last stand against the rebels. All civilian and military personnel, including volunteers from towns not yet in rebel hands, were ordered to go to Dagupan. Those who heeded this order were the volunteer forces of Mangaldan, San Jacinto, Pozorrubio, Manaoag, and Villasis. Among the items brought to Dagupan was the image of the Most Holy Rosary of the Virgin of Manaoag, the patroness of Pangasinan.

The siege began when the forces of Maramba and Prado converged in Dagupan on July 18, 1898. The arrival of General Makabulos strengthened the rebel forces until the Spanish, holed up inside the Catholic Church, surrendered five days later. The poorly armed Filipino rebels were no match for the Spanish and loyal Filipino soldiers holed inside the Church. The tempo of battle changed when the attackers under the command of Don Vicente Prado devised a crude means of protection to shield them from Spanish fire while advancing. They used trunks of bananas bundled up in sawali which enabled them to move towards the Church.

### American Colonial Era

In 1901, towns of Nueva Ecija, [Balungao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balungao), [Rosales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosales,_Pangasinan), [San Quintin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Quintin,_Pangasinan) and [Umingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umingan) were annexed to the province of Pangasinan, because they were further away from the capital and already considered pacified by US forces.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

On November 30, 1903, several municipalities from northern Zambales including Agno, Alaminos, Anda, Bani, Bolinao, Burgos, Dasol, Infanta and Mabini were ceded to Pangasinan by the American colonial government for historical basis.

Pangasinan and other parts of the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies) were ceded to the Americans after the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1898\)), which ended the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War). During the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War) Lieutenant Col. [José Torres Bugallón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Torres_Bugall%C3%B3n) of Salasa fought together with Gen. Antonio Luna to defend the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) against American colonization in Northern Luzon. Bugallon was killed in battle on February 5, 1899. The First Philippine Republic was abolished in 1901. In 1907 the Philippine Assembly was established and for the first time five residents of Pangasinan were elected as its district representatives.

In 1921, Mauro Navarro, representing Pangasinan in the [Philippine Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Assembly), sponsored a law to rename the town of Salasa to Bugallon in honor of General Bugallon.

[Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) was inaugurated as the first president of the [Commonwealth of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) with collaboration from the [United States of America](3434750.md) on November 15, 1935.

The 21st Infantry Division were stationed in Pangasinan during the pre-World War II era. Anti-Japanese Imperial military operations included the fall of Bataan and Corregidor along with aiding the USAFFE ground force from January to May 1942 and the Japanese Insurgencies and Allied Liberation in Pangasinan from 1942 to 1945.

### Postwar era

After the declaration of Independence on July 4, 1946, Eugenio Perez, a [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Philippines\)) congressman representing the [fourth district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Pangasinan#4th_District) of Pangasinan, was elected Speaker of the lower [Legislative House](293605.md). He led the House until 1953 when the [Nacionalista Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalista_Party) became the dominant party.

### Martial law era

Main articles: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) and [Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship)

Pangasinan, which is historically and geographically part of the [Central Luzon Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), was made politically part of the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region) (Region I) by the gerrymandering of [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), even though Pangasinan has a distinct language and culture, Pangasinan. The political classification of Pangasinan as part of the Ilocos Region generated confusion among some Filipinos. The residents of Pangasinan are Ilocanos, even though Ilocanos constitute a minority in the province. Its economy is larger than the Ilocano provinces of Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur and La Union and its population is more than 50 percent of the population of Region 1.

The Philippine economy took a turn for the worse in the late 1960s and early 1970s, with the [1969 Philippine balance of payments crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_balance_of_payments_crisis) being one of the early landmark events.[^cite_note-Balbosas1992-16] Economic analysts generally attribute this to the ramp-up on loan-funded government spending to promote [Ferdinand Marcos’ 1969 reelection campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos%E2%80%99_1969_reelection_campaign).[^cite_note-Balbosas1992-16][^cite_note-Balisacan\&Hill2003-17][^cite_note-Cororaton1997-18][^cite_note-Kessler1989-19] <sup>: "43" </sup>[^cite_note-Celoza1997-20][^cite_note-21] Many students from Pangasinan were part of the protests that began in 1970 and continued through 1971 and early 1972, calling for government and economic reforms.[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-22] In September 1972, one year before the expected end of his last constitutionally allowed term as president in 1973, Ferdinand Marcos finally placed the Philippines under [Martial Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos)[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-22] - an act which allowed him to remain in power for fourteen more years, during which Pangasinan went through many social and economic ups and downs.[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-22]

The [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship), [crony capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronies_of_Ferdinand_Marcos), [propagandistic construction projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edifice_complex), and [personal expensive lifestyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unexplained_wealth_of_the_Marcos_family) of the Marcos Family[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-22] prompted opposition from various Filipino citizens despite the risks of arrest and [torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torture_methods_used_by_the_Marcos_dictatorship).[^cite_note-23] The Pangasinan [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) (PC) Provincial Camp in [Lingayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen)[^cite_note-Panaglagip-24]<sup>: 48 </sup> (renamed Camp Antonio Sison in 2017)[^cite_note-25] was so full of [political prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_detainees_under_the_Marcos_dictatorship) that the chapel, which measured a mere 4x9 meters, was divided in two by a wall of bars, with one part treated as a jail.[^cite_note-Panaglagip-24]<sup>: 174 </sup> Among the most prominent human rights violations victims the regime were student leader Eduardo Aquino who was from [Mapandan, Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapandan,_Pangasinan) and beauty queen turned activist Maita Gomez of [Bautista, Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bautista,_Pangasinan). Aquino was ambushed by Soldiers during a meeting of activists in Tarlac, while Gomez was jailed numerous times, but survived to be co-founder of women's rights group [GABRIELA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GABRIELA). Both were eventually recognized by having their names posthumously inscribed on the wall of remembrance of the Philippines' [Bantayog ng mga Bayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayog_ng_mga_Bayani), which honors the martyrs and heroes which fought the dictatorship.

### During and after the People Power revolution

In February 1986, both the COMELEC and the NAMFREL counts showed that Corazon Aquino had won the city of Dagupan (in the north along with the provinces of Benguet and Mountain Province) during the [1986 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Philippine_presidential_election),[^cite_note-Pawilen2021SolidNorthMyth-26] despite widely acknowledged election fraud meant to assure a victory for [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Weeks later, Vice Chief of Staff General [Fidel V. Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_V._Ramos), a Pangasinense who was head of the Philippine Integrated National Police, became an instrumental figure in the [EDSA people power revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution) which removed Ferdinand Marcos and compelled him to flee the country.

After the downfall of Marcos all local government unit executives were ordered by [President](271126.md) [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) to vacate their posts. Some local executives were ordered to return to their seats, as in the case of Mayor Ludovico Espinosa of [Dasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasol), who claimed to have joined UNIDO during the height of the [EDSA Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution). Fidel Ramos was appointed as AFP Chief of Staff and later as Defense Secretary replacing [Juan Ponce Enrile](3322486.md). [Oscar Orbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Orbos), a congressman from Bani, was appointed by Aquino to head the [Department of Transportation and Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation_and_Communications) and later to Executive Secretary.

On May 11, 1992, Fidel V. Ramos [ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Philippine_general_election) for [President](271126.md). He became the first Pangasinense President of the Philippines. Under his leadership, the Philippines recovered from the oil and power crisis of 1991. His influence sparked the economic growth of Pangasinan when it hosted the 1995 *Palarong Pambansa* (Philippine National Games).

[Jose de Venecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_de_Venecia), who represented the same district as Eugenio Perez (his former father-in-law), was the second Pangasinense to become Speaker of the House of Representatives in 1992. He was reelected again in 1995. De Venecia was selected by the Ramos' administration party Lakas NUCD to be its presidential candidate in 1998. De Venecia [ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Philippine_general_election) but lost to [Vice President](2271639.md) [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada). Oscar Orbos [ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Philippine_general_election) for vice president but lost to [Senator](299521.md) [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo), whose mother, former [First Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ladies_and_Gentlemen_of_the_Philippines) [Evangelina Macaraeg-Macapagal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Macapagal), hails from [Binalonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binalonan).

Arroyo ascended to the presidency after the second [EDSA Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_II) when President [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada) was overthrown.

### Contemporary

In May 2004, actor-turned-politician [Fernando Poe, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Poe,_Jr.) of San Carlos ran for president against incumbent [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo). The Pangasinan vote was split by the two presidential candidates, both with Pangasinan roots. Arroyo was elected President, but her victory was marred by charges of electoral fraud and vote-buying.

## Geography

### Physical

Pangasinan is located in the west central area of [Luzon](69375.md) in the Philippines. It is bordered by [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union) to the north, [Benguet](274018.md) and [Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Vizcaya) to the northeast, [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) to the southeast, and [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) and [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) to the south. To the west of Pangasinan is the [West Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philippine_Sea). The province also encloses [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf).

The province has a land area of 5,451.01 square kilometres (2,104.65 sq mi).[^cite_note-nscb-29] It is 170 kilometres (110 mi) north of [Manila](184334.md), 50 kilometres (31 mi) south of [Baguio](162480.md), 115 kilometres (71 mi) north of Subic International Airport and Seaport, and 80 square kilometres (31 sq mi) north of [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport). At the coast of [Alaminos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaminos,_Pangasinan), the [Hundred islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Islands_National_Park) have become a famous tourist spot.

The terrain of the province, as part of the Central Luzon plains, is typically flat, with a few parts being hilly and/or mountainous. The northeastern municipalities of [San Manuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Manuel,_Pangasinan), [San Nicolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Nicolas,_Pangasinan), [Natividad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natividad,_Pangasinan), [San Quintin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Quintin,_Pangasinan) and [Umingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umingan,_Pangasinan) have hilly to mountainous areas at the tip of the [Cordillera mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)). The [Zambales mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales_mountains) extend to the province's western towns of [Labrador](2069089.md), [Mabini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabini,_Pangasinan), [Bugallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugallon,_Pangasinan), [Aguilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguilar,_Pangasinan), [Mangatarem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangatarem,_Pangasinan), [Dasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasol,_Pangasinan), and [Infanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanta,_Pangasinan) forming the mountainous portions of those towns.

The [Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology](12830378.md) (PHIVOLCS) reported several [inactive volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano#Popular_classification_of_volcanoes) in the province: [Amorong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Amorong), [Balungao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Balungao), Cabaluyan, Cahelietan, Candong, and Malabobo. PHIVOLCS reported no active or potentially active volcanoes in Pangasinan. A [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera)-like [landform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landform) is located between the towns of [Malasiqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malasiqui) and [Villasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villasis) with a center at about 15° 55′ N and 120° 30′ E near the Cabaruan Hills.

Several rivers traverse the province. The longest is the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River) which originates in the [Cordillera Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)) of [Benguet](274018.md) and eventually terminates at [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf). Other major rivers include the [Bued River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bued_River), [Angalacan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angalacan_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sinocalan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinocalan_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Pantal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantal_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Patalan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patalan_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Cayanga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayanga_River?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### Barangays

Pangasinan has 1,364 barangays comprising its 44 municipalities and 4 cities, ranking the province at 3rd with the most barangays in a Philippine province, only behind the Visayan provinces of [Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte_\(province\)) and [Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo).

Longos Amangonan Parac‑Parac Fabrica is the longest named barangay in the Philippines, it is situated in the municipality of [San Fabian, Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fabian,_Pangasinan). The most populous barangay in the province is [Bonuan Gueset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonuan_Gueset) in [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan) with a population of 22,042 as of 2010. If cities are excluded, *Poblacion* in the municipality of [Lingayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen,_Pangasinan) has the highest population at 12,642. *Iton* in [Bayambang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayambang) has the lowest with only 99 as of the 2010 census.[^cite_note-NSO10–01-32]

Further information: [List of barangays in Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_barangays_in_Pangasinan)

The [Pangasinan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people) (Totoon Pangasinan) are called Pangasinan or the Hispanicized name *Pangasinense*, or simply *taga-Pangasinan*, which means "native of Pangasinan". Pangasinan people were known as traders, businesspeople, farmers and fishers. Pangasinan is the third most-populated province in the Philippines. The estimated population of the indigenous speakers of the Pangasinan language in the province of Pangasinan is almost 2 million and is projected to double in about 30 years. According to the 2000 census, 47 percent of the population are native Pangasinan and 44 percent are [Ilocano settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people). Indigenous [Sambal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people) predominate in the westernmost municipalities of [Bolinao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolinao,_Pangasinan) and [Anda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anda,_Pangasinan), with others scattered in towns formerly under Zambales. The few ethnic Tagalogs mostly live in towns bordering Tagalog-speaking Nueva Ecija, mostly descendants of settlers from Nueva Ecija itself, with the rest from [Bulacan](252408.md) and [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)). The Pangasinan people are closely related to the [Austronesian-speaking peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people) of the [Philippines](23440.md) as well as [Indonesia](14579.md) and [Malaysia](3607937.md).

Main articles: [Pangasinan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language), [Ilocano language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Bolinao language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolinao_language), and [Sambal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_language)

The Pangasinan language belongs to the [Malayo-Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian_languages) branch of the [Austronesian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages) family.[^cite_note-Pang-Austro-39] It is classified under the **[Southern Cordilleran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cordilleran_languages)** branch of the [Northern Philippine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Philippine_languages) family.

While closely related to the languages of neighboring [Benguet](274018.md), Pangasinan is the sole member of the **Pangasinic** sub-branch. It is a sister language to the **Nuclear Southern Cordilleran** group.[^cite_note-Himes1998-40]

The relationship within the Southern Cordilleran branch is structured as follows:

- **Pangasinic**&#x20;

  - [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language)

- **Nuclear Southern Cordilleran**&#x20;

  - [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language)

  - [Karao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karao_language)

  - [Iwak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwaak_language)

  - [Kallahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallahan_language) (including Keley-i, Kayapa, and Tinoc)

Aside from their native language, many educated Pangasinans are highly proficient in [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language). Pangasinan is mostly spoken in the central part of the province in the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th districts, is also spoken by many residents as 1st language in eastern part of the province in the 5th and 6th districts, and is the second language in other parts of Pangasinan. [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language) is widely spoken in the westernmost and easternmost parts of Pangasinan in the 1st, 5th and 6th districts, and is the second language in other parts of Pangasinan. Ilocanos and Pangasinans speak Ilocano with a Pangasinan accent, as descendants of Ilocanos from first generation who lived within Pangasinan population learned Pangasinan language. Not all of Pangasinans speak Ilocano, as Pangasinan was part of Central Luzon before it was transferred to Ilocos Region. [Bolinao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolinao_language), a [Sambalic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalic_languages) is widely spoken in the western tip of the province in the towns of Bolinao and Anda; [Sambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_language), another Sambalic language, is spoken in other towns formerly under Zambales. Like Pangasinan people, many educated Bolinao-speaking Sambals and other Sambals are highly fluent in Ilocano, Tagalog, & English, aside from Pangasinan. Sambals are already fluent in Ilocano, as west Pangasinan formerly under Zambales was settled by Ilocano settlers, & interaction between Sambals & Ilocanos happened as years pass by. Tagalog is spoken by residents in towns along the border with Nueva Ecija.

Languages not native in Pangasinan (aside from Ilocano) are spoken by other minority ethnic groups, such as [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language) which is related to Sambalic languages, [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language) and [Cordilleran languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordilleran_languages) to varying degrees by their respective ethnic communities within the province.

The dominant religion in Pangasinan is [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) with 80% affiliation in the population. The [Aglipayan Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aglipayan_Church) comes in second with 9% of the population. Other religious denominations are divided with other Christian groups such as Members Church of God International. Iglesia Ni Cristo has 5 Ecclesiastical Districts (Rosales, Urdaneta, San Carlos, Lingayen and Alaminos). Each town and barangays has already INC locale chapels and has 5-6% adherence in the province. Baptist, Methodist, [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) has a temple in Pangasinan, dedicated on April 28, 2024, called the [Urdaneta Philippines Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdaneta_Philippines_Temple), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) which have 4 Pangasinan-speaking Circuits (PN-03 (A & B), PN-04, PN-05, and PN-06) and 5 Iloko-speaking Circuits (PN-01, PN-02, PN-07, PN-08, and PN-09), and Seventh-day Adventist. Few are strict believers and continue to practice their indigenous [anito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anito) beliefs and rituals, like most of the people of the Philippines.

[Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_people) and [American](3434750.md) missionaries introduced Christianity to Pangasinan. Prior to the Spanish conquest in 1571, the predominant religion of the people of Pangasinan was similar to the indigenous religion of the highland [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot) or the inhabitants of the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region) on the island of Luzon, who mostly retained their indigenous culture and religion. A translation of the New Testament (excluding Revelation) in the Pangasinan language by [Fr. Nicolas Manrique Alonzo Lallave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manrique_Alonso_Lallave), a Spanish Dominican friar assigned in Urdaneta, was the first ever translation of a complete portion of the Bible in a Philippine language. Pangasinan was also influenced by [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Islam](6037917.md) to a lesser extent before the introduction of Christianity. Some Pangasinense people have reverted to their indigenous religion of worshiping Ama Kaoley or Ama-Gaolay, while the [Sambal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people) of the west have reverted to their indigenous religion worshiping Malayari.[^cite_note-ncca.gov.ph-42][^cite_note-aswangproject.com-43]

### Economic indicators

The province's **economy** is mainly agricultural due to its vast fertile plains. More than 44 percent of its agricultural area is devoted to crop production. Aside from being one of the Philippine's rice granaries, **Pangasinan** is also a major producer of coconut, mango and eggplant. Pangasinan is the richest province in the Ilocos Region.[^cite_note-53]

Within Pangasinan are many highways, notably the [N114 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N114_highway), [N56 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N56_highway_\(Philippines\)), [Urdaneta–Dagupan Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdaneta%E2%80%93Dagupan_Road), [Romulo Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulo_Highway), [MacArthur Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Highway) and [Olongapo–Bugallon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo%E2%80%93Bugallon_Road), as well as the [Tarlac–Pangasinan–La Union Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac%E2%80%93Pangasinan%E2%80%93La_Union_Expressway).

### Energy

The [1200 megawatt Sual coal-fired power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sual_Power_Station) and [345 megawatt San Roque multi-purpose dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Roque_Dam_\(Philippines\)) in the municipalities of Sual and San Manuel, respectively, are the primary sources of energy in the province.

### Marine

Pangasinan is a major fish supplier in Luzon and a major producer of salt in the Philippines. It has extensive fishponds mostly for raising *bangus* or "milkfish" along the coasts of [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf) and the [West Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philippine_Sea). Pangasinan's aquaculture includes oyster and sea urchin farms.

Salt is also a major industry. In [salt evaporation ponds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_evaporation_pond) seawater is mixed with [sodium bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_bicarbonate) until the water evaporates and the salt remains. This is their ancient tradition inspired from Egypt.

### Agriculture

The major crops in Pangasinan are [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangoes), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), and [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane). Pangasinan has a land area of 536,819 hectares, and 44 percent of the total land area of Pangasinan is devoted to agricultural production.

### Financial

Pangasinan has 593 banking and financing institutions.

## Health and education

There are thousands of public schools and hundreds of private schools across the province for primary and secondary education. The province has six Schools Division Offices (SDOs), including four city SDOs corresponding to its number of cities. In 1991, the Schools Division Office of Pangasinan was split into Pangasinan I (based in [Lingayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen)) and Pangasinan II (based in [Binalonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binalonan)) to address the growing population and educational demands. Pangasinan I covers the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd congressional districts, while Pangasinan II covers the 4th, 5th, and 6th. Many Pangasinans go to [Metro Manila](72340.md), Baguio, and the [United States](3434750.md) for tertiary and higher education.

Pangasinan has 51 hospitals and clinics and 68 rural health units (as of July 2002). Although some residents go to other parts of the Philippines, Metro Manila, Europe and the United States for extensive medical tests and treatment, almost all Pangasinense go to the major medical centers in the cities of [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan),[Alaminos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaminos,_Pangasinan), [San Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos_City,_Pangasinan) and [Urdaneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdaneta_City).

## Culture

The culture of Pangasinan is a blend of the indigenous Malayo-Polynesian and western Hispanic culture with some [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) influences as well as American influences. Pangasinan is westernized yet retains a strong native Austronesian background.

The main centers of Pangasinan culture are [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan), [Lingayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen,_Pangasinan), [Manaoag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaoag,_Pangasinan), [Calasiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calasiao) and [San Carlos City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos_City,_Pangasinan).
