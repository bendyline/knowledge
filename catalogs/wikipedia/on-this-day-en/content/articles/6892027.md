**Melissa Anne Hortman** ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Haluptzok**; May 27, 1970 – June 14, 2025) was an American politician and lawyer who served as the 61st [speaker of the Minnesota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives). A member of the [Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party), she represented northern parts of the [Twin Cities metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_metropolitan_area) in the [Minnesota House of Representatives](254414.md) from 2005 to 2025, serving as the [House minority leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minority_leaders_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives) from 2017 to 2019 and as speaker from 2019 to January 2025. During her tenure, she advocated for transportation, environmental rights, abortion rights, police reform, and gun control policies. She was also the chief author of the state's solar energy standard.

On June 14, 2025, Hortman and her husband were [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Melissa_Hortman) in a politically motivated attack at their home in [Brooklyn Park, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Park,_Minnesota). Her dog was also killed in the attack.[^cite_note-1]

## Early life and education

Hortman was born Melissa Anne Haluptzok in [Fridley, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridley,_Minnesota), on May 27, 1970.[^cite_note-archive-2] She grew up in [Spring Lake Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Lake_Park,_Minnesota) and [Andover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover,_Minnesota), and became interested in being a politician at age ten, while watching coverage of the [1980 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-\:1-3] She graduated from [Blaine High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine_High_School_\(Minnesota\)) in [Blaine, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine,_Minnesota), in 1988.[^cite_note-4]

Hortman received a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) (*magna cum laude*) with a major in philosophy and political science from [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) in 1991, a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) (*cum laude*) from the [University of Minnesota Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Law_School) in 1995, and a [Master in Public Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_in_Public_Administration) from [Harvard Kennedy School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Kennedy_School) in 2018.[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-6]

## Early career

Hortman worked as an intern in the [United States Senate](24909346.md) for [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) and [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry), and clerked for Judge John Sommerville while in law school. She served on the [Brooklyn Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Park,_Minnesota) City Human Relations Commission and worked as an assistant county attorney for [Hennepin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hennepin_County).[^cite_note-\:0-5] She first gained public attention in 1997, as a lawyer on a case involving [housing discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_discrimination) by landlords; she won a \$490,181 civil award for her client, which was "then the largest such award in state history".[^cite_note-7]

## Minnesota House of Representatives

Hortman on the House Floor surrounded by reporters

Hortman speaks to press after becoming Speaker of the House (January 2019).

Hortman was elected to the Minnesota House of Representatives in 2004, defeating [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Minnesota) incumbent [Stephanie Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Olsen), and was reelected every two years thereafter until her death. She first ran unsuccessfully in 1998, and again in 2002.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

Hortman served as an assistant majority leader from 2007 to 2010 and as minority whip from 2011 to 2012. From 2013 to 2014 she served as House speaker pro tempore and chaired the Energy Policy Committee. She served as deputy minority leader from 2015 to 2016. After [Paul Thissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thissen) retired, Hortman was elected by her caucus to be [minority leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_Leader_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives) from 2017 to 2018. When the DFL caucus retook the majority in the 2018 election, her colleagues elected her Speaker of the House.[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-8]

In her first term, Hortman was an outspoken advocate for the [Northstar Commuter Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northstar_Commuter_Rail) line, which ran through her district. She also supported a new stadium for the [Minnesota Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Twins). She was an advocate for environmental issues and the effort to bring the 2020 Summer Olympics to Minnesota. She was pro-choice,[^cite_note-9] supported gun control policies,[^cite_note-10] and opposed voter identification initiatives.[^cite_note-11] In 2008, Hortman managed the DFL floor operation during a successful attempt to override then-Governor [Tim Pawlenty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Pawlenty)'s veto of a gas tax increase.[^cite_note-12] While chair of the Energy Policy Committee, she was the chief author of the state's solar energy standard and [community solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_solar) laws.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Hortman served as the [minority leader of the Minnesota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minority_leaders_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives) from 2017 to 2019[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-15] and as the 61st [Speaker of the Minnesota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives) from 2019 to 2025.[^cite_note-16]

In April 2017, Hortman drew attention when she criticized several Republican representatives for playing a card game in the retiring room off the House chamber during discussions on a public safety budget bill. Specifically, she called attention to the fact that the game was occurring while two Democratic women from ethnic minorities were delivering personal speeches. Hortman continued to back her statement, while some Republicans called for her to resign.[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-17]

In July 2020, Hortman helped negotiate a police reform bill that "limits the use of neck restraints and chokeholds, prohibits departments from offering controversial warrior-style training, and creates an independent state unit to investigate when a police officer kills someone or is accused of sexual misconduct".[^cite_note-18]

From the start of the [94th Minnesota Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Minnesota_Legislature) in January 2025 until early February, Hortman led a boycott of House sessions to deny Republicans a [quorum](237014.md) to conduct business.[^cite_note-KAALTV-19] Republican [Lisa Demuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Demuth) was elected speaker on February 6.[^cite_note-20] After a power-sharing agreement was reached, Hortman served again as minority leader until March 17, when a special election created a tie between the parties in the House; her title then changed to "DFL Leader" and she was granted significant powers alongside Demuth.[^cite_note-21]

In June 2025, during a one-day special legislative session to complete the state budget, Hortman voted with Republicans in favor of legislation removing undocumented adult immigrants from eligibility for [MinnesotaCare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MinnesotaCare) as part of a negotiated compromise between the GOP and the DFL to pass a budget. She was the only DFL member of the House to support the provision, which passed by a single vote.[^cite_note-22]

## Personal life

Hortman was married to Mark Hortman, with whom she had two children. They lived in [Brooklyn Park, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Park,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-\:0-5] Hortman was [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) and taught [Sunday school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_school) in [Blaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-\:2-23]

## Assassination

Main article: [2025 shootings of Minnesota legislators](80209838.md)

On June 14, 2025, Hortman, her husband, and their golden retriever Gilbert[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] were shot and killed at their home by a man impersonating a police officer.[^cite_note-auto1-26][^cite_note-27] Another lawmaker, [John Hoffman](38683745.md), and his wife were injured earlier that night by the same man.[^cite_note-auto1-26] [Minnesota](19590.md) governor [Tim Walz](2216593.md) said the shooting appeared to be a politically motivated assassination.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

The suspect in the shooting was 57-year-old [Vance Luther Boelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vance_Luther_Boelter). Boelter was arrested on June 15 after a large-scale manhunt.[^cite_note-axios/20250614220932-30][^cite_note-startribune/601373135-31][^cite_note-wired/2025-06-14--20-06-01-32][^cite_note-33]

Earlier on June 15, U.S. Senator [Amy Klobuchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Klobuchar) called Hortman's killing a "politically motivated assassination" and said that authorities believed Boelter was hiding somewhere in the Midwest and "might" still be in Minnesota.[^cite_note-34] Boelter was apprehended in [Sibley County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibley_County) after being spotted by a resident's [trail camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_camera) and by drones operated by law enforcement.[^cite_note-35] The capture of and charges against Boelter were announced at a press conference led by the U.S. Attorney for the District of Minnesota, [Joseph H. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_H._Thompson_\(attorney\)), on June 16.[^cite_note-36]

On July 23, 2026, Vance Luther Boelter was sentenced to two consecutive life terms plus 40 years in prison for the politically motivated crime. Boelter pled guilty to federal charges in June 2026 to avoid the death penalty. Separate state charges, including murder, attempted murder, and animal cruelty remain pending as of July.[^cite_note-37]

### Memorials, lying in state and funeral



Melissa Hortman, her husband Mark, and their dog Gilbert all lying in state at the Minnesota State Capitol



A flag at [Yosemite National Park](48664.md) flying at half-staff on June 16 after Hortman's assassination

Flowers, notes and pictures were left at the Minnesota State Capitol to memorialize Hortman. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) issued a statement on the day of the murders. On June 16, Sophie and Colin Hortman, the children of Melissa and Mark, issued a statement calling for "hope and resilience".[^cite_note-38] Red roses, a photograph, and a gavel were placed on her desk.[^cite_note-39] On June 17, a formal procession of law enforcement vehicles transported the bodies of Hortman and her husband to a funeral home in Minneapolis. Remarks by U.S. Senate Minority Leader [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer), Senate Minority Whip [Dick Durbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Durbin), Senators [Amy Klobuchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Klobuchar) and [Tina Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Smith), and Representative [Kelly Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Morrison) were read into the Congressional record.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

On the evening of June 18, a candlelight vigil was held on the Minnesota State Capitol steps.[^cite_note-45] Minnesota lawmakers and Governor Walz and his wife attended.[^cite_note-46]

Melissa Hortman, Mark Hortman and the ashes of their dog Gilbert [lay in state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_in_state) in the Minnesota State Capitol rotunda on June 27.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Hortman was the first woman to receive that honor.[^cite_note-50] Former President [Joe Biden](145422.md) came through the line to pay respects to the Hortmans; Walz and his wife were among the first in line of 7,500 for the viewing.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

The funeral took place at the [Basilica of Saint Mary in Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Mary_\(Minneapolis\)) on June 28. Attendees included former U.S. President Joe Biden and former Vice President Kamala Harris, along with Governor Tim Walz, Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison, Lieutenant Governor Peggy Flanagan, First Lady Gwen Walz, Second Gentleman Tom Weber, and former Governor Mark Dayton.[^cite_note-53] Current and former state legislators from both parties and other elected officials were among the 1,500 people in attendance.[^cite_note-54] Walz gave a eulogy and the Hortmans' children made remarks, as well as serving as pallbearers[^cite_note-55] along with Minnesota state representative [Zack Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Stephenson). Also present were Archbishop [Bernard Hebda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Hebda), of the [Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Saint_Paul_and_Minneapolis), and [Richard Pates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pates), ninth bishop of the [Diocese of Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Des_Moines). The service concluded with a state honors ceremony, including a flyover and tolling of the bells.[^cite_note-56] Representative [John Hoffman](38683745.md) and his family issued a statement after the funeral.[^cite_note-57] Hortman's love of gardening was honored with pins of trees or shrubs for all funeral attendees, a tree planting initiative,[^cite_note-58] and the I-35W bridge lit green on June 27 and 28.[^cite_note-59]

On the first day of its 2026 session, the Minnesota Legislature held a ceremony honoring Hortman.[^cite_note-convene-26-60]

## Honors and accolades

In 2019, the Minnesota Milk Producers Association recognized Hortman as its Legislator of the Year for her work "securing the 2019 Agriculture, Rural Development, and Housing budget which included investing \$8 million for the Dairy Assistance Investment, and Relief Initiative".[^cite_note-61] Hortman won awards from the 2020 caucus for her bipartisan work.[^cite_note-62] She also won awards from Conservation Minnesota.[^cite_note-63]

In 2023, Hortman was honored at the [Humphrey-Mondale Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey-Mondale_Awards) and received the Joan and Walter Mondale Award for Public Service.[^cite_note-64] In 2026, the Melissa & Mark Hortman Award for Dedication and Leadership in Public Policy was created as a sixth Humphrey-Mondale Award in honor of the Hortmans. The award's first recipients were Hortman's senior staff.[^cite_note-65]
