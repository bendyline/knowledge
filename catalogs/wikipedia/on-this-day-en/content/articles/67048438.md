&#x20;**Syria–United Kingdom** relations refers to the bilateral relations between the [Syrian Arab Republic](7515849.md) and the [United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland](31717.md).

During the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), the United Kingdom supported the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition).[^cite_note-MacFarquhar_Mourtada_2012-1] The United Kingdom closed its embassy in Syria in 2012, and the [Embassy of Syria, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Syria,_London) was closed the following year.[^cite_note-2] After the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md), the resumption of relations was announced in July 2025.[^cite_note-3]

## History

### 20th century

#### World War I and Deir ez-Zor occupation

During [World War I](4764461.md), the United Kingdom played a significant role in the [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) victory in Syria, collaborating with Arab [Sharifian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharifian_Army) during the [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt), which was supported by British Army officer [T. E. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._E._Lawrence). This partnership helped secure major victories, including the [capture of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Damascus_\(1918\)) in 1918.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFalls1930589-4]

Following the war, the implementation of the [Sykes-Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes-Picot_Agreement) shaped the division of the region, granting Britain influence outside Syria. On 11 January 1919, British forces occupied [Deir ez-Zor](1429146.md), annexing it to Iraq. However, local resistance, supported by Iraqi officers and tribes, culminated in the city's liberation on 27 December 1919, as British troops withdrew to Iraq.[^cite_note-5]

#### World War II and Levant Crisis

In 1941, the British launched the [Syria-Lebanon Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria-Lebanon_Campaign) to counter [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French) influence in the region, fearing it could bolster [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers). British troops, alongside [Free French forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_forces) and Commonwealth allies, successfully invaded Syria and Lebanon, driving out Vichy French forces and establishing control under the Free French government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoulthard-Clark1998192–193-6]

The British later became involved in the [Levant Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant_Crisis) of 1945, as tensions between the French and Syrians escalated. When French forces bombarded Damascus in May 1945 to suppress growing independence movements, the British intervened militarily, pressuring the French to withdraw. This intervention paved the way for [Syrian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_independence) and the end of French colonial rule, solidifying Britain's role in shaping the region's post-war order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoubayed201365-7]

#### 1950s

On 6 November 1956, during the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis), a [Royal Air Force](25679.md) [Canberra PR.7 was sent to overfly Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._58_Squadron_RAF#Post-war_operations) on a photo reconnaissance mission, and was shot down by a [Syrian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Air_Force) [Gloster Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloster_Meteor). As of 2022, this was the last RAF aircraft shot down in an enemy air-to-air engagement.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-aerialcombat-20160802-9]

### 21st century

#### Ba'athist Syria



Former [First Lady of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lady_of_Syria) [Asma al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asma_al-Assad) was born and raised in [England](9316.md).

[^cite_note-10]



A pro-Assad demonstrator holds up a "Don't bomb Syria" sign during a protest in [London](17867.md) in November 2015.

In 2001, positive relations were developed between [Prime Minister](24150.md) [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) and the [Syrian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Government), as part of the [war on terror](13425800.md).[^cite_note-11]

In 2002, [President](516106.md) [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) made an official visit to the United Kingdom, the first Syrian leader to do so.[^cite_note-12] He and his wife Asma met with Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-13]

In 2003, the [British Syrian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Syrian_Society) was established in [London](17867.md) by [Fawaz Akhras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fawaz_Akhras), father-in-law of Bashar al-Assad.[^cite_note-14]

#### Syrian civil war (2011–2024)

Since the emergence of [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) in 2011, relations have deteriorated, and the UK was one of the first countries to recognise the opposition as the sole legitimate representative of the Syrian people.[^cite_note-MacFarquhar_Mourtada_2012-1] The [Embassy of Syria in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Syria,_London) closed in 2013.[^cite_note-15]

In 2018, the UK took part in the [missile strikes against Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_missile_strikes_against_Syria) alongside the [United States](3434750.md) and [France](5843419.md).[^cite_note-16]

In March 2021, the [British Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Government) placed sanctions on key allies of [Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_Government).[^cite_note-17]

#### Post-Assad regime (2024–present)



Prime Minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) met Syrian President [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) at [Downing Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downing_Street), 31 March 2026

British prime minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) and foreign secretary [David Lammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lammy) welcomed the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) on 8 December 2024.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] A week later, the UK announced a £50 million aid package to support food, shelter, healthcare, and restoring essential services in Syria.[^cite_note-20]

In January 2025, the UK took part in the [Riyadh meetings on Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_meetings_on_Syria_\(2025\)), which focused on addressing the ongoing situation in Syria and exploring measures to promote stability in the country. During the meetings, UK Foreign Secretary David Lammy held talks with Syrian Foreign Minister [Asaad al-Shaibani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asaad_al-Shaibani).[^cite_note-21] Two months later, the United Kingdom pledged an additional £160 million to Syria for aid.[^cite_note-22]

On 24 April 2025, the UK lifted sanctions on several Syrian government agencies, including the [Ministry of the Interior](34679004.md), the [Ministry of Defense](2774942.md), and the [General Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Intelligence_Service_\(Syria\)), as well as the police, air force, military, and state-run media.[^cite_note-23]

On 5 July 2025, British foreign secretary Lammy announced that relations would be reestablished during a visit to Damascus.[^cite_note-24] In addition, the United Kingdom pledged £94.5 million to provide urgent humanitarian aid, support Syria's long-term recovery, and assist neighboring countries hosting Syrian refugees.[^cite_note-25] The Syrian embassy in London was reopened on 13 November 2025.[^cite_note-26]

On 31 March 2026, Syrian President [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) met UK Prime Minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) in London to discuss cooperation, security, and reconstruction.[^cite_note-27] The following day, he was received by [King Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Charles_III) at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace), in a symbolic step reflecting renewed engagement between the United Kingdom and Syria.[^cite_note-28] Al-Sharaa became the first Syrian head of state to visit London since former president [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) visited the United Kingdom in 2002.[^cite_note-29]
