**MoM-BH\*-1** is an astronomical object classified as a [little red dot](78148157.md), a class of astronomical objects of reddish colouration, located in the constellation of [Cetus](6362.md). In 2026, it was proposed that MoM-BH\*-1 is a [black hole star](14493178.md) (also known as a [quasi-star](14493178.md)), composed of a central black hole surrounded by a large and dense gas cloud, the first object to be identified as such.[^cite_note-Naidu-2026-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Sample-2026-3]

## Discovery

The object was found during the "Mirage or Miracle" (MoM) survey conducted with the [James Webb Space Telescope](434221.md) (JWST). MoM-BH\*-1 was imaged by JWST in three surveys, the first in the PRIMER survey, using the [Mid-Infrared Instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Infrared_Instrument) (MIRI) on 5 and 16 January 2023 and [NIRCam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIRCam) on 7 and 9 August 2023, the second by the EXCELS survey on 19 December 2023, and by the MoM survey on 15 December 2024.[^cite_note-Naidu-2026-1] It was named after the survey, MoM, and an abbreviation of "black hole star – one".[^cite_note-4]

### Black hole star candidate

In 2026, a research team led by Rohan P. Naidu, a [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi)[^cite_note-5] astronomer, identified MoM-BH\*-1 as an "enshrouded black hole" of around 10<sup>6.3</sup> [*M*<sub>☉</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_mass).[^cite_note-Naidu-2026-1] The similarity of the object with other little red dots suggests that they may be black hole stars as well.[^cite_note-Sample-2026-3][^cite_note-6]

## Location

Location of MoM-BH\*-1 (circled) within the constellation Cetus

The object, at billions of light years from Earth, is thought to have formed 660 million years after the Big Bang.[^cite_note-Sample-2026-3] Its host galaxy is a low-mass [dwarf galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_galaxy), "perhaps" [metal-poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallicity), with a mass of less than 10<sup>8.5</sup> *M*<sub>☉</sub>. According to Naidu et al. (2026), "\[r]adiation from the black hole seems to dominate almost all observed light, leaving limited room for contribution from its host galaxy."[^cite_note-Naidu-2026-1] Naidu described the discovery as "one in a billion!"[^cite_note-7]
