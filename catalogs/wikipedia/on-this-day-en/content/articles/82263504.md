**Storm Kristin** was a compact, catastrophic and record-breaking [extratropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone) that severely impacted Portugal, as well as parts of the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) and [Southeastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Europe) in late January 2026. Storm Kristin was the twenty-sixth storm of the [2025–26 European windstorm season](80394473.md), and the eleventh to be named by the south-western naming group, which consists of [France](5843419.md), Spain, Portugal, [Belgium](3343.md), and [Luxembourg](17515.md).[^cite_note-9] Kristin was named by the [IPMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPMA) on 27 January, as a significant impact was forecast.[^cite_note-10] The impact of Kristin and its successors was described as a [humanitarian crisis](1251700.md) by a researcher in extreme weather and climate change.[^cite_note-11]

Kristin achieved record-breaking status by rapidly intensifying in the hours before landfall. Kristin made [landfall](5016402.md) at peak intensity in the [Leiria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiria_district) district, which resulted in extremely intense winds in multiple Portuguese districts. The IPMA measured the strongest gust in Portuguese history, surpassing the record of [Hurricane Leslie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Leslie) in 2018. The extreme wind gusts of more than 200 km/h were attributed to a [sting jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_jet), that developed as the cyclone made landfall.[^cite_note-12] A storm like Kristin had an estimated return period of 80–90 years, based on a reanalysis.[^cite_note-13] Before disappearing, Kristin also affected Spain severely and caused heavy precipitation and thunderstorms in Italy and Greece.[^cite_note-14]

Kristin especially affected the Portuguese districts of Leiria, [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra_district), and [Santarém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santar%C3%A9m_District) severely, but also affected other districts in Portugal such as the [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_District) district. After moving through Portugal, Kristin also struck Spain with wind gusts of more than 170 km/h. As a result of Kristin, Portugal experienced its largest [blackout](296636.md) in history.[^cite_note-15] Around 1,000,000 customers in Portugal were without power, according to [E-Redes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-Redes).[^cite_note-16] Approximately 170,000 customers experienced power outages in [Andalusia](2736.md). Kristin caused long-lasting and widespread communication outages in Portugal, lasting for several months.[^cite_note-17] Kristin was responsible for a significant ecological impact in parts of Portugal, as tens of millions of trees fell, which raised concerns for potential wildfires in the summer of 2026.[^cite_note-18] The storm had a widespread and severe impact on emotional well-being in the affected areas. Kristin prompted national and international solidarity actions and humanitarian aid efforts.

Over 2,000 injuries were reported in Portugal, 177 direct and 2,000 indirect. Furthermore, 14 deaths were attributed to Kristin in Portugal, six direct and eight indirect. One death and 12 injuries were reported in Spain. Estimated monetary losses in Portugal were more than €6 billion, which was more than 1.6% of the country's [GDP](12594.md) in 2026, making Kristin the most damaging storm on record for Portugal. Initial government estimates indicated losses of €1.2 billion in Spain, bringing the total amount of losses by Kristin to more than €7.2 billion.

Kristin's successor, [storm Leonardo](82296738.md), was responsible for causing major floodings in parts of Portugal, which exacerbated the impact of Kristin.[^cite_note-19]

## Storm name

On 27 January, the IPMA used the name "Kristin" for the twenty-sixth storm of the 2025–26 European windstorm season and the eleventh storm to be named by the south-western naming group, as red warnings were issued for parts of Portugal. Kristin is a Scandinavian name and its meanings are "Christian" or "follower of Christ".[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Meteorological history



An ERA5 data visualization of the jet stream on January 28 2026.

At the end of January 2026, there was a persistent anticyclonic [blocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(meteorology\)) between [Greenland](12118.md) and [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), which resulted in the [polar jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream) descending in latitude, resulting in a very favorable configuration for the strong development of [low-pressure areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area). Under influence of storm Joseph, also named *Marilu* by the [FUB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin), a secondary low formed. This secondary low was named Kristin by the IPMA, as a significant impact was forecast for parts of Portugal.

Enabled by very dynamic and favorable atmospheric conditions, Kristin rapidly intensified in the night before it made landfall.[^cite_note-22] During its [explosive cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_cyclogenesis), Kristin deepened to a minimum atmospheric pressure of 975 hPa. In the hours before Kristin made landfall in the Leiria district, the storm developed a sting jet, which is a rare phenomenon that exclusively happens in extratropical cyclones with a warm core.[^cite_note-23]



RGB imagery of Kristin at its peak intensity on 28 January 2026 at 04\:40 UTC.



Cloud top height of Kristin on 28 January 2026 at 04\:30 UTC.

Around 4\:45 AM UTC, Kristin made landfall as an extremely powerful storm in the Leiria district, with gusts exceeding 200 km/h in some parts of the Leiria and Coimbra districts. In [Soure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soure,_Portugal), which is a municipality in the Coimbra district, a gust of 208.8 km/h has been measured, exceeding the previous record of 176.4 km/h by Leslie in 2018. Unofficially, a gust of 238 km/h has been measured in [Lavos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavos_\(Figueira_da_Foz\)), which is also a parish in the Coimbra district.[^cite_note-Weer-2] The [Monte Real Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Real_Air_Base), which is located in the Leiria district, reported a gust of 178 km/h before the [anemometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemometer) got damaged. The Leiria aerodrome measured a gust of 156.2 km/h, according to the IPMA. Lisbon measured a peak gust of 115 km/h.[^cite_note-24] [Doppler weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar#Velocity) imagery were indicative of wind speeds in excess of 216 km/h (>60 m/s) at an altitude of 600 metres during Kristin's peak intensity.[^cite_note-IPMA-25]

After moving through Portugal, Kristin caused strong winds and excessive precipitation in Spain. A maximum gust of 174 km/h was measured in Dehesa del Camarate by the [AEMET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEMET).[^cite_note-26] A gust of 156 km/h was measured in Villena.[^cite_note-ElPeriodico-27] Gusts of 150.5 km/h were recorded in [Benizalón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benizal%C3%B3n), which is one of the strongest gusts ever recorded in [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almer%C3%ADa), alongside with gusts of 131 km/h in [Carboneras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboneras), 124 km/h in [Xodos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xodos), 118 km/h in [Laujar de Andarax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laujar_de_Andarax), 116.3 km/h in the [Calar Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calar_Alto) mountains, 113.8 km/h in [Albox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albox), and 111 km/h in Garuccha.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] A maximum gust of 108 km/h was measured in Cáceres.[^cite_note-30] In A Gándara, a gust of 120.6 km/h was measured. [Madrid](41188263.md) experienced snow, which happens a couple times per year.[^cite_note-31]

### Effect of climate change

According to a research conducted by Manning et al. (2018), the frequency of extreme windstorms increases with 25–35% as a result of climate change. The main cause is the increased contribution of storms that develop a sting jet as a result of the [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming).[^cite_note-32] Kristin had a sting jet, which was responsible for its intensity, according to the IPMA.[^cite_note-IPMA-25] Global warming also alters large-scale atmospheric circulations, which contributes to environments being more favorable for rapid cyclogenesis and persistent cyclonic influences.[^cite_note-33]

## Warnings and preparation

### Portugal

On 25 January, the first indications for a potentially destructive weather event appeared, according to [Duarte Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Costa).[^cite_note-34] The IPMA issued widespread red warnings on 27 January for the northern and central districts, as gusts up to 140 km/h and an extremely rough sea was forecast. The IPMA also warned that the impact could be catastrophic.[^cite_note-35]

The National Emergency and Civil Protection Authority in Portugal elevated the country to Level 4 (Maximum State of Readiness) in the 24 hours prior to landfall, the highest possible tier of mobilisation. The sandbars in the northern zone were closed and the entrance to [Viana do Castelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viana_do_Castelo) was restricted.[^cite_note-36] A warning SMS was sent to parts of the country on 27 January by the National Emergency and Civil Protection Authority.[^cite_note-37] The IPMA advised everyone to stay at home during Kristin, to keep their blinds closed, and to protect structures.[^cite_note-38] On 27 January, the Civil Protection agency of [Torres Vedras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Vedras) ordered the closure of all schools and advised everyone to not leave home unless necessary.[^cite_note-39] Local authorities closed the access to the [Sintra Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintra_Mountains) on 27 January due to Kristin.[^cite_note-40] The municipality of [Oliveira do Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliveira_do_Hospital) announced on January 27 that all schools had to close due to Kristin.[^cite_note-41] The municipality of Montanegre also closed their schools on 27 January.[^cite_note-42] The municipality of [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra) also announced a closure of all schools. In the night of January 28, the municipality of [Miranda do Corvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_do_Corvo) decided to close all schools and to cancel the weekly outdoor market in the municipality.[^cite_note-43] On 27 January, The Municipal Civil Protection of Santarém issued an advice for the population to stay at home and to cancel sporting activities.[^cite_note-44] The Maritime Promenade and some municipal gardens and parks were closed in [Oeiras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeiras,_Portugal).[^cite_note-45]

### Spain

The AEMET issued a red alert for the interior of [Pontevedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontevedra) for excessive precipitation and parts of the southeast (Almanzora Valley/Almería) for hurricane-force gusts. Orange and yellow alerts covered the Spanish interior for gusts up to 100 km/h, while coastal regions braced for 8-meter waves along the Atlantic. Warnings were issued for the northern plateau, threatening major transport arteries like the A-1 and A-6 motorways.[^cite_note-46]

### Malta

Warnings were issued for Malta, as strong to gale-force winds were forecast. Waves up to 6 metres were also forecast. Fast ferry trips were cancelled on 28 January.[^cite_note-47]

### Italy and Greece

The Italian Civil Protection issued orange alerts for [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio), [Sardinia](29376.md), and [Molise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise), citing high-speed winds and hydrogeological risks. Red alerts for [avalanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanches) were active in the Western Alps. Though the storm was named by IPMA, Greece issued an orange warning for [Western Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Greece) and the Aegean, forecasting intense thunderstorms and gale-force winds (9 Beaufort). Much of Southern Italy remained under a State of Emergency due to the compounded risk from the previous [Storm Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Harry), which had already destabilized terrain in Niscemi, [Sicily](27619.md).[^cite_note-auto-48]

## Impact

### Portugal

Kristin made landfall as an extremely powerful storm in the Leiria district in the night of 28 January 2026. The storm claimed six lives directly across the country, with fatalities caused by falling trees, structural collapses, and an incident in the Algarve where a woman was swept away by a flooding stream. Another eight people died indirectly, as a result of recovery and cleaning operations, [carbon monoxide poisoning](488743.md) by misusage of generators, or [suicide](16831059.md) in one case[^cite_note-49], bringing the total amount of deaths by Kristin to 14 in Portugal. One of the eight indirect deaths was an employee of E-Redes, who got electrocuted while repairing electrical structures in Leiria.[^cite_note-50] Kristin caused €6 billion in damage in Portugal, according to the Mission Structure for the Reconstruction of the Central Region. Right after Kristin, nearly one million people—approximately 10% of the population—were left without power. In the Leiria district, around 300,000 people—approximately 64% of the population—lost access to power.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] More than 300,000 customers were left without telecommunication services. The municipal stadium of Leiria was severely damaged and Kristin damaged the Monte Real air base severely, including parts of the F-16 fighter fleet.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Some parishes and municipalities, especially in the Leiria, Coimbra, and Santarém districts, lost all communication. It was estimated that 170 of the 180 companies in the [Meirinhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meirinhas) parish lost their roof, as a result of storm Kristin.[^cite_note-rtppt-3] 95% of all houses were damaged in the Meirinhas parish, where losses exceed €500 million.[^cite_note-55] The Leiria Hospital got overwhelmed by the amount of polytrauma patients in the first days after Kristin.[^cite_note-56] There were 84 people displaced in the municipality of Leiria.[^cite_note-57] 177 people in the Leiria district were directly injured by Kristin.[^cite_note-SapoPT-4] Kristin caused estimated losses of €5 billion in the Leiria district, which is around 65% of its regional GDP.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

Nearly all homes in the municipality of Pombal suffered damage and the losses in Portugal were significantly higher than during the [2024 Portugal wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Portugal_wildfires) and the [2025 Portugal wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portugal_wildfires).[^cite_note-60] A large part of the Leiria National Forest got devastated by Kristin.[^cite_note-61] Between 4,000 and 4,500 homes–around 80% or 85% of the municipality's homes–were estimated to be damaged in [Ferreira do Zêzere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferreira_do_Z%C3%AAzere), according to the mayor there. The mayor of Ferreira do Zêzere estimated that full recovery will take a year and that 70% of the forest cover has fallen. Several houses were damaged in [Monte Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Real).[^cite_note-62] 55 buildings in the municipality of [Ourém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our%C3%A9m) and 68 buildings in the municipality of [Figueiró dos Vinhos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figueir%C3%B3_dos_Vinhos) experienced a total collapse due to Kristin.[^cite_note-63]

Around 90% of businesses in [Marinha Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinha_Grande) reported damage after Kristin. The municipality of Marinha Grande reported ten minor injuries and rehoused 75 people.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] According to the [REN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redes_Energeticas_Nacionais), 61 very high voltage poles and 774 kilometres of REN's very high voltage lines were taken down.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The municipality of Figueiró dos Vinhos has 850 houses that suffered roof damage, as a result of Kristin.[^cite_note-rtppt-3] More than a thousand homes were damaged as a whole in the municipality and there were 30 hectares in the municipal forest of Figueiró dos Vinhos where not a single tree was left standing.[^cite_note-68] The bus terminal of Leiria collapsed, which resulted in around 45 vehicles being damaged.[^cite_note-69] Kristin caused damage to cultural heritage in nearly 20 municipalities, such as the complete destruction of the Charolinha da Mata dos Sete Montes in [Tomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomar) and the loss of archaeological complexes at Forto Novo in Loulé.[^cite_note-70] According to the Confederation of Farmers of Portugal, the agricultural and forestry sections suffered losses of €775 million.[^cite_note-71] 25 people were displaced in [Alvaiázere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvai%C3%A1zere) and 95% of all businesses and homes were affected there. Furthermore, the local health center in Alvaiázere had to close due to structural damage.[^cite_note-72] At the entrance of Marinha Grande, a group of modular houses were blown away. Along the waterfront of Praia da Vieira, all restaurants had broken windows and there was damage to swimming pools and hotels. 185 church buildings in Portugal suffered significant damage.[^cite_note-73] Pine forests and woodlands were destroyed in the Leiria and Coimbra districts.[^cite_note-74] There were estimated losses of €30 million in the [poultry sector](12116844.md), as Kristin led to an expected drop of 5% in egg production.[^cite_note-75] Kristin caused at least €35 million in damage to municipal equipment in [Pombal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pombal,_Portugal), alongside with at least €80 million in damage to businesses and the displacement of 90 people.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-Pombal-77] Kristin caused €15 million in damage to municipal infrastructure in [Nazaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazar%C3%A9,_Portugal) and almost €15 million in damage to public and private property in [Porto de Mós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_de_M%C3%B3s).[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] 80% of homes were affected by Kristin in [Pedrógão Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedr%C3%B3g%C3%A3o_Grande).[^cite_note-80] More than 90 of the 125 clubs in the [Leiria Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiria_Football_Association) suffered significant damage due to Kristin.[^cite_note-81] According to Bruno Gomes, the mayor of Ferreira do Zêzere, estimated losses exceeded €200 million in the municipality.[^cite_note-82] More than 250 libraries have suffered damage.[^cite_note-83] The total amount of damage in Marinha Grande exceeded €143 million.[^cite_note-84]

Around 10,000 houses have had their roofs blown off in the municipality of Ourém.[^cite_note-85] Around 300 people were displaced and 17 houses were destroyed in Ourém.[^cite_note-86] 97 to 98% of the homes were affected by Kristin in the [Caxarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caxarias) parish.[^cite_note-87] The total amount of damage in the municipality of Leiria exceeded €1 billion.[^cite_note-88] Up to eight million trees fell in the municipality of Leiria.[^cite_note-89] The municipality of Alvaiázere has estimated losses of more than €60 million.[^cite_note-90] Estimated losses in the business sector of [Figueira da Foz](861506.md) were several tens of millions of euros.[^cite_note-91] Municipal losses exceeded €15.6 million in the municipality of [Batalha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batalha,_Portugal).[^cite_note-92] Approximately 90% of the homes in the Arrabal parish were affected by water infiltration. ### Spain

As the storm moved inland across Spain, it brought a combination of hurricane-force winds and heavy snowfall. In the south, a woman was killed in [Málaga](184763.md) by a falling palm tree, and the [Almanzora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almanzora) Valley was placed under a red alert for extreme wind gusts. Central Spain faced significant travel chaos as over 170 roads were affected by snow; the A-6 motorway in Madrid was blocked, leaving dozens of drivers stranded. Additionally, the agricultural sector in [Huelva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huelva) and [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almer%C3%ADa) suffered massive losses as torrential rains flooded greenhouses and high winds destroyed berry-producing tunnels. There were 170,000 power outages and 5 injuries reported in Andalusia.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] Power outages were reported in several towns in [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain).[^cite_note-133] Maximum gusts of 99 km/h were reported in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville_\(province\)), which led to a partial roof collapse of the Plaza Artena sports pavilion in [Alcalá del Río](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_del_R%C3%ADo) and a partial roof collapse of a high school in [Lebrija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebrija).[^cite_note-134] There were several fallen trees at the [Lagunas de Ruidera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagunas_de_Ruidera).[^cite_note-135] A roof collapsed at the CEIP Javier Paulino school in [La Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Solana).[^cite_note-136] Kristin caused significant damage to tunnel and greenhouse structures in [Huelva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huelva), which affected the soft fruit sector.[^cite_note-137] Nine parks were closed in Madrid due to the heavy snowfall caused by Kristin.[^cite_note-138]

In [Extremadura](68204.md), the storm caused widespread wind- and rain-related incidents, prompting emergency coordination by the regional 112 Extremadura service. Between midnight and 18\:00 on 28 January 112 handled 2,500 calls and 455 incidents directly linked to the storm; from 26 January (15\:00) to 28 January (18\:00), it managed 5,260 calls and 1,462 incidents, including 535 related to the weather alert. Several people were evacuated due to rising river levels as a result of Kristin.[^cite_note-139] Roofs of school sports facilities in Cádiz collapsed.[^cite_note-140] Power pylons collapsed and wires broke in the province of Almería, which resulted in numerous power outages.[^cite_note-141] A landslide occurred in Pinos Puente. 11 schools in Andalusia were damaged by Kristin. The dome of the Faculty of Business Studies in Huelva partially collapsed. More than 200 people in Jimena and San Roque were without power. One day after Kristin, around 900 residents of the province of Seville were still without power.[^cite_note-142]

In the city of Cáceres, municipal incidents included falling trees and branches, as well as detached roof elements and masonry; no injuries were reported locally. #### Balearic Islands

There were 19 fallen trees reported on the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands), alongside with six floods. 79 incidents were reported on the islands, 49 of them being on [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza).[^cite_note-Infobae-153] Landslides were reported.[^cite_note-ElPeriodico-27]

### Morocco

The northern and coastal areas of Morocco experienced wind gusts of 80-120 km/h, heavy rain, and high waves.[^cite_note-155] Tetuan measured a wind gust of 117 km/h, according to observations.[^cite_note-156] Severe floodings were reported in the northern part of Morocco.[^cite_note-157] The Tangier port was closed.[^cite_note-158]

### Italy

In Italy, the arrival of Kristin compounded a pre-existing state of emergency in the south, following the damage already inflicted by Storm Harry that affected the country a week before Kristin. The [Italian Civil Protection Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Civil_Protection_Department) issued orange alerts for Lazio, Sardinia, and Molise due to the risk of landslides and torrential rain, particularly threatening the town of Niscemi, Sicily, where a massive [4km-long landslide](82238400.md) remained active. In the north, the storm triggered high-level avalanche warnings in the Western Alps, while transport networks faced widespread delays, with rail protocols activated to allow for emergency rebookings as gale-force winds hit the [Mediterranean coast](19006.md).
