The **Payment and Control Agency for Guidance and Guarantee Community Aid** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Οργανισμός Πληρωμών και Ελέγχου Κοινοτικών Ενισχύσεων Προσανατολισμού και Εγγυήσεων; **OPEKEPE**) was the Greek government agency responsible for distributing agricultural subsidies from the [European Union](9317.md) to farmers in Greece.

The agency, which had been in operation since 2001, was governed by a board of directors who were accountable to the [Ministry of Rural Development and Food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Rural_Development_and_Food_\(Greece\)). Its headquarters were in [Athens](1216.md), with six regional directorates and thirty-nine local offices.[^cite_note-3] The agency was closed in 2025 amid a scandal involving misappropriation of EU funds, and its functions were transferred to the tax authorities.[^cite_note-Politico1-4] The agency distributed about €2 billion annually in subsidies from the EU to Greek farmers.[^cite_note-ekm1-5]

## Corruption scandal

In 2020, the [European Public Prosecutor's Office](1707190.md) (EPPO), whose remit is to combat the misuse of EU funds, opened an investigation into OPEKEPE, amid allegations of organised fraud involving agricultural subsidies.[^cite_note-Guardian1-6] Suspects are alleged to have made claims for land they did not own and to have exaggerated the number of animals on farms. The Greek authorities estimated that €23 million had been paid out fraudulently since 2018, with some of the money going to people not involved with agriculture.[^cite_note-Signal-7] Claims included banana plantations on [Mount Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus), olive groves on military airports, and pasture for sheep in the sea.[^cite_note-Guardian1-6] An auditor at OPEKEPE, Paraskevi Tycheropoulou, was demoted after blowing the whistle on malpractice and then worked with EPPO as a scientific advisor. In March 2026, a court in Athens ordered her re-instatement and awarded her damages.[^cite_note-8]

In 2024 and early 2025, in conjunction with Greek police, EPPO charged 100 suspects with agricultural fraud, mostly concerning pasture land in [Crete](6591.md).[^cite_note-9]

In May 2025, the Athens office of EPPO carried out raids in Athens, including the headquarters of OPEKEPE, and on the island of [Crete](6591.md).[^cite_note-10] The Greek government announced that OPEKEPE would be shut down and its functions transferred to the tax authorities.[^cite_note-Politico1-4] The following month, EPPO handed a 3,000 page file to the Greek parliament. By this time, Greece had already been fined €415 million by the EU on account of agricultural fraud.[^cite_note-11] Five ministers, whose names appeared in the file, resigned in June 2025. They were: migration minister Voridis; deputy foreign minister [Tasos Chatzivasileiou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasos_Chatzivasileiou); deputy minister of rural development Dionysis Stamenitis; deputy minister of digital governance Christos Boukoros; secretary general for rural development and food Giorgos Stratakos.[^cite_note-12]

There were further developments in the scandal at the end of March 2026, when EPPO asked the Greek government to lift the immunity of eleven members of parliament.[^cite_note-ekm1-5] Three of those named (agriculture minister [Konstantinos Tsiaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantinos_Tsiaras), civil protection minister [Ioannis A. Kefalogiannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioannis_A._Kefalogiannis) and deputy health minister Dimitris Vartzopoulos) resigned from their ministerial positions on 3 April 2026, while the EPPO added another two names to the list. Opposition party [PASOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PASOK) called for a general election, saying that cabinet reshuffles were being decided not by prime minister [Kyriakos Mitsotakis](2780816.md) but by the EPPO.[^cite_note-13]
