**Yunnan**[^cite_note-8] is an inland [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [Southwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_China), spanning approximately 394,000 km<sup>2</sup> (152,000 sq mi) and has a population of 47.2 million (as of 2020). The capital of the province is [Kunming](197181.md). The province borders the Chinese provinces of [Guizhou](173815.md), [Sichuan](65185.md), [autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_China) of [Guangxi](47836950.md) and [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region), as well as Southeast Asian countries [Myanmar](19457.md) (Burma), [Vietnam](202354.md), and [Laos](17752.md). Yunnan is China's fourth least developed province based on disposable income per capita in 2014.[^cite_note-9]

Yunnan is situated in a mountainous area, with high elevations in the Northwest and low elevations in the Southeast. Most of the population lives in the eastern part of the province. In the west, the altitude can vary from the mountain peaks to river valleys as much as 3,000 m (9,800 ft). Yunnan is rich in natural resources and has the largest diversity of plant life in China. Of the approximately 30,000 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant) in China, Yunnan has perhaps 17,000 or more.[^cite_note-Paterson-10] Yunnan's reserves of [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium), [lead](17747.md), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) and [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) are the largest in China, and there are also major reserves of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel). Historically, the southwestern Silk Road to [Bhitargarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhitargarh) in [Bangladesh](3454.md) passed through modern Yunnan.

Parts of Yunnan formed the [Dian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_Kingdom) during the 3rd and 2nd centuries BC. The [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) [conquered the Dian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Dian) in the late 2nd century BC, establishing the Yizhou Commandery in its place. During the chaos of the [Three Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms), imperial Chinese authority in Yunnan weakened, and much of the region came under the control of the [Cuanman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuanman). The area was later ruled by the [Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages)-speaking kingdom of [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao) from (738–937), followed by the [Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_people)-ruled [Dali Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_Kingdom) (937–1253). After the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) conquest of the region in the 13th century, Yunnan was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_conquest_of_Yunnan) and [ruled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_under_Ming_rule) by the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty).

From the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) onward, the area was part of a central government-sponsored population movement towards the southwestern frontier, with two major waves of migrants arriving from [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese)-majority areas in northern and southeast China.[^cite_note-Lee_1982-11] As with other parts of China's southwest, [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) occupation in the north during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) forced another migration of [Han people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) into the region. These two waves of migration contributed to Yunnan being one of the most ethnically diverse provinces of China, with ethnic minorities accounting for about 34 percent of its total population.[^cite_note-12] Major ethnic groups include [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), [Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_people), [Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hani_people), [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people), [Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_people), and [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people).[^cite_note-13] Yunnan has also been identified as the birthplace of [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea),[^cite_note-14] and as the region of origin of the plant genus *[Cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis)*.[^cite_note-15]

## Etymology

The name "Yunnan" first referred to a place when the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) created Yunnan County near modern [Xiangyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangyun_County).[^cite_note-16] During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), [Emperor Xuanzong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Xuanzong_of_Tang) gave [Piluoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piluoge), the chief of [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao), the title of "King of Yunnan",[^cite_note-尤中-17] because Nanzhao originated from Yunnan county.[^cite_note-吴光泛-18] Gradually the king of Yunnan controlled more and more territory, and "Yunnan" became the common name of this area.[^cite_note-谭其骧-19] Therefore, the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) created the province of Yunnan after it occupied the [Dali Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_Kingdom).[^cite_note-尤中-17]

Han dynasty literature did not record the [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) of "Yunnan", and there are many theories about its origin. One common theory states that the name means "south of colorful clouds" (彩云之南; *cǎiyún zhī nán*). Some annals in the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), for example *Dian Lüe* (滇略) and *Yunnan General Annals* (云南通志), support this.[^cite_note-吴光泛-18] However, modern historian [Tan Qixiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Qixiang) states that this theory is a superficial explanation of the literal meaning.[^cite_note-谭其骧-19]

Another common theory is that the name means "south of [Yun Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yun_Range)" (云岭之南) However, this has been disproven because the name "Yunling Mountains" first appeared in Tang dynasty (618–907) literature, but the name "Yunnan" first appeared during the Han dynasty (202 BC – 220 AD).[^cite_note-吴光泛-18] Modern research gives more conjectures. You Zhong said "Yunnan" means "south of the mountain (referring to the [Cang Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cang_Mountain)) with clouds".[^cite_note-尤中-17] Wu Guangfan said "Yunnan" might be a [Loloish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loloish_languages) or [Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_language) name.[^cite_note-吴光泛-18]

### Prehistory

The [Yuanmou Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuanmou_Man), a *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* fossil unearthed by railway engineers in the 1960s, has been determined to be the oldest-known hominid fossil in China, dating back to an estimated 1.7 million years ago.[^cite_note-20] By the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period, there were human settlements in the area of [Lake Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Dian). These people used stone tools and constructed simple wooden structures.

### Dian Kingdom



Bronze sculpture of the [Dian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_Kingdom), 3rd century BC

Around the 3rd century BC, the central area of Yunnan around present day [Kunming](197181.md) was known as [Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_Kingdom). The [Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_\(state\)) general [Zhuang Qiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_Qiao?action=edit\&redlink=1) (庄蹻) entered the region from the upper [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River)[^cite_note-21] and set himself up as "King of Dian".[^cite_note-22] He and his followers brought into Yunnan an influx of Chinese influence,[^cite_note-23] the start of a long history of migration and cultural expansion.

### Qin and Han dynasties

In 221 BC, [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang) unified China and extended his authority south. Commanderies and counties were established in Yunnan. An existing road in [Sichuan](65185.md) – the "Five Foot Way" – was extended south to around present day [Qujing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qujing), in eastern Yunnan. In 109 BC, the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) [conquered Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Dian) during its [southern expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southward_expansion_of_the_Han_dynasty). Under orders from [Emperor Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han), General [Guo Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Chang?action=edit\&redlink=1) (郭昌) was sent south to Yunnan, eventually establishing the [Yizhou commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yizhou_commandery?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:0-24] By this time, agricultural technology in Yunnan had improved markedly. The local people used bronze tools, plows and kept a variety of livestock, including cattle, horses, sheep, goats, pigs and dogs. Anthropologists have determined that these people were related to the people now known as the [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples). They lived in tribal congregations, sometimes led by exiled Chinese.

During the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms), the territory of present-day Yunnan, western [Guizhou](173815.md) and southern Sichuan was collectively called [Nanzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhong). The dissolution of Chinese central authority led to increased autonomy for Yunnan and more power for the local tribal structures. In AD 225, the famed statesman [Zhuge Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuge_Liang) led three columns into Yunnan to pacify the tribes. His seven captures of [Meng Huo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Huo), a local magnate, is mythologized in the [Romance of the Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_of_the_Three_Kingdoms).[^cite_note-25]

### Cuan Kingdom

Main article: [Cuanman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuanman)

In the 4th century, northern China was largely overrun by nomadic tribes from the north. In the 320s, the Cuan (爨) clan migrated into Yunnan. [Cuan Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuan_Chen?action=edit\&redlink=1) (爨琛) named himself king and held authority from [Lake Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Dian), then known as [Kunchuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunchuan). Henceforth the Cuan clan ruled eastern Yunnan for over four hundred years.

International trade flowed through Yunnan.[^cite_note-26] An ancient overland pre-[Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) trade route from Yunnan passed through [Irrawaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayeyarwady_Region) in Burma to reach [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal). Yunnan was inhabited by so-called barbarians not fully under the control of the Tang government and the route, though ancient, was not used much in pre-Tang times, and Chinese attempts to control the route were disrupted by the rise of [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao).[^cite_note-27]

### Nanzhao period

Main article: [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao)

Yunnan was settled by several local tribes, clans, and cultures before the 8th century. Around [Lake Erhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erhai), namely, the [Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_Bai_Autonomous_Prefecture) area, there emerged six *zhao*: Mengzi (蒙巂), Yuexi (越析), Langqiong (浪穹), Dengdan (邆赕), Shilling (施浪), and Mengshe (蒙舍). *Zhao* (诏) was an indigenous non-Chinese language term meaning "king" or "kingdom." Among the six regimes Mengshe was located south of the other five; therefore given the new, larger context, it was called Nanzhao (Southern Kingdom).[^cite_note-28]

By the 730s Nanzhao had succeeded in bringing the Erhai Lake–area under its authority. In 738, the western Yunnan was united by [Piluoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piluoge), the fourth king of Nanzhao, who was confirmed by the imperial court of the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) as [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_nobility) of Yunnan.[^cite_note-29] Ruling from [Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_City), the thirteen kings of Nanzhao ruled over more than two centuries and played a vital role in the dynamic relationship between the Tang dynasty and the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire) as a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state).[^cite_note-30]

By the 750s, Nanzhao had conquered Yunnan and became a potential rival to Tang China. The following period saw several conflicts between Tang China and Nanzhao. In 750, Nanzhao attacked and captured Yaozhou, the largest Tang settlement in Yunnan. In 751, Xianyu Zhongtong (鮮于仲通), the regional commander of Jiannan (present-day [Sichuan](65185.md)), led a Tang campaign against Nanzhao. The king of Nanzhao, Geluofeng, regarded the previous incident as a personal affair and wrote to Xianyu to seek peace. However, Xianyu Zhongtong detained the Nanzhao envoys and turned down the appeal. Confronted with Tang armies, Nanzhao immediately turned its allegiance to the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire).[^cite_note-31] The Tubo and Nanzhao agreed to be "fraternal states"; Geluofeng was given the titles zanpuzhong ("younger brother"). The Nanzhao-Tubo alliance ensured a disastrous defeat for Xianyu's expedition, with the Tang general's army of 80,000 men being reduced to a quarter of its original size.[^cite_note-32]

Tang China did not give up after one failure. In 753, another expedition was prepared, but this was also defeated by Nanzhao. In 754, the Tang organized an army of more than 100,000 troops that advanced to the Dali plain, resulting in only another slaughter. By the end of the eighth century, Tang was no longer a major threat to Nanzhao.



A gold statue of Acharya Lokeshvara ([Guanyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanyin)), recovered from the [Qianxun Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pagodas), [Dali Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_Kingdom)

[^cite_note-33]

Nanzhao's expansion lasted for several decades. In 829, Nanzhao suddenly plundered [Sichuan](65185.md) and entered [Chengdu](170375.md). When it retreated, hundreds of Sichuan people, including skilled artisans, were taken to Yunnan. In 832, the Nanzhao army captured the capital of the [Pyu kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyu_city-states) in modern upper [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma). Nanzhao also attacked the [Khmer peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_people) of [Zhenla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenla). Generally speaking, Nanzhao was then the most powerful kingdom in mainland Southeast Asia, and played an extremely active role in multistate interactions. In 859, Nanzhao captured [Bozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zunyi), and this event exacerbated the Nanzhao-Tang clashes. When the Tang governor of [Annam](202354.md) took Bozhou back in the following year, Nanzhao, with the help of native peoples, occupied Hanoi as the Tang army moved to Bozhou. When the Tang forces returned, Nanzhao troops retreated from [Hanoi](56667.md) but attacked and plundered [Yongzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning). In the winter of 862, Nanzhao, allying with local groups, led an army of over 50,000 men to invade Annam again. It is reported that the Tang forces lost over 150,000 soldiers (either killed or captured by Nanzhao) in the two Annam battles. The autumn of 866 saw Tang victory in Hanoi and soon all of the Nanzhao forces were driven away. But Tang China had lost its ability to attack Nanzhao.

While Nanzhao was being defeated in Annam, it still occasionally attacked Sichuan. In 869, Shilong (世隆), the eighth king and the first empire of Nanzhao, invaded Sichuan. In 874, Nanzhao attacked Sichuan again.

In 902, Zheng Maisi, the *Qingpingguan* (清平官,"Prime Minister") of Nanzhao, murdered the infant king of Nanzhao, and established a new kingdom called [Dachanghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachanghe). Nanzhao, a once-powerful empire, disappeared. In 928, Yang Ganzhen (楊干貞) usurped the Dachanghe king and established Zhao Shanzheng, a *qingpingguan* as emperor of Datianxing (大天興). In 929, Yang Qianzhen abolished Zhao Shanzheng and established himself as Emperor of Dayining (大義寧).

### Dali Kingdom and Yuan dynasty

Refer to caption

The [Three Pagodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pagodas) of Dali

In 937, [Duan Siping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duan_Siping) overthrew the Dayining Kingdom and established the [Dali Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_Kingdom). The kingdom was conquered by the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) in 1253 after Dali King Duan Xingzhi defected to the Mongols. The Duans were incorporated into the Mongol administration as [Maharajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja) of the new province. The Mongolian prince sent to administer the region with them was killed. In 1273, [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan) reformed the province and appointed the [semu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semu) [Ajall Shams al-Din Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajall_Shams_al-Din_Omar) as its governor.[^cite_note-34] During the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), Yunnan included significant portions of [Upper Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Burma) after the [First Mongol invasion of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mongol_invasion_of_Burma) in the 1270s and 1280s. The withdrawal of garrison troops from Burma in 1303 gave local leaders the freedom to expand their own power bases, eventually leading to the rapid rise and rebellion of [Möng Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ng_Mao) in the 1340s.[^cite_note-35] With the fall of the Yuan dynasty in 1368, the Ming dynasty destroyed the Yuan loyalists led by [Basalawarmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalawarmi) and the remnants of the House of Duan in the [Ming conquest of Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_conquest_of_Yunnan) by the early 1380s.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-Dardess2012-37]

### Ming and Qing dynasties

The Ming installed [Mu Ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_Ying) and his family as hereditary aristocrats in Yunnan.



A scene of the Qing campaign against the [Miao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people) in 1795.

During the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) and [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) dynasties, large areas of Yunnan were administered under the [native chieftain system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_chieftain_system). Under the Qing dynasty a [war with Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Burmese_War_\(1765%E2%80%931769\)) also occurred in the 1760s due to the attempted consolidation of borderlands under local chiefs by both China and Burma.[^cite_note-38]

Yunnan was a destination for Han Chinese during Yuan rule.[^cite_note-39] Migrants moved into the area during Ming and Qing rule.[^cite_note-Lee_1982-11] During the Ming dynasty, 3 million Han Chinese mostly from [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) (the original Nanjing population was later largely replaced by Wu-speakers), and some from Shanxi and Hebei, settled in Yunnan.

Although largely forgotten, the bloody [Panthay Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthay_Rebellion) of the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_China) [Hui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) and other local minorities against the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchus) rulers of the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) caused the deaths of up to a million people in Yunnan.[^cite_note-chineseciv-40] The Manchu official Shuxing'a started an anti-Muslim massacre, which led to the rebellion. Shuxing'a developed a deep hatred of Muslims after an incident in which he was stripped naked and nearly lynched by a mob of Muslims. He ordered several Muslim rebels to be [slowly sliced to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingchi).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] [Tariq Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Ali) wrote about the real incident in one of his novels and claimed the Muslims who had nearly lynched Shuxing'a were not Hui but belonged to another ethnicity. Nevertheless, the Manchu official blamed all Muslims for the incident.[^cite_note-43] A British officer testified that the Muslims did not rebel for religious reasons and that the Chinese were tolerant of different religions and were unlikely to have caused the revolt by interfering with the practice of Islam.[^cite_note-fytche_301-44] Loyalist Muslim forces helped Qing forces crush the rebel Muslims. The Qing armies massacred only Muslims who had rebelled or supported the rebels and spared Muslims who took no part in the uprising.[^cite_note-dillon_77-45]

In 1894, [George Ernest Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ernest_Morrison), an [Australian](4689264.md) correspondent for *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)*, traveled from [Beijing](18603746.md) to British-occupied [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) via Yunnan. His book, *An Australian in China*,[^cite_note-46] details his experiences.

The [1905 Tibetan Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Tibetan_Rebellion) in which Tibetan Buddhist Lamas attacked and killed French Catholic missionaries spread to Yunnan.

### Post-Imperial

See also: [Yunnan clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_clique)



Yunnan as part of the Republic of China era.

Following the collapse of the Qing Dynasty in 1911, Yunnan came under the control of local warlords, who had more than the usual degree of autonomy due to Yunnan's remoteness. They financed their regime through opium harvesting and traffic.

[Cai E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cai_E) is regarded as the founder of the Yunnan clique when at the request of [Liang Qichao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_Qichao) in 1915, he declared Yunnan's opposition to [Yuan Shikai's monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_China_\(1915%E2%80%931916\)). Cai died from natural causes shortly after the successful [National Protection War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Protection_War). His chief lieutenant, [Tang Jiyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Jiyao), took over Yunnan and demanded that the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Republic_of_China) be restored. When this was accomplished, Yunnan officially reunified with the national government but kept its provincial army separate due to the [Beiyang Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiyang_Army)'s grip in Beijing politics. In 1927, [Long Yun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Yun) seized control of the clique; Tang died shortly after. Long then re-aligned Yunnan under the Nationalist government in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) but stringently guarded the province's autonomy.[^cite_note-47]

Yunnan was transformed by the events of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), which caused many east coast refugees and industrial establishments to relocate to the province. Yunnan served as, among other things, a home base for the [Flying Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Tigers). It assumed strategic significance, particularly as the [Burma Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Road) from [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio), in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) to [Kunming](197181.md) was a fought over supply line of vital importance to China's war effort.[^cite_note-48]

University faculty and students in the east had originally decamped to [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha), capital of [Hunan](166409.md). But as Japanese forces were gaining more territory they eventually bombed Changsha in February 1938. The 800 faculty and students who were left had to flee and made the 1,000 mile journey to [Kunming](197181.md), capital of Yunnan in China's mountainous southwest. It was here that the [National Southwest Associated University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Southwest_Associated_University) (commonly known as Lianda University) was established. For eight years, staff, professors and students had to survive and operate in makeshift quarters that were subject to sporadic bombing campaigns by the Japanese.[^cite_note-49] There were dire shortages of food, equipment, books, clothing and other essential needs, but they managed to conduct the running of a modern [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University). Over those eight years of war (1937–1945), Lianda became famous nationwide for having and producing many, if not most, of China's most prominent academics, scholars, scientists and intellectuals. Both of China's only [Nobel laureates in physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureates_in_physics) [Yang Chen-Ning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning) and [Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee) studied at Lianda in Kunming.

After the end of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), Long was removed from office.[^cite_note-50] During the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), [Nationalist](16903.md) forces retreated to the southwest provinces of [Szechwan](65185.md), [Sikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikang), and Yunnan. In 9 December 1949, Chairperson of the Provincial Government [Lu Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Han_\(general\)) defected to the [Communists](7175.md) and most of the Nationalist troops were defeated in the province. Remnants of the Nationalist forces, led by [Li Mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Mi_\(Republic_of_China_general\)) and using [Mong Hsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Hsat) as a base, engaged in guerrilla warfare against the Communists, briefly capturing parts of Yunnan territory. In 1951 the provincial government in exile was dissolved and in 1954, Li Mi's remaining troops [retreated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROC_retreat_to_Taiwan) to [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan). The [Kuomintang in Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang_in_Burma) continued to operate until 1961.

The establishment of the PRC brought gradual but definite consolidation to China's southwestern borders, solidifying what were previously murky claims to various regions. For example, [Colonial French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_French) and [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma#Colonial_era_.281886.E2.80.931948.29) had been active at sites in neighbouring northern Laos, Vietnam and Burma, and even inside of modern Yunnan at sites like [Tengchong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengchong) and [Gejiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gejiu). In fact, between 1904 and 1909 the French had built the 885 km (550 mi)long [Sino-Vietnamese Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_Railway) which ran from [Hanoi](56667.md) to [Kunming](197181.md) via [Hekou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hekou_Yao_Autonomous_County), with an offshoot to [Gejiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gejiu). The English tried to match the French effort with the [Yunnan–Burma Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Burma_Railway), but failed to complete it prior to the outbreak of war, with the project ultimately abandoned.

The PRC also saw a major conflict [near the border in 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%931961_campaign_at_the_China%E2%80%93Burma_border) to expel the remaining Nationalists who then left for Taiwan with some [settled in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang_Chinese_in_Thailand). It also saw a bitter [Sino-Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_War) that was fought along Yunnan's south eastern border, which was properly demarcated only on 23 February 2009,[^cite_note-sinoviet-finish-51] with renewed agreements taking effect as late as 14 July 2010.[^cite_note-sinoviet-effect-52]

### Naturalists



Lijiang

Thousands of plant, insect and mammal species were described in the 19th century by scientists of the [French National Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Natural_History_\(France\)), Paris, in connection with permanent settlements of missionaries of the *[Missions étrangères de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society)* in north-west Yunnan, among them noticeably [Jean-André Soulié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Andr%C3%A9_Souli%C3%A9) and [Felix Biet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Biet). From 1916 to 1917, [Roy Chapman Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Chapman_Andrews) and [Yvette Borup Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Borup_Andrews) led the Asiatic Zoological Expedition of the [American Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Museum_of_Natural_History) through much of western and southern Yunnan, as well as other provinces of China. The book, *[Camps and Trails in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camps_and_Trails_in_China?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, records their experiences. Other notable explorers include [Heinrich Handel-Mazzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Handel-Mazzetti); [George Forrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Forrest_\(botanist\)); [Joseph Francis Charles Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Francis_Charles_Rock), who from 1922 to 1949 spent most of his time studying the flora, peoples and languages of southwest China, mainly in Yunnan; and [Peter Goullart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Goullart), a [White Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9) who studied [Naxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhi_people) culture and lived in [Lijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijiang,_Yunnan) from 1940 to 1949.

## Geography



[Honghe Hani Rice Terraces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honghe_Hani_Rice_Terraces) in Yunnan

Yunnan is the most southwestern province in China, with the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) running through its southern part. The province has an area of 394,100 km<sup>2</sup> (152,200 sq mi), 4.1% of the nation's total. The northern part of the province forms part of the [Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guizhou_Plateau). The province borders [Guangxi](47836950.md) and [Guizhou](173815.md) in the east, [Sichuan](65185.md) in the north, and the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) in the northwest. It shares a border of 4,060 km (2,520 mi) with [Myanmar](19457.md) ([Kachin](493369.md) and [Shan States](493376.md)) in the west, [Laos](17752.md) ([Luang Namtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luang_Namtha_Province), [Oudomxay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudomxay_Province), and [Phongsaly Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phongsaly_Province)) in the south and [Vietnam](202354.md) ([Hà Giang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A0_Giang_Province), [Lào Cai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A0o_Cai_Province), [Lai Châu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_Ch%C3%A2u_Province), and [Điện Biên Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90i%E1%BB%87n_Bi%C3%AAn_Province)) in the southeast. For practical purposes, all of Yunnan falls within the [Zomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomia_\(region\)) region of Asia.

### Geology



Snowy mountains in [Dêqên](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%AAq%C3%AAn_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture), northwestern Yunnan

Yunnan is at the far eastern edge of the [Himalayan uplift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Himalaya), and was pushed up in the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene), primarily in the [Middle Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene), although the uplift continues into the present. The eastern part of the province is a [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) [plateau](538005.md) with [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) topography and unnavigable rivers flowing through deep mountain gorges. The main surface formations of the plateau are the [Lower Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Permian) Maokou Formation, characterized by thick limestone deposits, the Lower Permian Qixia Formation, characterised by dolomitic limestones and [dolomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)), the [Upper Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Permian) [basalts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) of the Ermeishan Formation (formerly Omeishan plateau basalts), and the red [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), [mudstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudstone), [siltstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siltstone), and [conglomerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(geology\)) of the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic)–[Paleogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleogene), including the Lufeng Formation and the Lunan Group (Lumeiyi, Xiaotun, and Caijiacong formations). In this area is the noted [Stone Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Forest) or Shilin, eroded vertical pinnacles of limestone (Maokou Formation). In the eastern part the rivers generally run eastwards. The western half is characterized by mountain ranges and rivers running north and south.[^cite_note-53]

### Climate

Köppen–Geiger climate classification map for Yunnan

Köppen climate classification map of Yunnan

Yunnan has a generally mild climate with pleasant and fair weather because of the province's location on south-facing mountain slopes, receiving the influence of both the Pacific and Indian oceans, and although the growing period is long, the rugged terrain provides little [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land). See *[Agriculture in Yunnan](#Agriculture)*. Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), much of the province lies within the [subtropical highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_highland) (Köppen *Cwb*) or [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) zone (*Cwa*), with cool to warm winters, and temperate summers, except in the truly tropical south, where temperatures regularly exceed 30 °C (86 °F) in the warmer half of the year.[^cite_note-54] *In general*, January average temperatures range from 8 to 17 °C (46 to 63 °F); July averages vary from 21 to 27 °C (70 to 81 °F). Average annual rainfall ranges from 600 to 2,300 mm (24 to 91 in), with over half the rain occurring between June and August. The [plateau region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guizhou_Plateau) has moderate temperatures. The western canyon region is hot at the valley bottoms, but there are freezing winds at the mountaintops.[^cite_note-55]

### Topography



Meili Snow Mountains

The terrain is largely mountainous, especially in the north and west. A series of high mountain chains spreads across the province. There is a distinct [canyon](78478.md) region to the west and a [plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guizhou_Plateau) region to the east. Yunnan's major rivers flow through the deep valleys between the mountains.

The average elevation is 1,980 m (6,500 ft). The mountains are highest in the north where they reach more than 5,000 m (16,000 ft); in the south they rise no higher than 3,000 m (9,800 ft). The highest point in the north is the [Kawagebo Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawagebo) in [Deqin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deqin_County) on the [Diqing Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diqing_Plateau), which is about 6,740 m (22,110 ft); and the lowest is in the [Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_\(Asia\)) Valley in [Hekou County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hekou_County), near the Vietnamese border, with an elevation of 76.4 m (251 ft).

The eastern half of the province is a limestone plateau with karst scenery and unnavigable rivers flowing through deep mountain gorges; the western half is characterised by mountain ranges and rivers running north and south. These include the Nu Jiang ([Salween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salween)), the Lancang ([Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong)), and the Jinsha ([Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze)), which flow in close proximity in the [Three Parallel Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Parallel_Rivers) protected area. The rugged, vertical terrain produces a wide range of flora and fauna, and the province has been called a natural zoological and botanical garden.

### Borders

Bordering Chinese provincial-level divisions are [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region), [Sichuan](65185.md), [Guizhou](173815.md) and [Guangxi](47836950.md). ### Rivers



The [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) near the [Tiger Leaping Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Leaping_Gorge)

Yunnan is the source of two rivers, the [Xi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_River) (there known as the [Nanpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanpan_River) and [Hongshui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongshui_River)) and the [Yuan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_\(Asia\)). The Hongshui is a principal source stream of the Xi River. Rising as the Nanpan in eastern Yunnan, it flows south and east to form part of the boundary between [Guizhou](173815.md) province and [Guangxi](47836950.md) autonomous region. Flowing for 345 km (214 mi), it unites with the [Yu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_River_\(Guangxi\)) at [Guiping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiping) to form what eventually becomes the [Xi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_River).

The province is drained by six major river systems:

- the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), here known as the [Jinsha Jiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_Jiang) (River of Golden Sands), drains the province's north.

- the [Pearl River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_\(China\)), with its source near [Qujing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qujing), collects the waters from the east.

- the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong) (Lancang), which flows from [Tibet](31516.md) into the [South China Sea](74209.md) forming the boundaries between [Laos](17752.md) and [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma), between Laos and [Thailand](30128.md) and through Laos, [Cambodia](334751.md) and [Vietnam](202354.md)

- the [Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_\(Asia\)) (Yuan or Honghe) has its source in the mountains south of [Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali,_Yunnan) and enters the South China Sea through [Hanoi](56667.md), Vietnam

- the [Salween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salween) (Nujiang), which flows into the [Gulf of Martaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Martaban) and the [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea) through Burma

- the [Irrawaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_River), which arises from the confluence of two rivers in [Kachin State](493369.md) in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma), has a few small tributaries in Yunnan's far west, such as the [Dulongjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulongjiang?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Taping River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taping_River), and rivers in the prefecture of Dehong.

### Biodiversity



Lincang mountains

Smiling young woman dressed in colourful clothes riding a yak, with coloured tassels on its horns, across a ford

Girl riding a yak in Yunnan

Yunnan is China's most biologically diverse province.[^cite_note-Bio-56] The province contains snow-capped mountains and true tropical environments, supporting a wide variety of species and vegetation types. The Yunnan camellia (*[Camellia reticulata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camellia_reticulata)*) is the provincial emblem.[^cite_note-Camelia-57]

During summer, the [Great Plateau of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plateau_of_Tibet) acts as a barrier to monsoon winds, trapping moisture in the province. This gives the [alpine flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_plant) in particular what one source has called a "lushness found nowhere else".

This topographic range combined with a [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) moisture sustains extremely high [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) and high degrees of [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism), probably the richest botanically in the world's temperate regions.[^cite_note-Bio-56] Perhaps 17,000 [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) of higher plants, of which an estimated 2,500 are endemic, can be found in the province. The province is said to have "as much flowering plant diversity as the rest of the Northern Hemisphere put together".[^cite_note-Paterson-10]

Yunnan has less than 4% of the land of China, yet the province harbors around 42.6% of all protected plant species and 72.5% of all protected wild animals in the country, of which 15% are strictly endemic to Yunnan.[^cite_note-58] Accordingly, Yunnan has been referred to as the "Kingdom of Plants" (植物王国) in China.[^cite_note-59] Yunnan is home to, most notably, the southeast Asian [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur), a giant forest-dwelling [bovine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovinae), the [Indochinese tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochinese_tiger) and the [Asian elephant](379035.md).[^cite_note-Smith2008-60] Other extremely rare species are the [Yunnan box turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_box_turtle)[^cite_note-61] and the [Yunnan snub-nosed monkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_snub-nosed_monkey).[^cite_note-62] It is feared that the [Yunnan lar gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_lar_gibbon), another moribund species, has already gone extinct.[^cite_note-63] Yunnan has 11 national and regional nature reserves. In total, the covered protected area in China is about 510,000 hectares.[^cite_note-ECNS-64]

The freshwater fish fauna is highly diverse with about 620 species, including more than 580 natives (the remaining are [introduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species)).[^cite_note-Chen2013-65] This equals almost 40% of the freshwater fish species in China. Of the Yunnan natives, more than 250 are endemic to the province and many of these are threatened.[^cite_note-Chen2013-65] Several species that are restricted to single lakes (notably [Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_Lake), [Erhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhai_Lake), [Fuxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuxian_Lake) and [Yilong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yilong_Lake)) are likely already are extinct.[^cite_note-66] By far, the most diverse order in Yunnan are [Cypriniformes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriniformes), both in total species number and number of endemics.[^cite_note-Chen2013-65]

The unique *[Sinopyrophorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinopyrophorus)* bioluminescent beetles were described from Yunnan in 2019.[^cite_note-Bi_He_Chen_Kundrata_pp._79–97-67]

### Designation

Yunnan has been designated:

- "Center of Plant Diversity" (IUCN/WWF: Davis et al. 1995)

- "Global 200 List Priority Ecoregion" for biodiversity conservation (WWF: Olsen and Dinerstein 1998)

- "Endemic Bird Area" (Birdlife International: Bibby, C. et al. 1992) and

- "Global Biodiversity Hotspot", as a part of the Hengduan Mountain Ecosystem (Conservation International: Mittermeier and Mittermeier 1997)

### Natural resources

A main source of wealth lies in its vast [mineral resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_resources); indeed, mining is the leading industry in Yunnan. Yunnan has proven deposits of 86 kinds of minerals in 2,700 places. Some 13% of the proved deposits of minerals are the largest of their kind in China, and two-thirds of the deposits are among the largest of their kind in the Yangtze River valley and in south China. Yunnan ranks first in the country in deposits of [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [lead](17747.md), [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin), [cadmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium), [indium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indium), [thallium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thallium) and [crocidolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocidolite). Other deposits include [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [gold](12240.md), [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(element\)), [silver](27119.md), [antimony](898.md) and [sulfur](27127.md). More than 150 kinds of minerals have been discovered in the province. The potential value of the proven deposits in Yunnan is 3 trillion [yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_yuan), 40% of which come from fuel minerals, 7.3% from metallic minerals and 52.7% from nonmetallic minerals.

Yunnan has sufficient rainfall and many rivers and lakes. The annual water flow originating in the province is 200 cubic kilometres, three times that of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River). The rivers flowing into the province from outside add 160 cubic kilometres, which means there are more than ten thousand cubic metres of water for each person in the province. This is four times the average in the country. The rich water resources offer abundant hydro-energy. China is constructing a series of dams on the Mekong to develop it as a waterway and source of power; the first was completed at [Manwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manwan_Dam) in 1993.

### Ethnicity



Major autonomous areas within Yunnan (excluding [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people))

Yunnan is noted for a very high level of ethnic diversity.[^cite_note-102] It has the highest number of ethnic groups among the provinces and autonomous regions in China. Among the country's [56 recognised ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China), twenty-five are found in Yunnan. Some 38% of the province population are members of ethnic minorities, including the [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), [Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_people), [Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hani_people), [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples), [Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_people), [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), [Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Lahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahu_people), [Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_people), [Nakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhi_people), [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)), [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), [Jingpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_people), [Blang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blang_people), [Pumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumi_people), [Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_people), [Achang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achang_people), [Jinuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jino_people), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_Mongols), [Derung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derung), [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people), [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_people), and [Buyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouyei_people). Several other groups are represented, but they live neither in compact settlements nor do they reach the required threshold of five thousand to be awarded the official status of being present in the province. Some groups, such as the [Mosuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosuo), who are officially recognised as part of the [Naxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxi_people), have in the past claimed official status as a national minority, and are now recognised with the status of Mosuo people.[^cite_note-103]

Ethnic groups are widely distributed in the province. Some twenty-five minorities live in compact communities, each of which has a population of more than five thousand. Ten ethnic minorities living in border areas and river valleys include the [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people), [Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_people), [Naxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxi_people), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people), [Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_people), [Achang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achang), [Buyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyei) and [Shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shui_people), with a combined population of 4.5 million; those in low mountainous areas are the [Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hani_people), [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)), [Lahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahu_people), [Va](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Va_people), [Jingpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_people), [Blang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blang_people), and [Jino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jino), with a combined population of 5 million; and those in high mountainous areas are [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), [Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people), [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), [Pumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumi_people), and [Drung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derung), with a total population of four million.[^cite_note-104]

### Languages



CIA map showing the territory of the settlement of [ethnolinguistic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnolinguistic) in Yunnan (1971).

Most [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect) of the [Chinese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) spoken in Yunnan belong to the [southwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin) subdivision of the [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) group, and are therefore very similar to the dialects of neighboring [Sichuan](65185.md) and [Guizhou](173815.md) provinces. Notable features found in many Yunnan dialects include the partial or complete loss of distinction between finals /n/ and /ŋ/, as well as the lack of /y/. In addition to the local dialects, most people also speak Standard Chinese (*[Putonghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putonghua)*, commonly called "Mandarin"), which is used in the media, by the government, and as the language of instruction in education.[^cite_note-105]

Spoken in the provincial capital, the [Kunming dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming_dialect) belongs to the [Central Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Yunnan) group of Mandarin varieties.[^cite_note-106] Even in Kunming, different districts have developed their own variations of Kunming dialect. For example, residents of Guandu district of Kunming speak the Guandu dialect, which have differences between pronunciation and intonation with Kunming dialect.

Yunnan's ethnic diversity is reflected in its linguistic diversity. Languages spoken in Yunnan include [Tibeto-Burman languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages) such as [Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_language), [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_language), [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Tibetan), [Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hani_language), [Jingpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_language), [Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_language), [Lahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahu_language), [Naxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxi_language); [Tai languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_languages) like [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_languages), [Bouyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouyei_language), [Dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_language_\(China\)), [Shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shui_language), [Tai Lü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_L%C3%BC_language) and [Tai Nüa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_N%C3%BCa_language); [Austroasiatic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages) such as [Palaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaung_language), [Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_language), [U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_language), [Hu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_language), [Mang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mang_language), [Man Met](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Met_language), [Va](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Va_languages), [Bugan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugan_language), [Muak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muak_language), and [Bumang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumang_language); as well as [Hmong–Mien languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong%E2%80%93Mien_languages).

The [Naxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxi_language), in particular, use the [Dongba script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongba_script), which is the only pictographic writing system in use in the world today. The Dongba script was mainly used to provide the Dongba priests with instructions on how to carry out their rituals: today the Dongba script features more as a tourist attraction. Perhaps the best known Western Dongba scholar was [Joseph Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Rock).

### Literacy

By the end of 1998, among the province's population, 419,800 had received college education or above, 2.11 million senior middle school education, 8.3 million junior middle school education, 18.25 million primary school education, and 8.25 million aged 15 or above were illiterate or semi-literate.

Most of the population of the province practices traditional [indigenous religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_religion) including the [Chinese folk religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion) among the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), [Bimoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimoism) among the [Yi peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people) and [Benzhuism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzhuism) among the [Bai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_people). Most of the [Hui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) of the region are [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). Christianity is dominant among the [Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people), the [Jingpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_people) and the [Derung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derung_people) ethnic groups.[^cite_note-religion2005-107]

## Agriculture



View of Duoyishu sunrise in Yuanyang

The region maintains a strong agricultural focus. Agriculture is restricted to the few upland plains, open valleys, and terraced hillsides. Level land for agriculture is extremely scarce and only about 5 percent of the province is under cultivation. [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is the main crop; [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean) (as a food crop), [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_tea), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [tobacco](30942.md), and [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) are also grown. On the steep slopes in the west [livestock](25160767.md) is raised and [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber), a valuable resource, is cut ([teak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teak) in the southwest).

Yunnan produces most of [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) grown in China (although there are also much smaller plantations in [Fujian](88585.md) and [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan)). Large-scale [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) cultivation started in Yunnan in 1988. The most commonly grown [variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coffee_varieties) in the province is catimor.[^cite_note-109]

Yunnan is famous for its quality teas. Most notably Yunnan tea. Yunnan black tea, also known as ‘[Dianhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianhong)’ (‘Dian’ is the short name for Yunnan, ‘hong’ meaning red after the deep, red liquor of the brewed tea) is a fully oxidised tea grown high in the mountainous regions between approximately 1000 metres to 2000 metres above sea level. Its unique earthy flavour makes it a highly sought after tea.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] [^cite_note-Meegahakumbura2017-113]

&#x20;\
&#x20;[Tobacco](30942.md) is the main (export) product and makes up a big part of the provincial GDP.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-114] Furthermore, Yunnan has a strong competitive potential in the fruit and vegetable industries, especially in low value-added commodities such as fresh and dried vegetables and fresh apples.



[Strawberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry) fields near [Yuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuxi)

Yunnan is one of the regions in the world with the most abundant resources of wild edible [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom). In China, there are 938 kinds of edible mushrooms, and over 800 varieties can be found in Yunnan. In 2004, around 7,744 tons of wild edible mushrooms were exported, making up for 70% of the total export of this product in China. The so-called ['pine mushroom'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsutake) is the main product in Yunnan and is exported to [Japan](15573.md) in large quantities.

Due to China's growing consumption of dairy products, Yunnan's dairy industry is also developing very rapidly and is also aiming to export to its [ASEAN](106539.md) neighbors.

The flower industry in Yunnan started to develop towards the end of the 1980s. Yunnan accounts for 50% of China's total cut flower production. The size of the planting area for cut flowers in Yunnan amounts to 4000 hectares. In 2003, the output totaled 2.3 billion stems. In 2002 the flower industry in Yunnan had a total output of RMB 3.4 billion. Export amounted to US\$18 million. Apart from sales on the domestic market, Yunnan also exports to a number of foreign countries and regions such as Japan, Korea, Hong Kong, Thailand and Singapore. According to state media, the [Dounan Flower Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dounan_Flower_Market?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Kunming supplies the majority of fresh-cut flowers in China, and is the largest flower trading market of its kind in Asia.[^cite_note-115]

## Economy

As of the mid-19th century, Yunnan exported birds, brass, tin, gemstones, musk, nuts, and [peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peafowl) feathers mainly to stores in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou). They imported silk, wool, cotton cloth, tobacco, and books.[^cite_note-Roberts1-116]

It was a major recipient of China's investment in industrial capacity during the [Third Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Front_\(China\)) campaign.[^cite_note-\:6-117]<sup>: 298 </sup>

Yunnan is one of China's relatively [undeveloped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undeveloped) provinces with more [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty)-stricken [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(People's_Republic_of_China\)) than the other provinces. In 1994, about 7 million people lived below the poverty line of less than an annual average [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income) of 300 [yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_yuan) [per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita). They were distributed in the province's 73 counties mainly and financially supported by the [central government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_government). With an input of 3.15 billion yuan in 2002, the absolutely poor rural population in the province has been reduced from 4.05 million in 2000 to 2.86 million. The [poverty alleviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_alleviation) plan includes five large projects aimed at improving infrastructure facilities. They involve planned attempts at [soil improvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_improvement), [water conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conservation), [electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power), roads, and "[green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt)" building. Upon the completion of the projects, the province hopes this will alleviate the shortages of [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain), water, electric power and roads.

Yunnan lags behind the east coast of China in relation to [socio-economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socio-economic_development). However, because of its geographic location the province has [comparative advantages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_advantage) in regional and border trade with [Southeast Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian) countries. The [Lancang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancang_River) (upper reaches of Mekong River) is the waterway to southeast Asia. In recent years land transportation has been improved to strengthen economic and trade co-operation among countries in the [Greater Mekong Subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Mekong_Subregion). Yunnan's abundance in resources determines that the province's pillar [industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(economics\)) are: [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [tobacco](30942.md), [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), hydro-[electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_industry_in_China), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism). In general, the province still depends on the [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources). The [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector) is currently the largest industrial tier in Yunnan, contributing more than 45 percent of GDP. The [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector) contributes 40 percent and agriculture 15 percent. [Investment](63121.md) is the key driver of Yunnan's [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth), especially in [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction).

The main challenge that Yunnan faces is its lack of major [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics). Its low [productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Productivity) and [competitiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_\(companies\)) restrict the rapid development of the province. The province also faces great challenges in [social issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_issues) such as [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection), [poverty elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_elimination), [illegal migration](7124412.md), [drug trafficking](218449.md) and [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_Yunnan).

Yunnan's four pillar industries include [tobacco](30942.md), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture)/[biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism). The main [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) industries are iron and steel production and copper-smelting, commercial vehicles, chemicals, fertilizers, textiles, and optical instruments.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-114] Yunnan has trade contacts with more than seventy countries and regions in the world. Yunnan established the [Muse border trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_\(Shan_State\)) (located in [Ruili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruili)) along its border with Burma.[^cite_note-118] Yunnan mainly exports [tobacco](30942.md), [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery) and electrical equipment, chemical and agricultural products, and non-ferrous metals. In 2008, its total two-way trade (imports and exports) reached US\$9.6 billion. The province signed [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) contracts involving US\$1.69 billion, of which US\$777 million were actually utilized during the year.

Yunnan's employment rate remains stable. The total number of newly entered-employee were 49.35 thousand people. The [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) at the end of 2020 was 3.92%. Yunnan's nominal GDP in 2020 was 24521.9 billion Yuan( US\$3850.92 billion), an annual growth rate of 4.0%. Its per capita GDP was 50,299 Yuan ( US\$7,898.96). The share of GDP of Yunnan's [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy), [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) and [tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) industries were 3598.91 billion Yuan, 8287.54 billion Yuan, and 12635.45 billion Yuan respectively.[^cite_note-119]

Yunnan is one of the major production bases of copper, lead, zinc, tin and aluminum in China. [Gejiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gejiu) is well known as "the Kingdom of Zinc" with the reserves ranked first in the country. The Yunxi brand refined tin is one of the main products in Gejiu, which is registered on the [London Metal Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Metal_Exchange) (LME). Besides, reserves of germanium, indium, zirconium, platinum, rock salt, sylvite, nickel, phosphate, mirabilite, arsenic and blue asbestos are also high. Significant copper deposits are found at [Dongchuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongchuan_District), iron ore at [Wuding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuding_County), and coal at [Xuanwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanwei) and [Kaiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiyuan_City,_Honghe,_Yunnan). [Economic policy](210522.md) to locate new industry in interior areas with substantial mineral wealth, led to major industrial development in Yunnan, especially in the [Kunming area](197181.md).

The electricity industry is another important economic pillar of Yunnan, which plays a key role in the "West-East Electricity Transmission Project". The electricity produced in Yunnan is mainly transported to [Guangdong](65109.md).

## Education

See also: [List of universities and colleges in Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Yunnan)

Since the 1960s, improvements have been achieved in the overall educational level, which can be seen in the increase in average years of regular education received. The development of part-time [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_education) have brought [adult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_education), [distance](8997.md) and [continuing education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuing_education) to farms, factories, offices, and other places. Evening, time off work / study leave classes allow people to receive education without leaving their jobs. Policies to upgrade adult education have begun to complement the campaign against [illiteracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiteracy). A basic Chinese vocabulary in [simplified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters) [strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke_order) is taught to millions of illiterate people in short, intensive courses. Despite progress made, Yunnan's [illiteracy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_illiteracy_rate) remains one of the highest in China mainly due to insufficient education among minority peoples.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

In [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_China), Yunnan has one "[National Key University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Key_University)"—[Yunnan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_University) in [Kunming](197181.md). There is also a growing number of technical schools, among which the most prominent are the [Yunnan Normal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_Normal_University), the [Southwest Forestry University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Forestry_University), [Yunnan Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_Agricultural_University), Yunnan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, [Kunming Medical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming_Medical_University), [Yunnan University of Traditional Chinese Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_University_of_Traditional_Chinese_Medicine), and [Kunming University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming_University_of_Science_and_Technology). Other notable establishments of learning are the [Kunming branch of the Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template\:CAS_Kunming), the [Yunnan Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_Astronomical_Observatory), and the [Yunnan Provincial Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_Provincial_Library). As of 2000, there were 24 [institutions of higher learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions_of_higher_learning) in Yunnan, with an enrollment of over 90,400 students and a faculty of 9,237; 2,562 secondary schools with an enrollment of more than 2,137,400 students and 120,461 teachers; and 22,151 primary schools with an enrollment of 4,720,600 pupils and a faculty of 210,507. The [gross enrollment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_enrollment_rate) of school-age children was 99.02%.

### HIV-AIDS

This section is an excerpt from [HIV/AIDS in Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_Yunnan).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=HIV/AIDS_in_Yunnan\&action=edit)]

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md)'s first reported [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS) case was identified in 1985 in a dying tourist.[^cite_note-132] In 1989, the first indigenous cases were reported as an outbreak in 146 infected [heroin](14034.md) users in Yunnan province, near [China's southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_China) border.[^cite_note-133]

Yunnan is the area most affected by [HIV/AIDS in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_the_People's_Republic_of_China). In 1989 first infections appeared among needle sharing drug users near the [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) border. Up until 1993, the disease had remained a problem in the border areas before mobile people (truck drivers, construction and migrant workers and travelers) brought the virus further into the country. In 1995, the provinces of [Sichuan](65185.md) and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) reported their first HIV cases, and by 1998, the virus had spread all over China.

### Railways



Viaduct of the [Dali–Lijiang Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali%E2%80%93Lijiang_Railway) near [Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_City)

The first railway in Yunnan was the [narrow gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_gauge) [Yunnan–Vietnam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Vietnam_Railway) built by France from 1904 to 1910 to connect [Kunming](197181.md) with [Vietnam](202354.md), then a French colony. The Chinese section spanned from Kunming to [Hekou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hekou_Yao_Autonomous_County), entered Vietnamese territory at [Lào Cai](27784276.md), and terminated at the seaport of [Haiphong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiphong).[^cite_note-134] During the Second World War, Britain began building a [railway from Yunnan to Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Burma_Railway) but abandoned the effort due to Japanese advance.[^cite_note-135]

Due in part to difficult terrain both locally and in surrounding provinces and the shortage of capital for rail construction, Yunnan remained outside of China's domestic rail network until 1966 when the [Guiyang–Kunming Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway) was completed.[^cite_note-136] The line would not enter into operation until 1970, the same year that the [Chengdu-Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway) was completed.[^cite_note-137] The [Nanning–Kunming Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway) to [Guangxi](47836950.md) was completed in 1997, followed by the [Neijiang–Kunming Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neijiang%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway) in 2001. The [Panxi Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_County_West_Railway), originally built in 1975 to draw coal from neighboring Guizhou, was electrified in 2001 and adds to eastern Yunnan's outbound rail transport capacity.



Kunming–Yuxi railway in Haikou Town, Kunming

Within the province, the [Kunming–Yuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming%E2%80%93Yuxi_Railway), opened in 1993, and the [Guangtong–Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangtong%E2%80%93Dali_Railway), opened in 1998, expanded the rail network to southern and western Yunnan, respectively. The [Dali–Lijiang Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali%E2%80%93Lijiang_Railway), opened in 2010, brought rail service to northwestern Yunnan. That line is planned to be [extended further north to Xamgyi'nyilha County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijiang%E2%80%93Xamgyi'nyilha_Railway).

The province is extending the railway network to neighboring countries in Southeast Asia. From [Yuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuxi), the [Yuxi–Mengzi Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuxi%E2%80%93Mengzi_Railway), built from 2005 to 2013, and the [Mengzi–Hekou Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzi%E2%80%93Hekou_Railway), under construction since 2008, will form a [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) railway connection with [Vietnam](202354.md). The [Dali–Ruili Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali%E2%80%93Ruili_Railway), under construction since May 2011, will bring rail service to the border with [Myanmar](19457.md). Also under planning is a rail line from Yuxi to Mohan, in Xishuangbana Prefecture, on the border with Laos. This line could be extended further south to [Thailand](30128.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md).

### Burma Road

The [Burma Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Road) was a highway extending about 1,100 km (680 mi) through mountainous terrain from [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio), northeast Burma northeastward to Kunming, China. Undertaken by the Chinese after the start of the Sino-Japanese War in 1937 and completed in 1938, it was a vital transportation route for wartime supplies to the Chinese government from Rangoon and shipped by railroad to Lashio from 1938 to 1946. An extension runs east through China from Kunming, then north to Chongqing. This traffic increased in importance to China after the Japanese took effective control of the Chinese coast and of Indochina. It was seized by the Japanese in 1942 and reopened when it was connected to the [Stilwell Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilwell_Road) from India. The [Ledo Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledo_Road) (later called the Stilwell Road) from [Ledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledo,_Assam), India, into Burma was begun in December 1942. In 1944 the Ledo Road reached [Myitkyina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myitkyina) and was joined to the Burma Road. Both roads have lost their former importance and are in a state of disrepair. The Burma Road's importance diminished after World War II, but it has remained a link in a 3,400-km road system from [Yangon](57404.md), [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma), to [Chongqing](171752.md).

### Highways

Road construction in Yunnan continues unabated: over the last years the province has added more new roads than any other province. Today expressways link Kunming through Dali to Baoshan, Kunming to Mojiang (on the way to Jinghong), Kunming to Qujing, Kunming to Shilin (Stone Forest). The official plan is to connect all major towns and neighbouring capitals with expressways by 2010, and to complete a high-speed road network by 2020.



Roadway in Lijiang with the [Jade Dragon Snow Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade_Dragon_Snow_Mountain) in the distance.

All county towns are now accessible by paved, all-weather roads from Kunming, all townships have a road connection (the last to be connected was Yangla, in the far north, but Dulongjiang remains cut off for about six months every year), and about half of all villages have road access.

Second-level national highways stretch 958 km (595 mi), third-level highways, 7,571 km (4,704 mi) and fourth-level highways, 52,248 km (32,465 mi). The province has formed a network of communication lines radiating from Kunming to Sichuan and Guizhou provinces and Guangxi and Tibet autonomous regions, and further on to Burma, Laos, Vietnam and Thailand.

### Waterways



[Yangzi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzi_River)

Generally, rivers are obstacles to transport in Yunnan. Only very small parts of Yunnan's river systems are navigable. However, China is constructing a series of dams on the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong) to develop it as a waterway and source of power; the first was completed at [Manwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manwan_Dam) in 1993.

In 1995, the province put an investment of 171 million yuan to add another 807 km (501 mi) of navigation lines. It built two wharfs with an annual handling capacity of 300,000 to 400,000 tons each and four wharfs with an annual handling capacity of 100,000 tons each. The annual volume of goods transported was two million tons and that of passengers transported, two million.

### Bridges

Bridge-building in Yunnan date back at least 1,300 years when the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire) built an iron chain bridge over the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) to the neighboring [Nanzhao Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao_Kingdom) at what is today [Weixi Lisu Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weixi_Lisu_Autonomous_County) during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty). Iron chain bridges are still found across high river valleys of Yunnan. The Jinlong Bridge on the [Jinsha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_River) in Lijiang remains the oldest bridge over the Yangtze. With the expansion of the highway and railway network in Yunnan, numerous large-scale bridges have been built across the region's myriad of rivers, including the [Yangtze which has dozens of crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels) in Yunnan.

### Metro

Kunming is the only city in Yunnan that has a [metro system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming_Metro). According to the [Ministry of Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_\(China\)), the Kunming Metro has six operational lines with a total length of 165.9 kilometers, and transported 23.244 million passengers in the month of February 2025.[^cite_note-139]

## Culture

See also: [Bashu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashu_culture) and [Major national historical and cultural sites (Yunnan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_national_historical_and_cultural_sites_\(Yunnan\))



Hand-painted Chinese New Year's poetry pasted on the sides of doors leading to people's homes, Old Town, [Lijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijiang_City).

Yunnan's cultural life is one of remarkable diversity. Archaeological findings have unearthed sacred burial structures holding elegant bronzes in [Jinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinning), south of [Kunming](197181.md). In northeastern Yunnan, frescoes of the [Jin dynasty (266–420)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(266%E2%80%93420\)) have been discovered in the city of [Zhatong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhaotong). Many Chinese cultural relics have been discovered in later periods. The lineage of tribal way of life of the indigenous peoples persisted uninfluenced by modernity until the mid-20th century. Tribal traditions, such as [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people) [slaveholding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) and [Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_people) [headhunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headhunting), have since been abolished. After the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) (1966–1976), in which several minority cultural and religious practices were suppressed, Yunnan has come to celebrate its cultural diversity and subsequently many local customs and festivals have flourished.[^cite_note-140]

### Tea

For the [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) from this region, see [Yunnan tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_tea).



Pu'er tea from Yunnan

Yunnan has several different tea growing regions.[^cite_note-141] One of Yunnan's best known products is [Pu-erh tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu-erh_tea) (or Puer), named after the old tea trading town of [Pu-erh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu-erh_Yunnan) (Puer). The province is also known for its [Yunnan Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Needle_tea) and other [Dianhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_hong_tea) teas, developed in the 20th century.

### Music

Main article: [Music of Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Yunnan)

Several forms of folk music from Yunnan have been identified as elements of national [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage) by the Chinese government. These include the multi-ethnic Huadeng [Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera), and musical traditions from Zhuang, Yi, and Dai people.[^cite_note-142] Huadeng Opera originated in the Ming Dynasty or earlier from [Shehuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehuo) folk celebrations.[^cite_note-143]

### Tourism



Rice-terraced mountains of Yuanyang county



[Ganden Sumtseling Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Sumtseling_Monastery) in [Shangri-La City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri-La,_Yunnan)



[Old Town of Lijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_of_Lijiang)



[Baishui River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baishui_River) with [Jade Dragon Snow Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade_Dragon_Snow_Mountain) in background

Yunnan, due to its landscapes, mild climate and cultural diversity, is one of China's major tourist destinations. Most visitors are Chinese tourists, although trips to Yunnan are organized by an increasing number of foreign travel agencies as well. Mainland tourists travel by the masses; 2.75 million Chinese visited Yunnan last October during National Holiday. Also a different trend is slowly developing; small scale and environmentally friendly [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism). At the moment projects in this field are often being set up with help of NGOs.

In 2004, tourism revenues amounted to 37 billion RMB, and thus accounting for 12.6% of the provincial GDP. The capital, [Kunming](197181.md), hosts the China International Travel Mart every two years. This tourism trade fair is the largest of its kind in Asia and serves as an important platform for professionals in the sector. More than 80 countries and regions were present during the 2005 edition.

Tourism is expected to grow further. In 2010, the province welcomed over 2.3 million overseas tourists and the Yunnan Provincial Tourism Bureau aims to draw 4.3 million overseas arrivals under the 12th Five-Year Tourism Development Plan. Kunming city is expected to add 11 new mid- to high-end hotels with an inventory of under 4,000 rooms between 2012 and 2016.[^cite_note-145]

[The Nature Conservancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nature_Conservancy) and the Chinese government came together to form a partnership and explore the possibility of bringing [adventure tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_tourism) onto the rivers of Southwest China. A [two-month white-water expedition](https://web.archive.org/web/20190702053425/https://www.snagfilms.com/films/title/the_yunnan_great_rivers_expedition) explored from the Mekong River's Moon Gorge to Yangze River's Great Bend. The expedition provided valuable information to the partnership, encouraging them to take into account the safety, culture, economics, and conservation of the Yunnan. Creating an adventure tourism sector would bring valuable economic resources to the economically struggling population, who had once relied on logging as income prior to it being banned due to deforestation.Yunnan's diverse landscapes and rich cultural heritage have made the province one of China's leading tourist destinations.&#x20;

Tourist centres in Yunnan include:

- [Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali,_Yunnan), the historic center of the [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nanzhao) and [Dali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dali) kingdoms.

- [Chuxiong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuxiong_Yi_Autonomous_Prefecture), the first stop on the way to Dali and Lijiang. Home of the Yi ethnic minority and their respective ancient town.

- [Jinghong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinghong), the center and prefectural capital of the [Xishuangbanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xishuangbanna) [Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_people) minority autonomous prefecture.

- [Lijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijiang_City), a [Naxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxi_people) minority city. It has been a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) since 1997.

- [Xamgyi'nyilha County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri-La_County) (also known as [Shangri-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri-La) and formerly Zhongdian), an ethnic [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) township and county set high in Yunnan's northwestern mountains.

- [Shilin (Stone Forest)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilin_\(Stone_Forest\)), a series of [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) outcrops east of [Kunming](197181.md).

- [Yuanyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuanyang_County,_Yunnan), a [Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hani_people) minority settlement with vast rice-terraced mountains.

- [Xishuangbanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xishuangbanna), a national scenic resort, noted for its natural and cultural attractions.
