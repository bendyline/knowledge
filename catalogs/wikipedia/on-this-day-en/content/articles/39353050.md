**International Workers' Day**, also called **Labour Day** in some countries[^cite_note-GMGApr2008-1] and often referred to as **May Day**,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] is a celebration of [labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wage_labour), the [labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement) and the [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) that is marked every year on 1 May,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Foner-5] or the first Monday in May.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Traditionally, 1 May is the date of the European [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(season\)) festival of [May Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day). The [International Workers Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers_Congresses_of_Paris,_1889) held in Paris in 1889 established the [Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International) for labor, socialist, and Marxist parties. It adopted a resolution for a "great international demonstration" in support of working-class demands for the [eight-hour day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day). The date was chosen by the [American Federation of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Labor) to commemorate a general strike in the United States, which had begun on 1 May 1886 and culminated in the [Haymarket affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair) on 4 May. The demonstration subsequently became a yearly event.[^cite_note-Foner-5] The 1904 [Sixth Conference of the Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Socialist_Congress,_Amsterdam_1904), called on "all Social Democratic Party organisations and trade unions of all countries to demonstrate energetically on the First of May for the legal establishment of the eight-hour day, for the class demands of the [proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletariat), and for [universal peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace)".[^cite_note-Lunacharsky-8]

The 1st of May, or first Monday in May, is a national [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) in many countries, in most cases known as "International Workers' Day" or a similar name. Some countries celebrate a [Labour Day](18051.md) on other dates significant to them, such as the [United States](3434750.md) and [Canada](5042916.md), which celebrate [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day) on the first Monday of September.[^cite_note-9] In 1955, the [Catholic Church](606848.md) dedicated 1 May to "[Saint Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph) the Worker". Replacing another feast of St. Joseph, this date was chosen by [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII) in 1955 as a counterpoint to the communist International Workers' Day celebrations on May Day.[^cite_note-10] Saint Joseph is the [patron saint](68055.md) of workers and craftsmen, among others.[^cite_note-\:3-11][^cite_note-Saint_Joseph-12]

## Origin

Labor celebration days have existed in some European countries since the end of the 18th century[^cite_note-Ferrand-13] – sometimes on January 20th (France, 1793)[^cite_note-14] or on June 5th (France, 1867).[^cite_note-15]

On 21 April 1856, Australian [stonemasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonemasons) in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)) undertook a mass stoppage as part of the [eight-hour workday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day) movement.[^cite_note-16] It became a yearly commemoration, inspiring American workers to have their first stoppage.[^cite_note-17] 1 May was chosen to be International Workers' Day to commemorate the 1886 [Haymarket affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair) in [Chicago](6886.md).[^cite_note-18] In that year beginning on 1 May, there was a general strike for the eight-hour workday. On 4 May, the police acted to disperse a public assembly in support of the strike when an unidentified person threw a bomb. The police responded by firing on the workers. The event led to the deaths of seven police officers and at least four civilians; sixty police officers were injured, as were one hundred and fifteen civilians.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Hundreds of labour leaders and sympathizers were later rounded-up and four were executed by hanging, after a trial that was seen as a [miscarriage of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage_of_justice).[^cite_note-the_bomb-21][^cite_note-23] The following day on 5 May, in [Milwaukee, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), the [state militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_National_Guard) fired on a crowd of strikers killing seven, including a schoolboy and a man feeding chickens in his yard.[^cite_note-\:0-24]



1889 design by [Walter Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Crane) celebrating International Workers' Day

In 1889, the first [meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers_Congresses_of_Paris,_1889) of the [Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International) was held in [Paris](22989.md), following a proposal by [Raymond Lavigne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Lavigne_\(syndicalist\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) that called for international demonstrations on the 1890 anniversary of the Chicago protests.[^cite_note-Foner-5] On 1 May 1890, demonstrations took place in the United States and most countries in Europe.[^cite_note-andy-25] Demonstrations were also held in Chile and Peru.[^cite_note-andy-25] International Workers' Day was formally recognized as an annual event at the International's [second congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Socialist_Labor_Congress_of_Brussels,_1891) in 1891.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Subsequently, the [May Day riots of 1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day_riots_of_1894) occurred. The [International Socialist Congress, Amsterdam 1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Socialist_Congress,_Amsterdam_1904) called on "all [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party) organisations and [trade unions](17626.md) of all countries to demonstrate energetically on the First of May for the legal establishment of the 8-hour day, for the class demands of the proletariat, and for universal peace."[^cite_note-Lunacharsky-8] The congress made it "mandatory upon the [proletarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian) organisations of all countries to stop work on 1 May, wherever it is possible without injury to the workers."[^cite_note-Lunacharsky-8]

In the United States and Canada, a September holiday, called Labor or [Labour Day](18051.md), was first proposed in the 1880s. In 1882, [Matthew Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Maguire_\(labor_activist\)), a [machinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinist), first proposed a Labor Day holiday on the first Monday of September[^cite_note-29] while serving as secretary of the [Central Labor Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Labor_Union) (CLU) of New York.[^cite_note-labordept-28] Others argue that it was first proposed by [Peter J. McGuire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_J._McGuire) of the [American Federation of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Labor) in May 1882,[^cite_note-Bridgemens1921-30] after witnessing the annual [labour festival](18051.md#Canada_\(country\)) held in [Toronto](64646.md), [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-origins-31] In 1887, [Oregon](26811621.md) was the first state of the United States to make it an official [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday). By the time it became an official [federal holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_holiday) in 1894, thirty [US states](18618239.md) officially celebrated Labor Day.[^cite_note-Bridgemens1921-30] Thus by 1887 in North America, Labour Day was an established, official holiday but in September,[^cite_note-Knights_of_Labor-32] not on 1 May.



A 1 May rally in [Bucharest](36877.md), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania), 1967

International Workers' Day has also been a focal point for [demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_\(protest\)) by various socialist, communist and anarchist groups since the Second International. It is one of the most important holidays in communist countries such as [China](5405.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), [Cuba](5042481.md), [Laos](17752.md), [North Korea](21255.md), and the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) countries. Celebrations in these countries typically feature elaborate workforce parades, including displays of military hardware and soldiers.

In 1955, the [Catholic Church](606848.md) dedicated 1 May to "[Saint Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph) the Worker". Saint Joseph is the [patron saint](68055.md) of workers and craftsmen, among others.[^cite_note-\:3-11][^cite_note-Saint_Joseph-12]

Today, the majority of countries around the world celebrate a workers' day on 1 May.

### Soviet Union and Eastern Bloc under socialist governments



International Workers' Day rally in [Moscow](19004.md), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), 1960

[Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries such as the Soviet Union and most countries of central and [eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) that were under the rule of [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) governments held official Workers' Day celebrations in every town and city, during which party leaders greeted the crowds. Workers carried banners with political slogans and many companies decorated their company cars. The biggest celebration of 1 May usually occurred in the capital of a particular socialist country and usually included a military display and the presence of the president and the secretary general of the party. During the [Cold War](325329.md), International Workers' Day became the occasion for large [military parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_parade) in [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and attended by the top leaders of the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin), especially the [Politburo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union), atop [Lenin's Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin's_Mausoleum). It became an enduring symbol of that period. In [Poland](22936.md), since 1982, party leaders led the official parades. In [Hungary](13275.md), International Workers' Day was officially celebrated under the communist rule, and remains a public holiday. Traditionally, the day was marked by dancing around designated "May trees".[^cite_note-33] Some factories in socialist countries were named in honour of International Workers' Day, such as [1 Maja Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Maja_Coal_Mine) in Wodzisław Śląski, Poland. In [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), the holiday was officially known as *_Internationaler Kampf- und Feiertag der Werktätigen für Frieden und Sozialismus_* ("International Day of the Struggle and Celebration of the Workers for Peace and Socialism"); similar names were used in other Eastern Bloc countries.

#### Egypt



Egyptian Communist Party flags in [Tahrir Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahrir_Square)

In [Ethiopia](187749.md), 1 May is a public holiday and celebrated as Worker's Day.[^cite_note-38] May day was most often celebrated under the previous government of the [Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg) and the [People's Democratic Republic of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Republic_of_Ethiopia).

#### Ghana

1 May is a holiday in [Ghana](12067.md). It is a day to celebrate all workers across the country. It is celebrated with a parade by trade unions and labour associations.[^cite_note-\:1-39] The parades are normally addressed by the Secretary General of the trade union congress and by regional secretaries in the regions.[^cite_note-\:1-39] Workers from different workplaces through banners and T-shirts identify their companies.[^cite_note-\:1-39]

#### Kenya

In [Kenya](188171.md), 1 May is a public holiday and celebrated as Labour Day. It is a big day addressed by the leaders of the workers' umbrella union body – the [Central Organisation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Organization_of_Trade_Unions_\(Kenya\)) (COTU). The Cabinet Secretary in charge of Ministry of Labour and Social Protection (and occasionally the president) address the workers. Each year, the government approves (and increases) the [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage) on Labour Day.[^cite_note-40]

#### Libya

In [Libya](17633.md), International Workers' Day was declared a national public holiday by the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) in 2012 the first year of the post-Qaddafi era.[^cite_note-41] On 1 May 1978, then [Libyan](17633.md) leader [Colonel Mu'ammar Al-Qaddafi](53029.md) addressed the nation in the capital city of [Tripoli](31232.md) calling for [administrative](8824684.md) and also economic [reforms across Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi#Reforms_\(1977%E2%80%931980\)).[^cite_note-42]

#### Mauritius

In [Mauritius](19201.md), 1 May is a public holiday celebrated as Labour Day. It was celebrated for the first time in Mauritius on 1 May 1938, and for the first time as an official public holiday on 1 May 1950. This was thanks largely to the efforts of Guy Rozemont, Dr. Maurice Curé, Pandit Sahadeo and Emmanuel Anquetil, as a day of special significance for Mauritian workers who for many years had struggled for their social, political and economic rights.[^cite_note-43]

#### South Africa

In [South Africa](17416221.md), Workers' Day has been celebrated as a national public holiday on 1 May each year since 1995.[^cite_note-49] Workers' Day started to get more attention by African workers in 1928, which saw thousands of workers in a mass march. In 1950, the [South African Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Communist_Party) called for a strike on 1 May in response to the [Suppression of Communism Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppression_of_Communism_Act,_1950) declaring it illegal. Police violence caused the death of 18 people across Soweto. It has its origins within the historical struggles of workers and their trade unions internationally for solidarity between working people in their struggles to win fair employment standards and more importantly, to establish a culture of human and worker rights and to ensure that these are enshrined in international law and the national law.[^cite_note-50]

In 1986, the hundredth anniversary of the [Haymarket affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair), the [Congress of South African Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_South_African_Trade_Unions) (COSATU) called for the government to establish an official holiday on 1 May. It also called for workers to stay home from work that day.[^cite_note-SABC-51] COSATU was joined by a number of prominent anti-[apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) organizations, including the [National Education Crisis Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_resistance_to_apartheid#National_Education_Crisis_Committee) and the [United Democratic Front (South Africa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Democratic_Front_\(South_Africa\)).[^cite_note-SAHO-52] The call was also supported by a number of organizations regarded as conservative, such as the [African Teachers' Association of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Teachers'_Association_of_South_Africa), the [National African Federated Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_African_Federated_Chamber_of_Commerce), and the Steel and Engineering Industries Federation of South Africa, an organization that represented employers in the metal industries.[^cite_note-SAHO-52] More than 1,500,000 workers observed the call and stayed home, as did thousands of students, taxi drivers, vendors, shopkeepers, domestic workers, and self-employed people.[^cite_note-SAHO-52] In the following years, 1 May became a popular, if not official, holiday.[^cite_note-SABC-51] As a result of the killings on 1 May 1950 and the success of COSATU's call in 1986, 1 May became associated with resistance to the apartheid government. After its [first universal election in 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election), 1 May was adopted as a public holiday, celebrated for the first time in 1995.[^cite_note-SABC-51] On its website, the city of [Durban](313650.md) states that the holiday "celebrate\[s] the role played by trade unions and other labour movements in the fight against South Africa's apartheid regime".[^cite_note-53]

### Americas

#### Antigua and Barbuda

In [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md), Labour Day is a public holiday on the first Monday in May.[^cite_note-58]

#### Argentina

In [Argentina](18951905.md), Workers' Day is an official holiday on 1 May, and is frequently associated with labour unions. Celebrations related to labour are held including demonstrations in major cities. The first Workers' Day celebration was in 1890, when Argentinian unions organized several celebrations in [Buenos Aires](255919.md) and other cities, at the same time that the international labour movement celebrated it for the first time.[^cite_note-ElHistoriador-59] In 1930, it was established as an official holiday by the [Radical Civic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Civic_Union) president [Hipólito Yrigoyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip%C3%B3lito_Yrigoyen). The day became particularly significant during the worker-oriented government of [Juan Domingo Perón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Domingo_Per%C3%B3n) (1946–55).[^cite_note-60] He permitted and endorsed national recognition of the holiday during his tenure in office.

#### Barbados

In [Barbados](3455.md), International Workers' Day is a public holiday celebrated on 1 May.[^cite_note-61]

#### Bolivia

1 May is known as Labour Day and is a holiday.[^cite_note-62] By custom, it is usually the day on which wage increases (e.g., the national minimum wage) and other labour improvements are announced by the Government. In recent years it was also the day chosen by the Bolivian government to announce the (re)nationalization of strategic sectors of the economy (e.g. hydrocarbons in 2006, telecommunications in 2008, electricity in 2010, etc.).

#### Brazil

In [Brazil](3383.md), "Workers' Day" is an official holiday celebrated on 1 May, and unions commemorate it with day-long public events.[^cite_note-63]

#### Canada

In Canada, [Labour Day](18051.md) is celebrated in September. In 1894, the government of Prime Minister [John Sparrow David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sparrow_David_Thompson) declared the first Monday in September as Canada's official Labour Day. Labor Day in the United States is on the same day.

International Workers' Day is however marked by unions and leftists on 1 May. It is an important day of trade union and community group protest in the province of [Quebec](7954867.md) (though not a provincial [statutory holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Canada)). Celebration of the International Labour Day (or "International Workers' Day"; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Journée internationale des travailleurs*) in [Montreal](7954681.md) goes back to 1906, organized by the Mutual Aid circle. The tradition had a renaissance at the time of a mass strike in 1972. On the 1973 Labour Day, the first contemporary demonstration was organized by the major trade union confederations; over 30,000 trade unionists took part in this demonstration. Further, it is the customary date on which the [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_Canada) rises.[^cite_note-64]

Since 2019, the Labour May Day Committee has organized the International Workers' Day march with a rally at [Nathan Phillips Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Phillips_Square) on either May 1 or the closest Saturday.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] The Mayworks Festival of Working People & the Arts has been held annually in Toronto since 1985 throughout the month of May.[^cite_note-67]

#### Chile

President General of the Army [Carlos Ibáñez del Campo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ib%C3%A1%C3%B1ez_del_Campo) decreed 1 May a national holiday in 1931, in honour of the dignity of workers.[^cite_note-68] All stores and public services must close for the entire day, and the major trade unions of Chile, represented in the national organization [Workers' United Center of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_United_Center_of_Chile) (Central Unitaria de Trabajadores), organize rallies during the morning hours, with festivities and cookouts in the later part of the day, in all the major cities of Chile. During these rallies, representatives of the major left-wing political parties speak to the assemblies on the issues of the day concerning workers' rights.

#### Colombia

1 May has long been recognized as Labour Day and almost all workers respect it as a national holiday.[^cite_note-69] As in many other countries, it is common to see rallies by the trade unions in all over the main regional capitals of the country.[^cite_note-70]

#### Costa Rica

First celebrated in 1913,[^cite_note-71] labor day is a public holiday, and at the same time an important day for government activities. On this day, the [President of Costa Rica](273637.md) gives a speech to the citizens and the [legislature of Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_of_Costa_Rica) about the duties that were undertaken through the previous year. The president of the legislature is also chosen by its members.[^cite_note-72]

#### United States

Main article: [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day)



Socialists in Union Square, New York City, on 1 May 1912

In the U.S. starting in 1887, a "Labor Day" celebrated on the first Monday in September was given increasing state recognition.[^cite_note-86] Then in 1894, in the wake of the nationwide [Pullman Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman_Strike),[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] [Congress](31756.md) and President [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland) passed a law declaring Labor Day a [federal holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_holiday).[^cite_note-Knights_of_Labor-32]

Efforts to recognize 1 May as a holiday have not been successful.[^cite_note-89]

In 1947, 1 May was established as [Loyalty Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalty_Day) by the U.S. [Veterans of Foreign Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_of_Foreign_Wars) as a way to counter communist influence and recruitment at International Workers' Day rallies.[^cite_note-CredoLoyalty-90] Loyalty Day was celebrated across the country with patriotic parades and ceremonies, however the growing conflict over U.S. involvement in Vietnam detracted from the popularity of these celebrations.[^cite_note-CredoLoyalty-90] In 1958, the [American Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bar_Association) campaigned to have 1 May designated as [Law Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Day_\(United_States\)), which was acknowledged in 1961 by a joint resolution of Congress.[^cite_note-91] Law Day exercises, such as mock trials and courthouse tours, are often sponsored by the American Bar Association.

Unions and Political organizations including [anarchist groups and socialist and communist parties](28903916.md) have kept the International Workers' Day tradition alive with rallies and demonstrations. In 1919 especially large demonstrations took place, and violence greeted the normally peaceful parades in [Boston](24437894.md), [New York](645042.md), and [Cleveland](5951.md) and [a number of people were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day_Riots_of_1919).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] In Milwaukee, an annual commemoration takes place at the site of the [killing of seven workers during a march for the 8-hour workday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_View_Massacre).[^cite_note-\:0-24] Some of the largest examples of this occurred during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) of the 1930s, when hundreds of thousands of workers marched in International Workers' Day [parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade) in New York's [Union Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Square_\(New_York_City\)), while cities like [Chicago](6886.md) and [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota) saw large demonstrations organized by the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA).



[San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California), Workers' Day March, 1 May 2006

In 2006, 1 May was chosen by mostly [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) [immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant) groups in the United States as the day for the [Great American Boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Boycott), a [general strike](41138909.md) of undocumented immigrant workers and supporters to protest [H.R. 4437](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Protection,_Anti-terrorism,_and_Illegal_Immigration_Control_Act_of_2005), immigration reform legislation that they felt was draconian. From 10 April to 1 May of that year, immigrant families in the U.S. called for immigrant rights, workers' rights and amnesty for undocumented workers. They were joined by socialist and other leftist organizations on 1 May.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] On 1 May 2007, a mostly peaceful demonstration in [Los Angeles](18110.md) in support of undocumented immigrant workers ended with a widely televised [dispersal by police officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_May_Day_m%C3%AAl%C3%A9e). In March 2008, the [International Longshore and Warehouse Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Longshore_and_Warehouse_Union) announced that [dockworkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevedore) will move no [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo) at any [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) ports on 1 May 2008, as a protest against the continuation of the [Iraq War](5043324.md) and the diversion of resources from domestic needs.[^cite_note-96]

On 1 May 2012, members of [Occupy Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupy_Wall_Street) and labor unions held protests together in a number of cities in the United States and Canada to commemorate International Workers' Day and to protest the state of the economy and economic inequality.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

refer to caption

An [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World) group marching in [Minneapolis](6097240.md) on 1 May 2010

On 1 May 2017, immigrants' rights advocates, labor unions and leftists held protests against the immigration and economic policies of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in cities throughout the US, Chicago and Los Angeles having some of the largest marches.[^cite_note-wapo-1may2017-99][^cite_note-nytimes-1may2017-100]

On 1 May 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), "workers at [Amazon](90451.md), [Whole Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Foods_Market), [Instacart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instacart), [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart), [FedEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx), [Target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Corporation), and [Shipt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipt) say they will walk off the job ... to protest their employers' failure to provide basic protections for frontline workers who are risking and losing their lives at work."[^cite_note-101] Additionally, on the same day, there will be a [rent strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_strike), the largest in nearly a century.[^cite_note-102]

On 1 May 2021, [black bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_bloc) protesters clashed with police in Oakland and Portland. Numerous other activities occurred across the country.[^cite_note-Its_Going_Down-103]

In 1 May 2026, May Day protests were held across the United States in support of workers’ rights, union protections, and public education funding. Labor unions, teachers, immigrant-rights organizations, and community groups organized rallies and marches in a number of cities. Some demonstrations were associated with the slogan “No School, No Work, No Shopping”, which promoted consumer and workplace boycotts.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

The protests focused on issues including wages, labor protections, economic inequality, and opposition to anti-union policies. Some reports also noted concerns over rising living costs and reduced purchasing power in the context of higher global energy prices and geopolitical tensions, including the [Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

Protesters voiced opposition to a series of government policies and economic conditions that they argued systematically favored wealthy individuals while neglecting the working class. Central to their grievances was the perceived erosion of basic labor rights, including the difficulty or inability of federal employees to appeal wrongful dismissals—a consequence of policies that reclassified many workers as "at-will" employees. Demonstrators also expressed concern over repeated budget cuts to workplace safety programs, which they believed directly endangered the lives of public sector workers such as nurses and teachers. Additionally, protesters highlighted the stagnation of the federal minimum wage, which had remained unchanged at \$7.25 per hour for more than fifteen years—a duration that, in their view, reflected a broader pattern of inattention to the worsening living standards of ordinary workers.[^cite_note-111]

#### Japan



2011 [National Trade Union Council (*Zenrokyo*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trade_Union_Council_\(Japan\)) May Day march, Tokyo

See also: [Labor Thanksgiving Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Thanksgiving_Day)

International Workers' Day is not officially designated by the Japanese government as a national holiday, but as it lies between other national holidays, it is a day off work for the vast majority of Japanese workers. Many employers give it as a day off, and otherwise workers take it as "paid leave". 1 May occurs during "[Golden Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Week_\(Japan\))", together with 29 April ("[Shōwa Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dwa_Day)"), 3 May ("[Constitution Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Memorial_Day)"), 4 May ("[Greenery Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenery_Day)") and 5 May ("[Children's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodomo_no_hi)").[^cite_note-122] Workers generally take the day off work not so much to join street rallies or labour union gatherings, but more to go on holiday for several consecutive days (in Japanese corporate culture, taking weekdays off for personal pleasure is widely frowned upon).[^cite_note-123]

Some major labour unions organize rallies and demonstrations in [Tokyo](30057.md),[^cite_note-124] [Osaka](52230.md), and [Nagoya](61720.md).[^cite_note-125] Japan has a long history of labour activism and has had a communist and socialist party in the [Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Diet) since 1945. In 2008, the National Confederation of Trade Unions (*[Zenrōren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenr%C5%8Dren)*) held a rally in [Yoyogi Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoyogi_Park) attended by 44,000 participants.[^cite_note-126] In 2026, *[Rengō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RENGO)*, the largest Japanese trade union, held its International Workers' Day rally on April 29th with the Prime Minister attending.[^cite_note-127]

In Belgium, Labour Day ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Dag van de Arbeid*, *Feest van de Arbeid*, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Journée des travailleurs*, *Fête du travail*), is observed on 1 May and is an [official holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Belgium) since 1948.[^cite_note-134] Various socialist and communist organizations hold parades and other events in different cities.[^cite_note-135]

#### Bosnia and Herzegovina

#### Bulgaria

Labour Day is one of the [public holidays in Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Bulgaria), where it is known as *Labour Day and International Workers' Solidarity Day* ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Ден на труда и на международната работническа солидарност) and celebrated annually on 1 May.[^cite_note-137] The first attempt to celebrate it was in 1890 by the Bulgarian Typographical Association. Since 1945 the communist authorities in the [People's Republic of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria) began to celebrate the holiday every year. After the end of socialism in Bulgaria in 1989 Labour Day continues to be an official and public holiday, but state authorities are not committed to the organization of mass events.[^cite_note-138]

#### Croatia

In Croatia, 1 May is a national holiday, Labour Day. Many public events are organized and held all over the country where bean soup is given out to all people as a symbol of a real workers' dish. In [Zagreb](55906.md), the capital, a major gathering is in [Maksimir Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksimir_Park), which is located in the east part of Zagreb. #### Cyprus

#### Czech Republic

#### Denmark

The location of the first celebration, the [Fælledparken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A6lledparken), still plays an important part today with speeches by politicians and trade unionists to mark the occasion. #### Finland

In Finland, 1 May is an official and national holiday. #### France



Workers' Day demonstration in [Lyon](8638634.md), France

In France, 1 May is a public holiday called Workers' Day ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Fête des Travailleurs*). It is, in fact, the only day of the year when employees are legally obliged to be given leave, save professions that cannot be interrupted due to their nature (such as workers in hospitals and public transport).[^cite_note-147] Demonstrations and marches are a Labour Day tradition in France, where trade unions organize parades in major cities to defend workers' rights. It is also customary to offer a [lily of the valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_of_the_valley) to friends or family. This custom dates back to 1561, when king [Charles IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IX_of_France), aged 10, waiting for his accession to the throne, gave a lily of the valley to all ladies present. Today, the fiscal administration exempts individuals and workers' organizations from any tax or administrative duties related to the sales of lilies of the valley, provided they are gathered from the wild, and not bought to be resold.

#### Germany

In April 1933, the recently installed [Nazi government](21212.md) declared 1 May the "Day of National Work", an official state holiday, and announced that all celebrations were to be organized by the government. Any separate celebrations by [Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany), [Social Democrats](60585.md) or [labour unions](17626.md) were banned.[^cite_note-148] After [World War II](32927.md), 1 May remained a state holiday in both [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) and [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). In communist [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), workers were *de facto* required to participate in large state-organized parades on International Workers' Day.[^cite_note-149] Today in Germany it is simply called "Labour Day" (*Tag der Arbeit*), and there are numerous demonstrations and celebrations by independent workers' organizations. Today, [Berlin](3354.md) witnesses yearly demonstrations on Labour Day, the largest organised by labour unions, political parties, the [far left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_left) and the leftist *[Autonomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism)*.



Labour Day rally in [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), Germany, 1 May 2013

Since 1987, Labour Day has also become known for riots in some districts of Berlin. After police actions against radical leftists in that year's annual demonstrations, the [Autonomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism) scattered and sought cover at the ongoing annual street fair in [Kreuzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzberg). Three years prior to the [reunification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Germany), violent protests would only take place in the former [West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Berlin). The protesters began tipping over police cars, violently resisting arrest, and began building [barricades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barricade) after the police withdrew due to the unforeseen resistance. Cars were set on fire, shops plundered and burned to the ground. The police eventually ended the riots the following night. These violent forms of protests by the radical left later increasingly involved participants without political motivation.[^cite_note-150]

Annual street fairs have proven an effective way to prevent riots, and Labour Day in 2005 and 2006 have been among the most peaceful known to Berlin in nearly 25 years. [Neo-Nazis](54361.md) and other groups on the [far right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_right), such as the [National Democratic Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Party_of_Germany), have used May Day to schedule public demonstrations.[^cite_note-151]

Labour Day violence flared up again in 2010. After an approved far-right demonstration was blocked by leftists, a parade by an estimated 10,000 leftists and anarchists turned violent and resulted in an active response by the [Berlin Police](34339160.md).[^cite_note-152]

#### Greece

In Greece, 1 May is an optional public holiday, but is treated by workers as a strike. The [Ministry of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Labour_and_Social_Affairs_\(Greece\)) retains the right to classify it as an official public holiday on an annual basis, and it customarily does so.[^cite_note-153] The day is called *Ergatikí Proto-magiá* (Εργατική Πρωτομαγιά, lit. "Workers' 1 May") and celebrations are marked by demonstrations in which left-wing political parties, anti-authority groups, and workers' unions participate. On Workers' Day in 2010, there were major protests all over Greece, most notably [Athens](1216.md) and [Thessaloniki](40471.md), by many left, anarchist and communist supporters and some violent clashes with [riot police](1148542.md) who were sent out to contain the protesters. They opposed economic reforms, an end to job losses and wage cuts in the face of the government's proposals of massive public spending cuts. These reforms are to fall in line with the [IMF](15251.md)-[EU](9317.md)-[ECB](9474.md) loan proposals, which demand that Greece liberalize its economy and cut its public spending and private sector wages, which many believe will decrease living standards.[^cite_note-154]

#### Hungary

Hungary celebrates 1 May as a national holiday, Workers' Day ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *A munka ünnepe*), with open-air festivities and fairs all over the country. Many towns raise May poles and festivals with various themes are organized around the holiday. Left-wing parties and trade unions hold public rallies commemorating Labour Day.[^cite_note-155]

#### Iceland

#### Ireland



International Workers' Day parade in [Belfast](5046.md), 2011

The [Irish Congress of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Congress_of_Trade_Unions) (ICTU) marks International Workers' Day with rallies in [Belfast](5046.md) and [Dublin](8504.md) and other events such as lectures, concerts and film screenings also take place around a wider International Workers' Day festival.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] The first Monday in May has been a [public holiday in the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland) since 1994[^cite_note-160] and in Northern Ireland since 1978.[^cite_note-161] In the Republic the public holiday was demanded by the ICTU[^cite_note-162] and proposed by the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)) in negotiating [its 1992–94 coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Government_of_Ireland) with [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il), and marked the centenary of the ICTU's predecessor, the [Irish Trades Union Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Trades_Union_Congress).[^cite_note-163] The public holiday has no official designation, as "Workers' Day" or otherwise.[^cite_note-164] In 2005, Labour's [Ruairi Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruairi_Quinn) condemned an alleged Fianna Fáil proposal to replace the May holiday with one on 24 April commemorating the [1916 Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_Rising) as a slight to workers. The proposal was, in actuality, for an extra holiday – rather than a replacement.[^cite_note-165]

#### Italy



Traditional 1 May Concert in St. John Lateran square, [Rome](25458.md)

The first International Workers' Day celebration in [Italy](14532.md) took place in 1890. Industrialists threatened to fire absent workers, so most industrial workers showed up to work. The event succeeded due to artisan and craft support.[^cite_note-166] It was abolished under the [Fascist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_under_Fascism_\(1922%E2%80%931943\)) and immediately restored after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). (During the fascist period, a "Holiday of the Italian Labour" (*Festa del lavoro italiano*) was celebrated on 21 April, the date of *[Natale di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natale_di_Roma)*, when [Rome](25458.md) was allegedly founded.[^cite_note-167]) In 1947, following an unexpected electoral victory of the [Popular Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Democratic_Front_\(Italy\)) in [Sicily](27619.md), local secessionists and pro-USA mafia hitmen killed 14 and injured 27 firing machine guns at an International Workers' Day celebration in the [Portella della Ginestra Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portella_della_Ginestra_massacre). International Workers' Day is now an important celebration in Italy and is a national holiday regardless of what day of the week it falls. The *Concerto del Primo Maggio* ("1st of May Concert"), organized by Italian labour unions in Rome in [Piazza di Porta San Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_San_Giovanni_\(Rome\)) has become an important event in recent years. Every year the concert is attended by a large audience of mostly young people and involves the participation of many famous bands and songwriters, lasting from 15\:00 until midnight. The concert is usually broadcast live on [Rai 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_3).[^cite_note-168] A second big concert is organised in the city of [Taranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taranto) and it is transmitted locally by [TGR Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGR).

As Lithuania [restored its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_the_Re-Establishment_of_the_State_of_Lithuania) in 1990, Work Day lost its public holiday status, but regained it in 2001.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

#### Malta

In Malta, 1 May is an official public holiday celebrated as "Workers' Day",[^cite_note-maltagov-174] together with the [religious feast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_feast) of [Saint Joseph the Worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph). #### Montenegro

In Montenegro, 1 May is an official public holiday and a day off work and a day out of school. It is the only official holiday from socialist times that is still officially celebrated.[^cite_note-175]

#### Netherlands

In the Netherlands, 1 May or Labour Day ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Dag van de Arbeid*) is not an official holiday. This is due in part to its proximity to the national holiday, [Koningsdag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koningsdag), which was celebrated on the day before until 2013. Labour movements also did not see the need to agitate for an extra day off during the [Post–World War II recovery efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93World_War_II_economic_expansion). [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) who joined the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Netherlands\)) in this same period also wanted to distance themselves from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) because of [Cold War](325329.md) sentiments.[^cite_note-\:2-176] A small number of public servants has the day off, as well as bank employees and financial traders as the European banking system is not open that day.

#### Norway

In Norway, Labour Day ([Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language): *Arbeidernes Dag*) is celebrated 1 May and has been an official public holiday since 1947.[^cite_note-178] It was first introduced by the workers movement in 1890,[^cite_note-179] and recognized as an official flag day in 1935.[^cite_note-180] The program for the day is presented by local unions and labour organizations.

#### Poland



[1 May Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Maja_Coal_Mine) in Wodzisław Śląski, Poland

In Poland, since the fall of communism, 1 May is officially celebrated as [Labour Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Poland).[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] It is customary for labour activists to organize parades in cities and towns across Poland. The holiday is also commonly referred to as "Labour Day" ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Święto Pracy*).[^cite_note-183]

In Poland, Labour Day is closely followed by [May 3rd Constitution Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_3rd_Constitution_Day). These two dates combined often result in a [long weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_weekend) called *Majówka*, which may last for up to 9 days from 28 April to 6 May, at the cost of taking only 3 days off. People often travel, and *Majówka* is unofficially considered the start of barbecuing season in Poland.[^cite_note-184]

Between these two, on 2 May, there is a patriotic holiday, the Day of the Polish Flag (*[Dzień Flagi Rzeczypospolitej Polskiej](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzie%C5%84%20Flagi%20Rzeczypospolitej%20Polskiej)*), introduced by a parliamentary act of 20 February 2004. The day, however, does not force paid time off.[^cite_note-185]

In Soviet times, streets, places, squares, parks and also factories were frequently named in honor of International Workers' Day, such as [1 Maja Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Maja_Coal_Mine) in [Wodzisław Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodzis%C5%82aw_%C5%9Al%C4%85ski)[^cite_note-186].

#### Portugal

In Portugal, Workers' Day ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Dia do Trabalhador*) on 1 May was suppressed during the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)) dictatorship. The first workers' day demonstration was held a week after the [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution) of 25 April 1974. It is still the largest demonstration in the history of Portugal. It is used as an opportunity for workers and workers' groups to voice their discontent over working conditions in demonstrations across Portugal, the largest being held in Lisbon. It is an official public holiday.[^cite_note-187]

#### Romania



Delegates of the [Romanian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Communist_Party) on 1 May 1965

In Romania, 1 May, known as the "International Labour Day" ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Ziua internațională a muncii*), the "International Workers' Day" (*Ziua internațională a muncitorilor*), or simply "1/First of May" (*1/Întâi Mai*), is an official [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Romania). During the [communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Romania), like in all former Eastern Bloc countries, the day was marked by large state-organized parades in most towns and cities. After the [Romanian Revolution of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution_of_1989), 1 May continues to be an official public holiday, but without any state organized events or parades. Most people celebrate together with friends and family, organising [picnics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picnic) and [barbecues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbecue). It is also the first day of the year when people, especially those from the southeastern part of the country including the capital [Bucharest](36877.md), go to spend the day in one of the [Romanian Black Sea resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Black_Sea_resorts).[^cite_note-188]

In 1979, ten years after the last annual parade, a brief [exhibition drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_drill) and [military tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tattoo) of the forces of the Moscow Garrison took place.[^cite_note-193]

In 1991, which preceded the last year that demonstrations were held in Red Square, International Workers' Day grew into high-spirited political action. Around 50,000 people participated in a rally in Red Square in 1991 after which the tradition was interrupted for 13 years. In the early post-Soviet period the holiday turned into massive political gatherings of supporters of radically minded politicians. For instance, an action dubbed as "a rally of communist-oriented organisations" was held in Red Square in 1992. The rally began with performance of the Soviet Union anthem and raising the [Red Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_\(politics\)) and ended with appeals from the leader of opposition movement Working Moscow, [Viktor Anpilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Anpilov), "for early dismissal of President [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin), ousting [Moscow Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mayor) [Gavriil Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavriil_Kharitonovich_Popov) from power and putting the latter on trial". Since 1992, International Workers' Day is officially called "Spring and Labor Day", and remains a major holiday in present-day Russia.

#### Serbia

In Serbia, 1 May (and also 2 May) is a day off work and a day out of school. It is one of the major popular holidays, and the only official holiday from socialist times that is still officially celebrated. People celebrate it all over the country. By tradition 1 May is celebrated by countryside picnics and outdoor barbecues. May is marked by warm weather in Serbia. In [Belgrade](55904.md), the capital, most people go to [Avala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avala) or [Košutnjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1utnjak), which are parks located in Rakovica and Čukarica. People go around the country to enjoy nature. A major religious holiday of [Djurdjevdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djurdjevdan) is on 6 May so quite often days off work are given to connect these two holidays and weekend, creating a small spring break. 1 May is celebrated by most of the population regardless of political views.

In Slovakia, 1 May is an official holiday. Celebrations are held surrounding workers' day but are also connected with the commemoration of the [entry of the Slovak Republic into the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) (1 May 2004).[^cite_note-205]

#### Spain

In Spain, the first Workers' Day (*Día del Trabajador*) was celebrated in 1889 but only became a public holiday with the beginning of the [Spanish Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Second_Republic) in 1931. #### Sweden



[Swedish Social Democratic Party](202978.md) at International Workers' Day demonstration in [Stockholm](26741.md), [Sweden](5058739.md), in 2006. The party has dominated Swedish politics for nearly a century. The [trade union palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Trade_Union_Confederation) in Stockholm is seen at the end of the picture.

1 May has been an important part of Swedish history since the late 19th century. The day was made a [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) in 1938 but had been celebrated by the [Swedish Social Democratic Party](202978.md) and the [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4nsterpartiet) since 1890. The first International Workers' Day celebration gathered more than 50,000 people in central Stockholm. The crowd went to hear speeches by the leading figures in the [Swedish labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_labour_movement) such as [Hjalmar Branting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Branting) (later [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister)), [August Palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Palm) and [Hinke Bergegren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinke_Bergegren). During [World War I](4764461.md) the demonstrations mainly had a peace message and the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_People's_Party_\(Sweden\)) also joined the demonstrations. The [eight-hour working day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-hour_working_day) and [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) were the principal themes during the troubled times after World War I.

Recognizing the central contributions of workers and international worker solidarity in Swedish social, economic, political and cultural development, International Workers' Day demonstrations are an important part of Swedish politics and culture for social democrats and democratic socialists. In Stockholm the [Social Democratic Party](202978.md) always marches towards [Norra Bantorget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norra_Bantorget), the historical, physical centre of the Swedish labour movement, where they hold speeches in front of the headquarters of the [Swedish Trade Union Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Trade_Union_Confederation), while the smaller [Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4nsterpartiet) marches in larger numbers[^cite_note-212] towards [Kungsträdgården](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kungstr%C3%A4dg%C3%A5rden).

Since 1967, the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_\(Sweden\)) and its [youth wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_wing), [Revolutionary Communist Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Communist_Youth_\(Sweden\)), have held their own International Workers' Day march, known as *Röd Front* ('Red Front').[^cite_note-213] In 2016, Röd Front marches were held at 33 locations across the country.[^cite_note-214] The largest Röd Front marches are usually held in the industrial and financial port town of [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg), Sweden's second-largest city and one of the party's strongholds.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216]

#### Switzerland

In Switzerland, the status of 1 May differs depending on the [canton](180752.md) and sometimes on the municipality. Labour Day is known as *Tag der Arbeit* in German-speaking cantons, as *Fête du travail* in the French-speaking cantons, and as *Festa del lavoro* in the Italian-speaking canton of [Ticino](84147.md).

- In the cantons of [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft), [Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Stadt), [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura), [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuch%C3%A2tel), and [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zurich), Labour Day is an official public holiday equal to Sundays, based on federal law (*Bundesgesetz über die Arbeit in Industrie, Gewerbe und Handel*, article 20a).

- In the cantons of [Schaffhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schaffhausen), [Thurgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgau), and Ticino, Labour Day is an official "day off" (*Ruhetag*). This is equal in practice to an official public holiday, but is not based on federal law and cantonal regulations may differ in details.

- In the canton of [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn) it is an official half-day holiday (starting at 12 noon).

- In the canton of [Fribourg](292842.md), public servants get the afternoon off, many companies follow this practice.

- In the canton of [Aargau](2467.md) it is not an official holiday, but most employees get the afternoon off.

- In the municipalities of [Hildisrieden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildisrieden) and [Schüpfheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%BCpfheim) (both in the canton of [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lucerne)) as well as in [Muotathal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muotathal) (canton of [Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz)), 1 May is an official public holiday, but as commemoration day of the local [patron saint](68055.md), not as Labour Day. In the other parts of the cantons of Lucerne and Schwyz, 1 May is a regular work day.

- In all other cantons, 1 May is a regular work day.

  [^cite_note-217]

The largest Labour Day celebrations in Switzerland are held in the city of [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich). Each year, Zürich's 1 May committee, together with the [Swiss Federation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federation_of_Trade_Unions), organizes a festival and 1 May rally. It is the largest rally held on a regular basis in Switzerland.[^cite_note-218]

#### Turkey



Istanbul May Day clashes in 2013



Workers marching to [Taksim Square](631961.md), 1 May 2012

1 May is an official holiday celebrated in Turkey. It was a holiday as "Spring Day" until 1981 when it was canceled after the [1980 coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). In 2009, the Turkish government restored the holiday after some casualties and demonstrations. [Taksim Square](631961.md) is the centre of the celebrations due to the [Taksim Square massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taksim_Square_massacre).[^cite_note-219]

Workers' Day was first celebrated in 1912 in [Istanbul](3391396.md) and in 1899 in [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir). After the establishment of the Turkish Republic in 1923, the celebrations continued. In 1924, it was forbidden by a decree of the [Kemalist government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) in both 1924 and 1925, demonstrations were intervened by arm floats. In 1935, The National Assembly declared 1 May as "Spring Day" to be a public holiday.[^cite_note-marksist.com-220]

During the events leading to the 1980 Turkish coup d'état, a massacre occurred on 1 May 1977 (Taksim Square massacre), in which unknown people ([agents provocateurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_provocateur)) opened fire on the crowd. The crowd was the biggest in Turkish workers' history with the number of people approximating 500,000. In the next two years, provocations and confusion continued and peaked before the 1980 coup d'état. The 1 May holiday was cancelled after the coup d'état. Still, demonstrations continued with small crowds, and in 1996, three people were killed by police bullets, and a plain-clothes man who spied in the crowd was revealed and lynched by workers. On the same evening, a video broadcast on TV showed that two participants in the demonstration were lynched by far right-wing nationalist groups and this lynching occurred in front of police forces who were watching the scene with happy faces. Thus, 1 May 1996 has been remembered by workers' movements.[^cite_note-marksist.com-220]

In 2007, the 30th anniversary of the Taksim Square Massacre, leftist workers' unions wanted to commemorate the massacre in [Taksim Square](631961.md). Since the government would not let them into the square, 580–700 people were stopped and 1 person died under police control. After these events, the government declared 1 May as "Work and Solidarity Day" but not as a holiday. In the next year, the day was declared as a holiday, but people were still not allowed to gather in Taksim Square.[^cite_note-221] The year 2008 was remembered with police violence in Istanbul. Police fired tear gas grenades among the crowds, and into hospitals and a primary school. Workers pushed forward so that thousands gathered in Taksim in 2011.[^cite_note-222]

After three years of peaceful meetings in 2013, meetings in Taksim Square were forbidden by the government. Clashes occurred between police and workers; [water cannon](431462.md) and [tear gas](1202646.md) have been widely used.[^cite_note-223]

#### Ukraine

International Workers' Day is a public holiday in Ukraine, inherited from the Soviet era. The 1st May as a day of workers' solidarity in Kyiv began as early as 1894.[^cite_note-224] Until 2018, 2 May was also a public holiday (as in the Soviet era), instead in 2017 [Western Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity)'s [Christmas](6237.md) celebrated 25 December became a new Ukrainian public holiday.[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-BBCU42008437b-226] The 1 May International Workers' Day remained a Ukrainian public holiday, although it was renamed (also in 2017) from "Day of International Solidarity of Workers" to "Labour Day".[^cite_note-BBCU42008437b-226]

In 2015, the [Communist Party's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Ukraine) Labor Day rallies were banned in [Kyiv](585629.md) and [Kharkiv](2788558.md).[^cite_note-227]

Late May 2015 [laws that ban communist symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine) came into effect in Ukraine, thus banning communist symbols, singing the Soviet national hymn or the Internationale.[^cite_note-228]

According to Interior Minister [Arsen Avakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsen_Avakov) during the 2016 International Workers' Day rallies in some major cities the number of [police officers](44656231.md) far outnumbered the number of rally participants.[^cite_note-mptrmd16U-229] With in [Dnipro](989795.md) 193 policemen protecting 25 rally participants.[^cite_note-mptrmd16U-229]

#### United Kingdom

A public bank holiday in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) was created in 1978.[^cite_note-230] It is called the "Early May bank holiday" and is held on the first Monday in May each year.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-UK_bank_holidays-232]

### Oceania

#### Australia

See also: [Labour Day § Australia](18051.md#Australia)



The Labour Day March in [Brisbane, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane,_Queensland), is the largest rally in [Australia](4689264.md).

While unofficial activities and commemorations associated with International Workers' Day occur on 1 May in Australia, [Labour Day](18051.md) in the various states and territories generally falls on other days. In the [Northern Territory](21638.md) and [Queensland](59051.md), Labour Day is a public holiday on the first Monday in May.[^cite_note-233] In Australia, one of the first Labour Day marches occurred in Queensland on 1 May 1891.[^cite_note-234]

#### New Zealand

[New Zealand](4913064.md) workers were among the first in the world to claim the right for an eight-hour working day when, in 1840, the carpenter [Samuel Parnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Duncan_Parnell)[^cite_note-235] won an eight-hour day in [Wellington](33804.md). Labour Day was first celebrated in New Zealand on 28 October 1890.[^cite_note-236] Labour Day falls every year on the fourth Monday of October.

### South Asia

#### Bangladesh

In Bangladesh, 1 May is a public holiday and called International Workers' Solidarity Day. A parade and other events are held on the day to commemorate the occasion.[^cite_note-237]

#### India



*[Triumph of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumph_of_Labour)* at the [Marina Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Beach) in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai)

In India, Labour Day is a not a public holiday on 1 May.[^cite_note-238] The International Workers' Day is tied to labour movements for communist and socialist political parties. Labour Day is known as "Uzhaipalar dhinam" in [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) and was first celebrated in [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), "Kamgar Din" in [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), "Karmikara Dinacharane" in [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada), "Karmika Dinotsavam" in [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), "Kamgar Divas" in [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language), "Thozhilaali Dinam" in [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam) and "Shromik Dibosh" in [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language). Since Labour day is not a national holiday, Labour day is observed as public holiday at [State Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_India)'s discretion. Many parts especially in [North Indian States](1552939.md) it is not a public holiday.[^cite_note-239]

The first celebration in [India](14533.md) was organized in Madras (now [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai)) by the [Labour Kisan Party of Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Kisan_Party_of_Hindustan) on 1 May 1923.[^cite_note-240] This was also the first time the [red flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_\(politics\)) was used in India.[^cite_note-rao1-241] The party leader [Singaravelu Chettiar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayapuram_Singaravelu_Chettiar) made arrangements to celebrate International Workers' Day in two places in 1923. One meeting was held at the beach opposite to the [Madras High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_High_Court); the other meeting was held at the [Triplicane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triplicane) beach. *[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu)* newspaper, published from Madras reported,[^cite_note-242]

> The Labour Kisan party has introduced May Day celebrations in Madras. Comrade Singaravelar presided over the meeting. A resolution was passed stating that the government should declare May Day as a holiday. The president of the party explained the non-violent principles of the party. There was a request for financial aid. It was emphasised that workers of the world must unite to achieve independence.

1 May is also celebrated as "[Maharashtra Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Day)"[^cite_note-243] and "[Gujarat Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Day)" to mark the date in 1960, when the two western states attained statehood after the erstwhile [Bombay State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_State) was divided on linguistic lines. Maharashtra Day is held at [Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji) Park in central [Mumbai](19189.md). Schools and offices in Maharashtra remain closed on 1 May. A similar parade is held to celebrate [Gujarat Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Day) in [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar).

[Vaiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaiko) (Vai Gopalsamy), General Secretary of [Marumalarchi Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marumalarchi_Dravida_Munnetra_Kazhagam), appealed to the then Prime Minister [V. P. Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._P._Singh) to declare 1 May as a national holiday, to which the PM heeded and from then on it became a national holiday to celebrate International Labour Day.[^cite_note-244]

#### Maldives

[Maldives](19117.md) first observed the holiday in 2011, after a declaration by [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Maldives) [Mohamed Nasheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Nasheed). He noted that this move highlighted the government's commitment as well as efforts of private parties to protect and promote workers' rights in the Maldives.[^cite_note-245]

#### Nepal

International Workers' Day was first observed in [Nepal](171166.md) since 1962.[^cite_note-246] The day became a public holiday in 2007.[^cite_note-247]

#### Pakistan

International Labour Day is observed in Pakistan on 1 May to commemorate the social and economic achievements of workers. It is a public and national holiday. Many organized street demonstrations take place on Labour Day (also known as Youm-e-Mazdoor) in which workers and labour unions protest against labour repression and demand more rights, better wages and benefits.[^cite_note-248]

#### Sri Lanka

In Sri Lanka, International Workers' Day was declared a public, bank, and mercantile holiday in 1956.[^cite_note-249] Rallies are held in the capital, [Colombo](56636.md).[^cite_note-250]

### Southeast Asia

#### Cambodia

In Cambodia, it is known as International Labour Day and is a public holiday.[^cite_note-251] No marches for labour day were permitted in Cambodia for several years after the [2013 Cambodian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Cambodian_general_election) and surrounding mass protests. A tightly controlled march on a limited scale was first permitted again in 2019.[^cite_note-252]

#### Indonesia



[Anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Indonesia) demonstration in Jakarta, 2007

International Workers' Day, or Labour Day, in [Indonesia](14579.md) was first observed by *Kung Tang Hwee* labour union in Semarang on 1918. The organizations such as [Sarekat Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarekat_Islam), [Boedi Oetomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boedi_Oetomo), and Insulinde also took parts on strike that day through *Radicale Concentratie* alliance. But in 1927 the celebrations banned by the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) colonial government.[^cite_note-253]

After independence, the 1 May Labour Day was observed again from 1946, with [Soekarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soekarno), the first president of the Republic, always attending the celebration. Two years later, he signed Law No. 12 of 1948 concerning workers' rights. The [Soeharto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soeharto) [New Order](2864794.md) regime banned Labour Day celebrations again from 1967 because of the day's association with Marxist and communist movements. The regime changed Labour Day to 20 February from 1973, marking the day on which all labour unions were merged by the government into the All Indonesian Federation of Workers (now [Confederation of All Indonesian Workers' Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_All_Indonesian_Workers'_Unions)).[^cite_note-254]

After the [fall of the New Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Suharto), the day began celebrated again as a day for protests by labourers and workers. The day was officially made a public holiday in 2014. Every year on the day, labourers and students take over the streets in major cities across the country, voicing their demands for better income and supportive policies from the government.[^cite_note-255]

#### Malaysia

[Malaysia](3607937.md) began observing the holiday in 1972 following an announcement by the late [Malaysian Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Malaysia), [Ismail Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Abdul_Rahman).[^cite_note-256]

#### Myanmar

In Myanmar, 1 May is known as Labour Day ([Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language): အလုပ်သမားနေ့) and is a public holiday.[^cite_note-257]

#### Philippines

1 May is known as "Labor Day" ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Araw ng Manggagawa*, also known as *Araw ng Paggawa*) and is a [public holiday in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday_in_the_Philippines). On this day, labour organizations and unions hold protests in major cities. On 1 May 1903, during the [American colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Colonial_Period_of_the_Philippines) the *[Unión Obrera Democrática Filipina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_Obrera_Democr%C3%A1tica_Filipina)* (Filipino Democratic Labor Union) held a rally in front of the [Malacañang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaca%C3%B1ang_Palace) demanding workers' economic rights and Philippine independence. In 1908, the [Philippine Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Assembly) passed a bill officially recognizing 1 May as a national holiday. In 1913, the first official celebration was held on 1 May 1913 when 36 labour unions convened for a congress in [Manila](184334.md).[^cite_note-258]

During the [Presidency of Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Gloria_Macapagal-Arroyo), a policy was adopted called [holiday economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Philippines#%22Holiday_Economics%22) policy that moved holidays to either a Monday or a Friday to create a [long weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_weekend) of three days. In 2002, Labor Day was moved to the Monday nearest to 1 May. Labour groups protested, as they accused the Arroyo administration of belittling the holiday.[^cite_note-259] By 2008, Labor Day was excluded in the holiday economics policy, returning the commemorations to 1 May, no matter what day of the week it falls on.[^cite_note-GMGApr2008-1]

#### Singapore

In Singapore, it is known as Labour Day and is a public holiday.[^cite_note-260] The [Prime Minister](350647.md), who is the leader of the ruling [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party), gives a Labour Day Rally championing the [tripartite relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartism) between government, employers, and employees.

#### Thailand

In Thailand, the day is known in English as National Labour Day, and is one of 17 official [public holidays in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Thailand).[^cite_note-261]

#### Vietnam

In Vietnam, it is known as International Labour Day ([Vietnamese](32511.md): *Ngày Quốc tế Lao động*) and is a public holiday.[^cite_note-Ngày-Quốc-tế-Lao-động-262] It was first adopted by the [Nguyễn dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_dynasty) on the 11th day of the 9th month of the 16th year of the [Bảo Đại](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%A3o_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i) Emperor (30 October 1941) by imperial decree.[^cite_note-Ngày-Quốc-tế-Lao-động-262] Later on 29 April 1946 President [Hồ Chí Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%93_Ch%C3%AD_Minh) issued *Sắc lệnh số 56* (Decree No. 56) which adopted the holiday for the [Democratic Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam).[^cite_note-Ngày-Quốc-tế-Lao-động-262]

According to the decree "workers in public offices, private offices and factories throughout the country are entitled to a day off from work. International Labour 1.5 and still receive the same salary as a working day...".[^cite_note-Ngày-Quốc-tế-Lao-động-262] On 1 May 1946 the first International Labour Day of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam was held.[^cite_note-Ngày-Quốc-tế-Lao-động-262]

#### Israel



Labour Day march in Israel, 1 May 2007

After historically varying popularity of Labour Day, 1 May is not an official holiday in the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel). During the 1990s businesses began to treat it like a regular working day as the number of Labour Day-related activities decreased.[^cite_note-266]
