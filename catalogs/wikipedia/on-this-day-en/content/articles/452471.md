**Cox's Bazar District** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): কক্সবাজার জেলা) is a district in the [Chittagong Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Division) of [Bangladesh](3454.md).[^cite_note-Banglapedia-3] It is named after the town of [Cox's Bazar](2271204.md). It is located 150 kilometres (93 mi) south of [Chittagong](380451.md). Cox's Bazar is also known by the names *Panowa* ("yellow flower") and *Palongkee*. The modern Cox's Bazar derives its name from Captain [Hiram Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Cox) (died 1799), an army officer who served in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_raj). It is one of the fishing ports of Bangladesh, and contains one of the world's longest natural sea beaches (120 kilometres (75 mi) long including [mud flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_flats)).

In 1984 during the [Ershad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Muhammad_Ershad), Cox's Bazar district was established by dividing [Chittagong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_District). Today, Cox's Bazar is a major tourist destination within Bangladesh.

## Geography

Cox's Bazar District has an area of 2,491.86 km<sup>2</sup> (962.11 sq mi). It is bounded by [Chittagong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_District) on the north, [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) in the south, [Bandarban District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandarban_District) on the east, and the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) on the west. Major rivers include [Matamuhuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matamuhuri), Bakkhali, [Reju Khal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reju_Khal?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Naf River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naf_River), [Maheshkhali channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maheshkhali_channel?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Kutubdia channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutubdia_channel?action=edit\&redlink=1). The area of the city of Cox's Bazar is 6.85 km<sup>2</sup> (2.64 sq mi).

## History

The known history of Cox's Bazar begins in the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) period. On his way to [Arakan](493373.md), when the Mughal Prince [Shah Shuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Shuja_\(Mughal\)) (1616–1660) passed through the hilly terrain of the present day Cox's Bazar, he was attracted to the scenic nature of the region. He commanded his forces to camp there. A place named *[Dulahazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulahazara?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, meaning "one thousand palanquins", still exists in the area.

Cox's Bazar is named after [Captain Hiram Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Cox), an officer of the East India Company, who was assigned with the charges of the current day Cox's Bazar and its adjacent areas.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The town of Cox's Bazar was established in 1799 as a market town to honour Captain Cox. In 1854, Cox's Bazar was made a Sub Divisional headquarters of [Chittagong district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_District) under the Bengal Presidency of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India).

After the end of British rule in 1947, Cox's Bazar remained a part of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) under [Pakistan](23235.md) until 1971. [Captain Advocate Fazlul Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Advocate_Fazlul_Karim) was the first chairman of Cox's Bazar municipality following independence from the British. He established the [Tamarisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarisk) Forest along the beach to draw tourism to the town and to protect the beach from the tide. He donated many of his father-in-law's and his own lands to establish a public library and town hall. In 1971, the wharf was used as a naval port by the [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md)'s gunboats. This and the nearby airstrip of the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) were the scene of intense shelling by the [Indian Navy](604522.md) during the [Bangladesh War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_War_of_Independence) in 1971.

In the year 1984, Cox's Bazar was upgraded into a [District](37210460.md) from a Sub Division under the Chittagong Division.

Starting in 2017, a "mass human exodus" of the [Rohingya](1918632.md) Muslim minority group from neighboring [Myanmar](19457.md)'s [Rakhine State](493373.md) has led to Cox's Bazar housing the "world's largest refugee settlement" over the following years. In the first year, the [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR) estimated that 725,000 refugees had sought safety in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-6]

The annual average temperature in Cox's Bazar is 32.8 °C (91.0 °F) and a minimum of 16.1 °C (61.0 °F). The climate remains hot and humid with some seasons of temperate weather. The average amount of rainfall is 4,285 mm (168.7 in).

According to the [2022 Census of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Census_of_Bangladesh), Cox's Bazar District had 587,114 households and a population of 2,823,268 with an average of 4.75 people per household. Among the population, 671,286 (23.78%) of the inhabitants were under 10 years of age. The population density was 1,133 people per km<sup>2</sup>. Cox's Bazar District had a literacy rate (age 7 and over) of 71.58%, compared to the national average of 74.80%, and a sex ratio of 103.32 males per 100 females. Approximately 43.62% (1,231,639) of the population lived in urban areas. The ethnic population was 14,881 (0.64%), mainly [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) with some [Chakma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people) and [Tanchangya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanchangya_people).[^cite_note-2022census-8]

Muslims are the largest religious community. Prior to Partition, the southern upazilas of Ukhiya and Teknaf had a large ethnic Buddhist [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) population, but most have moved to [the Rakhine state](493373.md) in Myanmar over the years. The local dialect is [Chittagonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagonian_language) as well as presence of many [Rohingya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_language) speakers due to the massive refugee camps.

## Economy

The most significant livelihood of Cox's Bazar district is tourism. Millions of foreign and Bangladeshi natives visit this coastal city every year. A number of hotels, guest houses, and motels have been built in the city and coastal region and the hospitality industry is a major employer in the area.

A number of people are involved in the fishing and collection of seafoods, sea products and salt-farming. Oysters, snails, pearls and jewelry made from shells are popular with the tourists in the seaside and city stores. A number of people are also employed by the transportation business for tourists. Additionally, many people of the district are farmers.

In 2002, a surfing club was initiated at Cox's Bazar by a local Bangladeshi. It has now extended to holding an annual competition including locals and foreign tourists. This is the first surfing initiative in Bangladesh's history.[^cite_note-13]
