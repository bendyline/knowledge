**Aargau** ([/ˈɑːrɡaʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AR-gow*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Swiss Standard German: [\[ˈaːrɡaʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cb/De-Aargau2.ogg/De-Aargau2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cb/De-Aargau2.ogg/De-Aargau2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), more formally[^cite_note-4] the **Canton of Aargau** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Kanton Aargau*; [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *Chantun Argovia*; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Canton d'Argovie*; [Italian](14708.md): *Canton Argovia*), is one of the [26 cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Switzerland) forming the [Swiss Confederation](26748.md). It is composed of eleven districts and its capital is [Aarau](2466.md).

Aargau is one of the most northerly cantons of Switzerland, and contains the lower course of the [Aare](1433.md) River from which the name *Aar-[gau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gau_\(country_subdivision\))* ("Aare province") is drawn. It is one of the most densely populated regions of Switzerland.[^cite_note-Colliers-5]

## History

Main article: [History of the Canton of Aargau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Canton_of_Aargau)

### Early history

Further information: [Early history of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_history_of_Switzerland)

The area of Aargau and the surrounding areas were controlled by the [Helvetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetians), a tribe of [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts), as far back as 200 BC.[^cite_note-oR-6] It was eventually occupied by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and then by the 6th century, the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks).[^cite_note-Cohen-7] The Romans built a major settlement called [Vindonissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindonissa), near the present location of [Brugg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brugg).[^cite_note-oR-6]

### Medieval Aargau

See also: [List of castles and fortresses in Switzerland § Aargau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_castles_and_fortresses_in_Switzerland#Aargau), and [Medieval Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Switzerland)



Aarau

The reconstructed Old High German name of Aargau is *Argowe*, first unambiguously attested (in the spelling *Argue*) in 795. The term described a territory only loosely equivalent to that of the modern canton, including the region between Aare and [Reuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuss_\(river\)) rivers, including [Pilatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_\(mountain\)) and [Napf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napf), i.e. including parts of the modern cantons of Bern ([Bernese Aargau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Aargau), [Emmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmental), parts of the [Bernese Oberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Oberland)), [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn), [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft), [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lucerne), [Obwalden](317636.md) and [Nidwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidwalden), but not the parts of the modern canton east of the Reuss ([Baden District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden_District,_Aargau)), which were part of [Zürichgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrichgau).

Within the [Frankish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankish_Empire) (8th to 10th centuries), the area was a disputed border region between the duchies of [Alamannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamannia) and [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy). A line of the von [Wetterau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetterau) ([Conradines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conradines)) intermittently held the countship of Aargau from 750 until about 1030, when they lost it (having in the meantime taken the name [von](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von) [Tegerfelden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegerfelden)). This division became the ill-defined (and sparsely settled) outer border of the early [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) at its formation in the second half of the 10th century. Most of the region came under the control of the ducal [house of Zähringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Z%C3%A4hringen) and the comital houses of [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) and [Kyburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Kyburg) by about 1200.

In the second half of the 13th century, the territory became divided between the territories claimed by the [imperial cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsfrei) of [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucerne) and [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solothurn) and the Swiss canton of [Unterwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterwalden). The remaining portion, largely corresponding to the modern canton of Aargau, remained under the control of the Habsburgs until the "conquest of Aargau" by the [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) in 1415.[^cite_note-8] [Habsburg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Castle) itself, the original seat of the House of Habsburg, was taken by Bern in April 1415.[^cite_note-9] The Habsburgs had founded a number of monasteries (with some structures enduring, e.g., in [Wettingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wettingen_Abbey) and [Muri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muri_Abbey)), the closing of which by the government in 1841 was a contributing factor to the outbreak of the Swiss civil war – the "[Sonderbund War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonderbund_War)" – in 1847.

### Under the Swiss Confederation



Switzerland in 1416, showing the part of the Aargau under Bernese control, the county of Baden and the Freie Ämter.

When [Frederick IV of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_IV,_Duke_of_Austria) sided with [Antipope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipope_John_XXIII) at the [Council of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Constance), [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) [Sigismund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) placed him under the [Imperial ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_ban).[^cite_note-11] In July 1414, the Pope visited [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) and received assurances from the that they would move against the Habsburgs.[^cite_note-Luck-10] A few months later the [Swiss Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Confederation) denounced the [Treaty of 1412](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1412?action=edit\&redlink=1). Shortly thereafter in 1415, Bern and the rest of the Swiss Confederation used the ban as a pretext to invade the Aargau. The Confederation was able to quickly conquer the towns of Aarau, Lenzburg, Brugg and Zofingen along with most of the Habsburg castles. Bern kept the southwest portion ([Zofingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zofingen), [Aarburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarburg), [Aarau](2466.md), [Lenzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenzburg), and [Brugg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brugg)), northward to the confluence of the [Aare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aare_\(river\)) and [Reuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuss_\(river\)).[^cite_note-Luck-10] The important city of [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden,_Switzerland) was taken by a united Swiss army and governed by all 8 members of the Confederation.[^cite_note-Luck-10] Some districts, named the *[Freie Ämter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freie_%C3%84mter)* (*free bailiwicks*) – [Mellingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellingen), [Muri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muri_District), [Villmergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villmergen), and [Bremgarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremgarten_\(district\)), with the [countship of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Baden) – were governed as "subject lands" by all or some of the Confederates. Shortly after the conquest of the Aargau by the Swiss, Frederick humbled himself to the Pope. The Pope reconciled with him and ordered all of the taken lands to be returned. The Swiss refused and years later after no serious attempts at re-acquisition, the Duke officially relinquished rights to the Swiss.[^cite_note-Luck1-12]

### Unteraargau or Berner Aargau



Districts in Bernese controlled Unteraargau

Bern's portion of the Aargau came to be known as the [Unteraargau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unteraargau), also referred to as the Berner or Bernese Aargau. In 1514 Bern expanded north into the [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura) and so came into possession of several strategically important mountain passes into the Austrian [Fricktal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fricktal). This land was added to the Unteraargau and was directly ruled from Bern. It was divided into seven rural [bailiwicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailiwick) and four administrative cities, Aarau, Zofingen, Lenzburg and Brugg. While the Habsburgs were driven out, many of their minor nobles were allowed to keep their lands and offices, though over time they lost power to the Bernese government. The bailiwick administration was based on a very small staff of officials, mostly made up of Bernese citizens, but with a few locals.[^cite_note-HDS_BA-13]

When Bern converted during the Protestant [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) in 1528, the Unteraargau also converted. At the beginning of the 16th century a number of [Anabaptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabaptists) migrated into the upper Wynen and Rueder valleys from [Zurich](40334603.md). Despite pressure from the Bernese authorities in the 16th and 17th centuries, Anabaptism never entirely disappeared from the Unteraargau.[^cite_note-HDS_BA-13]

Bern used the Aargau bailiwicks mostly as a source of grain for the rest of the city-state. The administrative cities remained economically only of regional importance. However, in the 17th and 18th centuries Bern encouraged industrial development in Unteraargau and by the late 18th century it was the most industrialized region in the city-state. The high industrialization led to high population growth in the 18th century, for example between 1764 and 1798, the population grew by 35%, far more than in other parts of the canton. In 1870 the proportion of farmers in Aarau, Lenzburg, Kulm, and Zofingen districts was 34–40%, while in the other districts it was 46–57%.[^cite_note-HDS_BA-13]

### Freie Ämter



Map of the Freie Ämter, including the 1712 line dividing the Upper and Lower Freie Ämter

The rest of the [Freie Ämter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freie_%C3%84mter) were collectively administered as subject territories by the rest of the Confederation. Muri *Amt* was assigned to Zürich, Lucerne, [Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz), [Unterwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterwalden), [Zug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zug) and [Glarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Glarus), while the *Ämter* of Meienberg, Richensee and [Villmergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villmergen) were first given to Lucerne alone. The final boundary was set in 1425 by an arbitration tribunal and Lucerne had to give the three *Ämter* to be collectively ruled.[^cite_note-HDS_FA-14] The four *Ämter* were then consolidated under a single Confederation [bailiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailiff) into what was known in the 15th century as the *Waggental* Bailiwick (German: *[Vogtei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogt) im Waggental*). In the 16th century, it came to be known as the *Vogtei der Freien Ämter*. While the *Freien Ämter* often had independent lower courts, they were forced to accept the Confederation's sovereignty. Finally, in 1532, the canton of Uri became part of the collective administration of the Freien Ämter.[^cite_note-15]

At the time of the Protestant Reformation, the majority of the Ämter converted to the new faith. In 1529, a wave of [iconoclasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconoclasm) swept through the area and wiped away much of the old religion. After the defeat of Zürich in the second [Battle of Kappel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kappel) in 1531, the victorious five Catholic cantons marched their troops into the Freie Ämter and reconverted them to Catholicism.[^cite_note-HDS_FA-14]

In the [First War of Villmergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_War_of_Villmergen), in 1656, and the [Toggenburg War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toggenburg_War) (or Second War of Villmergen), in 1712, the Freie Ämter became the staging ground for the warring Reformed and Catholic armies. While the peace after the 1656 war did not change the status quo, the fourth Peace of Aarau in 1712 brought about a reorganization of power relations. The victory gave Zürich the opportunity to force the Catholic cantons out of the government in the [county of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Baden) and the adjacent area of the Freie Ämter. The Freie Ämter were then divided in two by a line drawn from the gallows in [Fahrwangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrwangen) to the [Oberlunkhofen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlunkhofen) church steeple. The northern part, the so-called Unteren Freie Ämter (lower Freie Ämter), which included the districts of Boswil (in part) and Hermetschwil and the Niederamt, were ruled by Zürich, Bern and Glarus. The southern part, the Oberen Freie Ämter (upper Freie Ämter), were ruled by the previous seven cantons but Bern was added to make an eighth.[^cite_note-HDS_FA-14]

During the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic) (1798–1803), the county of Baden, the Freie Ämter and the area known as the [Kelleramt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelleramt?action=edit\&redlink=1) were combined into the [canton of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Baden).

### County of Baden



County of Baden

The [County of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Baden) was a shared [condominium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(international_law\)) of the entire [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy). After the Confederacy conquest in 1415, they retained much of the Habsburg legal structure, which caused a number of problems. The local nobility had the right to hold the [low court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High,_middle_and_low_justice) in only about one fifth of the territory. There were over 30 different nobles who had the right to hold courts scattered around the surrounding lands. All these overlapping jurisdictions caused numerous conflicts, but gradually the Confederation was able to acquire these rights in the county. The cities of Baden, [Bremgarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremgarten,_Aargau) and [Mellingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellingen) became the administrative centers and held the high courts. Together with the courts, the three administrative centers had considerable local autonomy, but were ruled by a governor who was appointed by the *Acht Orte* every two years. After the [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformed_Church) victory at the [Second Battle of Villmergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toggenburg_War), the administration of the County changed slightly. Instead of the *Acht Orte* appointing a bailiff together, Zürich and Bern each appointed the governor for 7 out of 16 years while [Glarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Glarus) appointed him for the remaining two years.[^cite_note-HDS_CB-16]

The chaotic legal structure and fragmented land ownership combined with a tradition of dividing the land among all the heirs in an inheritance prevented any large scale reforms. The governor tried in the 18th century to reform and standardize laws and ownership across the county, but with limited success. With an ever-changing administration, the County lacked a coherent long-term economic policy or support for reforms. By the end of the 18th century there were no factories or mills and only a few small [cottage industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_industry#Cottage_industry) along the border with Zürich. Road construction first became a priority after 1750, when Zürich and Bern began appointing a governor for seven years.[^cite_note-HDS_CB-16]

During the Protestant Reformation, some of the municipalities converted to the new faith. However, starting in 1531, some of the old parishes were [converted back to the old faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation). The governors were appointed from both Catholic and Protestant cantons and since they changed every two years, neither faith gained a majority in the county.[^cite_note-HDS_CB-16]

After the French invasion, on 19 March 1798, the governments of Zürich and Bern agreed to the creation of the short lived [canton of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Baden) in the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic). With the [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Mediation) in 1803, the canton of Baden was dissolved. Portions of the lands of the former County of Baden now became the District of Baden in the newly created canton of Aargau. After World War II, this formerly agrarian region saw striking growth and became the district with the largest and densest population in the canton (110,000 in 1990, 715 persons per km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-HDS_CB-16]

### Forming the canton of Aargau



Helvetic Republic c. 1798/99, with the newly created cantons of Aargau and Baden



Swiss Confederation following the 1803 Act of Mediation, with the modern canton of Aargau formed

The contemporary canton of Aargau was formed in 1803, a canton of the [Swiss Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Confederation_\(Napoleonic\)) as a result of the [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Mediation). It was a combination of three short-lived cantons of the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic): Aargau (1798–1803), Baden (1798–1803) and Fricktal (1802–1803). Its creation is therefore rooted in the [Napoleonic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_in_the_Napoleonic_era). In the year 2003, the canton of Aargau celebrated its 200th anniversary.

French forces occupied the Aargau from 10 March to 18 April 1798; thereafter the Bernese portion became the canton of Aargau and the remainder formed the [canton of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Baden). Aborted plans to merge the two halves came in 1801 and 1802, and they were eventually united under the name Aargau,[^cite_note-Colliers-5][^cite_note-17] which was then admitted as a full member of the reconstituted Confederation following the Act of Mediation. Some parts of the canton of Baden at this point were transferred to other cantons: the *Amt* of [Hitzkirch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitzkirch) to Lucerne, whilst [Hüttikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%BCttikon), [Oetwil an der Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oetwil_an_der_Limmat), [Dietikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietikon) and [Schlieren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlieren,_Z%C3%BCrich) went to Zürich. In return, Lucerne's *Amt* of [Merenschwand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merenschwand) was transferred to Aargau (district of Muri).

The [Fricktal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fricktal), ceded in 1802 by [Austria](26964606.md) via Napoleonic France to the Helvetic Republic, was briefly a separate canton of the Helvetic Republic (the [canton of Fricktal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Fricktal)) under a *[Statthalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statthalter)* ('Lieutenant'), but on 19 March 1803 (following the Act of Mediation) was incorporated into the canton of Aargau.

The former cantons of Baden and Fricktal can still be identified with the contemporary [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Switzerland#Aargau) – the [canton of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Baden) is covered by the districts of Zurzach, Baden, Bremgarten, and Muri (albeit with the gains and losses of 1803 detailed above); the [canton of Fricktal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Fricktal) by the districts of Rheinfelden and Laufenburg (except for [Hottwil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hottwil) which was transferred to that district in 2010).

### Jewish history in Aargau



Two separate doors (one for Jews and one for Christians) on a house in Lengnau



18th century etching of the synagogue in Lengnau. In the [Jewish Museum of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_of_Switzerland)'s collection.&#x20;

In the 17th century, Aargau was the only federal condominium where Jews were tolerated. In 1774, they were restricted to just two towns, [Endingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endingen,_Switzerland) and [Lengnau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lengnau,_Aargau). While the rural upper class pressed incessantly for the expulsion of the Jews, the financial interests of the authorities prevented it. They imposed special taxes on peddling and cattle trading, the primary Jewish professions. The Protestant occupiers also enjoyed the discomfort of the local Catholics by the presence of the Jewish community.[^cite_note-E+L-18] The Jews were directly subordinate to the governor; from 1696, they were compelled to renew a letter of protection from him every 16 years.[^cite_note-HDS_CB-16]

During this period, Jews and Christians were not allowed to live under the same roof, neither were Jews allowed to own land or houses. They were taxed at a much higher rate than others and, in 1712, the Lengnau community was "pillaged."[^cite_note-is-19] In 1760, they were further restricted regarding marriages and procreation. An exorbitant tax was levied on marriage licenses; oftentimes, they were outright refused.[^cite_note-E+L-18] This remained the case until the 19th century. In 1799, the [Helvetic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_republic) abolished all special tolls, and, in 1802, removed the poll tax.[^cite_note-is-19] On 5 May 1809, they were declared citizens and given broad rights regarding trade and farming. They were still restricted to Endingen and Lengnau until 7 May 1846, when their right to move and reside freely within the canton of Aargau was granted. On 24 September 1856, the [Swiss Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Council) granted them full political rights within Aargau, as well as broad business rights; however the majority Christian population did not fully abide by these new liberal laws. The time of 1860 saw the canton government voting to grant suffrage in all local rights and to give their communities autonomy. Before the law was enacted, it was however repealed due to vocal opposition led by the [Ultramonte Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramontanism).[^cite_note-is-19]

In July 1863, the federal authorities granted all Jews full rights of citizens. However, they did not receive all of the rights in Endingen and Lengnau until a resolution of the [Grand Council](27902141.md), on 15 May 1877, granted citizens' rights to the members of the Jewish communities of those places, giving them charters under the names of New Endingen and New Lengnau.[^cite_note-is-19] The *Swiss Jewish Kulturverein* was instrumental in this fight from its founding in 1862 until it was dissolved 20 years later.[^cite_note-is-19] During this period of diminished rights, they were not even allowed to bury their dead in Swiss soil and had to bury their dead on an island called *[Judenäule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juden%C3%A4ule)* (Jews' Isle) on the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) near [Waldshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldshut-Tiengen).[^cite_note-is-19] Beginning in 1603, the deceased Jews of the [Surbtal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surbtal) communities were buried on the river island which was leased by the Jewish community. In 1750, as the island was repeatedly flooded and devastated, the [Surbtal Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surbtal_Jews) asked the *[Tagsatzung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagsatzung)* to establish the [Endingen cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCdischer_Friedhof_Endingen) in the vicinity of their communities.[^cite_note-hds-judenäule-20][^cite_note-judaica-21]

## Geography



View of the [Lägern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A4gern) from [Bözberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6zberg)

The capital of the canton is [Aarau](2466.md), which is located on its western border, on the [Aare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aare_\(river\)). The canton borders [Germany](11867.md) ([Baden-Württemberg](66401.md)) to the north, the Rhine forming the border. To the west lie the Swiss cantons of [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft), [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn) and [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern); the [canton of Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lucerne) lies south, and [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Z%C3%BCrich) and [Zug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zug) to the east. Its total area is 1,404 square kilometers (542 sq mi). Besides the Rhine, it contains two large rivers, the Aare and the [Reuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuss_\(river\)).[^cite_note-Cohen-7]

The canton of Aargau is one of the least mountainous Swiss cantons, forming part of the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau), to the north of the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alps) and the east of the [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_mountains), above which rises low hills. Slightly over one-third of the canton is wooded (518 square kilometers (200 sq mi)), while nearly half is used from farming (635.7 square kilometers (245.4 sq mi)). 33.5 square kilometers (12.9 sq mi) or about 2.4% of the canton is considered unproductive, mostly lakes (notably [Lake Hallwil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hallwil)) and streams. With a population density of 450/km<sup>2</sup> (1,200/sq mi), the canton has a relatively high amount of land used for human development, with 216.7 square kilometers (83.7 sq mi) or about 15% of the canton developed for housing or transportation.[^cite_note-22]

It contains the hot sulphur springs of [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden,_Switzerland) and [Schinznach-Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schinznach-Bad), while at [Rheinfelden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinfelden_\(Aargau\)) there are very extensive saline springs. Just below [Brugg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brugg) the [Reuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuss_\(river\)) and the [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat) join the Aar, while around Brugg are the ruined castle of [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg), the old convent of [Königsfelden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigsfelden_Abbey) (with fine painted medieval glass) and the remains of the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) settlement of [Vindonissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindonissa) ([Windisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windisch,_Switzerland)).

[Fahr Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahr_Monastery) forms a small [exclave](4848945.md) of the canton, otherwise surrounded by the [canton of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Z%C3%BCrich), and since 2008 is part of the Aargau municipality of [Würenlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrenlos).

## Demographics

Aargau has a population (as of December 2020) of 694,072.[^cite_note-Stat2020_AG-2] As of 2010, 21.5% of the population are resident foreign nationals. Over the last 10 years (2000–2010) the population has changed at a rate of 11%. Migration accounted for 8.7%, while births and deaths accounted for 2.8%.[^cite_note-SFSO-25] Most of the population (as of 2000) speaks German (477,093 or 87.1%) as their first language, Italian is the second most common (17,847 or 3.3%) and [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian) is the third (10,645 or 1.9%). There are 4,151 people who speak French and 618 people who speak [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language).[^cite_note-STAT2000-26]

Of the population in the canton, 146,421 or about 26.7% were born in Aargau and lived there in 2000. There were 140,768 or 25.7% who were born in the same canton, while 136,865 or 25.0% were born somewhere else in Switzerland, and 107,396 or 19.6% were born outside of Switzerland.[^cite_note-STAT2000-26]

As of 2000, children and teenagers (0–19 years old) make up 24.3% of the population, while adults (20–64 years old) make up 62.3% and seniors (over 64 years old) make up 13.4%.[^cite_note-SFSO-25]

As of 2000, there were 227,656 people who were single and never married in the canton. There were 264,939 married individuals, 27,603 widows or widowers and 27,295 individuals who are divorced.[^cite_note-STAT2000-26]

As of 2000, there were 224,128 private households in the canton, and an average of 2.4 persons per household.[^cite_note-SFSO-25] There were 69,062 households that consist of only one person and 16,254 households with five or more people. As of 2009, the construction rate of new housing units was 6.5 new units per 1000 residents.[^cite_note-SFSO-25] The vacancy rate for the canton, in 2010, was 1.54%.[^cite_note-SFSO-25]

The majority of the population is centered on one of three areas: the [Aare Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aare_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1), the side branches of the Aare Valley, or along the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_\(river\)).[^cite_note-Colliers-5]

## Politics

In the 2011 [federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Swiss_federal_election), the most popular party was the [SVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) which received 34.7% of the vote. The next three most popular parties were the [SP/PS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland) (18.0%), the [FDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDP.The_Liberals) (11.5%) and the [CVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (10.6%).[^cite_note-30]

The SVP received about the same percentage of the vote as they did in the [2007 Federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Swiss_federal_election) (36.2% in 2007 vs 34.7% in 2011). The SPS retained about the same popularity (17.9% in 2007), the FDP retained about the same popularity (13.6% in 2007) and the CVP retained about the same popularity (13.5% in 2007).[^cite_note-31]

### Cantonal politics

The Grand Council of the [canton](180752.md) of Aargau is called [Grosser Rat](27902141.md). It is the [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of the canton, has 140 seats, with members elected every four years.

## Religion



Catholic City Church in Baden, Aargau

From the 2000 census, 219,800 or 40.1% were Roman Catholic, while 189,606 or 34.6% belonged to the [Swiss Reformed Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformed_Church). Of the rest of the population, there were 11,523 members of an [Orthodox](10186.md) church (or about 2.10% of the population), there were 3,418 individuals (or about 0.62% of the population) who belonged to the [Christian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Catholic_Church_of_Switzerland), and there were 29,580 individuals (or about 5.40% of the population) who belonged to another Christian church. There were 342 individuals (or about 0.06% of the population) who were [Jewish](25955086.md), and 30,072 (or about 5.49% of the population) who were Muslim. There were 1,463 individuals who were Buddhist, 2,089 individuals who were Hindu and 495 individuals who belonged to another church. 57,573 (or about 10.52% of the population) belonged to no church, are agnostic or [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and 15,875 individuals (or about 2.90% of the population) did not answer the question.[^cite_note-STAT2000-26]

## Education

In Aargau about 212,069 or (38.7%) of the population have completed non-mandatory [upper secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Switzerland#Secondary), and 70,896 or (12.9%) have completed additional higher education (either [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Switzerland) or a *[Fachhochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule)*). Of the 70,896 who completed tertiary schooling, 63.6% were Swiss men, 20.9% were Swiss women, 10.4% were non-Swiss men and 5.2% were non-Swiss women.[^cite_note-STAT2000-26]

## Economy



[Leibstadt Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibstadt_Nuclear_Power_Plant)

As of  2010, Aargau had an unemployment rate of 3.6%. As of 2008, there were 11,436 people employed in the [primary economic sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy) and about 3,927 businesses involved in this sector. 95,844 people were employed in the [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) and there were 6,055 businesses in this sector. 177,782 people were employed in the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy), with 21,530 businesses in this sector.[^cite_note-SFSO-25]

In 2008 the total number of [full-time equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-time_equivalent) jobs was 238,225. The number of jobs in the primary sector was 7,167, of which 6,731 were in agriculture, 418 were in forestry or lumber production and 18 were in fishing or fisheries. The number of jobs in the secondary sector was 90,274 of which 64,089 or (71.0%) were in manufacturing, 366 or (0.4%) were in mining and 21,705 (24.0%) were in construction. The number of jobs in the tertiary sector was 140,784. In the tertiary sector; 38,793 or 27.6% were in the sale or repair of motor vehicles, 13,624 or 9.7% were in the movement and storage of goods, 8,150 or 5.8% were in a hotel or restaurant, 5,164 or 3.7% were in the information industry, 5,946 or 4.2% were the insurance or financial industry, 14,831 or 10.5% were technical professionals or scientists, 10,951 or 7.8% were in education and 21,952 or 15.6% were in health care.[^cite_note-33]

Of the working population, 19.5% used public transportation to get to work, and 55.3% used a private car.[^cite_note-SFSO-25] Public transportation – bus and train – is provided by [Busbetrieb Aarau AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbetrieb_Aarau_AG).

The farmland of the canton of Aargau is some of the most fertile in Switzerland. [Dairy farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming), cereal and fruit farming are among the canton's main economic activities.[^cite_note-Cohen-7] The canton is also industrially developed, particularly in the fields of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), precision instruments, iron, steel, cement and [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles).[^cite_note-Cohen-7]

Three of Switzerland's five nuclear power plants are in the canton of Aargau (Beznau I + II and Leibstadt). Additionally, the many rivers supply enough water for numerous hydroelectric power plants throughout the canton. The canton of Aargau is often called "the energy canton".

A significant number of people commute into the financial center of the city of [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), which is just across the cantonal border. As such the per capita cantonal income (in 2005) is 49,209 [CHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Franc).[^cite_note-34]

Tourism is significant, particularly for the hot springs at [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden,_Switzerland) and [Schinznach-Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schinznach-Bad), the ancient castles, the landscape, and the many old museums in the canton.[^cite_note-eb-35] Hillwalking is another tourist attraction but is of only limited significance.
