The **University of Iowa** (**U of I**, **UIowa**, or **Iowa**[^cite_note-University_of_Iowa-7]) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) in [Iowa City, Iowa](113933.md), United States. Founded in 1847, it is the oldest and largest university in the state. The University of Iowa is organized into 12 colleges offering more than 200 areas of study and 7 professional degrees.[^cite_note-University_of_Iowa-7]

On an urban 1,880-acre campus on the banks of the [Iowa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_River), the University of Iowa is [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education) among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity".[^cite_note-8] In fiscal year 2021, research expenditures at Iowa totaled \$818 million.[^cite_note-9] The university was the original developer of the [Master of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts) degree, and it operates the [Iowa Writers' Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Writers'_Workshop), whose alumni include 17 of the university's 46 [Pulitzer Prize](24230.md) winners.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Iowa is a member of the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities) and the [Universities Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_Research_Association).

Among public universities in the United States, UI was the first to become [coeducational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-sex_education) and host a department of religious studies; it also opened the first coeducational [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carver_College_of_Medicine).[^cite_note-12] The University of Iowa's 32,000 students take part in nearly 500 student organizations.[^cite_note-13] Iowa's 22 varsity athletic teams, the [Iowa Hawkeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes), compete in [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_\(NCAA\)) of the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA) and are members of the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference). The [University of Iowa alumni network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_University_of_Iowa_alumni) exceeds 250,000 graduates.

## History

### Founding and early history



[Iowa Old Capitol Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Old_Capitol_Building), Iowa City, 1855

The University of Iowa was founded on February 25, 1847, just 59 days after Iowa was admitted to the [Union](3434750.md). The Constitution of the State of Iowa refers to a State University to be established in Iowa City "without branches at any other place."[^cite_note-14] The legal name of the university is the **State University of Iowa** (frequently abbreviated as **SUI**), but the [Board of Regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Board_of_Regents) approved using "The University of Iowa" for everyday usage in October 1964.[^cite_note-15]

The first faculty offered instruction at the university beginning in March 1855 to students in the Old Mechanics Building, located where the student recreational area east of Van Allen Hall is now.[^cite_note-16] In September 1855, there were 124 students, of whom forty-one were women. The 1856–57 catalog listed nine departments offering [ancient languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_language), modern languages, intellectual [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_philosophy), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History), [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history), [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics), [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy), and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry). The first president of the university was [Amos Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Dean).

The original campus consisted of the [Iowa Old Capitol Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Old_Capitol_Building) and the 10 acres (40,000 m<sup>2</sup>) (4.05 hectares) of land on which it stood. Following the placing of the cornerstone July 4, 1840, the building housed the Fifth Legislative Assembly of the [Territory of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Iowa) (December 5, 1842) and then became the first capitol building of the State of Iowa on December 28, 1846. Until that date, it had been the third capitol of the Territory of Iowa. When the capital of Iowa was moved to [Des Moines](9163.md) in 1857, the Old Capitol became the first permanent "home" of the university.

In 1855, the university became the first public university in the United States to [admit men and women on an equal basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mixed-sex_colleges_and_universities_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-Roch-17] In addition, Iowa was the world's first university to accept creative work in theater, writing, music, and art on an equal basis with academic research.[^cite_note-University_of_Iowa_Firsts-18]

The university was one of the first institutions in America to grant a law degree to a woman ([Mary B. Hickey Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_B._Hickey_Wilkinson?action=edit\&redlink=1), 1873), to grant a law degree to an African American ([Alexander G. Clark, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Clark) in 1879), and to put an African American on a varsity athletic squad ([Frank Holbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Holbrook) in 1895). The university awarded its first [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1898.[^cite_note-University_of_Iowa_Firsts-18]

### 20th century–present



Schaeffer Hall, location of the [College of Liberal Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_College_of_Liberal_Arts_and_Sciences)



Old Capitol Museum

The University of Iowa established the first law school and dental school[^cite_note-19] west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River). It was the first university to use radio (later television, too) in education, in 1932, and it pioneered in the field of [standardized testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardized_testing).[^cite_note-20] Also, the University of Iowa was the first Big Ten institution to promote an African American to the position of administrative vice president ([Phillip Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Hubbard?action=edit\&redlink=1), promoted in 1966).

Under the leadership of Carl Seashore in 1922, Iowa became the first university in the United States to accept creative projects as theses for advanced degrees. Traditionally, graduate study culminates in the writing of a scholarly thesis, but Iowa accepted creative works including a collection of poems, a musical composition, or a series of paintings to be presented to the graduate college in support of a degree. In so doing, Iowa established a creative standard in qualifying for the Master of Fine Arts degree and secured a place for writers and artists in the academy. The university's Program in Creative Writing, known worldwide as the [Iowa Writers' Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Writers'_Workshop), was founded in 1936 with the gathering together of writers of both poetry and fiction. It was the first creative writing program in the country, and it became the prototype for more than 300 writing programs, many of which were founded by Workshop alumni. The workshop remains the most prestigious creative writing program in the country and one of the most selective graduate programs of any kind, typically admitting fewer than five percent of its applicants.

The [U.S. Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission) conducted [several experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unethical_human_experimentation_in_the_United_States) examining the effects of [iodine-131](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine-131) administration in pregnant women (scheduled for abortions) and infants in 1953 and 1963, respectively.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

The university was the first state university to recognize the Gay, Lesbian, Bisexual, Transgender, and Allied Union (in 1970).[^cite_note-24]

[A shooting took place on campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_shooting) on November 1, 1991. Six people died in the shooting, including the perpetrator, and one other person was wounded. This was one of the deadliest university campus shootings in United States history.

In the summer of 2008, flood waters breached the Coralville Reservoir spillway, damaging more than 20 major campus buildings.[^cite_note-25] Several weeks after the floodwaters receded, university officials placed a preliminary estimate on flood damage at \$231.75 million. Later, the university estimated that repairs would cost about \$743 million.[^cite_note-26] The reconstruction and renovation work took a decade, but the university has recovered and taken several preventive measures with the hope of avoiding a tragic repeat of the event.[^cite_note-27]

In January 2009, [UNESCO](21786641.md) designated Iowa City the world's third [City of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Literature), making it part of the UNESCO [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

In 2015, the [Iowa Board of Regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Board_of_Regents) selected [Bruce Harreld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Harreld), a business consultant with limited experience in academic administration, to succeed Sally Mason as president. The regents' choice of Harreld provoked criticism and controversy on the UI campus due to his corporate background, lack of history in leading an institution of higher education, and the circumstances related to the search process.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-\:0-32][^cite_note-33] The regents said they had based their decision on the belief that Harreld could limit costs and find new sources of revenue beyond tuition in an age of declining state support for universities.[^cite_note-\:0-32]

On July 15, 2021, [Barbara J. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_J._Wilson) was named the 22nd president of the University of Iowa.[^cite_note-34]

On April 19, 2026, a mass shooting occurred near the campus. Five people were injured and one is in critical condition.[^cite_note-35]

## Campus



Art Building West, [University of Iowa School of Art and Art History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_School_of_Art_and_Art_History)



[General Hospital's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_Hospitals_and_Clinics) [collegiate Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_Gothic) tower is prominent on the west side of campus



Seamans Center for the Engineering Arts and Sciences



The Old Iowa State Capitol sits at the center of [The Pentacrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacrest)

The University of Iowa's main campus is in [Iowa City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City). The campus is roughly bordered by Park Road and U.S. Highway 6 to the north and Dubuque and Gilbert streets to the east. The [Iowa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_River) flows through the campus, dividing it into west and east sides.

Of architectural note is the [Pentacrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacrest) which comprises five major buildings—[Old Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Old_Capitol_Building), Schaeffer Hall, MacLean Hall, Macbride Hall, and Jessup Hall—at the center of the University of Iowa Campus. The Pentacrest reflects the [Beaux-Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaux-Arts_architecture) in addition to [Greek Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Revival) architectural styles and the [Collegiate Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_Gothic) architecture, which is dominant in sections of the campus east of the [Iowa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_River). The Old Capitol was once the home of the state legislature and the primary government building for the State of Iowa but is now the symbolic heart of the university with a restored ceremonial legislative chamber and a museum of Iowa history.

Also on the east side of the campus are six residence halls (Burge, Daum, Stanley, Currier, Mayflower, and Catlett), the Iowa Memorial Union, the [Women's Resource & Action Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Resource_&_Action_Center), the Pappajohn Business Building, Seamans Center for the Engineering Arts and Sciences, the Lindquist Center ([home of the College of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_College_of_Education)), Phillips Hall (the foreign language building), Van Allen Hall (home to physics and astronomy), Trowbridge Hall (home to Earth & Environmental Sciences, as well as the Iowa Geological Survey), the English-Philosophy Building, the Becker Communication Building, the Adler Journalism Building, [Voxman Music Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voxman_Music_Building), and the buildings for biology, chemistry, and psychology. The Main Library can also be found on the east side.

The Colleges of Law, Medicine, Nursing, Dentistry, Pharmacy, and [Public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) are on the west side of the Iowa River, along with the [University of Iowa Hospitals and Clinics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_Hospitals_and_Clinics), Art Building West and Visual Arts Building, and the Theatre Building. Additionally, five residence halls (Hillcrest, Slater, Rienow, Parklawn, and Petersen), [Kinnick Stadium](1873811.md), and [Carver-Hawkeye Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carver-Hawkeye_Arena) are located on the west campus.

The campus is home to several museums, including the [University of Iowa Stanley Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa_Stanley_Museum_of_Art), the [Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Museum_of_Natural_History), the Old Capitol Museum, the Medical Museum, the Athletic Hall of Fame and Museum, and Project Art at the University Hospitals and Clinics.

A flood of the Iowa River in 2008 had a major impact on several campus buildings, forcing many to temporarily or permanently close. The upper levels of the Iowa Memorial Union remained open while its lower level was renovated. The arts campus, which included Art Building West, Old Art Building, Hancher Auditorium, Voxman Music Building, Clapp Recital Hall, and the Theatre Building, sustained significant damage. Art Building West reopened in 2012 after repairs were completed. Sections of Old Art Building were razed, leaving only the historic WPA-era building, which includes regionalist artist Grant Wood's former studio. Esteemed artists Elizabeth Catlett, Ana Mendieta, and Charles Ray were all trained in this building. The new Visual Arts Building was opened on a higher plot of land adjacent to Art Building West in 2016 after years when studio arts were housed in a temporary facility. Hancher Auditorium was rebuilt near its current site on the West bank of the Iowa River, and Voxman Music Hall was constructed adjacent to downtown Iowa City and the main campus on South Clinton Street. The new Hancher Auditorium and the new Voxman Music Building opened in 2016.

The Oakdale Campus, which is home to some of the university's research facilities and the driving simulator, is located north of [Interstate 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80) in adjacent [Coralville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coralville,_Iowa).

### Campus public art collection

The University of Iowa holds and continues to commission an extensive collection of public art. The program began under the Iowa State 'Art in State Buildings Program,' one of the first percent for art programs in the United States[^cite_note-36] since repealed in 2017. The collection includes many important works, including works by artists Sol LeWitt (2-3-1-1, 1994), [El Anatsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Anatsui) (Anonymous Creature 2009), [Dale Chihuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Chihuly) (Forest Amber and Gilded Chandelier, 2004), [Auguste Rodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Rodin) (Jean de Fiennes, draped, 1889), and [Peter Randall-Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Randall-Page) (Ridge and Furrow, 2011).[^cite_note-Ina_Cole-37][^cite_note-38]

### Sustainability

The University of Iowa is one of the EPA's Green Power Partners,[^cite_note-Green_Power_Partners-39] burning oat hulls instead of coal and reducing coal consumption by 20%.[^cite_note-UI_President_Mason_announces_strengthened_sustainability_focus_for_university-40] In May 2004, the university joined the Chicago Climate Exchange,[^cite_note-Examples_of_Sustainability_Practices_and_Initiatives-41] and in April 2009, a student garden was opened.[^cite_note-New_Student_Garden_opens_on_UI_west_campus-42]

The university also offers a Certificate in Sustainability through the Office of Sustainability (OS).[^cite_note-43] The OS recently coordinated the university's first sustainability plan: "2020 Vision UIowa Sustainability Targets," proposed by UI president Sally Mason on October 29, 2010.[^cite_note-44]

## Organization and administration

Main article: [Iowa Board of Regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Board_of_Regents)

The [Iowa Board of Regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Board_of_Regents), a statewide body, governs the University of Iowa, as well as the state's two other public universities (Iowa State University and the University of Northern Iowa), along with certain other institutions. Created by the Iowa General Assembly in 1909, the board is composed of nine volunteer members appointed by the governor and confirmed by the Iowa Senate to serve staggered six-year terms.[^cite_note-45] The Iowa Board of Regents hires the president of the University of Iowa and the university president reports to the board.[^cite_note-46] The 22nd and current president of the University of Iowa is [Barbara J. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_J._Wilson), who has served since July 15, 2021.[^cite_note-47]

The University of Iowa is a member of the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities). The university is home to [ISCABBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISCABBS), a public [bulletin board system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system) that was the world's largest Internet community before the commercialization of the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web).

The [Iowa Writers' Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Writers'_Workshop) was founded in 1936. Since 1947 it has produced thirteen [Pulitzer Prize](24230.md) winners. Twenty-five people affiliated with the Writers' Workshop have won a Pulitzer Prize. The **Hanson Center for Technical Communication** was founded at The University of Iowa and named after a 1960 graduate, Thomas R. Hanson, who funded the institution with \$800,000.[^cite_note-64]

The university has educated many of the state's professionals, including 79% of Iowa's dentists, 50% of physicians, 48% of pharmacists, as well as many teachers and administrators in each of the state's K–12 school districts.[^cite_note-University_of_Iowa-7]

- IIHR–Hydroscience & Engineering (Iowa Institute of Hydraulic Research). IIHR—Hydroscience & Engineering is a world-renowned center for education, research, and public service focusing on hydraulic engineering and fluid mechanics. Based in the C. Maxwell Stanley Hydraulics Laboratory, a five-story red brick building on the banks of the Iowa River, IIHR is a unit of the University of Iowa's College of Engineering. Because of its contributions to water's study and use, the [American Society of Civil Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Civil_Engineers) recognized the Stanley Hydraulics Lab as a [National Historic Civil Engineering Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Historic_Civil_Engineering_Landmarks). The ASCE distinguishes the lab as the "oldest university-based hydraulics laboratory in the nation continuously focusing on research and education in hydraulic engineering."

### Libraries

The University of Iowa library system is the state's largest library and comprises the Main Library, the Hardin Library for the Health Sciences,[^cite_note-78] five branch libraries, and the Law Library. The University Libraries' holdings include more than five million bound volumes, more than 200,000 rare books, and 1000 historical manuscript collections. Significant holdings include Hardin Library's John Martin Rare Book Room, the [Iowa Women's Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Noun-Mary_Louise_Smith_Iowa_Women's_Archives),[^cite_note-\:1-79] the [Louis Szathmary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Szathmary) culinary arts collections, the Ruth and Marvin Sackner Archive of [Concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_poetry) and [Visual Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_poetry),[^cite_note-\:1-79] science fiction collections, and works of [Walt Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman). The comic books collection in the Special Collections contains original art for 6,000 cartoons, film and television scripts, magazines and other underground or amateur publications, as well as mainstream books from throughout the 20th and 21st centuries.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

The university also tries to sponsor events that give students an alternative to the typical drinking scene.[^cite_note-latenight-85] In 2004 the university established an annual \$25,000 contract with the newly reopened [Iowa City Englert Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City_Englert_Theatre) to host concerts and performances for as many as 40 nights a year.[^cite_note-86] Students participate in a variety of student media organizations. ## Athletics

Main article: [Iowa Hawkeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes)

The University of Iowa has 22 varsity athletic teams, known as the [Hawkeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes). All teams are members of the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) in the [National Collegiate Athletic Association's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA) [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_\(NCAA\)). There are 10 men's teams and 12 women's teams. Three of these teams—men's gymnastics, men's swimming and diving, and men's tennis—were eliminated after the 2020–21 academic year to help address a projected \$60–75 million deficit related to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_education).[^cite_note-87]

### Wrestling

Main article: [Iowa Hawkeyes wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes_wrestling)

Iowa's most successful team is [men's wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes_wrestling), which has won 24 of the school's 26 NCAA championships. Fifteen of those championships occurred during [Dan Gable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gable)'s 21-year tenure as head coach (1977–1997). It has 35 Big Ten titles, 81 individual NCAA Titles, and has graduated 17 [Olympians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-88] The team is currently coached by alumnus [Tom Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brands).

### Football

Main article: [Iowa Hawkeyes football](963244.md)



The facade of the west stand of [Kinnick Stadium](1873811.md)

Iowa's [football team](963244.md) is one of the most financially valuable college programs in the country.[^cite_note-89] They have won 11 Big Ten championships and claim 5 national championships.[^cite_note-90] The program has produced 10 members of the [College Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Football_Hall_of_Fame), 27 consensus first-team All-Americans, 5 members of the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) and 245 NFL Draft Picks.[^cite_note-91] The team is currently coached by [Kirk Ferentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Ferentz), who has completed his 23rd year following coach [Hayden Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden_Fry), who coached the previous 20 seasons.

### Field hockey

Main article: [Iowa Hawkeyes field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes_field_hockey)

Iowa's [field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Hawkeyes_field_hockey) team is the most successful women's team at the university, winning the 1986 national championship. They have won 13 conference titles and have made 11 Final Four appearances in the 33-year history of the NCAA tournament, despite field hockey not being a high school sport in Iowa.[^cite_note-B1G-92] The program has produced 85 All-Americans and 13 [Olympians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-B1G_AA_2014-93] The program is currently coached by Lisa Celluci.

### Other sports

Other sports at the university include basketball, soccer, baseball, softball, gymnastics, golf, swimming and diving, tennis, track and field, volleyball, cross country, and rowing. Most of the school's athletic facilities are located on the west end of campus.[^cite_note-94] The largest venue is the 70,585-seat [Kinnick Stadium](1873811.md), home to the football program.[^cite_note-95] Opening in 1929 as Iowa Stadium, it was renamed in 1977 after [Nile Kinnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Kinnick), winner of the 1939 [Heisman Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisman_Trophy). The basketball, wrestling, gymnastics, and volleyball teams play at [Carver-Hawkeye Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carver-Hawkeye_Arena), which seats 15,400.[^cite_note-96] Other venues include the [Beckwith Boathouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckwith_Boathouse), [Duane Banks Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duane_Banks_Field), and the old [Iowa Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Fieldhouse).
