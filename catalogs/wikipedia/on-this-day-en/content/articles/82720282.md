On 18 March 2026, [Israel](9282173.md) conducted an attack on [Iranian](14653.md) gas and oil sites in [South Pars gas field](9037100.md) and [Asaluyeh](3393482.md) oil refinery amid the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). South Pars—the world's largest natural gas field—is jointly operated by Iran and [Qatar](8238258.md), which Qatar calls North Dome.[^cite_note-2] The attack was intended to cut off a large source of revenue for the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC).[^cite_note-3]

The attack is described as a major escalation, exacerbating worldwide energy supply disruptions of worldwide disruptions.[^cite_note-4] Iran promptly retaliated by attacking energy infrastructures of Israel and several [Gulf Arab states](3088555.md). While it has been confirmed that the U.S. coordinated and approved the attack,[^cite_note-\:0-5] President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) later denied knowledge of the attack in advance and ruled out further strikes on Iranian energy sites.[^cite_note-\:9-6]

## Background

South Pars gas field is crucial to Iran's energy supply, amounting up to 70 percent of the country's gas production.[^cite_note-\:5-7] As the fourth-largest consumer of natural gas globally, behind the United States, [China](5405.md), and [Russia](25391.md), Iran relies heavily on gas to produce electricity and heat homes due to its cold climate.[^cite_note-\:6-8]

Israel first targeted several Iranian fuel sites in [Tehran](57654.md) and the neighboring [Karaj](978698.md) on 7 March 2026. Israel confirmed its "significant strike" targeting fuel storage and energy complexes in the capital, claiming they were being used by the [Iranian Armed Forces](14305018.md).[^cite_note-9] The United States opposed the Israeli strikes on oil facilities, expressing concern that the strikes could backfire strategically by producing support for the [Islamic Republic](50913538.md).[^cite_note-10] During the [bombing raid on Kharg Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kharg_Island_raid) on 13 March, the United States struck military targets but spared the oil infrastructure "for reasons of decency."[^cite_note-11]

## Attacks

Israel conducted airstrikes on the South Pars natural gas field as well as oil and petrochemical facilities in Asaluyeh, according to Iranian state media, blaming both Israel and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-updatefromrebecca-12] Although it initially objected Israeli targeting of Iranian oil sites, the United States approved the attack and coordinated with Israel,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-\:1-14] supporting the strike as a message to Iran over its [closure of the Strait of Hormuz](82550763.md).[^cite_note-\:7-15] U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) has ruled out any further strikes on Iranian energy sites, however.[^cite_note-\:4-16] Initial reports revealed that the damage caused to sections of South Pars gas field make up nearly 12 percent of Iran's total gas production.[^cite_note-\:5-7]

According to [Fars News Agency](5000099.md), Israel struck petrochemical facilities in Asaluyeh, damaging storage tanks, gas installations, and refineries. The attack targeted supply and transmission pipelines and took place around 2\:10 p.m. local time.[^cite_note-17] Iran's [Ministry of Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_\(Iran\)) said that a number of facilities were damaged and that a fire at the gas field was being put under control. No casualties were immediately reported.[^cite_note-\:2-18] The attack halted output at two refineries with a combined capacity of 100 million cubic meters per day.[^cite_note-19] A number of phases at the gas field were shut down to stop the fire from spreading.[^cite_note-20]

Independent evaluation by journalists did show massive damage to the Iranian installations, with the biggest refinery of the complex, number 4, based on an assessment from satellite imagery taken 3 days after the Israeli attack, almost completely destroyed and the smaller refinery number 7 showing signs of heavy damage as well. The gas tanks, which had been reported to have been hit, seemed to be intact.[^cite_note-21]

## Aftermath

The IRGC issued evacuation orders for several oil facilities across [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), and Qatar, deeming them "direct and legitimate targets and will be targeted in the coming hours."[^cite_note-\:8-22] These targets include SAMREF refinery and [Jubail](632008.md) petrochemical complex in Saudi Arabia, Al Hosn gas field in the UAE, and [Ras Laffan](24864449.md) refinery and [Mesaieed petrochemical complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesaieed_Industrial_Area) in Qatar.[^cite_note-\:2-18]

Following news of the attack, international oil prices increased from \$103 a barrel to \$108.[^cite_note-updatefromrebecca-12] Gas prices in [Europe](9239.md) increased by 7 percent amid fears of Iranian retaliatory action against energy infrastructure in the [Gulf Arab states](3088555.md).[^cite_note-\:6-8] Iran halted its gas flows to [Iraq](7515928.md), which relies on Iran for between a third and 40 percent of its gas and power needs.[^cite_note-\:1-14]

### Iranian retaliation

[QatarEnergy](11292570.md) reported that same-day Iranian retaliatory missile strikes on [Ras Laffan Industrial City](24864449.md) caused "extensive damage." Qatar's ministry of interior said that it had brought a fire under control and that no injuries were reported. The Qatari foreign ministry strongly condemned "the brutal" Iranian attack, considering it "a dangerous escalation, a flagrant violation of its sovereignty, and a direct threat to its national security." Qatar also declared the Iranian embassy's military and security personnel and staff as *[persona non grata](521241.md)*.[^cite_note-\:3-23] [United Kingdom](31717.md) Prime Minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) condemned "in the strongest terms" the Iranian strike on Qatari gas facilities.[^cite_note-24]

Saudi Arabia said its air defenses had intercepted four ballistic missiles launched toward [Riyadh](51554.md) and another two launched toward the [Eastern Province](468721.md). It reported four injuries as a result of falling shrapnel from an intercepted ballistic missile.[^cite_note-25] Saudi foreign minister Prince [Faisal bin Farhan Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_bin_Farhan_Al_Saud) said his country's patience with "Iranian aggression is not unlimited" and that trust in Iran has been "completely shattered."[^cite_note-26]

The UAE said its air defenses dealt with 13 ballistic missiles and 27 drones from Iran.[^cite_note-\:3-23] Operations at gas facilities in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) were suspended as a result of two incidents of fallen debris.[^cite_note-27] The UAE condemned the "Iranian terrorist attack" targeting its [Habshan](8495134.md) gas facility, stating that it constitutes a "dangerous escalation and a violation of the principles of international law."[^cite_note-28]

An Iranian missile attack struck the oil refinery at [Haifa](55606.md) in northern [Israel](9282173.md) which, according to Israeli Energy Minister [Eli Cohen](46195208.md), did not cause "significant damage" but briefly disrupted power for 45 minutes and caused "localized" damage to the power grid.[^cite_note-29]

  [^cite_note-\:10-31]

-  [Oman](22316.md): Oman condemned the attack as a "dangerous escalation" and a "direct threat" to the energy supplies and security of the region, as well as a "violation of international laws and norms."

  [^cite_note-32]

*  [Qatar](8238258.md): Qatar condemned the strikes on South Pars as "dangerous and irresponsible." Blaming Israel for the strikes, Qatari foreign ministry spokesman [Majed al-Ansari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majed_al-Ansari) stated that "the Israeli targeting of facilities linked to Iran’s South Pars field, an extension of Qatar’s North Field, is a dangerous & irresponsible step amid the current military escalation in the region," and that "targeting energy infrastructure constitutes a threat to global energy security."

  [^cite_note-33]

-  [United Arab Emirates](69328.md): The UAE condemned the attack, noting that South Pars field is connected to Qatar's North Dome, and warned that the strikes "represented a dangerous escalation" which "entails serious environmental repercussions and directly endangers civilians."

We are rapidly putting them out of business!"
