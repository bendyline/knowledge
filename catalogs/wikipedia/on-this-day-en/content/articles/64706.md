# Communications in Iran



Iran is among the first five countries which have had a growth rate of over 20% and the highest level of development in telecommunications.

**Iran's telecommunications industry** is almost entirely state-owned, dominated by the [Telecommunication Company of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication_Company_of_Iran) (TCI). Fixed-line penetration in 2004 was relatively well-developed by regional standards, standing at 22 lines per 100 people, higher than Egypt with 14 and Saudi Arabia with 15, although behind the UAE with 27.[^cite_note-EIU-2] Iran had more than 1 [mobile phone](19644137.md) per inhabitant by 2012.[^cite_note-zawya.com-5]

Iran has a population of 80 million with some 56% of [Iranians](14653.md) under the age of 25.[^cite_note-EIU-2] In 2008, there were more than 52,000 rural offices, providing Telecom services to the villages across the country. The number of fixed telephone lines is above 24 million, with penetration factor of 33.66%. In 2012, there were 43 million internet users in Iran, making the country first in the Middle East in terms of number.[^cite_note-payvand.com1-6][^cite_note-ReferenceC-7][^cite_note-atiehbahar.com-8] As of 2020, 70 million Iranians are using high-speed [mobile internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_internet).

Iran is among the first five countries which have had a growth rate of over 20 percent and the highest level of development in telecommunications.[^cite_note-9] Iran has been awarded the [UNESCO](21786641.md) special certificate for providing telecommunication services to rural areas. By the end of 2009, Iran's [telecommunications](33094374.md) market was the fourth-largest market in the region at \$9.2 billion and is expected to grow to \$12.9 billion by 2014 at a [CAGR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAGR) of 6.9 percent.[^cite_note-voice-quality.tmcnet.com-10]

According to the *Electronic Journal on Information Systems in Developing Countries* (EJISDC), the [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology) (ICT) sector had a 1.1–1.3% share of GDP in 2002. About 150,000 people are employed in the ICT sector, including around 20,000 in the software industry.[^cite_note-ebusinessforum.com-11] There were 1,200 registered information technology (IT) companies in 2002, 200 of which were involved in software development. Software exports stood around \$50 million in 2008.[^cite_note-iran-daily.com-12] Between 2009 and 2020 the Telecommunications market more than doubled.[^cite_note-13]

## Overview

See also: [Demographics of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Iran)

The government runs the [broadcast media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_media), which includes three national [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) stations and two national [television](29831.md) networks, as well as dozens of local radio and television stations. In 2000 there were 252 radios, 158 television sets, 219 [telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone) lines, and 110 personal computers for every 1,000 residents. [Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computers) for home use became more affordable in the mid-1990s, and since then demand for access to the Internet has increased rapidly. In 1998, the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications (renamed [the Ministry of Information & Communication Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Communications_Technology_\(Iran\))) began selling [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) accounts to the general public. In 2006, the Iranian telecom industry's revenues were estimated at \$1.2 billion.[^cite_note-14] By the end of 2009, Iran's telecommunications market was the fourth-largest market in the region at \$9.2 billion and is expected to grow to \$12.9 billion by 2014 at a [CAGR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAGR) of 6.9 percent.[^cite_note-voice-quality.tmcnet.com-10]

The Fourth Five Year Economic Development Plan has proposed the following key benchmarks for 2010: 36 million fixed lines; 50% penetration rate for mobile phones; establishment of reliable rural ICT connections and 30 million internet users. Given the recent developments of the industry, the objectives are very likely to be achieved.[^cite_note-atiehbahar.com-8]

According to one report,[^cite_note-15] Iran has seen above average growth, specially in mobile subscriber numbers. Mobile data services are available but account for a small proportion of total data consumption in 2014. Some telecom parameters of 2012 & 2014 are as below:

## Press

See also: [Media of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Iran)

The press in Iran is privately *and* government owned and reflects a diversity of political and social views. A special court has authority to monitor the print media and may suspend publication or revoke the licenses of papers or journals that a jury finds guilty of publishing anti-religious material, slander, or information detrimental to the national interest. Since the late 1990s the court has shut down many pro-reform newspapers and other periodicals. Most Iranian newspapers are published in Persian, but newspapers in English and other languages also exist. The most widely circulated periodicals are based in Tehrān. Popular daily and weekly newspapers include *[Ettelaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettelaat)*, *[Kayhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayhan)*, *Resalat*, *[Iran Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Daily)* and the *[Tehran Times](5997270.md)* (Iran Daily and Tehran Times are both English-language papers).[^cite_note-20]

## Satellite

See also: [Iranian Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Space_Agency) and [Iran Electronics Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Electronics_Industries)

Since the 1970s, there have been a number of proposals for a state-owned [communications satellite](45207.md), called *Zohreh* (en:[Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus)) from 1993 onwards.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The planned satellite would have similar capabilities to a commercially produced Western satellite, while such capabilities are already provided through leases owned by the Iranian telecommunications sector. An agreement was signed between [Iran](14653.md) and [Russia](25391.md) in 2005 to develop the satellite with a planned launch date of 2007,[^cite_note-23] but the launch has been postponed until at least 2009, set to follow actual construction of the satellite.[^cite_note-24]

## Telephone

The telephone system is inadequate but being modernized and expanded with the goal of not only improving the efficiency and increasing the volume of the urban service but also bringing telephone service to several thousand villages that are not connected now.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-7]

### Domestic

As a result of heavy investing in the telephone system since 1994, thousands of mobile cellular subscribers are being served; moreover, the technical level of the system has been raised by the installation of thousands of digital switches.

Iran's telecommunication network enjoys the highest growth rate in the Middle East. One of the indicators that clearly illustrates this growth rate is the increase in the number of installed main lines. In 1978 there were only about 850,000 fixed lines installed in Iran. This number rose to about 1,767,000 by 1986 and to about 2,380,000 by 1989. One of the objectives of the first five-year development plan (1989–1994) for the telecommunication sector was to add one million new fixed lines to the network. However, due to the establishment and expansion of many low-capacity and high-capacity exchanges, the actual growth rate that was achieved was considerably higher. In fact more than 3.1 million new main lines were installed during the period of the first five-year plan. Four million new lines were expected to be added by the end of the second five-year plan (1994–1999), which would increase the number of installed main lines to about 9,510,000 fixed lines. Iran's telecom market, which generated an estimated \$9.1 billion in 2010 (of which 2/3 from "voice" mobile-phone and 1/10 from "data" mobile-phone services), has been growing significantly, especially its mobile-phone business. As of 2010, Iran had about 66 million mobile-phone subscribers covering about 70% of the population. In contrast, about 36% of Iranians had fixed-line phones.[^cite_note-stecklow1-25]

##### Operators

Major mobile operators as of 2008: [Mobile Telecommunication Company of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Telecommunication_Company_of_Iran) (MCI Hamrahe Aval) with 70% market share, [MTN Irancell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Irancell) (28%), and Emirates Telecommunications Corp ([Etisalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etisalat)) who won a license for 300 million euros in December 2008.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Etisalat has been replaced by an Iranian consortium and operates under the name *[RighTel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RighTel)*. The two national operators, Mobile Telecommunication Company of Iran and MTN Irancell both offer [GPRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPRS)-based data services.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-38] Recently a new [LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTE_\(telecommunication\)) mobile broadband service provider called ApTel has started its work in Iran.

As of 2011, major foreign supplier to Iran's mobile-phone networks are: [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei) of China along, [Telefon AB L.M.](9424.md) and Nokia Siemens Networks, a joint venture between [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia) and [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens).[^cite_note-stecklow1-25] As of 2016, France's [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Group) and U.K.'s [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone) (through *HiWEB.ir*) are also developing mobile IT in Iran.

##### 3G network

Abu Dhabi-based [Etisalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etisalat) and *Tamin Telecom*, the telecommunications investment arm of [Iran's social security and pensions department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Organization_\(Iran\)#Affiliated_institutions), will gain exclusive rights for two years to offer second- and third-generation services ([3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G)) in Iran (2008). Assuming a minimum network investment of \$4 billion, Etisalat can gain about 20 percent to 25% market share over five years of its operations (by 2013).[^cite_note-39] In 2009 it was announced that Etisalat, however, failed to secure the right to be Iran's exclusive 3G operator for two years.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-38] Later, *Tamin Telecom* announced that, [IDRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Development_and_Renovation_Organization_of_Iran) and *Imam Khomeini Decree Center* have replaced Etisalat because of contractual disagreements. In 2011, Tamin Telecom revealed plans to cover 60 percent of the population with its [2G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2G) network and 40 percent with its [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) network by 2014.[^cite_note-40]

##### 4G network

As of 2020, [4G LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4G_LTE) population coverage in Iran has reached around 90%. The government is preparing regulations for 5G deployment and aims to launch services in the next couple of years.

5G network

In 2021, [Irancell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Irancell) and [MCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Communications_of_Iran) launched 5G networks in Iran. In 2025, most major cities in Iran have 5G internet support.[^cite_note-41]

In 1993 Iran became the second country in the [Middle East](19323.md) to be connected to the Internet, and since then the government has made significant efforts to improve the nation's ICT infrastructure.[^cite_note-EIU-2] Iran's national Internet connectivity infrastructure is based on two major networks: the public switched telephone network (PSTN) and the public data network. The [PSTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSTN) provides a connection for end-users to [Internet service providers](100245.md) (ISPs) over mostly digital lines and supports modem-based connections. The Data Communication Company of Iran (DCI), a subsidiary of TCI, operates the public data network. Iran's external Internet links use the basic Internet protocol of [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCP/IP) (transmission control protocol/Internet protocol) via satellite exclusively. For data lines, copper, fiber, satellite and microwave are the available media, and popular services are high-speed Internet via digital subscriber lines ([DSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSL)), high-bandwidth lease lines and satellite. About 33 Iranian cities are connected directly by the [Trans-Asia-Europe cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ERMC_\(cable_system\)) network, or "silk road", connecting China to Europe.[^cite_note-EIU-2] According to the *Statistical Center of Iran*, 13.5 million households (i.e. [55.5% of all Iranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Iran)) have access to the internet (2016). Of this number, 7 million households have access to fixed [high-speed internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_internet) connection, and 10.7 million households have access to wireless high-speed internet.[^cite_note-techrasa.com-47] In 2016 64.8% of urban households and 36.1% of rural households had access to computers at their home.[^cite_note-techrasa.com-47] As of 2020, mobile broadband coverage had exceeded 91 percent in Iran.

Since 2005 the Iranian government has been developing its "[National Information Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Information_Network)" to tighten its control over content as well as increasing speed.[^cite_note-48] The project, which is separate from the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web), will be completed by 2017.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] This network will be separated from the rest of the internet, specifically for domestic use. Creating such a network, similar to [one used by North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwangmyong_\(network\)) would prevent unwanted information from outside of Iran getting into the closed system, such as with an [intranet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intranet) network. [Myanmar](19457.md) and [Cuba](5042481.md) also use similar systems.[^cite_note-51] Iran has announced that all government ministries and state bodies will be available through the secure "national information network" (NIN).[^cite_note-52]

The current internet services will not be replaced by the National Information Network or "Clean Internet" as it is called. In order to protect the privacy of Iranian internet users, a number of non-governmental organizations are currently developing domestic search engines that people can use through the NIN.[^cite_note-http-53]

Iranian officials have accused U.S.-based technology companies such as [Google](1092923.md), [Twitter](9988187.md) and [Microsoft](19001.md) of working in tandem with U.S. authorities to spy on Iranian online trends, search behavior, [social networking sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_networking_site) and [e-mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mail). These companies have denied those allegations despite [NSA leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_leaks).[^cite_note-54] As of 2013, 90% of all of [Internet traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_traffic) is being [routed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Router_\(computing\)) to [hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webhosting) outside the country.[^cite_note-BYI-55] Iran said it set a "world record" of 46% in online participation using NIN during the national [census](6889.md) in 2016.[^cite_note-56]

Much has been reported on the ways in which NIN aids the government with censorship and controls on the internet. Whilst to date, the existence of the NIN has not resulted in long-term disconnection from the global Internet, in the short-term it has been used by the authorities for this purpose.[^cite_note-article19.org-57] This was illustrated during the widespread deliberate disruptions to both mobile and networked Internet connections in the midst of nationwide protests in late 2017 and into early 2018. Research by the Campaign for Human Rights in Iran (CHRI) identified that the authorities had ordered IXPs to intentionally interrupt international traffic while maintaining national connections hosted on the NIN.[^cite_note-58]

Strengthening and driving local users towards local platforms is central to the NIN strategy. This has included efforts to undermine [net neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_neutrality), by requiring domestic ISPs to zero-rate domestic platforms, in effect subsidising users’ use of local Internet platforms. These domestic platforms are subject to the close oversight, influence and enforcement powers of the authorities. Pushing users to use these services strengthens the government's surveillance and monitoring capabilities and raises concerns for the protection of the right to privacy.[^cite_note-article19.org-57] These platforms are required to apply Iranian law, including content-based restrictions on content outlined in the [Islamic Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_system_of_Iran), the Press Laws and the Computer Crimes Law.[^cite_note-59] Over-reliance on domestic platforms is therefore likely to have a detrimental effect on the diversity and pluralism of content available on these platforms.

80 percent of modem imports are smuggled to Iran.[^cite_note-60]

### Internet service providers

The leading Data Communication Company of Iran (DCI) which belongs to TCI (now privatized) and the [Iranian Research Organization for Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Research_Organization_for_Science_and_Technology) (IROST) are two bodies that act as ISPs. As of 2008, the largest privately owned ISP was Parsnet, which serves only [Tehran](57654.md).[^cite_note-EIU-2] The leading ISP with a provincial focus is Isfahan-based Irangate.net.[^cite_note-EIU-2] The [Neda Rayaneh Institute](https://web.archive.org/web/20080727015312/http://www.austrade.gov.au/ICT-to-Iran/default.aspx) was the first private ISP in Iran.[^cite_note-EIU-2]

Domain names with the ".ir" suffix are assigned by the Institute for Studies in Theoretical Physics and Mathematics (IPM). DCI maintains the network infrastructure, providing Internet access via the IRANPAK X.25 packet-switching network, which covers most major cities. DCI is the only ISP with a permit for supplying government agencies. DCI supplies both [dial-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dial-up) and [leased lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leased_lines) to its users.[^cite_note-EIU-2]

By the regulations of Iran, these ISPs should rely on TCI for their bandwidth. Previously serviced by TCI's Public Switch Telephone Network, the ISPs have been provided with modern data line capacity through a national IP-based network. With the completion of this new network, Internet services in Iran is expected to improve dramatically.[^cite_note-Australian_trade-81]

#### Speed and costs

Iranian internet has an average speed of 2 Mbit/s, about one-tenth of the global average (2014). [Hong Kong](13404.md), the world leader, boasts an average of 72 Mbit/s; the United States ranks 31st, at about 21 Mbit/s.[^cite_note-washingtonpost.com-82] A 2 Mbit/s subscription costs \$5 a month, daily traffic is charged at 60¢/GB but traffic is free at night at up to 5 GB per night. Upload speeds are typically 40% of the download speed.People must pay four times price for download and upload using internet than national internet network.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

According speedtest.net in 2019, the average landline Internet speed in Iran is 12.84 Mbit/s and the country is ranked 104 among 130 audited countries. The global average speed of landline Internet services is 49.26 Mbit/s. Mobile Internet services is significantly better in Iran compared to landline services, with an average speed of 27.71 Mbit/s — 4 Mbit/s higher than the global average. Iran is ranked 51.[^cite_note-en.eghtesadonline.com-85]

### Usage

See also: [Distance education in Iran](1332627.md#Internet_and_distance_education), [Blogging in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blogging_in_Iran), and [Internet censorship in Iran](12831280.md)

Full [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) service is available in all major cities and it is very rapidly increasing. Many small towns and even some villages now have full Internet access. The government aims to provide 10% of government and commercial services via the Internet by end-2008 and to equip every school with computers and Internet connections by the same date.[^cite_note-EIU-2] The Internet has become an expanding means to accessing information and self-expression among the younger population. Iran is also the world's fourth largest country of [bloggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_blogs) with approx. 60,000 [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) blogs[^cite_note-opennet.net-89][^cite_note-100] although [Internet censorship in Iran](12831280.md) is amongst the most restrictive and sophisticated in the world.[^cite_note-101] As of 2014, 67.4 percent of Iranian young people (between the ages of 15 and 29) use the internet.[^cite_note-tehrantimes.com-102] 69.3% of Iranian young people reported using anti-internet [filtering software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtering_software) to be able to surf through blocked websites.[^cite_note-tehrantimes.com-102] Three-fifths of Iranians use [Facebook](7529378.md).[^cite_note-payvand.com3-103] Iranians spend an average of nine hours on [social media](5897742.md) websites every day.[^cite_note-payvand.com3-103] according of the [government of Iran](50913538.md), the first stage of smart filtering for the internet has been successful and the second stage has been launched in 2015.[^cite_note-104] As of 2016, 40 percent of content used by Iranian Internet citizens are produced in the country.[^cite_note-105]

#### Electronic commerce

See also: [Shetab Banking System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shetab_Banking_System), [Payment systems in Iran](3961876.md#Payment_systems), [Retail industry in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Iran#Retail_industry), [E-commerce and intellectual property in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property_in_Iran#E-commerce), and [E-trading in the Tehran Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Stock_Exchange#Electronic_trading_and_market_data)

Iran's electronic commerce was 10,000 billion rials (\$1 billion) in March 2009 and growing 60% annually.[^cite_note-106] Online-sales reached \$300,000 per day in 2014 with more than 20,000 active [online stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_stores) with electronics being the most popular sale product.[^cite_note-washingtonpost.com-82] In 2007, Tetra-Tech IT Company announced that using [Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Inc.) and [Mastercard](378791.md) is now possible for online sales and in Iranian e-card terminals at shopping malls, hotels, restaurants, and travel agencies for Iranians and foreign tourists.[^cite_note-107] [Saman Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saman_Bank) was the first bank to introduce [online banking services in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shetab_Banking_System). Since, it has been at the forefront of expansion and enhancement of electronic banking. In 2010, a state-linked technology group (the Rouyesh Technical Centre) established the country's first online supermarket.[^cite_note-108]

In 2015, the dollar value of [e-commerce](9611.md) reached \$17.4 billion. With the new *national information network*, Internet speed has increased drastically. Iran's penetration rate of the internet stands at 82.12% (2016), with 19 million people using the internet on their mobile phones. As of 2016, 600 cities in Iran had access to [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) networks and 200 cities had access to [4G](486547.md) Internet. The rate of mobile phone penetration in Iran stands at 94.46%.[^cite_note-109]

Iran is presently implementing a [barcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcode) system across the country in order to facilitate e-commerce and [tax collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Iran). The executive state organizations, which are the largest buyers of goods in the country, cannot buy and use goods that do not have the "[Irancode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irancode)".[^cite_note-110]

In 2004, the [Majlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_of_Iran) adopted the Electronic Commerce Law:[^cite_note-111]

- Articles 62–66 of this law specify that [Iran's existing intellectual-property laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property_in_Iran) apply to all electronic transactions.

- Articles 33–49 of the Electronic Commerce Law of 2004 seek to ensure consumer protection in electronic transactions. This legislation ensures the right of consumers to complete disclosure of information by suppliers before, during and after electronic transactions. Also specified are the terms under which contracts for electronic commerce may be broken.

- Article 37 gives consumers seven days to withdraw from any contract without penalty.

- Article 42 stipulates that the protections laid down in this law do not apply to financial services, immovable property, automatic vending machines, transactions over public payphones and auctions.

The [Fifth Five-Year Development Plan (2010–15)](64704.md) has set the target of conducting electronically:[^cite_note-112]

- 80 percent of governmental transactions,

- 20 percent of domestic trade

- 30 percent of foreign trade.

## Software development

See also: [Science and technology in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Iran), [Foreign Direct Investment in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran), and [Intellectual property in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property_in_Iran)

According to the *Electronic Journal on Information Systems in Developing Countries* (EJISDC), the [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology) (ICT) sector had a 1.1–1.3% share of GDP in 2002. About 150,000 people are employed in the ICT sector, including around 20,000 in the software industry.[^cite_note-ebusinessforum.com-11] There were 1,200 registered information technology (IT) companies in 2002, 200 of which were involved in software development. Software exports stood around \$50 million in 2008[^cite_note-iran-daily.com-12] and \$400 million in 2014.[^cite_note-113]

Iran imports a large part of its software. According to the EJISDC, around 95% of the output of the domestic industry is [bespoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bespoke) development (including translation/adaptation from foreign source) —as opposed to product work—meaning export possibilities are low.[^cite_note-EIU-2] Domestic firms involved in software production include Sena Soft, Dadeh-Pardazi, Iran Argham, Kafa System Information Network, Iran System and Puya.[^cite_note-EIU-2] [IDRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Development_and_Renovation_Organization_of_Iran)'s subsidiaries "Magfa" (Information Technology Development Center) and Iran Info-Tech Development Co. are also leading players in this sector. The largest private computer software producer in Iran is [Hamkaran Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Group) with 7000 customers.[^cite_note-114]

[Iran's National Foundation for Computer Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Computer_and_Video_Games_Foundation) unveiled the country's first [online video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_video_game) in 2010, capable of supporting up to 5,000 users at the same time.[^cite_note-115]

Although there are no established clusters in Tehran, a major facility, the Tehran Software and Information Technology Park, is planned. *International Development Ireland* was selected as the consultant for the project in mid-2004. There are also plans for a technology park in the free-trade zone on [Kish Island](847277.md) in the Persian Gulf, which already contains the necessary technological infrastructure.[^cite_note-EIU-2]

The government's drive to automate manual processes is expected to account for about 70% of demand for [software development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development), but there are also some 15,000 private-sector factories that have software needs.[^cite_note-EIU-2] Foreign investment in the sector is minimal, although some links are being developed with the [Indian software industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_in_India). Poor [intellectual property protection in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property_in_Iran) has also hindered the development of Iranian software companies causing a lack of [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran) in this sector.[^cite_note-116]

Mobile and smartphones make up 41 percent of the entire digital consumer market with sales of \$10 billion expected in 2008. It's followed by desktop and notebook computers at \$5 billion and LCD televisions at \$4 billion. The study also reveals the market structure by channel where, in Iran and the UAE, independent retailers still lead with 60-percent share in volume for product categories such as digital cameras, LCD televisions and mobile phones. However, large retailers like Plug-Ins, Emax and Sharaf DG are also making their presence felt by capturing the remaining 40 percent. The digital consumer technology sector is expected to see retail sales in excess of \$24 billion by the end of 2008.[^cite_note-142]

[Iran](14653.md)’s domestic [consumer electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronic) market, defined as including computing devices, mobile handsets and video audio and gaming products, was estimated at \$7.3 billion in 2008 (\$8.2 billion in 2010), with 47% market share for [computer hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware), 28% Audio/Video and 25% [mobile phone](19644137.md) (with growing demand for [PDAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_digital_assistant), [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) and [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) handsets).[^cite_note-CE-143][^cite_note-144] Business Monitor International (BMI) forecasts that Iran’s demands for domestic consumer electronic devices will reach \$10 billion by 2013 and \$16 billion by 2016.[^cite_note-wsj.com-90][^cite_note-CE-143]

*[ELECOMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_International_Exhibitions_Company)* is an annual commercial event in Iran’s market of electronics and computer products and services.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

### Computers

See also: [Industrial Development and Renovation Organization of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Development_and_Renovation_Organization_of_Iran), [Parsé Semiconductor Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pars%C3%A9_Semiconductor_Co.), and [Data Processing Iran Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_Processing_Iran_Co.)

Iran is manufacturing some computer components under license from international companies, predominantly in the area of monitors. Eight Iranian companies are manufacturing monitors under licence of LG, Samsung, Hyundai, Benq, Tatung and CTX. Motherboard, keyboard, mouse, computer case, power supply, CPU, hard drive and printers are other components that are now manufactured locally.[^cite_note-Australian_trade-81] Personal computer (PC) ownership in Iran stood at 7.3m in 2005, or 10.5 PCs per 100 people.[^cite_note-EIU-2]

Among the leading domestic telecoms equipment manufacturers is the Iran Telephone Manufacturing Company (ITMC), which has licensing agreements with [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) and [Alcatel-Lucent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatel-Lucent) of France. ITMC is owned by TCI (45%), Industry Bank (35%) and Siemens (20%).[^cite_note-EIU-2] Other manufacturers include Iran Communications Industries Incorporated and [Parstel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parstel?action=edit\&redlink=1)—which produces under licence from [Daewoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewoo) Corporation, a South Korean firm. [IDRO's Iran Info-Tech Development Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Development_and_Renovation_Organization_of_Iran#Subsidiaries) is producing [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) under the brand name "SAHAND". Overall, ICT hardware sales were estimated to total US\$700m a year in 2008.[^cite_note-EIU-2]

## Ministry of Information and Communication Technology

See also: [Ministry of Information and Communications Technology (Iran)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Communications_Technology_\(Iran\))

The main functions of the [Ministry of Information and Communication Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Communications_Technology_\(Iran\)) are laying out and implementing policies pertaining to post and communications in Iran. The Ministry is also in charge of issuing import licenses for certain communication equipment and parts thereof. This ministry was originally named "Ministry of Post, Telegraph & Telephone".

### Privatization

Main article: [Privatization in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization_in_Iran)

Based on Note C of the general policies of the constitution's Article 44, the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology announced that it will float the shares of affiliated companies such as Telecommunications Company in the stock market.[^cite_note-147]

Under the general policies of Article 44, telecom companies are categorized in four groups as follows:

- **Group One**: Among the 30 provincial telecom networks, the fixed telecom networks pertain to those of Tehran, Isfahan, Fars, Hamedan, Ahvaz, Khorasan Razavi, Khuzestan and East Azarbaijan. The first group concerns fixed line telecom networks, including those in the public sector with 30 subsidiary telecom networks in provinces. The non-governmental sector includes companies such as Iraphone, Novin, Zahi Kish, Kouh-e Nour, Montazeran Adlgostar and Pouya Ertebat with each having hundreds of thousands of subscribers.

- **Group Two**: The second group concerns [mobile telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_telephone) networks. In the public sector, they include TCI. In the non-governmental sector, they include telecommunications companies such as Omran Kish, Isfahan, Rafsanjan Complex and [Irancell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irancell). [Privatization Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization_of_the_Iranian_economy) has forecast that shares of TCI will be floated in the stock market by late September 2007.

  [^cite_note-148]

- **Group Three**: There is only one public network in the [data network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_network) sector, namely Data and Telecommunications Company of Iran which is considered a basic telecom network in terms of mobile networks and Shomal IT Company. In the non-governmental sector, there are over 100 companies with a shared data network.

- **Group Four**: The subsidiary telecom network named Subsidiary Telecommunications Company is another basic telecom network. They are completely owned by the state and not targeted for privatization.

TCI's Infrastructure Telecom Company will be detached from it and it would continue its activities as a part of the ICT Ministry.[^cite_note-149] 33 companies in the telecom sector are to be privatized by September 2007.[^cite_note-150] This happened simultaneously with the launch of [MTN Irancell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Irancell), a private second carrier with foreign ownership. The privatization and introduction of a second operator has created a significantly more competitive environment which has led to significant cost reductions for mobile owners and service benefits.

#### 2009 – IPO

In 2009, 51% of the shares of TCI was sold to [Mobin Trust Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobin_Trust_Consortium), a consortium which some media claimed it was affiliated with the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md), for the sum of \$7.8 billion.[^cite_note-151]

#### Other ICP/ISP IPOs

In late 2011, Iranian ICP/ISP Afranet (Symbol in [Tehran Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Stock_Exchange): AFRZ1) went public.[^cite_note-152] As of 2012, there is no other ICP/ISP companies listed in Tehran Stock Exchange.
