**Rawalpindi**,[^cite_note-9] colloquially known as **Pindi**,[^cite_note-10] is the [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Punjab,_Pakistan_by_population) city in the [Pakistani province](446017.md) of [Punjab](24751.md) and the [fourth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Pakistan_by_population) in Pakistan. Located along the [Soan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soan_River) in north-western Punjab, Rawalpindi lies adjacent to Pakistan's capital, [Islamabad](51153.md), and the two are jointly known as the "[twin cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_cities)".[^cite_note-13]

Rawalpindi is located on the [Pothohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau) and is close to the ancient city of [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila), a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It was founded in 1493 by a Gakhar chief and remained a small town until the 18th century.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Following the [annexation of Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Sikh_War) by the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in 1849, Rawalpindi became the largest [garrison town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison) of the Northern Command of the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) with the establishment of the [Rawalpindi Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Cantonment). It was granted [municipal status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Metropolitan_Corporation) in 1867. Following the independence of Pakistan on 14 August 1947, the city became home to the [General Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Headquarters_\(Pakistan_Army\)) (GHQ) of the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md).[^cite_note-21]

Rawalpindi also served as the secondary capital of Pakistan, under [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan),[^cite_note-Mazumder_2003-19][^cite_note-Burki_2015-22] before the new capital was established in Islamabad. Rawalpindi is linked to the national highways through the [M-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) and [M-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-2_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) motorways, and serves as a major logistics and transportation centre for [northern Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pakistan). The city is home to historic [havelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haveli) and temples, and serves as a hub for tourists visiting [Azad Kashmir](2745.md), Taxila and [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md).[^cite_note-31]

## Etymology

Previously known as *Fatehpur Baori*, the town fell into decay during the [Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_of_India) of the 14th century. Afterwards, it came in hand of the [Gakhars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakhars) and a chief named *Jhanda Khan* restored it naming it Rawalpindi in 1493, literally meaning the "Village of Rawal" in [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Some accounts propose that a group of [ascetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asceticism) named Rawal arrived in this area and established the town.[^cite_note-36]

## History

### Origins

The region around Rawalpindi has been inhabited for thousands of years. Rawalpindi falls within the ancient region of [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara), and thus the area contains many Buddhist ruins. In the north-west of Rawalpindi, traces have been found of at least 55 [stupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupa), 28 Buddhist monasteries, 9 temples, and various artifacts in the [Kharoshthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharoshthi) script.[^cite_note-37]



Possible [Kushano-Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushano-Sasanian) plate, excavated in Rawalpindi, fourth century CE. [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum) 124093.

[^cite_note-38]



The "Fasting [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha)", on display at the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum) in [London](17867.md), was discovered in Rawalpindi.

To the southeast are the ruins of the [Mankiala stupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankiala_stupa) – a second-century stupa where, according to the [Jataka tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jataka_tales), a previous incarnation of the [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha) leapt off a cliff in order to offer his corpse to seven hungry tiger cubs.[^cite_note-39] The town of Taxila is thought to have been home to one of the early universities or education centres of South Asia.[^cite_note-40]

### Medieval

The first mention of Rawalpindi's earliest settlement dates from when [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) destroyed Rawalpindi and the town was restored by Gakhar chief *Kai Gohar* in the early 11th century. The town fell into decay again after Mongol invasions in the 14th century.[^cite_note-IGI_2013-41] Situated along an invasion route, the settlement did not prosper and remained deserted until 1493, when Jhanda Khan Gakhar re-established the ruined town, and named it *Rawal*.[^cite_note-42]

### Mughal period



The 16th century [Rawat Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawat_Fort), a fortified caravanserai.

During the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) era, Rawalpindi remained under the rule of the [Gakhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakhars) clan, who in turn pledged allegiance to the Mughal Empire.[^cite_note-Agrawal_1983-43] Gakhars also fortified a nearby [caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), the [Serai Rawat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawat_Fort).[^cite_note-44] The [Attock Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock_Fort) was constructed in 1581 after [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) led a campaign against his brother [Mirza Muhammad Hakim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Muhammad_Hakim).[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] In December 1585, the Emperor Akbar arrived in Rawalpindi, and remained in and around Rawalpindi for 13 years as he extended the frontiers of the empire,[^cite_note-Agrawal_1983-43] in an era described as a "glorious period" in his career as Emperor.[^cite_note-Agrawal_1983-43]

With the onset of rivalry between Gakhar chiefs after the death of Kamal Khan in 1559, Rawalpindi was awarded to his brother Said Khan by the Mughal Emperor.[^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46] Emperor [Jehangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehangir) visited the royal camp in Rawalpindi in 1622, where he first learned of Shah [Abbas I of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_I_of_Persia)'s plan to invade [Kandahar](17260.md).[^cite_note-47]

### Sikh rule

After the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) power declined, the town was captured in the mid-1760s from [Muqarrab Khan Gakhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqarrab_Khan_Gakhar) by the [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misl) under [Sardar Gujjar Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujjar_Singh_Banghi) and his son Sahib Singh.[^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46] The city's administration was handed to [Sardar Milkha Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkha_Singh_Thepuria), who invited traders from the neighbouring commercial centers of [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_\(city\)) and [Shahpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahpur_\(Punjab\)) to settle in the territory in 1766.[^cite_note-IGI_2013-41][^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46] The city began to prosper, although the population in 1770 is estimated to have been only about 300 families.[^cite_note-Rogers_2022-48] For a time Rawalpindi became the refuge of [Shah Shuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuja_Shah_Durrani), the exiled king of [Afghanistan](737.md), and of his brother Shah Zaman in the early 19th century.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45]

The Sikh ruler [Maharaja Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh) allowed the son of Sardar Milkha Singh to continue as Governor of Rawalpindi, after he seized it in 1810.[^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46] Sikh rule over Rawalpindi was consolidated by defeat of the Afghans [at Haidru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Haidru) in July 1813.[^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46] In late 1820s, Rawalpindi and surrounding areas became a centre of jihad movement of [Syed Ahmad Barelvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Ahmad_Barelvi). The Mujahideen victory at [Akora Khattak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Akora_Khattak) in 1827 was followed by defeat and death of Ahmad Barelvi in 1831 [at Balakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Balakot).[^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46] Jews first arrived in Rawalpindi's *Babu Mohallah* neighbourhood from [Mashhad](406512.md), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) in 1839,[^cite_note-Jewish_History_2016-49] in order to flee from anti-Jewish laws instituted by the [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar) dynasty. In 1841, Diwan Kishan Kaur was appointed *Sardar* of Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-Griffin_1890-46]

On 14 March 1849, Sardar Chattar Singh and Raja Sher Singh of the Sikh Empire surrendered to General Gilbert near Rawalpindi, ceding the city to the British.[^cite_note-Grewal_1998-50] The Sikh Empire then came to an end on 29 March 1849.

### British period



Rawalpindi's [Fatima Jinnah Women University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_Jinnah_Women_University) is housed in a Victorian mansion.

Following Rawalpindi's capture by the British East India Company, 53rd Regiment of the company army took quarters in the newly captured city.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] The decision to man a permanent military cantonment in the city was made in 1851 by the [Marquess of Dalhousie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Broun-Ramsay,_1st_Marquess_of_Dalhousie).[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45]

The city saw its first [telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph) office in the early 1850s.[^cite_note-Orient_Blackswan_2003-51] The city's Garrison Church was built shortly after in 1854,[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] and is the site where [Robert Milman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Milman), Bishop of Calcutta, was buried following his death in Rawalpindi in 1876.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] The city was home to 15,913 people in the 1855 census.[^cite_note-Rogers_2022-48] During the [1857 War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1857_War_of_Independence), the Gakhar and [Janjua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janjua) tribes remained loyal to the British.[^cite_note-Orient_Blackswan_2003-51] Numerous civil and military buildings were built during the British era, and the Municipality of Rawalpindi was constituted in 1867,[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] while the city's population as per the 1868 census was 19,228, with another 9,358 people residing in the city's cantonment.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45]

The city was also connected to railways that offered connection to India and the northwest frontier in [Peshawar](66230.md) in the 1880s.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] The Commissariat Steam Flour Mills were the first such mills in Punjab, and supplied most of the needs of British cantonments throughout Punjab.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] Rawalpindi's cantonment served as a feeder to other cantonments throughout the region.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45]

Rawalpindi flourished as a commercial centre, though the city remained largely devoid of an industrial base during the British era.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] A large portion of Kashmir's external trade passing through the city; in 1885, 14% of Kashmir's exports, and 27% of its imports passed through the city.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] A large market was opened in central Rawalpindi in 1883 by Sardar Sujan Singh, while the British further developed a shopping district for the city's elite known as *Saddar* with an archway built to commemorate Brigadier General Massey.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45]



Statue of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria), Rawalpindi, 1939

Rawalpindi's cantonment became a major centre of military power of the Raj after an arsenal was established in 1883.[^cite_note-IGI_2013-41] Britain's army elevated the city from a small town, to the third largest city in Punjab by 1921.[^cite_note-Orient_Blackswan_2003-51] In 1868, 9,358 people lived in the city's cantonment – by 1891, the number rose to 37,870.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] In 1891, the city's population excluding the Cantonment was 34,153.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45] The city was considered to be a favourite first posting for newly arrived soldiers from England, owing to the city's agreeable climate, and the nearby [hill station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_station) of [Murree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murree).[^cite_note-Gazetteer_1895-45]

In 1901, Rawalpindi was made the winter headquarters of the Northern Command and of the Rawalpindi military division. Riots broke out against British rule in 1905, following a famine in Punjab that peasants were led to believe was a deliberate act.[^cite_note-Nijjar_1996-52]

During [World War I](4764461.md), [Rawalpindi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District) "stood first" among districts in recruiting for the British war effort, with greater financial assistance from the British government channeled into the area in return.[^cite_note-Orient_Blackswan_2003-51]

By 1921, Rawalpindi's cantonment had overshadowed the city – Rawalpindi was one of seven cities of Punjab in which over half the population lived in the cantonment district.[^cite_note-Orient_Blackswan_2003-51] Communal riots erupted between Rawalpindi's Sikh and Muslim communities in 1926 after Sikhs refused to silence music from a procession that was passing in front of a mosque.[^cite_note-Nijjar_1996-52]

[HMS *Rawalpindi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Rawalpindi) was launched as an [ocean liner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_liner) in 1925 by [Harland and Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harland_and_Wolff), the same company which built [RMS *Titanic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic). The ship was converted into an armed vessel, and was sunk in October 1939. Scientists from [Porton Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porton_Down) carried out poison gas tests on [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) soldiers during the [Rawalpindi experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_experiments) over the course of more than a decade beginning in the 1930s.[^cite_note-53]

### Partition



Andaroon Raja Bazaar streets

On 5 March 1947, members of Rawalpindi's Hindu and Sikh communities took a procession against the formation of a Muslim League ministry within the Government of Punjab. Policemen fired upon protestors, while Hindus and Sikhs fought against weaker Muslim counter-protestors.[^cite_note-54] The area's first Partition riots erupted the next day on 6 March 1947, when the city's Muslims, angered by the actions of Hindus and Sikhs and encouraged by the Pir of [Golra Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golra_Sharif), raided nearby villages after they were unable to do so in the city on account of Rawalpindi's heavily armed Sikhs.[^cite_note-Noorani_2014-55] Thousands of Sikhs and Hindus were killed in villages surrounding Rawalpindi, in large scale and widespread violence that came to be known as the [Rawalpindi massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_massacres).[^cite_note-56]

At the dawn of Pakistan's independence in 1947 following the success of the [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement), Rawalpindi was 43.79% Muslim, while Rawalpindi District as a whole was 80% Muslim.[^cite_note-Ispahani_2017-57] The region, on account of its large Muslim majority, was thus awarded to Pakistan. Rawalpindi's [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) and [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) population, who had made up 33.72% and 17.32% of the city,[^cite_note-Ispahani_2017-57] migrated *en masse* to the newly independent [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India) after anti-Hindu and anti-Sikh pogroms in western Punjab, while Muslim refugees from India settled in the city following anti-Muslim pogroms in eastern Punjab and northern India.[^cite_note-Noorani_2014-55]

### Modern



Murree Road is one of the main arteries in Rawalpindi.



[Bahria Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahria_Town_Rawalpindi) is an affordable locality in Rawalpindi



Rehman Abad Clock Tower Rawalpindi

In the years following independence, Rawalpindi saw an influx of [Muhajir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhajir_\(Urdu-speaking_people\)), [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) and [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_people) settlers. Having been the largest British Cantonment in the region at the dawn of Pakistan's independence, Rawalpindi was chosen as headquarters for the Pakistani Army, despite the fact that Karachi had been selected as the first capital.[^cite_note-Burki_2015-22]

In 1951, the [Rawalpindi conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_conspiracy) took place in which leftist army officers conspired to depose the first elected [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md), [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan).[^cite_note-Burki_2015-22] Rawalpindi later became the site of the Liaquat Ali Khan's assassination, in what is now known as [Liaquat Bagh Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaqat_National_Bagh). In 1958, Field Marshal [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(Field_Marshal\)) launched his *[coup d'etat](25914154.md)* from Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-Burki_2015-22] In 1959, the city became the interim capital of the country under Ayub Khan, who had sought the creation of a new planned capital of Islamabad in the vicinity of Rawalpindi. As a result, Rawalpindi saw most major central government offices and institutions relocate to nearby territory, and its population rapidly expand.

The construction of Pakistan's new capital city of [Islamabad](51153.md) in 1961 led to greater investment in Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-Lonely_Planet_2016-27] Rawalpindi remained the headquarters of the [Pakistani Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Army) after the capital shifted to Islamabad in 1969, while the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) continues to maintain an airbase in the [Chaklala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaklala) district of Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] The military dictatorship of General [Zia ul Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_ul_Haq) hanged Pakistan's deposed [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister), [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto), in Rawalpindi in 1979.[^cite_note-60]

In 1980, tens of thousands of Shia protestors led by Mufti Jaffar Hussain marched to Rawalpindi to protest a provision of Zia ul Haqs Islamization programme.[^cite_note-Ispahani_2017-57] A spate of bombings in September 1987 took place in the city killing 5 people, in attacks that are believed to have been orchestrated by agents of Afghanistan's communist government.[^cite_note-61]

On 10 April 1988, Rawalpindi's [Ojhri Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojhri_Camp_disaster), an ammunition depot for Afghan [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) fighting against [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) forces in Afghanistan, exploded and killed many in Rawalpindi and Islamabad.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] At the time, the New York Times reported more than 93 were killed and another 1,100 wounded;[^cite_note-Gordon_1988-64] many believe that the toll was much higher.[^cite_note-65]

Riots erupted in Rawalpindi in 1992 as mobs attacked Hindu temples in retaliation for the destruction of the [Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babri_Masjid) in India.[^cite_note-Ispahani_2017-57] On 27 December 2007, Rawalpindi was the site of the [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto_assassination) of former [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Benazir Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto).[^cite_note-66]

Modern Rawalpindi is socially and economically intertwined with Islamabad, and the greater [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Rawalpindi_metropolitan_area). The city is also home to numerous suburban housing developments that serve as bedroom-communities for workers in Islamabad.[^cite_note-Abbasi_2015-28][^cite_note-Furniture_2016-29] In June 2015, the [Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi-Islamabad_Metrobus), a new [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) line with various points in [Islamabad](51153.md), opened for service.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Rawalpindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Rawalpindi)

Rawalpindi features a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification_system): Cwa)[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org_2015-67] with hot and wet summers, a cooler and drier winter. Rawalpindi and its twin city [Islamabad](51153.md), during the year experiences an average of 91 thunderstorms, which is the highest frequency of any plain elevation city in the country. Strong windstorms are frequent in the summer during which wind gusts have been reported by [Pakistan Meteorological Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Meteorological_Department) to have reached 176 km/h (109 mph). In such thunder/wind storms, which results in some damage of infrastructure.[^cite_note-68] The weather is highly variable due to the proximity of the city to the foothills of Himalayas.

The average annual rainfall is 1,254.8 mm (49.40 in), most of which falls in the summer monsoon season. However, westerly disturbances also bring quite significant rainfall in the winter. In summer, the record maximum temperature has soared to 47.7 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (118 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) recorded in June 1954, while it has dropped to a minimum of −3.9 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (25 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) several occasions, though the last of which was in January 1967.

Social structures in Rawalpindi's historic core centre around neighbourhoods, each known as a *Mohallah*. Each neighbourhood is served by a nearby bazaar (market) and mosque, which in turn serves as a place where people can gather for trade and manufacturing.[^cite_note-Hull_2013-70] Each *Mohallah* has narrow *gallies* (streets), and the grouping of houses around short lanes and cul-de-sacs lends a sense of privacy and security to residents of each neighbourhood. Major intersections in the neighbourhood are each referred to as a *chowk*.

Rawalpindi is relatively a new city contrasted with Pakistan's millennia-old cities such as [Lahore](125315.md), [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan), and [Peshawar](66230.md).[^cite_note-71] South of Rawalpindi's historic core, and across the Lai Nullah, are the wide lanes of the Rawalpindi Cantonment. With tree-lined avenues and historic architecture, the cantonment was the main European area developed during British colonial rule. British colonialists also built the Saddar Bazaar south of the historic core, which served as a retail center geared towards Europeans in the city. Beyond the cantonment are the large suburban housing developments that serve as bedroom communities for Islamabad's commuter population.[^cite_note-Hull_2013-70]

96.7% of Rawalpindi's population is Muslim, 3.1% is Christian, 0.2% belong to other religious groups. The city's *Kohaati Bazaar* is site of large [Shia](26961.md) mourning-processions for [Ashura](488563.md).[^cite_note-Rida_2015-77] The neighbourhoods of *Waris Shah Mohallah* and *Pir Harra Mohallah* form the core of Muslim settlement in Rawalpindi's old city.

Prior to partition there was a sizable [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) and [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) community living in Rawalpindi. Today, the city is still home to a few hundred Hindu families.[^cite_note-Dawn_2015-78] Despite the fact that the vast majority of the city's Hindus fled *en masse* to India after Partition, most Hindu temples in the old city remain standing, although in disrepair and often abandoned.[^cite_note-Dawn_2015-78] Many of the old city's neighbourhoods continue to bear Hindu and Sikh names, such as Krishanpura, Aria Mohallah, Akaal Garh, Mohanpura, Amarpura, Kartarpura, Bagh Sardaraan, Angatpura.

Rawalpindi was a majority [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) and [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) city prior to the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947,[^cite_note-Dawn_2015-78] combined composing 51.05 percent of the total population according to the 1941 census.[^cite_note-Census_of_India_1941-79]<sup>: 32 </sup> The same census detailed Muslims as the largest single group who made up 43.79 percent of the total population.[^cite_note-Ispahani_2017-57][^cite_note-Census_of_India_1941-79]<sup>: 32 </sup> The Baba Dyal Singh Gurdwara in Rawalpindi was where the reformist [Nirankari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirankari) movement of Sikhism originated.[^cite_note-Rida_2015-77] The city still has a small Sikh population, but has been bolstered by the arrival of Sikhs fleeing political instability in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md).[^cite_note-80]

There are 3 main active Hindu temples in the city- [Shri Krishna Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Temple,_Rawalpindi) in Saddar Cantonment, [Lal Kurti Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Kurti_Temple) in Lal Kurti area, and the [Valmiki Swamiji Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiki_Swamiji_Mandir,_Rawalpindi) in Gracy lines.[^cite_note-81]The [Shri Krishna Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Krishna_Mandir,_Rawalpindi) is the only major functional Hindu temple in Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-82] It was built in the Kabarri Bazaar in 1897.[^cite_note-Dawn_2015-78] Other temples are abandoned or were repurposed. Rawalpindi's large [Kalyan Das Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyan_Das_Temple) from 1880 has been used as the "Gov't. Qandeel Secondary School for the Blind" since 1973.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] The Ram Leela Temple in *Kanak Mandi*, and the Kaanji Mal Ujagar Mal Ram Richpal Temple in the Kabarri Bazaar, are both currently used to house Kashmiri refugees. Mohan Temple in the *Lunda Bazaar* remains standing, but is abandoned and the building no longer used for any purpose. The city's "Shamshan Ghat" serves as the city's cremation grounds, and was partly renovated in 2012.[^cite_note-85]

The city's *Babu Mohallah* neighbourhood was once home to a community of Jewish traders who had fled [Mashhad](406512.md), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) in the 1830s.[^cite_note-Jewish_History_2016-49]

In the British era many churches were built for the British soldiers to come to the churches for Sunday prayer because Rawalpindi Cantonment was the home for the [British Army](4887.md).[^cite_note-Jewish_History_2016-49][^cite_note-86]

## Transportation

### Public transportation



A Double road in Rawalpindi



Map of Pindi-Islamabad public transport

The [Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi-Islamabad_Metrobus) is a 48.1 km (29.9 mi) [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) system operating in the [Islamabad-Rawalpindi metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad-Rawalpindi_metropolitan_area). The Metrobus network's first phase was opened on 4 June 2015, and stretches 22.5 kilometres between [Pak Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pak_Secretariat), in [Islamabad](51153.md), and [Saddar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddar,_Rawalpindi) in Rawalpindi. The second stage stretches 25.6 kilometres between the [Peshawar Morr Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_Morr_Interchange) and [New Islamabad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Islamabad_International_Airport) and was inaugurated on 18 April 2022.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] The system uses e-ticketing and an [Intelligent Transportation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_Transportation_System) and is managed by the [Punjab Mass Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Mass_Transit_Authority).

The Rawalpindi Green Electric Bus Service, launched in November 2025, operates on key city routes for providing free travel for women, students, and senior citizens covering four main routes, including Saddar to Qabristan Chowk, Fawara Chowk to Koral Chowk, and others connecting major areas such as Mall Road and Peshawar Road which is expanding the facilitation to all other routes.[^cite_note-101]

### Road

Rawalpindi is situated along the historic [Grand Trunk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road) that connects Peshawar to [Islamabad](51153.md) and [Lahore](125315.md). The road is roughly paralleled by the [M-1 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) between Peshawar and Rawalpindi, while the [M-2 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-2_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) provides an alternate route to Lahore via the [Salt Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Range). The Grand Trunk Road also provides access to the Afghan border via the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass), with onwards connections to [Kabul](16826.md) and [Central Asia](6742.md) via the [Salang Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salang_Pass). The [Karakoram Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_Highway) provides access between Islamabad and western China, and an alternate route to Central Asia via [Kashgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashgar) in the Chinese region of [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang).

The [Islamabad Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Expressway) connects Rawalpindi's eastern portions with the [Rawal Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawal_Lake) and heart of [Islamabad](51153.md). The IJP Road separates Rawalpindi's northern edge from Islamabad.

### Motorways



The [M-2 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-2_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) connects Rawalpindi to [Lahore](125315.md), and is part of a network of motorways under construction that will continue to the port city of [Karachi](17123.md).

Rawalpindi is connected to [Peshawar](66230.md) by the [M-1 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_motorway_\(Pakistan\)). The motorway also links Rawalpindi to major cities in the [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) province, such as [Charsadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charsadda) and [Mardan](1844876.md). The [M-2 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-2_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) offers high speed access to Lahore via the [Potohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potohar_Plateau) and [Salt Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Range). The [M-3 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-3_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) branches off from the M-2 at the city of [Pindi Bhattian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pindi_Bhattian), where the M-3 offers onward connections to [Faisalabad](401880.md), and connects to the [M-4 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-4_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) which continues onward to [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan) and from there onwards to [Sukkur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukkur). A new motorway network is under construction to connect Sukkur and [Karachi](17123.md) as part of the [China Pakistan Economic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Pakistan_Economic_Corridor). The [Hazara Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E35_expressway_\(Pakistan\)) is also under construction as part of CPEC, and will provide control-access motorway travel all the way to [Mansehra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansehra) via the M-1 or Grand Trunk Road.

### Rail



Rawalpindi Railway Station

[Rawalpindi railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_railway_station) in the [Saddar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddar,_Rawalpindi) neighbourhood serves as a stop along Pakistan's 1,687 kilometres (1,048 mi)-long [Main Line-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi%E2%80%93Peshawar_Railway_Line) railway that connects the city to the port city of [Karachi](17123.md) to [Peshawar](66230.md). The stations is served by the [Awam Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awam_Express), [Hazara Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_Express), [Islamabad Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Express), [Jaffar Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffar_Express), [Khyber Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Mail_\(passenger_train\)) trains, and serves as the terminus for the [Margalla Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margalla_Express), [Mehr Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehr_Express), [Rawal Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawal_Express), [Pakistan Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Express), [Subak Raftar Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subak_Raftar_Express), [Green Line Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_Express), [Sir Syed Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Syed_Express?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Subak Kharam Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subak_Kharam_Express) and [Tezgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tezgam) trains.

The entire Main Line-1 railway track between Karachi and Peshawar is to be overhauled at a cost of \$3.65 billion for the first phase of the project,[^cite_note-102] with completion by 2021.[^cite_note-103] Upgrading of the railway line will permit train travel at speeds of 160 kilometres per hour, versus the average 60 to 105 km per hour speed currently possible on existing track.[^cite_note-104]

### Air

Rawalpindi is served by the [Islamabad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_International_Airport). The airport is located 21 km west of the city. It offers non-stop flights throughout Pakistan, as well as to the Middle East, Europe, North America, Central Asia, East Asia and Southeast Asia.

## Development Project

### Rawalpindi Ring Road

The Rawalpindi Ring Road (RRR) is a 38.3 km, 6-lane controlled-access highway project, aimed at bypassing city traffic from Banth (N-5) to Thalian (M-2). Key developments include 12 bridges, 11 overpasses, and 10 subways, to boost economic activity and real estate in the region.

### Kutchery Chowk Remodelling

The Kutchery Chowk Remodelling Project, a crucial Rs. 14–19 billion initiative in Rawalpindi, aims to resolve chronic traffic congestion by building a signal-free corridor with flyovers and underpasses. It connects key routes including GT Road, Jhelum Road, and Saddar, improving traffic flow for over 200,000 daily vehicles. New underpasses are planned at Race Course Park, Army Graveyard Square, and Chairing Cross, complementing the Kutchery Chowk project.

### Dadocha Dam project

The Dadocha Dam project, a major initiative to resolve Rawalpindi's water scarcity, is construction of Dam to meet the needs of the citizens of Rawalpindi. Located 25km from Rawalpindi, the Rs52.7B+ project with a supply of 35 MGD of water, include a recreation park, and create a migratory bird habitat. [^cite_note-105]

## Administrative divisions

The city of Rawalpindi is one of five [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil_\(Pakistan\)) (sub-districts) of [Rawalpindi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District). The city is sub-divided between a Metropolitan Corporation, two Cantonment Boards (or military towns), and a number of rural [union councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_council_\(Pakistan\)) (the lowest level of local government in Pakistan), the latter of which are directly governed by the Rawalpindi district council. The Metropolitan Corporation includes 78 urban union councils.[^cite_note-106]

## Parks



The gate of [Paharwala Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paharwala_Fort).

Ayub National Park is located beyond the old Presidency on [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_City) Road. It covers an area of about 2,300 acres (930 ha) and has a playland, lake with boating facility, an aquarium and a garden-restaurant. Rawalpindi Public Park is on [Murree Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto_Road) near Shamsabad. The Park was opened to the public in 1991. It has a playland for children, grassy lawns, fountains and flower beds.

In 2008 [Jinnah Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinnah_Park) was inaugurated at the heart of Rawalpindi and has since become a hotspot of activity for the city. It houses a state-of-the-art cinema, Cinepax,[^cite_note-111] a [Metro Cash and Carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Cash_and_Carry) supermart, an outlet of [McDonald's](2480627.md), gaming lounges, [Motion Rides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Rides) and other recreational facilities. The vast lawns also provide an adequate picnic spot.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]



A view of [Rawal Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawal_Lake)

Rawalpindi is situated near the *[Ayub National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_National_Park)* formerly known as 'Topi Rakh' (keep the hat on) is by the old Presidency, between the [Murree Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murree_Brewery) Co. and [Grand Trunk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road). It covers an area of about 2,300 acres (930 ha) and has a play area, lake with boating facility, an aquarium, a garden-restaurant and an open-air theater. This park hosts "[The Jungle Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_World,_Rawalpindi)" which is particularly popular among young residents.[^cite_note-114]

- [Liaquat Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Bagh), formerly known as the "company bagh" (East India Company's Garden), is of great historical interest. The first prime minister of Pakistan, [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan), was assassinated here in 1950. Pakistan's Prime Minister Benazir Bhutto was assassinated here on 27 December 2007. She was the youngest and the only woman to be elected as Prime Minister of Pakistan.

- Rawalpindi Public Park (previously Nawaz Sharif Park, renamed Iqbal Park in 2019

  [^cite_note-115]

  ) is located on Murree Road just opposite to the Pir Mehr Ali Shah Arid Agriculture University Rawalpindi. The park was opened in 1991. It has a play area for children, lawns, fountains and flower beds. A [cricket stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Cricket_Stadium) was built in 1992 opposite the public park. Several matches in the [1996 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Cricket_World_Cup) were held on this cricket ground.
