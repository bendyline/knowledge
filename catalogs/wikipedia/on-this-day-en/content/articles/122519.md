**Butler** is a city in and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Bates County, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bates_County,_Missouri), United States and is part of the [Kansas City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area). The population was 4,220 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Census). [^cite_note-GR6-6]The city is named for [William Orlando Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Orlando_Butler), an American military and political figure of the early and mid-19th century.[^cite_note-Ray-7] It is located approximately 50 mi (80 km) south of [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri) on [Interstate 49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_49).

## History

When laid out in April 1852, Butler was a short distance from its modern location, with John C. Kennett being recognized as the first settler to build a home.[^cite_note-Ray-7] The plat for Butler was filed in August, 1853 and consisted of five lots on fifty-five acres of donated land.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] The first county seat for Bates County was Papinville.

After a large portion of the county was split off to form [Vernon County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon_County,_Missouri) in 1855, Papinville was no longer near the geographic center, and Butler was selected in 1856 as the county seat.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] County officials shortly thereafter selected the contracting firm of Fitzpatrick & Hurt to construct a fifty-by-fifty foot brick courthouse at a cost of \$5,000. This building served the county until being gutted by fire in 1861.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] The year 1856 also saw the establishment of Butler's first general mercantile store was established in 1856, with several others following in the years prior to the Civil War's outbreak in 1861.

### Civil War

During the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) western Missouri was engulfed in insurgent Confederate actions and raids by Union forces. Personal conflicts were also played out in the war's violence. Early in the conflict a major fire destroyed not only the Bates County courthouse but nearly all of the surrounding square of businesses and the town's first church.[^cite_note-Ray-7] The arson fire was the handiwork of a squad of volunteer Kansas cavalry acting on orders of [Jayhawker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayhawker) [Colonel James Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Montgomery_\(colonel\)). A company of pro-[Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) [Missouri State Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_Guard) cavalry under [Sidney D. Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_D._Jackman) arrived too late to stop the arson, but they pursued the Kansans back across the border, killing and wounding several.[^cite_note-Ray-7] During much of the spring and summer of 1862, the town was occupied by [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) Colonel [Fitz Henry Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitz_Henry_Warren) and elements of the [1st Regiment Iowa Volunteer Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Regiment_Iowa_Volunteer_Cavalry). Arriving in April, they stayed until mid-August when they left for [Clinton, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton,_Missouri), followed closely by Confederate troops under Colonel Jackman and General [Vard Cockrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_V._Cockrell). The opposing groups would finally meet a few days later at the [Battle of Lone Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lone_Jack) in [Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_County,_Missouri) county.[^cite_note-Ray-7]



The Battle of Island Mound as depicted in an 1863 *Harper's Weekly* woodcut.

Two months later a Civil War milestone took place in Bates County at a site approximately eight miles southwest of Butler. This was the first combat against Confederates by African Americans fighting for the Union. During the [Battle of Island Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skirmish_at_Island_Mound) (aka "Battle of Fort Toothman") on October 28–29, 1862, the Union [1st Kansas Colored Volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Regiment_Kansas_Volunteer_Infantry_\(Colored\))—composed of former slaves who had escaped from [Arkansas](1930.md) and [Missouri](19571.md)—and a scouting element from the [5th Kansas Volunteer Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Regiment_Kansas_Volunteer_Cavalry) engaged a numerically superior force of Confederate guerrillas and recent Missouri State Guard recruits led by Vard Cockrell and Bill Truman (a relative of future President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman)). The Union forces crossed into Missouri on a mission to clear Confederate guerrillas from an area known as "Hog Island".[^cite_note-Battle-9] The Union troops commandeered and fortified the homestead of Confederate guerrilla Enoch John Toothman. The next day a detachment engaged with mounted Confederate cavalry, but stood their ground. The Confederates withdrew from the area on October 29. Eight Union men and an estimated 30 to 40 Confederates were killed.[^cite_note-Battle-9]

On August 25, 1863 [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) General [Thomas Ewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Ewing,_Jr.) issued his controversial [Order No. 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Order_No._11_\(1863\)), requiring evacuation of all rural residents from the county.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] Ostensibly it was a response to the raid on [Lawrence, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas) by [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army) guerrillas under [William Quantrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Quantrill) and a means to cut off materiel support for further [bushwhacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushwhacker) activity. Opponents believed it was a personal vendetta by Ewing, an abolitionist from Kansas who lost several friends in the raid. Order No. 11 called for the forced evacuation of the rural residents of Bates and three other border counties ([Cass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cass_County,_Missouri), Jackson, and Vernon) within 15 days of issuance.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] All rural residents of the affected counties, regardless of their allegiance, were forced to leave their farms and homes.

Those who could prove their loyalty to the Union were allowed to remain in the counties but required to move to communities near Union military outposts. Those unwilling or unable to prove loyalty were ordered to move from the counties altogether or face imprisonment. Among the forced evacuees were Solomon and Hattie (Gregg) Young and their daughter [Martha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Ellen_Young_Truman), the maternal grandparents and mother of future President [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman). Butler was left a virtual ghost town. When residents returned later near war's end, the town and much of the county as a whole had been burned, looted, and otherwise destroyed by regular Union forces, pro-Union Jayhawkers, and Kansas "Red Legs".

### Post-Bellum

A temporary structure for county business and court activities was constructed in late 1865 but proved inadequate. In 1869 a much larger two-story brick courthouse was constructed at a cost of \$23,000.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] While slowly continuing its recovery, Butler was finally incorporated as a village on June 19, 1872. Seven years later, on April 7, 1879 Butler was reincorporated as a fourth-class city, with William Page serving as the first mayor.[^cite_note-Ray-7] Numerous mills and grain elevators became part of the growing Butler business community through the 1870s, providing opportunities for surrounding farms to process wool and various grains.[^cite_note-Ray-7]

Butler gained electric power in 1881, being one of the first communities west of the Mississippi River to offer that convenience. Four large lights were mounted atop the Bates County courthouse, providing illumination for the downtown area. Butler became known as "The Electric City."[^cite_note-Juiced-1][^cite_note-Ray-7] The 1869 courthouse survived until 1899 when it was found to be structurally unsound; it was closed and demolished.[^cite_note-Chamber-8]

After passage of a bond issue, construction of the new [Bates County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bates_County_Courthouse) began in 1901. The eighty-by-one hundred five foot Romanesque-style structure was built of "Carthage stone" from southern Missouri at a total project cost of \$50,000 and was occupied in July, 1902.[^cite_note-Chamber-8] By the early 20th century Butler's business listings included three banks, an opera house, four hotels, and a large number of general and specialty stores.[^cite_note-Ray-7]

In 2008 Bates county residents erected a statue and plaque honoring the 1st Kansas Colored Volunteers and the Battle of Island Mound on the grounds of the Bates County courthouse in Butler.[^cite_note-Battle-9] The State of Missouri purchased a 40-acre property including part of the former Toothman homestead and established the "[Battle of Island Mound State Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Island_Mound_State_Historic_Site)" in October 2012.[^cite_note-Battle-9]

The Bates County Courthouse and [Palace Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Hotel_\(Butler,_Missouri\)) are listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).[^cite_note-nris-10]

A field near the Butler Memorial Airport was the site of a skydiving plane crash on June 14, 2026.

## Geography

The city is on [US Route 71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Route_71) and [Missouri Route 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Route_52). [Rich Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Hill,_Missouri) is ten miles south and [Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian,_Missouri) is 8.5 miles north.[^cite_note-11]

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 4.08 square miles (10.57 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 4.07 square miles (10.54 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 0.01 square miles (0.03 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_files-12]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Butler had a population of 4,220. The median age was 38.4 years. 23.9% of residents were under the age of 18 and 20.7% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 97.5 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 92.6 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-16][^cite_note-Census2020PL-17]

0.0% of residents lived in urban areas, while 100.0% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-18]

There were 1,685 households in Butler, of which 30.4% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 38.9% were married-couple households, 18.3% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 33.4% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 36.6% of all households were made up of individuals and 19.1% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-16]

There were 1,908 housing units, of which 11.7% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 6.1% and the rental vacancy rate was 9.8%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-16]

### 2010 census

As of the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-wwwcensusgov-5] of 2010, there were 4,219 people, 1,739 households, and 1,031 families living in the city. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 1,036.6 inhabitants per square mile (400.2/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 2,047 housing units at an average density of 502.9 per square mile (194.2/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the city was 94.5% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 2.7% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.5% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.2% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.5% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(U.S._Census\)), and 1.7% from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race were 2.3% of the population.

There were 1,739 households, of which 29.7% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 42.8% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) living together, 12.2% had a female householder with no husband present, 4.3% had a male householder with no wife present, and 40.7% were non-families. Of all households, 35.5% were made up of individuals, and 17.6% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.30 and the average family size was 2.97.

The median age in the city was 39.3 years. 24.1% of residents were under the age of 18; 7.9% were between the ages of 18 and 24; 23.2% were from 25 to 44; 23.6% were from 45 to 64; and 20.9% were 65 years of age or older. The gender makeup of the city was 46.8% male and 53.2% female.

### 2000 census

As of the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-wwwcensusgov-5] of 2000, there were 4,209 people, 1,723 households, and 1,047 families living in the city. The population density was 1,089.5 inhabitants per square mile (420.7/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 1,914 housing units at an average density of 495.4 per square mile (191.3/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the city was 95.65% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.92% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.43% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.38% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.02% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.55% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 1.05% from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race were 0.97% of the population.

## Education

Butler R-V School District operates one elementary school and Butler High School.[^cite_note-19] The elementary school serves children from kindergarten to 5th grade, and the high school oversees grades 6 to 12.

The town has a lending library, the Butler Public Library.[^cite_note-20]
