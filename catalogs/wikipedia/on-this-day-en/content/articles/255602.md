**Pampanga**, officially the **Province of Pampanga** ([Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language): *Lalawigan ning Pampanga*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Lalawigan ng Pampanga*), is a [province](165100.md) in [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), [Philippines](23440.md). Lying on the northern shore of [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay), Pampanga is bordered by [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) to the north, [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) to the northeast, [Bulacan](252408.md) to the east, Manila Bay to the south, [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan) to the southwest, and [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) to the west. Its capital is [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga), the regional center of Central Luzon. [Angeles City](842728.md) is the largest city in Pampanga but is administratively independent. It has been self-governing since receiving its charter in 1964.

The name *La Pampanga* was given by the Spaniards, who found natives living along the banks (*pampáng*) of the [Pampanga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga_River). It was created in 1571 as the first Spanish province on Luzon (the province of [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_\(province\)) in the [Visayas](72418.md) is older, founded in 1565). The town of [Villa de Bacolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolor) briefly served as the Spanish colonial capital when [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) occupied [Manila](184334.md) during the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War). On the eve of the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) in 1896, Pampanga was one of eight provinces placed under [martial law](50477.md) for rebelling against the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire). It is represented on the [Flag of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Philippines) by one of the eight rays of the sun.

Pampanga is served by [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport) (formerly *Diosdado Macapagal International Airport*), which is in [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone), some 16 kilometers (9.9 mi) north of the provincial capital. The province is home to two [Philippine Air Force](27918720.md) [airbases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbase): [Basa Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basa_Air_Base) in [Floridablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floridablanca,_Pampanga) and the former United States [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) in Angeles. Due to its growing population and developments, the Clark Global City is now being developed and is located in [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone). In 2015, the province had 2,198,110 inhabitants,[^cite_note-PSA15–03-4] while it had 1,079,532 registered voters.[^cite_note-5]

The territorial area of old Pampanga included portions of the modern provinces of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac), [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan), [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales), [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), [Bulacan](252408.md) and [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)); i.e. covered almost the entire Central Luzon.[^cite_note-Tant1-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-lang49-61-8][^cite_note-mega-Pampanga-9] When the Spanish arrived at Luzon, they found Pampanga to be thickly populated with several towns and that there were 3 castles or forts protecting Pampanga.[^cite_note-10] Pampanga was re-organized as a province by the Spaniards on December 11, 1571. La Provincia de La Pampanga included areas mentioned above except Tondo, along with modern provinces of Aurora and parts of [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) (including [Polillo Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polillo_Islands)) and [Rizal](232458.md) (Pampanga also included portions of [Metro Manila](72340.md), which are [Novaliches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaliches) and [Valenzuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valenzuela,_Metro_Manila) to be exact, which was formerly known as Polo, then towns in Bulacan).[^cite_note-Tant1-6][^cite_note-mega-Pampanga-9] For better administration and taxation purposes, the Spanish authorities subdivided Pampanga into *pueblos*, which were further subdivided into districts (*[barrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay)*) and in some cases into royal and private estates (*[encomiendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda)*).

Due to excessive abuses committed by some *encomenderos*, King [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) in 1574 prohibited the further awarding of private estates, but this decree was not fully enforced until 1620. In a report of [Philippine](23440.md) encomiendas on June 20, 1591, [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines) [Gómez Pérez Dasmariñas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B3mez_P%C3%A9rez_Dasmari%C3%B1as) reported to the Crown that La Pampanga's encomiendas were Bataan, Betis y Lubao, Macabebe, Candaba, Apalit, Calumpit, Malolos, Binto, Guiguinto, Caluya, Bulacan and [Mecabayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meycauayan). The encomiendas of La Pampanga at that time had eighteen thousand six hundred and eighty whole tributes.

Pampanga, which is about 850 square miles (2,200 km<sup>2</sup>) in area and inhabited by more than 1.5 million people, had its present borders drawn in 1873. During the Spanish regime, it was one of the richest Philippine provinces. [Manila](184334.md) and its surrounding region were then primarily dependent on Kapampangan agricultural, fishery and forestry products as well as on the supply of skilled workers. As other Luzon provinces were created due to increases in population, some well-established Pampanga towns were lost to new emerging provinces in Central Luzon.

During the 17th century, the [Dutch](23476997.md) recruited men from Pampanga as mercenaries who served the [Royal Netherlands East Indies Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_East_Indies_Army), known as [Papangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papanggo)[^cite_note-11] part of the larger [Mardijkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardijker_people) community. Their legacy can be found in [North Jakarta](2728733.md);[^cite_note-12] however, there are few traces of their descendants, except for a small community in [Kampung Tugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koja,_Jakarta#Kampung_Tugu).

The historic province of Bataan which was founded in 1754 under the administration of Spanish [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines) [Pedro Manuel Arandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Manuel_de_Arand%C3%ADa_Santisteban), absorbed from the province of Pampanga the municipalities of Abucay, Balanga (now a city), Dinalupihan, Llana Hermosa, Orani, Orion, Pilar, and Samal. During the [British occupation of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Manila) (1762–1764), [Bacolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolor) became the provisional Spanish colonial capital and military base. By the end of the 1700s, Pampanga had 16,604 native families and 2,641 [Spanish Filipino families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos),[^cite_note-Estadismo1-13]<sup>: 539 </sup>[^cite_note-Estadismo2-14]<sup>: 31, 54, 113 </sup> and 870 [Chinese Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipinos) families.[^cite_note-Estadismo1-13]<sup>: 460 </sup> In Rafael Bernal's book: *Mexico En Filipinas*, Pampanga was listed among other places which include Cavite and Masbate, as areas where there were large concentrations of Mexican immigrants to the Philippines.[^cite_note-15]

The old Pampanga towns of Aliaga, Cabiao, Gapan, San Antonio and San Isidro were ceded to the province of Nueva Ecija in 1848 during the term of Spanish Governor-General [Narciso Claveria y Zaldua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narciso_Claveria_y_Zaldua). The municipality of *San Miguel de Mayumo* of Pampanga was yielded to the province of Bulacan in the same provincial boundary configuration in 1848.

In 1860, the northern towns of Bamban, Capas, Concepcion, Victoria, Tarlac, Mabalacat, Magalang, Porac and Floridablanca were separated from Pampanga and were placed under the jurisdiction of a military command called *Comandancia Militar de Tarlac*. However, in 1873, the four latter towns were returned to Pampanga and the other five became municipalities of the newly created province of Tarlac.

### Japanese invasion

#### Attack on Clark Field

Main article: [Attack on Clark Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Clark_Field)

On December 8, 1941, Japanese planes bombed [Clark Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) in what has come to be known as the [Attack on Clark Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Clark_Field), marking the beginning of the invasion of Pampanga.

In keeping with [War Plan Orange-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Plan_Orange), Douglas McArthur ordered the evacuation of [Fort Stotsenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Stotsenburg) on December 24, 1941, and what could not be evacuated of the fort's stocks of high octane fuel, food, and clothing were destroyed.

Between 1941 and 1942, occupying Japanese forces began entering Pampanga.

#### The Japanese occupation

The establishment of the military general headquarters and military camp bases of the [Philippine Commonwealth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) was active from 1935 to 1946. The [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) was active from 1935 to 1942 and 1944 to 1946 in the province of Pampanga. During the military engagements of the anti-Japanese Imperial military operations in central Luzon from 1942 to 1945 in the province of Bataan, Bulacan, Northern Tayabas (now Aurora), Nueva Ecija, Pampanga, Tarlac, and Zambales, the local guerrilla resistance fighters and Hukbalahap Communist guerrillas, helped the U.S. military forces fight the [Imperial Japanese armed forces.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_armed_forces.?action=edit\&redlink=1)

#### The Liberation of Pampanga

In the 1945 liberation of Pampanga, Kapampangan guerrilla fighters and the Hukbalahap Communist guerrillas supported combat forces from Filipino and American ground troops in attacking Japanese Imperial forces during the Battle of Pampanga until the end of the Second World War. Local military operations soldiers and officers of the Philippine Commonwealth Army 2nd, 26th, 3rd, 32nd, 33rd, 35th, 36th and 37th Infantry Division and the Philippine Constabulary 3rd Constabulary Regiment recaptured and liberated the province of Pampanga and fought against the Japanese Imperial forces during the Battle of Pampanga.

### Post-war era



Old seal of the province since 1950



[Flag seal of Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_provinces_of_the_Philippines)

After the Second World War, operations in the main province of Pampanga was downfall insurgencies and conflicts between the Philippine Government forces and the Hukbalahap Communist rebels on 1946 to 1954 during the [Hukbalahap Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukbalahap_Rebellion).

Under a 1947 Military Bases Agreement,[^cite_note-16] the Philippines granted the United States a 99-year lease on several U.S. bases, including [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base).[^cite_note-17] A later amendment in 1966 reduced the original 99-year term of the agreement to 25 years.[^cite_note-NYTimes1991-09-16-18] A renewal of the agreement in 1979[^cite_note-19] allowed the U.S. to continue operating Clark Air Base until November 1991,[^cite_note-20] when the Philippine Senate rejected a bill for the renewal of U.S. bases in the Philippines.[^cite_note-NYTimes1991-09-16-18]

#### Clark Air Base controversies

Due to its proximity to the capital and the presence of Clark Air Base, Pampanga was became one of the flashpoint of social upheavals of the early 1970s, and the ensuing [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) of [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch2-22] Even during the first demonstrations of the [First Quarter Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Quarter_Storm) in 1970, Clark and the other [US Bases in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Bases_in_the_Philippines) were a major issue for protesters, who saw them as a continuation of the US' colonial hegemony, and a way of dragging the Philippines into the cold war, since Clark had become a staging point for the increasingly unpopular [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch2-22][^cite_note-Bonner1987-24]

1970 saw a major diplomatic incident at Clark Air Base in what US Presidential Assistant for National Security Affairs [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger) eventually called "the Williams Case."[^cite_note-25] In June 1970, Angeles City Court of First Instance Ceferino Gaddi ordered the arrest of Base Commander Colonel Averill Holman and Base Chief of International Law Lt. Col. Raymond Hodges, citing in contempt for allowing the transfer of US Air Force Staff Sergeant Bernard Willams to the US in November 1969 despite the fact that he had been arraigned in the Angeles Court in August 1969 on criminal charges of abduction and attempted rape.[^cite_note-26] Williams was eventually returned to the Philippines,[^cite_note-27] although the Marcos administration refused to enforce the court ordered arrests against Holman and Hodges.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileGaddi-28] The incident helped the push for the renegotiation of the US-Philippines Bases Treaty in 1979, in an effort to clarify the issue of Philippine sovereignty and jurisdiction over the bases.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileGaddi-28]

In 1971, unfair labor practices led the civilian workers at the base to stage a three-day strike which began on March 3, followed by a half-month strike which began on July 25.[^cite_note-Clarklab_history-29] The Federation of Filipino Civilian Employees Associations would organize further major strikes in 1979, 1983, and in March 1986.[^cite_note-Clarklab_history-29][^cite_note-30]

#### Implementation of martial law

Upon the declaration of martial law in September 1972, [Camp Olivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Olivas) in the City of San Fernando was designated as one of the four provincial camps to become a Regional Command for Detainees (RECAD). It was designated RECAD I and it housed detainees from Northern and Central Luzon.[^cite_note-asa350191977en-31] Prominent detainees imprisoned there include Edicio de la Torre,[^cite_note-32] [Judy Taguiwalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Taguiwalo),[^cite_note-33] Tina Pargas,[^cite_note-34] [Marie Hilao-Enriquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Hilao-Enriquez),[^cite_note-35] and Bernard-Adan Ebuen.[^cite_note-36] Prisoners who were documented to have been tortured include the sisters [Joanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Cari%C3%B1o) and Josefina Cariño,[^cite_note-nordisjoannacarino-37] the brothers Romulo and [Armando Palabay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Palabay),[^cite_note-asa350191977en-31] and Mariano Giner Jr of Abra.[^cite_note-asa350191977en-31] About 50 [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_people) and [Bontoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bontoc_people) leaders, including Butbut tribe leader [Macli-ing Dulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macli-ing_Dulag), were also brought to Camp Olivas from their detainment center in Tabuk, Kalinga, arrested for their opposition to the [Chico River Dam Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_River_Dam_Project).[^cite_note-Cariño1980-38] Seminarian Teresito Sison had campaigned for the rights of teachers, farmers, and of laborers in Clark Air Base, but torture during two stints in Marcos' detention centers caused a decline in his health which led to his death in 1980.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileSitoSison-39]

Others were killed without being arrested, such as close friends Pepito Deheran, Rolando Castro and Lito Cabrera were sleeping in Cabrera's property in Sapang Bato, Angeles when they were attacked, captured, and tortured by Marcos' Civilian Home Defense Force militia forces after they participated in the protest movement that grew out of the [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ninoy_Aquino) of opposition leader [Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino). Deheran managed to escape the ordeal alive and was taken to the hospital, but was stabbed by unknown assailants in his own hospital bed.[^cite_note-40]

Jennifer Cariño, the Palabay brothers, Macli-ing Dulag, Castro, Cabrera, and Deheran would later be honored by having their names inscribed on the wall of remembrance of the Philippines' [Bantayog ng mga Bayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayog_ng_mga_Bayani), which honors the martyrs and heroes who dared to resist the dictatorship.[^cite_note-41]

#### 1980 Assassination of Congressman Lingad

[Jose B. Lingad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_B._Lingad), congressman for [Pampanga's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga's_1st_congressional_district), had been one of the first to be detained upon the declaration of martial law, but was released after three months.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileLingad-42] He retired to his farm after that, but was later convinced by Benigno Aquino Jr. to run for the governorship of Pampanga in the [January 1980 Philippine local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Philippine_local_elections).[^cite_note-BantayogProfileLingad-42] Lingad lost the election to the Marcos administration's candidate, but filed a protest, which was still ongoing when Lingad was assassinated in December 1980 at a gasoline station in barangay San Agustin, [San Fernando, Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga).[^cite_note-43] His assassin, a former sergeant in the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary), was himself killed in a mysterious car accident before he could reveal who had ordered the killing.[^cite_note-hhenares-44]

### During the People Power revolution

Main article: [People Power revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_revolution)

#### The Bamban barricade

Central Luzon played an important role during the People Power revolution because of its proximity to the Capital and its hosting of various Philippine and US Armed Force Bases. In a notable incident in Pampanga, 20,000 people blocked the Bamban Bridge on Feb. 25 to prevent loyalist forces from the Army's 5th Infantry Division in Tarlac from getting through to Manila, in an event which came to be known as "the Bamban barricade."[^cite_note-Lacorte2022-45]

#### The flight of the Marcoses

[Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) also played a key part in the ousting of the Marcoses, since they were flown to Clark after fleeing Malacañang and it was there that they boarded the U.S. provided plane that flew them into exile in Hawaii.[^cite_note-Bonner1987-24][^cite_note-AmBoy1999-46]

### Mount Pinatubo eruption and closure of Clark Air Base

Main articles: [1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo) and [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base)

Major events that took place in Pampanga after the People Power revolution include the Mount Pinatubo eruption and the end of the Philippines' Bases Treaty with the United States, which resulted in the closure of Clark Air Base and the later creation of the [Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_and_Special_Economic_Zone).

The June 15, 1991, eruption of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) displaced a large number of people with the submersion of whole towns and villages by massive [lahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) floods. This led to a large-scale advancement in disaster preparation in government. It also hastened the closure of Clark Air Base, which would close as a result of the November 1991 decision of the Philippine senate not to renew the Philippines' Bases treaty with the United States.[^cite_note-NYTimes1991-09-16-18]

### Creation of the Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone

Main article: [Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_and_Special_Economic_Zone)

1992 saw the signing of the Bases Conversion and Development Act (Republic Act 7227 ser. 1992), which authorized the President to issue a decree converting the military reservation in the Clark area covering [Angeles City](842728.md), [Mabalacat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabalacat), and [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac), Pampanga and [Capas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capas), [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) into a special economic zone. The legislation also created the [Bases Conversion and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bases_Conversion_and_Development_Authority) (BCDA) to facilitate the conversion process.[^cite_note-47] President [Fidel Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Ramos) issued Proclamation No. 163 on April 3, 1993, creating the Clark Special Economic Zone (CSEZ) and transferring the administration of the area to the BCDA. The proclamation included the [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) and portions of the Clark reverted baselands not reserved for military use to the CSEZ.[^cite_note-proc1035-48] On June 14, 1996, the CSEZ was expanded with the addition of the Sacobia area, which includes lands from [Mabalacat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabalacat), Pampanga and [Bamban, Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamban,_Tarlac), through Ramos' Proclamation No. 805.[^cite_note-proc1035-48]

The Clark Air Base area would later be declared a Freeport Zone and was separated from the special economic zone through Republic Act 9400 of 2007[^cite_note-repa9400-49] Since then the Freeport Zone and the Clark Special Economic Zone were considered as separate areas but collectively they are referred to as the "Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone".

### Contemporary

In 2010, a Kapampangan, [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III), son of former President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino), was elected as president.

On April 22, 2019, the province suffered severe damage due to [6.1 magnitude earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Luzon_earthquake) which originated from [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) and was the most affected area by the earthquake due to the province sitting on soft sediment and [alluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial) soil.[^cite_note-quake005-50] Several structures in the province were damaged by the quake, including a 4-story supermarket in Porac, the [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan)-Pampanga boundary arch and the main terminal of [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport), as well as old churches in [Lubao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubao,_Pampanga) and [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac,_Pampanga), where the stone bell tower of the 19th-century [Santa Catalina de Alejandria Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Catalina_de_Alejandria_Church_\(Porac\)) collapsed.[^cite_note-51]

## Geography

Pampanga covers a total area of 2,002.20 square kilometers (773.05 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-52] occupying the south-central section of the [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) region. When Angeles is included for geographical purposes, the province's area is 2,062.47 square kilometers (796.32 sq mi).[^cite_note-nscb-52] The province is bordered by [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) to the north, [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) to the northeast, [Bulacan](252408.md) to the east, [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay) to the central-south, [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan) to the southwest, and [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) to the northwest.

Its terrain is relatively flat with one distinct mountain, [Mount Arayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Arayat) and the notable [Pampanga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga_River). Among its municipalities, [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac,_Pampanga) has the largest area with 314 square kilometers (121 sq mi); [Candaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candaba,_Pampanga) comes in second with 176 square kilometers (68 sq mi); followed by [Floridablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floridablanca,_Pampanga) with 175 square kilometers (68 sq mi). [Santo Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Tomas,_Pampanga), with an area of only 21 square kilometers (8.1 sq mi), is the smallest.[^cite_note-nscb-52]

### Climate

The province of Pampanga has two distinct climates, rainy and dry. The rainy or wet season normally begins in May and runs through October, while the rest of the year is the dry season. The warmest period of the year occurs between March and April, while the coolest period is from December through February. The wet season will be from June to October and also the dry season will be from November to April in the province of Pampanga.

### Population

The population of Pampanga in the 2024 census was 2,586,446 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–03-54] with a density of 1,300 inhabitants per square kilometer or 3,400 inhabitants per square mile. If Angeles is included for geographical purposes, the population is 2,900,637, with a density of 1,406/km<sup>2</sup> (3,643/sq mi). The native inhabitants of Pampanga are generally referred to as the [Kapampangans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people) (alternatively *Pampangos* or *Pampangueños*). Tagalogs live in areas on the boundaries with Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, & Bataan; they are mostly descendants of settlers arrived from those provinces, w/ others from [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)). Because of its proximity to Metro Manila from its south, people from farther provinces settled in Pampanga, resulting to minor populations of [Bicolano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicolano_people), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people), [Ilonggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people), [Pangasinense](1691800.md) and [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans), mostly living in city areas.

### Languages

See also: [Kapampangan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), [Tagalog language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), and [Sambal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_language)

The whole population of Pampanga speak [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), which is one of the [Central Luzon languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon_languages) along with the [Sambalic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalic_languages). Tagalog is generally spoken in areas bordering Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, and Bataan. [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English) and [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) are also spoken and used as secondary languages. A language shift to Filipino has been seen in the province,[^cite_note-guerrerodelapaz-56][^cite_note-lang49-61-8] this is because of proximity to the native Tagalog-speaking provinces and migration from those, and also of influence of mass media. There are a few [Sambal speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_language) in the province, especially near the border of [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales).

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines) (0.017%) is also present in the province, mainly due to migrants originating from [the south](20546.md), as well as [Buddhism](3267529.md), which is practiced by a few people of [Chinese descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Chinese).

#### Iglesia Ni Cristo

The [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) is the largest majority religion in the province (5.14-7.5%) and the first Ecclesiastical District in the history of the Church. At the time of Bro. Eduardo V. Manalo, current Executive Minister, the province has grown in five districts with multiple locales scattered in the cities and municipalities.

According to 2010 Census, other prominent Christian groups includes [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicals) (1.34%), [Aglipayan Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aglipayan_Church) (0.60%), [Jesus is Lord Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_is_Lord_Church) (0.48%), [Baptist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_Church) (0.39%), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) (0.27%), Church of Christ (0.23%), United Church of Christ in the Philippines (0.22%), [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md) (0.18%) and many others.

Farming and fishing are the two main industries. Major products include rice, corn, sugarcane, and tilapia. Pampanga is the tilapia capital of the country because of its high production reaching 214,210.12 metric tons in 2015. In addition to farming and fishing, the province supports thriving cottage industries that specialize in wood carving, furniture making, guitars and handicrafts. Every [Christmas](6237.md) season, the province of Pampanga, especially in the capital city of [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga) becomes the center of a thriving industry centered on handcrafted lighted lanterns called *parols* that display a kaleidoscope of light and color. Other industries include the casket industry and the manufacturing of all-purpose vehicles in the municipality of Santo Tomas.

The province is famous for its sophisticated culinary work: it is called the "food capital" of the Philippines.

In December 2024, the [Senate](299521.md) declared Pampanga as the country's [culinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culinary) capital.[^cite_note-66] The [Senate](299521.md) approved the [Third Reading](4062253.md) [Senate Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) 2797, authored by Senators [Mark Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Villar), [Loren Legarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loren_Legarda) and [Lito Lapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lito_Lapid). Its counterpart, authored by Representatives [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) and [Aurelio Gonzales Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelio_Gonzales_Jr.) had also been approved by the [House of Representatives](293605.md).[^cite_note-67]

Kapampangans are well known for their culinary creations. Famous food products range from the mundane to the exotic. Roel's Meat Products, Pampanga's Best and Mekeni Food are among the better known meat brands of the country producing Kapampangan favorites such as pork and chicken [tocinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocino), [beef tapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef_tapa), hotdogs, [longganizas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longaniza#Philippines) (Philippine-style cured sausages) and [chorizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorizo).

Specialty foods such as the [siopao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siopao), [pandesal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandesal), tutong, [lechon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechon) (roasted pig) and its sarsa (sauce) are popular specialty foods in the region. The more exotic *betute tugak* (stuffed frog), *kamaru* (mole crickets) cooked adobo, *bulanglang* (pork cooked in guava juice), *[lechon kawali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechon_kawali)* and *[bringhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bringhe)* (a green sticky rice dish like paella) are a mainstay in Kapampangan feasts.

Native sweets and delicacies like pastillas, turonnes de casuy, buro, are the most sought after by Filipinos including a growing number of tourists who enjoy authentic Kapampangan cuisine. The famous cookie in Mexico, Pampanga, *Panecillos de San Nicolas*, which is known as the mother of all Philippine cookies, is made here, famously made by [Lillian Borromeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atching_Lillian) at her restaurant, [Kusinang Matua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusinang_Matua).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-\:0-69] The cookies are made with arrowroot, sugar, coconut milk and butter and are blessed in Catholic parishes every year on the feast of San Nicolas Tolentino.[^cite_note-The_Daily_Meal-70] The cookies are believed to have a healing power and bestow good luck and are sometimes crumbled then thrown into rice fields before planting.[^cite_note-The_Daily_Meal-70]

Tourism is a growing industry in the province of Pampanga. Clark Freeport Zone is home to [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport), designated as the Philippines' future premier gateway. Other developing industries include semiconductor manufacturing for electronics and computers mostly located within the freeport.

Within the Clark Special Economic Zone are well-established hotels and resorts. Popular tourist destinations include St. Peter Shrine in Apalit, Mt. Arayat National Park in San Juan Bano, Mount Arayat, the [Paskuhan Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paskuhan_Village) in the City of San Fernando, the Casino Filipino in Angeles and, for nature and wildlife, "Paradise Ranch and Zoocobia Fun Zoo" in Clark. Well-known annual events include the [Giant Lantern Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Lantern_Festival) in December, the [hot air balloon festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon_festival) in Clarkfield in February and in Lubao in April, the [San Pedro Cutud Lenten Rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Cutud_Lenten_Rites) celebrated two days before Easter, and the Aguman Sanduk in [Minalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minalin) celebrated on the afternoon of New Year's Day.

Renewable energy initiatives in Pampanga include rooftop and utility-scale solar projects. In the City of San Fernando, the Bureau of Internal Revenue's Revenue Region 4 complex, inaugurated in March 2018, incorporates a rooftop solar photovoltaic system of about 400 kW as part of its “green building” features.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] In May 2025, listed developer Raslag Corp. signed a 10-year power supply agreement to deliver 15 MW to Pampanga I Electric Cooperative (PELCO I), sourced from its Raslag-4 plant in Magalang.[^cite_note-73] In February 2025, the Board of Investments granted a green lane endorsement to Sapang Balen Solar Sustainable Energy Corp., a Tigon Power unit, for a proposed ground-mounted project spanning Mabalacat and Magalang with a planned installed capacity of 302 MW (DC).[^cite_note-74] Additional municipal-level projects include Angeles City's program to install solar panels across city facilities and schools. In August 2024 the city began installations in public schools, and by April 2025 local reports stated that panels had been completed on all 33 barangay halls and various government buildings.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] In March 2025, the city inaugurated what was reported as Central Luzon's first solar-powered, fully air-conditioned public school at Belen Homesite Elementary School.[^cite_note-77] The Arayat–Mexico solar farm, a 116 MW facility developed by ACEN and Citicore, began commercial operations in 2022.[^cite_note-78]

There have been proposals to revitalize the [karakoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoa) shipbuilding tradition of the Kapampangan people in recent years. The karakoa was the warship of the Kapampangan from the classical eras (before 15th century) up to the 16th century. The production of the karakoa and its usage were stopped by the Spanish colonialists to establish the galleon ship-making tradition instead, as a sign of Spanish dominance over the Kapampangan.

## Wildlife

Pampanga's geography has made the province an important rallying point for biodiversity conservation, particularly in the case of the Candaba Wetlands, which provides critical habitat for migratory bird species which visit the Philippines from sites further north in Asia. Migratory birds frequently seen in the swamp include the Shrenck's bittern, the great bittern, the Eurasian spoonbill, the purple swamp wen, the Chinese pond heron and the black-crowned heron.[^cite_note-79]

## Infrastructure

### Telecommunication

Telephone services are provided by [PLDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLDT), [Digitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Telecommunications_Philippines), Converge Telecom, Datelcom, the Evangelista Telephone Company, and the Pampanga Telecom Company in the town of Macabebe. The province has 24 public telegraph offices distributed among its towns while the facilities of PT\&T and RCPI were set up to serve the business centers in Angeles, San Fernando City and Guagua.[^cite_note-T1-80]

Several Internet Service provider are available. These include the Angeles Computer Network Specialist, Information Resources Network System, Inc., Mosaic communications Inc., Net Asia Angeles, Phil World On Line and Comclark Network and Technology Corp.

[United Parcel Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Parcel_Service) (UPS) and [Federal Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Express) (FedEx) provide international courier services. Their hubs are in the Clark Freeport Zone. They are complemented by four local couriers operating as the communication and baggage of the province. There are three postal district offices and 35 post office stations distributed in the 20 municipalities and two cities of the province.[^cite_note-T2-81]

### Water and power



Pampanga electrical cooperative service areas

Potable water supply in the province reaches the populace through three levels: Level I (point source system), Level II (communal faucet system), and Level III (individual connections). A well or spring is the pinpointed water source in areas where houses are few as the system is only designed to serve 15 to 25 households. As of 1997, there were 128,571 Level I water system users in the province. The communal faucet system (Level II) serves the rural areas while the Level III system is managed by the [Local Water Utilities Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Water_Utilities_Administration) (LWUA). The system provides individual house connections to all second and first class private subdivisions.

Electric power is distributed to the majority of the towns through the distribution centers of the Pampanga Electric Cooperative (PELCO) which include PELCO I, II, III. Small parts of Candaba and Apalit are also supplied by [Manila Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meralco) (Meralco). Angeles and small parts of Mabalacat are supplied by Angeles Electric Corporation (AEC) Villa de Bacolor, Guagua, Sta, Rita, Lubao, Sasmuan, Porac, Mabalacat and small part of Floridablanca are supplied by Pampanga Electric Cooperative II (PELCO II). City of San Fernando and Floridablanca is supplied by San Fernando Electric Company (SFELAPCO).[^cite_note-T1-80]

Power is also transmitted to the province through various transmission lines and substations located within the province, such as the Mexico and Clark substations, and Hermosa–Duhat–Balintawak, Mexico–Hermosa, and Hermosa–San Jose transmission lines, all of which are operated and maintained by the [National Grid Corporation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Grid_Corporation_of_the_Philippines) (NGCP).

In 2018, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Regional Office 4 in San Fernando commissioned a 400-kilowatt rooftop solar installation. The project was implemented to reduce operating costs and support government sustainability initiatives.[^cite_note-Saur2019-82]

### Transportation

The province of Pampanga is strategically located at the crossroads of central Luzon and is highly accessible by air and land. The province is home to two airstrips: [Basa Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basa_Air_Base) in [Floridablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floridablanca,_Pampanga), which is used by the military, and [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport) in [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone). Pampanga has five municipal ports that function as fish landing centers. These are in the municipalities of Guagua, Macabebe, Masantol, [Minalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minalin), and Sasmuan.[^cite_note-T1-80]

#### Road transport

Land travel to Pampanga is provided by highways and by buses. Buses that travel the routes of Manila-Bataan, Manila-Zambales, Manila-Tarlac, Manila-Nueva Ecija, Manila-Bulacan-Pampanga, and Manila-Pampanga-Dagupan serve as connections with the nearby provinces and Metro Manila.

The 84 kilometers (52 mi) [North Luzon Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Luzon_Expressway) (NLEX) extends from Balintawak in [Quezon City](284223.md), Metro Manila, to Santa Ines in [Mabalacat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabalacat). It passes through the cities and municipalities of [Apalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apalit), [San Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Simon,_Pampanga), [Santo Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Tomas,_Pampanga), [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga), [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico,_Pampanga), [Angeles City](842728.md), and ends on Santa Ines in Mabalacat.

The 94 kilometers (58 mi) four-lane [Subic–Clark–Tarlac Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic%E2%80%93Clark%E2%80%93Tarlac_Expressway) (SCTEx) to date, is the longest toll expressway in the Philippines. Its southern terminus is in the Subic Bay Freeport Zone and passes through the [Clark Freeport Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Freeport_Zone) in two interchanges: Clark North and Clark South. The expressway is linked to the [North Luzon Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Luzon_Expressway) through the Mabalacat Interchange. Its northern terminus is located at the Central Techno Park in Tarlac City, Tarlac.

Aside from the expressways, national highways also serve the province. Two major national highways serve Pampanga, the [MacArthur Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Highway) (N2) and [Jose Abad Santos Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Abad_Santos_Avenue) (N3). Secondary and tertiary national roads, and provincial roads complement the highway backbone.

## Government and politics

Like other provinces in the Philippines, Pampanga is governed by a governor and vice governor who are elected to three-year terms. The governor is the executive head and leads the province's departments in executing the ordinances and improving public services. The vice governor heads a legislative council (Sangguniang Panlalawigan) consisting of board members from the districts.



Pampanga Provincial Capitol

### Provincial government

Further information: [Governor of Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Pampanga)

Just as the national government, the provincial government is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judiciary. The judicial branch is administered solely by the [Supreme Court of the Philippines](1335534.md). The LGUs have control of the executive and legislative branches.

The executive branch is composed of the governor for the province, mayors for the cities and municipalities, and the barangay captains for the barangays.[^cite_note-85] The provincial assembly for the provinces, Sangguniang Panlungsod (city assembly) for the cities, Sangguniang Bayan (town assembly) for the municipalities, Sangguniang Barangay (barangay council), and the [Sangguniang Kabataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Kabataan) for the youth sector.

The seat of government is vested upon the governor and other elected officers who hold office at the Provincial Capitol building. The Sangguniang Panlalawigan is the center of [legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation).

The Provincial government is composed of a Governor as the Local Chief Executive of the Province, Vice-Governor and Members of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan. ### Court system



Façade of Halls of Justice (view from the rear of the Capitolio)

The [Supreme Court of the Philippines](1335534.md) recognizes Pampanga regional trial courts and metropolitan or municipal trial courts within the province and towns, that have an overall jurisdiction in the populace of the province and towns, respectively.[^cite_note-86]

Batas Pambansa Blg. 129, "The Judiciary Reorganization Act of 1980", as amended, created Regional, Metropolitan, Municipal Trial and Circuit Courts. The Third Judicial Region includes RTCs in Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, Pampanga, Palayan and San Jose, inter alia:

xxx. There shall be – (c) Seventy-five Regional Trial judges shall be commissioned for the Third Judicial Region: Twenty-two branches (Branches XLI to LXII) for the province of Pampanga and the city of Angeles, Branches XLI to XLVIII with seats at San Fernando, Branches XLIX to LIII at Guagua, Branches LIV and LV at Macabebe, and Branches LVI to LXII at Angeles;

The law also created Metropolitan Trial Court in each metropolitan area established by law, a Municipal Trial Court in each of the other cities or municipalities, and a Municipal Circuit Trial Court in each circuit comprising such cities and/or municipalities as are grouped together pursuant to law: three branches for Cabanatuan; in every city which does not form part of a metropolitan area, there shall be a Municipal Trial Court with one branch, except as hereunder provided: Three branches for Angeles;

In each of the municipalities that are not comprised within a metropolitan area and a municipal circuit there shall be a Municipal Trial Court which shall have one branch, except as hereunder provided: Four branches for San Fernando and two branches for Guagua, both of Pampanga.[^cite_note-87]
