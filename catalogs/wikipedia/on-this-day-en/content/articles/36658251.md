The **Axis of Resistance**[^cite_note-2] or **Resistance Front**[^cite_note-3] is an informal [Iran](14653.md)-led military coalition that operates across [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia).[^cite_note-AlKassab-2023-4] It currently unites a variety of armed groups allied with [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khomeinism) and committed primarily to countering the influence of Iran's rivals in the region: the [United States](310477.md), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_proxy_conflict), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_proxy_war), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), and sometimes [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Turkey_proxy_conflict).[^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5][^cite_note-AlKassab-2023-4][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] It is supported by [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93North_Korea_relations) and was formerly supported by [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Venezuela_relations) until the [capture of Nicholas Maduro](82021123.md) and [Syria](57134800.md), which hosted both Iranian troops and allied non-Iranian fighters until the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) in 2024.[^cite_note-nytimes20241001-9][^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5]

The "Axis" in question mainly consists of [Shia Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islamism) [militant organizations](7826589.md#Shia_Islamism), such as [Hezbollah](13919.md) in Lebanon, the [Islamic Resistance](75274618.md) and the [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md) in Iraq, and the [Houthis](17240332.md) in Yemen.[^cite_note-10] It intermittently[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] includes certain [Palestinian militant organizations](2646043.md) (typically [Sunni Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islamism)),[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-crisisgroup20100802-15] such as [Hamas](13913.md) and [Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Movement_in_Palestine).[^cite_note-16] Iran's continued support for this coalition is said to be motivated in part by a desire to keep confrontations away from its borders as part of its "forward defence" philosophy, which pursue domestic interests while collectively mobilizing for a shared broader goal of [militarily exhausting Israel](7960202.md) and imposing a cost on [United States foreign policy in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy_in_the_Middle_East).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-soufancenter-18][^cite_note-19] The United States designates most of the coalition's members as [terrorist organizations](63578.md),[^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5] but between 2014 and 2017, the United States military and Iranian proxy forces under [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md) effectively conducted their offensives in unison during the [War against the Islamic State](43517689.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

On several occasions, the Axis of Resistance has carried out direct attacks on [United States troops in Iraq](21506445.md).[^cite_note-nytimes20241001-9][^cite_note-Master_of_Iran's-22] Through the [Quds Force](4028355.md) of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md), the Iranian government had been spending an estimated US\$700 million annually on providing extensive military and logistical support to the coalition's members, but this expenditure began dwindling after 2019 as a result of [international sanctions against Iran](25699056.md).[^cite_note-AlKassab-2023-4][^cite_note-economist20231115-23]

The [Middle Eastern crisis](75895273.md), which began with the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) against Israel in 2023, has weakened the Axis of Resistance and the strategy behind it, according to an analysis by the [Associated Press](18935732.md).[^cite_note-apnews-collapse-syria-24] The network suffered significant and repeated setbacks to Israeli military operations over the course of the [Gaza war](74998519.md),[^cite_note-cfr20241208-25][^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26] the [Israel–Hezbollah conflict](75005425.md), and the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War).[^cite_note-cfr20241208-25][^cite_note-apnews-collapse-syria-24][^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-27][^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5] It was also greatly disrupted by the [2024 Syrian opposition offensives](78460726.md), which entirely reversed the progress of the [Iranian intervention in the Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war).[^cite_note-apnews-collapse-syria-24][^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26][^cite_note-washingtonpost.com-28] The rest of the Axis of Resistance remains intact as of December 2024.[^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26]

## History

### Etymology

The term "Axis of Resistance" was first used by the Libyan daily newspaper *Al-Zahf Al-Akhdar* in response to American president [George W. Bush](3414021.md)'s claim that [Iran](14653.md), [Ba'athist Iraq](26215470.md), and [North Korea](21255.md) formed an [axis of evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_evil). Its 2002 article, "Axis of evil or axis of resistance", said "the only common denominator among Iran, Iraq, and North Korea is their resistance to U.S. hegemony".[^cite_note-def\&foraffairs-29] The Iranian newspaper *[Jomhuri-ye Eslami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republican_\(newspaper\))* subsequently adopted the language in reference to the [Shia insurgency in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_spring_fighting_of_2004), writing in 2004: "If the line of [Iraq's Shi'is](22350732.md) needs to be linked, united, and consolidated, this unity should be realized on the axis of resistance and struggle against the occupiers."[^cite_note-jomhuri-30]

In 2006, the [Palestinian minister of the interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Palestine\)), Said Saim, used the term during an interview at [Al-Alam television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Alam_News_Network) to refer to common political goals among Arabs in opposition to those of Israel or the United States. Noting the large number of [Palestinian refugees in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians_in_Syria), Saim stated, "Syria is also an Islamic Arab country and is also targeted by the Americans and the Zionists. Hence, we see in Syria, Iran, Hezbollah, and Hamas an axis of resistance in front of these pressures."[^cite_note-fednews-31]

The term "axis of resistance" was used as early as August 2010.[^cite_note-32] After two years, [Ali Akbar Velayati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Velayati), senior advisor for foreign affairs to Iran's supreme leader, used the term and said:

> The chain of resistance against Israel by Iran, Syria, Hezbollah, the new Iraqi government, and Hamas passes through the Syrian highway. ... Syria is the golden ring of the chain of resistance against Israel.[^cite_note-Goodarzi-33]

The phrase was used again in August 2012 during a meeting between Syrian president [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) and the secretary of Iran's [Supreme National Security Council](6295948.md), [Saeed Jalili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Jalili), regarding the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).[^cite_note-cbs-34] Velayati said:

> What is happening in Syria is not an internal issue, but a conflict between the axis of resistance and its enemies in the region and the world. Iran will not tolerate, in any form, the breaking of the axis of resistance, of which Syria is an intrinsic part.[^cite_note-Goodarzi-33]

### Foundation

In the wake of the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), some of the most radical founders of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) such as [Mohammad Montazeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Montazeri) (who had been trained by the Palestinian [Fatah](11104.md) in Southern Lebanon and maintained close relations with [Gaddafi](53029.md)'s [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi)) and [Mostafa Chamran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Chamran) (who had visited [Cuba](5042481.md) and was influenced by revolutionary [internationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalism_\(politics\))) strove to create what is often called an "Islamic Internationale",[^cite_note-Alf2013-35] drawing upon [Ali Shariati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Shariati)'s and [Ayatollah Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Khomeini)'s notions of the "solidarity of the oppressed".[^cite_note-36] Montazeri and Chamran, along with [Ali Akbar Mohtashamipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Mohtashamipur), Iran's ambassador to [Syria](7515849.md) from 1982, created the Department for Islamic Liberation Movements, as part of the [People's Revolutionary Organization of the Islamic Republic of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Revolutionary_Organization_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran), whose aim was to bring together the activities of the outlawed Iraqi [Islamic Dawa Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Dawa_Party) and [Badr Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badr_Organization) with those of the Lebanese [Amal Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Movement) and [Hezbollah](13919.md).[^cite_note-Alf2013-35][^cite_note-Merav-Dascalu-37] The Department for Islamic Liberation Movements is thought to have been the starting point of Iranian attempts of forging what was later to become known as the Axis of Resistance.[^cite_note-38]

### Evolution









Current leaders of the Axis of Resistance, except the Chairman of Popular Mobilisation Commission Falih Al-Fayyadh: [Mojtaba Khamenei](23324096.md), [Masoud Pezeshkian](2650383.md), [Naim Qassem](28105310.md) and [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md)

Although Iran had been funding Shia militants since the [Iran-Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Iraq_War) and the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) (such as the [War of Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Brothers)), a complete network of Iranian proxies was not formed until the [2003 Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Iraq_War) led to the rise of Iran's influence.[^cite_note-Merav-Dascalu-37] At first, the alliance consisted of the [Assad](27334130.md)-led Ba'athist Syria and Lebanese Hezbollah. Years later, Iran, already closely aligned with Syria and Hezbollah, would form stronger relations between the three, creating the axis. Iraqi and Yemeni militants coordinating with Iran came in as the newest members of this alliance.[^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39] After the beginning of [Russian involvement in the Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_involvement_in_the_Syrian_civil_war), a slew of posters showing images of [Hassan Nasrallah](313436.md), [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), Syrian President Bashar al-Assad, and Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), have appeared with an Arabic caption meaning "men who bow to no one but God". The posters suggest another emerging regional Axis of Resistance, according to *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(newspaper\)).* This coalition has been described as "deeply polarising" for its sectarian targeting of [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Syrians](8862873.md).[^cite_note-Putinhullin-40] Hezbollah's actions have also arisen denunciation in [Lebanon](17771.md), most notably from [Lebanese President](2032981.md) [Michel Suleiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Suleiman), who demanded an end to unilateral armed maneuvers by Hezbollah. Grievance is also widespread amongst [Lebanon's Sunni minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Lebanon), who charge Hezbollah with engaging in sectarian violence against other [Muslims](19541.md), and of forfeiting its [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) stance.[^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39][^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39][^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39]<sup>: 25 </sup> With Hezbollah's intensifying participation in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) following the years after 2013, the coalition has become explicitly [Khomeinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khomeinism) and [anti-Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Sunnism); with the [Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime) becoming beholden and subservient to Iran and its proxies for continued existence. Alienated by sectarian policies, [Sunni Islamists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islamism) such as [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) and [Hamas](13913.md) began publicly opposing Iran and Hezbollah and have aligned closely with [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md), countries which are engaged in geo-political competition with Iran.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

### Middle Eastern crisis (2023–present)

The [conflicts engulfing the Middle East in 2023](75895273.md), beginning with the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), have weakened the Axis of Resistance and the strategy behind it, according to an analysis by Associated Press.[^cite_note-apnews-collapse-syria-24] The network has suffered major blows in the [Gaza war](74998519.md)[^cite_note-cfr20241208-25][^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26] and the [Israel–Hezbollah conflict](75005425.md).[^cite_note-cfr20241208-25][^cite_note-apnews-collapse-syria-24][^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-27][^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5] Additionally, the fall of the Assad regime in Syria, marked by rebel forces [capturing Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)) and ending over five decades of [Assad family rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assadization), further disrupted the network.[^cite_note-apnews-collapse-syria-24][^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26][^cite_note-washingtonpost.com-28] The Houthis and militias in Iraq remain intact as of December 2024.[^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26] In April 2025 it was reported that Hezbollah has withdrawn the majority of its military infrastructure from southern Lebanon, transferring control to the Lebanese army.[^cite_note-middleeasteye20250412-43] The report claims that 190 out of Hezbollah's 265 military positions were ceded to the army.[^cite_note-middleeasteye20250412-43] This move aligns with the November 2024 U.S.-brokered ceasefire agreement, which mandates Hezbollah's repositioning north of the Litani River and the deployment of approximately 5,000 Lebanese troops to the south.[^cite_note-reuters20241127-44] The withdrawal aims to reduce tensions along the Israel–Lebanon border and facilitate the return of displaced civilians. While Hezbollah has removed heavy weaponry, some fighters from southern villages remain with light arms. The situation remains delicate, with ongoing monitoring by international observers to ensure compliance with the ceasefire terms.[^cite_note-middleeasteye20250412-43][^cite_note-reuters20241127-44]

In July 2025, reports emerged indicating that Iran has intensified its support for proxy groups, supplying advanced weaponry to both the Houthis in Yemen and Hezbollah in Lebanon, despite mounting regional and international pressure. According to U.S.-aligned forces in Yemen, a large shipment, estimated at 750 tons, of Iranian-supplied arms was intercepted in late June near Houthi-controlled territory. The cache reportedly included anti-ship and anti-aircraft missiles, drone components, warheads, and instruction manuals in Farsi, reinforcing long-standing assessments of Tehran’s direct involvement through the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and affiliated networks. Simultaneously, Western intelligence suggests that Iran has resumed missile deliveries to Hezbollah via overland routes through Iraq and Syria, aiming to replenish the group's arsenal following its 2024 confrontation with Israel. Despite domestic economic and military constraints following the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War), Iran has prioritized rearming its regional allies.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

During January 2026, [the US began a military buildup](82251337.md) in the Middle East,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] mainly because of the [nationwide protests in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025-2026_Iran_protests) and [ensuing massacres](82036668.md), a well as growing tension between Iran and the US, due to Iran's alleged ongoing push to develop [nuclear weapons](721807.md), rebuild its ballistic missile program, and its continued support of regional armed groups such as [Hezbollah](13919.md), [Hamas](13913.md), and the [Houthis](17240332.md).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] This culminated in the [joint American–Israeli war on Iran and its regional allies](82533582.md) starting on 28 February, including [the assassination](82537558.md) of Iranian supreme leader [Ali Khamenei](385653.md). In July 2026, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) opted to fly back on the [old Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_VC-25) when leaving Turkey that month after credible threats from the "axis" targeting him and [Qatari-donated Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_One#Boeing_747-8_from_the_royal_family_of_Qatar), as it lacks the same advanced defensive abilities of the old Air Force One. White House press secretary [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md) said that the new Qatari plane will undergo additional upgrades over the following month.[^cite_note-52]

### Iran

[Ali Khamenei](385653.md), who served as the [Supreme Leader of Iran](36814344.md) from 1989 until 2026, repeatedly defined the [Islamic Republic government](50913538.md) as a "resistance government" (i.e., against Western and Israeli influence).[^cite_note-55] [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md) played an important role in Iran's battle with ISIS in Iraq. He has been described as the focal point for bringing together Kurdish and Shia forces for the war against ISIS.[^cite_note-56] Soleimani's achievements led to the creation of an axis of Shia influence throughout the Middle East centered on Iran.[^cite_note-Master_of_Iran's-22] During a meeting with Cuban president [Miguel Díaz-Canel](38630938.md) in December 2023, Khamenei said that the political and economic potentials of Iran and [Cuba](5042481.md) should be used to form a coalition against the "bullying" of [United States](3434750.md) and its Western allies, in order to adopt a "common and effective position" on important global issues such as the Palestinian issue.[^cite_note-57]

### Hezbollah

[Hezbollah](13919.md) was founded by Lebanese clerics to fight [Israel's invasion of Lebanon in 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War).[^cite_note-bbc-hi-me-53][^cite_note-Reuters-2024-54] The group is an influential political actor in Lebanon, shares the [Shiite Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiite_Islam) ideology of Iran. It has increased its attacks on Israeli targets on the Lebanon-Israel border almost daily since the start of [Gaza war](74998519.md) on October 7, 2023, in order to stop Israel from attacking Gaza, sparking the heaviest exchange of fire between the adversaries since full-scale war in 2006.[^cite_note-Reuters-2024-54] [Iran was Hezbollah's chief supplier of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_smuggling_to_Lebanon). In a direct reference to the axis of resistance, Hezbollah leader, Sheikh [Naim Qassem](28105310.md), said in December 2024 that since the fall of the Assad regime, "Hezbollah has lost the military supply route through Syria at this stage, but this loss is a detail in the resistance's work."[^cite_note-58] The group's [Unit 3800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_3800?action=edit\&redlink=1) was responsible for training and providing strategic aid to militant groups in Iraq and Yemen.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

### Houthis

Main article: [Iranian support for the Houthis](78962952.md)

[Abdul-Malik al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Malik_al-Houthi), the leader of the [Houthis](17240332.md) in [Yemen](350939.md), declared on 10 October 2023 that the organisation would retaliate by using missiles and drones in addition to other military measures if the United States got involved in the Gaza conflict.[^cite_note-62]

On 19 October 2023, Yemen begun their missile and drone campaign against Israel in which the [United States Navy](20518076.md) [destroyer](232005.md) [USS *Carney*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Carney) shot down three [land-attack cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-attack_cruise_missile) and several drones heading toward Israel. This was the first action by the Houthis in Yemen on Israel. It was later reported that the ship shot down four cruise missiles and 15 drones.[^cite_note-63] Another missile was reportedly intercepted by Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-64] More were intercepted by Israel's [Arrow anti-ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(missile_family\)); others fell short of their targets or were intercepted by the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) and the [French Navy](383256.md).

The Houthis then [launched attacks on ships](75207766.md) they claim are linked to Israel in a self-proclaimed bid to end the war, prompting a [military response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian) from a number of countries led by the United States.[^cite_note-will-not-stop-65]

### Iraqi groups

Main article: [Islamic Resistance in Iraq](75274618.md)

Armed militias emerged in Iraq after the [US invasion of the country](201936.md) in 2003. These groups became exponentially stronger when they organized as a collective front to counter the terrorist group [ISIS](9087364.md).[^cite_note-what-is-axis-of-resistance-66]

Iranian officials claimed on 30 October 2023 that attacks against U.S. forces in Iraq and other parts of the region were the consequence of "wrong American policies", which included Washington's backing of Israel during the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-67]

Accounts on social media have claimed that [Ya Ali Popular Formations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya_Ali_Popular_Formations) is affiliated with the [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md), or the [IRGC](1285472.md).[^cite_note-IFTSOW-68]

### Syrian groups

According to Jubin Goodarzi, an assistant professor and researcher at [Webster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster_University), the Iranian–[Ba'athist Syrian](57134800.md) alliance that was formed in 1979 is of great importance to the emergence and continuity of the axis of resistance. Both countries are in key locations of the Middle East, and they have been affecting Middle Eastern politics during the past three decades.[^cite_note-Goodarzi-33] Also, the alliance is considered to be an enduring one, lasting 34 years "in spite of the many challenges that it has faced and periodic strains in the relationship". The axis has been described as altering "the strategic balance in the Middle East" by assisting Syrian leader [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) to remain in power and backing his [war-crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crimes_in_Syria) against Syrian civilians.[^cite_note-69] According to Marisa Sullivan, the programme and aims of the Axis have three main pillars; shared regional objective in preserving the Assad regime, maintaining access to supplies of weapons and money from Iran, and stopping a Sunni-majority government from ever coming to power in Syria.[^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39][^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39]<sup>: 5 </sup> The then-ruling [Syrian Ba'ath party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Syria_Region) elites were primarily made up of [Alawites](436616.md), who are an offshoot sect of [Shia](26961.md), which is also the [majority sect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Iran) of Iran.[^cite_note-70] This common background has made them strategic allies on various issues, including defense.[^cite_note-71] The Syrian state-run news agency, [SANA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arab_News_Agency), has stated that the two governments discussed their "strategic cooperation relationship" and "attempts by some Western countries and their allies to strike at the axis of resistance by targeting Syria and supporting terrorism there".[^cite_note-cbs-34] The alliance has been described as an "Axis of Terror" by the prime minister and ambassadors of Israel.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

The [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) in 2024 was described by several Western media as a crippling blow to the Axis of Resistance: the collapse of the [Ba'athist government in Syria](57134800.md) undermining Iran's ability to supply [Hezbollah](13919.md).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-27][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-washingtonpost.com-28][^cite_note-nytimes-assad-fall-26][^cite_note-78] This was denied by Grand Ayatollah [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), who reiterated that the Axis will continue.[^cite_note-79]

The [Islamic Resistance Front in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Resistance_Front_in_Syria) is a militant organization established by the [Syrian Social Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Social_Nationalist_Party) (SSNP) in response to the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) and the [Israeli invasion of Syria](78549398.md) in 2024. It is part of the Axis of Resistance.[^cite_note-80]

### Palestinian groups

Despite the Axis of Resistance being composed of primarily Shia Islamist factions, the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), a secular [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) formation, is generally considered part of the Axis of Resistance, and receives support from Iran.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] The [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Palestinian](23267.md) [Islamist](15012.md) movement [Hamas](13913.md) has also at times been considered part of the axis due to its opposition to Israel and the United States. As of March 2012, the group has since pulled its headquarters out of [Damascus](8914.md) and thrown its support behind the anti-Assad [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition).[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] In October 2022, Hamas restored ties with Syria after reconciliation with the support of mediation by Iran.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

## Funding

Via the [Qods Force](4028355.md)[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-AlKassab-20232-88][^cite_note-Hubbard-20242-89]—a special forces unit of the [Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md)—Iran has allocated substantial resources to strengthen each group's capabilities over decades. The funding of the Axis of Resistance directly impacts Iran's economy and the livelihoods of its civilians by diverting substantial financial resources away from critical domestic needs, and channeling them toward sustaining proxy groups and military engagements. The funding of the axis includes using money through oil revenues, taxes and state managed funds.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

### Assad regime in Syria

Iran had long envisioned establishing a dominant influence in [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md)'s Syria, leveraging the nation's reconstruction after the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) that began in 2011. The [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md), however, marked a serious setback for Iran's ambitions in the Middle East. Tehran had long envisioned establishing a dominance. This act brought an end to years of investment and strategy.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]



Bashar al-Assad, former Syrian autocrat of the [Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party_\(Syrian-dominated_faction\))

Iran's involvement in Syria has been marked by significant financial and military investment. During the Syrian civil war, Iran spent an estimated \$50 billion supporting Assad's regime.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-Amwaj20241210-96] Beyond direct financial and material support, Iran sought to entrench its influence in Syria through a series of economic agreements, particularly after 2017. These agreements often prioritized Iranian interests over Syrian sovereignty, granting Iran advantages in key sectors, including telecommunications, agriculture, and infrastructure. One agreement involved the construction of a strategic oil port on the Mediterranean Sea.[^cite_note-97]

The financial toll of Iran's involvement in Syria has been immense. On December 7, 2024, former Iranian lawmaker [Bahram Parsaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_Parsaei) revealed that Syria's debt to Iran had exceeded \$30 billion.[^cite_note-Amwaj20241210-96]

Since 2000, Iran has consistently expanded funding for proxy groups and military programs, while its civilian population has borne the economic burden. As military and proxy spending surged, especially post‑2011 sanctions, state revenues were redirected from public services and subsidies, resulting in inflation, rising unemployment, widespread poverty, and food insecurity.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] Iran's Ministry of Social Welfare reported that 57 percent of the population suffers from some degree of malnourishment, while 30 percent now live below the poverty line. The Iranian rial lost 46 percent of its value, making it the least valuable currency in the world.[^cite_note-100]

### Other groups



Hezbollah soldiers in a drill

[Hezbollah](13919.md), the [Lebanese Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims) militant group and political party, is among the primary beneficiaries of Iranian support. According to the [US State Department](31975.md), Iran is the primary source of the group's training, arms, and financial support, contributing hundreds of millions of dollars annually to the group.[^cite_note-101] In 2018, US officials estimated Iran transfers \$700 million to Hezbollah annually.[^cite_note-thenational.ae-102][^cite_note-ynetnews.com-103]

In [Yemen](350939.md), Iran backs the [Houthi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement), providing them with weapons, training, and financial assistance.

Various Shia militias in Iraq, such as Kata'ib Hezbollah and Asa'ib Ahl al-Haq, receive funding, training, and arms, including unmanned aerial systems, from Iran.[^cite_note-104]

Iran also contributes approximately \$350 million per year to Palestinian militant organizations, including Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad.[^cite_note-105]

## Opponents

### Israel and the United States

Main articles: [Destruction of Israel in Iranian policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_Israel_in_Iranian_policy), [Iran–Israel proxy conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_proxy_conflict), [Anti-American sentiment in Iran](36762635.md), and [Sarkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarkha)

See also: [2024 Iran–Israel conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iran%E2%80%93Israel_conflict), [Gaza war](74998519.md), [Israel–Hezbollah conflict (2023–present)](75005425.md), [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md), and [Middle Eastern crisis (2023–present)](75895273.md)

Because Iran lacks military superiority of the US and Israel, by cultivating an extensive network of proxy forces, Iran created a strategy designed to bog down adversaries outside its own borders.[^cite_note-132] The 'Axis of Resistance' views Israel as a common adversary and [calls for destruction of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_Israel_in_Iranian_policy).[^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5] The axis claims to be against Israel in order to shore up popular support across the Islamic world. According to analyst Afshon Ostovar, the aim of this network is to present Israel with a long-term existential challenge by "slowly strangling" it through a series of "increasingly destructive, unwinnable wars."[^cite_note-\:8-133] According to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, in the event of a major war with Israel, all member groups were expected to take part in a coordinated, region-wide military campaign with the shared goal of destroying Israel.[^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5] Iran devised this strategy and has heavily invested in strengthening the combat capabilities of each group while fostering coordination among them.[^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5] Despite the alliance members' differing ideologies and domestic interests,[^cite_note-soufancenter-18] their activities serve the broader goal of complicating Israel's operations and making United States support for Israel costly.[^cite_note-Barnes-Dacey-134][^cite_note-Hezbollah_in_Syria-39]<sup>: 5 </sup> *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* pointed out that it "is a network of autonomous militant Islamist groups through which Iran can project power, determine the course of events and deter attack by Israel or the US".[^cite_note-135]

As a result of the outbreak of the [Gaza war](74998519.md) on 7 October 2023, [Hezbollah](13919.md) of Lebanon, the [Yemeni Houthis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement), the [Islamic Resistance in Iraq](75274618.md), and other factions in Syria have launched [drone and missile attacks](75895273.md) on Israel. Hezbollah launched its [operations against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Hezbollah_conflict_\(2023%E2%80%932024\)) on 8 October 2023, whereas Yemen began launching [its operations](75207766.md) on 19 October 2023, and the Islamic Resistance of Iraq began launching its operations on 2 November 2023. In April 2024, Iran launched a [missile and drone attack against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2024_Iranian_strikes_against_Israel) with its supporting factions in response to the [Israeli airstrike on the Iranian embassy complex in Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_airstrike_on_the_Iranian_consulate_in_Damascus).

The [United States has given Israel extensive military aid](65284610.md) and [vetoed](1900272.md) multiple [UN Security Council](31956.md) ceasefire resolutions, concerning Gaza war.[^cite_note-136] Groups of the Axis of Resistance have [attacked American military bases in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_US_bases_during_the_Gaza_war). Additionally, the Yemeni [Houthi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement) have engaged in [attacks](75207766.md) in the [Red Sea](26191.md) on commercial vessels allegedly linked to Israel, incurring a US-led [military response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian).[^cite_note-will-not-stop-65] The United States designates most of the members of the axis of resistance as [terrorist organizations](63578.md).[^cite_note-Hubbard-2024-5]

According to the *[Middle East Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Monitor)*, the axis suffered a severe blow after the [2015 Israeli Mazraat Amal air strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2015_Mazraat_Amal_incident).[^cite_note-137] Three days before the airstrike against the Hezbollah convoy, Hezbollah leader, [Hassan Nasrallah](313436.md) said: "We consider that any strike against Syria is a strike against the whole of the resistance axis, not just against Syria."[^cite_note-138]

### Islamic State

Main articles: [Russia–Syria–Iran–Iraq coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Syria%E2%80%93Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_coalition) and [War against the Islamic State](43517689.md)

This axis became the main fighters against [ISIS](9087364.md) after the group took over almost a third of Iraq in 2014.[^cite_note-Master_of_Iran's-22] According to the Los Angeles Times [Amirli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amirli) was the first city to successfully resist an ISIS offensive and was secured thanks to an unusual cooperation between Iraqi and Kurdish troops, Iranian-backed Shia militias and US warplanes.[^cite_note-139] In 2012, Hezbollah [deployed troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah_involvement_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) to support Syrian government forces against the [FSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) rebels, al-Qaeda and the Islamic State. In 2013, Iran began [deploying troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) to support the [Syrian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_government) against the rebels and other factions. In both instances, the Iraqi government provided support, even having volunteers joining the [battlefields in Syria](45671374.md#Engagement_in_Syria).[^cite_note-140] Also, the [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md), which was established in 2014, became the main force in the fight against ISIS in [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-what-is-axis-of-resistance-66] In 2014, Hezbollah rejected the idea of Lebanon helping in the [US-led intervention in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US-led_intervention_in_Iraq_\(2014%E2%80%932021\)), against the [Islamic State](9087364.md) arguing that it may lead to the U.S. domination in the region or "substituting terrorism with flagrant US occupation".[^cite_note-141] During the war against ISIS in Iraq and Syria, the Axis of Resistance became more united, forming the [4+1 coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Syria%E2%80%93Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_coalition) which was a joint military cooperation coalition with Iran, Iraq, Syria, Russia and Hezbollah of Lebanon.[^cite_note-142]

The coalition supported one another in many battles in the Syrian civil war, such as in [Hama Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Syria_offensive_\(October%E2%80%93November_2015\)), [Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyra_offensive_\(March_2016\)), [Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aleppo_\(2012%E2%80%932016\)) and [Idlib Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Syria_offensive_\(December_2019%E2%80%93March_2020\)). The Russian Air Force has used Iranian airbases for refueling namely the [Hamadan Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadan_Airbase).[^cite_note-143]

The IRGC Quds Force leader claimed: "the resistance of the Iraqi and Syrian governments and the perseverance of the armies and young men of these two countries ... played an important role in overturning this dangerous event … \[I can announce] the termination of the rule of this vicious cursed entity, following [the liberation operation of Abu Kamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Abu_Kamal_offensive), as the last fort of ISIS, bringing down the flag of this US-Zionist made terrorist group and raising the flag of Syria".[^cite_note-144]

## Relationships with other countries

Main article: [CRINK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRINK)

### Relations with Russia

See also: [Iran–Russia relations](3912038.md)

Russia's efforts to expand its role in the [Middle East](19323.md) are entwined with its relations with the Iranian-led Axis of Resistance. It is not a meaningful strategic alliance, but Russia and Iran share a common interest in limiting [Western](21208200.md) influence in the region.

In [Syria](7515849.md), Russia maintains military bases in [Latakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmeimim_Air_Base) and [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_naval_facility_in_Tartus).[^cite_note-Barnes-Dacey-134] During the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), Iran and Russia sought to preserve the [Ba'athist government](57134800.md) of [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md), which was friendly to Russia and allowed Iranian elements to operate throughout the country.[^cite_note-Barnes-Dacey-134] They sought to resist Western intervention and [regime change](37258993.md) efforts in the country, which they believe, if successful, would challenge their own influence in the region and potentially lead to regime change in Iran. [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) intervened in the civil war in support of the Assad government, with Russia's involvement allegedly prompted by a visit to Moscow by Iran's [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md), where he asked Moscow to directly support the regime against the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) forces. Russia provided extensive air support to [Assad's forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arab_Armed_Forces) and to Iranian-backed forces in Syria, such as Hezbollah and pro-Iranian militias.[^cite_note-Barnes-Dacey-134] Assad's regime survived over 10 years of civil war but was [ultimately overthrown](78548066.md) in 2024. The [post-Assad transitional government](79584988.md) became more partial to Western influence and relatively hostile to Russian and pro-Iranian elements, signaling a strategic failure for the 'axis' interventions in Syria.[^cite_note-145]

Despite cooperation with Iran in Syria, Russia has maintained positive relations with Israel–allowing repeated [Israeli airstrikes inside Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_conflict_during_the_Syrian_civil_war)[^cite_note-Barnes-Dacey-134]–and with [Arab Gulf states](3088555.md) while also supporting normalisation of ties between Iran and Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-146] During the [Gaza war](74998519.md), Russia condemned both the October 7 attacks and [Israel's response](74998519.md),[^cite_note-147] but Russia's foreign minister [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) also said that Israel's goals in Gaza were similar to Russia's goals in its [invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]
