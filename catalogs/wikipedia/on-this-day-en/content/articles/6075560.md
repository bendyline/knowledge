The **Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples**, also known as the **United Nations General Assembly Resolution 1514**, was a [resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_resolution) of the [United Nations](31769.md) [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_the_United_Nations) during its fifteenth session that affirmed [independence](62450.md) for countries and peoples under [colonial rule](7299.md).

The declaration characterized foreign rule as a violation of human rights, affirmed the right to [self-determination](29269.md), and called for an end to colonial rule.[^cite_note-\:0-1] [Adom Getachew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adom_Getachew) writes, "Within fifteen years, anticolonial nationalists had successfully captured the UN and transformed the General Assembly into a platform for the international politics of decolonization."[^cite_note-\:0-1] According to [Christian Reus-Smit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Reus-Smit), the resolution "produced a tectonic shift in international legitimacy", as it "successfully undermined the institution of empire."[^cite_note-2]

It was adopted by the [UN General Assembly](31957.md) on December 14, 1960.[^cite_note-UN_ARES1514XV-3] 89 countries voted in favour, none voted against, and nine abstained: [Australia](4689264.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Dominican Republic](8060.md), [France](5843419.md), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain), [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa), [United Kingdom](31717.md), and [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-4]

The Declaration is cited by [International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Racial_Discrimination).[^cite_note-5]

## Context

A declaration on [decolonization](302084.md) was first proposed by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), calling for the rapid independence of the remaining colonies. However a number of states considered that too rapid a decolonization would lead to chaos in those former colonies, so they resolved to bring the matter more rapidly to the General Assembly. Forty-three Asian and African states brought forward this compromise declaration which called for "immediate steps to be taken", which had many possible interpretations short of rapid decolonization.[^cite_note-morse-1961-6]

The United States had long encouraged decolonization, and was expected to support the declaration, but abstained as a result of pressure from the United Kingdom. Despite abstaining, one U.S. representative, [Zelma George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelma_George), led an ovation after the vote. Many states felt the U.S. had let them down.[^cite_note-morse-1961-6]

Many European and [NATO](21133.md) states had voted for the declaration, so there was not a broad Western position on it. The United Kingdom, with most remaining colonies, thought the declaration was an implied criticism of it, and also there should be a period of proper preparation for independence. While having limited influence on other delegates, the UK views influenced the U.S. vote, which was escalated to a final decision by [President Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Eisenhower). U.S. Ambassador [James Wadsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Jeremiah_Wadsworth) in his speech explained that they agreed with the overall objective but there were "difficulties in the language and thought" and raised various technical issues. Senator [Wayne Morse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Morse) characterized it in one instance as "the United States State Department bent over backwards to read the paragraph incorrectly".[^cite_note-morse-1961-6]

## Legacy



Soviet stamp commemorating the 10th anniversary of Resolution 1514

The declaration was a milestone in the process of [decolonization](302084.md).

In 2000, on the occasion of the 40th anniversary of Resolution 1514, UN General Assembly adopted **Resolution 55/146** that declared 2001–2010 the [Second International Decade for the Eradication of Colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International_Decade_for_the_Eradication_of_Colonialism).[^cite_note-UN_ARES55146-7] This follows on from 1990 to 2000 having been the [International Decade for the Eradication of Colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Decade_for_the_Eradication_of_Colonialism).[^cite_note-UN_ARES4347-8]
