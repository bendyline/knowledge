The **Ford Motor Company** (commonly known as **Ford**, sometimes abbreviated as **FoMoCo**) is an American [multinational](214491.md) automobile manufacturer headquartered in [Dearborn, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dearborn,_Michigan), United States. It was founded by [Henry Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford) and incorporated on June 16, 1903. The company sells automobiles and commercial vehicles under the [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ford_vehicles) brand, and luxury cars under its [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Motor_Company) brand. The company is listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md) under the single-letter [ticker symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker_symbol) F and is controlled by the [Ford family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_family_\(Michigan\)). They have minority ownership but a plurality of the voting power.[^cite_note-FordFamily-3][^cite_note-5]

Ford introduced methods for large-scale manufacturing of cars and large-scale management of an industrial workforce using elaborately engineered manufacturing sequences typified by moving [assembly lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line). By 1914, these methods were known around the world as [Fordism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordism). Ford's former British subsidiaries [Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Cars) and [Land Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover), acquired in 1989 and 2000, respectively, were sold to the Indian automaker [Tata Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Motors) in March 2008. Ford owned the Swedish automaker [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Cars) from 1999 to 2010.[^cite_note-6] In the third quarter of 2010, Ford discontinued the [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(automobile\)) brand, under which it had marketed middle priced cars in the United States, Canada, Mexico, and the Middle East since 1938.[^cite_note-7]

Ford is the second-largest American-based automaker, behind [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), and the [sixth-largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_automotive_manufacturers_by_production), behind [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota), [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group), [Hyundai Motor Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_Group), [Stellantis](64563392.md), and General Motors, based on 2022 vehicle production.[^cite_note-8] The company went public in 1956 but the Ford family, through special [Class B shares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preferred_stock#Other_features_or_rights), retain 40 percent of the voting rights.[^cite_note-FordFamily-3][^cite_note-9] During the [2008–2010 automotive industry crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932010_automotive_industry_crisis), the company struggled financially but did not have to be rescued by the federal government, unlike the other two major US automakers.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Ford Motors has since returned to profitability,[^cite_note-12] and was the eleventh-ranked overall American-based company in the 2018 [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) list, based on global revenues in 2017 of \$156.7 billion.[^cite_note-13] In 2023, Ford produced 4.4 million automobiles, and employed about 177,000 employees worldwide. The company operates joint ventures in China ([Changan Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changan_Ford) and [Jiangling Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangling_Ford)), Taiwan ([Ford Lio Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Lio_Ho)), Thailand ([AutoAlliance Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AutoAlliance_Thailand)), and Turkey ([Ford Otosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Otosan)). Ford owns a 32% stake in China's [Jiangling Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangling_Motors).[^cite_note-AR-14][^cite_note-15]

## History

Main article: [History of the Ford Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Ford_Motor_Company)

### 20th century



Founder [Henry Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford), 1919

The [Henry Ford Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford_Company) was Henry Ford's first attempt at a car manufacturing company and was established on November 3, 1901. This became the [Cadillac Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_Motor_Company) on August 22, 1902, after Ford left with the rights to his name.[^cite_note-16] In 1903, the Ford Motor Company was launched in a converted factory, with \$28,000, equivalent to \$1,003,000 in 2025, in cash from twelve investors, most notably [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Dodge) and [Horace Dodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Elgin_Dodge), who later founded the [Dodge Brothers Motor Vehicle Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Brothers_Motor_Vehicle_Company).

The first president was not Ford, but local banker [John S. Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_S._Gray_\(Michigan\)), who was chosen in order to assuage investors' fears that Ford would leave the new company the way he had left its predecessor. During its early years, the company produced just a few cars a day at [its factory on Mack Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mack_Avenue_Plant) and later at its [factory on Piquette Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Piquette_Avenue_Plant) in [Detroit](8687.md), Michigan. Groups of two or three men worked on each car, assembling it from parts made mostly by supplier companies contracting for Ford. Within a decade the company led the world in the expansion and refinement of the [assembly line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line) concept, and Ford soon brought much of the part production in-house, via [vertical integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_integration).

Henry Ford was 39 years old when he founded the Ford Motor Company, which became one of the world's largest and most profitable companies. It has been in continuous family control for over 100 years, and is one of the largest family-controlled companies in the world.[^cite_note-17]

The first gasoline-powered [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) was created in 1885 by the [German](11867.md) inventor [Karl Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Benz), with his [Benz Patent-Motorwagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benz_Patent-Motorwagen). More efficient production methods were needed to make automobiles affordable for [middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class) people; Ford contributed to this effort through a variety of innovations, including the introduction of the first moving [assembly line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line) in 1913 at the Ford [factory in Highland Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park_Ford_Plant).[^cite_note-18]



A 1910 [Model T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_T), introduced in 1908, photographed in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City)

Between 1903 and 1908, Ford produced the Models [A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_A_\(1903-04\)), B, C, F, K, N, R, and S. Hundreds or a few thousand of most of these were sold per year. In 1908, Ford introduced the mass-produced [Model T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_T), which totaled millions sold over nearly 20 years. In 1927, Ford replaced the T with the [Model A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_A_\(1927%E2%80%931931\)), the first production car with [laminated safety glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminated_glass) in the windshield.[^cite_note-19] Ford launched the [first low-priced car with a V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_flathead_V8) engine in 1932.[^cite_note-20]



[Ford Model AA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_AA) truck (based on the Model A) displayed in [Kilgore, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilgore,_Texas)

In an attempt to compete with General Motors' mid-priced Pontiac, Oldsmobile, and Buick, Ford created the Mercury in 1939 as a higher-priced companion car to Ford. Henry Ford purchased the Lincoln Motor Company in 1922,[^cite_note-21] in order to compete with such brands as Cadillac and Packard for the luxury segment of the automobile market.[^cite_note-22]

In 1929, Ford was contracted by the government of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) to set up the [Gorky Automobile Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAZ) in Russia initially producing Ford Model A and AAs, thereby playing an important role in the [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) of that country and consequently the Soviet war effort during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-23] To that end, in 1944, Stalin wrote a letter to the [U.S. Chamber of Commerce](1448498.md) stating that Henry Ford was "one of the world's greatest industrialists".[^cite_note-24]



[B-24 Liberator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) bombers being mass-produced at Ford's [Willow Run assembly plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow_Run), 1944

During World War II, the [United States Department of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) picked Ford to mass-produce the [Consolidated B-24 Liberator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) bomber at its Willow Run assembly plant.[^cite_note-25] [Ford Werke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Germany) and [Ford SAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_SAF), Ford's subsidiaries in Germany and France, respectively, produced military vehicles and other equipment for [Nazi Germany](21212.md)'s [war effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_re-armament). Some of Ford's operations in Germany at the time were run [using forced labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II).

The creation of a scientific laboratory in Dearborn, Michigan, in 1951, doing unfettered basic research, led to Ford's involvement in [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity) research. In 1964, Ford Research Labs made a key breakthrough with the invention of a superconducting quantum interference device or [SQUID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQUID).[^cite_note-26]

Ford offered [the Lifeguard safety package from 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeguard_\(automobile_safety\)), which included such innovations as a standard deep-dish steering wheel, optional front, and, for the first time in a car, rear seatbelts, and an optional padded dash.[^cite_note-27] Ford introduced child-proof door locks into its products in 1957, and, in the same year, offered the first retractable hardtop on a mass-produced six-seater car.[^cite_note-Flory_Jr._2008-28]

In late 1955, Ford established the Continental division as a separate luxury car division. This division was responsible for the manufacture and sale of the famous [Continental Mark II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Mark_II). At the same time, the [Edsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsel) division was created to design and market that car starting with the 1958 model year. Due to limited sales of the Continental and the Edsel disaster, Ford merged Mercury, Edsel, and Lincoln into "M-E-L", which reverted to "Lincoln-Mercury" after Edsel's November 1959 demise.[^cite_note-Flory_Jr._2008-28]



The introduction of the [Ford Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang) at the [1964 New York World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_New_York_World's_Fair).

The [Ford Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang) was introduced on April 17, 1964, during the [1964 New York World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_New_York_World's_Fair), where Ford had a pavilion made by [The Walt Disney Company](37398.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] In 1965, Ford introduced the seat belt reminder light.[^cite_note-31]

With the 1980s, Ford introduced several highly successful vehicles around the world. During the 1980s, Ford began using the advertising slogan, "Have you driven a Ford, lately?" to introduce new customers to its brand and make its vehicles appear more modern. In 1990 and 1994, respectively, Ford also acquired [Jaguar Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Cars) and [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin).[^cite_note-32] During the mid-to-late 1990s, Ford continued to sell large numbers of vehicles, in a booming [American](3434750.md) economy with a soaring stock market and low fuel prices.[^cite_note-33]

With the dawn of the new century, legacy [health care costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_prices_in_the_United_States), higher fuel prices, and a faltering economy led to falling market shares, declining sales, and diminished profit margins. Most of the corporate profits came from financing consumer automobile loans through [Ford Motor Credit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Credit_Company).[^cite_note-34]

### 21st century



[William Clay Ford Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clay_Ford_Jr.), great-grandson of Henry Ford, serves as the [executive chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_chairman) at the board of Ford Motor Company.

[William Clay Ford Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clay_Ford_Jr.), the great-grandson of Henry Ford (better known as "Bill"), was appointed executive chairman in 1998, and also became chief executive officer of the company in 2001, with the departure of [Jacques Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Nasser), becoming the first member of the Ford family to head the company since the retirement of his uncle, [Henry Ford II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford_II), in 1982. Ford sold motorsport engineering company [Cosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosworth) to [Gerald Forsythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Forsythe) and [Kevin Kalkhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kalkhoven) in 2004, the start of a decrease in Ford's motorsport involvement. Upon the retirement of president and chief operations officer Jim Padilla in April 2006, Bill Ford assumed his roles as well. Five months later, in September, Ford named [Alan Mulally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Mulally) as president and CEO, with Ford continuing as executive chairman.

By 2005, both Ford and [GM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors)'s corporate bonds had been downgraded to junk status[^cite_note-35] as a result of high U.S. health care costs for an [aging workforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_in_the_American_workforce), soaring gasoline prices, eroding market share, and an overdependence on declining [SUV](47696.md) sales. Profit margins decreased on large vehicles due to increased "incentives" (in the form of rebates or low-interest financing) to offset declining demand.[^cite_note-36] In the latter half of 2005, Chairman Bill Ford asked newly appointed Ford Americas Division President [Mark Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Fields_\(businessman\)) to develop a plan to return the company to profitability. Fields previewed the plan, named *[The Way Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Way_Forward)*, at the board meeting of the company on December 7, 2005, and it was unveiled to the public on January 23, 2006. "The Way Forward" included resizing the company to match market realities, dropping some unprofitable and inefficient models, consolidating production lines, closing 14 factories and cutting 30,000 jobs.[^cite_note-37]

Ford moved to introduce a range of new vehicles, including "[Crossover SUVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_SUV)" built on [unibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unibody) car platforms, rather than more [body-on-frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body-on-frame) chassis. In developing the hybrid electric powertrain technologies for the [Ford Escape Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape_Hybrid) SUV, the company licensed similar Toyota hybrid technologies[^cite_note-38] in order to avoid patent infringements.[^cite_note-39] Ford announced  that it would team up with electricity supply company [Southern California Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California_Edison) (SCE) to examine the future of [plug-in hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) in terms of how home and vehicle energy systems will work with the electrical grid. Under the multimillion-dollar, multi-year project, Ford is to convert a demonstration fleet of [Ford Escape Hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape_Hybrid) into plug-in hybrids, and SCE is to evaluate how the vehicles might interact with the home and the utility's electrical grid. Some of the vehicles are to be evaluated "in typical customer settings", according to Ford.[^cite_note-akhrum-40][^cite_note-edison-41]

In December 2006, the company raised its borrowing capacity to about \$25 billion, placing substantially all corporate assets as collateral.[^cite_note-42] Chairman Bill Ford has stated that "bankruptcy is not an option".[^cite_note-43] Ford and the [United Auto Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Auto_Workers), representing approximately 46,000 hourly workers in North America, agreed to a historic contract settlement in November 2007 giving the company a substantial break in terms of its ongoing retiree health care costs and other economic issues. The agreement included the establishment of a company-funded, independently run [Voluntary Employee Beneficiary Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_Employee_Beneficiary_Association) (VEBA) trust to shift the burden of retiree health care from the company's books, thereby improving its balance sheet. This arrangement took effect on January 1, 2010. As a sign of its currently strong cash position, Ford contributed its entire current liability (estimated at US\$5.5 billion as of December 31, 2009) to the VEBA in cash, and also pre-paid US\$500 million of its future liabilities to the fund. The agreement also gave hourly workers the job security they were seeking by having the company commit to substantial investments in most of its factories.

The automaker reported the largest annual loss in company history in 2006 of \$12.7 billion,[^cite_note-44] and estimated that it would not return to profitability until 2009.[^cite_note-45] However, Ford surprised [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) in the second quarter of 2007 by posting a \$750 million profit. Despite the gains, the company finished the year with a \$2.7 billion loss, largely attributed to finance restructuring at [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Cars).[^cite_note-46] On June 2, 2008, Ford sold its Jaguar and Land Rover operations to [Tata Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Motors) for \$2.3 billion.[^cite_note-r_20080602-47][^cite_note-48]



A worker installs a seat into a Ford F-150 at the [Ford Kansas City Assembly Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Kansas_City_Assembly_Plant) in 2008 which was a critical time for the automaker due to the [2008–2010 automotive industry crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932010_automotive_industry_crisis)

During congressional hearings held in November 2008 at Washington D.C., Ford's Alan Mulally stated that "We at Ford are hopeful that we have enough liquidity. But we also must prepare ourselves for the prospect of further deteriorating economic conditions". He went on to state that "The collapse of one of our competitors would have a severe impact on Ford" and that Ford Motor Company supported both Chrysler and General Motors in their search for government bridge loans during the [2008–2010 automotive industry crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932010_automotive_industry_crisis).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Together, the three companies presented action plans for the sustainability of the industry. Mulally stated that "In addition to our plan, we are also here today to request support for the industry. In the near-term, Ford does not require access to a government bridge loan. However, we request a credit line of \$9 billion as a critical backstop or safeguard against worsening conditions as we drive transformational change in our company".[^cite_note-51] GM and Chrysler received government loans and financing through [T.A.R.P.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T.A.R.P.) legislation funding provisions.[^cite_note-52]

On December 19, the cost of [credit default swaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_default_swap) to insure the debt of Ford was 68 percent of the sum insured for five years, in addition to annual payments of 5 percent. That meant \$6.8 million paid upfront to insure \$10 million in debt, in addition to payments of \$500,000 per year.[^cite_note-53] In January 2009, Ford reported a \$14.6 billion loss in the preceding year, a record for the company. The company retained sufficient liquidity to fund its operations. Through April 2009, Ford's strategy of [debt-for-equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt-for-equity) exchanges erased \$9.9 billion in liabilities (28% of its total) in order to leverage its cash position.[^cite_note-Dolan-54] These actions yielded Ford a \$2.7 billion profit in fiscal year 2009, the company's first full-year profit in four years.[^cite_note-55] In 2012, Ford's corporate bonds were upgraded from junk to investment grade again, citing sustainable, lasting improvements.[^cite_note-56]

On October 29, 2012, Ford announced the sale of its climate control components business, its last remaining automotive components operation, to Detroit Thermal Systems LLC for an undisclosed price.[^cite_note-57] On November 1, 2012, Ford announced that [CEO](52234.md) Alan Mulally would stay with the company until 2014. Ford also named Mark Fields, its president of operations in the Americas, as its new chief operating officer[^cite_note-58] Mulally was paid a compensation of over \$174 million in his previous seven years at Ford since 2006. The generous amount has been a sore point for some workers of the company.[^cite_note-59]

In April 2016, Ford announced a plan to modernize its Dearborn engineering and headquarters campuses through a ten-year building project. The result would see the number of Ford employees working in these areas doubling, to 24,000. During construction, some 2000 of the employees were relocated out of the campus to a temporary location in a disused section of the local shopping mall.[^cite_note-60] Facilities would also be altered to allow ride-sharing and electric and self-driving vehicles.[^cite_note-61] Estimates of the construction cost were \$1.2 billion.[^cite_note-62]



The historic, once abandoned [Michigan Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Central_Station) was purchased by Ford Motor Company in May 2018 and is expected to undergo a significant four-year renovation.

On January 3, 2017, Ford [CEO](52234.md) Mark Fields announced that in a "vote of confidence" because of the pro-business climate being fostered in part by [President-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States) [Donald Trump](4848272.md), Ford had canceled plans to invest \$1.6 billion in a new plant in Mexico to manufacture the [Ford Focus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus); instead, the company would invest \$700 million in [Michigan](18859.md), which it planned to use to create 700 new jobs. The Focus would now be manufactured in the existing plant in Mexico.[^cite_note-63]

Also in 2017, Ford began development of a new mixed-use urban campus in [the Corktown neighborhood of Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corktown,_Detroit), with its purchase, renovation, and occupation of The Factory at Michigan and Rosa Parks. ### Ford Philanthropy

The Ford Philanthropy, formerly known as the Ford Motor Company Fund (also known as Ford Fund, not affiliated with the [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation)), based in Dearborn, Michigan, is the philanthropic arm of the Ford Motor Company.[^cite_note-fordfund2016-110] Established in 1949[^cite_note-111] by [Henry Ford II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford_II), the organization is a nonprofit corporate foundation[^cite_note-112] financed by contributions from Ford Motor Company. In 2017, it contributed \$63 million[^cite_note-113] to various causes[^cite_note-fordfund2016-110] with a focus on education, driving safely and community building.

The Ford Driving Skills for Life program is a driver safety program aimed at teens that were developed together with the Governors Highway Safety Association and safety experts.[^cite_note-114] The Ford Volunteer Corps allows Ford employees and retirees to sign up for volunteering work on local projects in more than 40 countries.[^cite_note-115] The organization invests \$18 million annually in education in the United States and around the world, but accepts applications only from nonprofit organizations registered in the U.S.[^cite_note-116] Education programs and scholarships include Alan Mulally Engineering Scholarship, Ford Blue Oval Scholars Program, Ford College Community Challenge (Ford C3), Ford Driving Dreams Tour, Ford Fund/Detroit Free Press Journalism Scholarship, Ford Next Generation Learning (Ford NGL), Grants to Individuals Program, HBCU Community Challenge, Smithsonian Latino Center Young Ambassadors Program, and William Clay Ford Automotive Design Scholarship.

On April 29, 2024, the Ford Fund announced its official name has changed to Ford Philanthropy.[^cite_note-117]

### Europe

Main article: [Ford of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_of_Europe)



The Ford Research Center in [Aachen](1520.md), Germany, photographed in 2006

At first, [Ford in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Germany) and [Ford in Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_of_Britain) built different models from one another until 1965, when the [Ford Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit) and later the [Ford Escort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escort_\(Europe\)) and the [Ford Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Capri) became common to both companies. In 1970, the [Ford Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Taunus) and the [Ford Cortina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Cortina) came into production with a common base construction, both models being produced in [left hand drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_hand_drive) and [right hand drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_hand_drive). Later on, the models became identical and the respective models right- and left-hand-drive exclusively. Rationalisation of model ranges meant that production of many models in the UK switched to elsewhere in Europe, including Belgium and Spain as well as Germany. The [Ford Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Sierra) replaced the Taunus and Cortina in 1982, drawing criticism for its radical aerodynamic styling, which was soon given nicknames, the "Jellymould" and "The Salesman's Spaceship".



Ford's [Dunton Technical Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunton_Technical_Centre) in [Laindon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laindon), United Kingdom, the largest automotive research and development facility in the country,

In February 2002, Ford ended car production in the UK. It was the first time in 90 years that Ford cars had not been made in Britain, although production of the [Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit) van continued at the company's [Southampton](7920751.md) facility until mid-2013, engines at [Bridgend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgend) and [Dagenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Dagenham), and transmissions at [Halewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halewood). Development of European Ford is broadly split between [Dunton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunton_Wayletts) in Essex (powertrain, Fiesta/Ka, and commercial vehicles) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) (body, chassis, electrical, Focus, Mondeo) in Germany. Ford also produced the [Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Trader) range of commercial vehicles, although the use of this brand name was discontinued with the introduction of the [Ford Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit) in 1965. Elsewhere in continental Europe, Ford assembles the Mondeo, [Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Galaxy), [S-Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_S-Max)[^cite_note-127] and [Kuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Kuga)[^cite_note-128] in Valencia (Spain), Fiesta in Cologne (Germany), Focus in [Saarlouis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarlouis) (Germany), Ecosport[^cite_note-129] and [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Puma_\(crossover\))[^cite_note-130] in [Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova) (Romania).

Ford also owns a joint-venture production plant in Turkey. [Ford Otosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otosan), established in the 1970s, manufactures the [Transit Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit_Connect) compact panel van as well as the "Jumbo" and long-wheelbase versions of the full-size Transit. This new production facility was set up near [Kocaeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmit) in 2002, and its opening marked the end of Transit assembly in Genk.

Another joint venture plant near [Setúbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set%C3%BAbal) in Portugal, set up in collaboration with [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen), formerly assembled the [Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Galaxy) people-carrier as well as its sister ships, the [VW Sharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Sharan) and [SEAT Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEAT_Alhambra). With the introduction of the third generation of the Galaxy, Ford has moved the production of the people-carrier to the Genk plant, with Volkswagen taking over sole ownership of the Setúbal facility.

In 2008, Ford acquired a majority stake in [Automobile Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Craiova), Romania. Starting 2009, the [Ford Transit Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit_Connect) was Ford's first model produced in Craiova, followed, in 2012, by low-capacity car engines and a new small class car, the [B-Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_B-Max).[^cite_note-131] In 2022, [Ford Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Romania) was acquired by Ford Otosan and in 2023, production of the [Ford Transit Courier/Tourneo Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit_Courier#Second_generation_\(2023\)) started at the facility.[^cite_note-132]

Its 1959 [Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Anglia) two-door saloon was one of the most quirky-looking small family cars in Europe at the time of its launch, but buyers soon became accustomed to its looks and it was hugely popular with British buyers in particular. It was still selling well when replaced by the more practical [Escort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escort_\(Europe\)) in 1967.

The third incarnation of the Ford Escort was launched in 1980 and marked the company's move from [rear-wheel-drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear-wheel-drive) saloons to [front-wheel-drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front-wheel-drive) hatchbacks in the small family car sector.

The fourth-generation Escort was produced from 1990 until 2000, although its successor—the [Focus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus_\(international\))—had been on sale since 1998. On its launch, the Focus was arguably the most dramatic-looking and fine-handling small family cars on sale and sold in huge volumes right up to the launch of the next-generation Focus at the end of 2004.

The 1982 [Ford Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Sierra)—replacement for the long-running and massively popular [Cortina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Cortina) and [Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Taunus) models—was a style-setter at the time of its launch. Its ultramodern aerodynamic design was a world away from a boxy, sharp-edged Cortina, and it was massively popular just about everywhere it was sold. A series of updates kept it looking relatively fresh until it was replaced by the front-wheel-drive [Mondeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mondeo) at the start of 1993.

The rise in popularity of small cars during the 1970s saw Ford enter the mini-car market in 1976 with its [Fiesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fiesta) hatchback. Most of its production was concentrated at [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Spain) in Spain, and the Fiesta sold in huge figures from the very start. An update in 1983 and the launch of an all-new model in 1989 strengthened its position in the small car market.

On October 24, 2012, Ford announced that it would close its [Genk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genk) assembly plant in eastern Belgium by the end of 2014.[^cite_note-133]

In 2015, Ford announced that it took control of [Ford Sollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Sollers), Ford's joint venture with Russian company [Sollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sollers_JSC).[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

In September 2018, at the IAA Commercial Vehicles show in Hannover, Germany, Ford introduced an electric tractor-trailer concept vehicle dubbed the F-Vision, which would have Level 4 autonomous driving capability.[^cite_note-136]

On March 1, 2022, Ford announced that it was suspending its [Sollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Sollers) joint venture operation in Russia, in response to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] On October 26, 2022, Ford sold its 49 percent share in the joint venture, and exited the Russian market,[^cite_note-oct2022-139] adding that it retains the option to buy them back within a 5-year period "should the global situation change".[^cite_note-140]

### East and Southeast Asia

Ford formed its first passenger-vehicle joint venture in China in 2001, six years behind GM and more than a decade after VW. It has spent as of 2013 \$4.9 billion to expand its lineup and double production capacity in China to 600,000 vehicles. This includes Ford's largest-ever factory complex in the southwestern city of Chongqing. Ford had 2.5% of the Chinese market in 2013, while VW controlled 14.5% and GM had 15.6%, according to consultant LMC Automotive. GM outsells Ford in China by more than six-to-one.[^cite_note-141] Ford's presence in Asia has traditionally been much smaller, confined to Malaysia, Singapore, Hong Kong, the Philippines, and Taiwan, where Ford has had a joint venture with [Lio Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Lio_Ho_Motor) since the 1970s. Ford began assembly of cars in Thailand in 1960, but withdrew from the country in 1976, and did not return until 1995 when it formed a joint venture with [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda) called Auto Alliance.[^cite_note-142] Now based in the Bo-win Sub District of the Sriracha District in [Chonburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonburi_\(city\)), the factory still produces passenger automobiles. The factory, built in 1941 in Singapore, was soon taken over by the Japanese during the war and was the site of a surrender of the British to the Japanese, at the [factory site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Ford_Motor_Factory) which is now a national monument in Singapore. On April 30, 2013, Ford Motor Co. launched its car and pickup truck line in [Myanmar](19457.md). Previously, heavy importation taxes had stifled imported car purchases in Myanmar, but due to currency reform, lifting of previous import restrictions, and the abolishment of [shadow currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_controls), Myanmar's car market had grown in demand.[^cite_note-investvine-143]

Ford decided to shut down its entire operations in [Indonesia](14579.md), including its dealer network by second half of 2016. #### South Korea

In 1967, Ford partnered with the South Korean company [Hyundai](42257.md), and at the new factory in [Ulsan, South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan,_South_Korea), built the European [Ford Cortina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Cortina) until 1974 when Hyundai introduced its all-new [Hyundai Pony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Pony) in 1975. Ford then developed a relationship with Korea's oldest car manufacturer [Kia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Motors) which built vehicles co-engineered with [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda), later selling the [Ford Festiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Festiva) from 1988 to 1993, and the [Ford Aspire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Festiva) from 1994 to 1997 for export to the United States. With the acquisition of a stake in Japanese manufacturer [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda) in 1979, Ford began selling Mazda's [Familia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_Familia) and [Capella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_Capella) as the [Ford Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Laser) and [Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Telstar) throughout the region, replacing the European-sourced Escort and Cortina. From 1989 to 1996, Kia imported the [Mercury Sable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Sable) from Ford in the U.S. and sold them in South Korea as the Kia Sable. Though the Sable was branded and marketed as a Kia, it retained the Mercury badges and emblem. Ford lost its Kia interest to Hyundai in 1998 after the [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis). Kia had declared bankruptcy in 1997; in 1998, [Hyundai Motor Company](42257.md) acquired 51% of the company, outbidding Ford which had owned an interest in Kia Motors since 1986.[^cite_note-146] After subsequent divestments,[^cite_note-147] Hyundai Motor Company owns less than 50% of the company but remains Kia's largest stakeholder.

As of 2020, Ford sells the Explorer, Mondeo, and Mustang,[^cite_note-148] as well as the Lincoln Aviator, Continental, Corsair, MKZ, and Nautilus in South Korea.[^cite_note-149]

### South and West Asia

[Ford India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_India_Private_Limited) began production in 1998 at [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), [Tamil Nadu](29918.md), with its Ford Escort model, which was later replaced by the locally produced [Ford Ikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Ikon) in 2001. It has since added the Fusion, Fiesta, Mondeo and Endeavour models to its product line.

On March 9, 2010, Ford launched its first made-for-India compact car. Starting at [₹](490783.md)349,900, the [Figo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Figo) was Ford's first car designed and priced for the mass Indian market.[^cite_note-150] On July 28, 2011, Ford India signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) with the State of [Gujarat](53707.md) for the construction of an assembly and engine plant in [Sanand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanand) and planned to invest approximately US\$1 billion on a 460-acre site.[^cite_note-151] In 2019, the company and [Mahindra & Mahindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_&_Mahindra) formed a joint venture to develop, market and distribute Ford-branded vehicles in India.[^cite_note-152] In September 2021 Ford India announced plans to shut down both its assembly plants. The company said that it intends to maintain its parts and service network.[^cite_note-153]

Ford's market presence in the Middle East has traditionally been small, partly due to previous Arab boycotts of companies dealing with Israel. Ford and Lincoln vehicles are currently marketed in ten countries in the region.[^cite_note-154] Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and the United Arab Emirates are the biggest markets. Ford also established itself in Egypt in 1926 but faced an uphill battle during the 1950s due to the hostile nationalist business environment.[^cite_note-155] Ford's distributor in Saudi Arabia announced in February 2003 that it had sold 100,000 Ford and Lincoln vehicles since commencing sales in November 1986. Half of the Ford and Lincoln vehicles sold in that country were [Ford Crown Victorias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Crown_Victoria).[^cite_note-156] In 2004, Ford sold 30,000 units in the region, falling far short of General Motors' 88,852 units and [Nissan Motors](21240.md)' 75,000 units.

### South America

### Africa

In Africa, Ford's market presence has traditionally been strongest in South Africa and neighbouring countries, with only trucks being sold elsewhere on the continent. Ford in South Africa began by importing kits from Canada to be assembled at its facility in [Port Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Elizabeth); the company later sourced its models from the UK and Australia, with local versions of the Ford Cortina including the XR6, with a 3.0 V6 engine, and a [Cortina-based 'bakkie' or pick-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_P100), which was exported to the UK. In the mid-1980s Ford merged with a rival company, owned by [Anglo American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo_American_plc), to form the [South African Motor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Motor_Corporation) ([Samcor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samcor)).[^cite_note-161]

Following international condemnation of [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid), Ford divested from South Africa in 1988, and sold its stake in Samcor, although it licensed the use of its brand name to the company.[^cite_note-162] Samcor began to assemble Mazdas as well, which affected its product line-up and saw the European Fords like the Escort and Sierra replaced by the [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda)-based Laser[^cite_note-163] and [Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Telstar).[^cite_note-164] Ford bought a 45 per cent stake in [Samcor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samcor) following the demise of apartheid in 1994, and this later became, once again, a wholly owned subsidiary, the Ford Motor Company of Southern Africa. Ford now sells a local sedan version of the Fiesta (also built in India and Mexico), and the Focus. The Falcon model from Australia was also sold in South Africa, but was dropped in 2003; the Mondeo, after briefly being assembled locally, was dropped in 2005. The Mondeo was later reintroduced in 2015, badged as the [Fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fusion_\(Americas\)), but was dropped in 2017.[^cite_note-165]

## Former operations

### East and Southeast Asia

#### Ford of Japan

Main article: [Ford Motor Company of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company_of_Japan)

Ford established a manufacturing facility in the port city of Yokohama in February 1925, where Model T vehicles were assembled using imported [knock-down kits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock-down_kit).[^cite_note-172] The factory subsequently produced 10,000 Model A's up to 1936. Production ceased in 1940 as a result of political tensions between Japan and the United States.

After World War II, Ford did not have a presence in Japan, as the Ford facility was appropriated by the Japanese Government until 1958 when property was returned as a possession of the Ford Motor Company and became a [research and development location](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mazda_facilities#R\&D) for Ford partner [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda). In 1979, Ford acquired a 24.5% ownership stake in Mazda, and in 1982, Ford and Mazda jointly established a sales channel to sell Ford products in Japan, including vehicles manufactured in North America, at a dealership called *Autorama ([Japanese](15606.md): [オートラマ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%9E))*. The Autorama sales channel was renamed Ford Sales of Japan in 1997.[^cite_note-173]

Vehicles sold at *Autorama* locations were the North American assembled Ford Explorer, Probe (1989–1998), Mustang, Taurus (1989–1997), Thunderbird (1990–1993), Lincoln Continental, and Lincoln LS. Ford products manufactured in Europe that were sold in Japan were the [Ford Mondeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mondeo), [Ka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Ka), [Focus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus), [Focus C-MAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_C-Max), [Fiesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fiesta), and the [Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Galaxy). Mazda manufactured Ford vehicles in Japan and sold them as Fords at the Autorama locations. They were the [Ford Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Telstar) (Mazda Capella), [Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Laser), [Festiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Festiva), [Festiva Mini Wagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_Demio), [Ixion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Ixion) (Mazda Premacy), [Freda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Freda) (Mazda Bongo Friendee), [Spectron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Spectron) (Mazda Bongo), and commercial trucks J80 and the J100 (Mazda Bongo truck).

Ford increased its shareholding in Mazda to 33.4% in 1996, but as of July 2016, it is listed at 11%.[^cite_note-174] Ford did sell a small range of vehicles in Japan; as of October 2010, the Ford Mustang, Escape, Explorer (and Explorer pickup truck), [Ford Kuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Kuga), [Lincoln Navigator](954829.md), [Lincoln MKX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_MKX), and more recently, the [Ford Ecosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Ecosport) were available in Japan. As of February 2016, Ford no longer maintains a regional office in Minato, Tokyo, Japan, and sales of new cars in Japan have ended.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

### Oceania



Frank Delandro Authorised Ford dealer, Crows Nest, Sydney, 1936



Ford FG X Falcon (Australia) (2014–2016; 2014 model shown)



The Ford stamping plant in [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong), Victoria, Australia. It closed in 2016.

In Australia and New Zealand, the popular [Ford Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Falcon_\(Australia\)) (1960–2016) had long been considered the average family car and is considerably larger than the Mondeo, Ford's largest car sold in Europe. Between 1960 and 1972, the Falcon was based on the U.S. model of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Falcon_\(North_America\)), but since then was entirely designed and manufactured in Australia until 2016, occasionally being manufactured in New Zealand. Like its [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) rival, the [Holden Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore), the Falcon used a rear-wheel-drive layout. High-performance variants of the Falcon running locally built engines (the [Barra engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Barra_engine)) produced up to 436 hp (325 kW). A [ute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ute_\(vehicle\)) ([coupé utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup%C3%A9_utility)) version was also available with the same range of drivetrains. In addition, Ford Australia sold highly tuned limited-production Falcon sedans and utes through its performance car division, [Ford Performance Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Performance_Vehicles) from 2002 until it closed in 2014.

In Australia, the Commodore and Falcon had traditionally outsold all other cars and constituted over 20% of the new car market. In New Zealand, Ford was second in market share in the first eight months of 2006 with 14.4%.[^cite_note-177] More recently, Ford has axed its Falcon-based LWB variant of its lineup– the [Fairlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fairlane_\(Australia\)) and LTD ranges. Ford discontinued the Fairlane in 2007 and LTD in 2008. Ford had announced that its Geelong engine manufacturing plant would be shut down between 2013 and 2016. They had earlier announced local manufacturing of the Focus small car starting from 2011, but instead decided to import the model from Ford's plant in [Thailand](30128.md).[^cite_note-178]

In Australia, the Laser was one of [Ford Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company_of_Australia)'s most successful models and was manufactured in Ford's [Homebush West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homebush_West,_New_South_Wales) plant from 1981 until the plant's closure in September 1994. It outsold the [Mazda 323](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_323), despite being almost identical to it because the Laser was manufactured in Australia and Ford was perceived as a local brand.[^cite_note-179] According to research carried out by [Ford Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Australia) in 1984, a third of Laser buyers were unaware that the Ford model was based on the [Mazda 323](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_323).[^cite_note-180]

In New Zealand, the [Ford Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Laser) and [Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Telstar) were assembled alongside the [Mazda 323](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_323) and [626](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_626) until 1997, at the Vehicle Assemblers of New Zealand (VANZ) plant in [Wiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiri), [Auckland](18660332.md).[^cite_note-181] The [Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Sierra) wagon was also assembled in New Zealand, owing to the popularity of [station wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_wagon) in that market.[^cite_note-182]

The scheduled closure of Ford's Australian manufacturing base in 2016 was confirmed on May 23, 2013. Headquartered in the Victorian suburb of Broadmeadows, the company had registered losses worth AU\$600 million over the five years prior to the announcement. It was noted that the corporate fleet and government sales that account for two-thirds of large, local car sales in Australia are insufficient to keep Ford's products profitable and viable in Australia. The decision affected 1200 Ford workers—over 600 employees in Geelong and more than 500 in Broadmeadows—who lost their jobs by October 2016. The closure of Ford's plants in Norlane Geelong and Broadmeadows Melbourne occurred on October 7, 2016.[^cite_note-183]

### Automobiles

See also: [List of Ford vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ford_vehicles), [list of Lincoln vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lincoln_vehicles), and [list of Mercury vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mercury_vehicles)



[Ford Mustangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang) of various years on display at Wenceslas Square, Prague, Czech Republic in 2019. The vehicle is a popular model sold in many countries.

Ford Motor Company sells a broad range of automobiles under the Ford [marque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marque) worldwide, and an additional range of luxury automobiles under the [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_\(automobile\)) marque in the United States. The company has sold vehicles under a number of other marques during its history. The [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(automobile\)) brand was introduced by Ford in 1939, continuing in production until 2011 when poor sales led to its discontinuation.[^cite_note-184] In 1958, Ford introduced the [Edsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsel) brand, but poor sales led to its discontinuation in 1960. In 1985, the [Merkur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkur) brand was introduced in the United States to market products produced by Ford of Europe; it was discontinued in 1989.

Ford acquired the British sports car maker [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin) in 1989, later selling it on March 12, 2007,[^cite_note-185] although retaining an 8% stake.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] Ford purchased [Volvo Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Cars) of Sweden in 1999,[^cite_note-188] selling it to [Zhejiang Geely Holding Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_Geely_Holding_Group) in 2010.

In November 2008, it reduced its 33.4% [controlling interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlling_interest) in [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda) of Japan to a 13.4% non-controlling interest.[^cite_note-189] On November 18, 2010, Ford reduced its stake further to just 3%, citing the reduction of ownership would allow greater flexibility to pursue growth in emerging markets. Ford and Mazda remain strategic partners through exchanges of technological information and joint ventures, including an American joint venture plant in [Flat Rock, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_Rock,_Michigan) called [Auto Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Alliance).[^cite_note-190] In 2015, Ford sold its remaining 3% stake in Mazda.[^cite_note-191]

Ford sold the United Kingdom-based [Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Cars) and [Land Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover) companies and brands to [Tata Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Motors) of India in March 2008.

On April 25, 2018, Ford announced that it planned to phase out all but one of its North American automobile models (the [Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang) will be the sole surviving model) to focus primarily on pickup trucks and SUVs. Ford had also planned to introduce an "Active" crossover version of the next-generation [Focus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus), but canceled those plans due to tariff issues between the United States and China.[^cite_note-192]

### Tractors



A Ford N series tractor

The "Henry Ford and Son Company" began making [Fordson tractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordson_tractor) in Henry's hometown of Springwells (later part of [Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dearborn,_Michigan)), Michigan, from 1907 to 1928, from 1919 to 1932, at [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_\(city\)), Ireland, and between 1933 and 1964 at [Dagenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Dagenham), England, later transferred to [Basildon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basildon). They were also produced in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad) beginning in 1924.

Ford reentered the tractor market in 1939 with the [Ford N-series tractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_N-series_tractor). The Ford 8N, introduced in 1947, became the most popular tractor of all time in North America. Production of the N line of models ended in 1952.

The [Ford NAA tractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_NAA_tractor) was introduced as an entirely new model in 1953. It was a replacement for the Ford N-Series tractors. Larger than the 8N, with a four-cylinder engine, and streamlined styling.

In 1986, Ford expanded its tractor business when it purchased the Sperry-New Holland skid-steer loader and hay baler, hay tools and implement company from [Sperry Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperry_Corporation) and formed Ford-New Holland which bought out [Versatile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versatile_\(company\)) tractors in 1988. This company was bought by [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat) in 1991 and the name changed from Ford New Holland to New Holland. [New Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Holland_Ag) is now part of [CNH Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNH_Global).[^cite_note-203]

## Motorsport

Main article: [Ford Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Racing)

### FIA World Championships

Along with [Shelby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroll_Shelby_International)[^cite_note-206] and [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet),[^cite_note-207] Ford is one of only three American constructors to win titles on the international scene at the [FIA World Championships](248019.md#FIA_World_Championships). As a constructor, Ford won the [World Sportscar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Sportscar_Championship) three times: in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_World_Sportscar_Championship_season) (both [sports prototype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_prototype) category and [Grand Touring (GT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_tourer) category), [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_World_Sportscar_Championship_season) (GT category), and [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_World_Sportscar_Championship_season) (sports prototype category). At the [World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championship) Ford won 4 [World Drivers' Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Rally_Championship_Drivers'_champions) (in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_World_Rally_Championship_season), [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_World_Rally_Championship_season), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Rally_Championship_season) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_World_Rally_Championship_season)) as well as 4 [World Manufacturers' Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Rally_Championship_Manufacturers'_champions) (in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_World_Rally_Championship_season), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Rally_Championship_season), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Rally_Championship_season) and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Rally_Championship_season)). At the [World Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Touring_Car_Championship) Ford helped a Swiss team [Eggenberger Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eggenberger_Motorsport) win entrants' title in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_World_Touring_Car_Championship). As an engine supplier, Ford also won 13 [Formula One World Drivers' Championships](3437295.md) and 10 [Formula One World Constructors' Championships](3437393.md).

Ford is also one of only three constructors, alongside [Mercedes-Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_motorsport) and [McLaren](20994.md), to complete the [Triple Crown of Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_of_Motorsport#Teams_and_manufacturers) (wins at the [Indianapolis 500](159018.md), [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md), and [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix)), a feat that Ford achieved as an *engine manufacturer* by winning the [1968 Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Monaco_Grand_Prix).

### Open-wheel racing

#### IndyCar



Arie Luyendyk's record-setting Ford Cosworth-powered IndyCar from 1996

[IndyCars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_open-wheel_car_racing) with Ford engines first competed in 1935 using a [production-based Ford V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_flathead_V8_engine) in the Miller-Ford racer.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209] A [pushrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_valve_engine) Ford V8 raced with Lotus in 1963, and Ford's first Indy win was in 1965 with a [DOHC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_camshaft#Dual_overhead_camshaft) V8.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] Ford motors, including the Ford-sponsored [DFX engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosworth_DFV#DFX) developed by [Cosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosworth), have won the [Indianapolis 500](159018.md) eighteen times.[^cite_note-212] On May 12, 1996, [Arie Luyendyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arie_Luyendyk), while driving an IndyCar powered by a Ford Cosworth XB engine, broke the [Indianapolis 500 records](3484665.md) for fastest qualification lap (237.498 mph; 382.216 km/h) and fastest qualification four-lap average (236.986 mph; 381.392 km/h).[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] These speed records still stand as of 2022.[^cite_note-215]

#### Formula Ford

[Formula Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Ford), conceived in the UK in 1966, is an entry-level type of [formula racing](1618495.md) with wingless single-seater cars. Many of today's formula racing drivers started their car racing careers in this category.

#### Formula One

Main article: [Ford in Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_in_Formula_One)

See also: [Cosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosworth) and [Red Bull Ford Powertrains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Ford_Powertrains)

Ford was heavily involved in [Formula One](10854.md) for many years and supplied engines to a large number of teams from 1967 until 2004. These engines were designed and manufactured by [Cosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosworth), the racing division that was owned by Ford from 1998 to 2004. Ford-badged engines won 176 Grands Prix between 1967 and 2003 for teams such as [Team Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Lotus) and [McLaren](20994.md). Ford entered Formula One as a constructor in 2000 under the [Jaguar Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_in_Formula_One) name, after buying the [Stewart Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Grand_Prix) team which had been its primary 'works' team in the series since 1997. Jaguar achieved little success in Formula One, and after a turbulent five seasons, Ford withdrew from the category after the [2004 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Formula_One_season), selling both Jaguar Racing (which became [Red Bull Racing](1173193.md)) and Cosworth (to [Gerald Forsythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Forsythe) and [Kevin Kalkhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kalkhoven)).[^cite_note-216]

Ford has announced that it will return to Formula One in 2026 following a partnership with [Red Bull Powertrains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Powertrains), supplying power units to Red Bull Racing and its second team [Racing Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Bulls).[^cite_note-217]

### Stock car racing



[Ryan Blaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Blaney)'s No. 12 car at [Sonoma Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoma_Raceway) in 2023

Ford is one of three manufacturers in [NASCAR](21114.md)'s three major series: the [Cup Series](56571945.md), [Xfinity Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Xfinity_Series), and [Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series). Major teams include [RFK Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFK_Racing), [Team Penske](2321219.md) and [Wood Brothers Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_Brothers_Racing). Ford is represented by the [Mustang GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang) in the Cup Series, and the Xfinity Series and by the [F-150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-150) in the Truck Series. Some of the most successful NASCAR Fords were the aerodynamic fastback [Ford Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Torino), [Ford Torino Talladega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Torino_Talladega), [Mercury Cyclone Spoiler II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Cyclone_Spoiler_II), and [Mercury Montegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Montego), and the aero-era [Ford Thunderbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Thunderbird). The Ford nameplate has won eight [manufacturer's championships](56571945.md#Manufacturers'_Championship) in the Cup Series while Mercury has won one. In the Cup Series, Ford earned its 1,000th victory in the [2013 Quicken Loans 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Quicken_Loans_400) at [Michigan International Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_International_Speedway).[^cite_note-218] The Ford Fusion is also used in the [ARCA Menards Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_Menards_Series).

### Rally

Main article: [Ford World Rally Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_World_Rally_Team)



[Jari-Matti Latvala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jari-Matti_Latvala) driving the [Ford Focus RS WRC 09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus_RS_WRC_09) in 2010

Ford has a long history in [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying) and has been active in the [World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championship) since the beginning of the world championship, the [1973 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_World_Rally_Championship_season). Ford took the [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_World_Rally_Championship_season) manufacturers' title with [Hannu Mikkola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannu_Mikkola), [Björn Waldegård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Waldeg%C3%A5rd), and [Ari Vatanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari_Vatanen) driving the [Ford Escort RS1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escort_RS1800). In the [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_B) era, Ford achieved success with [Ford RS200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_RS200). Since the [1999 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Rally_Championship_season), Ford has used various versions of the [Ford Focus WRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus_WRC) to much success. In the [2006 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Rally_Championship_season), [BP-Ford World Rally Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP-Ford_World_Rally_Team) secured Ford its second manufacturers' title, with the Focus RS WRC 06 built by [M-Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Sport) and driven by "[Flying Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Finns)" [Marcus Grönholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Gr%C3%B6nholm) and [Mikko Hirvonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikko_Hirvonen).[^cite_note-219] Continuing with Grönholm and Hirvonen, Ford successfully defended the manufacturers' world championship in the [2007 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Rally_Championship_season). Ford is the only manufacturer to score in the points for 92 consecutive races; since the [2002 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_World_Rally_Championship_season) opener [Monte Carlo Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Rally).[^cite_note-220]

In March 1951, the Henri Loos and Henri Berney broke the cross-Africa record of the [Algiers-Cape Town Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers-Cape_Town_Rally) with a 1950 Ford V8, from Cape Town to Paris.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223]

### Rallycross

[Toomas Heikkinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toomas_Heikkinen) won the Global RallyCross Championship title in 2013 and [Joni Wiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Wiman) won it in 2014. ### Sports cars

Main article: [Ford GT § Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_GT#Racing)

Ford sports cars have been visible in the world of sports car racing since 1964. Most notably the [Ford GT40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_GT40) won the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) four times (in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) and [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans)) and is the only American car to ever win overall at this prestigious event. Ford also won four titles at the [World Sportscar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Sportscar_Championship) with the GT40. Swiss team [Matech GT Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matech_Concepts), in collaboration with Ford Racing, opened a new chapter with the [Ford GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_GT), winning the Teams title in the 2008 [FIA GT3 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_GT3_European_Championship).

#### Ford Mustang

Main article: [Ford Mustang § Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang#Racing)



A GT racing version of the [Ford Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang), competing in the [Koni Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_Continental_Tire_Sports_Car_Challenge) in 2005

The [Ford Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang) has arguably been Ford's most successful sports car. [Jerry Titus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Titus) won the 1965 SCCA Pro B National Championship with a Mustang and the model went on to earn Ford the SCCA [Trans-Am Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Am_Series) title in both 1966 and 1967. Ford won the Trans-Am Championship again in 1970 with [Parnelli Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parnelli_Jones) and [George Follmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Follmer) driving [Boss 302 Mustangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boss_302_Mustang) for [Bud Moore Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud_Moore_Engineering). Ford took the 1985 and 1986 [IMSA GTO Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_GT_Championship) with Mustangs driven by [John Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jones_\(racing_driver\)) and [Scott Pruett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Pruett) before returning to Trans-Am glory with a championship in 1989 with [Dorsey Schroeder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsey_Schroeder). Ford dominated Trans-Am in the 1990s with [Tommy Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Kendall) winning championships in 1993, 1995, 1996, and 1997 with [Paul Gentilozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gentilozzi) adding yet another title in 1999. In 2005 the Ford Mustang FR500C took the championship in the Rolex [Koni Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_Continental_Tire_Sports_Car_Challenge) Series in its first year on the circuit. In 2007, Ford added a victory in the GT4 European Championship. 2008 was the first year of the Mustang Challenge for the Miller Cup, a series that pits a full field of identical factory-built Ford Mustang race cars against each other. Also, in 2008, Ford won the manufacturers championship in the [Koni Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_Continental_Tire_Sports_Car_Challenge) Series and HyperSport drivers Joe Foster and Steve Maxwell won the drivers title in a Mustang GT.

Ford and [Michelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin) teamed up to provide custom-engineered tires for the [Ford Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Performance) vehicle lineup. [Ford Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Performance) director Dave Pericak said: "That confidence extends from our upcoming racing effort at Le Mans in 2016 with the all-new [Ford GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_GT), to the Ford Performance vehicle lineup, including the [Shelby GT350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_GT350) and F-150 Raptor".[^cite_note-224]

## Environmental initiatives

### Flexible fuel vehicles



The Ford Focus Flexifuel was the first [E85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ethanol_fuel_mixtures#E85) [flexible fuel vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible_fuel_vehicle) commercially available in the European market.

[Flexible fuel vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible_fuel_vehicle) are designed to operate smoothly using a wide range of available [ethanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) mixtures—from pure gasoline to [bioethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethanol)-gasoline blends such as [E85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E85) (85% [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) and 15% gasoline) or [E100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ethanol_fuel_mixtures) (neat [hydrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrous) ethanol) in Brazil. Part of the challenge of successful marketing alternative and flexible fuel vehicles in the U.S. is the general lack of establishment of sufficient [fueling stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fueling_station), which would be essential for these vehicles to be attractive to a wide range of consumers. Significant efforts to ramp up production and distribution of [E85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E85) fuels are underway and expanding.[^cite_note-225]

#### Hybrid electric vehicles

See also: [Hybrid electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle)



[Ford Escape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape) [plug-in hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) test vehicle



[Alan Mulally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Mulally) (wearing red tie) with then-President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) at the [Kansas City Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Assembly) plant in [Claycomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claycomo,_Missouri), Missouri, on March 20, 2007, touting Ford's new hybrid cars

In 2004, Ford and Toyota agreed a patent-sharing accord that granted Ford access to certain hybrid technology patented by Toyota; in exchange, Ford licensed some of its own patents to Toyota.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228] In 2004, Ford introduced the [Escape Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape_Hybrid). With this vehicle, Ford was third to the automotive market with a [hybrid electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle) and the first hybrid electric [SUV](47696.md) to market. This was also the first hybrid electric vehicle with a [flexible fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible_fuel) capability to run on [E85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E85).[^cite_note-229] The Escape's platform mate [Mercury Mariner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Mariner) was also available with the hybrid-electric system in the 2006 model year—a full year ahead of schedule. The similar [Mazda Tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_Tribute) will also receive a hybrid-electric powertrain option, along with many other vehicles in the Ford vehicle line.

In 2005, Ford announced a goal to make 250,000 hybrids a year by 2010, but by mid-2006 announced that it would not meet that goal, due to excessively high costs and the lack of sufficient supplies of the hybrid-electric batteries and drivetrain system components.[^cite_note-230] Instead, Ford has committed to accelerating development of next-generation hybrid-electric power plants in Britain, in collaboration with Volvo. This engineering study is expected to yield more than 100 new hybrid-electric vehicle models and derivatives.

In September 2007, Ford announced a partnership with [Southern California Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California_Edison) (SCE) to examine how [plug-in hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) will work with the electrical grid. Under the multimillion-dollar, multi-year project, Ford will convert a demonstration fleet of [Ford Escape Hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape_Hybrid) into plug-in hybrids, and SCE will evaluate how the vehicles might interact with the home and the utility's electrical grid. Some of the vehicles will be evaluated "in typical customer settings", according to Ford.[^cite_note-akhrum-40][^cite_note-edison-41]

On June 12, 2008, [USDOE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDOE) expanded its own fleet of alternative fuel and advanced technology vehicles with the addition of a Ford Escape Plug-In Hybrid Flex-Fuel Vehicle. The vehicle is equipped with a 10-kilowatt (13 hp) [lithium-ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion) battery supplied by [Johnson Controls-Saft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Controls-Saft) that stores enough electric energy to drive up to 30 miles (48 km) at speeds of up to 40 mph (64 km/h).[^cite_note-231] In March 2009, Ford launched hybrid versions of the [Ford Fusion Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fusion_Hybrid) and the [Mercury Milan Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Milan_Hybrid) in the United States, both as 2010 models.[^cite_note-GreenCar0409-232]

As of November 2014, Ford has produced for retail sales the following hybrid electric vehicles: [Ford Escape Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape_Hybrid) (2004–2012), [Mercury Mariner Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Mariner#Hybrid) (2005–2010), [Mercury Milan Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Milan_Hybrid) (2009–2010), [Ford Fusion Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fusion_Hybrid) (2009–present), [Lincoln MKZ Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_MKZ_Hybrid) (2010–present), [Ford C-Max Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_C-Max_Hybrid) (2012–present), and [Ford Mondeo Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mondeo_Hybrid) (2014–present). By June 2012, Ford had sold 200,000 full hybrids in the U.S. since 2004,[^cite_note-Ford200K-233] and, as of September 2014, the carmaker has sold over 344,000 hybrids in the United States.[^cite_note-Ford200K-233][^cite_note-Sales2012US-234][^cite_note-Sales2013US-235][^cite_note-Sales092014US-236] The top selling hybrids in the U.S. market are the Fusion Hybrid with 127,572 units, followed by Escape Hybrid with 117,997 units, and the C-Max Hybrid with 54,236.[^cite_note-Sales2012US-234][^cite_note-Sales2013US-235][^cite_note-Sales092014US-236][^cite_note-Sales2011US-237][^cite_note-DoEHEV10-238] As of November 2014, Ford is the world's second-largest manufacturer of hybrids after [Toyota Motor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Motor_Corporation), with 400,000 hybrid electric vehicles produced since their introduction in 2004.[^cite_note-239]

#### 2025 investment

In August 2025, Ford announced that it was investing almost \$5 billion to produce lower-cost EV products. \$2 billion will be directed towards its [Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky) factory to develop lower-cost electric vehicle components as well as a new "assembly tree" manufacturing process. Rather than one long conveyor, three subassembly lines will operate simultaneously and then converge together. The first vehicle from the Louisville factory is set to launch in 2027 and will be a medium, four-door pickup truck at a starting price of \$30,000. The Louisville location will also stop production of the [Ford Escape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Escape) and [Lincoln Corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Corsair) by 2026.[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-Boudette-267] Meanwhile, \$3 billion is intended to build a new battery plant in Michigan.[^cite_note-268] The announcement follows Ford's electric vehicle division losing \$12 billion over the last two and a half years, including \$2.2 billion in the first half of 2025.[^cite_note-Boudette-267] Low utilization of the battery factories motivated Ford to create a new division for containerized [grid batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_energy_storage_system) in 2026.[^cite_note-269]

### Hydrogen

Ford also continues to study [fuel cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell)-powered electric powertrains and has demonstrated hydrogen-fueled [internal combustion engine](41228673.md) technologies, as well as developing the next-generation hybrid-electric systems. Compared with conventional vehicles, [hybrid vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle) and fuel cell vehicles decrease air pollution emissions as well as sound levels with favorable impacts upon respiratory health and decrease of [noise health effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_health_effects).

Ford has launched the production of [hydrogen-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_vehicle) shuttle buses, using hydrogen instead of gasoline in a standard [internal combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_vehicle#Internal_combustion_vehicle), for use at airports and convention centers.[^cite_note-270] At the 2006 [Greater Los Angeles Auto Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Auto_Show), Ford showcased a hydrogen fuel cell version of its Explorer SUV. The Fuel cell Explorer has a combined output of 174 hp (130 kW). It has a large hydrogen storage tank which is situated in the center of the car taking the original place of the conventional model's automatic transmission. The centered position of the tank assists the vehicle reach a notable range of 350 miles (563 km), the farthest for a fuel cell vehicle so far. The fuel cell Explorer the first in a series of prototypes partly funded by the [United States Department of Energy](62866.md) to expand efforts to determine the feasibility of hydrogen-powered vehicles. The fuel cell Explorer is one of several vehicles with green technology being featured at the L.A. show, including the 2008 Ford Escape Hybrid, PZEV emissions compliant Fusion and Focus models and a 2008 Ford F-Series Super Duty outfitted with Ford's clean diesel technology.

### Increased fuel efficiency

In July 2008, Ford Motor Company announced that it would accelerate its plans to produce more fuel-efficient cars, changing both its North American manufacturing plans and its lineup of vehicles available in the United States. In terms of North American manufacturing, the company planned to convert three existing pickup truck and sport utility vehicle ([SUV](47696.md)) plants for small car production, with the first conversion at its Michigan Truck Plant. In addition, Ford's assembly plants near Mexico City, Mexico, and in Louisville, Kentucky, were to be converted from pickups and SUVs to small cars, including the Ford Fiesta, by 2011. Ford then also planned to introduce to North America six of its European small vehicles, including two versions of the Ford Fiesta, by the end of 2012.[^cite_note-271]

Ford of Europe developed the [ECOnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECOnetic) programme to address the market and legislative need for higher [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency) and lower CO<sub>2</sub> emissions. As opposed to the [hybrid engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_engine) technology used in competitor products such as the [Toyota Prius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Prius), ECOnetic improves existing technology. Using lower-consuming [Duratorq TDCi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Duratorq_engine) diesel engines, and based on a combination of improved aerodynamics, lower resistance, and improved efficiency, the [Ford Fiesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fiesta) was the lowest-emitting mass-produced car in Europe[^cite_note-272] while the [2012 Ford Focus ECOnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus_\(international\)) will have better fuel consumption than the Prius or the [Volkswagen Golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Golf) [BlueMotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlueMotion).[^cite_note-273] ECOnetic is not presently planned to be sold in North American due to current perceived lower consumer demand.[^cite_note-274]

Ford has challenged University teams to create a vehicle that is simple, durable, lightweight, and comes equipped with a base target price of only \$7,000. The students from [Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen_University) created the "2015 Ford Model T".[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276]

In 2000, under the leadership of the current Ford chairman, William Clay Ford, the company announced[^cite_note-277] a planned 25 percent improvement in the average mileage of its SUVs—to be completed by the 2005 [calendar year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_year). In 2003, Ford announced that competitive market conditions and technological and cost challenges would prevent the company from achieving this goal.[^cite_note-278]

For the 2007 model year, Ford had thirteen U.S. models that achieve 30 miles per gallon or better (based on the highway fuel economy estimates of the EPA) and several of Ford's vehicles were recognized in the EPA and Department of Energy Fuel Economy Guide for best-in-class fuel economy. Ford claimed to have eliminated nearly three million pounds of smog-forming emissions from its U.S. cars and light trucks over the 2004 to 2006 model years.[^cite_note-279] However, the United States Environmental Protection Agency has linked Ford to 54 [Superfund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfund) toxic waste sites, twelve of which have been cleaned up and deleted from the list.[^cite_note-280]

### Efficient buildings



[Green roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_roof) that covers part of the [Ford River Rouge Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_River_Rouge_Complex).

As part of a renovation of the [Ford River Rouge Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_River_Rouge_Complex), in 2010 Ford unveiled a 10.4-acre (42,000 m<sup>2</sup>) living roof covering part of the Dearborn Truck plant, consisting of [sedum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedum), a low-growing groundcover. The sedum retains and cleanses rainwater and moderates the internal temperature of the building, saving energy.[^cite_note-281]

In 2016, Ford announced a ten-year renovation plan for its Dearborn campus.[^cite_note-282] The plan features consolidation of office and lab spaces in to fewer and much larger buildings, which will be built to [LEED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LEED) standards, and will feature extensive use of wetlands and park spaces.[^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284] The new campus features new multi-story parking decks with solar power-generating roofs,[^cite_note-285] and a new natural gas power plant. The DTE Ford Central Energy Plant is a 34MW combined heat-and-power plant which features a high efficiency design and LEED Gold buildings.[^cite_note-286]
