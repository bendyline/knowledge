**Kweon Seong-dong** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 권성동; born 29 April 1960) is a South Korean lawyer and politician. A member of the [People Power Party](63143730.md), he served as the [member of the National Assembly](601423.md) for [Gangneung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangneung) from 2009 to 2026.

Before entering politics, Kweon worked as a prosecutor and lawyer at various prosecution offices and the [Ministry of Justice](38952613.md). He made his political debut at the 2009 Gangneung by-election which he won with about 51% of popular votes. He made himself known through his role at the [2016 political scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_South_Korean_political_scandal), which led to the [impeachment of Park Geun-hye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Park_Geun-hye).

Kweon has held several high-ranking leadership positions within his party. He served as the floor leader of the [People Power Party](63143730.md) in 2022 and again from 2024 to 2025. Additionally, he has stepped in as the party's acting leader on four separate occasions: twice in 2022, once in December 2024, and most recently in May 2025. He also previously served as the secretary-general for both the [People Power Party](63143730.md) and its predecessor, the [Saenuri Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saenuri_Party).

In January 2026, Kweon was convicted of taking bribes from the [Unification Church](512933.md) and sentenced to two years in prison. On July 16, 2026, the [Supreme Court](4899540.md) upheld the conviction, which resulted in his removal from political office.

## Early life and education

Kweon was born in [Gangneung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangneung), [Gangwon Province, South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangwon_Province_\(South_Korea\)) in 1960.[^cite_note-kweon-1] He is the eldest of the three sons and a daughter of a housewife and Kweon Seung-ohk (died in 2022), was an educator and the former Director of Gangneung Myeongnyun Institution.[^cite_note-seong-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] He attended [Gangneung Myeongnyun High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangneung_Myeongnyun_High_School?action=edit\&redlink=1) before joining [Chung-Ang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chung-Ang_University), where he studied law.[^cite_note-kweon-1]

## Career

Although Kweon participated in anti-dictatorship movements in 1980, he chose to prepare for the [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(law\)) instead of continuing his activism as he did not agree with "radical changes".[^cite_note-seong-2] He was qualified for the bar in 1985.[^cite_note-kweon-1]

Shortly after completing [national service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_South_Korea), he began his prosecutor career at [Suwon](502693.md) District Prosecutor's Office in March 1991, where he worked till transferred to [Gangneung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangneung) branch of Chuncheon District Public Prosecutors Office in September 1993.[^cite_note-kweon-1] He then moved to Seoul, where he worked at Seoul Central District Prosecutors' Office, the [Ministry of Justice](38952613.md), Seoul Eastern District Prosecutors' Offices, and the [Supreme Prosecutors' Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Prosecutors'_Office_of_the_Republic_of_Korea), between 1994 and 2000, and again between 2003 and 2005.[^cite_note-kweon-1] His prosecutor career came to the end at [Gwangju](323128.md) High Prosecutor's Office in 2006.[^cite_note-kweon-1] He then briefly worked as a lawyer at Sojong Partners.[^cite_note-kweon-1]

While he was working at the Ministry of Justice in 1999, Kweon made a draft of the National Human Rights Commission Act that was later submitted to the [National Assembly](601423.md).[^cite_note-seong-2] The bill was, however, scrapped by the then ruling party, [Millennium Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Democratic_Party) (MDP), who instead established the [National Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Human_Rights_Commission_of_Korea) based on the demand of [left](18499.md)-leaning [non-governmental organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organisations) (NGO).[^cite_note-seong-2] This made him to join politics.[^cite_note-seong-2]

### Politics

In the [2008 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_South_Korean_legislative_election), Kweon made an unsuccessful attempt to contest for Gangneung under the [Grand National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Party) (GNP) banner.[^cite_note-kweon-1][^cite_note-seong-2]

On 8 April 2022, Kweon was elected parliamentary leader of the People Power Party, defeating Cho Hae-jin.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

On 7 May 2025, Kweon went on a hunger strike as part of efforts to pressure the PPP into committing to a common candidate for the [2025 South Korean presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_South_Korean_presidential_election). This followed inconclusive negotiations between PPP candidate [Kim Moon-soo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Moon-soo_\(politician\)) and independent candidate [Han Duck-soo](4827727.md).[^cite_note-9] On 11 September 2025, the National Assembly approved a motion to arrest Kweon on charges of bribery from the [Unification Church](512933.md).[^cite_note-10] On 17 September, the Seoul Central District Court issued an arrest warrant against him.[^cite_note-11]

In January 2026, Kweon was convicted of taking bribes from the Unification Church.[^cite_note-12] The conviction was upheld by the [Seoul High Court](82175690.md)[^cite_note-13] and the [Supreme Court](4899540.md). He was given a two-year jail term and ordered to forfeit the sum he received. His conviction resulted in his removal from political office.[^cite_note-14]
