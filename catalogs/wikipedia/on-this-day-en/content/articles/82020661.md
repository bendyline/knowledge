A constitutional referendum was held in [Thailand](30128.md) alongside the [general election](74706351.md) on 8 February 2026 to ask voters whether they approve beginning the process of writing a new constitution to replace the [current one](6840408.md) adopted in 2017. It was approved by 60% of voters with a 69% turnout.[^cite_note-1]

Caretaker Prime Minister [Anutin Charnvirakul](60867789.md) officially began the process of holding a referendum on 23 December 2025 and the date was officially announced by a *[Royal Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gazette_\(Thailand\))* published on 2 January 2026. The date of the referendum was chosen to coincide with the [2026 general election](74706351.md) for the efficient use of public funds.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

See also: [2007 Thai constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Thai_constitutional_referendum) and [2016 Thai constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Thai_constitutional_referendum)

Following the [2006 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_coup_d'etat_in_Thailand) that ousted Prime Minister [Thaksin Shinawatra](360815.md), the ruling military junta of the [Council for National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_National_Security) repealed the [1997 "People's" Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_constitution_of_Thailand) and introduced an [interim constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_interim_constitution_of_Thailand). The 1997 constitution was Thailand's most democratic constitution: it created a bicameral legislature where both houses were directly and democratically elected, and the prime minister was elected solely by the [National Assembly](14846736.md).[^cite_note-Criminal_Justice-4] The new [2007 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_constitution_of_Thailand) was drafted and then adopted after Thailand's first [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Thai_constitutional_referendum) was held on 19 August 2007. A majority (57.81%) of voters were in favour of the new constitution, whilst opposition was concentrated in the [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thailand) and [northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Thailand) where Thaksin and his parties were popular.[^cite_note-5] The referendum was followed by the return of [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Thai_general_election) in December. However, the new referendum was also seen as an attempt to contain the influence of Thaksin and his allies.[^cite_note-6] Between 2007 and 2014, Thaksin-friendly politicians became Prime Ministers and in 2011 his sister, [Yingluck Shinawatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingluck_Shinawatra), was elected prime minister. A [political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%932014_Thai_political_crisis) led to her removal in 2014, followed by a coup d'état in 2014 that replaced the 2007 constitution with the [2014 interim constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_interim_constitution_of_Thailand). The ruling military junta of the [National Council for Peace and Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_for_Peace_and_Order) began drafting a new constitution and criticism of it was banned. The [2016 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Thai_constitutional_referendum) saw 61.4% of voters approve of it, and it was adopted in 2017.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-WT-20160329-8][^cite_note-ST-20160404-9] The 2017 constitution had no clause that allows for a complete rewrite.[^cite_note-\:3-10] Attempts to amend or rewrite the 2017 constitution have existed since its adoption.[^cite_note-11]

## Constitutional reform

### Rulings by the Constitutional Court

Following the removal of Prime Minister [Paetongtarn Shinawatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paetongtarn_Shinawatra) by the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Thailand) on 29 August 2025, the conservative [Bhumjaithai Party](20737916.md) entered into negotiations with the progressive [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(Thailand,_2024\)) to secure their support to back Anutin Charnvirakul as prime minister. In an agreement between the two parties, the People's Party agreed to support Anutin on the terms that Anutin dissolve the House within four months, Bhumjaithai must maintain a minority coalition, and must also initiate the process of amending the 2017 constitution and hold a referendum if required.[^cite_note-12] The referendum on whether to amend the constitution would then lead to the establishment of an elected constitution drafting assembly and must be held no later than the general election. If the Constitutional Court did not find a referendum necessary, an elected drafting assembly would be established.[^cite_note-13]

On 10 September, the Constitutional Court voted 6-1 that three referendums are required to begin the process of drafting a new constitution, and members of the Constitution Drafting Assembly (CDA) cannot be directly elected. The ruling followed a petition by [Wan Muhamad Noor Matha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wan_Muhamad_Noor_Matha), speaker of the [House of Representatives](4232934.md), under Section 210 (1)(2). In a 5–2 vote, the court found that the House had the authority to begin the charter rewrite process but this must be preceded by a referendum asking voters if they approve of a rewrite. In the three referendums required by the court, the first should ask whether a new constitution should be drafted, the second will seek public opinion on the principles and methods of the rewrite, whilst the third will ask if voters approve of the new constitution. The first and second referendums may be combined.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Constitutional Amendment bill



A citizen casting ballot for the referendum at a polling station in [Sawankhalok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawankhalok), [Sukhothai province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhothai_province) on 8 February 2026

Following the Court's ruling, the People's Party, [Pheu Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheu_Thai_Party) and Bhumjaithai submitted their own separate draft constitutional amendment bills to [Parliament](14846736.md) to amend Article 256 of Chapter 15 and to add an extra chapter to allow for a new constitution to be drafted.[^cite_note-\:3-10] Chapter 15 describes the process to amend the constitution.[^cite_note-16] Parliament met on October 14 and 15 to discuss the three proposals.[^cite_note-\:3-10] Pheu Thai's version proposed the establishment of a CDA with 151 members. 100 members were to be chosen by Parliament from an election of 300 candidates, whilst the other 51 would be nominated and chosen by Parliament and the Cabinet. A drafting committee of 27 members would then be formed and would consist of 14 members from the CDA and 13 from other fields like law and political science. After draft amendments are completed, they would be submitted to Parliament for approval before a public referendum would confirm them.[^cite_note-17] The People's Party's version proposed that a CDA of 35 members selected by Parliament from a list of 70 people elected by voters be formed along an advisory council of 100 elected members with the goal of gathering public opinion to advise the CDA. Bhumjaithai's version proposes that the CDA should be made of 99 members made up of 77 selected by Parliament from a list so each province is represented by one member and 22 experts in law, political science, and other relevant fields. Members of the drafting committee would then be selected by the CDA. It also reduces the number of Senate votes needed to pass amendments from a third to a fifth of senators. The Bhumjaithai and Pheu Thai bills prohibit changes to the political system and state structure described in Chapters 1 and 2, whilst the People's Party contains elements from those two chapters.[^cite_note-\:3-10]

On October 15, Parliament (both the House and Senate) voted to pass the People's Party and Bhumjaithai's bills in their first readings. The People's Party's bill received 460 votes from MPs and 108 from Senators (total of 568), whilst the Bhumjaithai's bill received 462 votes from MPs and 167 votes from Senators (total of 629). Pheu Thai's bill received 561 votes, only 60 coming from the Senate. It failed to pass as it failed to gain the support of a third of Senators. A 43 member committee of 12 Senators and 31 MPs from eight parties (People's Party, Pheu Thai, Bhumjaithai, [Kla Tham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kla_Tham_Party), [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Thailand\)), [Thai Sang Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Sang_Thai_Party), [Palang Pracharath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palang_Pracharath_Party), [Chart Thai Pattana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart_Thai_Pattana_Party), and [Prachachart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prachachat_Party)) was then formed to scrutinise the bills, with the People's Party's bill being the main draft.[^cite_note-18] The committee held its first meeting on October 19 and elected Nutthawut Buaprathum, party-list MP for People's Party, as chairman. The three deputy chairmen were Chawengsak Rengpaiboon, MP for Bhumjaithai; Tawee Sodsong, MP for Prachachart, and Senator Premsak Piayura.[^cite_note-19] The panel was empowered to recommend changes to the draft bills to ensure they met the requirements stated by the Constitutional Court.[^cite_note-20] By the end of November, the committee finished its review of the bill. At the same time, the cabinet approved a special parliamentary session for December 10 and 11 for a second reading on the bill.[^cite_note-21]

On December 10, Parliament voted to approve establishing a 35 member CDA and a 35 member public participation committee. Parliament also voted 328 to 266 to use the "20 pick 1" formula to choose members.[^cite_note-22] The formula stipulates that a group of 20 MPs and Senators each nominates one member for the CDA. Pheu Thai were the main opponents of the formula.[^cite_note-23] Parliament later voted 494 to 1 to approve the first referendum question on whether there should be a new constitution and the question was sent to the Cabinet.[^cite_note-24] On the amendment to Article 256/28, Parliament voted to reject the committee's proposal that future amendments on the constitution should be approved by a simple majority of a joint-sitting to preserve the Senate's one-third veto power. The article was rejected by several MPs from Bhumjaithai and the governing coalition.[^cite_note-25] The People's Party then began the process to call a vote of no confidence. Before a vote could be called, Anutin dissolved the House of Representatives and began the process for holding a general election in 2026 on December 12.[^cite_note-26] On December 19, the cabinet approved the referendum question and forwarded it to the Electoral Commission.[^cite_note-27]

### Organisations

On 6 February 2026, a group of 80 pro-democracy and human rights organisations issued a joint statement in support of the referendum, saying that the current constitution lacked any democratic legitimacy. Such groups included the Anti-Corruption People's Network, Campaign Committee for Democracy, the Human Rights Lawyers Association, the People's Movement for a Just Society, and the People's Network for Elections.[^cite_note-38]
