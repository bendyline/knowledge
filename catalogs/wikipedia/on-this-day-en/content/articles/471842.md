The **prime minister of Fiji** is the [head of government](37570.md) of the [Republic of Fiji](10707.md). The prime minister is appointed under the terms of the [2013 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Constitution_of_Fiji). The prime minister is the head of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Fiji) and appoints and dismisses ministers.

## Description of the office

As a [former British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Fiji), [Fiji](10707.md) has largely adopted British political models and follows the [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system), or Cabinet, system of government, in which the executive branch of government is responsible to the legislature. Under the [2013 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Constitution_of_Fiji), the prime minister is the leader of the political party which has won more than half of the total number of seats in Parliament. If no such party exist, the Parliament elects the prime minister.

The prime minister of Fiji is technically the "[first among equals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_among_equals)," whose vote in meetings of the Cabinet carries no greater weight than that of any other [minister](739237.md). In practice, the prime minister dominates the government. Other ministers are appointed by the prime minister.

## History of the office

*Ratu* Sir [Kamisese Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamisese_Mara) was appointed Fiji's first prime minister on 10 October 1970, when Fiji attained its independence from Britain. Mara previously served as Fiji's first and only [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Fiji), from 20 September 1967 (while Fiji still was a British colony). Mara's first term as prime minister lasted until 13 April 1987. He returned to the office for the second term on 5 December 1987, serving until 2 June 1992. As of 2014, Mara is the longest-serving prime minister of Fiji.[^cite_note-3]
