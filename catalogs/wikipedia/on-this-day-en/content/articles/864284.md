**Hargeisa**[^cite_note-Jubba-4][^cite_note-5] ([/hɑːrˈɡeɪsə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*har-GAY-sə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language): *Hargeysa*; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): هرجيسا, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Hargēsā*) is the capital and largest city of the [Republic of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Somaliland), a [breakaway state](144732.md) of [Somalia](27358.md), where it is the second-largest city. It is still considered internationally to be part of [Somalia](27358.md). It is also the regional capital of the [Maroodi Jeex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroodi_Jeex) region of Somaliland.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Hargeisa was founded as a watering and trading stop between the coast and the interior by the [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_Sultanate). Initially it served as a watering well for the vast livestock of the [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq) clan that inhabited that specific region and later were joined by other Isaaq clans that currently inhabit Hargeisa. In 1960, the [Somaliland Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Protectorate) gained independence from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and as scheduled united days later with the [Trust Territory of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_Somaliland) (former [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland)) to form the [Somali Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Republic) on 1 July.[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2002_p.835-8] Up to 90% of the city was destroyed during the [Isaaq genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_genocide), a state-sponsored campaign of violence during the [Somaliland War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_War_of_Independence).

Hargeisa is situated in a valley in the [Galgodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogo_Mountains) (Ogo) highlands, and sits at an elevation of 1,334 m (4,377 ft). Home to [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art) from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period, the city is also a commercial hub for precious stone-cutting, construction, retail services and trading, among other activities.[^cite_note-Jubba-4]

## Etymology

The town evolved in the latter half of the 1800s as a [Qadiriyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadiriyya) settlement established by [Sheikh Madar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Madar), near a water-stop used by nomadic stock-herders on the way to the town of Harar. It proposes a possible derivation of the name "Hargeisa" from the sobriquet *Harar as-sagir*, meaning "[Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) the little"[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Encyclopaedia_Aethiopica-10] According to historian Norman Bennett, Madar named the settlement Hargeisa or *Little Harar* since he aspired for it to emulate the city of Harar as a center for Islamic teachings.[^cite_note-11] Another etymological root for Hargeisa's name derives from the town's connection to the skins trade. Hargeisa has been a watering and trading stop between the coast and the interior, and chief amongst the goods traded were the hide skins procured from the interior to be processed in the settlement. In this etymological version, 'Hargeisa' is derived from *hargageys*, which means "place to sell hides and skins" in [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## History

### Prehistory



Rock art from the [Laas Geel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laas_Geel) complex on the outskirts of Hargeisa.

Numerous [cave paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting) from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period are found in the [Laas Geel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laas_Geel) complex, on the outskirts of Hargeisa. During November and December 2002, an archaeological survey was carried out in the area by a French team of researchers. The expedition's objective was to search for rock shelters and caves containing stratified archaeological infills capable of documenting the period when production economy appeared in this part of Somaliland (c. [5th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_millennium_BCE) and 2nd millennium BCE). During the course of the survey, the French archaeological team discovered the Laas Geel [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art), encompassing an area of ten rock alcoves (caves). In an excellent state of preservation, the paintings show human figures with their hands raised and facing long-horned, humpless [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle).[^cite_note-African_Archeology_Volume_1_2003-15]

The rock art had been known to the area's inhabitants for centuries before the French discovery. However, the existence of the site had not been broadcast to the international community. In November 2003, a mission returned to Laas Geel and a team of experts undertook a detailed study of the paintings and their prehistoric context.

Somaliland generally is home to numerous such [archaeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site), with similar rock art and/or ancient edifices such as the [Dhambalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhambalin) rock art. However, many of these old structures have yet to be properly explored, a process which would help shed further light on local history and facilitate their preservation for posterity.[^cite_note-Hodd-16]

### The Big Commune and Sheikh Madar



Close-up of an 1885 [Royal Geographical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Geographical_Society) map, showing Hargeisa (Harrer-es-Sagheer) as well as the [Eidagale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garhajis#Eidagale) subtribe (Eed-a-galleh) residing within and around the town. The Naasa Hablood hills (Nas Hubla) can also be seen in the map

According to traditional poetic (gabay) oral accounts, Hargeisa was founded by the [Arap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arap) as a watering and trading stop for passing nomads and caravans. It is believed that and the [Habar Yoonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habar_Yoonis) and the [Eidagale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidagale) subtribes were amongst the early settlers of Hargeisa.[^cite_note-17] Hargeisa continued to grow with the arrival of [Sheikh Madar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Madar) Shirwa, widely considered to be the founder of Hargeisa religious commune and the modern iteration of the settlement.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]



[Sheikh Madar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Madar) (right) and Sultan [Deria Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deria_Hassan) in Hargeisa 1912

Madar Shirwa was born in [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) and belonged to the Nuh Ismail subdivision of the Sa'ad Musa [Habr Awal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habr_Awal). Madar arrived in [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) to study the Islamic Sciences under the top ulema of Harar, which consisted of [Harari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harari_people) and [Somali](1571696.md) scholars.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Sheikh Khalil, one of the ulema of Harar, advised [Sheikh Madar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Madar) to establish a [Qadiriyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadiriyya) [tariqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqa) commune in present-day Hargeisa and spread the teachings he was taught, which lead Sheikh Madar and his companions to found the Big Commune (Jama'a weyne) of Little Harar (Hargeisa) in c. 1860. Somali pastoralists heavily follow rain and pastures this would change with the agricultural and stationary lifestyle Madar would introduce on the back of large [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum) plantations. This was to maintain self-sufficiency and Sheikh Madar and the other Mullahs would take care of the sick and elderly inhabitants of the growing settlement. Lastly, Sheikh Madar pushed towards a common religious identity rather than identifying solely by tribe. Stone houses and other structures would be built and Hargeisa would develop into a large permanent settlement irrespective of the caravan trade that defined it in decades prior.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] [Sheikh Madar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Madar) met with the 4th Isaaq Grand Sultan [Deria Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deria_Hassan) outside Hargeisa in a famous 1870 *shir* (meeting) to discuss issues regarding the new town of Hargeisa and agreed that poaching and tree cutting in the vicinity should be banned.[^cite_note-25]

H. Swayne, a British soldier and explorer who traversed the Somali peninsula between the 1880-1890s wrote about Hargeisa in his journals:

> This town is built some five hundred yards from the right bank of the Aleyadera nala, and at an elevation of thirty or forty feet above it. Round the place is a patch of jowari (sorghum) cultivation, two and a half miles long and a quarter of a mile broad. Quantities of livestock of all kinds graze on the low undulating hills for half a mile from the Aleyadera nala on either bank. Hargeisa is situated on two important caravan routes, one from Ogaden and the other from Harar. There are good direct camel-roads to [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) and [Bulhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulhar). Supplies of rice, tobacco, and dates can sometimes be bought here in the trading season. Some four hundred people are employed looking after the jowari fields, and may be seen sitting on platforms, shouting and throwing stones to scare birds from the crops. There is abundance of good water in the bed of the river, and a masonry well has been built, and is kept in order by an Arab from Aden. The town is full of blind and lame people, who are under the protection of Sheikh Mattar and his mullahs.[^cite_note-26]

### British Somaliland



Women's market in Hargeisa, [British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somaliland) [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate).

In 1888, after signing successive treaties with the then ruling [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people) [Sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) and chiefs, the British established a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) in the region referred to as [British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somaliland) with [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) becoming the capital.[^cite_note-27] The British garrisoned the protectorate from [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Settlement) in present-day [Yemen](350939.md), and administered it from their [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire) until 1898. British Somaliland was then administered by the [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office) until 1905 and afterwards by the [Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Office).

The capital was moved from Berbera to Hargeisa, and the city was granted capital status in 1941. During the [East African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Campaign_\(World_War_II\)), the protectorate was [invaded by Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_British_Somaliland) in August 1940, but recaptured by the British in March 1941. In 1945 the British administration had grown weary of the influence *[wadaads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh)* had wielded in the protectorate and moved to arrest several in Hargeisa. They had been deemed nuisances to implementing 'modern education' and agricultural reforms in Somaliland. Following their arrests on June 4, a mob formed and temporarily freed the imprisoned *wadaads* who were recaptured the next day. Religious leaders motivated the city's inhabitants and a crowd of several thousand rioters marched on the Hargeisa District Headquarters determined to free the imprisoned *wadaads*. They were fired upon by the District Commissioner's troops with one dying and dozens imprisoned for trying to free the Sheikhs.[^cite_note-28] The protectorate gained its independence on 26 June 1960 as the [State of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Somaliland),[^cite_note-nytimes6-29][^cite_note-autogenerated1-30] before uniting as planned days later with the [Trust Territory of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_Somaliland) (the former [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland)) to form the [Somali Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Republic).[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2002_p.835-8]

### 1960–1991



[MiG monument in Hargeisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_War_Memorial) commemorating Somaliland's breakaway from the rest of Somalia during the 1980s.

[^cite_note-31]

In the post-independence period, Hargeisa was administered as the capital of the [Woqooyi Galbeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woqooyi_Galbeed) province of Somalia. Numerous new development projects were subsequently launched in the city by the Somali government. Among these initiatives was the creation of the [Hargeisa Provincial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_Provincial_Museum). It was the first museum to be established in Somalia since independence in 1960.[^cite_note-mi1-32] The [Hargeisa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_International_Airport) was also renovated and modernized, with the ultimate aim of equipping the facility to accommodate larger aircraft and offer more flight destinations.[^cite_note-Eiugb-33]

#### SNM offensive

Main articles: [1988 Hargeisa-Burao offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Hargeisa-Burao_offensive) and [Somaliland War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_War_of_Independence)

On 31 May 1988, at 2\:15 a.m, the [Somali National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_National_Movement) (shortened SNM) attacked Hargeisa. With two Habar yunis leaders, one in Burco and the other in the capital city Hargeisa.[^cite_note-\:51-34] The SNM force attacking Hargeisa was estimated at 500 men equipped with 84 vehicles, of whom only 14 were left due to vehicles being sent to the front in Adadley.[^cite_note-\:4-35] The SNM captured the headquarters of the [26th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Division_\(Somalia\)), as well as capturing the Birjeex arms depot where the SNM collected ammunition.[^cite_note-\:4-35] An SNM fighter who partook in the Hargeisa offensive described Somali troops dropping their uniforms on the ground and fleeing.[^cite_note-\:4-35] The SNM encountered stiff resistance from the [Somali Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Army) as they surrounded Radio Hargeisa's headquarters.[^cite_note-\:4-35] Due to heavy bombardment from Somali heavy artillery and tanks, the SNM force tasked with capturing Hargeisa airport fell back and retreated to Adadley.[^cite_note-\:4-35]

By 1 June, with the exception of [Hargeisa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_Airport), the SNM overran the city.[^cite_note-\:02-36] During the Somali army counterattack the SNM line of defense in the city was behind Hargeisa's radio station.[^cite_note-\:51-34]

#### Hargeisa campaign



Up to 90% of Hargeisa (2nd largest city of the Somali Republic) was destroyed.

Dictator [Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre)'s response to the SNM attacks was of unparalleled brutality; with explicit aims of handling the "Isaaq problem", he ordered "the shelling and aerial bombardment of the major cities in the northwest and the systematic destruction of Isaaq dwellings, settlements and water points. In one of the most brutal episodes of the [Isaaq genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_genocide), the regime targeted civilian members of the Isaaq group specifically,[^cite_note-37] especially in the cities of Hargeisa and [Burco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burco) and to that end employed the use of indiscriminate [artillery shelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_shell) and [aerial bombardment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bombing_of_cities) against civilian populations belonging to the Isaaq ethnic group.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Up to 90% of the city was destroyed.

### Reconstruction

After the collapse of the Somali central government and the unilateral declaration of independence of the Republic of Somaliland, a slow process of infrastructural reconstruction subsequently began in Hargeisa and other towns in the country.

Since 1991, Hargeisa has undergone a large-scale facelift. The renovations have been largely financed by local entrepreneurs, as well as Somali expatriates sending [remittance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) funds to relatives in the region through some of the various Somali-owned money transfer operators.[^cite_note-Usrs-40] Most of the destroyed residential and commercial buildings have since been reconstructed, with many newer structures erected. Single-storey buildings downtown are also progressively giving way to multi-storey [high-rises](118767.md).

## Climate

Hargeisa has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSh*). The city generally features very warm winters and hot summers. However, despite its location in the tropics, due to the high altitude Hargeisa seldom experiences either very hot or very cold weather, a trait rarely seen in semi-arid climates. The city receives the bulk of its rain between the months of April and September, averaging just over 400 millimetres (16 in) of rainfall annually. Average monthly temperatures in Hargeisa range from 18 °C or 64.4 °F in the months of December and January to 24 °C or 75.2 °F in the month of June.

## Administration



Meeting hall of Somaliland [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Somaliland\)).

Situated in the Maroodi Jeh region of Somaliland, Hargeisa is the seat of the Somaliland's [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Somaliland), the [presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Somaliland), and government ministries.[^cite_note-Hgc-46]

The first mayor of Hargeisa in 1960 was Aadan Cumar fure, who held the position from 1950 to 1959. The current municipal administration is led by [Abdikarim Ahmed Mooge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdikarim_Ahmed_Mooge). A member of the [Waddani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waddani) party, he was elected the [Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Hargeisa) during the [2021 Somaliland municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Somaliland_municipal_elections).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

## Districts

According to a more recent December 2017 report from the [Social Research and Development Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Research_and_Development_Institute?action=edit\&redlink=1) (SORADI) based in Hargeisa, the city divided into eight sub-districts; being the [31 May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_May_District), [26 June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26_June_District), [Ahmed Dhagah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Dhagah_District), [Ahmed Moallim Haruun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Moallim_Haruun_District), [Gacan Libaax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gacan_Libaax_District), [Ibrahim Kodbuur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Kodbuur_District), [Mohamoud Haibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamoud_Haibe_District), and [Mohamed Moge Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Moge_District).[^cite_note-SORADI-49] This information is contradictory to an April 2003 report by the [Famine Early Warning Systems Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine_Early_Warning_Systems_Network) (FEWS NET), which states only five sub-districts exist: being the 26 June, Ahmed Dhagah, Gacan Libaax, Ibrahim Kodbuur, and Mohamoud Haibe sub-districts, each having between four and six sub-sections of their own.[^cite_note-50] Presumably a redistricting occurred sometime between 2004 and 2017.[^cite_note-SORADI-49]

Each sub-district has their own elected council members, whose responsibility is to represent their district and fulfil their populations needs.[^cite_note-SORADI-49] To increase the effectiveness of these sub-districts, especially underrepresented ones such as Gacan Libaax, a so-called "accountability [forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_\(legal\))" has been established to discuss and create policy to better represent marginalized communities.[^cite_note-SORADI-49]

&#x20;In 2005, the UNDP said that the city had a population of 560,028.[^cite_note-56] According to CIA factbook, Hargeisa had an estimated population of around 760,000 as of 2015. The main residents of Hargeisa are the [Sa'ad Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa'ad_Musa) subclan of the [Habr Awal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habr_Awal), the [Eidagale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidagale) and [Habar Yoonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habar_Yoonis) subclans of the [Habr Garhajis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garhajis), the [Arap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arap), and the [Ayub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq), all of which are subdivisions of the larger [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq) clan. Also reside the [Rer Nuur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reer_Nuur) subclan if the [Makahil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makahil), the [Habar Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahad_'Ase) and [Habar Xusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahad_'Ase) Subclans of [Maxad'Ase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahad_'Ase), the [Habar Afaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habar_Afaan) and the [Jibril Yonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibril_Yonis) all of which are subdivisions of the [Gadabursi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadabuursi) clan and Madigan, [Gabooye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_\(clan\)) also reside that area.[^cite_note-Cwfsom-57] As of 2019, it is estimated that Hargeisa has a population of 1.2 million[^cite_note-invciha-3] The urban area occupies 75 square kilometres (29 sq mi), with a population density of 12,600 inhabitants per square kilometre (33,000/sq mi).[^cite_note-Cwfsom-57]

## Economy



[Dahabshiil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahabshiil) bank in Hargeisa.

Hargeisa is the [financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) hub to many entrepreneurial industries ranging from finance, retail, imports/export warehouses to gem cutters, construction, food processing, textiles and livestock trading.&#x20;

In June 2012, the Partnership Fund for the private sector in Somaliland was launched at Hargeisa's Ambassador Hotel. Part of the larger Partnership for Economic Growth program, the initiative will see \$900,000 [USD](18717338.md) allocated to 13 private local businesses, as well as the creation of 250 new full-time jobs, half of which are to be earmarked for youth and one third for women. The fund is expected to improve job opportunities for 1,300 entrepreneurs through ameliorated product distribution and investment in new technologies and processing facilities. Eligibility is determined through a competitive and transparent selection process overseen by the Partnership program, the [Somaliland Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Chamber_of_Commerce), and government officials.[^cite_note-Ultfgpftpsis-58]

## Transportation



Bus station in Hargeisa at night

Buses are the most commonly used form of public transportation in Hargeisa. They travel on a number of routes serving nearly all of the city's districts. Intercity bus services are also available, which connect Hargeisa to other major cities and towns across Somaliland, including [Burao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burao), [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) and [Borama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borama).

With the growth of urban development, several new [taxi](22026933.md) companies have sprung up in Hargeisa.[^cite_note-Tcihcjet-59]

### International Airport

For air transportation, the city is served by the [Hargeisa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_International_Airport). The Somali-owned private carriers [Daallo Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daallo_Airlines) and [Jubba Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubba_Airways) offer flights to various other towns in neighbouring Somalia, such as [Mogadishu](5613319.md), [Bosaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bender_Qassim_International_Airport) and [Galkayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullahi_Yusuf_International_Airport). Trips to international destinations are also available, including to [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti-Ambouli_International_Airport) and [Dubai](154742.md). It has recently gained more airlines such as [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) and [flydubai](18432521.md).[^cite_note-60] However, both airlines have listed the city as being in Somalia since August 2024, following an ultimatum from the Somali government, which would have otherwise seen them prohibited from using Somali airspace.[^cite_note-61]

## Education



A young librarian arranging books at the [National Library of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Somaliland) in Hargeisa

Various primary schools and [nurseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool) are located in Hargeisa. There are several state-run and privately owned [secondary](554992.md) and high schools in the city. Among these are the [SOS Sheikh Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOS_Sheikh_Secondary_School), [Abaarso School of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abaarso_School_of_Science_and_Technology), [Hargeisa Technical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_Technical_School?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Salaama Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaama_Secondary_School?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Smart Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Secondary_School?action=edit\&redlink=1), the latter of which is the pre-eminent secondary institution in the area, about 13 km (8 mi) west of Hargeisa.

The city is home to a number of colleges and universities including the [University of Hargeisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hargeisa), [Admas University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admas_University_College,_Hargeisa), [Somaliland University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_University_of_Technology), [Frantz Fanon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frantz_Fanon_University?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Gollis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gollis_University), [New Generation University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Generation_University?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Hope University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_University) and [Marodijeh International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marodijeh_International_University?action=edit\&redlink=1). The [Edna Adan Maternity Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_Adan_Maternity_Hospital) offers nursing programmes to students. The [Institute for Practical Research and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Practical_Research_and_Training) specializes in (applied) research and training.
