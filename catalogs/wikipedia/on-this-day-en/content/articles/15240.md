**Imam** ([/ɪˈmɑːm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): إمام, *_imām_*; pl.: أئمة, *_a'immah_*) is an [Islamic](6037917.md) leadership position. In [Shia Islam](26961.md), the Imams are absolute infallible leaders after the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad). Shia Muslims consider the term to be only applicable to the members of the [Ahl al-Bayt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl_al-Bayt), the family of Muhammad. In [Twelver Shi'ism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver_Shi'ism) there are [The Fourteen Infallibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fourteen_Infallibles), [twelve of which](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Imams) are Imams.[^cite_note-1] In [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), an Imam generally refers to a religious leader, [scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_scholar), or the person who leads [Muslims](19541.md) in [prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah). The position is not necessarily hereditary or divinely appointed. Thus in Sunni Islam, anyone can study the basic Islamic teachings and become an Imam.

## Sunni Islam



[Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_dynasty) [Qadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadi) acts as Imam delivers Khutbah in Mosque on the [Minbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbar).

[Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) does not conceive of the role of imams in the same sense as [Shia Islam](26961.md): an important distinction often overlooked by non-Muslims. In everyday terms, an *imam* for Sunni Muslims is the person charged with leading formal Islamic prayers ([Fard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fard)) even in locations besides the [mosque](19894.md)—whenever prayer is performed in a group of two or more. The imam leads the worship and the congregation copies his actions. [Friday sermons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Sermon) are most often given by an appointed imam. All mosques have an imam to lead the congregational prayers—even though it may sometimes just be a member from the gathered congregation rather than an officially appointed, salaried person. [Women cannot be imams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_as_imams) when men are present but are allowed to be when no men are present. An imam should be chosen, according to multiple [ahadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith), based on his knowledge of the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) and [Sunnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah) and his moral character.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

### Title of scholarly authority

Another well-known use of the term is as an honorary title for a recognized religious scholarly authority in Islam.[^cite_note-4] It is especially used for a [jurist (*faqih*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faq%C4%ABh) and often for the founders of the four Sunni *[madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab)*&#x73; or schools of [jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence) (*[fiqh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh)*), as well as an authority on [Quranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) [exegesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exegesis) (*[tafsir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafsir)*), such as [Al-Tabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tabari) or [Ibn Kathir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Kathir).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

It may also refer to the *[Muhaddithūn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhaddithin)* or scholars who created the analytical sciences related to [Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith); due to their scholarly authority, the term may also refer to the heads of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_in_Islam)'s family in their generational times.[^cite_note-7]

### The position of Imams in Turkey

Imams are appointed by the state to work at mosques and they are required to be graduates of an [İmam Hatip high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0mam_Hatip_high_school) or have a university degree in theology.[^cite_note-8] This is an official position regulated by the [Presidency of Religious Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Religious_Affairs)[^cite_note-9] in Turkey and only men are appointed to this position, whilst female officials under the same state organisation work as preachers and Qur'an course tutors, religious services experts, etc. These officials are supposed to belong to the [Hanafi school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi_school) of the Sunni sect.[^cite_note-10]

A central figure in an Islamic movement is also called an imam, like [Imam Nawawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nawawi) in Syria.[^cite_note-11]

## Shia Islam

Main articles: [Imamate in Shia doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamate_in_Shia_doctrine) and [Twelve Imams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Imams)

In Shia Islam, an imam is not only presented as the man of God *par excellence*, but as participating fully in the names, attributes, and acts that theology usually reserves for God alone.[^cite_note-12] Imams have a meaning more central to belief, referring to leaders of the community. [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver) and [Ismaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili) Shi'a believe that these imams are chosen by God to be perfect examples for the faithful and to lead all humanity in all aspects of life. They also believe that all the imams chosen are free from committing any sin, impeccability which is called *[ismah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismah)*. These leaders must be followed since they are appointed by God.

## Imams as secular rulers



*Prayer in the House of the Arnaut Chief*, painting by [Jean-Léon Gérôme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me),1857

At times, imams have held both secular and religious authority. This was the case in [Oman](22316.md) among the [Kharijite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharijite) or [Ibadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadi) sects. At times, the imams were elected. At other times the position was inherited, as with the [Yaruba dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaruba_dynasty) from 1624 and 1742. See [List of rulers of Oman](405325.md), the [Rustamid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustamid_dynasty): 776–909, [Nabhani dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabhani_dynasty): 1154–1624, the [Yaruba dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaruba_dynasty): 1624–1742, the [Al Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Said): 1744–present for further information.[^cite_note-46] The [Imamate of Futa Jallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamate_of_Futa_Jallon) (1727–1896) was a [Fulani](102383.md) state in West Africa where secular power alternated between two lines of hereditary imams, or *almami*.[^cite_note-47] In the [Zaidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaidiyyah) [Shiite](26961.md) sect, imams were secular as well as spiritual leaders who held power in [Yemen](350939.md) for more than a thousand years. In 897, a Zaidi ruler, [al-Hadi ila'l-Haqq Yahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hadi_ila'l-Haqq_Yahya), founded a line of such imams, a theocratic form of government which survived until the second half of the 20th century (See details under [Zaidiyyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaidiyyah), [History of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Yemen), [Imams of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imams_of_Yemen)).[^cite_note-48]

[Ruhollah Khomeini](26232.md) is officially referred to as imam in [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-49] Several Iranian places and institutions are named "Imam Khomeini", including a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Emam_Khomeyni), an [international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Imam_Khomeini_International_Airport), a [hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Khomeini_Hospital_Complex), and a [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Khomeini_International_University).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]
