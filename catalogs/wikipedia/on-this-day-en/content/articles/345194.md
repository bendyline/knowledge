**Podkarpackie Voivodeship** (Polish pronunciation: [\[pɔt.karˈpat͡s.kjɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish); **lit. 'Subcarpathian'**) is a [voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland), or [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province), in the southeastern corner of [Poland](22936.md). Its administrative capital and largest city is [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w). It is governed by the [Subcarpathian Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcarpathian_Regional_Assembly) and the [voivodeship marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_marshal).

The name derives from the region's location near the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains), and the voivodeship comprises areas of two historic regions of Central Europe—[Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland) (western and northwestern counties) and [Cherven Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherven_Cities)/[Red Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia).

It is bordered by [Lesser Poland Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship) to the west, [Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awi%C4%99tokrzyskie_Voivodeship) to the north-west, [Lublin Voivodeship](344869.md) to the north, [Ukraine](31750.md) ([Lviv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Oblast) and [Zakarpattia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast)) to the east and [Slovakia](26830.md) ([Prešov Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre%C5%A1ov_Region)) to the south. It covers an area of 17,844 square kilometres (6,890 sq mi), and has a population of 2,627,462 (as at 2019). The voivodeship is mostly hilly or mountainous (see [Bieszczady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieszczady_Mountains), [Beskidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskids)); its northwestern corner is flat. It is one of the most wooded Polish voivodeships (35.9% of total area), within its borders is the entirety of [Bieszczady National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieszczady_National_Park), and parts of [Magura National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magura_National_Park).

## History



17th-century view of [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl), one of the oldest and for several centuries the largest city of the region

In the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages), the territory was inhabited by the [Vistulans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistulans) and [Lendians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lendians), old [Polish tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_tribes). It formed part of Poland since its first ever historic ruler [Mieszko I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieszko_I), however, later on, at various times, portions of the region fell under the [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde), and the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), before Poland regained full control in the 14th century. Following the [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) the entire region was annexed by [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and included within the newly established [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria). The oldest cities in the province, with over 1,000 years of history, are [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl) and [Sanok](390560.md). [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w), [Łańcut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81a%C5%84cut) and [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg), with their castles and palaces, were important residential cities of the powerful Polish magnate families of [Lubomirski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubomirski), [Potocki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potocki) and [Tarnowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnowski_family).

During the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period) (1918–1939), territory of the Subcarpathian Voivodeship was part of the [Lwów Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w_Voivodeship) and belonged to ["Poland B"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_A_and_B), the less-developed, more rural parts of Poland. To boost the local economy, the government of the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) began in the mid-1930s a massive program of industrialization, known as the [Central Industrial Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Industrial_Region_\(Poland\)). The program created several major armament factories, including [PZL Mielec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL_Mielec), PZL Rzeszów, [Huta Stalowa Wola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huta_Stalowa_Wola), and factories in other Subcarpathian towns such as [Dębica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%99bica), [Nowa Dęba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowa_D%C4%99ba), [Sanok](390560.md), [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg) and [Nowa Sarzyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowa_Sarzyna).

Following the joint German–Soviet [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), which started [World War II](32927.md) in September 1939, most of the current province was [occupied by Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) with the eastern outskirts occupied by the Soviet Union, and the city of Przemyśl divided between the occupiers until 1941, and then the entire region occupied by Germany until 1944.

Following the Soviet annexation of the regional capital of [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w), [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w) was chosen as the new regional capital and the [Rzeszów Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w_Voivodeship) was founded.

The [voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship) was created on 1 January 1999 out of the former [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w_Voivodeship), [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl_Voivodeship), [Krosno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno_Voivodeship) and (partially) [Tarnów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarn%C3%B3w_Voivodeship) and [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg_Voivodeship) Voivodeships, pursuant to the [Polish local-government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Poland) adopted in 1998.

## Government

Main article: [Subcarpathian Voivodeship Sejmik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcarpathian_Voivodeship_Sejmik)

Subcarpathian Voivodeship's government powers are shared between the [voivode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivode) (governor), the [sejmik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_sejmik) (regional assembly), and the [marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_marshal).

## Economy

The [Gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the province was 19.4 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 3.9% of Polish economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 15,100 euros or 50% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 59% of the EU average. Podkarpackie Voivodship is the province with the third lowest GDP per capita in Poland.[^cite_note-5]

## Transportation

The [Rzeszów–Jasionka Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w%E2%80%93Jasionka_Airport) is the province's international airport.

The [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_autostrada_\(Poland\)) and [S19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressway_S19_\(Poland\)) highways pass through the province, with the [S74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressway_S74_\(Poland\)) also planned for construction.

16th-century [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) organs at the Bernardine Monastery in [Leżajsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C5%BCajsk)

Other preserved historic old towns include [Krosno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno), nicknamed "Little Kraków", [Jarosław](386732.md), [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w), [Sanok](390560.md). In Jarosław, Przemyśl and Rzeszów there are underground tourist routes in historic cellars under the old town market squares. There are numerous castles and palaces in the province, including the Royal Castles in [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl_Castle) and [Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Castle), and former noble castles and palaces in [Baranów Sandomierski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baran%C3%B3w_Sandomierski_Castle), [Dukla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukla), [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w_Castle), [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg) and [Tyczyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyczyn).

There are five [spa towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_town): [Horyniec-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horyniec-Zdr%C3%B3j), [Iwonicz-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwonicz-Zdr%C3%B3j), [Polańczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pola%C5%84czyk), [Rymanów-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryman%C3%B3w-Zdr%C3%B3j), [Solina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solina,_Poland).

There are several museums, including the National Museum in Przemyśl and Regional Museum in Rzeszów. The more unique museums include the [Museum of Folk Architecture](12812780.md) in Sanok, [Markowa Ulma-Family Museum of Poles Who Saved Jews in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markowa_Ulma-Family_Museum_of_Poles_Who_Saved_Jews_in_World_War_II), Museum of Oil and Gas Industry at the location of the world's oldest oil field in [Bóbrka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B3brka,_Krosno_County), and Museum of the Polish [Sulfur](27127.md) Industry in [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg).

There are several monuments and memorials to inventor [Ignacy Łukasiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_%C5%81ukasiewicz), pioneer of the global oil industry, in places where he studied and worked, including Bóbrka, [Krosno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno), [Łańcut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81a%C5%84cut), [Jasło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jas%C5%82o) and [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w). There are memorials to the Hungarian [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) poet [Bálint Balassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1lint_Balassi) in [Odrzykoń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrzyko%C5%84), [Nowy Żmigród](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowy_%C5%BBmigr%C3%B3d) and [Rymanów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryman%C3%B3w), where he stayed at various times.[^cite_note-14]

## Cuisine

In addition to traditional nationwide [Polish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine), Subcarpathian Voivodeship is known for its variety of regional and local [traditional foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_food), which include especially various [cheeses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese), meat products (incl. various types of [kiełbasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kie%C5%82basa), [bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon) and [salceson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salceson)), cakes, [honeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) and various dishes and meals, officially protected by the [Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development_\(Poland\)). There are local types of [pierogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierogi), [gołąbki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go%C5%82%C4%85bki), [barszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barszcz) and other [soups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup).

## Curiosities

- In the 17th century, there was a sizeable [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_in_Poland) community in [Krosno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno).

  [^cite_note-jf-15]

  &#x20;There is a monument to [Robert Gilbert Porteous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gilbert_Porteous?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Krosno, Scottish immigrant, the city's wealthiest 17th-century merchant.

  [^cite_note-jf-15]

- In 1711, [Jarosław](386732.md) was the place of refuge of Hungarian leader [Francis II Rákóczi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_II_R%C3%A1k%C3%B3czi) and his court, including essayist [Kelemen Mikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelemen_Mikes), after the failure of the [Rákóczi's War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1k%C3%B3czi's_War_of_Independence) against [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy).
