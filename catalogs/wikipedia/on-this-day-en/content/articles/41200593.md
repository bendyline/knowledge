**Planet Labs PBC** (formerly **Planet Labs, Inc.** and **Cosmogia, Inc.**), known as "**Planet.**", is a [publicly traded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_company) American [Earth imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_imaging) company based in [San Francisco](49728.md), California.[^cite_note-PL-1][^cite_note-\:3-4] Their goal is to image the entirety of the Earth daily to monitor changes and pinpoint trends.[^cite_note-5]

The company designs and manufactures 3U-[CubeSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat) [miniature satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniaturized_satellite) called **Doves** that are then delivered into orbit as secondary payloads on other rocket launch missions. Each *Dove* is equipped with a high-powered telescope and camera programmed to capture different swaths of the [Earth](9228.md).[^cite_note-\:0-6] Each *Dove* [Earth observation satellite](46597.md) continuously scans Earth, sending data once it passes over a [ground station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_station), by means of a [frame image sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_image_sensor).[^cite_note-7]

The images gathered by *Doves*, which can be accessed online and some of which are available under an [open data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_data) access policy,[^cite_note-PL-explorer-8] provide up-to-date information relevant to climate monitoring, crop yield prediction, urban planning, and disaster response.[^cite_note-PL-1] With acquisition of [BlackBridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidEye) in July 2015, Planet Labs had 87 *Dove* and 5 *[RapidEye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidEye)* satellites launched into orbit.[^cite_note-TechCrunch-2015-9] In 2017, Planet launched an additional 88 Dove satellites, and [Google](1092923.md) sold its subsidiary [Terra Bella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Bella) and its SkySat [satellite constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_constellation) to Planet Labs.[^cite_note-PlanetLabsReuters-10][^cite_note-auto1-11][^cite_note-12] By September 2018, the company had launched nearly 300 satellites, 150 of which are active.[^cite_note-tc20180914-13] In 2020, Planet Labs launched six additional high-resolution SkySats, SkySats 16–21, and 35 Dove satellites.[^cite_note-14]

Through a deal funded by Norway's Climate and Forests Initiative (NICFI), Planet and its partners Airbus and KSAT provided access to high-resolution basemaps of 64 tropical countries to help combat deforestation until April 2025.[^cite_note-15] It also provides data to [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization)'s Framework for Ecosystem Monitoring (Ferm).[^cite_note-16]

Following a January 2021 launch of 48 Planet SuperDoves, the company operated a global constellation of over 200 active satellites.[^cite_note-Amos-17]

In July 2021 Planet Labs announced that they plan to become a [public company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_company) and list on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md) through a merger with the [SPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special-purpose_acquisition_company) DMY Technology Group Inc IV. The deal would value Planet at US\$2.8B.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The business combination was completed on December 7, 2021. Planet registered as a [public benefit corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefit_corporation) and formally changed its name to Planet Labs PBC.[^cite_note-\:3-4] The stock began trading on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md) on December 8, 2021. After the merger, Planet had more than \$500 million in capital and about 190 satellites in orbit. The company expected it to take the next few years until they get to cashflow breakeven, funding their operations with \$200 million from the aforementioned \$500 million (the rest \$300 million forming a "strategic warchest"). At the time of the merger, the company had over 600 customers (most customers, 90%, are annual subscriptions to Planet's data service) and it generated \$113 million in revenue in 2020. At the time of the merger, Planet aimed to be profitable on an adjusted EBITDA basis by early 2025, and to grow its annual revenue to nearly \$700 million by early 2026.[^cite_note-20]

Planet Labs has contracts to supply imagery to various agencies of the US government, including the [FAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Agricultural_Service), [NOAA](37876.md), [Oak Ridge](38147.md), [Sandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories), the [Bureau of Reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bureau_of_Reclamation), and [NASA](18426568.md).[^cite_note-21]

## History



First pair of the 28 Planet Labs satellites launched from the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS) via the [NanoRacks CubeSat Deployer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NanoRacks_CubeSat_Deployer) (2014)

Planet Labs was founded in 2010 as *Cosmogia* by former [NASA](18426568.md) scientists Chris Boshuizen, Will Marshall, and Robbie Schingler, who teamed up with John Kuolt in 2011 during his studies at Oxford University with a thesis to leverage the processing power of mobile phones to power the [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) of a nano-satellite. Mr. Kuolt's EP (Entrepreneurial Project) at Oxford University became the original business plan for Planet.[^cite_note-brewster-22][^cite_note-graham-23][^cite_note-solon-24] The initial goal of the company was to make use of information gathered from space to help with life on Earth. The group of scientists considered the problem with most satellites to be their large and clunky form, prompting them to build inexpensive and compact satellites to be manufactured in bulk, called CubeSats. The small group began building Planet's first satellite in a California garage.[^cite_note-25]

Planet Labs launched two demonstration [CubeSats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat), Dove 1 and [Dove 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dove-2), in April 2013.[^cite_note-wall-26] Both Dove 1 (aboard Antares 110 rocket) and Dove 2 (aboard a Soyuz Rocket) were placed in a [Sun-synchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit).[^cite_note-27] Dove 3 and Dove 4 were launched in November 2013.[^cite_note-graham-23]

In June 2013, it announced plans for [Flock-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flock-1), a constellation of 28 Earth-observing satellites.[^cite_note-wall-26]

The Flock-1 CubeSats were brought to the [International Space Station](15043.md) in January 2014[^cite_note-iss-28] and deployed via the [NanoRacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NanoRacks) [CubeSat Deployer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NanoRacks_CubeSat_Deployer) in mid-February.[^cite_note-discovery-29] The company planned to launch a total of 131 satellites by mid-2015.[^cite_note-taylor-30]

In January 2015, the firm raised \$95 million in funding.[^cite_note-31] As of May 2015, Planet Labs raised a total amount of \$183 million in [venture capital financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital_financing).[^cite_note-32]

In July 2015, Planet Labs [acquired](56119.md) [BlackBridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackBridge) and its [RapidEye constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidEye).[^cite_note-sn20150715-33]

On April 18, 2017, Google completed the sale of Terra Bella and its [SkySat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkySat) satellite constellation to Planet Labs.[^cite_note-PlanetLabsReuters-10][^cite_note-auto1-11][^cite_note-planet-34] As part of the sale, Google acquired an [equity stake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(finance\)#Investing) in Planet and entered into a multi-year agreement to purchase SkySat imaging data.[^cite_note-equity-35]

On January 21, 2018, a *Dove Pioneer* CubeSat was part of the payload of a [Rocket Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Lab) [Electron rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_\(rocket\)), the first orbital-entry craft launched from a privately owned and operated spaceport at [Māhia Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81hia_Peninsula) in [New Zealand](4913064.md).[^cite_note-36]

In July 2018, Planet laid off somewhat less than ten percent of its workforce.[^cite_note-37] In September 2018, the company had launched a total of 298 satellites, 150 of which were still active.[^cite_note-tc20180914-13]

On December 18, 2018, Planet announced they were in the process of acquiring the St Louis company, Boundless Spatial, Inc., a [geospatial data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geospatial_data) software company.[^cite_note-\:1-38][^cite_note-\:2-39]

Several weeks after their acquisition of Boundless, Planet's attorneys asserted that executives from Boundless had failed to disclose “information concerning material customer contracts,” and the acquisition was thereafter renegotiated down by more than half, from \$40M to \$16M. According to Quartz, the executives of Boundless had failed to disclose details concerning work, or details concerning future work, with the NGA.[^cite_note-40]

On 3 July 2020, it was mentioned in the news that the company had "more than 120" active satellites at the time "providing daily imaging coverage over all of the world's landmass".[^cite_note-41]

In August 2020, Planet completed its SkySat Constellation of 21 satellites by launching the final three SkySats on a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket.[^cite_note-42]

In May 2022, SES Government Solutions (now [SES Space & Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SES_S.A.#Corporate_structure)), a wholly owned subsidiary of [communications satellite](45207.md) owner and operator, [SES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SES_S.A.), in partnership with Planet Labs, was awarded a US\$28.96 million contract from [NASA](18426568.md)'s Communications Services Project for [real-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_communication), [always-on](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_availability) low-latency connectivity services to NASA spacecraft in low-Earth orbit for routine missions, contingency operations, launch and ascent, and early operations phase communications, using SES's [geostationary orbiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostationary_orbit) [C-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_band_\(IEEE\)) satellites and [medium Earth orbiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_Earth_orbit) [Ka-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka_band) satellites, including the [O3b mPOWER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O3b_mPOWER) constellation.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

In August 2023, Planet cited "restructuring" as the reason for laying off 10% of its employees, about 120 individuals.[^cite_note-Sheetz_2023-45] CEO William Marshall released a statement saying, "I want to be clear that I am responsible for the decisions that led us here. I know this has significant effects on the lives of our team and their families, and for that I am sorry."[^cite_note-46]

In March 2024, Planet Labs Federal, a subsidiary of Planet Labs, was awarded a contract by [NIWC-PAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Information_Warfare_Center_Pacific) to incorporate Planet's SkySat and PlanetScope data into [SeaVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaVision).[^cite_note-47]

In June 2024, Planet Labs announced a reduction of nearly 17% of its global workforce, equating to approximately 180 jobs. The company stated in a filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission that the layoffs were necessary to align resources with market opportunities, improve operational efficiency, and support long-term growth and profitability. The decision is expected to incur a one-off cost of around \$9.5 million to \$10.5 million.[^cite_note-48]

In August 2024, Planet launched its first hyperspectral satellite, Tanager-1, along with 36 SuperDove satellites on the SpaceX Transporter-11 mission.[^cite_note-49] Tanager-1 was developed as part of the Carbon Mapper Coalition to detect and quantify methane and carbon dioxide emissions.[^cite_note-50] The company released the first light images from Tanager-1 in September 2024.[^cite_note-51]

In January 2025, the company launched its second high-resolution Pelican satellite, Pelican-2, on SpaceX's Transporter-12 mission.[^cite_note-52] That same month, Planet signed a multi-year \$230 million agreement with [SKY Perfect JSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKY_Perfect_JSAT) to build and operate a dedicated constellation of ten Pelican satellites for the Japanese satellite operator.[^cite_note-53]

In July 2025, Planet announced a €240 million contract funded by the German government to provide dedicated satellite capacity and data services for European security and peace-keeping efforts.[^cite_note-54]

In August 2025, the company launched two additional high-resolution satellites, Pelican-3 and Pelican-4.[^cite_note-55]

In September 2025, Planet announced plans to open a new satellite manufacturing facility in [Berlin](3354.md). The facility is intended to double the production capacity of the Pelican satellite fleet and serves as an expansion of the company's European headquarters.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

In March 2026, at the request of the US government, Planet imposed restrictions on its imagery during the [2026 Iran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_War). Initially, images from the Middle East were subject to a 14 day delay. This was later expanded into a complete ban on the release of new imagery of the region.[^cite_note-58] In April 2026, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported that Chinese state media noted [MizarVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MizarVision)'s use of Planet Labs imaging in its analysis. A representative for Planet Labs stated that MizarVision, a Chinese geospatial analysis company, was not a client and denied the company's ownership of the images in question.[^cite_note-59]

## Flock satellite constellations&#x20;



Planet Labs mission control

Planet's PlanetScope Dove satellite constellation is designed to observe Earth. By using several small satellites, CubeSats, the constellation produces high resolution images of Earth — 3–5 metres (10–16 ft) per pixel. The flock collects images from latitudes that are within 52 degrees of Earth's equator.[^cite_note-Escobedo_Jr-60] A large portion of the world's agricultural regions and population lie within the area imaged by the flock.[^cite_note-Escobedo_Jr-60] Initially, the mission used the ISS ([International Space Station](15043.md)) and different track launch vehicles to get in orbit.[^cite_note-61]

### RapidEye

Main article: [RapidEye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidEye)

*[RapidEye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidEye)* was a five-satellite constellation producing 5 metres (16 ft) resolution imagery that Planet acquired from the German company BlackBridge.

The satellites were built by [Surrey Satellite Technology Ltd. (SSTL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey_Satellite_Technology_Ltd)[^cite_note-103] of [Guildford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guildford), subcontracted by [MacDonald Dettwiler (MDA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacDonald_Dettwiler) of Richmond, Canada. Each satellite was based on an evolution of the flight-proven [SSTL-150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_satellite_buses)[^cite_note-104] bus, measuring less than 1 cubic metre (35 cu ft) and weighing 150 kilograms (330 lb) (bus + payload) each. They were launched on 29 August 2008 on a [Dnepr rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnepr_\(rocket\)) from [Baikonur cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome) in [Kazakhstan](16642.md).[^cite_note-105]

Each of RapidEye's five satellites contained identical Jena-Optronik Spaceborne Scanner JSS 56[^cite_note-106] [multi-spectral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-spectral_image) [pushbroom sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push_broom_scanner) imagers. The five satellites traveled on the same orbital plane (at an altitude of 630 km), and together were capable of collecting over 4 million kilometres (2.5×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> mi) of 5 metres (16 ft) resolution, 5-band color imagery every day. They collected data in the Blue (440–510 nm), Green (520–590 nm), Red (630–690 nm), Red-Edge (690–730 nm) and [Near-Infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) (760–880 nm).

The RapidEye constellation was officially retired in April 2020.[^cite_note-107]

### SkySat

Main article: [SkySat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkySat)

*[SkySat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkySat)* is a constellation of sub-metre resolution [Earth observation satellites](46597.md) that provide imagery, high-definition video and analytics services.[^cite_note-planet-34] Planet acquired the satellites with their purchase of Terra Bella (formerly Skybox Imaging), a [Mountain View, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_California)-based company founded in 2009 by Dan Berkenstock, Julian Mann, John Fenwick, and Ching-Yu Hu,[^cite_note-IEEE_Spectrum-108] from Google in 2017.[^cite_note-auto-109]

The SkySat satellites are based on using inexpensive automotive grade electronics and fast commercially available processors,[^cite_note-110] but scaled up to approximately the size of a [minifridge](1036259.md).[^cite_note-startup-skybox-111] The satellites are approximately 80 centimetres (31 in) long, compared to approximately 30 centimetres (12 in) for a 3U CubeSat, and weigh 100 kilograms (220 lb).[^cite_note-startup-skybox-111]

The first SkySat satellite, SkySat-1, was launched on a [Dnepr (rocket)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnepr_\(rocket\)) from [Yasny, Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasny) on 21 November 2013,[^cite_note-Spaceflight_Now_Nov_2013-112] and the second, SkySat-2, launched on a [Soyuz-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-2)/Fregat rocket from Baikonur, Kazakhstan on 8 July 2014.[^cite_note-''\[\[9to5Google\]\]''_July_2014-113] Four more SkySat units were launched on 16 September 2016, by the [Vega rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_rocket)'s seventh flight from [Kourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiana_Space_Centre),[^cite_note-114] and six more SkySat satellites, along with four Dove CubeSats, were launched on a [Minotaur-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minotaur-C) rocket from [Vandenberg Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Air_Force_Base) on 31 October 2017.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-6skysats-116]

In 2020, Planet lowered their constellation of 15 SkySats from an altitude of 500 kilometers to 450 kilometers to improve the resolution of orthorectified imagery from 80 centimeters to 50 centimeters per pixel.[^cite_note-117]

On June 13, 2020, [SpaceX](832774.md)'s [Falcon 9](2647515.md) rocket successfully launched SkySats 16, 17 and 18 along with a batch of its [Starlink](45111627.md) communications satellites.[^cite_note-118]

SkySats 19, 20 and 21 were launched on August 18, 2020 on SpaceX's Falcon 9 rocket. This completed the SkySat fleet of 21 high-resolution satellites.[^cite_note-119]

When launched, the SkySat constellation was orbiting at an altitude of 450 kilometres (280 mi) and has a multispectral, panchromatic, and video sensor. It has a spatial resolution of 0.9 metres in its 400–900 nm panchromatic band, making it the smallest satellite to be put in orbit capable of such high resolution imagery. The multispectral sensor collects data in blue (450–515 nm), green (515–595 nm), red (605–695 nm), and near-infrared (740–900 nm) bands, all at 2 metre resolution.[^cite_note-120]

### Pelican

The Pelican constellation is a set of 0.5 m resolution Earth observation satellites designed to succeed the SkySat constellation.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] The spacecraft are based on a modular bus shared with the Tanager hyperspectral satellites and utilize Planet's next generation of imaging sensors. The constellation is designed for a revisit rate of up to 10 times per day globally and up to 30 times per day at mid-latitudes.[^cite_note-123]

The first technology demonstrator, Pelican-1, launched on 11 November 2023 on the SpaceX Transporter-9 mission.[^cite_note-124] Deployment of the operational constellation began in 2025:

- **Pelican-2** launched on 14 January 2025 on SpaceX’s Transporter-12 mission. Planet released first light imagery from the satellite in March 2025.

  [^cite_note-Pelican2Launch-125]

- **Pelican-3** and **Pelican-4** launched on 26 August 2025.

  [^cite_note-Pelican34Launch-126]

The satellites utilize the [NVIDIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NVIDIA) [Jetson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_Jetson) platform for on-orbit [edge computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_computing), designed to process data before downlink to reduce latency.[^cite_note-127]

In January 2025, Planet signed a \$230 million agreement with [SKY Perfect JSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKY_Perfect_JSAT) (JSAT) to build and operate ten Pelican satellites owned by JSAT.[^cite_note-128] In July 2025, Planet announced a €240 million contract funded by the German government for dedicated capacity and direct downlink services on the Pelican constellation.[^cite_note-129]
