**Rain** or **rainfall** is a form of [atmospheric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere) [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) composed of [droplets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_\(liquid\)) of [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water) [aerosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol) that have [condensed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensation) from [atmospheric water vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_water_vapor) and fall by [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity). Depending on the intensity, rain can be described as a *[drizzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drizzle)* (very light, sporadic rainfall), a *[shower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shower_\(precipitation\))* (brief, moderate rainfall), a *[cloudburst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudburst)* (brief heavy rainfall) or a *downpour* (prolonged heavy or "torrential" rainfall). It is also often accompanied by other [weather phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_phenomena) such as strong [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind) ([rainstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm)) and [lightning](61344.md) ([thunderstorm](70807.md)), and may be mixed with [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail)/[ice pellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_pellet), [snow](28191.md) ([sleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_and_snow_mixed)) and [sandstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstorm) ([dust rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_dust)). Rain is a major component of the [water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_cycle) and is responsible for depositing most of the [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) on Earth. It provides water for [hydroelectric power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power_plants), crop [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation), and suitable conditions for many types of [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem).

The major cause of rain production is moisture moving along three-dimensional zones of temperature and moisture contrasts known as [weather fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_fronts). If enough moisture and upward motion is present, [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) falls from [convective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection) clouds (those with strong upward vertical motion) such as [cumulonimbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulonimbus) (thunder clouds) which can organize into narrow [rainbands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbands). In mountainous areas, heavy precipitation is possible where [upslope flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upslope_flow) is maximized within [windward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward) sides of the [terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain) at elevation which forces moist air to condense and fall out as rainfall along the sides of mountains. On the [leeward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward) side of mountains, desert climates can exist due to the dry air caused by downslope flow which causes heating and drying of the [air mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mass). The movement of the [monsoon trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_trough), or [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone), brings [rainy seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) to [savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah) [climes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clime).

The [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) effect leads to increased rainfall, both in amounts and intensity, downwind of cities. [Global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) is also causing changes in the [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) pattern, including wetter conditions across eastern North America and drier conditions in the tropics. The globally averaged annual precipitation over land is 715 mm (28.1 in), but over the whole Earth, it is much higher at 990 mm (39 in).[^cite_note-1] [Climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_classification) systems such as the [Köppen classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system use average annual rainfall to help differentiate between differing climate regimes. Rainfall is measured using [rain gauges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_gauge). Rainfall amounts can be estimated by [weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar).

## Formation

### Water-saturated air

Air contains water vapor, and the amount of water in a given mass of dry air, known as the *mixing ratio*, is measured in grams of water per kilogram of dry air (g/kg).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The amount of moisture in the air is also commonly reported as [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity); which is the percentage of the total water vapor air can hold at a particular air temperature.[^cite_note-4] How much water vapor a parcel of air can contain before it becomes saturated (100% relative humidity) and forms into a [cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud) (a group of visible tiny water or ice [particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle) suspended above the Earth's surface)[^cite_note-5] depends on its temperature. Warmer air can contain more water vapor than cooler air before becoming saturated. Therefore, one way to saturate a parcel of air is to cool it. The [dew point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dew_point) is the temperature to which a parcel must be cooled in order to become saturated.[^cite_note-6]

There are four main mechanisms for cooling the air to its dew point: adiabatic cooling, conductive cooling, radiational cooling, and evaporative cooling. [Adiabatic cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabatic_lapse_rate#Dry_adiabatic_lapse_rate) occurs when air rises and expands.[^cite_note-7] The air can rise due to [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection), large-scale atmospheric motions, or a physical barrier such as a mountain ([orographic lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orographic_lift)). Conductive cooling occurs when the air comes into contact with a colder surface,[^cite_note-8] usually by being blown from one surface to another, for example from a liquid water surface to colder land. Radiational cooling occurs due to the emission of [infrared radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_radiation), either by the air or by the surface underneath.[^cite_note-9] Evaporative cooling occurs when moisture is added to the air through evaporation, which forces the air temperature to cool to its [wet-bulb temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet-bulb_temperature), or until it reaches saturation.[^cite_note-10]

The main ways water vapor is added to the air are wind convergence into areas of upward motion,[^cite_note-convection-11] precipitation or virga falling from above,[^cite_note-12] daytime heating evaporating water from the surface of oceans, water bodies or wet land,[^cite_note-13] transpiration from plants,[^cite_note-14] cool or dry air moving over warmer water,[^cite_note-15] and lifting air over mountains.[^cite_note-MT-16] Water vapor normally begins to condense on [condensation nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_condensation_nuclei) such as dust, ice, and salt in order to form clouds. Elevated portions of weather fronts (which are three-dimensional in nature)[^cite_note-17] force broad areas of upward motion within the Earth's atmosphere which form clouds decks such as [altostratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altostratus) or [cirrostratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirrostratus).[^cite_note-DR-18] [Stratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratus_cloud) is a stable cloud deck which tends to form when a cool, stable air mass is trapped underneath a warm air mass. It can also form due to the lifting of [advection fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_fog) during breezy conditions.[^cite_note-19]

### Coalescence and fragmentation

Diagram showing that very small rain drops are almost spherical in shape. As drops become larger, they become flattened on the bottom, like a hamburger bun. Very large rain drops are split into smaller ones by air resistance which makes them increasingly unstable.

The shape of raindrops depending upon their size:&#x20;

1. Contrary to popular belief, raindrops are never tear-shaped.

2. Very small raindrops are almost spherical.

3. Larger raindrops become flattened at the bottom due to air resistance.

4. Large raindrops have a large amount of air resistance, and begin to become unstable.

5. Very large raindrops split into smaller raindrops due to air resistance.

[Coalescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalescence_\(meteorology\)) occurs when water droplets fuse to create larger water droplets.[^cite_note-20] Air resistance typically causes the water droplets in a cloud to remain stationary. When air turbulence occurs, water droplets collide, producing larger droplets.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

As these larger water droplets descend, coalescence continues, so that drops become heavy enough to overcome air resistance and fall as rain. Coalescence generally happens most often in clouds above freezing (in their top) and is also known as the warm rain process.[^cite_note-23] In clouds below freezing, when ice crystals gain enough mass they begin to fall. This generally requires more mass than coalescence when occurring between the crystal and neighboring water droplets. This process is temperature dependent, as supercooled water droplets only exist in a cloud that is below freezing. In addition, because of the great temperature difference between cloud and ground level, these ice crystals may melt as they fall and become rain.[^cite_note-24]

Raindrops have sizes ranging from 0.1 to 9 mm (0.0039 to 0.3543 in) mean diameter but develop a tendency to break up at larger sizes. Smaller drops are called cloud droplets, and their shape is spherical. As a raindrop increases in size, its shape becomes more oblate, with its largest cross-section facing the oncoming airflow. Large rain drops become increasingly flattened on the bottom, like [hamburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger) buns; very large ones are shaped like [parachutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Villermaux-26] Contrary to popular belief, their shape does not resemble a teardrop.[^cite_note-27] The biggest raindrops on Earth were recorded over Brazil and the [Marshall Islands](19147.md) in 2004 — some of them were as large as 10 mm (0.39 in). The large size is explained by condensation on large [smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke) particles or by collisions between drops in small regions with particularly high content of liquid water.[^cite_note-28]

Raindrops associated with melting hail tend to be larger than other raindrops.[^cite_note-29]

Intensity and duration of rainfall are usually inversely related, i.e., high-intensity storms are likely to be of short duration and low-intensity storms can have a long duration.[^cite_note-JS-30][^cite_note-31]

### Raindrop impacts

Raindrops impact at their [terminal velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_velocity), which is greater for larger drops due to their larger mass-to-drag ratio. At sea level and without wind, 0.5 mm (0.020 in) [drizzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drizzle) impacts at 2 m/s (6.6 ft/s) or 7.2 km/h (4.5 mph), while large 5 mm (0.20 in) drops impact at around 9 m/s (30 ft/s) or 32 km/h (20 mph).[^cite_note-35]

Rain falling on loosely packed material such as newly fallen ash can produce dimples that can be fossilized, called [raindrop impressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raindrop_impressions).[^cite_note-36] The air density dependence of the maximum raindrop diameter together with fossil raindrop imprints has been used to constrain the density of the air 2.7 billion years ago.[^cite_note-37] The [sound of raindrops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droplet#Sound) hitting water is caused by bubbles of air [oscillating underwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnaert_resonance).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

The [METAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METAR) code for rain is RA, while the coding for rain showers is SHRA.[^cite_note-METAR-40]

### Virga

Main article: [Virga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virga)

In certain conditions, precipitation may fall from a cloud but then evaporate or [sublime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublimation_\(phase_transition\)) before reaching the ground. This is termed [virga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virga), also known as "fallstreaks" or "precipitation trails",[^cite_note-41] and also refers to an optical phenomenon where the brightness of precipitation appears to abruptly change under a cloud.[^cite_note-42] Virga is common in hot and arid climates,[^cite_note-43] but has been recorded in the Arctic[^cite_note-44] and Antarctica[^cite_note-45] and is known to occur on planets beyond Earth, including Mars[^cite_note-46] and Venus.[^cite_note-47]

## Causes

### Frontal activity

Main article: [Weather fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_fronts)

Stratiform (a broad shield of precipitation with a relatively similar intensity) and dynamic precipitation (convective precipitation which is showery in nature with large changes in intensity over short distances) occur as a consequence of slow ascent of air in [synoptic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synoptic_scale_meteorology) (on the order of cm/s), such as in the vicinity of [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) and near and poleward of surface [warm fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm_front). Similar ascent is seen around [tropical cyclones](8282374.md) outside the [eyewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_\(cyclone\)), and in comma-head precipitation patterns around [mid-latitude cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-latitude_cyclone).[^cite_note-Geerts-48]

A wide variety of weather can be found along an occluded front, with thunderstorms possible, but usually, their passage is associated with a drying of the air mass. Occluded fronts usually form around mature low-pressure areas.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-49] What separates rainfall from other precipitation types, such as [ice pellets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_pellets) and snow, is the presence of a thick layer of air aloft which is above the melting point of water, which melts the frozen precipitation well before it reaches the ground. If there is a shallow near-surface layer that is below freezing, freezing rain (rain which freezes on contact with surfaces in subfreezing environments) will result.[^cite_note-50] [Hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail) becomes an increasingly infrequent occurrence when the freezing level within the atmosphere exceeds 3,400 m (11,000 ft) above ground level.[^cite_note-mesoanal-51]

### Convection

Diagram showing that as moist air becomes heated more than its surroundings, it moves upward, resulting in brief rain showers.

Convective precipitation

Diagram showing how moist air over the ocean rises and flows over the land, causing cooling and rain as it hits mountain ridges.

Orographic precipitation

[Convective rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection_rain), or showery precipitation, occurs from convective clouds (e.g., [cumulonimbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulonimbus) or [cumulus congestus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulus_congestus)). It falls as showers with rapidly changing intensity. Convective precipitation falls over a certain area for a relatively short time, as convective clouds have limited horizontal extent. Most precipitation in the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics) appears to be convective; however, it has been suggested that stratiform precipitation also occurs.[^cite_note-Geerts-48][^cite_note-52] [Graupel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graupel) and [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail) indicate convection.[^cite_note-53] In mid-latitudes, convective precipitation is intermittent and often associated with baroclinic boundaries such as [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front), [squall lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squall_line), and warm fronts.[^cite_note-54]

### Orographic effects

Main articles: [Orographic lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orographic_lift), [Precipitation types (meteorology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_types_\(meteorology\)), and [United States rainfall climatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_rainfall_climatology)

Orographic precipitation occurs on the [windward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward) side of mountains and is caused by the rising air motion of a large-scale flow of moist air across the mountain ridge, resulting in [adiabatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabatic_lapse_rate) cooling and condensation. In mountainous parts of the world subjected to relatively consistent winds (for example, the [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_wind)), a more moist [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) usually prevails on the windward side of a mountain than on the [leeward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward) or downwind side. Moisture is removed by orographic lift, leaving drier air (see [katabatic wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katabatic_wind)) on the descending and generally warming, leeward side where a [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) is observed.[^cite_note-MT-16]

In [Hawaii](13270.md), [Mount Waiʻaleʻale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Wai%CA%BBale%CA%BBale), on the island of Kauai, is notable for its extreme rainfall, as it is amongst the places in the world with the highest levels of rainfall, with 9,500 mm (373 in).[^cite_note-55] Systems known as [Kona storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kona_storm) affect the state with heavy rains between October and April.[^cite_note-BIRCH-56] Local climates vary considerably on each island due to their topography, divisible into windward (*Koʻolau*) and leeward (*Kona*) regions based upon location relative to the higher mountains. Windward sides face the east to northeast [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds) and receive much more rainfall; leeward sides are drier and sunnier, with less rain and less cloud cover.[^cite_note-57]

In South America, the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) mountain range blocks Pacific moisture that arrives in that continent, resulting in a desert-like climate just downwind across western Argentina.[^cite_note-Andes-58] The [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_\(U.S.\)) range creates the same effect in North America forming the [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin) and [Mojave Deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

See also: [Monsoon](57630.md) and [Tropical cyclone](8282374.md)

Main article: [Wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season)

The wet, or rainy, season is the time of year, covering one or more months, when most of the average annual rainfall in a region falls.[^cite_note-61] The term *green season* is also sometimes used as a [euphemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemism) by tourist authorities.[^cite_note-62] Areas with wet seasons are dispersed across portions of the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics) and [subtropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics).[^cite_note-63] [Savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) climates and areas with [monsoon](57630.md) regimes have wet summers and dry winters. Tropical rainforests technically do not have dry or wet seasons, since their rainfall is equally distributed through the year.[^cite_note-Hyde-64] Some areas with pronounced rainy seasons will see a break in rainfall mid-season when the [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone) or [monsoon trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_trough) move poleward of their location during the middle of the warm season.[^cite_note-JS-30] When the wet season occurs during the warm season, or [summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer), rain falls mainly during the late afternoon and early evening hours. The wet season is a time when both [air quality](10934212.md)[^cite_note-65] and [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater) quality improves.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

[Tropical cyclones](8282374.md), a source of very heavy rainfall, consist of large air masses several hundred miles across with low pressure at the centre and with winds blowing inward towards the centre in either a clockwise direction (southern hemisphere) or counterclockwise (northern hemisphere).[^cite_note-68] Although [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone) can take an enormous toll in lives and personal property, they may be important factors in the precipitation regimes of places they impact, as they may bring much-needed precipitation to otherwise dry regions.[^cite_note-2005_EPac_outlook-69] Areas in their path can receive a year's worth of rainfall from a tropical cyclone passage.[^cite_note-70]

### Human influence

World map of temperature distribution shows the northern hemisphere was warmer than the southern hemisphere during the periods compared.

Surface air temperature change over the past 50 years

[^cite_note-71]

See also: [Effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change), [Effects of climate change on the water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_the_water_cycle), and [Urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island)

The fine particulate matter produced by car exhaust and other human sources of pollution forms [cloud condensation nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_condensation_nuclei) leads to the production of clouds and increases the likelihood of rain. As commuters and commercial traffic cause pollution to build up over the course of the week, the likelihood of rain increases: it peaks by Saturday, after five days of weekday pollution has been built up. In heavily populated areas that are near the coast, such as the United States' [Eastern Seaboard](89126.md), the effect can be dramatic: there is a 22% higher chance of rain on Saturdays than on Mondays.[^cite_note-72] The urban heat island effect warms cities 0.6 to 5.6 °C (33.1 to 42.1 °F) above surrounding suburbs and rural areas. This extra heat leads to greater upward motion, which can induce additional shower and thunderstorm activity. Rainfall rates downwind of cities are increased between 48% and 116%. Partly as a result of this warming, monthly rainfall is about 28% greater between 32 and 64 km (20 and 40 mi) downwind of cities, compared with upwind.[^cite_note-73] Some cities induce a total precipitation increase of 51%.[^cite_note-74]

Increasing temperatures tend to increase evaporation which can lead to more precipitation. Precipitation generally increased over land north of 30°N from 1900 through 2005 but has declined over the tropics since the 1970s. Globally there has been no statistically significant overall trend in precipitation over the past century, although trends have varied widely by region and over time. Eastern portions of North and South America, northern Europe, and northern and central Asia have become wetter. The Sahel, the Mediterranean, southern Africa and parts of southern Asia have become drier. There has been an increase in the number of heavy precipitation events over many areas during the past century, as well as an increase since the 1970s in the prevalence of droughts—especially in the tropics and subtropics. Changes in precipitation and evaporation over the oceans are suggested by the decreased salinity of mid- and high-latitude waters (implying more precipitation), along with increased salinity in lower latitudes (implying less precipitation and/or more evaporation). Over the contiguous United States, total annual precipitation increased at an average rate of 6.1 percent since 1900, with the greatest increases within the East North Central climate region (11.6 percent per century) and the South (11.1 percent). Hawaii was the only region to show a decrease (−9.25 percent).[^cite_note-75]

Analysis of 65 years of United States of America rainfall records show the lower 48 states have an increase in heavy downpours since 1950. The largest increases are in the Northeast and Midwest, which in the past decade, have seen 31 and 16 percent more heavy downpours compared to the 1950s. [Rhode Island](25410.md) is the state with the largest increase, 104%. [McAllen, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McAllen,_Texas) is the city with the largest increase, 700%. Heavy downpour in the analysis are the days where total precipitation exceeded the top one percent of all rain and snow days during the years 1950–2014.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

The most successful attempts at influencing weather involve [cloud seeding](449660.md), which include techniques used to increase [winter precipitation](28191.md) over mountains and suppress [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail).[^cite_note-AMSmod-78]

## Characteristics

### Patterns



Band of thunderstorms seen on a [weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar) display

Main article: [Rainband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainband)

[Rainbands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainband) are cloud and precipitation areas which are significantly elongated. Rainbands can be [stratiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratus_cloud) or [convective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_convection),[^cite_note-79] and are generated by differences in temperature. When noted on [weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar) imagery, this precipitation elongation is referred to as banded structure.[^cite_note-80] Rainbands in advance of warm [occluded fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occluded_front) and [warm fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm_front) are associated with weak upward motion,[^cite_note-81] and tend to be wide and stratiform in nature.[^cite_note-82]

Rainbands spawned near and ahead of [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) can be [squall lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squall_line) which are able to produce [tornadoes](37530.md).[^cite_note-83] Rainbands associated with cold fronts can be warped by mountain barriers perpendicular to the front's orientation due to the formation of a low-level [barrier jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_jet).[^cite_note-84] Bands of thunderstorms can form with [sea breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_breeze) and [land breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_breeze) boundaries if enough moisture is present. If sea breeze rainbands become active enough just ahead of a cold front, they can mask the location of the cold front itself.[^cite_note-85]

Once a cyclone occludes an [occluded front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occluded_front) (a trough of warm air aloft) will be caused by strong southerly winds on its eastern periphery rotating aloft around its northeast, and ultimately northwestern, periphery (also termed the warm conveyor belt), forcing a surface trough to continue into the cold sector on a similar curve to the occluded front. The front creates the portion of an occluded cyclone known as its *comma head*, due to the [comma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comma_\(punctuation\))-like shape of the mid-tropospheric cloudiness that accompanies the feature. It can also be the focus of locally heavy precipitation, with thunderstorms possible if the atmosphere along the front is unstable enough for convection.[^cite_note-TROW-86] Banding within the comma head precipitation pattern of an [extratropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone) can yield significant amounts of rain.[^cite_note-87] Behind extratropical cyclones during fall and winter, rainbands can form downwind of relative warm bodies of water such as the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes). Downwind of islands, bands of showers and thunderstorms can develop due to low-level wind convergence downwind of the island edges. Offshore [California](5407.md), this has been noted in the wake of cold fronts.[^cite_note-88]

Rainbands within tropical cyclones are curved in orientation. Tropical cyclone rainbands contain showers and thunderstorms that, together with the eyewall and the eye, constitute a [hurricane or tropical storm](8282374.md). The extent of rainbands around a tropical cyclone can help determine the cyclone's intensity.[^cite_note-ODT-89]

### Acidity



Sources of acid rain

See also: [Acid rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_rain)

The phrase *acid rain* was first used by Scottish chemist Robert Augus Smith in 1852.[^cite_note-90] The [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH) of rain varies, especially due to its origin. On America's East Coast, rain that is derived from the Atlantic Ocean typically has a pH of 5.0–5.6; rain that comes across the continental from the west has a pH of 3.8–4.8; and local thunderstorms can have a pH as low as 2.0.[^cite_note-91] Rain becomes acidic primarily due to the presence of two strong acids, [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid) (H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>) and [nitric acid](21655.md) (HNO<sub>3</sub>). Sulfuric acid is derived from natural sources such as volcanoes, and wetlands (sulfate-reducing bacteria); and anthropogenic sources such as the combustion of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels), and mining where H<sub>2</sub>S is present. Nitric acid is produced by natural sources such as lightning, soil bacteria, and natural fires; while also produced anthropogenically by the combustion of fossil fuels and from power plants. In the past 20 years, the concentrations of nitric and sulfuric acid has decreased in presence of rainwater, which may be due to the significant increase in ammonium (most likely as ammonia from livestock production), which acts as a [buffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_solution) in acid rain and raises the pH.[^cite_note-92]

Main article: [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)

The Köppen classification depends on average monthly values of temperature and precipitation. The most commonly used form of the Köppen classification has five primary types labeled A through E. Specifically, the primary types are A, tropical; B, dry; C, mild mid-latitude; D, cold mid-latitude; and E, polar. The five primary classifications can be further divided into secondary classifications such as [rain forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_forest), [monsoon](57630.md), [tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna), [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical), [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental), [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe), [subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate), [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra), [polar ice cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_ice_cap), and [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert).[^cite_note-94]

Rain forests are characterized by high rainfall, with definitions setting minimum normal annual rainfall between 1,750 and 2,000 mm (69 and 79 in).[^cite_note-95] A tropical savanna is a [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland) [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome) located in [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) to [semi-humid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid) climate regions of [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical) and [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) [latitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitudes), with rainfall between 750 and 1,270 mm (30 and 50 in) a year. They are widespread on Africa, and are also found in India, the northern parts of South America, Malaysia, and Australia.[^cite_note-SAVWOOD-96] The humid subtropical climate zone is where winter rainfall is associated with large [storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm) that the [westerlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerlies) steer from west to east. Most summer rainfall occurs during thunderstorms and from occasional tropical cyclones.[^cite_note-97] Humid subtropical climates lie on the east side continents, roughly between [latitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude) 20° and 40° degrees away from the equator.[^cite_note-98]

An oceanic (or maritime) climate is typically found along the west coasts at the middle latitudes of all the world's continents, bordering cool oceans, as well as southeastern Australia, and is accompanied by plentiful precipitation year-round.[^cite_note-99] The Mediterranean climate regime resembles the climate of the lands in the [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin), parts of western North America, parts of [Western](33613.md) and [South Australia](26716.md), in southwestern South Africa and in parts of central Chile. The climate is characterized by hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters.[^cite_note-100] A steppe is a dry [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland).[^cite_note-101] Subarctic climates are cold with continuous [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost) and little precipitation.[^cite_note-subritter-102]

### Pollution and composition

&#x20;Aside from contamination of rainwater by [sulfuric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur\(IV\)_oxide) and [nitric oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitric_oxides), which produces acid rain, various pollutants from industry and household wastes can end up in rainwater, causing detrimental effects on aquatic and human life. Pollutants from solid waste, leaking vehicles and machinery, fertilizers and other potentially hazardous substances enter water sources directly through dumping or as [surface runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff) following heavy rain.[^cite_note-103] One classification of pollutants of particular note is [perfluoroalkyl substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfluoroalkyl_acid), synthetic chemical compounds used in a wide variety of consumer products.[^cite_note-Evidence-104] Rain has the potential to dissolve and transport different compounds, including the aforementioned toxic substances, with certain ions such as [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_ion) and [bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicarbonate) appearing frequently, in more acidic rainwater.[^cite_note-105] However, the composition of rainwater at any given time and place is highly variable based on the ongoing manufacturing, farming, and waste-producing activities.[^cite_note-106]

This section is an excerpt from [PFAS § Prevalence in rain, soil, water bodies, and air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFAS#Prevalence_in_rain,_soil,_water_bodies,_and_air).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=PFAS\&action=edit)]

In 2022, levels of at least four perfluoroalkyl acids (PFAAs) in rain water worldwide greatly exceeded the EPA's lifetime [drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water) health advisories as well as comparable Danish, Dutch, and [European Union](9317.md) safety standards, leading to the conclusion that "the global spread of these four PFAAs in the atmosphere has led to the [planetary boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_boundary) for chemical pollution being exceeded".[^cite_note-107] The most common PFAS found in the environment is [Trifluoroacetic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trifluoroacetic_acid) (TFA).[^cite_note-108] Its presence is ubiquitous in the environment, especially in aquatic ecosystems, where it persists with increasing concentrations globally.[^cite_note-109]

It had been thought that PFAAs would eventually end up in the oceans, where they would be diluted over decades, but a field study published in 2021 by researchers at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) found that they are often transferred from water to air when waves reach land, are a significant source of [air pollution](10934212.md), and eventually get into rain. The researchers concluded that pollution may impact large areas.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Soil is also contaminated and the chemicals have been found in remote areas such as [Antarctica](18959138.md).[^cite_note-113] [Soil contamination](4229946.md) can result in higher levels of PFAS found in foods such as white rice, coffee, and animals reared on contaminated ground.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-PFAS_auto-115][^cite_note-116] In 2024, a worldwide study of 45,000 groundwater samples found that 31% of samples contained levels of PFAS that were harmful to human health; these samples were taken from areas not near any obvious source of contamination.[^cite_note-117]

Contamination has also been seen in water wells and other sources of drinking water. This contamination is seen in US, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, and Canada, but information from most developing countries is nearly nonexistent.[^cite_note-118] The lack of information on PFAS contamination in [developing countries](78449.md), especially those in Africa, are due to structural socioeconomic inequality and lack of access to the expensive laboratory capabilities required for PFAS quantification.[^cite_note-PFAS_Aborode-2025-119]

### Gauges

See also: [Rain gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_gauge), [Disdrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disdrometer), and [Snow gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_gauge)



Standard rain gauge

Rain rate is measured in units of length per unit time, typically in millimeters per hour,[^cite_note-120] or in countries where [imperial units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_units) are more common, inches per hour.[^cite_note-121] The "length", or more accurately, "depth" being measured is the depth of rain water that would accumulate on a flat, horizontal and impermeable surface during a given amount of time, typically an hour.[^cite_note-122] This is [dimensionally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional_analysis) equivalent to volume of water per unit area: one millimeter of rainfall is the equivalent of one liter of water per square meter.[^cite_note-123] This measurement is done with a [gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_gauge). A cylindrical can with straight sides is the most inexpensive and simple used that can be made and left out in the open, but its accuracy will depend on what ruler is used to measure the rain with.[^cite_note-124] Meteorologists have a standard type of gauge for both rainfall or snowfall with an inner cylinder and an outer cylinder that adds to the volume of the full inner cylinder.[^cite_note-125] Other types of gauges include the popular wedge gauge (the cheapest rain gauge and most fragile), the tipping bucket rain gauge, and the weighing rain gauge.[^cite_note-126]

When a precipitation measurement is made, various networks exist across the United States and elsewhere where rainfall measurements can be submitted through the Internet, such as [CoCoRAHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Collaborative_Rain,_Hail_and_Snow_network) or GLOBE.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] If a network is not available in the area where one lives, the nearest local weather or met office will likely be interested in the measurement.[^cite_note-129]

One of the main uses of weather radar is to be able to assess the amount of precipitations fallen over large basins for [hydrological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrology) purposes.[^cite_note-130] For instance, river [flood control](20913655.md), sewer management and dam construction are all areas where planners use rainfall accumulation data. Radar-derived rainfall estimates complement surface station data which can be used for calibration. ### Return period

See also: [100-year flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100-year_flood)

The average time between occurrences of an event with a specified intensity and duration is called the [return period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_period).[^cite_note-glossary-137] The intensity of a storm can be predicted for any return period and storm duration, from charts based on historic data for the location.[^cite_note-138] The return period is often expressed as an *n*-year event. For instance, a 10-year storm describes a rare rainfall event occurring on average once every 10 years. The rainfall will be greater and the flooding will be worse than the worst storm expected in any single year. A 100-year storm describes an extremely rare rainfall event occurring on average once in a century. The rainfall will be extreme and flooding worse than a 10-year event. The probability of an event in any year is the inverse of the return period (assuming the probability remains the same for each year).[^cite_note-glossary-137] For instance, a 10-year storm has a probability of occurring of 10 percent in any given year, and a 100-year storm occurs with a 1 percent probability in a year. As with all probability events, it is possible, though improbable, to have multiple 100-year storms in a single year.[^cite_note-139]

## Forecasting

Main article: [Quantitative precipitation forecast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_precipitation_forecast)



Example of a five-day rainfall forecast from the [Hydrometeorological Prediction Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrometeorological_Prediction_Center)

The Quantitative Precipitation Forecast (abbreviated QPF) is the expected amount of liquid precipitation accumulated over a specified time period over a specified area.[^cite_note-SERFC-140] A QPF will be specified when a measurable precipitation type reaching a minimum threshold is forecast for any hour during a QPF valid period. Precipitation forecasts tend to be bound by synoptic hours such as 0000, 0600, 1200 and 1800 [GMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMT). Terrain is considered in QPFs by use of topography or based upon climatological precipitation patterns from observations with fine detail.[^cite_note-141] Starting in the mid to late 1990s, QPFs were used within hydrologic forecast models to simulate impact to rivers throughout the United States.[^cite_note-142]

[Forecast models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_weather_prediction) show significant sensitivity to humidity levels within the [planetary boundary layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_boundary_layer), or in the lowest levels of the atmosphere, which decreases with height.[^cite_note-143] QPF can be generated on a quantitative, forecasting amounts, or a qualitative, forecasting the probability of a specific amount, basis.[^cite_note-144] Radar imagery forecasting techniques show higher [skill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forecast_skill) than model forecasts within 6 to 7 hours of the time of the radar image. The forecasts can be verified through use of rain gauge measurements, weather radar estimates, or a combination of both. Various skill scores can be determined to measure the value of the rainfall forecast.[^cite_note-Canada-145]

## Impact

### Agricultural



Rainfall estimates for southern Japan and the surrounding region from 20 to 27 July 2009

Precipitation, especially rain, has a dramatic effect on agriculture. All plants need at least some water to survive, therefore rain (being the most effective means of watering) is important to agriculture. While a regular rain pattern is usually vital to healthy plants, too much or too little rainfall can be harmful, even devastating to crops. [Drought](37335.md) can kill crops and increase erosion,[^cite_note-146] while overly wet weather can cause harmful [fungus](19178965.md) growth.[^cite_note-147] Plants need varying amounts of rainfall to survive.[^cite_note-148] For example, certain [cacti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus) require small amounts of water,[^cite_note-149] while crops such as [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) require thousands of liters of water to provide good yields and must be continuously [irrigated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) in addition to normal watering through rainfall.[^cite_note-150] Plants that thrive in drier climates thrive in conditions with infrequent, large rainfall events, while plants in wetter ecosystems prefer the opposite: frequent, mild rainfall.[^cite_note-151]

In areas with wet and dry seasons, [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) nutrients diminish and erosion increases during the wet season.[^cite_note-JS-30] Animals have adaptation and survival strategies for the wetter regime. The previous dry season leads to food shortages into the wet season, as the crops have yet to mature.[^cite_note-152] Developing countries have noted that their populations show seasonal weight fluctuations due to food shortages seen before the first harvest, which occurs late in the wet season.[^cite_note-153] Rain may be [harvested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainwater_harvesting) through the use of [rainwater tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainwater_tank); treated to potable use or for non-potable use indoors or for irrigation.[^cite_note-154] Excessive rain during short periods of time can cause [flash floods](244391.md).[^cite_note-155]

### Culture and religion

See also: [List of rain deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rain_deities)

photograph

A [rain dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainmaking_\(ritual\)) being performed in [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar), Ethiopia

Cultural attitudes towards rain differ across the world. In [temperate climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate), people tend to be more stressed when the weather is unstable or cloudy, with its impact greater on men than women.[^cite_note-156] Rain can also bring joy, as some consider it to be soothing or enjoy the aesthetic appeal of it. In dry places, such as India,[^cite_note-157] or during periods of [drought](37335.md),[^cite_note-158] rain lifts people's moods. In [Botswana](3464.md), the [Setswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setswana) word for rain, *pula*, is used as [the name of the national currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana_pula), in recognition of the economic importance of rain in its country, since it has a desert climate.[^cite_note-159] Several cultures have developed means of dealing with rain and have developed numerous protection devices such as [umbrellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella) and [raincoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raincoat), and diversion devices such as [gutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_gutter) and [storm drains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_drain) that lead rains to sewers.[^cite_note-160] Many people find the scent during and immediately after rain pleasant or distinctive. The source of this scent is [petrichor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrichor), an oil produced by plants, then absorbed by rocks and soil, and later released into the air during rainfall.[^cite_note-Bear1964-161]



Rain, depicted in the 1493 *[Nuremberg Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle)*

Rain holds an important religious significance in many cultures.[^cite_note-Mersereau-162] The ancient [Sumerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer) believed that rain was the [semen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semen) of the [sky god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_deity) [An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anu),[^cite_note-NemetNajat1996-163] which fell from the heavens to inseminate his consort, the [earth goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_goddess) [Ki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ki_\(goddess\)),[^cite_note-NemetNajat1996-163] causing her to give birth to all the plants of the earth.[^cite_note-NemetNajat1996-163] The [Akkadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire) believed that the clouds were the breasts of Anu's consort [Antu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antu_\(goddess\))[^cite_note-NemetNajat1996-163] and that rain was milk from her breasts.[^cite_note-NemetNajat1996-163] According to Jewish tradition, in the first century BC, the Jewish miracle-worker [Honi ha-M'agel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honi_ha-M'agel) ended a three-year drought in [Judaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_\(Roman_province\)) by drawing a circle in the sand and praying for rain, refusing to leave the circle until his prayer was granted.[^cite_note-164] In his *[Meditations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditations)*, the Roman emperor [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius) preserves a prayer for rain made by the [Athenians](1216.md) to the Greek sky god [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus).[^cite_note-Mersereau-162] Various [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) tribes are known to have historically conducted [rain dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainmaking_\(ritual\)) in effort to encourage rainfall.[^cite_note-Mersereau-162] Rainmaking rituals are also important in many African cultures.[^cite_note-165] In the present-day United States, various [state governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(United_States\)) have held [Days of Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Prayer) for rain, including the [Days of Prayer for Rain in the State of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Prayer_for_Rain_in_the_State_of_Texas) in 2011.[^cite_note-Mersereau-162]

## Global climatology

See also: [Earth rainfall climatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_rainfall_climatology)

Approximately 505,000 km<sup>3</sup> (121,000 mi<sup>3</sup>) of water falls as precipitation each year across the globe with 398,000 km<sup>3</sup> (95,000 mi<sup>3</sup>) of it over the oceans.[^cite_note-chow-166] Given the Earth's surface area, that means the globally averaged annual precipitation is 990 mm (39 in). Deserts are defined as areas with an average annual precipitation of less than 250 mm (10 in) per year,[^cite_note-usgsdesert-167][^cite_note-168] or as areas where more water is lost by [evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration) than falls as precipitation.[^cite_note-brittanica-169]

### Deserts

Main article: [Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert)



Largest deserts



Isolated towering vertical desert shower

The northern half of Africa is dominated by the world's most extensive hot, dry region, the [Sahara Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_Desert). Some deserts also occupy much of southern Africa: the [Namib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namib) and the [Kalahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahari). Across Asia, a large annual rainfall minimum, composed primarily of deserts, stretches from the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert) in Mongolia west-southwest through western Pakistan ([Balochistan](8431743.md)) and Iran into the [Arabian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Desert) in Saudi Arabia. Most of Australia is semi-arid or desert,[^cite_note-170] making it the world's driest inhabited continent. In South America, the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) mountain range blocks Pacific moisture that arrives in that continent, resulting in a desert-like climate just downwind across western Argentina.[^cite_note-Andes-58] The drier areas of the United States are regions where the [Sonoran Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoran_Desert) overspreads the Desert Southwest, the Great Basin, and central Wyoming.[^cite_note-USatl-171]

### Polar deserts

Main articles: [Polar desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_desert) and [Polar climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_climate)

Since rain only falls as liquid, it rarely falls when surface temperatures are below freezing unless there is a layer of warm air aloft, in which case it becomes [freezing rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freezing_rain). Due to the entire atmosphere being below freezing, frigid climates usually see very little rainfall and are often known as [polar deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_desert). A common biome in this area is the [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra), which has a short summer thaw and a long frozen winter. Rainfall in these polar deserts and precipitation in general is very low, though they cannot be described as [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridity), as the soil is predictably moist during brief growing seasons and air humidity remains relatively high, with evaporation rates being very low.[^cite_note-172] Due to its location, [Antarctica](18959138.md) contains some of the driest regions in the world.[^cite_note-173]

### Rainforests

See also: [Rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest)

Rainforests are characterized mainly as areas of the world with very high humidity. [Tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) and [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_rainforest) rainforests exist, as do the less common dry rainforests.[^cite_note-\:0-174] Tropical rainforests occupy a large band of the planet, mainly along the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), as climates associated with tropical rainforests are most often found within ten degrees of latitude of the equator. They do not experience natural seasons as do many other regions, with the average daylight hours and temperatures remaining fairly constant throughout the year.[^cite_note-McKnight-175] Temperate rainforests are often located much further from the equator, but still have high rainfall and in many cases have a closed tree [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(botany\)).[^cite_note-Definition-176] Dry rainforests maintain a dense canopy, but can face periods of [drought](37335.md).[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-\:0-174]

### Monsoons

See also: [Monsoon](57630.md) and [Monsoon trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_trough)

The equatorial region near the [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone) (ITCZ), or monsoon trough, is the wettest portion of the world's continents. Annually, the rain belt within the tropics marches northward by August, then moves back southward into the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere) by February and March.[^cite_note-178] Within Asia, rainfall is favored across its southern portion from India east and northeast across the Philippines and southern China into Japan due to the monsoon advecting moisture primarily from the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) into the region.[^cite_note-179] The monsoon trough can reach as far north as the [40th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_parallel_north) in East Asia during August before moving southward after that. Its poleward progression is accelerated by the onset of the summer monsoon, which is characterized by the development of lower air pressure (a [thermal low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_low)) over the warmest part of Asia.[^cite_note-NCFMRF-180][^cite_note-181] Similar, but weaker, monsoon circulations are present over North America and Australia.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

During the summer, the Southwest monsoon combined with [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California) and [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) moisture moving around the [subtropical ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_ridge) in the Atlantic Ocean brings the promise of afternoon and evening thunderstorms to the southern tier of the United States as well as the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains).[^cite_note-JHorel-184] The eastern half of the contiguous United States east of the [98th meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/98th_meridian_west), the mountains of the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest), and the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_\(U.S.\)) range are the wetter portions of the nation, with average rainfall exceeding 760 mm (30 in) per year.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-185] [Tropical cyclones](8282374.md) enhance precipitation across southern sections of the United States,[^cite_note-186] as well as [Puerto Rico](23041.md), the [United States Virgin Islands](32135.md),[^cite_note-187] the [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md),[^cite_note-188] [Guam](11974.md),[^cite_note-GuamTyphoons-189] and [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa).[^cite_note-190]

### Impact of the Westerlies

See also: [Westerlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerlies)



Long-term mean precipitation by month

Westerly flow from the mild North Atlantic leads to wetness across western Europe, in particular Ireland and the United Kingdom, where the western coasts can receive between 1,000 mm (39 in), at sea level and 2,500 mm (98 in), on the mountains of rain per year. [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen), Norway is one of the more famous European rain-cities with its yearly precipitation of 2,250 mm (89 in) on average. During the fall, winter, and spring, Pacific storm systems bring most of [Hawaii](13270.md) and the western United States much of their precipitation.[^cite_note-JHorel-184] Over the top of the ridge, the jet stream brings a summer precipitation maximum to the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes). Large thunderstorm areas known as [mesoscale convective complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoscale_convective_complex) move through the Plains, Midwest, and Great Lakes during the warm season, contributing up to 10% of the annual precipitation to the region.[^cite_note-Walker-191]

The [El Niño-Southern Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o-Southern_Oscillation) affects the precipitation distribution by altering rainfall patterns across the western United States,[^cite_note-192] Midwest,[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194] the Southeast,[^cite_note-195] and throughout the tropics. There is also evidence that [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) leads to increased precipitation and higher frequency of extreme precipitation events in the eastern portions of North America, while rainfall is becoming less frequent and in lower amounts in the tropics, subtropics, and the western United States.[^cite_note-196]

### Wettest known locations



Torrential rain in Ballina: 280mm of rain in 24 hours

[Cherrapunji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherrapunji), situated on the southern slopes of the [Eastern Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) in [Shillong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shillong), India, is the confirmed wettest place on Earth, with an average annual rainfall of 11,430 mm (450 in). The highest recorded rainfall in a single year was 22,987 mm (905.0 in) in 1861. The 38-year average at nearby [Mawsynram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawsynram), [Meghalaya](326019.md), India, is 11,873 mm (467.4 in).[^cite_note-197] The wettest spot in Australia is [Mount Bellenden Ker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Bellenden_Ker) in the north-east of the country which records an average of 8,000 mm (310 in) per year, with over 12,200 mm (480.3 in) of rain recorded during 2000.[^cite_note-198] The [Big Bog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bog,_Maui) on the island of [Maui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maui) has the highest average annual rainfall in the Hawaiian Islands, at 10,300 mm (404 in).[^cite_note-199] [Mount Waiʻaleʻale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Waialeale) on the island of [Kauaʻi](70961.md) achieves similar torrential rains, while slightly lower than that of the Big Bog, at 9,500 mm (373 in)[^cite_note-200] of rain per year over the last 32 years, with a record 17,340 mm (683 in) in 1982. Its summit is considered one of the rainiest spots on earth, with a reported 360 days of rain per year.[^cite_note-201]

[Lloró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llor%C3%B3), a town situated in [Chocó](549667.md), Colombia, is probably the place with the largest rainfall in the world, averaging 13,300 mm (523.6 in) per year.[^cite_note-NCDCxrain-202] The Department of Chocó is extraordinarily humid. Tutunendaó, a small town situated in the same department, is one of the wettest estimated places on Earth, averaging 11,394 mm (448.6 in) per year; in 1974 the town received 26,303 mm (1,035.6 in), the largest annual rainfall measured in Colombia. Unlike Cherrapunji, which receives most of its rainfall between April and September, Tutunendaó receives rain almost uniformly distributed throughout the year.[^cite_note-203] [Quibdó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quibd%C3%B3), the capital of Chocó, receives the most rain in the world among cities with over 100,000 inhabitants: 9,000 mm (354 in) per year.[^cite_note-NCDCxrain-202]&#x20;
