**Denmark**[^cite_note-17] is a [Nordic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) in [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe). It is the [metropole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropole) and most populous constituent of the Kingdom of Denmark,[^cite_note-18] also known as the [Danish Realm](11746442.md), a [constitutionally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) that includes the [autonomous territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) of the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) and [Greenland](12118.md) in the north [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-territory-19] Metropolitan Denmark,[^cite_note-proper-20] also called "continental Denmark" or "Denmark proper",[^cite_note-21] consists of the northern [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) and an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) of 406 islands.[^cite_note-islands-22] It is the southernmost of the [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) countries, lying southwest of Sweden, south of Norway, and north of Germany, with which it shares [a short border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Germany_border). Metropolitan Denmark is situated between the [North Sea](21179.md) to the west and the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) to the east.[^cite_note-23]

The Kingdom of Denmark, including the Faroe Islands and Greenland, has roughly [1,400 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Denmark) greater than 100 square metres (1,100 sq ft) in area; 443 have been named and 78 are inhabited.[^cite_note-24] Denmark's population is over 6 million (1 May 2025),[^cite_note-Pop.Denmark-12] of which roughly 40% live in [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand), (Sjælland) the largest and most populated island in Metropolitan Denmark; [Copenhagen](5166.md), (København) the capital and largest city of the Danish Realm, is situated on Zealand and Amager and Slotsholmen.[^cite_note-25] Composed mostly of flat, [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land), Denmark is characterised by sandy coasts, low elevation, and a [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate). Denmark exercises [hegemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony) influence in the [Danish Realm](11746442.md), [devolving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) powers to the other constituent entities to handle their internal affairs. [Home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) was established in the Faroe Islands in 1948; [Greenland](12118.md) achieved home rule in 1979 and [further autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Greenlandic_self-government_referendum) in 2009.[^cite_note-26]

The [unified Kingdom of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Denmark) emerged in the eighth century AD as a [maritime power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_power) amid the struggle for [control of the Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominium_maris_baltici).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStoneBainBoothParnell200831-4] In 1397, it formed the [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union) with Norway and Sweden. This union persisted until Sweden's [secession](144732.md) in 1523. The remaining Kingdom of [Denmark–Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway) endured a [series of wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Wars) in the 17th century that resulted in [territorial cessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cession) to Sweden. A surge of [nationalist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Denmark#The_19th_century) in the 19th century were defeated by the Danes in the [First Schleswig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Schleswig_War) of 1848. The adoption of the [Constitution of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark) on 5 June 1849 ended the [absolute monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy). In the [Second Schleswig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Schleswig_War), Denmark lost [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md), which led to changes in Danish politics henceforth emphasising social cohesion in the diminished realm, as well as the clearing of the vast moors of Jutland for agriculture, new Christian movements split between [Indre Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre_Mission) and [Grundtvig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._F._S._Grundtvig), but generally a stronger self-perception among the people of belonging to a unified country and state. In 1920, [North Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Schleswig) became Danish again.

Denmark began industrialising in the mid 19th century, becoming a major agricultural exporter. It introduced [social and labour market reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanslergade_Agreement) in the early 20th century, forming the basis for the present [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_model) and advanced [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy). [Denmark remained neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_during_World_War_I) during [World War I](4764461.md); Danish neutrality was violated in [World War II](32927.md) by a rapid [German invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_\(1940\)) in April 1940. During occupation, a [resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_resistance_movement) emerged in 1943, while Iceland declared independence in 1944; Denmark was [liberated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_World_War_II#Hardship_and_the_end_of_the_war) after the end of the war in May 1945. In 1973, Denmark, together with [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_Greenland_from_the_European_Communities) but not the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md), became a member of what is now the [European Union](9317.md); however, it negotiated [certain opt-outs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opt-outs_in_the_European_Union), such as retaining its own currency, the [krone](236323.md).

Denmark is a [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) with an advanced [high-income economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_high-income_economy), high [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living), and robust [social welfare policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_model). Danish culture and society are broadly progressive egalitarian, and [socially liberal](765430.md); Denmark was the first country to legally recognise [same-sex partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Denmark). It is a founding member of [NATO](21133.md), the [Nordic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council), the [OECD](33853117.md), the [OSCE](22796.md), the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), and the [United Nations](31769.md), and is part of the [Schengen Area](16565139.md). Denmark maintains close political, cultural, and linguistic ties with its Scandinavian neighbours.

## Etymology

Main article: [Etymology of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Denmark)

The etymology of the name "Denmark", the relationship between "Danes" and "Denmark", and the emergence of Denmark as a unified kingdom are topics of continuous scholarly debate.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] This is centred primarily on the morpheme *"Dan"* and whether it refers to the [Dani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_\(tribe\)) or a historical person [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_\(king\)) and the exact meaning of the -*"mark"* ending.

Most etymological dictionaries and handbooks derive "Dan" from a word meaning "flat land", related to German *Tenne* "threshing floor", English *den* "cave".[^cite_note-et1-29] The element *mark* is believed to mean [woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland) or [borderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border) (see [marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_\(territory\))), with probable references to the border forests in south [Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Schleswig).[^cite_note-30]

The first recorded use of the word *Danmark* within Denmark itself is found on the two [Jelling stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelling_stones), which are [runestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runestone) believed to have been erected by [Gorm the Old](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorm_the_Old), considered to be Denmark's first king, (c. 955) and [Harald Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Bluetooth) (c. 965). The larger of the two stones is popularly cited as the "baptismal certificate" (*dåbsattest*) of Denmark,[^cite_note-31] though both use the word "Denmark", in the [accusative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusative_case) ᛏᛅᚾᛘᛅᚢᚱᚴ *tanmaurk* (\[danmɒrk]) on the large stone, and the [genitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genitive_case) ᛏᛅᚾᛘᛅᚱᚴᛅᚱ "tanmarkar" (pronounced \[danmarkaɽ]) on the small stone, while the [dative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dative_case) form *tąnmarku* (pronounced \[danmarkʊ]) is found on the contemporaneous Skivum stone. The inhabitants of Denmark are there called *tani* (\[danɪ]), or "Danes", in the accusative.

## History

Main article: [History of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Denmark)

See also: [History of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [History of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greenland)

### Prehistory



The gilded side of the [Trundholm sun chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trundholm_sun_chariot) dating from the Nordic Bronze Age

The earliest [archaeological finds in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Denmark) date back to the [Eem interglacial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eemian) from 130,000 to 110,000 [BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichaelsen200219-32] Denmark has been inhabited since around 12,500 BC and [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) has been evident since 3900 BC.[^cite_note-foreign_ministry-33] The [Nordic Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Bronze_Age) (1800–600 BC) in Denmark was marked by [burial mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus), which left an abundance of findings including [lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lur) and the [Sun Chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trundholm_sun_chariot).

During the [Pre-Roman Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Roman_Iron_Age) (500 BC – AD 1), native groups began migrating south, and the first tribal [Danes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_\(tribe\)) came to the country between the Pre-Roman and the [Germanic Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_Iron_Age),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBusck200220-34] in the [Roman Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Iron_Age) (AD 1–400).[^cite_note-foreign_ministry-33] The [Roman provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) maintained [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route) and relations with native tribes in Denmark, and [Roman coins](299259.md) have been found in Denmark. Evidence of strong [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_nations) cultural influence dates from this period in Denmark and much of North-West Europe and is among other things reflected in the finding of the [Gundestrup cauldron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundestrup_cauldron).

The tribal Danes came from the east [Danish islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Denmark) ([Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand)) and [Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania), today Skåne in Southern Sweden, and spoke an early form of [North Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_languages). Historians believe that before their arrival, most of [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) and the nearest islands were settled by tribal [Jutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutes). Many Jutes migrated to [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain), according to legend some as mercenaries of [Brythonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brythonic_languages) King [Vortigern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vortigern), and formed the south-eastern territories of [Kent](16766.md), the [Isle of Wight](15102.md) and other areas, where they settled. They were later absorbed or [ethnically cleansed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) by the invading [Angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_\(tribe\)) and [Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxons), who formed the [Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons). The remaining Jutish population in Jutland assimilated in with the settling [Danes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_\(tribe\)).

A short note about the *Dani* in *[Getica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getica)* by the historian [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes) is believed to be an early mention of the Danes, one of the [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) from whom modern [Danes](419786.md) are descended.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBusck200219-35][^cite_note-36] The [Danevirke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danevirke) defence structures were built in phases from the 3rd century forward and the sheer size of the construction efforts in AD 737 are attributed to the emergence of a Danish king.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichaelsen2002122–123-37] A [new runic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younger_Futhark) was first used around the same time and [Ribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribe), the oldest town of Denmark, was founded about AD 700.

### Viking and Middle Ages

Main articles: [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age) and [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union)



The [Ladby ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladby_ship), the largest ship burial found in Denmark

From the 8th to the 10th century the population of the wider [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Scandinavia) region was called [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) by non-Scandinavians. While they mostly lived off agriculture, fishing and trade, they were excellent sailors and would travel as far as Iceland, Greenland and Canada. They traded in all parts of Europe, down to Constantinople and beyond, but would also raid local settlements and set up colonies in far-flung places. The Danish Vikings were most actively raiding the eastern and southern [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles) and [Western Europe](33800.md). They settled in parts of England (known as the [Danelaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danelaw)) under King [Sweyn Forkbeard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweyn_Forkbeard) in 1013, and in France where Danes and Norwegians were allowed to settle in what would become [Normandy](21724.md) in exchange of allegiance to [Robert I of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_I_of_France) with [Rollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo) as first ruler.[^cite_note-38] Some [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons) [pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pence) of this period have been found in Denmark.[^cite_note-Lund-39]

Denmark was largely consolidated by the late 8th century and its rulers are consistently referred to in [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankish_language) sources as kings (*reges*). Under the reign of [Gudfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudfred) in 804 the Danish kingdom may have included all the [lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lands_of_Denmark) of Jutland, [Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania) and the Danish islands, excluding Bornholm.[^cite_note-40]

Large stone containing a carved depiction of Jesus Christ

Larger of the two Jelling stones, raised by [Harald Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Bluetooth)

The extant Danish monarchy traces its roots back to [Gorm the Old](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorm_the_Old), who established his reign in the early 10th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStoneBainBoothParnell200831-4] As attested by the [Jelling stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelling_stones), the Danes were [Christianised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianised) around 965 by [Harald Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Bluetooth), the son of Gorm and [Thyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyra). It is believed that Denmark became Christian for political reasons so as not to get invaded by the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). A rising Christian power in Europe, the Holy Roman Empire was an important trading partner for the Danes. As a deterrent against this threat, Harald built six [fortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortresses) around Denmark called [Trelleborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_ring_fortress) and built a further [Danevirke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danevirke). In the early 11th century, [Canute the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canute_the_Great) won and united Denmark, England, and Norway for almost 30 years with a Scandinavian army.[^cite_note-Lund-39][^cite_note-41]

Throughout the [High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages) and [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages), Denmark also included [Skåneland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%A5neland) (the areas of Scania, [Halland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halland), and [Blekinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blekinge) in present-day southern Sweden) and Danish kings ruled [Danish Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Estonia), as well as the [duchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy) of [Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig) and [Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holstein). Most of the latter two now form the state of [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md) in northern Germany.[^cite_note-42]

In 1397, Denmark entered into a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) known as the [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union) with Norway and Sweden, united under Queen [Margaret I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_I_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStoneBainBoothParnell200833-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The three countries were to be treated as equals in the union. However, even from the start, Margaret may not have been so idealistic; treating Denmark as the clear "senior" partner of the union.[^cite_note-Lauring-46][^cite_note-47] Thus, much of the next 125 years of [Scandinavian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Scandinavia) revolves around this union, with Sweden breaking off and re-joining the Kalmar Union repeatedly. The issue was for practical purposes resolved on 17 June 1523, as [Swedish King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swedish_monarchs) [Gustav Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_I_of_Sweden) conquered the city of [Stockholm](26741.md). The Protestant [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) spread to Scandinavia in the 1530s, and following the [Count's Feud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count's_Feud) civil war, [Denmark converted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation_in_Denmark%E2%80%93Norway_and_Holstein) to [Lutheranism](23371382.md) in 1536. Later that year, Denmark entered into a new union with Norway.

### Early modern history (1536–1849)

Extent of the [Dano-Norwegian Realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway). After the Napoleonic Wars, Norway was ceded to Sweden while Denmark kept the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md), [Greenland](12118.md), and [Iceland](14531.md).

After Sweden permanently [broke away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sweden_\(1523%E2%80%931611\)) from the Kalmar Union, Denmark tried on several occasions to reassert control over its neighbour. King [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV_of_Denmark) attacked Sweden in the 1611–1613 [Kalmar War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_War) but failed to accomplish his main objective of forcing it to return to the union. The war led to no territorial changes, but Sweden was forced to pay a [war indemnity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reparations) of 1 million silver [riksdaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riksdaler) to Denmark, an amount known as the *[Älvsborg ransom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Stettin_\(1570\))*.[^cite_note-48] King Christian used this money to found several towns and fortresses, most notably [Glückstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gl%C3%BCckstadt) (founded as a rival to [Hamburg](13467.md)) and [Christiania](22309.md). Inspired by the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company), he founded a similar [Danish company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_East_India_Company) and planned to claim Ceylon as a colony, but the company only managed to acquire [Tranquebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharangambadi) on India's [Coromandel Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coromandel_Coast).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Denmark's large colonial aspirations included a few key [trading posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_posts) in Africa and India. While Denmark's trading posts in India were of little note, it played an important role in the highly lucrative [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md), through its trading outposts in [Fort Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osu_Castle) in [Osu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osu,_Accra), [Ghana](12067.md) through which 1.5 million slaves were traded.[^cite_note-guardian6nov2018-51][^cite_note-52] While the Danish colonial empire was sustained by trade with other major powers, and [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) – ultimately a lack of resources led to its stagnation.[^cite_note-53]

In the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), Christian tried to become the leader of the [Lutheran](23371382.md) states in Germany but suffered a crushing defeat at the [Battle of Lutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lutter).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParker198478-54] The result was that the Catholic army under [Albrecht von Wallenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_von_Wallenstein) was able to invade, occupy, and pillage Jutland, forcing Denmark [to withdraw from the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_L%C3%BCbeck).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParker198479-55] Denmark managed to avoid territorial concessions, but King [Gustavus Adolphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Adolphus_of_Sweden)' intervention in Germany was seen as a sign that the military power of Sweden was on the rise while Denmark's influence in the region was declining. Swedish armies [invaded Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torstenson_War) in 1643 and claimed Scania in 1644. In the 1645 [Treaty of Brømsebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Treaty_of_Br%C3%B6msebro_\(1645\)), Denmark surrendered Halland, [Gotland](284507.md), the last parts of Danish Estonia, and several provinces in Norway.



The [Assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_on_Copenhagen_\(1659\)) on [Copenhagen](5166.md) on 11 February 1659 during the [Second Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Northern_War). Danish defenders under King [Frederick III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III_of_Denmark) successfully repelled the forces of the [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire). Painting by [Frederik Christian Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_Christian_Lund).

Seeing an opportunity to tear up the Treaty of Brømsebro, King [Frederick III of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III_of_Denmark), in 1657, declared war on Sweden, the latter being deeply involved in the [Second Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Northern_War) (1655–1660), and marched on [Bremen-Verden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen-Verden). This led to a massive Danish defeat as the armies of King [Charles X Gustav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_X_Gustav) of Sweden conquered [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) and, following the [Swedish March across](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_Across_the_Belts) the frozen [Danish Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Straits), occupied [Funen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funen) and much of [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand) before signing the [Peace of Roskilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Roskilde) in February 1658, which gave Sweden control of Scania, [Blekinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blekinge), [Bohuslän](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohusl%C3%A4n), [Trøndelag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%B8ndelag), and the island of [Bornholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornholm). Charles X Gustav quickly regretted not having ruined Denmark and in August 1658, he launched a [second attack on Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dano-Swedish_War_\(1658%E2%80%931660\)), conquered most of the Danish islands, and began a two-year-long siege of [Copenhagen](5166.md). King Frederick III actively led the defence of the city, rallying its citizens to take up arms, and [repelled the Swedish attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_on_Copenhagen_\(1659\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIsacson2002229-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEnglund2000610-57] The siege ended following the death of Charles X Gustav in 1660.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStoneBainBoothParnell200835-58] In the ensuing [peace settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Copenhagen_\(1660\)), Denmark managed to maintain its independence and regain control of Trøndelag and Bornholm.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrost2000180–183-59] Attaining great popularity following the war, Frederick III used this to [disband the elective monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Law) in favour of [absolute monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy), which lasted until 1848 in Denmark.[^cite_note-60]

Denmark tried but failed to regain control of Scania in the [Scanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanian_War) (1675–1679). After the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) (1700–21), Denmark managed to regain control of the parts of [Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig) and [Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holstein) ruled by the house of [Holstein-Gottorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holstein-Gottorp) in the 1720 [Treaty of Frederiksborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Frederiksborg) and the 1773 [Treaty of Tsarskoye Selo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tsarskoye_Selo), respectively. Denmark prospered greatly in the last decades of the 18th century due to its [neutral status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_neutrality_\(international_relations\)) allowing it to trade with both sides in the many contemporary wars. In the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), Denmark traded with both [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and joined the [League of Armed Neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_League_of_Armed_Neutrality) with [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), Sweden, and [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia).[^cite_note-61] British fears that Denmark-Norway would ally with France led to two attacks against Danish targets in Copenhagen in [1801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Copenhagen_\(1801\)) and [1807](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Copenhagen_\(1807\)). These attacks resulted in the British capturing most of the Dano-Norwegian navy and led to the outbreak of the [Gunboat War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_War). In 1807 much of Copenhagen was burned down as a result of these bombardments, including Vor Frue Kirke, the main Danish cathedral. British control of the waterways between Denmark and Norway proved disastrous to the union's economy and in 1813 Denmark–Norway went bankrupt.

The union was dissolved by the [Treaty of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kiel) in 1814; the Danish monarchy "irrevocably and forever" renounced claims to the Kingdom of Norway in favour of the Swedish king.[^cite_note-62] Denmark kept the possessions of [Iceland](14531.md) (which retained the Danish monarchy until 1944), the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) and [Greenland](12118.md), all of which had been governed by Norway for centuries.[^cite_note-63] Apart from the Nordic colonies, Denmark continued to rule over [Danish India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_India) from 1620 to 1869, the [Danish Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Gold_Coast) (Ghana) from 1658 to 1850, and the [Danish West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_West_Indies) from 1671 to 1917.

### Constitutional monarchy (1849–present)



The National Constitutional Assembly was convened by King [Frederick VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_VII_of_Denmark) in 1848 to adopt the [Constitution of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark).

A nascent Danish liberal and national movement gained momentum in the 1830s; after the European [Revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848), Denmark peacefully became a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) on 5 June 1849. A new constitution established a [two-chamber parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigsdagen). Denmark faced war against both [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) and the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) in what became known as the [Second Schleswig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Schleswig_War), lasting from February to October 1864. Denmark was defeated and obliged to [cede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cession) [Schleswig and Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Schleswig-Holstein) to Prussia. This loss came as the latest in the long series of defeats and [territorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Royal_Enclaves) losses that had begun in the 17th century. After these events, Denmark pursued a policy of neutrality in Europe.[^cite_note-64]

[Industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation) came to Denmark in the second half of the 19th century.[^cite_note-65] The [nation's first railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transport_in_Denmark) were constructed in the 1850s, and improved communications and overseas trade allowed industry to develop in spite of Denmark's lack of natural resources. [Trade unions](17626.md) developed, starting in the 1870s. There was a considerable migration of people from the countryside to the cities, and Danish agriculture became centred on the export of dairy and meat products.

Denmark [maintained its neutral stance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_during_World_War_I) during [World War I](4764461.md). After the defeat of Germany, the [Versailles powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) offered to return the region of Schleswig-Holstein to Denmark. Fearing German [irredentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irredentism), Denmark refused to consider the return of the area without a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite); the two [Schleswig Plebiscites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig_Plebiscites) took place on 10 February and 14 March 1920, respectively. On 10 July 1920, Northern Schleswig was recovered by Denmark, thereby adding some 163,600 inhabitants and 3,984 square kilometres (1,538 sq mi). The country's first social democratic government took office in 1924.[^cite_note-Politico-66]

In 1939, Denmark signed a 10-year nonaggression pact with [Nazi Germany](21212.md). However, [Germany invaded Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_\(1940\)) on 9 April 1940, and the Danish government quickly surrendered. Because roughly 10% of the German army's food supply depended on Danish agriculture, the occupation remained relatively cooperative compared to other European nations to ensure continued output. [World War II in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_World_War_II) was characterised by economic co-operation with Germany until 1943, when the Danish government refused further co-operation and [its navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy) [scuttled most of its ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Safari) and sent many of its officers to Sweden, which was neutral. At that point the government fell. The [Danish resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_resistance_movement) performed a [rescue operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_of_the_Danish_Jews) that managed to evacuate several thousand [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Denmark) and their families to safety in Sweden before the Germans could send them to death camps. Some Danes supported [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) by joining the [Danish Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_Workers'_Party_of_Denmark) or volunteering to fight with Germany as part of the [Frikorps Danmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frikorps_Danmark).[^cite_note-67] Iceland severed ties with Denmark and [became an independent republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_the_republic_of_Iceland) in 1944; [Germany surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Denmark) in May 1945. In 1948, the Faroe Islands gained [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule). In 1949, Denmark became a founding member of [NATO](21133.md).



Denmark became a member of the European Union in 1973 and signed the [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Treaty) in 2007.

Denmark was a founding member of the [European Free Trade Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association) (EFTA). During the 1960s, the EFTA countries were often referred to as the [Outer Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Seven), as opposed to the [Inner Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Six) of what was then the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) (EEC).[^cite_note-68] In 1973, along with Britain and Ireland, Denmark joined the European Economic Community (now the [European Union](9317.md)) after a [public referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Danish_European_Communities_membership_referendum). The [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty), which involved further European integration, [was rejected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Danish_Maastricht_Treaty_referendum) by the Danish people in 1992; it was only accepted after a [second referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Danish_Maastricht_Treaty_referendum) in 1993, which provided for [four opt-outs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opt-outs_in_the_European_Union) from policies. The Danes rejected the euro as the national currency in [a referendum in 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Danish_euro_referendum). Greenland gained home rule in 1979 and was awarded [self-determination](29269.md) in 2009. Neither the [Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroe_Islands_and_the_European_Union) nor [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland%E2%80%93European_Union_relations#Outside_the_EU) are members of the European Union, the Faroese having declined membership of the EEC in 1973 and Greenland in 1986, in both cases because of fisheries policies.

Constitutional change in 1953 led to a [single-chamber](204304.md) parliament elected by proportional representation, female accession to the Danish throne, and Greenland becoming an integral part of Denmark. The [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) [Social Democrats](403131.md) led a string of coalition governments for most of the second half of the 20th century, introducing the [Nordic welfare model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_model). The [Liberal Party](403109.md) and the [Conservative People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_People's_Party_\(Denmark\)) have also led [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right) governments.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

Since 2025 the Danish territory of [Greenland](12118.md) has been the target of [US hybrid warfare](82044223.md) under [Donald Trump](4848272.md).[^cite_note-dab-72] [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) said that "the Kingdom of Denmark—and thus Greenland—is a member of NATO and is therefore covered by the Alliance's [collective security guarantee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty#Article_5) \[...] I \[...] strongly urge the United States to cease its threats against a historically close ally."[^cite_note-mefr-73]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Denmark)

See also: [Geography of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Geography of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Greenland)

Satellite image

A satellite image of Jutland and the Danish islands

Located in [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe), north of [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton), Denmark[^cite_note-proper-20] consists of the northern part of the [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) peninsula and an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) of [406 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Denmark). Of these, the largest island is [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand), on which the capital [Copenhagen](5166.md) is situated, followed by the [North Jutlandic Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jutlandic_Island), [Funen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funen), and [Lolland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolland).[^cite_note-75] Some of the larger islands are connected by bridges; a [bridge-tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Bridge) across the [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund) connects Zealand with Sweden;[^cite_note-76] the [Great Belt Fixed Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Belt_Fixed_Link) connects Funen with Zealand;[^cite_note-77] and the [Little Belt Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Belt_Bridge_\(1970\)) connects Jutland with Funen.[^cite_note-78] The four [largest cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Denmark_by_population) are Copenhagen on Zealand; [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus) and [Aalborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg) in Jutland; and [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense) on Funen.[^cite_note-79]

A labelled map of Denmark

A map showing [major urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Denmark_by_population), islands and some of the connecting bridges

&#x20;The metropolitan part occupies a total area of 42,957 [square kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (16,586 sq mi).[^cite_note-area-80] The area of inland water is 600 km<sup>2</sup> (232 sq mi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJensen199910-81] [Post-glacial rebound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-glacial_rebound) raises the land by a bit less than 1 cm (0.4 in) a year in the north and east, extending the coast.[^cite_note-82] It shares a border of 68 kilometres (42 mi) with Germany to the south.[^cite_note-83] Denmark's [waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_Denmark) total 105,000 square kilometres (40,541 square miles).[^cite_note-84]



[Bay of Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Aarhus) viewed from southern [Djursland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djursland)

The highest natural point is [Møllehøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B8lleh%C3%B8j), at 170.89 metres (560.66 ft). [Lammefjorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lammefjorden), around −7 m (−23 ft) (near Audebo), is the lowest point.[^cite_note-85] A sizeable portion of Denmark's [terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain) consists of rolling [plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain).[^cite_note-86]<sup>: 8 </sup> Although once extensively forested, today Denmark largely consists of [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land).[^cite_note-87] It is drained by a [dozen or so streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Denmark), and the most significant include the [Gudenå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guden%C3%A5), [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense_River), [Skjern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skjern_River), [Suså](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sus%C3%A5_River) and [Vidå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vid%C3%A5)—a stream that flows along its southern border with Germany. Lake [Arresø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arres%C3%B8), located northwest of Copenhagen, is the largest lake.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJensen199910-81]

The Kingdom of Denmark includes two overseas territories, both well to the west of Denmark: Greenland, the [world's largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area), and the Faroe Islands in the North Atlantic Ocean. These territories are self-governing under their own parliaments (the [Inatsisartut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inatsisartut) and [Løgting](535570.md)) and form part of the [Danish Realm](11746442.md).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

### Climate

Denmark has a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) climate with a [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Cfa,[^cite_note-90] characterised by cool to cold winters, with mean temperatures in January of 1.1 °C (34.0 °F). It has mild summers, with a mean temperature in July of 16.5 °C (61.7 °F).[^cite_note-Denmark_climate-91] The most extreme temperatures recorded in Denmark since 1874 were 37.0 °C (98.6 °F) in 2026[^cite_note-92] and −31.2 °C (−24.2 °F) in 1982.[^cite_note-93] Denmark has an average of 200 days per year with precipitation, on average receiving a total of 791 millimetres (31 in) per year; autumn is the wettest season and spring the driest.[^cite_note-Denmark_climate-91] The position between a continent and an ocean means that the weather is often unstable.[^cite_note-Denmark_weather_change-94]

Because of Denmark's northern location, there are large seasonal variations in daylight. Days are short during the winter with a sunrise around 8\:45 am and sunset 3\:45 pm (standard time), as well as long summer days with sunrise at 4\:30 am and sunset at 10 pm ([daylight saving time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_saving_time)).[^cite_note-sunrise_sunset-95]

### Ecology

Further information: [List of forests in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_forests_in_Denmark), [List of mammals of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Denmark), and [List of birds of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_Denmark)

See also: [Flora of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_of_the_Faroe_Islands), [Fauna of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_the_Faroe_Islands), and [Flora and fauna of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_and_fauna_of_Greenland)



[Beech trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagus_sylvatica) are common throughout Denmark, especially in the sparse woodlands.

Denmark belongs to the [Boreal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_Kingdom) and can be subdivided into two [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion), the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_mixed_forests) and [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_mixed_forests) mixed forests. Almost all of Denmark's [primeval temperate forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primeval_forest) have been destroyed or fragmented, chiefly for agricultural purposes during the last millennia.[^cite_note-96] The deforestation has created large swaths of [heathland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathland) and devastating [sand drifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltation_\(geology\)).[^cite_note-97] In spite of this, there are several larger [second growth woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_growth_forest) in the country and, in total, 12.9% of the land is now forested.[^cite_note-98] [Norway spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picea_abies) is the most common forest tree.[^cite_note-99]

[Roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer) and the large-antlered [red deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_deer) can be found in Denmark,[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] along with smaller mammals, such as [polecats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_polecat), [hares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare), and [hedgehogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedgehog),[^cite_note-102] and approximately 400 bird species.[^cite_note-103] Large marine mammals include healthy populations of [harbour porpoises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_porpoise),[^cite_note-104] [pinnipeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped),[^cite_note-105] and occasional visits of whales, including [blue whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_whale) and [orcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orca).[^cite_note-106] [Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod), [herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herring) and [plaice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaice) are abundant [culinary fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_as_food) in Danish waters and form the basis for [a large fishing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry_in_Denmark).[^cite_note-107]

### Environment



The [European Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Environment_Agency) in Copenhagen

Denmark historically taken a progressive stance on [environmental preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_preservation), establishing a [Ministry of Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Environment_\(Denmark\)) in 1971,[^cite_note-108] and implementing an [environmental law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_law) in 1973.[^cite_note-109] Air and [water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution) are two of Denmark's most significant [environmental issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issue), although much of its household and industrial waste is now increasingly filtered and sometimes recycled. Denmark is a signatory to the [Climate Change-Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-factbook-110] However, its national [ecological footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_footprint) in 2025 was 7.5 global hectares per person, which is very high compared to a world average of 2.6 in 2024.[^cite_note-111]

Notwithstanding its relatively high emissions, Denmark topped the list of the 2015 [Climate Change Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Change_Performance_Index) due to its implementation of effective climate protection policies.[^cite_note-112] Denmark ranked 10th in the [Environmental Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Index), which measures progress at mitigating [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-113] In 2021, Denmark joined Costa Rica to start the "Beyond Oil and Gas alliance" for stopping the use of fossil fuels.[^cite_note-114] The Danish government will stop issuing new licences for oil and gas extraction in 2050.[^cite_note-115]

## Government services and politics

Main article: [Politics of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Denmark)

See also: [Politics of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Politics of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Greenland)



[Frederik X](339155.md)\
[King](20714373.md)&#x20;



[Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md)\
[Prime Minister](273720.md)&#x20;

Politics in Denmark operate under a framework laid out in the [Constitution of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-117] First written in 1849, it establishes a sovereign state in the form of a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy), with a representative [unicameral](204304.md) [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system). The [monarch](20714373.md) officially retains [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) and presides over the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Council_of_State) ([privy council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_council)).[^cite_note-\:0-118] In practice, the duties of the monarch are strictly representative and [ceremonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial),[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] such as the formal appointment and dismissal of the [Prime Minister](273720.md) and other Government ministers. The monarch is not answerable for their actions, and their [person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_personality) is [sacrosanct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrosanct).[^cite_note-\:0-118] [Hereditary monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_monarchy) King [Frederik X](339155.md) has been head of state since 14 January 2024.[^cite_note-121]

The [Danish political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Denmark), which emphasises broad [consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making), is used by American [political scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) [Francis Fukuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Fukuyama) as a reference point for near-perfect [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance); his phrase "getting to Denmark" refers to the country's status as a global model for stable social and political institutions.[^cite_note-122]

### Government

Main articles: [Folketing](429743.md) and [Cabinet of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Denmark)

See also: [Løgting](535570.md), [Cabinet of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Faroe_Islands), [Inatsisartut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inatsisartut), and [Cabinet of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Greenland)



Denmark disposable income after tax, not including [Value-added tax](52177473.md) or [Property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax)

The Danish parliament is [unicameral](204304.md) and called the Folketing ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Folketinget*). It is the [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of the Kingdom of Denmark, passing [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament) that apply in Denmark and, variably, Greenland and the Faroe Islands. The Folketing is also responsible for adopting the [state's budgets](4360909.md), approving the state's accounts, appointing and exercising control of the government, and taking part in international co-operation. [Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(proposed_law\)) may be initiated by the government or by [members of parliament](98066.md). All bills passed must be presented before the Council of State to receive [Royal Assent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Assent) within thirty days in order to become law.[^cite_note-\:0-118]



[Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace) houses the Folketing, the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Denmark), and government offices.

Denmark is a [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) with [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage).[^cite_note-124] Membership of the Folketing is based on [proportional representation](23298.md) of political parties,[^cite_note-\:0-118] with a 2% electoral threshold. Denmark elects 175 members to the Folketing, with Greenland and the Faroe Islands electing an additional two members each—179 members in total.[^cite_note-125] Parliamentary elections are held at least every four years, but it is within the powers of the prime minister to ask the monarch to call for an election before the term has elapsed. In a [vote of no confidence](208101.md), the Folketing may force a single minister or an entire government to resign.[^cite_note-\:0-118]

The Government of Denmark operates as a [cabinet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)), where executive authority is exercised—formally, on behalf of the monarch—by the prime minister and other [cabinet ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_minister), who head [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Danish_government_ministries). As the executive branch, the Cabinet is responsible for proposing bills and a budget, executing the laws, and guiding the foreign and internal policies of Denmark. The monarch usually, but not necessarily, requests the person most likely to command the [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) majority in the Folketing to become prime minister; this is often the current leader of the largest [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) or, more effectively, through a [coalition of parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance). A single party generally does not have sufficient political power in terms of the number of seats to form a cabinet on its own; Denmark has often been ruled by [coalition governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_governments), themselves usually [minority governments](271434.md) dependent on non-government parties.[^cite_note-126]

Following the [2026 Danish general election](72208286.md) in March 2026, incumbent prime minister and [Social Democratic leader](403131.md) [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) in June 2026 formed the current [Frederiksen III Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_III_Cabinet), a coalition government with [Green Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Left_\(Denmark\)), [Moderate party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderates_\(Denmark\)), and [Social Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Social_Liberal_Party).

### Law and judicial system

Main articles: [Law of Denmark](6067786.md) and [Courts of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Denmark)

See also: [Crime in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Denmark) and [Judiciary of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Greenland)



"With law shall land be built", preamble to the [Code of Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Holmiensis) above [Copenhagen Court House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Court_House). In 2023, the [World Justice Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Justice_Project) ranked Denmark no. 1 on their rule of law index.

[^cite_note-127]

Denmark has a [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) system with some references to [Germanic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_law). Denmark resembles Norway and Sweden in never having developed a [case-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-law) like that of [England](85441.md) and the United States nor comprehensive [codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_\(law\)) like those of France and Germany. Much of its law is [customary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custom_\(law\)).[^cite_note-128]

The judicial system of Denmark is divided between courts with regular civil and [criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law) jurisdiction and administrative courts with jurisdiction over litigation between individuals and the public administration. Articles 62 and 64 of the Constitution (*grundloven*) ensure [judicial independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_independence) from government and Parliament by providing that judges shall only be guided by the law, including acts, statutes and practice.[^cite_note-\:0-118] The Kingdom of Denmark does not have a single unified judicial system – Denmark has one system, Greenland another, and the Faroe Islands a third.[^cite_note-129] However, decisions by the highest courts in Greenland and the Faroe Islands may be appealed to the Danish High Courts. The [Danish Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Denmark) is the highest civil and criminal court responsible for the administration of justice in the Kingdom.

### Danish Realm

Main article: [Danish Realm](11746442.md)



The village of [Kunoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunoy_\(village\)) on [Kunoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunoy) island, in the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md). [Kalsoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalsoy) island is at right

The Kingdom of Denmark is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) that comprises, in addition to metropolitan Denmark, two [autonomous territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division)[^cite_note-territory-19] in the [North Atlantic Ocean](698.md): the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) and [Greenland](12118.md). They have been integrated parts of the Danish Realm since the 18th century; however, due to their separate historical and cultural identities, these parts of the Realm have extensive political powers and have assumed [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) and administrative responsibility in a substantial number of fields.[^cite_note-130] [Home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) was granted to the Faroe Islands in 1948 and to Greenland in 1979, each having previously had the status of [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-stm-131]

The Faroe Islands and Greenland have their own home governments and parliaments and are effectively [self-governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) in regards to domestic affairs apart from the judicial system and monetary policy. [High Commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner) (*_Rigsombudsmand_*) act as representatives of the Danish government in the Faroese [Løgting](535570.md) and in the [Greenlandic Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Greenland), but they cannot vote.[^cite_note-stm-131] The Faroese home government is defined to be an equal partner with the Danish national government,[^cite_note-132] while the [Greenlandic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_Inuit) are defined as a separate people with the right to [self-determination](29269.md).[^cite_note-133]

### Administrative divisions

Denmark, with a total area of 43,094 [square kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (16,639 sq mi), is divided into five administrative regions ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *regioner*). The regions are further subdivided into [98 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Denmark) (*_kommuner_*). The easternmost land in Denmark, the [Ertholmene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ertholmene) archipelago, with an area of 39 hectares (0.16 sq mi), is neither part of a municipality nor a region but belongs to the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Denmark\)).[^cite_note-136] The [provinces of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Denmark) are statistical divisions of Denmark, positioned between the [administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Denmark) and [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Denmark). They are not administrative divisions, nor subject for any kind of political elections, but are mainly for statistical use.

The regions were created on [1 January 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Danish_local_elections) to replace the 16 [former counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Denmark). At the same time, smaller municipalities were merged into larger units, reducing the number from 271 to 98.[^cite_note-nuts-137] Most municipalities have a population of at least 20,000 to give them financial and professional sustainability, although a few exceptions were made to this rule.[^cite_note-138] The administrative divisions are led by directly elected councils, elected proportionally every four years; the most recent [Danish local elections](74940717.md) were held on 18 November 2025. Other regional structures use the municipal boundaries as a layout, including the [police districts](14536794.md), the [court districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Denmark) and the [electoral wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_\(country_subdivision\)).

#### Regions

The governing bodies of the regions are the [regional councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government), each with forty-one councillors elected for four-year terms. The councils are headed by regional district chairmen (*_regionsrådsformand_*), who are elected by the council. The areas of responsibility for the regional councils are the [national health service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_Denmark), [social services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_services) and [regional development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_development).[^cite_note-brief-139][^cite_note-regioner-140] Unlike the counties they replaced, the regions are not allowed to levy taxes and the health service is partly financed by a national health care contribution until 2018 (*_sundhedsbidrag_*), partly by funds from both government and municipalities.[^cite_note-taxation1-141] On 1 January 2019 this contribution was abolished and replaced by higher income tax instead.

The [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area) and populations of the regions vary widely; for example, the [Capital Region](2521189.md) has a population three times larger than that of [North Denmark Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Denmark_Region). Under the county system certain densely populated municipalities, such as [Copenhagen Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Municipality) and [Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Municipality), had been given a status equivalent to that of counties, making them first-level administrative divisions. These *[sui generis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_generis)* municipalities were incorporated into the new regions under the 2007 reforms.

On 1 January 2027, the Capital Region and [Region Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_Zealand) will be merged to form the [Region of Eastern Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_Eastern_Denmark).[^cite_note-about-142]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Denmark)

See also: [Foreign relations of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Foreign relations of Greenland](12127.md)



Danish prime minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) (second from left) with foreign counterparts at the [Nordic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council) in Copenhagen, 2021

Denmark wields considerable influence in Northern Europe and is a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power) in international affairs.[^cite_note-143] In recent years, Greenland and the Faroe Islands have been guaranteed a say in foreign policy issues such as fishing, [whaling](33557.md), and geopolitical concerns. The foreign policy of Denmark is substantially influenced by [its membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_and_the_European_Union) of the [European Union](9317.md) (EU); Denmark including Greenland joined the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) (EEC), the EU's predecessor, in 1973.[^cite_note-144] Denmark held the [Presidency of the Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union) on seven occasions, most recently from January to June 2012.[^cite_note-145] Following World War II, Denmark ended its two-hundred-year-long policy of [neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_neutrality_\(international_relations\)). It has been a founding member of the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](21133.md) (NATO) since 1949, and membership remains highly popular.[^cite_note-146]

As a member of [Development Assistance Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Assistance_Committee) (DAC), Denmark has for a long time been among the countries of the world contributing the largest percentage of gross national income to [development aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid). In 2015, Denmark contributed 0.85% of its [gross national income](1242956.md) (GNI) to foreign aid and was one of only six countries meeting the longstanding UN target of 0.7% of GNI.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] The country participates in both bilateral and multilateral aid, with the aid usually administered by the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Denmark\)). The organisational name of [Danish International Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_International_Development_Agency) (DANIDA) is often used, in particular when operating bilateral aid. According to the 2024 [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index), Denmark is the 8th most peaceful country in the world.[^cite_note-149]

### Military

See also: [Danish Defence](8038.md) and [Military history of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Denmark)



Danish Military Police conducting advanced law-enforcement training

Denmark's [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces) are known as the [Danish Defence](8038.md) ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Forsvaret*). The Minister of Defence is [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the Danish Defence, and serves as chief [diplomatic](28165911.md) official abroad. During peacetime, the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Denmark\)) employs around 33,000 people in total. The main military branches employ almost 27,000: 15,460 in the [Royal Danish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Army), 5,300 in the [Royal Danish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy) and 6,050 in the [Royal Danish Air Force](548819.md) (all including conscripts). The [Danish Emergency Management Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Emergency_Management_Agency) employs 2,000 (including conscripts), and about 4,000 are in non-branch-specific services like the [Danish Defence Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Command_\(Denmark\)) and the [Danish Defence Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Defence_Intelligence_Service). Furthermore, around 44,500 serve as volunteers in the [Danish Home Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Guard_\(Denmark\)).[^cite_note-150]

Denmark is a long-time supporter of international [peacekeeping](84617.md), but since the [NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](322473.md) in 1999 and the [War in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) in 2001, Denmark has also found a new role as a warring nation, participating actively in several wars and invasions. This relatively new situation has stirred some internal critique, but the Danish population has generally been very supportive, in particular of the War in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] The Danish Defence has around 1,400[^cite_note-153] staff in international missions, not including standing contributions to [NATO SNMCMG1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_NRF_Mine_Countermeasures_Group_1). Danish forces were heavily engaged in the former Yugoslavia in the UN Protection Force ([UNPROFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNPROFOR)), with [IFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFOR),[^cite_note-154] and now [SFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFOR).[^cite_note-155] Between 2003 and 2007, there were approximately 450 Danish soldiers in Iraq.[^cite_note-156] Denmark also strongly supported [American operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in Afghanistan and has contributed both monetarily and materially to the [ISAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force).[^cite_note-157] These initiatives are often described by the authorities as part of a new "active foreign policy" of Denmark.

### Postal service

See also: [Tusass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusass) and [Post Danmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Danmark)

Denmark's postal service was known as [Post Danmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Danmark). It merged with Sweden's postal service organisation to form [PostNord Danmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostNord_Danmark). After 31 December 2025, PostNord Danmark ceased all delivery of paper letter envelopes to encourage the use of [electronic mailing](9738.md) in Denmark, reduce the costs of recycling products derived from trees in Denmark, focus mostly on delivering parcels, and make its delivery service more efficient throughout the country of Denmark.[^cite_note-158] Since June 2025, approximately 1,500 mailboxes have been eliminated in Denmark. It is the first country in the world to end the delivery of paper letter envelopes within the national postal system. Greenland's postal delivery system is separate from Denmark's.[^cite_note-159] A third of employees working for PostNord Danmark or 1,500 workers will be unemployed in 2026.[^cite_note-160]

## Economy

Further information: [Economy of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Denmark), [List of companies of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_of_Denmark), and [List of largest Danish companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_Danish_companies)

See also: [Economy of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Economy of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Greenland)



[Lego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lego_Group), world's largest toy company by revenue, headquartered in [Billund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billund,_Denmark)

Denmark has a [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_economy) [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy) that is classed as a [high-income economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_high-income_economy) by the [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-161] In 2022, it ranked 8th in the world in terms of [gross national income (PPP) per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GNI_\(PPP\)_per_capita) and 10th in [nominal GNI per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GNI_\(nominal,_Atlas_method\)_per_capita).[^cite_note-wb-162] Denmark's economy stands out as one of the most free in the [Index of Economic Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_Economic_Freedom) and the [Economic Freedom of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Freedom_of_the_World).[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-2011-09-20_fraserinstitute-164] It is the 10th most competitive economy in the world, and 7th in Europe and North America, according to the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) in its *Global Competitiveness Report 2019*.[^cite_note-wefcomp-165]

Denmark has the fourth highest ratio of [tertiary degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) holders in the world.[^cite_note-166] The country ranks highest in the world for [workers' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_rights).[^cite_note-167] GDP per hour worked was the 16th highest in 2022. The country has a market income inequality close to the [OECD](33853117.md) average,[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] but after taxes and public cash transfers the income inequality is [considerably lower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_income_equality). According to [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat), Denmark's [Gini coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gini_coefficient) for disposable income was the 7th-lowest among EU countries in 2017.[^cite_note-eurostat-170]

According to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), Denmark has [the world's highest minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minimum_wages_by_country).[^cite_note-171] As Denmark has no minimum wage legislation, the high wage floor has been attributed to the power of [trade unions](17626.md). For example, as the result of a collective bargaining agreement between the [3F trade union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagligt_F%C3%A6lles_Forbund) and the employers group [Horesta](https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horesta), workers at [McDonald's](2480627.md) and other [fast food chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food_chain) make the equivalent of [US\$](18717338.md)20 an hour, which is more than double what their counterparts earn in the United States, and have access to paid vacation, [parental leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_leave) and a pension plan.[^cite_note-172] Union density in 2015 was 68%.[^cite_note-173]

Once a predominantly [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) country on account of its [arable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land) landscape, since 1945 Denmark has greatly expanded its [industrial base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_base) and [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector). By 2017 services contributed circa 75% of GDP, manufacturing about 15% and agriculture less than 2%.[^cite_note-174] Major industries include [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine), [pharmaceuticals](560876.md), [medical equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_equipment), [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery) and transportation equipment, [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), and [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction). Circa 60% of the total export value is due to export of goods, and the remaining 40% is from service exports, mainly sea transport. The country's main export goods are: wind turbines, pharmaceuticals, machinery and instruments, meat and meat products, dairy products, fish, furniture and design.[^cite_note-factbook2-175] Denmark is a net exporter of food and energy and has for a number of years had a [balance of payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_payments) surplus which has transformed the country from a net debitor to a net creditor country. By 1 July 2018, the [net international investment position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_international_investment_position) (or [net foreign assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_foreign_assets)) of Denmark was equal to 64.6% of GDP.[^cite_note-176]



Denmark is a major producer and exporter of [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork) products.

Denmark is part of the [European Union](9317.md)'s [internal market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market), which represents more than 508 million consumers. Several domestic commercial policies are determined by agreements among European Union (EU) members and by EU legislation. Support for [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade) is high among the Danish public; in a 2016 poll 57% of respondents saw globalisation as an opportunity whereas 18% viewed it as a threat.[^cite_note-177] 70% of trade flows are inside the European Union. As of 2017, Denmark's largest export partners are Germany, Sweden, the United Kingdom, and the United States.[^cite_note-factbook-110]

Denmark's currency, the *[krone](236323.md)* (DKK), is [pegged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_exchange_rate) at approximately 7.46 kroner per euro through the [ERM II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Exchange_Rate_Mechanism). Although a [September 2000 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Danish_euro_referendum) rejected adopting the [euro](9472.md),[^cite_note-denmarkandtheeuro-178] the country follows the policies set forth in the [Economic and Monetary Union of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Monetary_Union_of_the_European_Union) (EMU) and meets the economic [convergence criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_convergence_criteria) needed to adopt the euro. The majority of the political parties in the Folketing support joining the EMU, but since 2010 opinion polls have consistently shown a clear majority against adopting the euro. In March 2018, 29% of respondents from Denmark in a [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) opinion poll stated that they were in favour of the EMU and the euro, whereas 65% were against it.[^cite_note-179] The exact same poll conducted in November 2023, was almost unchanged with 31% in favour and 63% against.[^cite_note-180]

Ranked by turnover in Denmark, the largest Danish companies are: [A.P. Møller-Mærsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.P._M%C3%B8ller-M%C3%A6rsk) (international shipping), [Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_\(company\)) (pharmaceuticals), [ISS A/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_A/S) (facility services), [Vestas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestas) ([wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine)), [Arla Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arla_Foods) (dairy), [DSV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSV_\(company\)) (transport), [Carlsberg Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Group) (beer), [Salling Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salling_Group) (retail), [Ørsted A/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98rsted_\(company\)) (power), [Danske Bank](738035.md).[^cite_note-181]

The Danish government focused into methods to increase [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxes) on [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) dealers in 2023.[^cite_note-182]

### Public policy

See also: [Flexicurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexicurity), [Taxation in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Denmark), [Taxation in the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_the_Faroe_Islands), and [Taxation in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Greenland)

Danes enjoy a high standard of living and the Danish economy is characterised by extensive government [welfare provisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state). Denmark has a [corporate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax) rate of 22% and a special time-limited tax regime for expatriates.[^cite_note-183] The Danish taxation system is broad based, with a 25% [value-added tax](52177473.md), in addition to excise taxes, income taxes and other fees. The overall level of taxation (sum of all taxes, as a percentage of GDP) was 46% in 2017.[^cite_note-184] The tax structure of Denmark (the relative weight of different taxes) differs from the OECD average, as the Danish tax system in 2015 was characterised by substantially higher revenues from taxes on personal income and a lower proportion of revenues from taxes on corporate income and gains and property taxes than in OECD generally, whereas no revenues at all derive from social security contributions. The proportion deriving from payroll taxes, VAT, and other taxes on goods and services correspond to the OECD average.[^cite_note-185]

As of 2022, 6.5% of the population was reported to live below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), when adjusted for taxes and transfers. Denmark had the 2nd lowest relative poverty rate in the [OECD](33853117.md) after Czech Republic, below the 11.4% OECD average. The 6% of the population reporting that they could not afford to buy sufficient food was less than half of the OECD average.[^cite_note-OECD1-186]

### Labour market

Like other Nordic countries, Denmark has adopted the [Nordic Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Model), which combines [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) capitalism with a comprehensive [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) and strong [worker protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worker_protection).[^cite_note-Nordic_Model-187] As a result of its acclaimed "[flexicurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexicurity)" model, Denmark has the freest [labour market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_economics) in Europe, according to the World Bank. Employers can hire and fire whenever they want (flexibility), and between jobs, [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) compensation is relatively high (security). According to OECD, initial as well as long-term net replacement rates for unemployed persons were 65% of previous net income in 2016, against an OECD average of 53%.[^cite_note-188] No restrictions apply regarding overtime work, which allows companies to operate 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.[^cite_note-189] With an employment rate in 2017 of 74.2% for people aged 15–64-years, Denmark ranks 9th highest among the OECD countries, and above the OECD average of 67.8%.[^cite_note-190] The unemployment rate was 5.7% in 2017,[^cite_note-191] which is considered close to or below its structural level.[^cite_note-192]

Danish workers work on average 1,394 hours per year, which is 22% lower than the OECD average of 1,746 hours.[^cite_note-193] The job satisfaction is high in Denmark. Among Danes, 94% are satisfied with their work conditions.[^cite_note-194] Danish work-life balance is generally considered to be among the best in the world and also a key contributor to Denmark's consistently high rank in the World Happiness Index.

The level of [unemployment benefits](378430.md) is dependent on former employment and normally on membership of an unemployment fund, which is usually closely connected to a trade union, and previous payment of contributions. Circa 65% of the financing comes from earmarked member contributions, whereas the remaining third originates from the central government and hence from general taxation.[^cite_note-195]

### Business

Establishing a business in Denmark can be undertaken in a matter of hours and at very low costs.[^cite_note-196] The Danish government operates a "Danish Business Authority", and launched a series of initiatives in 2012 aiming to simplify business rules, making it easier to run a business without jeopardising the intended goals of relevant legislation.[^cite_note-197]

### Science and technology

See also: [Internet in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_Denmark)



With an investment of 8.5 million euros over the ten-year construction period, Denmark confirms participation in [E-ELT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-ELT).

[^cite_note-198]

Denmark has a long tradition of scientific and technological invention and engagement, and has been involved internationally from the start of the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution). In current times, Denmark is participating in many high-profile international science and technology projects, including [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN), [ITER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITER), [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA), [ISS](15043.md) and [E-ELT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-ELT). Denmark was ranked 9th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

In the 20th century, Danes have also been innovative in several fields of the technology sector. Danish companies have been influential in the shipping industry with the design of the largest and most energy efficient container ships in the world, the [Maersk Triple E class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maersk_Triple_E_class), and Danish engineers have contributed to the design of [MAN Diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Diesel) engines. In the software and electronic field, Denmark contributed to design and manufacturing of [Nordic Mobile Telephones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Mobile_Telephone), and the now-defunct Danish company [DanCall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DanCall?action=edit\&redlink=1) was among the first to develop [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) mobile phones.

[Life science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_science) is a key sector with extensive research and development activities. Danish engineers are world-leading in providing [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes) care equipment and medication products from Novo and, since 2000, the Danish [biotech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotech) company [Novozymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novozymes), the world market leader in enzymes for first generation starch-based [bioethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethanol), has pioneered development of enzymes for converting waste to [cellulosic ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulosic_ethanol).[^cite_note-201] *[Medicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicon_Valley)*, spanning the [Øresund Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Region) between Zealand and Sweden, is one of Europe's largest life science [clusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cluster).

Danish-born computer scientists and software engineers have taken leading roles in some of the world's programming languages: [Anders Hejlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Hejlsberg) ([Turbo Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo_Pascal), [Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi_\(programming_language\)), [C#](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_Sharp_\(programming_language\))); [Rasmus Lerdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Lerdorf) ([PHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHP)); [Bjarne Stroustrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjarne_Stroustrup) ([C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++)); [David Heinemeier Hansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Heinemeier_Hansson) ([Ruby on Rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_on_Rails)); [Lars Bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Bak_\(computer_programmer\)), a pioneer in virtual machines ([V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_\(JavaScript_engine\)), [Java VM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_virtual_machine), [Dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_\(programming_language\))). Physicist [Lene Vestergaard Hau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lene_Vestergaard_Hau) is the first person to stop light, leading to advances in [quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing), [nanoscale engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoscale_engineering), and [linear optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_optics).

### Energy

Main article: [Energy in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Denmark)

See also: [Energy in the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Energy in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Greenland)



[Middelgrunden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelgrunden_wind_farm), an offshore wind farm near Copenhagen

Denmark has considerably large deposits of oil and natural gas in the [North Sea](21179.md) and ranks as number 32 in the world among net exporters of [crude oil](23195.md)[^cite_note-202] and was producing 259,980 barrels of crude oil a day in 2009.[^cite_note-203] Denmark is a long-time leader in [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power): In 2015 [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) provided 42.1% of the total electricity consumption.[^cite_note-204] In May 2011 Denmark derived 3.1% of its gross domestic product from renewable (clean) energy technology and energy efficiency, or around €6.5 billion (\$9.4 billion).[^cite_note-205] Denmark is connected by [electric transmission lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_transmission_lines) to other European countries.

[Denmark's electricity sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_Denmark) has integrated energy sources such as wind power into the national grid. Denmark now aims to focus on intelligent battery systems ([V2G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle-to-grid)) and [plug-in vehicles](279350.md) in the transport sector.[^cite_note-206] The country is a member nation of the [International Renewable Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Renewable_Energy_Agency) (IRENA).[^cite_note-IRENAstates-207]

### Transport

Main article: [Transport in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Denmark)

See also: [Transport in the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Transport in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Greenland)



Denmark railway network



[Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) is the largest airport in Scandinavia and the [15th-busiest in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe).

[^cite_note-cph-209]

Significant investment has been made in building road and rail links between regions in Denmark, most notably the [Great Belt Fixed Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Belt_Fixed_Link), which connects [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand) and [Funen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funen). It is now possible to drive from [Frederikshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederikshavn) in northern [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) to [Copenhagen](5166.md) on eastern Zealand without leaving the motorway. The main railway operator is [DSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSB_\(railway_company\)) for passenger services and [DB Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Cargo) for freight trains. The railway tracks are maintained by [Banedanmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banedanmark). The North Sea and the Baltic Sea are intertwined by various, international ferry links. Construction of the [Fehmarn Belt Fixed Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fehmarn_Belt_Fixed_Link), connecting Denmark and Germany with a second link, Started in 2021.[^cite_note-210] Copenhagen has a [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system, the [Copenhagen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Metro), and an extensive electrified suburban railway network, the [S-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-train). In the four largest cities – Copenhagen, [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus), [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense), [Aalborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg) – [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) systems are planned to be in operation around 2020.[^cite_note-211]

[Cycling in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Denmark) is a very common form of transport, particularly for the young and for city dwellers. With a network of bicycle routes extending more than 12,000 km[^cite_note-212] and an estimated 7,000 km[^cite_note-vcta-213] of [segregated dedicated bicycle paths and lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregated_cycle_facilities), Denmark has a solid [bicycle infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_infrastructure).

Private vehicles are increasingly used as a means of transport. Because of the [high registration tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_taxation) (150%), [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT) (25%), and one of the world's highest income tax rates, new cars are very expensive. The purpose of the tax is to discourage car ownership. In 2007, an attempt was made by the government to favour environmentally friendly cars by slightly reducing taxes on high mileage vehicles. However, this has had little effect, and in 2008 Denmark experienced an increase in the import of fuel inefficient old cars,[^cite_note-214] as the cost for older cars—including taxes—keeps them within the budget of many Danes. As of 2011, the average car age is 9.2 years.[^cite_note-215]

With Norway and Sweden, Denmark is part of the [Scandinavian Airlines](162868.md) [flag carrier](465816.md). [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) is Scandinavia's busiest passenger airport, handling almost 30 million passengers in 2024.[^cite_note-cph-209] Other notable airports are [Billund Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billund_Airport), [Aalborg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_Airport), and [Aarhus Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_Airport).

### Population

In May 2025, the population of Denmark, as registered by [Statistics Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Denmark), was 6 million.[^cite_note-pop1-216] Denmark has one of the oldest populations in the world, with the [average age of 42.2 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_pyramid),[^cite_note-217] with 0.99 males per female. Despite a low [birth rate](515898.md) of 11.3 births per 1000 population, the population is growing at an average annual rate of 0.44%[^cite_note-factbook2-175] because of net immigration and increasing [longevity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity). The [World Happiness Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Happiness_Report) frequently ranks Denmark's population as the happiest in the world.[^cite_note-happiness-218][^cite_note-Earth_Institute_2013-219][^cite_note-BruceStokes-220] This has been attributed to the country's highly regarded education and [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_Denmark) systems,[^cite_note-Taylor-221] and its low level of [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality).[^cite_note-eurogini-222] People in Denmark feel responsible for [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare). The rate of taxation is among the world's highest and can be half a Dane's income but they get most healthcare free, university tuition is also free and students get grants, there is subsidised [child care](389615.md) and [old people get pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensions_in_Denmark) and care helpers.[^cite_note-223]

Denmark is a historically [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_\(statistics\)) nation.[^cite_note-Thomas2016-224] However, as with its Scandinavian neighbours, Denmark has recently transformed from a nation of [net emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_migration), up until World War II, to a nation of net immigration. Today, [residence permits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_permit) are issued mostly to immigrants from other EU countries (54% of all non-Scandinavian immigrants in 2017). Another 31% of residence permits were study- or work-related, 4% were issued to [asylum seekers](276005.md) and 10% to persons who arrive as family dependants.[^cite_note-225] Overall, the net migration rate in 2017 was 2.1 migrant(s)/1,000 population, somewhat lower than the United Kingdom and the other Nordic countries.[^cite_note-factbook2-175][^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227]

There are no official statistics on [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group), but according to 2020 figures from Statistics Denmark, 86.1% of the population in Denmark was of [Danish](419786.md) descent (including *[Faroese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_Dane)* and *[Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_people_in_Denmark)*), defined as having at least one parent who was born in the [Kingdom of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realm_of_Denmark) and holds [Danish nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_nationality_law).[^cite_note-pop1-216][^cite_note-denonly_group=N-11] The remaining 13.89% were of foreign background, defined as immigrants or descendants of recent immigrants. With the same definition, the most common countries of origin were [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Turkey), Poland, [Syria](8862873.md), Germany, [Iraq](4184636.md), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians), Lebanon, Pakistan, [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), and [Somalia](1571696.md).[^cite_note-pop1-216] Minorities in Denmark include [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_\(people\)), Syrians, Germans, Iraqis, Romanians and people from former [Yugoslavia](34244.md). There are also other Asian and African populations in the country. Small numbers of [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) and [Hungarians](17106358.md) live in Denmark. There is also a small [Jewish](25955086.md) population.[^cite_note-228]

The [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_Inuit) are Indigenous to Greenland in the Kingdom and have traditionally inhabited Greenland and the northern parts of Canada and Alaska in the [Arctic](36971.md). From the 18th century up to the 1970s, the Danish government (Dano-Norwegian until 1814) tried to assimilate the Greenlandic Inuit, encouraging them to adopt the majority language and culture. Because of this "Danization process", some persons of Inuit ancestry now identify their mother tongue as Danish.

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Denmark)

See also: [Languages of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Languages of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Greenland)

Danish is the *de facto* [national language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language) of Denmark. [Faroese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_language) and [Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_language) are the official languages of the Faroe Islands and Greenland respectively. German is a recognised [minority language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_language) in the area of the former [South Jutland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jutland_County) (now part of the [Region of Southern Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_Southern_Denmark)), which was part of the German Empire prior to the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles).[^cite_note-Ethnologue-229] Danish and Faroese belong to the [North Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_languages) (Nordic) branch of the [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages), along with [Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_language), [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language), and Swedish. There is some degree of [mutual intelligibility between Danish, Norwegian, and Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_languages#Mutual_intelligibility). Danish is more distantly related to German, which is a [West Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germanic_languages) language. Greenlandic or "Kalaallisut" is an [Inuit language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit_languages), and is entirely unrelated to Danish,[^cite_note-language-230] although it has adopted many Danish [loanwords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanword) including the words for numbers.

A large majority of Danes (87%) speak English at a conversational level. German is the second most widely spoken foreign language, with 49% reporting conversational proficiency.[^cite_note-EU-Lang-231] Denmark had 25,900 [native speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of German in 2007 (mostly in the South Jutland area).[^cite_note-Ethnologue-229]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Denmark)

See also: [Religion in the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Religion in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Greenland)



[Roskilde Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_Cathedral) has been the [burial](177497.md) place of Danish royalty since the 15th century. In 1995 it became a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).

Religion in Denmark ([The World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook) 2024)

[^cite_note-auto-7]

1. [Lutheranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Christianity_in_Denmark)  (71.4%)

2. [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Denmark)  (4.30%)

3. [Other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Denmark) / [none](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) (24.3%)

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Denmark) is the dominant religion in Denmark. As of 2026, 70%[^cite_note-232] of the population of Denmark were members of the [Church of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Denmark) (*Den Danske Folkekirke*), the [officially established](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion) church, which is Protestant in classification and [Lutheran](23371382.md) in orientation.[^cite_note-\:0-118][^cite_note-233] The membership percentage has been in steady decline since the 1970s, mainly as fewer newborns are being [baptised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) into it.[^cite_note-234] Only 3% of the population regularly attend Sunday services[^cite_note-report_2009-235][^cite_note-236] and only 19% of Danes consider religion to be an important part of their life.[^cite_note-gallup-religiosity-poor-nations-237]

The [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark) states that the sovereign be [Lutheran](23371382.md), though the rest of the population is [free to adhere to other faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_pluralism).[^cite_note-\:0-118][^cite_note-FoR-238] In 1682 the state granted limited recognition to three religious groups dissenting from the Established Church: [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Denmark), [the Reformed Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Synod_of_Denmark) and [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Denmark), although conversion to these groups from the Church of Denmark remained illegal initially. Until the 1970s, the state formally recognised "religious societies" by [royal decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_decree). Today, religious groups do not need official government recognition; they can be granted the right to perform weddings and other ceremonies without this recognition.[^cite_note-FoR-238] [Denmark's Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Denmark) make up approximately 4.4% of the population[^cite_note-jac2020-239] and form the country's second largest religious community and largest [minority religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_religion).[^cite_note-jac2018-240] The [Danish Foreign Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Denmark\)) estimates that other [religious groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_denomination) comprise less than 1% of the population individually and approximately 2% when taken all together.[^cite_note-241] Just under 20% of the Danish population identifies as [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism).[^cite_note-242]

According to a 2010 [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) poll,[^cite_note-243] 28% of Danish nationals polled responded that they "believe there is a [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God)", 47% responded that they "believe there is some sort of [spirit or life force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_\(animating_force\))" and 24% responded that they "do not believe there is any sort of spirit, God or life force". Another poll, carried out in 2009, found that 25% of Danes believe [Jesus](1095706.md) is the [son of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Son), and 18% believe he is the [saviour of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redeemer_\(Christianity\)).[^cite_note-244]

In its 2024 [Freedom in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World) report, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) rated the country 4 out of 4 for [religious freedom](46006.md).[^cite_note-FreedomintheWorld2024-245]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Denmark)

See also: [Education in the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Education in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Greenland)



The oldest surviving Danish lecture plan dated 1537 from the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen)

All educational programmes in Denmark are regulated by the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_Minister_of_Denmark) and administered by local municipalities. *[Folkeskole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Folkeskole_Education)* covers the entire period of compulsory education, encompassing [primary](25058.md) and lower [secondary education](554992.md).[^cite_note-edu-246] Most children attend *folkeskole* for 10 years, from the ages of 6 to 16. There are no final examinations, but pupils can choose to sit an exam when finishing [ninth grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_grade) (14–15 years old). The test is obligatory if further education is to be attended. Alternatively pupils can attend an [independent school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school) (*friskole*), or a private school (*privatskole*), such as [Christian schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_school) or [Waldorf schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldorf_education).

Following graduation from compulsory education, there are several continuing educational opportunities; the [Gymnasium (STX)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_\(Denmark\)) attaches importance in teaching a mix of humanities and science, [Higher Technical Examination Programme (HTX)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Technical_Examination_Programme_\(HTX\)) focuses on scientific subjects and the [Higher Commercial Examination Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Commercial_Examination_Programme?action=edit\&redlink=1) emphasises on subjects in economics. [Higher Preparatory Examination (HF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Preparatory_Examination_\(HF\)) is similar to *Gymnasium (STX)*, but is one year shorter. For specific professions, there is [vocational education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_secondary_education_in_Denmark), training young people for work in specific [trades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_\(occupation\)) by a combination of teaching and [apprenticeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apprenticeship).

The government records upper secondary school completion rates of 83% and [tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) enrolment and completion rates of 45% in 2018.[^cite_note-247] All [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) and college (tertiary) education in Denmark is free of charges; there are no tuition fees to enrol in courses. Students aged 18 or above may apply for state educational support grants, known as *[Statens Uddannelsesstøtte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_loans_in_Denmark) (SU)*, which provides fixed financial support, disbursed monthly.[^cite_note-248] Danish universities offer international students a range of opportunities for obtaining an internationally recognised qualification in Denmark. Many programmes may be taught in the English language, the academic [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca), in [bachelor's degrees](188874.md), [master's degrees](170346.md), [doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) and [student exchange programmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_exchange_program).[^cite_note-249]

### Health

See also: [Health care in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_Denmark)



[Rigshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigshospitalet) in Copenhagen, the most specialised hospital in Denmark, receiving over 350,000 unique patients a year

[^cite_note-250]

As of 2022, Denmark has a [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) of 81.3 years at birth (79.5 for men, 83.2 for women), up from 80.6 years in 2015.[^cite_note-WHO-251] This [ranks it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_life_expectancy) 26th among 193 nations, behind the other [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries). The *National Institute of Public Health* of the [University of Southern Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_Denmark) has calculated 19 major risk factors among Danes that contribute to a lowering of the life expectancy; this includes smoking, alcohol, [drug abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_abuse) and [physical inactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedentary_lifestyle).[^cite_note-health_report-252] Although the [obesity rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity_by_country) is lower than in North America and most other European countries,[^cite_note-253] the large number of [overweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overweight) Danes results in an annual additional consumption in the health care system of [DKK](236323.md) 1,625 million.[^cite_note-health_report-252] In a 2012 study, Denmark had the highest [cancer rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_OECD_countries_by_cancer_rate) of all countries listed by the World Cancer Research Fund International; researchers suggest the reasons are better reporting, but also lifestyle factors like heavy [alcohol consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_alcohol_consumption_per_capita), [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevalence_of_tobacco_consumption) and [physical inactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_inactivity).[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255]

Denmark has a [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) system, characterised by being publicly financed through taxes and, for most of the services, run directly by the regional authorities. One of the sources of income was a national health care contribution (*_sundhedsbidrag_*) (2007–11\:8%; '12\:7%; '13\:6%; '14\:5%; '15\:4%; '16\:3%; '17\:2%; '18\:1%; '19\:0%) but it was phased out from January 2019 in favour of income taxes.[^cite_note-taxation1-141] This means that most [health care provision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_provider) is free at the point of delivery for all residents. Additionally, roughly two in five have complementary [private insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance) to cover services not fully covered by the state, such as [physiotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_therapy). As of 2012, Denmark spends 11.2% of its GDP on health care; this is up from 9.8% in 2007 (US\$3,512 per capita). This places Denmark above the [OECD](33853117.md) average and above the other Nordic countries.[^cite_note-commonwealth-256][^cite_note-257]

### Vulnerable residential areas

Main articles: [Immigration to Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Denmark) and [Vulnerable residential area (Denmark)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_residential_area_\(Denmark\))



[Mjølnerparken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mj%C3%B8lnerparken) in Copenhagen

Certain [social housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_housing) districts in Denmark fulfilling specific statistical criteria of relatively low employment, school attendance, relatively low income, a relatively low educational level or relatively many convicted inhabitants are officially listed by the government as [vulnerable residential areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_residential_area_\(Denmark\)). In some cases, the majority of the neighbourhoods consist of non-Western immigrants and their descendants. Over the years, several government initiatives have been taken to further integration and counter [urban decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_decay) in these neighbourhoods. Major plans to this end were presented in 1994 and 2000 by the governments of [Poul Nyrup Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poul_Nyrup_Rasmussen), in 2004 by the [Anders Fogh Rasmussen I Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Fogh_Rasmussen_I_Cabinet), in 2010 by the [Lars Løkke Rasmussen I Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_L%C3%B8kke_Rasmussen_I_Cabinet), in 2013 by the [Helle Thorning-Schmidt I Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorning-Schmidt_I_Cabinet), in 2018 by the [Lars Løkke Rasmussen III Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_L%C3%B8kke_Rasmussen_III_Cabinet), and in 2021 by the [Mette Frederiksen I Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_I_Cabinet).[^cite_note-DenOffentlige-258][^cite_note-259][^cite_note-Time-260] Some of the policies have been criticised for undercutting 'equality before law' and for portraying immigrants, especially Muslim immigrants, in a bad light.[^cite_note-NPR-261][^cite_note-262]

During the years 2010–2021, the term "[ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghetto)" was used officially to designate some or all of the vulnerable areas.[^cite_note-NPR-261] The term was considered controversial, however, and removed in 2021.[^cite_note-Dybvad1-263][^cite_note-Dybvad2-264][^cite_note-BL2-265] Denmark is the only country to have officially used the word 'ghetto' in the 21st century to denote certain residential areas.[^cite_note-Reuters-266] From 2021, four different lists are published, depending on the residents' income levels, employment status, education levels, criminal convictions and origin (a statistical criterion based on parents' geographical birthplace and citizenship).[^cite_note-NYT-267][^cite_note-NPR-261][^cite_note-BBC-268] In 2023, there were 19 vulnerable residential areas in Denmark.[^cite_note-269]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Denmark)

See also: [Culture of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Culture of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Greenland)

Denmark shares strong cultural and [historic ties with its Scandinavian neighbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Scandinavia) Sweden and Norway. It has historically been one of the most socially progressive cultures in the world. In 1969, Denmark was the first country to legalise [pornography](5710507.md),[^cite_note-270] and in 2012, Denmark replaced its "[registered partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union)" laws, which it had been the first country to introduce in 1989,[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272] with [gender-neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_neutrality) [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage), and [allowed same-sex marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Denmark) to be performed in the [Church of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-273][^cite_note-274] [Modesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modesty) and [social equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality) are important parts of Danish culture.[^cite_note-275] In a 2016 study comparing [empathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empathy) scores of 63 countries, Denmark ranked 4th world-wide having the highest empathy among surveyed European countries.[^cite_note-276]



[Kronborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronborg) Castle in the town of [Helsingør](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsing%C3%B8r). Immortalized as Elsinore in [William Shakespeare](32897.md)'s play [Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet), Kronborg is one of the most important [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) castles in Northern Europe and was inscribed on the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage list in Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Northern_Europe) in 2000.

The astronomical discoveries of [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe), [Ludwig A. Colding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_A._Colding)'s neglected articulation of the principle of [conservation of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_energy), and the contributions to atomic physics of [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr) indicate the range of Danish scientific achievement. The fairy tales of [Hans Christian Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen), the philosophical essays of [Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard), the short stories of [Karen Blixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Blixen) (penname [Isak Dinesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isak_Dinesen)), the plays of [Ludvig Holberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludvig_Holberg), and the dense, aphoristic poetry of [Piet Hein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Hein_\(scientist\)), have earned international recognition, as have the symphonies of [Carl Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Nielsen). From the mid-1990s, Danish films have attracted international attention, especially those associated with [Dogme 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogme_95) like those of [Lars von Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_von_Trier) and [Thomas Vinterberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Vinterberg).

A major feature of Danish culture is [Jul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jul_\(Denmark\)) (Danish [Christmas](6237.md)). The holiday is celebrated throughout December, starting either at the beginning of Advent or on 1 December with a variety of traditions, culminating with the Christmas Eve meal.

There are seven heritage sites inscribed on the UNESCO [World Heritage list in Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Northern_Europe): [Christiansfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansfeld), a Moravian Church Settlement, the [Jelling Mounds (Runic Stones and Church)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelling_stones), [Kronborg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronborg_Castle), [Roskilde Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_Cathedral), and the [Par force hunting landscape in North Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par_force_hunting_landscape_in_North_Zealand) and 3 in the [World Heritage list in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_North_America): [Ilulissat Icefjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilulissat_Icefjord), Aasivissuit—[Nipisat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipisat_Island), [Kujataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kujataa) within the Kingdom of Denmark.[^cite_note-277]

### Human rights

Main article: [Human rights in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Denmark)

Denmark is usually considered a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) country, which has adopted [legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation) and [policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy) to support [women's rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Denmark), [minority rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_rights), and [LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Denmark). Human rights in Denmark are protected by the state's Constitution of the [Realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realm_of_Denmark) *([Danmarks Riges Grundlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark))*; applying equally in Denmark proper, [Greenland](12118.md) and the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md), and through the [ratification](429650.md) of [international human rights treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_human_rights_instruments).[^cite_note-\:8-278] Denmark has held a significant role in the adoption of both the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) and in the establishment of the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) (ECHR). In 1987, the [Kingdom Parliament](429743.md) (*Folketinget*) established a national human rights institution, the Danish Centre of Human Rights, now the [Danish Institute for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Institute_for_Human_Rights).[^cite_note-\:8-278]

In 2009, a referendum on changing the [Danish Act of Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Act_of_Succession) were held to grant [absolute primogeniture](136535.md) to the Danish throne, meaning that the eldest child, regardless of gender, takes precedence in the line of succession. As it was not retroactive, the current successor to the throne is the eldest son of the King, rather than his eldest child. The Danish constitution Article 2 states that "The monarchy is inherited by men and women".[^cite_note-279]

The [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) have for decades been the subject of [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) and [abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuse) by the [dominant colonisers from Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_colonization_of_the_Americas), those countries claiming possession of Inuit lands. The Inuit have never been a single community in a single region of Inuit.[^cite_note-280] From the 18th century up to the 1970s, the Danish government (Dano-Norwegian until 1814) tried to assimilate the [Indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people) of Greenland, the [Greenlandic Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_Inuit), encouraging them to adopt the majority language, culture and religion.[^cite_note-281] Denmark has been greatly criticised by the Greenlandic community for the politics of *Danisation* (1950s and 1960s) of and discrimination against the Indigenous population of the country.[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-\:2-283] Critical treatment paying non-Inuit workers higher wages than the local people, the relocation of entire families from their traditional lands into settlements, and separating children from their parents and sending them away to Denmark for schooling has been practised.[^cite_note-\:2-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286] Nevertheless, Denmark ratified, in 1996, to recognise the [ILO-convention 169](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_and_Tribal_Peoples_Convention,_1989) on Indigenous people recommended by the UN.

Denmark was the [first country in the world to grant legal recognition to same-sex unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_same-sex_unions) in the form of [registered partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union) in 1989. On 7 June 2012, the law was replaced by a new [same-sex marriage law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Denmark), which came into effect on 15 June 2012.[^cite_note-cphpost-287] [Greenland](12118.md) and the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) legalised same-sex marriage in April 2016,[^cite_note-Marriage_Greenland-288] and in July 2017 respectively.[^cite_note-PNews2017-289] In January 2016, a resolution was implemented by the [Danish parliament](429743.md) which prevented [transgender](19904525.md) identity being classified as a [mental health condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_disorder). In doing so, Denmark became the first country in Europe to go against the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) standards, which classified transgender identity as being a mental health issue until June 2018.[^cite_note-290][^cite_note-291]

In its 2024 [Freedom in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World) report, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) rated the country "[free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom)" with a score of 97 (out of 100).[^cite_note-FreedomintheWorld2024-245]

### Media

Main articles: [Cinema of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Denmark) and [Television in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Denmark)

See also: [Cinema of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Television in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Greenland)



Director [Lars von Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_von_Trier), who co-created the Dogme 95 film movement with Thomas Vinterberg

Danish cinema dates back to 1897 and since the 1980s has maintained a steady stream of productions due largely to funding by the state-supported [Danish Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Film_Institute). There have been three big internationally important waves of Danish cinema: erotic melodrama of the [silent era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film); the increasingly explicit sex films of the 1960s and 1970s; and lastly, the [Dogme 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogme_95) movement of the late 1990s, where directors often used hand-held cameras to dynamic effect in a conscious reaction against big-budget studios. Danish films have been noted for their realism, religious and moral themes, sexual frankness and technical innovation. The Danish filmmaker [Carl Th. Dreyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Theodor_Dreyer) is considered one of the greatest directors of [early cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_cinema).[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-autogenerated1-293]

Other Danish filmmakers of note include [Erik Balling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Balling), the creator of the popular *[Olsen-banden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsen-banden)* films; [Gabriel Axel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Axel), an [Oscar](324.md)-winner for *[Babette's Feast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babette's_Feast)* in 1987; and [Bille August](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bille_August), the Oscar-, [Palme d'Or](612946.md)- and [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award)-winner for *[Pelle the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelle_the_Conqueror)* in 1988. In the modern era, notable filmmakers in Denmark include [Lars von Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_von_Trier), who co-created the Dogme 95 movement with [Thomas Vinterberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Vinterberg), and multiple award-winners [Susanne Bier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Bier) and [Nicolas Winding Refn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Winding_Refn). [Mads Mikkelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mads_Mikkelsen) is a world-renowned Danish actor, as is [Nikolaj Coster-Waldau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaj_Coster-Waldau).

Danish [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) date back to the 1540s, when handwritten fly sheets reported on the news. In 1666, [Anders Bording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Bording), the father of Danish journalism, began a [state paper](20803507.md). In 1834, the first liberal, factual newspaper appeared, and the 1849 Constitution established lasting [freedom of the press in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_press_in_Denmark).

Modern Danish mass media and news programming are dominated by a few large corporations. In printed media [JP/Politikens Hus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP/Politikens_Hus) and [Berlingske Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlingske_Media), between them, control the largest newspapers *[Politiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politiken)*, *[Berlingske Tidende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlingske_Tidende)* and *[Jyllands-Posten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyllands-Posten)* and major tabloids *[B.T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.T._\(tabloid\))* and *[Ekstra Bladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekstra_Bladet)*. [In television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Denmark), publicly owned stations [DR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DR_\(broadcaster\)) and [TV 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_2_\(Denmark\)) have large shares of the viewers.[^cite_note-294] DR in particular is famous for its high quality TV-series often sold to foreign broadcasters and often with leading female characters like internationally known actresses [Sidse Babett Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidse_Babett_Knudsen) and [Sofie Gråbøl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofie_Gr%C3%A5b%C3%B8l). In radio, DR has a near monopoly, currently broadcasting on all four nationally available [FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_broadcasting) channels, competing only with local stations.[^cite_note-295]

### Music

Main article: [Music of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Denmark)

See also: [Music of the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Music of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Greenland)

A sample from [Carl Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Nielsen)'s [Wind Quintet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_Quintet_\(Nielsen\)) with the theme from *Min Jesus, lad mit hjerte få*

Denmark and its multiple outlying islands have a [wide range of folk traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_traditional_music). The country's most famous classical composer is [Carl Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Nielsen) (1865–1931), especially remembered for his six symphonies and his [Wind Quintet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_Quintet_\(Nielsen\)), while the [Royal Danish Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Ballet) specialises in the work of the Danish choreographer [August Bournonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Bournonville). The [Royal Danish Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Orchestra) is among the world's oldest orchestras.[^cite_note-296] Danes have distinguished themselves as [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) musicians, and the [Copenhagen Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Jazz_Festival) has acquired international recognition.

The modern [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) and rock scene has produced a few names of international fame, including [Aqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_\(band\)), [Alphabeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabeat), [D-A-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-A-D), [King Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Diamond), [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_\(Danish_band\)), [Lukas Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukas_Graham), [Mew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mew_\(band\)), [Michael Learns to Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Learns_to_Rock), [MØ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%98), [Oh Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Land), [The Raveonettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Raveonettes) and [Volbeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volbeat), among [others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Danish_bands). [Lars Ulrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Ulrich), the drummer of the band [Metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallica), has become the first Danish musician to be inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md).

[Roskilde Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_Festival) near Copenhagen is the largest music festival in Northern Europe since 1971 and Denmark has many [recurring music festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_festivals_in_Denmark) of all genres throughout, including [Aarhus International Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_International_Jazz_Festival), [Skanderborg Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderborg_Festival), The Blue Festival in Aalborg, Esbjerg International Chamber Music Festival and [Skagen Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skagen_Festival) among many others.[^cite_note-297][^cite_note-298]

Denmark has participated in the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) since 1957 and has won the contest three times, in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1963), [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2000) and [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013).

### Architecture and design

Main articles: [Architecture of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Denmark) and [Danish design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_design)



[Grundtvig's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundtvig's_Church) in Copenhagen, an example of expressionist architecture

Denmark's architecture became firmly established in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) when first [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_style), then [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_style) churches and cathedrals sprang up throughout the country. From the 16th century, Dutch and Flemish designers were brought to Denmark, initially to improve the country's fortifications, but increasingly to build magnificent royal castles and palaces in the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) style. During the 17th century, many impressive buildings were built in the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) style, both in the capital and the provinces. [Neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) from France was slowly adopted by native Danish architects who increasingly participated in defining architectural style. A productive period of [Historicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicism_\(art\)) ultimately merged into the 19th-century [National Romantic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Romantic_style).[^cite_note-299]

The 20th century brought along new architectural styles; including [expressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionist_architecture), best exemplified by the designs of architect [Peder Vilhelm Jensen-Klint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peder_Vilhelm_Jensen-Klint), which relied heavily on Scandinavian brick Gothic traditions; and [Nordic Classicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Classicism), which enjoyed brief popularity in the early decades of the century. It was in the 1960s that Danish architects such as [Arne Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Jacobsen) entered the world scene with their highly successful [Functionalist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Functionalist_style). This, in turn, has evolved into more recent world-class masterpieces including [Jørn Utzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B8rn_Utzon)'s [Sydney Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Opera_House) and [Johan Otto von Spreckelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Otto_von_Spreckelsen)'s [Grande Arche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Arche) in Paris, paving the way for a number of contemporary Danish designers such as [Bjarke Ingels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjarke_Ingels) to be rewarded for excellence both at home and abroad.[^cite_note-300]

Danish design is a term often used to describe a style of [functionalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functionalism_\(architecture\)) design and architecture that was developed in the mid-20th century, originating in Denmark. Danish design is typically applied to industrial design, furniture and household objects, which have won many international awards. The [Royal Porcelain Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Copenhagen) is famous for the quality of its ceramics. Danish design is also a well-known brand, often associated with world-famous, 20th-century designers and architects such as [Børge Mogensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rge_Mogensen), [Finn Juhl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finn_Juhl), [Hans Wegner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Wegner), [Arne Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Jacobsen), [Poul Henningsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poul_Henningsen) and [Verner Panton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verner_Panton).[^cite_note-301] Other designers of note include [Kristian Solmer Vedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Solmer_Vedel) in the area of industrial design, [Jens Quistgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Quistgaard) for kitchen furniture and implements and [Ole Wanscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Wanscher) who had a classical approach to furniture design.

### Literature and philosophy

Main articles: [Danish literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_literature) and [Danish philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_philosophy)



Renowned author Hans Christian Andersen, best remembered for [his literary fairy tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen_bibliography)



Søren Kirkegaard, existentialist philosopher considered one of the most important figures of the [Danish Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Golden_Age)

[^cite_note-302]

The first known Danish literature is myths and [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_folklore) from the 10th and 11th century. [Saxo Grammaticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxo_Grammaticus), normally considered the first Danish writer, worked on a chronicle of [Danish history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Denmark) (*[Gesta Danorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesta_Danorum)*). Very little is known of other Danish literature from the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). With the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) came [Ludvig Holberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludvig_Holberg) whose comedy plays are still being performed.

In the late 19th century, literature was seen as a way to influence society. Known as the [Modern Breakthrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Breakthrough), this movement was championed by [Georg Brandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Brandes), [Henrik Pontoppidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Pontoppidan) (awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md)) and [J. P. Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Peter_Jacobsen). [Romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) influenced the renowned writer and poet [Hans Christian Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen), known for his stories and [fairy tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_tale), e.g. *[The Ugly Duckling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ugly_Duckling)*, *[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid)* and *[The Snow Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Snow_Queen)*. In recent history [Johannes Vilhelm Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Vilhelm_Jensen) was also awarded the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature). [Karen Blixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Blixen) is famous for her novels and short stories. Other Danish writers of importance are [Herman Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Bang), [Gustav Wied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Wied), [William Heinesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Heinesen), [Martin Andersen Nexø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Andersen_Nex%C3%B8), [Piet Hein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Hein_\(Denmark\)), [Hans Scherfig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Scherfig), [Klaus Rifbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Rifbjerg), [Dan Turèll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Tur%C3%A8ll), [Tove Ditlevsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tove_Ditlevsen), [Inger Christensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inger_Christensen) and [Peter Høeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_H%C3%B8eg).

Danish philosophy has a long tradition as part of [Western philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_philosophy). Perhaps the most influential Danish philosopher was [Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard), the creator of [Christian existentialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_existentialism). Kierkegaard had a few Danish followers, including [Harald Høffding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_H%C3%B8ffding), who later in his life moved on to join the movement of [positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivism). Another Danish philosopher of note is [Grundtvig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._F._S._Grundtvig), whose philosophy gave rise to a new form of non-aggressive nationalism in Denmark, and who is also influential for his theological and historical works.

### Painting and photography

Main articles: [Danish art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_art) and [Photography in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography_in_Denmark)



*Woman in Front of a Mirror*, (1841), by [Christoffer Wilhelm Eckersberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffer_Wilhelm_Eckersberg)

While Danish art was influenced over the centuries by trends in Germany and the Netherlands, the 15th- and 16th-century [church frescos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_frescos_in_Denmark), which can be seen in many of the country's older churches, are of particular interest as they were painted in a style typical of native Danish painters.[^cite_note-303]

The [Danish Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Golden_Age), which began in the first half of the 19th century, was inspired by a new feeling of nationalism and romanticism, typified in the later previous century by [history painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_painting) [Nicolai Abildgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolai_Abildgaard). [Christoffer Wilhelm Eckersberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffer_Wilhelm_Eckersberg) was not only a productive artist in his own right but taught at the [Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Fine_Arts) where his students included [Wilhelm Bendz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Bendz), [Christen Købke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christen_K%C3%B8bke), [Martinus Rørbye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinus_R%C3%B8rbye), [Constantin Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Hansen), and [Wilhelm Marstrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Marstrand).

In 1871, [Holger Drachmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Drachmann) and [Karl Madsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Madsen) visited [Skagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skagen) in the far north of [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland) where they quickly built up one of Scandinavia's most successful [artists' colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skagen_Painters) specialising in [Naturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalism_\(arts\)) and [Realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_\(arts\)) rather than in the traditional approach favoured by the academy. Hosted by [Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ancher) and his wife [Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Ancher), they were soon joined by [P.S. Krøyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.S._Kr%C3%B8yer), [Carl Locher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Locher) and [Laurits Tuxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurits_Tuxen). All participated in painting the natural surroundings and local people. Similar trends developed on Funen with the *[Fynboerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fynboerne)* who included [Johannes Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Larsen), [Fritz Syberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Syberg) and [Peter Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hansen_\(painter\)),[^cite_note-Funish-304] and on the island of Bornholm with the [Bornholm school of painters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornholm_school_of_painters) including [Niels Lergaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Lergaard), [Kræsten Iversen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%A6sten_Iversen) and [Oluf Høst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oluf_H%C3%B8st).[^cite_note-305]

Painting has continued to be a prominent form of artistic expression in Danish culture, inspired by and also influencing major international trends in this area. These include [impressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism) and the modernist styles of [expressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionism), [abstract painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_painting) and [surrealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism). While international co-operation and activity has almost always been essential to the Danish artistic community, influential art collectives with a firm Danish base includes [De Tretten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Tretten) (1909–1912), [Linien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linien) (1930s and 1940s), [COBRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COBRA_\(avant-garde_movement\)) (1948–1951), [Fluxus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxus) (1960s and 1970s), [De Unge Vilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junge_Wilde) (1980s) and more recently [Superflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superflex) (founded in 1993). Notable Danish painters from modern times representing various [art movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_movements) include [Theodor Philipsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Philipsen) (impressionism and naturalism), [Anna Klindt Sørensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Klindt_S%C3%B8rensen) (expressionism), [Franciska Clausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciska_Clausen) (Neue Sachlichkeit, cubism, surrealism and others), [Henry Heerup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Heerup) (naivism), [Robert Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jacobsen) (abstract painting), [Carl Henning Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Henning_Pedersen) (abstract painting), [Asger Jorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asger_Jorn) (Situationist, abstract painting), [Bjørn Wiinblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rn_Wiinblad) (art deco, orientalism), [Per Kirkeby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Kirkeby) (neo-expressionism, abstract painting), [Per Arnoldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Arnoldi) (pop art), and [Michael Kvium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kvium) (neo-surrealism).

Danish photography has developed from strong participation and interest in the very beginnings of the [art of photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_photography) in 1839. Pioneers such as [Mads Alstrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mads_Alstrup) and [Georg Emil Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Emil_Hansen) paved the way for a rapidly growing profession during the last half of the 19th century. Today Danish photographers such as [Astrid Kruse Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrid_Kruse_Jensen) and [Jacob Aue Sobol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Aue_Sobol) are active in key exhibitions around the world.[^cite_note-306]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Danish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_cuisine)

See also: [Faroese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_cuisine) and [Greenlandic cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_cuisine)



*Smørrebrød*, a variety of Danish open sandwiches piled high with delicacies

The traditional [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine) of Denmark, like that of the other Nordic countries and of [Northern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Germany), consists mainly of meat, fish and potatoes. Danish dishes are highly seasonal, stemming from the country's agricultural past, its geography, and its climate of long, cold winters.

The open sandwiches on rye bread, known as *[smørrebrød](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%C3%B8rrebr%C3%B8d)*, can be considered a national speciality. Hot meals traditionally consist of [ground meats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_meat), such as *[frikadeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frikadeller)* (meat balls of veal and pork) and *hakkebøf* (minced beef patties), or of more substantial meat and fish dishes such as *[flæskesteg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fl%C3%A6skesteg)* (roast pork with crackling) and *kogt torsk* (poached cod) with mustard sauce. Denmark is known for its [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Group) and [Tuborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuborg) beers and for its [akvavit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akvavit) and [bitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitters).

Since around 1970, chefs and restaurants across Denmark have introduced [gourmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourmet) cooking, largely influenced by [French cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_cuisine). Also inspired by continental practices, Danish chefs have recently developed a new innovative cuisine and a series of gourmet dishes based on high-quality local produce known as [New Danish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Danish_cuisine).[^cite_note-307] As a result of these developments, Denmark now has a considerable number of internationally acclaimed restaurants of which several have been awarded [Michelin stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Guide). This includes [Geranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geranium_\(restaurant\)) and [Noma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noma_\(restaurant\)) in Copenhagen.

### Sports

Main article: [Sport in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Denmark)

See also: [Sport in the Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_the_Faroe_Islands) and [Sport in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Greenland)



[Michael Laudrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Laudrup) was named the best Danish football player of all time by the Danish Football Union.

Sports are popular in Denmark, and its citizens participate in and watch a wide variety. The [national sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_sport) is [football](10568.md), with over 320,000 players in more than 1600 [clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_club_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-308] Denmark qualified six times consecutively for the [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Football_Championship) between 1984 and 2004, and were crowned European champions in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_UEFA_European_Football_Championship); other significant achievements include winning the Confederations Cup in 1995 and reaching the quarter-final of the 1998 World Cup. The [Denmark women's national handball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_women's_national_handball_team) celebrated great successes during the 1990s and has won a total of 13 medals—seven gold (in 1994, 1996 (2), 1997, 2000, 2002 and 2004), four silver (in 1962, 1993, 1998 and 2004) and two bronze (in 1995 and 2013). On the [men's side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_men's_national_handball_team), Denmark has won 12 medals—four gold (in 2008, 2012, 2016 and 2019), four silver (in 1967, 2011, 2013 and 2014) and four bronze (in 2002, 2004, 2006 and 2007)—the most that have been won by any team in [European Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Men's_Handball_Championship) history.[^cite_note-309] In 2019, the Danish men's national handball team won their first [World Championship title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_men's_national_handball_team#2019_World_Championship).[^cite_note-310][^cite_note-311]

In recent years, Denmark has made a mark as a strong [cycling](857311.md) nation, with [Michael Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rasmussen_\(cyclist\)) reaching [King of the Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Mountains) status in the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) in 2005 and 2006. Other popular sports include golf—which is mostly popular among those in the older demographic;[^cite_note-312] [tennis](29773.md)—in which Denmark is successful on a professional level; [basketball](3921.md)—Denmark joined the international governing body [FIBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA) in 1951;[^cite_note-313] rugby—the [Danish Rugby Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Rugby_Union) dates back to 1950;[^cite_note-314] [ice hockey](14790.md)—often competing in the top division in the Men's World Championships; rowing—Denmark specialise in lightweight rowing and are particularly known for their lightweight coxless four, having won six gold and two silver World Championship medals and three gold and two bronze [Olympic](22576.md) medals; and several indoor sports—especially [badminton](3956.md), [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis) and gymnastics, in each of which Denmark holds World Championships and [Olympic medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_at_the_Olympics).
