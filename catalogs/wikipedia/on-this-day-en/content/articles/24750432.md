**Ejin** ([Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language): ᠡᠵᠡᠨ᠎ᠡ ᠬᠣᠰᠢᠭᠤ; [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 额济纳旗) is a [banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banner_\(Inner_Mongolia\)) in the far west of [Inner Mongolia](55064.md), China. It is under the administration of [Alxa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alxa_League) and is the westernmost [county-level division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_division) of Inner Mongolia, bordering [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) province to the west and [Mongolia](19271.md)'s [Bayankhongor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayankhongor_Province) and [Govi-Altai Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govi-Altai_Province). Its seat is located at Dalaihob Town. To the west, it shares a border with [Subei Mongol Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subei_Mongol_Autonomous_County) of [Jiuquan, Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan).

## History



Ejin [Torghut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torghut) Banner during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty)

The area was historically the hunting grounds of the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu), before being acquired by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) in BC 121. The ruins of the ancient city of [Khara-Khoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khara-Khoto), founded by the [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia), are located in Ejin.

Ejin was incorporated into the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) under [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) in 1226. During the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), the area was home to nomadic [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) populations. It was later incorporated into the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) (1644–1912). Under the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), the area was under the jurisdiction of [Ningxia province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia). The area fell under the control of the [Communist Party](7175.md) in September 1949. Subsequently, it was placed successively under government commissions in [Jiuquan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan), [Zhangye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangye), and Ningxia province.

On June 1, 1956, Ejin became part of the [Bayannur League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayannur) of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region. The [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center) was constructed at around the same time. On May 1, 1980, it became part of the Alxa League. Since the [Chinese economic reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_economic_reforms) in the 1980s, Ejin rapidly developed its economy. By the early 21st century it became one of the fastest-growing counties in the country measured by GDP.

## Geography

Ejin Banner is the westernmost banner of [Inner Mongolia](55064.md). It is located in the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert) and borders [Mongolia](19271.md) in the north, [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) Province in the southwest, and [Alxa Right Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alxa_Right_Banner) in the southeast.

Ejin Banner is geographically vast—approximately the size of South Korea, it covers an area larger than each of the provinces of [Jiangsu](166412.md) and [Zhejiang](96602.md).

Ejin Banner takes its name from the [Ejin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruo_Shui), also known as the Ruo Shui, which flows from the [Qilian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qilian_Mountains) of Gansu, and whose two [distributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributary) terminate in [Juyan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juyan_Lake) basin within Ejin Banner. Most of the banner's population resides in this river's valley.

## Transportation

Ejin Banner is served by the [Ejin Banner Taolai Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejin_Banner_Taolai_Airport).

Train transportation is provided by [Jiayuguan–Ceke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiayuguan%E2%80%93Ceke_railway) and the [Linhe–Ceke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linhe-Ceke_Railway) railways, which are primarily used to haul coal that has been trucked from [Nariin Sukhait mining complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nariin_Sukhait_mining_complex) in Mongolia to [Ceke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceke,_Inner_Mongolia) and is loaded on trains at the coal terminals there. Passenger service exists on this railway; as of 2015 there is daily passenger service between Ejin and the regional capital [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot), which takes approximately 16 hours.[^cite_note-5]

There is also a railway south to both sites (northern and southern) of [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center), which are also located within Ejin Banner.
