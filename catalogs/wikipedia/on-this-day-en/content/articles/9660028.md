The **Government of Malaysia**, officially the **Federal Government of Malaysia** ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Kerajaan Persekutuan Malaysia*; [Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_script): ‏كراجأن ڤرسكوتوان مليسيا), is based in the [Federal Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Territories_of_Malaysia) of [Putrajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrajaya), with the exception of the legislative branch, which is located in [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md). [Malaysia](3607937.md) is a federation composed of [the 11 States of Malaya, the Borneo States of Sabah and Sarawak, and 3 Federal Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_federal_territories_of_Malaysia) operating within a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) under the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system) and is categorised as a [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy). The [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government) of [Malaysia](3607937.md) adheres to and is created by the [Federal Constitution of Malaysia](534791.md), the supreme law of the land.

The federal government adopts the principle of [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) under Article 127 of the Federal Constitution of Malaysia,[^cite_note-2] and has three branches: the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), and [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary).[^cite_note-3] The [state governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_Malaysia) in Malaysia also have their respective executive and legislative bodies. The judicial system in Malaysia is a federalised court system operating uniformly throughout the country.
