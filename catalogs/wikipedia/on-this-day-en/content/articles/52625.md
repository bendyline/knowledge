The **Arab League**,[^cite_note-4] officially the **League of Arab States**,[^cite_note-5] is a [regional organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_organization) in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world). The Arab League was formed in [Cairo](6293.md) on 22 March 1945, initially with seven members: [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Egypt), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq), [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Syrian_Republic), and [North Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yemen).[^cite_note-6] Currently, the League has [22 members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Arab_League), including three non-Arab countries, namely [Comoros](5403.md), [Djibouti](17207794.md) and [Somalia](27358.md).[^cite_note-7]

The League's main goal is to "draw closer the relations between member states and co-ordinate collaboration between them, to safeguard their independence and sovereignty, and to consider in a general way the affairs and interests of the Arab countries".[^cite_note-yale-8] The organisation has received a relatively low level of cooperation throughout its history.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

Through institutions, notably the [Arab League Educational, Cultural and Scientific Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_League_Educational,_Cultural_and_Scientific_Organization) (ALECSO) and the Economic and Social Council of its [Council of Arab Economic Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Arab_Economic_Unity) (CAEU), the League facilitates political, economic, cultural, scientific, and social programmes designed to promote the interests of the Arab world.[^cite_note-10] It has served as a forum for the member states to coordinate policy, arrange studies of and committees as to matters of common concern, settle inter-state disputes and limit conflicts such as the [1958 Lebanon crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Lebanon_crisis). The League has served as a platform for the drafting and conclusion of many landmark documents promoting economic integration. One example is the [Joint Arab Economic Action Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Arab_Economic_Action_Charter), which outlines the principles for economic activities in the region.

Each member state has one vote in the [Council of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Arab_League), and decisions are binding only for those states that have voted for them. The aims of the league in 1945 were to strengthen and coordinate the political, cultural, economic and social programmes of its members and to mediate disputes among them or between them and third parties. Furthermore, the signing of an agreement on *Joint Defence and Economic Cooperation* on 13 April 1950 committed the signatories to coordination of military defence measures. In March 2015, the Arab League General Secretary announced the establishment of a Joint Arab Force with the aim of counteracting extremism and other threats to the Arab States. The decision was reached while [Operation Decisive Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Decisive_Storm) was intensifying in Yemen. Participation in the project is voluntary, and the army intervenes only at the request of one of the member states. Existing military cooperation between Arab league states and regional civil wars and terrorist threats were the impetuses for JAF's establishment.[^cite_note-11]

In the early 1970s, the Economic Council put forward a proposal to create the [Joint Arab Chambers of Commerce](14496822.md) across international states. That led to the setting up of mandates to promote, encourage and facilitate bilateral trade between the Arab world and significant trading partners.

## History

Following adoption of the [Alexandria Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Protocol) in 1944, the Arab League was founded on 22 March 1945.[^cite_note-History-12] The official headquarters of the League was the Boustan Palace in Cairo.[^cite_note-Baqai-13] It aimed to be a regional organisation of Arab states with a focus to developing the economy, resolving disputes and coordinating political aims.[^cite_note-Baqai-13] Other countries later joined the league.[^cite_note-14] Each country was given one vote in the council. The first major action was joint intervention to keep Palestine from being divided into two states in keeping with the decision of the United Nations General Assembly. When Transjordan agreed to this proposal, Egypt intervened to prevent this from happening.[^cite_note-15] It was followed by the creation of a mutual defence treaty two years later. A common market was established in 1965.[^cite_note-16]

According to [Michael Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_N._Barnett) and [Etel Solingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etel_Solingen), the Arab League "has achieved a relatively low level of cooperation" throughout its history. They state that the design of the Arab League reflects Arab leaders' individual concerns for regime survival: "the politics of Arab nationalism and a shared identity led Arab states to embrace the rhetoric of Arab unity in order to legitimize their regimes, and to fear Arab unity in practice because it would impose greater restrictions on their sovereignty."[^cite_note-\:0-9] The Arab League was "specifically designed to fail at producing the kind of greater collaboration and integration that might have weakened political leaders at home."[^cite_note-\:0-9]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of the Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Arab_world)



Joining dates of member states; the Comoros (circled) joined in 1993.\
&#x20; 1940s   1950s   1960s   1970s

The Arab League member states cover over 13,000,000 km<sup>2</sup> (5,000,000 sq mi) and straddles two continents: [Africa](5334607.md) and [Asia](689.md). The area largely consists of arid deserts, such as the [Sahara](325363.md). Nevertheless, it also contains several highly fertile lands like the [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley), the [Jubba Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubba_Valley) and [Shebelle Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebelle_Valley) in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md), the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains) in the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), and the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent) that stretches over [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) and the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant). The area comprises deep forests in southern [Arabia](47858.md) and parts of the world's longest river, the [Nile](21244.md).

## Membership

The [Charter of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_the_Arab_League), also known as the Pact of the League of Arab States, is the founding treaty of the Arab League. Adopted in 1945, it stipulates that "the League of Arab States shall be composed of the independent Arab States that have signed this Pact."[^cite_note-yale-8]

In 1945, there were seven members,[^cite_note-Brittanica-17] but the Arab League now has 22 members, including 10 African countries:

### Suspensions

[Egypt](8087628.md) was suspended from the Arab League on 26 March 1979 due to the [Egypt–Israel peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_peace_treaty), with the League's headquarters moving from Cairo to [Tunis](57659.md), Tunisia. In 1987, Arab League states restored diplomatic relations with Egypt, the country was readmitted to the League in May 1989 and the League's headquarters were moved back to Cairo in September 1990.[^cite_note-25]

[Libya](17633.md) was suspended on 22 February 2011, following the outbreak of the [first Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_civil_war).[^cite_note-26] The Arab League voted to restore Libya's membership on 27 August 2011 by accrediting a representative of the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council), which was the partially recognised interim government of the country.[^cite_note-27]

[Syria](7515849.md) was suspended on 16 November 2011 in the aftermath of the outbreak of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md). On 6 March 2013, the Arab League gave Syria's seat in the Arab League to the [Syrian National Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Coalition), the largest [opposition group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition_to_Bashar_al-Assad).[^cite_note-syriannationalcoalition-28] On 9 March 2014, secretary general [Nabil Elaraby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabil_Elaraby) stated that Syria's seat would remain vacant until the opposition completed the formation of its institutions.[^cite_note-29] In 2021, the Arab League initiated a process of normalisation between the [Syrian Ba'athist government](57134800.md) and other Arab nations.[^cite_note-30] On 7 May 2023, at the meeting of the [Council of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Arab_League) in Cairo, it was agreed to reinstate Syria's membership.[^cite_note-31]

## Politics and administration

The Arab League is a political organisation which tries to help integrate its members economically, and solve conflicts involving member states without asking for foreign assistance. It possesses elements of a state representative parliament while foreign affairs are often conducted under UN supervision.[^cite_note-32]

The Charter of the Arab League[^cite_note-yale-8] endorsed the principle of an [Arab nation-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism) while respecting the [sovereignty](28791.md) of the individual member states. The internal regulations of the Council of the League[^cite_note-haynes-33] and the committees[^cite_note-haynes2-34] were agreed in October 1951. Those of the Secretariat-General were agreed in May 1953.[^cite_note-haynes3-35]

Since then, governance of the Arab League has been based on the duality of supra-national institutions and the sovereignty of the member states. Preservation of individual statehood derived its strengths from the natural preference of ruling elites to maintain their power and independence in decision making. Moreover, [feuds among Arab rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Cold_War), and the influence of external powers that might oppose Arab unity can be seen as obstacles towards a deeper integration of the league.

Mindful of their previous announcements in support of the [Arabs](2185.md) of [Palestine](241405.md) the framers of the Pact were determined to include them within the League from its inauguration.[^cite_note-geddes-36] This was done by means of an annex that declared:[^cite_note-yale-8]

> Even though Palestine was not able to control her own destiny, it was on the basis of the recognition of her independence that the Covenant of the League of Nations determined a system of government for her. Her existence and her independence among the nations can, therefore, no more be questioned *de jure* than the independence of any of the other Arab States. \[...] Therefore, the States signatory to the Pact of the Arab League consider that in view of Palestine's special circumstances, the Council of the League should designate an Arab delegate from Palestine to participate in its work until this country enjoys actual independence

In September 1963, the Arab League appointed [Ahmad Shukeiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shukeiri) as Representative of Palestine at the Arab League.[^cite_note-\:32-37]<sup>: xxviii </sup> At the Cairo Summit of 1964, the Arab League initiated the creation of an organisation representing the Palestinian people. The first [Palestinian National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Council) convened in [East Jerusalem](343508.md) on 29 May 1964. The [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) was founded during this meeting on 2 June 1964. Palestine was shortly admitted in to the Arab League, represented by the PLO. Today, [State of Palestine](241405.md) is a full member of the Arab League.

At the [Beirut Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_Summit) on 28 March 2002, the league adopted the [Arab Peace Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative),[^cite_note-al-bab-38] a Saudi-inspired peace plan for the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md). The initiative offered full normalisation of the relations with [Israel](9282173.md). In exchange, Israel was required to withdraw from all [occupied territories](2781576.md), including the [Golan Heights](7515865.md), to recognise Palestinian independence in the [West Bank](33209.md) and [Gaza Strip](12047.md), with East Jerusalem as its capital, as well as a "just solution" for the [Palestinian refugees](74681.md). The Peace Initiative was again endorsed at 2007 in the Riyadh Summit. In July 2007, the Arab League sent a mission, consisting of the [Jordanian](7515964.md) and [Egyptian](8087628.md) foreign ministers, to Israel to promote the initiative. Following [Venezuela](32374.md)'s move to expel Israeli diplomats amid the [2008–2009 Israel–Gaza conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932009_Israel%E2%80%93Gaza_conflict), [Kuwaiti](7515890.md) member of parliament [Waleed Al-Tabtabaie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waleed_Al-Tabtabaie) proposed moving Arab League headquarters to [Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas,_Venezuela), [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-39] On 13 June 2010, Amr Mohammed Moussa, Secretary-General of the Arab League, visited the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), the first visit by an official of the Arab League since Hamas' armed takeover in 2007.

The Arab League is a member of the [China-Arab States Cooperation Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China-Arab_States_Cooperation_Forum) (CASCF), which was formed in 2004. CASCF is the Arab League's earliest participation in a cooperation forum with another country or region.[^cite_note-\:1-40] CASCF is the primarily multi-lateral coordination mechanism between the Arab states and China and within CASCF the Arab League represents its member states as a relatively unified force.[^cite_note-41] The Arab League's coordination allows Arab states to negotiate actively for collective projects involving multiple states, such as railway projects, nuclear power projects, and Dead Sea initiatives.[^cite_note-\:1-40]

In 2015, the Arab League voiced support for [Saudi Arabian-led military intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) against the [Shia Houthis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaidiyyah) and forces loyal to former President [Ali Abdullah Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abdullah_Saleh), who was deposed in the [2011 uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Revolution).[^cite_note-42]

On 15 April 2018, in response to the [Turkish invasion of northern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Olive_Branch) aimed at ousting U.S.-backed [Syrian Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Protection_Units) from the enclave of [Afrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrin,_Syria), the Arab League passed a resolution calling on Turkish forces to withdraw from Afrin.[^cite_note-43]

In September 2019, the Arab League condemned [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md)'s plans to [annex](18950961.md) the eastern portion of the occupied [West Bank](33209.md) known as the [Jordan Valley](12105764.md).[^cite_note-44]

The Arab League met in Cairo on 12 October 2019 to discuss the [Turkish offensive into north-eastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Turkish_offensive_into_north-eastern_Syria). Upon meeting, its member states voted to condemn the Turkish offensive, dubbing it both an 'invasion' and an 'aggression' against an Arab state, adding that the organisation saw it as a violation of international law.[^cite_note-45]

On 9 September 2020, the Arab League refused to condemn the [UAE](69328.md)'s decision to [normalise ties with Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_Arab_Emirates_peace_agreement). Nevertheless, "the goal all our Arab countries seek, without exception, is to end the occupation and establish an independent Palestinian state on the 1967 borders with East Jerusalem as its capital," [Aboul Gheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Aboul_Gheit) said.[^cite_note-46] In January 2024, the Arab League expressed support for South Africa's [ICJ genocide case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_v._Israel_\(Genocide_Convention\)) against Israel.[^cite_note-47]

## Military

Main article: [Military of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Arab_League)

The Joint Defence Council of the Arab League is one of the [Institutions of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions_of_the_Arab_League).[^cite_note-ABC-CLIO-53] It was established under the terms of the [Joint Defence and Economic Co-operation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Defence_and_Economic_Co-operation_Treaty) of 1950 to coordinate the [joint defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_security) of the Arab League [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Arab_League).[^cite_note-54]

The Arab League as an organisation has no military force, similar to the UN, but at the 2007 summit, the leaders decided to reactivate their joint defence policy and establish a peacekeeping force to deploy in South Lebanon, Darfur, Iraq, and other hot spots.

At a 2015 summit in Egypt, member states agreed in principle to form a joint military force.[^cite_note-55]

## Economic resources

Main article: [Economy of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Arab_League)

See also: [List of countries by GDP (PPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) and [List of countries by GDP (PPP) per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)_per_capita)

The Arab League is rich in resources, such as enormous [oil](4207510.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) resources in certain member states.

Economic achievements initiated by the League amongst member states have been less impressive than those achieved by smaller Arab organisations such as the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) (GCC).[^cite_note-56] Among them is the [Arab Gas Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Gas_Pipeline), that will transport Egyptian and Iraqi gas to Jordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Turkey. As of 2013, a significant difference in economic conditions exist between the developed oil states of [Algeria](358.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md) and the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE), and developing countries like the [Comoros](5403.md), [Djibouti](17207794.md), [Mauritania](18778516.md), [Somalia](27358.md), [Sudan](27421.md) and [Yemen](350939.md).



[OAPEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAPEC) Members

The Arab League also includes great fertile lands in the southern part of [Sudan](27421.md). It is referred to as the food basket of the [Arab World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_World), the region's instability including the independence of [South Sudan](32350676.md) has not affected its [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) industry, that is considered the fastest growing industry in the region, with [Egypt](8087628.md), [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), and [Jordan](7515964.md) leading the way. Another industry that is growing steadily in the Arab League is telecommunications.

Economical achievements within members have been low in the league's history, other smaller Arab Organizations have achieved more than the league has, such as the [GCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperation_Council_for_the_Arab_States_of_the_Gulf), but lately several major economic projects that are promising are to be completed, the [Arab Gas Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Gas_Pipeline) is to end by 2010, Connecting Egyptian and Iraqi Gas to Jordan, Syria and Lebanon, and then to Turkey thus Europe, a free trade Agreement ([GAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Arab_Free_Trade_Area)) is to be completed by 1 January 2008, making 95% of all Arab products tax free of customs.

### Transport

The Arab League is divided into five parts when it comes to transport, with the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) and the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) being entirely connected by air, sea, roads and railways. Another part of the League is the [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley), made up of [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Sudan](27421.md). These two member states have started to improve the River Nile's navigation system to improve accessibility and thus foster trading. A new railway system is also set to connect the southern Egyptian city of [Abu Simbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel) with the northern Sudanese city of [Wadi Halfa](1126657.md) and then to [Khartoum](17320.md) and [Port Sudan](213572.md). The third division of the League is the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), where a 3,000 km stretch of railway runs from the southern cities of [Morocco](19291.md) to [Tripoli](31232.md) in Western [Libya](17633.md). The fourth division of the League is the [Horn of Africa](62142.md), whose member states include [Djibouti](17207794.md) and [Somalia](27358.md). These two Arab League states are separated by only ten nautical miles from the Arabian Peninsula by the [Bab el Mandeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_el_Mandeb) and this is quickly changing as Tarik bin Laden, the brother of [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden), has initiated the construction of the ambitious [Bridge of the Horns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_of_the_Horns) project, which ultimately aims to connect the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) with the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) via a massive bridge. The project is intended to facilitate and accelerate the already centuries-old trade and commerce between the two regions. The last division of the League is the isolated archipelago of the [Comoros](5403.md) located off the coast of [East Africa](260987.md), which is not physically connected to any other Arab state, but still trades with other Arab League members.

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of the Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Arab_world)

While [Arabs](2185.md) constitute the vast majority of the Arab League, with [Egyptians](31912046.md) making up the largest specific population within this group, the region is also home to several other distinct ethnic groups. These include [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers), [Kurds](17068.md), [Somalis](1571696.md), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), [Armenians](387816.md), [Nubians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubians), [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans), and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians). Each of these groups have their own distinct cultures, languages, and traditions. As of 1 July 2013, about 359 million people live in the states of the Arab League. Its population grows faster than in most other global regions. The most populous member state is [Egypt](8087628.md), with a population of over 100 million.[^cite_note-Central_Agency_for_Public_Mobilization_And_Statistics-60] The least populated is the [Comoros](5403.md), with approximately 850,000 inhabitants.

### Religion

The majority of the Arab League's citizens adhere to [Islam](6037917.md), with [Christianity](5211.md) being the second largest religion. At least 15 million Christians combined live in [Egypt](8087628.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Palestine](241405.md), [Sudan](27421.md) and [Syria](7515849.md). In addition, there are smaller but significant numbers of [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze), [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis), [Shabaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabak_people) and [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans). Numbers for nonreligious [Arabs](2185.md) are generally not available, but research by the [Pew Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Forum) suggests around 1% of people in the [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA) region are "unaffiliated".[^cite_note-61]

### Languages

The official language of the Arab League is [Literary Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic), based on [Classical Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Arabic). However, several Arab League member states have other co-official or national languages, such as [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language), [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_language), [Comorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorian_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) and [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages). In most countries, there is a dominant non-[codified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codification_\(linguistics\)) [spoken Arabic dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic).

## Culture

### Sports

Main article: [Sport policies of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_policies_of_the_Arab_League)

The [Pan-Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arab_Games) are considered the biggest Arab sporting event, which brings together athletes from all the Arab countries to participate in a variety of different sports.

The [Union of Arab Football Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Arab_Football_Associations) organises the [Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Cup) (for national teams) and the [Arab Club Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Club_Champions_Cup) (for clubs). Arab sport federations also exist for several games, include [basketball](3921.md), [volleyball](32558.md), [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball), [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis), [tennis](29773.md), [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)) and [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)).
