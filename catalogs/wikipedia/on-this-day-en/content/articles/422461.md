**Queen Mary University of London** (**QMUL**, or informally **QM**, and formerly **Queen Mary and Westfield College**) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) in [Mile End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_End), [East London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London), [England](9316.md). It is a member institution of the federal [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London). Queen Mary is a member of the [Russell Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Group) of research-intensive British universities, the Association of Commonwealth Universities and Universities UK.

Today, Queen Mary has six campuses across East and Central London in [Mile End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_End), [Whitechapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitechapel), [Charterhouse Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charterhouse_Square), [Ilford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilford), [Lincoln's Inn Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln's_Inn_Fields) and [West Smithfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithfield,_London), as well as an international presence in China, France, Greece and Malta. The Mile End campus is the largest self-contained campus of any London-based university. Queen Mary is organised into three faculties – the Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences, the Faculty of Science and Engineering, and [Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barts_and_The_London_School_of_Medicine_and_Dentistry). In 2023/24 the university had around 26,000 students. The annual income of the institution for 2024–25 was £729.6 million of which £160.9 million was from research grants and contracts, with an expenditure of £693.4 million.[^cite_note-QMUL_2024-25-4]

Queen Mary is a major centre for medical teaching and research and is part of [UCLPartners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLPartners), the world's largest [academic health science centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_health_science_centre). Queen Mary runs programmes at the [University of London Institute in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London_Institute_in_Paris), taking over the functions provided by [Royal Holloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London).[^cite_note-\:0-7]

There are nine [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureates) among Queen Mary's alumni, and current and former staff.[^cite_note-nobel-8] Notable alumni include [Ronald Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Ross), who discovered the origin and cure for [malaria](20423.md); [Davidson Nicol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson_Nicol), who discovered the breakdown of insulin in the human body; British politician [Peter Hain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hain); and [Andrew Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Pollard_\(biologist\)), the chief investigator of the [Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford%E2%80%93AstraZeneca_COVID-19_vaccine).

## History

Main article: [History of Queen Mary University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Queen_Mary_University_of_London)

### St Bartholomew's Hospital Medical College and The London Hospital Medical College

Further information: [Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barts_and_The_London_School_of_Medicine_and_Dentistry)

The Medical College of The Royal London Hospital (now part of the School of Medicine and Dentistry) was England's first medical school when it opened in 1785.[^cite_note-thewharf-9] In 1850, [Elizabeth Blackwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Blackwell) became the first fully qualified female doctor in the UK, after training at St Bartholomew's Hospital.[^cite_note-thewharf-9]

### People's Palace

The predecessor to Queen Mary College was founded in the mid-[Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) as a People's Palace when growing awareness of conditions in London's [East End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End) led to drives to provide facilities for local inhabitants, popularised in the 1882 novel *All Sorts of Conditions of Men – An Impossible Story* by [Walter Besant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Besant), which told of how a rich and clever couple from [Mayfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfair) went to the East End to build a ”Palace of Delight, with concert halls, reading rooms, picture galleries, art and designing schools."[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 15–17 </sup> Although not directly responsible for the conception of the People's Palace, the novel did much to popularise it.

The trustees of the Beaumont Trust, administering funds left by [Barber Beaumont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_Beaumont), purchased the site of the former [Bancroft's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bancroft's_School) from the [Drapers' Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drapers'_Company). On 20 May 1885, the Drapers' Court of Assistants resolved to grant £20,000 "for the provision of the technical schools of the People's Palace."[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 21 </sup> The foundation stone was laid on 28 June 1886, and on 14 May 1887 [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) opened the palace's Queen's Hall, as well as laying the foundation stone for the technical schools in the palace's east wing.

The technical schools were opened on 5 October 1888, with the entire palace completed by 1892. However, others saw the technical schools as one day becoming a technical university for the East End.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 37 </sup> In 1892, the Drapers' Company provided £7,000 a year for ten years to guarantee the educational side income.

### East London College



Part of the [Charterhouse Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charterhouse_Square) site

In 1895 [John Leigh Smeathman Hatton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Leigh_Smeathman_Hatton), director of evening classes (1892–1896; later director of studies 1896–1908 and principal 1908–1933), proposed introducing a course of study leading to the Bachelor of Science degree of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London). By the start of the 20th century, the first degrees were awarded and Hatton, along with several other professors, were recognised as teachers of the University of London. In 1906 an application for Parliamentary funds "for the aid of Educational Institutions engaged in work of a University nature", led to the college being admitted on an initial three-year trial basis as a school of the University of London on 15 May 1907 as East London College.



The ground-breaking wind tunnel built in the first-ever aeronautical department in the UK

Teaching of aeronautical engineering began in 1907, which led to the first UK aeronautical engineering department being established in 1909, boasting a ground-breaking [wind tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_tunnel) and creating what became (following the demise of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris)) the oldest aeronautical programme in the world.[^cite_note-11]

In 1910 the college's status in the University of London was extended for a further five years, with unlimited membership achieved in May 1915. During this period the organisation of the governors of the People's Palace was rearranged, creating the separate People's Palace Committee and East London College Committee, both under the Palace Governors, as a sign of the growing separation of the two concepts within a single complex.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 39–48 </sup>

During the First World War, the college admitted students from The [London Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Hospital_Medical_College) who were preparing for the preliminary medical examination, the first step in a long process that would eventually bring the two institutions together. After the war, the college grew, albeit constrained by the rest of the People's Palace to the west and a burial ground immediately to the east. In 1920 it obtained both the Palace's Rotunda (now the Octagon) and rooms under the winter gardens at the west of the palace, which became chemical laboratories. The college's status was also unique, being the only School of the University of London that was subject to both the Charity Commissioners and the [Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Education_\(United_Kingdom\)).

In April 1929 the College Council decided it would take the steps towards applying to the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council) for a [Royal Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Charter), but on the advice of the Drapers' Company first devised a scheme for development and expansion, which recommended among other things to re-amalgamate the People's Palace and the college, with guaranteed provision of the Queen's Hall for recreational purposes, offering at least freedom of governance if not in space.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 49–57 </sup>

### Queen Mary College

"Queen Mary College" redirects here. For other colleges, see [Queen Mary's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary's_College_\(disambiguation\)).

In the early hours of 25 February 1931 a fire destroyed the Queen's Hall, though both the college and the winter gardens escaped. In the coming days discussions on reconstruction led to the proposal that the entire site be transferred to the college which would then apply for a charter alone. The Drapers' Company obtained St Helen's Terrace, a row of six houses neighbouring the site, and in July 1931 it was agreed to give these over to the People's Palace for a new site adjacent to the old, which would now become entirely the domain of the college. Separation was now achieved. The Charter was now pursued, but the Academic Board asked for a name change, feeling that "East London" carried unfortunate associations that would hinder the college and its graduates. With the initial proposed name, "Queen's College", having already been taken by [The Queen's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_College,_Oxford) and "Victoria College" felt to be unoriginal, "Queen Mary College" was settled on. The Charter of Incorporation was presented on 12 December 1934 by [Queen Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_of_Teck) herself.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 57–62 </sup>

#### Under the charter



The Queen's Building

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the college was evacuated to [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), where it shared with [King's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge). After the war the college returned to London, facing many of the same problems but with prospects for westward expansion.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 75–85 </sup> The East End had suffered considerable bomb damage (although the college itself had incurred little) and consequently several areas of land near to the college site now became vacant. New buildings for physics, engineering, biology and chemistry were built on the new sites, whilst the arts took over the space vacated in the original building, now renamed the Queens' Building.

Limited accommodation resulted in the acquisition of further land in [South Woodford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Woodford) (now directly connected to [Mile End tube station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_End_tube_station) by means of the [Central line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_line_\(London_Underground\))'s eastward extension), upon which tower blocks were established. The college also obtained the Co-operative Wholesale Society's clothing factory on the Mile End Road which was converted into a building for the Faculty of Laws (and some other teaching), as well as the former headquarters of [Spratt's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratt's) Patent Ltd[^cite_note-12] (operators of the "largest dog biscuit factory in the world" – see [Spratt's Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratt's_Complex)) at 41–47 Bow Road, which was converted into a building for the Faculty of Economics founded by [Maurice Peston, Baron Peston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Peston,_Baron_Peston). Both faculties were physically separated from what was now a campus to the west.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 86–102 </sup>

From the mid-1960s until the mid-1980s the college proposed to link with the [London Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Hospital_Medical_College) and [St Bartholomew's Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bartholomew's_Hospital_Medical_College) with a joint facility in Mile End. A further link with both The London and St. Bartholomew's was made in 1974 when an anonymous donor provided for the establishment of a further hall of residence in Woodford, to be divided equally between Queen Mary College students and the two medical colleges.[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 103–117 </sup>

At the start of the 1980s changing demographics and finances led to a reorganisation of the University of London. At Queen Mary some subjects, such as Russian and [Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics) were discontinued, whilst the college became one of five in the university with a concentration of laboratory sciences, including the transfer of science departments from [Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_College), [Chelsea College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_College_of_Science_and_Technology), [Queen Elizabeth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_College) and [Bedford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_College_\(London\)).[^cite_note-mosssaville-10]<sup>: 117–130 </sup>

### 1989 to 2010



The arms of Queen Mary & Westfield College (prior to the merger with the medical schools), combining details from the arms of the two individual colleges. The triple crowns come from the arms of Queen Mary College, originating in the [Drapers' arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_the_Drapers_Company).

In 1989 Queen Mary College (informally known as QMC) merged with [Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_College) to form Queen Mary & Westfield College (often abbreviated to QMW). In the years that followed, operations became focused on the Queen Mary site, leading to the eventual sale of the Westfield site.

In 1990, The London Hospital was renamed The Royal London Hospital, after marking its 250th year, and a re-organisation of medical education within the University of London resulted in most of the free-standing medical schools being merged with existing large colleges to form multi-faculty institutions. In 1995 The [London Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Hospital_Medical_College) and [St Bartholomew's Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bartholomew's_Hospital_Medical_College) merged into Queen Mary & Westfield College to form an entity named Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry.[^cite_note-chronology-13]

In 2000 the college changed its name for general public use to Queen Mary, University of London; in 2013, the college legally changed its name to Queen Mary University of London.

The [VISTA telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VISTA_\(telescope\)) is a 4-metre class [wide-field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_of_view) [telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope) at the [Paranal Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranal_Observatory) in Chile that was conceived and developed by a consortium of UK universities led by Queen Mary University, costing approximately £36m.[^cite_note-VISTA-14]

The Westfield Student Village opened in 2004 on the Mile End Campus, bringing over 2,000 rooms to students and a huge array of facilities, restaurants, and cafes.[^cite_note-chronology-13][^cite_note-accommodation_landing_page-15]

The [Blizard Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizard_Building), home to the Medical School's Institute of Cell and Molecular Science opened at the [Whitechapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitechapel) campus in 2005. The award-winning building was designed by [Will Alsop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Alsop), and is named after [William Blizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Blizard), an English surgeon and founder of The [London Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Hospital_Medical_College) in 1785.[^cite_note-alsop_blizard_building-16][^cite_note-ICMS-17]

The year 2006 saw the refurbishment of The Octagon, the original library of the People's Palace dating back to 1888.[^cite_note-octagon_history-18]

In 2007 parts of the School of Law – postgraduate facilities and the Centre for Commercial Law Studies – moved to premises in [Lincoln's Inn Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln's_Inn_Fields) in central London. The Women at Queen Mary Exhibition was staged in the Octagon, marking 125 years of Westfield College and 120 years of Queen Mary College.[^cite_note-chronology-13]

In September 2009, the world's first science education centre located within a working research laboratory opened at the Blizard Institute of Cell and Molecular Science, hoping to inspire children with school tours and interactive games and puzzles.[^cite_note-19]

### 2010 to present

Queen Mary became one of the few university-level institutions to implement a requirement of the A\* grade at A-Level after its introduction in 2010 on some of their most popular courses, such as Engineering, Law, and Medicine.[^cite_note-requirements-20][^cite_note-2012_prospectus-21]

Following on from the [2010 UK student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_UK_student_protests), Queen Mary set fees of £9,000 per year for September 2012 entry, while also offering bursaries and scholarships.[^cite_note-2012_fees-22]

On 12 March 2012 it was announced that Queen Mary would be joining the [Russell Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Group) in August 2012.[^cite_note-RussellExpansion-23][^cite_note-24] Later in March, Queen Mary and the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) announced the creation of a strategic partnership, including research collaboration, joint teaching of English, history and computer science undergraduates, and the creation of eight joint post-doctoral research fellowships.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

In January 2013, Queen Mary established the world's first professorial chair in animal replacement science.[^cite_note-27]

From 2014, Queen Mary began awarding its own degrees, rather than those of the University of London.[^cite_note-28]

Queen Mary became the first Russell Group university to offer [Degree Apprenticeships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_Apprenticeships?action=edit\&redlink=1) and three years later was the first UK university to launch a social change degree,[^cite_note-29] the BSc in Chartered Manager Degree Apprenticeship.

In 2021, Queen Mary became the first UK university to receive the Platinum-level Engage Watermark from the National Co-ordinating Centre for Public Engagement.[^cite_note-30]

In 2024, Queen Mary University of London was affected by [pro-Palestinian campus protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pro-Palestinian_protests_on_university_campuses_in_the_United_States_in_2024) linked to the [Gaza conflict](16346266.md), forming part of a wider wave of student encampments and occupations at UK universities.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The university was reported to have been in contact with police during the demonstrations, alongside implementing security measures and disciplinary processes.[^cite_note-33] Queen Mary cited concerns over campus access, safety and operational disruption.[^cite_note-34]

## Campuses



Queen's Clocktower at the Mile End campus

Large white QMUL letters displayed on the lawn near the Queens Building at Queen Mary University of London

QMUL letters installation on the Mile End campus

The main Mile End campus contains the Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences, the Faculty of Science and Engineering, the Queens' Building, the main college library, the Students' Union, Drapers Bar and Kitchen, several restaurants, a number of halls of residences and a gym. The educational and research sites of the Arts Research Centre, Computer Science, the large Engineering building, G.E. Fogg Building, Francis Bancroft Building, G. O. Jones Building, Joseph Priestley Building, Lock-keeper's Graduate Centre, and the Mathematical Science Building, are all located within the Mile End campus.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-student_guide_2011/12-36]

The [Grade II listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_II_listed) Queens' Building is home to the Octagon. Built in 1887, the Octagon was originally the Queen Mary University of London library. It was designed by architect [ER Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robert_Robson) and inspired by the [British Museum Reading Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum_Reading_Room). In 2006, "brightly coloured leather bound books" were restored and reinstated to the bookshelves, along with "[busts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bust_\(sculpture\)) of famous [literati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual) looking down from the beautiful high domed ceiling."[^cite_note-37]



The [Statue of Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Clement_Attlee) at QMUL's Mile End Campus

The People's Palace is home to the Grade II listed [art deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) style Great Hall; this has a seating capacity of over 700 and standing of 1,000. It is complemented by 3 lecture theatres and a foyer.[^cite_note-38] The [night the votes were counted for the 1945 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election), the then local [MP](3776360.md) for [Limehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limehouse_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and [Leader of the Labour Party](19043368.md), [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee), was present at The People's Palace, where he learned the final result and received confirmation that he had become [Prime Minister](24150.md). A [statue of Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Clement_Attlee) that was originally displayed outside [Limehouse Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limehouse_Library) was, after its restoration, unveiled outside the Queen Mary library by [Peter Mandelson](216970.md) in April 2011.[^cite_note-39]

The [Whitechapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitechapel) campus encompasses [Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barts_and_The_London_School_of_Medicine_and_Dentistry), the Whitechapel Medical Library, the award-winning [Blizard Institute of Cell and Molecular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizard_Building), and The [Royal London Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_London_Hospital).

The West Smithfield campus of the School of Medicine and Dentistry, the West Smithfield Medical Library, the Wolfson Institute of Preventive Medicine, the John Vane Science Centre, the Heart Centre and [St Bartholomew's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bartholomew's_Hospital) are based in [Smithfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithfield,_London).[^cite_note-about-40]

The Centre for Commercial Law Studies and LLM teaching and postgraduate law research activities are based in [Lincoln's Inn Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln's_Inn_Fields) in [Holborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holborn).[^cite_note-about-40]

The Malta campus, situated on the island of [Gozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gozo), is part of Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry. Students taught at the Malta campus are offered the same curriculum as taught in London, for the MBBS Medicine and Medicine Foundation programmes.[^cite_note-41] Graduates of the Malta campus are not prioritised for UK Foundation Programme places, despite studying the same GMC-approved curriculum and receiving the same UK degree as London-based students.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### Harold Pinter Drama Studio

The Harold Pinter Drama Studio is the main teaching and performance space of the students and staff of the Department of Drama. On 26 April 2005, [Harold Pinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Pinter), who was to win the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md) later that year, gave a public reading and was interviewed by his official authorised biographer, [Michael Billington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Billington_\(critic\)), in the studio named for Pinter and located as part of the Faculty of Arts (Department of Drama, School of English and Drama) in the Mile End campus,[^cite_note-ME-44][^cite_note-HPDS-45] to celebrate its refurbishment.[^cite_note-recital-46]

## Organisation and administration

Queen Mary and Westfield College was established by Act of Parliament and the granting of a [Royal charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter) in 1989, following the merger of Queen Mary College (incorporated by charter in 1934) and Westfield College (incorporated in 1933).[^cite_note-finstat-47] The Charter has subsequently been revised three times: in 1995 (as a result of the merger of the college with the Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry); in 2008 (as a result of the Privy Council awarding the College Degree Awarding Powers; and in July 2010 (following a governance review).[^cite_note-finstat-47]

### Research



The G.E. Fogg Building

QMUL was ranked joint ninth in the UK among multi-faculty institutions for the quality (GPA) of its research.[^cite_note-54] In the 2014 [Research Excellence Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Excellence_Framework)[^cite_note-55] it was 20th for its Research Power, fifth in the UK for the quality of research outputs and the Linguistics department was ranked first in the UK.

In the UK [Research Assessment Exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Assessment_Exercise) results published in December 2008, Queen Mary was placed 11th according to an analysis by *The Guardian* newspaper[^cite_note-56] and 13th according to *[The Times Higher Education Supplement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_Higher_Education_Supplement)*,[^cite_note-57] out of the 132 institutions submitted for the exercise. *The Times Higher* commented "the biggest star among the research-intensive institutions was Queen Mary, University of London, which went from 48th in 2001 to 13th in the 2008 Times Higher Education table, up 35 places."[^cite_note-58]

Since 2007 Queen Mary University of London's *[Centre for Digital Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Digital_Music?action=edit\&redlink=1)* develops the [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeware) and [open source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) [*Sonic Visualizer*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Visualiser) for [audio analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_analysis), in particular for analyzing audio [spectrograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrogram).[^cite_note-59] With more than 100,000 downloads, it is the most famous software for its category.[^cite_note-60]

The QS World University Rankings 2019 ranked Queen Mary third in the world for research citations for the subject of medicine.[^cite_note-auto3-61]

The growth and strength of research at the college was rewarded with an invitation to join the [Russell Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Group) of research-intensive universities in the UK in 2012.[^cite_note-62]

The university is also a member of the [Screen Studies Group, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Studies_Group,_London). Other research highlights include an international team of scientists, led by astronomers at Queen Mary, discovering a planet orbiting [Proxima Centauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxima_Centauri), the closest star to the [Solar System](26903.md).[^cite_note-63] In 2018, a project involving Queen Mary researchers reached its goal of sequencing 100,000 whole genomes from [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service) patients.[^cite_note-64]

### Discoveries

Queen Mary University of London released the AMIGOS dataset, which contains neuro-physiological data of individuals and can be utilized for scientific reasons, to investigate human emotions.[^cite_note-65] The AMIGOS dataset has been used in hundreds of scientific studies on [signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_processing).[^cite_note-66]

### Libraries

Queen Mary's main library is located on the Mile End campus where most subjects are represented. It also has two medical libraries in Whitechapel and West Smithfield. Usual opening hours are 8 am to midnight. Since September 2017, the Mile End Library has been open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week during term time (including bank holidays).[^cite_note-67]

As members of a college of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), students at Queen Mary have access to [Senate House Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_House_Library), shared by other colleges such as [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) and [University College London](52029.md), in addition to library access throughout most of the individual University of London colleges, subject to approval at the given University.

### Partnerships

Queen Mary offers a joint degree programme with [Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_University_of_Posts_and_Telecommunications). The first of its kind to be approved by the PRC Ministry of Education, it is taught 50% by each institution in English, with staff from Queen Mary teaching part of the programme in Beijing. Students graduate with a dual Bachelor of Science degree in Engineering, meaning they receive two separate degrees, one from each university. The programmes are in Telecommunications Engineering with Management, e-Commerce Engineering with Law, and Internet of Things Engineering.[^cite_note-68] More than 9,600 students have graduated from the programme in the past 20 years.[^cite_note-69] In 2020 Queen Mary-BUPT opened a new Joint Teaching and Learning Centre that promotes excellence in teaching and learning as well as best practices for Transnational Education. In 2022, the QMUL-BUPT School Hainan was launched, establishing a similar program in Hainan, China.[^cite_note-70]. Queen Mary provides academic guidance for the Global [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) degree offered by the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London_\(worldwide\))'s distance learning.[^cite_note-UOL_QMUL_Global_MBA2-73]

## Student life

### Queen Mary Students' Union



Queen Mary Students' Union (QMSU) is the representative body for students of Queen Mary. The union is based at the Students' Hub. The union has a sub-division to represent students studying at the Faculty of Medicine and Dentistry, called Barts and The London Students' Association.[^cite_note-106]

QMSU is student-led and student-run, representing a community of home, international, postgraduate, and undergraduate students. The Union advocates for students’ academic interests, wellbeing, equality, and rights, while also creating spaces for community, leadership, and belonging. Through elected student officers, the student council, and representative networks, QMSU works to influence university policy and drive positive change. It supports hundreds of student societies and sports clubs, delivers campaigns and liberation work, and organises events that enhance student life on and off campus.

It is primarily led and directed by its elected Executive Officers, supported by a permanent staff team.

The Students' Union Hub replaces the previous office called the Blomeley Centre. It is named after a former president and VP Education, Laura Blomeley, who completed her term in office with terminal cancer.

#### Sports

&#x20;The **Merger Cup** is a series of annual sporting fixtures played between Queen Mary and Barts and The London (BL) sports clubs. The event has taken place since the merger of the two institutions in 1995. The results of a number of matches normally played on the same day are combined to determine the overall winner. Sporting fixtures include badminton, basketball, football, hockey, netball, rugby, squash, swimming, tennis and rowing.

The university has an alumni football club, Queen Mary College Old Boys FC, which was founded in 1989 and maintains close links with university. The club has three teams and competes in the Amateur Football Combination [^cite_note-107] The club's 1st XI won four league titles in five seasons, including two league and London Old Boys Cup doubles [^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

## Equality, diversity and inclusion

Students from more than 170 nationalities attend Queen Mary, approximately 41% of them from overseas.[^cite_note-115] According to [*The Times*' Good University Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankings_of_universities_in_the_United_Kingdom#The_Times/The_Sunday_Times), in 2021–2, 77% of Queen Mary students came from a black or ethnic minority background; this is the highest proportion of any university in the [Russell Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Group) and in the top 5 of any UK institution of higher education. *The Times* also cites QM as having one of the narrowest (i.e. best) black achievement gaps among UK institutions.[^cite_note-116]

In 2008, in recognition of the institution's commitment to advancing gender equality and diversity, the university achieved an [Athena SWAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena_SWAN) bronze award. This was elevated to a silver award in 2017, and the accreditation was renewed in 2022.[^cite_note-117]

In 2019, Queen Mary's Diversity and Inclusion Manager resigned, citing a "tick-box mentality" to equality, diversity and inclusion, as well as a "toxic" working environment that prioritised accolades over meaningful improvement; the departing manager further declared that Queen Mary was "institutionally racist."[^cite_note-118] Other academics have raised grievances regarding incidents of racism. In January 2020, a former lecturer who had brought the university to an [employment tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_tribunal) for direct racial and gender discrimination and harassment as a continuing act had her case dismissed on the basis that the alleged incidents had occurred after statutory time limits had expired.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

Queen Mary's [UCU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_and_College_Union) branch established an anti-racism working group in 2020 to “focus attention on institutional racism at Queen Mary University of London, but also to address questions of equality and diversity within the Union itself.”[^cite_note-121]

### QMUL and Leopold II of Belgium

In 2016, the university authorities removed plaques commemorating two visits by [Leopold II of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium) to the university site in 1887.[^cite_note-122] Leopold, a cousin of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria), had no links to Queen Mary other than a visit in April 1887 to Mile End and a subsequent visit in June 1887 to lay the foundation stone of the Octagon library, where the plaques were sited.[^cite_note-The_Daily_Telegraph-123] The brutal and exploitative nature of Leopold's rule over the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State) between 1885 and 1908, and his commemoration at Queen Mary, came under renewed scrutiny as of institutional links with colonial repression were re-evaluated in the wake of the global attention paid to the [Rhodes Must Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Must_Fall) campaign. However, the suitability of any commemoration of Leopold on university property had been raised as early as 2013.[^cite_note-124] In June 2016, the university's [Pan-African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African) society launched a petition for the removal of the plaques that celebrated a “genocidal colonialist” and were offensive to students from ethnic minority backgrounds. The society argued that their removal would help those students feel more “welcome, respected and integrated”.[^cite_note-125] By December 2016, the plaques had been removed from public view "as part of ongoing refurbishment work.” This attracted criticism from academics at other institutions who called the decision a "whitewashing" of history that "suggests a fear within the university authorities... of attracting negative attention."[^cite_note-The_Daily_Telegraph-123]

### UCU Strikes and Snitch Forms

In 2022, as part of ongoing national strikes by the [University and College Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_and_College_Union), more than 100 staff at Queen Mary University of London taking part in a national marking boycott were withheld 100% of their wages for 21 days.[^cite_note-126] Several other universities had threatened to dock full pay for action short of a strike, however Queen Mary University of London was the only institution to follow through on the threat. This led to the university being branded the worst university employer in the UK.

In the 2022/23 academic year, while strike action was continuing, the university continued to crack down and became the first university in the UK to implement a 'student snitch form'.[^cite_note-127] Students were encouraged to report striking staff, with threats of docking 39 days pay for those who did not reschedule missed teaching. Several senior academics resigned from the university in response to this action, citing a lack of trust leading to unbearable working conditions. Throughout this process, students and staff were sent very different messages in email updates by principal [Colin Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Bailey_\(educator\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), leading to increased division within the university.
