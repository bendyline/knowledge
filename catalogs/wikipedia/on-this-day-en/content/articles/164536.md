# Aviation accidents and incidents

For the Canadian film, see [*Air Collision*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Collision). For the American television series Air Disasters, see [Mayday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayday_\(Canadian_TV_series\)).



A [Bombardier CRJ900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_CRJ900), operating as [Delta Connection Flight 4819](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Connection_Flight_4819), after it crashed upon landing at [Toronto Pearson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Pearson_International_Airport) on February 17, 2025

An **aviation accident** is an event during aircraft operation that results in serious injury, death, or significant destruction. An **aviation incident** is any operating event that compromises safety but does not escalate into an aviation accident. Preventing both accidents and incidents is the primary goal of [aviation safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_safety).&#x20;

The most common causes of these accidents and incidents are technical or mechanical failures of the aircraft and [pilot error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_error). Adverse weather conditions, including turbulence, thunderstorms, [icing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icing_\(aeronautics\)), and low visibility, have also historically been major contributing factors in aviation accidents and incidents worldwide.[^cite_note-1]

Since civil aviation became widespread in the mid-20th century, hundreds of incidents have occurred every year, with most being minor and non-fatal.

## Definitions

According to Annex 13 of the [Convention on International Civil Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Convention_on_International_Civil_Aviation), an aviation accident is an occurrence associated with the operation of an aircraft, which takes place from the time any person boards the aircraft with the *intention of flight* until all such persons have disembarked, and in which (a) a person is fatally or seriously injured, (b) the aircraft sustains significant damage or structural failure, or (c) the aircraft goes missing or becomes completely inaccessible.[^cite_note-Annex13-2] Annex 13 defines an *aviation incident* as an occurrence, other than an accident, associated with the operation of an aircraft that affects or could affect the safety of operation.[^cite_note-Annex13-2]

A *[hull loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_loss)* occurs if an aircraft is damaged beyond repair, is lost, or becomes completely inaccessible.[^cite_note-3]

## History

One of the earliest recorded aviation accidents occurred on May 10, 1785, when a [hot air balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon) crashed in [Tullamore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullamore), County Offaly, Ireland. The resulting fire seriously damaged the town, destroying over 130 homes.[^cite_note-4]

The first accident involving a powered aircraft and the first involving an airplane occurred on September 17, 1908, when a [Wright Model A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Model_A) crashed at [Fort Myer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Myer), Virginia, USA. The pilot and co-inventor, [Orville Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orville_Wright), was injured, and the passenger, Signal Corps Lieutenant [Thomas Selfridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Selfridge), was killed.[^cite_note-5]



Accident Investigation Team from the Civil Aeronautics Board with Director, [Bobbie R. Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobbie_R._Allen) – about 1965

The period from 1958 to 1968 saw tremendous growth in aviation. Improvements in aviation safety and accident investigation procedures were rapidly advancing. In 1963, the Civil Aeronautics Board, under the leadership of then Deputy Director [Bobbie R. Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobbie_R._Allen), established the National Aircraft Accident Investigation School in Oklahoma City.

The [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO)'s third accident investigation division meeting, held in Montreal, Canada, in January 1965, laid the foundation for accident investigations throughout the world. The proposals were presented by the Director of the [Civil Aeronautics Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aeronautics_Board) Bureau of Safety, [Bobbie R. Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobbie_R._Allen), who headed the U.S. delegation. The U.S. formally adopted the proposals at the [White House](33057.md) on December 1, 1965.[^cite_note-6]

The top 10 countries with the highest number of fatal civil airliner accidents from 1945 to 2021 are the United States, Russia, Canada, Brazil, Colombia, United Kingdom, France, Indonesia, Mexico, and India.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The United Kingdom is noted to have the highest number of air crashes in Europe, with a total of 110 air crashes within the time period, and Indonesia is the highest in Asia at 104, followed by India at 95.[^cite_note-\:0-7]



[Computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) reconstruction of the moment immediately before the disaster: KLM Flight 4805 (right) is about to collide with Pan Am Flight 1736 (left). Part of the fog has been removed to give a clearer picture of the two planes.

The 1970s decade saw the most fatalities from civil aviation accidents and the highest total number of them.[^cite_note-b3aDeaths-8][^cite_note-b3aCrashs-9] The 1977 [Tenerife airport disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_airport_disaster), in which two [Boeing 747s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) collided, resulted in the largest loss of life in a single aviation accident, with 583 fatalities.[^cite_note-10]



JA8119, the Boeing 747 involved in Flight 123

The rate of fatal accidents declined significantly from the early 1960s to the 1980s, falling by nearly 50% in ICAO-member countries during that period of time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOsterStrongZorn199277-11] However, the 1980s saw many high-profile crashes. 1985 was the worst year for aviation in terms of death toll from accidents.[^cite_note-12] This includes the crash of [Japan Airlines Flight 123](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Airlines_Flight_123), which is the deadliest aviation accident involving a single aircraft, excluding intentional crashes.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The rate of fatal aviation accidents involving airliners continued to decline worldwide through the 1990s.[^cite_note-15] In the United States, [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_Deregulation_Act) generally did not impede this trend.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESavage199997-16]

## Safety

Main article: [Aviation safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_safety)



[Controlled Impact Demonstration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Impact_Demonstration) by [NASA](18426568.md) and the [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA), December 1984

In over one hundred years of implementation, aviation safety has improved considerably. In modern times, two major manufacturers still produce heavy passenger aircraft for the civilian market: [Boeing](18933266.md) in the United States, and the European company [Airbus](26220236.md). Both of these manufacturers place a huge emphasis on the use of aviation safety equipment, now a billion-dollar industry in its own right; safety is a key selling point for these companies, as they recognize that a poor safety record in the aviation industry is a threat to corporate survival.

Some major safety devices now required in commercial aircraft are:

- Evacuation slides, to aid rapid passenger exit from an aircraft in an emergency situation

  [^cite_note-17]

- Advanced [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics), incorporating computerized auto-recovery and alert systems

  [^cite_note-18]

- [Turbine engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbine_engines) with improved durability and failure containment mechanisms

  [^cite_note-19]

- [Landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear) that can be lowered even after loss of power and hydraulics

  [^cite_note-20]

Measured on a passenger-distance calculation, air travel is the safest form of transportation available. The BBC states, "UK airline operations are among the safest anywhere. When compared against all other modes of transport on a fatality per mile basis, air transport is the safest – six times safer than travelling by car and twice as safe as rail."[^cite_note-21]



[Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force) personnel recover the [flight data recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_data_recorder) of [Gol Transportes Aéreos Flight 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gol_Transportes_A%C3%A9reos_Flight_1907), which crashed on September 29, 2006.

When measured by fatalities per person transported, however, [buses](4146.md) are the safest form of transportation. The number of air travel fatalities per person are surpassed only by bicycles and motorcycles. This statistic is used by the insurance industry when calculating insurance rates for air travel.[^cite_note-22]

For every billion kilometers traveled, trains have a fatality rate that is 12 times higher than that of air travel, and the fatality rate for automobiles is 62 times greater than for air travel. By contrast, for every billion [journeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel) taken, buses are the safest form of transportation; using this measure, air travel is three times more dangerous than car transportation, and almost 30 times more dangerous than travelling by bus.[^cite_note-23]

A 2007 study by *[Popular Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mechanics)* magazine found that passengers sitting at the back of an aeroplane are 40% more likely to survive a crash than those sitting at the front. The article quotes Boeing, the FAA, and a website on aircraft safety, all of which claim that there is no "safest" seat. The study examined 20 crashes, not taking into account the developments in safety after those accidents.[^cite_note-24] However, a [flight data recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_data_recorder) is usually mounted in the aircraft's [empennage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empennage) (tail section) where it is more likely to survive a severe crash.

Between 1983 and 2000, the survival rate for people in U.S. plane crashes was greater than 95 percent.[^cite_note-Watt-25]

### Global Aeronautical Distress and Safety System

In an effort to prevent incidents such as the disappearance of [Malaysia Airlines Flight MH370](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_MH370), a new standard has been issued requiring all commercial aircraft to report their position every 15 minutes to air traffic controllers regardless of the country of origin. Introduced in 2016 by the [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO), the regulation has no initial requirement for any new aircraft equipment to be fitted. The standard is part of a long-term plan, called the Global Aeronautical Distress and Safety System (GADSS), which will require new aircraft to be equipped with data broadcast systems that are in constant contact with air traffic controllers.[^cite_note-26] The GADSS is similar to the [Global Maritime Distress and Safety System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Maritime_Distress_and_Safety_System) (GMDSS) used for maritime safety.[^cite_note-27]

### Aviation Safety Reporting System

The [Aviation Safety Reporting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Safety_Reporting_System) (ASRS) collects voluntarily submitted aviation safety incident/situation reports from pilots, controllers and others. The ASRS uses reports to identify system deficiencies, issue alert messages, and produce two publications, *CALLBACK*, and *ASRS Directline*. The collected information is made available to the public, and is used by the FAA, NASA and other organizations working in research and flight safety.[^cite_note-28]

### Bureau of Aircraft Accidents Archives (B3A)

The Bureau of Aircraft Accidents Archives (B3A),[^cite_note-29] formerly known as the Aircraft Crashes Record Office (ACRO), a non-government organization based in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), Switzerland, compiles statistics on aviation accidents of aircraft capable of carrying more than six passengers, excluding helicopters, balloons, and combat aircraft. ACRO only considers crashes in which the aircraft has suffered such damage that it is removed from service, which will further reduce the statistics for incidents and fatalities compared to some other data. The total fatalities due to aviation accidents since 1970 are 83,772. The total number of incidents is 11,164.[^cite_note-30]

According to ACRO, recent years have been considerably safer for aviation, with fewer than 170 incidents every year between 2009 and 2017, compared to as many as 226 as recently as 1998.[^cite_note-31]

The annual fatalities figure is less than 1,000 for ten of the fourteen years between 2007 and 2020, the year 2023 experiencing the lowest number of fatalities, at 246, since the end of World War II.[^cite_note-32]

2014 included the [disappearance of flight MH370](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH370) over the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and the [shootdown of flight MH17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH17) as part of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas). The total number of fatalities in 2014 was 869 more than in 2013.

### Annual Aviation Safety Review (EASA)

The [European Aviation Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Aviation_Safety_Agency) (EASA) is tasked by Article 15(4) of Regulation (EC) No 216/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council of February 20, 2008, to provide an annual review of aviation safety.

The **Annual Safety Review** presents statistics on European and worldwide civil aviation safety. Statistics are grouped according to type of operation, for instance, commercial air transport, and aircraft category, such as aeroplanes, helicopters, gliders, etc. The Agency has access to accident and statistical information collected by the [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md) (ICAO).[^cite_note-36] States are required, according to ICAO Annex 13, on Aircraft Accident and Incident Investigation, to report to ICAO information, on accidents and serious incidents to aircraft with a maximum certificated take-off mass (MTOM) over 2250 kg. Therefore, most statistics in this review concern aircraft above this mass. In addition to the ICAO data, a request was made to the EASA Member States to obtain light aircraft accident data. Furthermore, data on the operation of aircraft for commercial air transport were obtained from both ICAO and the [NLR Air Transport Safety Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLR_Air_Transport_Safety_Institute).[^cite_note-37]

## Investigation

See also: [Category\:Organizations investigating aviation accidents and incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Organizations_investigating_aviation_accidents_and_incidents)

Annex 13 of the [Chicago Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_International_Civil_Aviation) provides the international [Standards And Recommended Practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standards_And_Recommended_Practices) that form the basis for air accident and incident investigations by signatory countries, as well as reporting and preventive measures.[^cite_note-70] The [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md) (ICAO) is specifically focused on preventing accidents, rather than determining liability.

### Australia

In Australia, the [Australian Transport Safety Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Transport_Safety_Bureau) is the federal government body responsible for investigating transport-related accidents and incidents, covering air, sea, and rail travel. Formerly an agency of the [Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Infrastructure,_Transport,_Regional_Development_and_Local_Government), in 2010, in the interests of keeping its independence it became a stand-alone agency.[^cite_note-71]

### Brazil

In Brazil, the [Aeronautical Accidents Investigation and Prevention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_Accidents_Investigation_and_Prevention_Center) (CENIPA) was established under the auspices of the Aeronautical Accident Investigation and Prevention Center, a Military Organization of the Brazilian Air Force (FAB). The organization is responsible for the activities of aircraft accident prevention, and investigation of civil and military aviation occurrences. Formed in 1971, and in accordance with international standards, CENIPA represented a new philosophy: investigations are conducted with the sole purpose of promoting the "prevention of aeronautical accidents".[^cite_note-72]

### Canada

In Canada, the [Transportation Safety Board of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Safety_Board_of_Canada) (TSB), is an independent agency responsible for the advancement of transportation safety through the investigation and reporting of accident and incident occurrences in all prevalent Canadian modes of transportation – marine, air, rail and pipeline.[^cite_note-73]

### China

In China, the [Civil Aviation Administration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Administration_of_China) (CAAC) is solely responsible for all air investigations and safety inside the country after the split from the formal [CAAC Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAAC_Airlines).[^cite_note-74]

### Ethiopia

In Ethiopia, the Civil Aviation Accident Prevention and Investigation Bureau of the [Ethiopian Civil Aviation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Civil_Aviation_Authority) (ECAA), which is an agency of the [Ministry of Transport and Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_and_Communications_\(Ethiopia\)), conducts aircraft accident investigations in Ethiopia or involving Ethiopian aircraft.[^cite_note-75]

### France

In France, the agency responsible for investigation of civilian air crashes is the [Bureau d'Enquêtes et d'Analyses pour la Sécurité de l'Aviation Civile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_d'Enqu%C3%AAtes_et_d'Analyses_pour_la_S%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_de_l'Aviation_Civile) (BEA). Its purpose is to establish the circumstances and causes of the accident and to make recommendations for their future avoidance.[^cite_note-76]

### Germany

In Germany, the agency for investigating air crashes is the [Federal Bureau of Aircraft Accidents Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Aircraft_Accidents_Investigation) (BFU). It is an agency of the [Federal Ministry of Transport and Digital Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Transport_and_Digital_Infrastructure).[^cite_note-BFU-77] The focus of the BFU is to improve safety by determining the causes of accidents and serious incidents and making safety recommendations to prevent recurrence.[^cite_note-BFU-77]

### Hong Kong

The [Air Accident Investigation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Accident_Investigation_Authority) (AAIA) is responsible for investigating civil aviation accidents in Hong Kong, as well as those in other territories involving a Hong Kong-registered aircraft. It is led by Darren Straker, Chief Inspector of Accidents, and headquartered at [Hong Kong International Airport](248457.md). AAIA was established in 2018 in response to an ICAO directive instructing that member states maintain air accident investigation authorities that are independent of civil aviation authorities and related entities. Prior to 2018, accident investigation duties were held by the [Civil Aviation Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Department_\(Hong_Kong\))'s Flight Standards & Airworthiness Division and Accident Investigation Division.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

### India

Until May 30, 2012, the [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](23160430.md) investigated incidents involving aircraft. Since then, the [Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Accident_Investigation_Bureau_\(India\)) has taken over investigation responsibilities.[^cite_note-80]

### Indonesia

In Indonesia, the [National Transportation Safety Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Committee) (NTSC; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Komite Nasional Keselamatan Transportasi*, KNKT) is responsible for the investigation of incidents and accidents, including air accidents. Its aim is the improvement of transportation safety, not just aviation, in Indonesia.

### Italy

Created in 1999 in Italy, the [Agenzia Nazionale per la Sicurezza del Volo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agenzia_Nazionale_per_la_Sicurezza_del_Volo) (ANSV), has two main tasks: conducting technical investigations for civil aviation aircraft accidents and incidents, while issuing safety recommendations as appropriate; and conducting studies and surveys aimed at increasing flight safety. The organization is also responsible for establishing and maintaining the "voluntary reporting system". Although not under the supervision of the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport, the ANSV is a public authority under the oversight of the [Presidency of the Council of Ministers of Italy](273741.md).[^cite_note-81]

### Japan

The [Japan Transport Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Transport_Safety_Board) investigates aviation accidents and incidents. The [Aircraft Accident Investigation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Accident_Investigation_Commission) investigated aviation accidents and incidents in Japan until October 1, 2001, when the [Aircraft and Railway Accidents Investigation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_and_Railway_Accidents_Investigation_Commission) (ARAIC) replaced it,[^cite_note-82] and the ARAIC did this function until October 1, 2008, when it merged into the JTSB.[^cite_note-83]

### Malaysia

Established in 2016, the [Air Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Accident_Investigation_Bureau_\(Malaysia\)) is the main investigation body for aircraft accident/incident. Separate from [Civil Aviation Authority of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Authority_of_Malaysia) (CAAM) and [Malaysian Aviation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Aviation_Commission) (MAVCOM) that is the national aviation authority and commission that oversee aviation economy respectively. The AAIB operates from the ministry of transport headquarters in [Putrajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrajaya), and its [black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_recorder) laboratory situated in STRIDE, the ministry of defenses research institute. AAIB Malaysia is teamed by civilians and seconded [Royal Malaysian Airforce](1137609.md) senior officer and a group of pool investigators from [Malaysia Institute of Aviation Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Institute_of_Aviation_Technology)

### Mexico

In Mexico, the [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agencia_Federal_de_Aviaci%C3%B3n_Civil) (DGAC) investigates aviation accidents.[^cite_note-84]

### Netherlands

In the Netherlands, the [Dutch Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Safety_Board) (*Onderzoeksraad voor Veiligheid*) is responsible for the investigation of incidents and accidents, including air accidents. Its aim is the improvement of safety in the Netherlands. Its main focus is on those situations in which civilians are dependent on the government, companies or organizations for their safety. The Board solely investigates when incidents or accidents occur and aims to draw lessons from the results of these investigations. The Safety Board is objective, impartial and independent in its judgment. The Board will always be critical towards all parties concerned.[^cite_note-85]

### New Zealand

In New Zealand, the [Transport Accident Investigation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Accident_Investigation_Commission) (TAIC) is responsible for the investigation of air accidents.[^cite_note-86] *"The Commission's purpose, as set out in its Act, is to determine the circumstances and causes of aviation, rail and maritime accidents, and incidents, with a view to avoiding similar occurrences in the future, rather than to ascribe blame to any person."*[^cite_note-87] The TAIC investigates with accordance with annex 13 of the [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO)[^cite_note-88] and specific New Zealand legislation.[^cite_note-89]

### Poland

In Poland, [State Commission on Aircraft Accidents Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Commission_on_Aircraft_Accidents_Investigation) (Polish: *Państwowa Komisja Badania Wypadków Lotniczych*, *PKBWL*) is responsible for investigating all civil aviation accidents and incidents occurring in the country. Headquartered in Warsaw, the commission is a division of the [Ministry of Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Infrastructure_\(Poland\)). As of November 2022, the head of the PKBWL is Bogusław Trela.

### Russia

In Russia, the [Interstate Aviation Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Aviation_Committee) (IAC, MAK according to the original Russian name) is an executive body overseeing the use and management of civil aviation in the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md). This organization investigates air accidents in the former [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) area under the umbrella of the Air Accident Investigation Commission of the Interstate Aviation Committee.[^cite_note-90] There are active discussion to dismantling the committee, and in 2020, Armenia and Russia has signed on a joint agreement establishing the [International Bureau for investigating aviation accidents and serious incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_for_investigating_aviation_accidents_and_serious_incidents?action=edit\&redlink=1) (In [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Международное бюро по расследованию авиационных происшествий и серьезных инцидентов), designed to replace the committee and to act as upper body for investigation of aviation incidents and, subordinate to the [Eurasian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Union). The new body has been assigned duties to investigate serious accidents and incidents in accordance with the requirements of ICAO documents, ensuring independent investigation of accidents, cooperation and interaction between the parties in relation to investigating aircraft accidents, development and use of common rules and procedures for investigating aircraft accidents.

### Taiwan

In Taiwan, the [Taiwan Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Transportation_Safety_Board) (TTSB) is the independent government agency that is responsible for major transportation accident investigations. TTSB's predecessor was ASC, which was established in 1998. TTSB is under the administration of the Executive Yuan and independent from [Civil Aviation Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Administration_\(Republic_of_China\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). The TTSB consisted of five to seven board members, including a chairman and a vice chairman, appointed by the [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Republic_of_China). The managing director of TTSB manages the day-to-day function of the organization, including accident investigations.[^cite_note-91]

### United States

United States civil aviation incidents are investigated by the [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) (NTSB). ### Venezuela

In Venezuela, the organization tasked with investigating aviation accidents is the [Ministry of Aquatic and Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Aquatic_and_Air_Transport_\(Venezuela\)), more specifically the Directorate General for the Prevention and Investigation of Aeronautical Accidents.

## Retirement of flight numbers

Out of respect for the deceased and injured, airlines commonly retire the flight number associated with a fatal or major crash.[^cite_note-94] Examples in which airlines retired a flight number after a crash include [Malaysia Airlines Flight 370](42142305.md), [Malaysia Airlines Flight 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_17), [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007), [Air India Flight 171](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_171), and all four flights involved in the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md).[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] However, that is not always the case; for example, [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) still used flight numbers [723](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines_Flight_723) and [1288](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines_Flight_1288) as of 2013.[^cite_note-97]
