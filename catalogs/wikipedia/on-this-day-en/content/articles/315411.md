**Korean Air Co., Ltd.** (**KAL**; [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 대한항공) is the [flag carrier](465816.md) of [South Korea](27019.md) and its largest airline based on fleet size, international destinations, and international flights. It is owned by the [Hanjin Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanjin). Korean Air is a founding member of [SkyTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTeam) [alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_alliance) and [SkyTeam Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTeam_Cargo). As of 2024, it is one of the 11 airlines ranked [5-star airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skytrax#The_World's_5-star_Airlines) by [Skytrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skytrax),[^cite_note-8] and the top 20 airlines in the world in terms of passengers carried and is also one of the top-ranked international cargo airlines.

The present-day Korean Air traces its history to March 1, 1969, when the Hanjin group acquired government-owned Korean Air Lines, which had operated since June 1962.[^cite_note-\:1-9] Korean Air's international passenger division and related subsidiary **Korean Air Cargo** division together serve 126 cities in 44 countries. Its domestic division serves 13 destinations. The airline's global headquarters is located in [Seoul](19159283.md), South Korea. The airline had approximately 20,540 employees as of December 2014.[^cite_note-10]

The airline was, around 1999, known as "an industry pariah, notorious for fatal crashes" due to its poor safety record and [a large number of incidents and accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_incidents_and_accidents).[^cite_note-\:0-11] The airline's reputation has significantly improved by 2009 as it has focused investment on improving its safety record including by hiring consultants from [Boeing](18933266.md) and [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines).[^cite_note-\:2-12] In November 2020, it was announced that Korean Air would [merge](72100742.md) with competitor [Asiana Airlines](331239.md), but was switched to only acquire a major stake after the original merger plan was blocked by the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) for monopoly concerns. The acquisition was completed on December 12, 2024.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## History



A Korean National Airlines [Douglas DC-4](518813.md) at [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland) in 1953

### Founding

In 1962, [government of the Republic of Korea](4389689.md) acquired [Korean National Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_National_Airlines), which was founded in 1946, and changed its name to Korean Air Lines to become a state-owned airline. On 1 March 1969, the [Hanjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanjin) Group acquired the state-owned airline and it is the beginning of Korean Air.[^cite_note-History_EN-15][^cite_note-History_KR-16] Long-haul trans-pacific freight operations were introduced on April 26, 1971, followed by passenger services to [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md) on April 19, 1972.[^cite_note-FI-17]

### Expansion



Korean Air Lines logo from 1969 to 1984.



A Korean Air Boeing 707 at [Fukuoka Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Airport) in August 1987 with a previous livery. This aircraft is the only Boeing 707 ordered by Korean Air from Boeing, and was destroyed in 1987 as [Korean Air Flight 858](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Flight_858).

Korean Air operated international flights to destinations such as Hong Kong, Japan, Taiwan, and Los Angeles with [Boeing 707s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) until the introduction of the [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) in 1973. That year, the airline introduced Boeing 747s on its trans-Pacific routes and started a European service to Paris, France, using the 707 and then [McDonnell Douglas DC-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10). In 1975, the airline became one of the earliest Asian airlines to operate [Airbus](26220236.md) aircraft with the purchase of three [Airbus A300s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300), which were put into immediate service on Asian routes.[^cite_note-18] In 1981, Korean Air opened its cargo terminal at [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md).[^cite_note-History_EN-15] Since South Korean aircraft were prohibited from flying in the [airspace](353557.md) of North Korea and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) at the time, the European routes had to be designed eastbound from South Korea, such as [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport) \~ [Anchorage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Stevens_Anchorage_International_Airport) \~ [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Airport).

### Change to 'Korean Air'



Korean Air's logo from March 1, 1984 to March 10, 2025, still used on some aircraft.

A blue-top, silver and redesigned [livery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livery) with a new corporate "Korean Air" logo featuring a stylized [Taegeuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taegeuk) design was introduced on March 1, 1984, and the airline's name changed to **Korean Air** from Korean Air Lines. This livery was introduced on its [MD-80s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_MD-80) and [Boeing 747-300s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-300). It was designed in cooperation between Korean Air and [Boeing](18933266.md). In the 1990s, Korean Air became the first airline to use the new [McDonnell Douglas MD-11](382371.md) to supplement its new fleet of [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) aircraft; however, the MD-11 did not meet the airline's performance requirements, and they were eventually converted to freighters. Some older 747 aircraft were also converted for freight service. In 1984, Korean Air's head office was in the KAL Building on [Namdaemunno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namdaemunno), [Jung District, Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jung_District,_Seoul).[^cite_note-History_EN-15][^cite_note-History_KR-16][^cite_note-19]

### Checkered safety culture and record

Korean Air was once notorious for its abysmal safety record and high rate of fatal crashes.[^cite_note-\:4-20][^cite_note-Journal-21][^cite_note-See_2008_pp._177-22][^cite_note-\:7-23] In 1999, Korea's President [Kim Dae-jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Dae-jung) described the airline's safety record as "an embarrassment to the nation" and chose Korean Air's smaller rival, [Asiana](331239.md), for a flight to the United States.[^cite_note-24]

Between 1970 and 1999, Korean Air wrote off 16 aircraft due to serious incidents and accidents, with the loss of over 700 lives.[^cite_note-\:3-25] In the case of [Korean Air Flight 801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Flight_801), the [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) unanimously concluded that the airline's inadequate pilot training contributed to the pilot error that caused the fatal crash.[^cite_note-26]

In 1999, [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) suspended its code-sharing relationship with Korean Air, explicitly citing its poor safety record following the fatal crash of [Korean Air Cargo Flight 6316](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Cargo_Flight_6316). It marked the first time safety was explicitly cited as the reason for stopping a major code-sharing alliance by an airline.[^cite_note-27] Other partners including [Air Canada](145623.md) and [Air France](14998012.md) followed suit.

In 2001, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) downgraded South Korea's aviation safety rating and blocked South Korean carriers from expanding into the United States after the country and its carriers failed to improve sufficiently following a warning the previous year.[^cite_note-28] The move was driven by the country's lax oversight of its carriers including Korean Air.[^cite_note-29]

The rating has since been restored as the airline invested billions of dollars to improve safety, upgrade its fleet, install new technology, and overhaul its corporate culture including hiring consultants from [Boeing](18933266.md) and [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines).[^cite_note-\:2-12] In 2002, the *New York Times* noted that Korean Air had been removed from many "shun lists".[^cite_note-\:4-20]

### Early 21st century

On 23 June 2000, along with [Aeroméxico](84578.md), [Air France](14998012.md), and [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines), Korean Air founded major [airline alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_alliance), [SkyTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTeam) and [SkyTeam Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTeam_Cargo), founded on 28 September 2000.[^cite_note-\:3-25][^cite_note-30]

On 5 June 2007, Korean Air said that it would create a new low-cost carrier called [Jin Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Air) in Korea to compete with Korea's [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX) high-speed railway network system, which offered cheaper fares and less stringent security procedures compared to air travel. Jin Air started scheduled passenger service on July 17, 2008. Korean Air announced that some of its 737s and A300s would be given to Jin Air.

In mid-2010, a co-marketing deal with games company [Blizzard Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizzard_Entertainment) sent a [747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) and a [737-900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-900) taking to the skies wrapped in [StarCraft II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II) branding. In August 2010, Korean Air announced heavy second-quarter losses despite record-high revenue.[^cite_note-31] In August 2010, Hanjin Group, the parent of KAL, opened a new cargo terminal at [Navoiy International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navoiy_International_Airport) in Uzbekistan, which will become a cargo hub with regular Seoul-Navoi-Milan flights.[^cite_note-32]

In 2013, Korean Air acquired a 44% stake in [Czech Airlines](335703.md).[^cite_note-33] It sold the stake in October 2017. On 1 May 2018, the airline launched a [joint venture](538782.md) partnership with [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines).[^cite_note-34]

&#x20;In 2019, Korean Air began playing a [safety video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_video) with the [K-pop](629945.md) group [SuperM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperM).[^cite_note-35] It featured the song "Let's go everywhere", which was to be released as a single.[^cite_note-36] The airline also featured the group on a livery sported by a [Boeing 777-300ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER), with registration HL8010.[^cite_note-37]



A Korean Air Airbus A350-900

In 2023, Korean Air was certified a 5-star Airline by [Skytrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skytrax), an air transport rating organization.[^cite_note-38]

In 2025, a 10% shareholding was acquired in Canadian airline [WestJet](153851.md).[^cite_note-39]

### Nut rage incident

Main article: [Nut rage incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_rage_incident)

Cho Hyun-Ah, also known as "Heather Cho", is the daughter of then-chairman [Cho Yang-ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cho_Yang-ho). She resigned from some of her duties in late 2014 after she ordered a Korean Air jet to return to the gate to allow a flight attendant to be removed from the aircraft. The attendant had served Cho nuts in a bag instead of on a plate. As a result of further fallout, Cho Hyun-Ah was later arrested by Korean authorities for violating South Korea's aviation safety laws.[^cite_note-40]

### Merger with Asiana Airlines

Main article: [Merger of Korean Air and Asiana Airlines](72100742.md)

In November 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the [South Korean Government](4389689.md) officially announced that Korean Air will acquire [Asiana Airlines](331239.md).[^cite_note-M\&AwithKAL-41] The [Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport of the Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Land,_Infrastructure_and_Transport) will integrate subsidiaries [Air Busan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Busan), [Air Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Seoul), and [Jin Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Air) to form a combined low-cost carrier which will focus on regional airports in Korea.[^cite_note-42]

In March 2021, KAL announced that the merger with Asiana Airlines would be delayed as foreign authorities had not approved the deal.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] As of 2023, the deal has not been completed as essential countries have approached the deal with skepticism.[^cite_note-45]

On 12 December 2024, Reuters reported that Korean Air had completed the purchase of debt-laden Asiana Airlines in a deal worth 1.5 trillion won (USD 1.6 billion). The deal enables Korean Air to acquire 63.88% of the second-largest airline in the country, becoming the 12th largest airline in the world by international capacity.[^cite_note-46]

### Rebranding

On 11 March 2025, as part of its merger with Asiana Airlines, in which the latter brand will be fully absorbed in 2027,[^cite_note-47] Korean Air unveiled its first major corporate rebrand since 1984. The rebranding was done in partnership with design agency [Lippincott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippincott_\(brand_consultancy\))[^cite_note-KALBranding-48] and saw the existing "Taegeuk" symbol lose its blue and red color palette in favor of outlined monochromatic dark blue color of the same symbol.[^cite_note-49] It also replaced the wordmark "KOREAN AIR" written in a modified [Cooper Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Black) typeface with a sans-serif version named "Korean Air Sans" designed by [Dalton Maag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_Maag) for the same wordmark (in the case of aircraft liveries, the wordmark is just "KOREAN").[^cite_note-KALBranding-48] Lippincott notes that reinterpreting the Taegeuk symbol and removing it from the logotype enhances its visibility, and its calligraphic brush-strokes style adds elegance to the new identity.[^cite_note-KALBranding-48] Meanwhile, the new uniforms for Korean and Asiana employees signifying the unified "Korean Air" brand will be introduced in 2027.[^cite_note-50]

## Corporate affairs and identity



One of the airline's offices, the KAL building in [Seoul](19159283.md)

### Ownership

Korean Air is owned by [Hanjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanjin) Group, and it is majority controlled by Hanjin KAL Corporation. [Walter Cho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Cho), its current chairman and CEO, is the third generation of the family who controls Hanjin KAL to lead the airline. (Hanjin KAL's largest shareholder is [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines), at 14.90% ownership.) As of 5 June 2020, Hanjin KAL holds 29.27% of Korean Air shares.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

### Hubs and headquarters

[Incheon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport) Terminal 2 is Korean Air's international hub.[^cite_note-51]

Korean Air's headquarters (대한항공 빌딩/大韓航空 빌딩) is located on the grounds of [Gimpo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport) in [Gonghang-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonghang-dong), [Gangseo District, Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangseo_District,_Seoul).[^cite_note-일반현황_/_기업개요-52] The company also maintains a satellite headquarters campus at [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon).

Korean Air's other hubs are at [Jeju International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeju_International_Airport), [Jeju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeju_City) and [Gimhae International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimhae_International_Airport), [Busan](68115.md).[^cite_note-FI-17] The maintenance facilities are located in Gimhae International Airport. The majority of Korean Air's pilots, ground staff, and flight attendants are based in Seoul and Busan.

### Chaebol and nepotism

Korean Air has been cited as one of the examples of the South Korean "[chaebol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaebol)" system, wherein corporate conglomerates, established with government support, overreach diverse branches of industry. For much of the time between the foundation of Korean Air as Korean National Airlines in 1946 and the foundation of [Asiana Airlines](331239.md) in 1988, Korean Air was the only airline operating in South Korea.

The process of the sale of Korean National Airlines to Hanjin in 1969 was supported by [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee), the South Korean military general-turned [president](98630.md) who seized power of the country through a military coup d'état; and the monopoly of the airline was secured for two decades until his [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Park_Chung_Hee) in 1979.

After widening the chaebol branches, the subsidiary corporations of Korean Air include marine and overland transportation businesses, hotels, and real estate among others; and the previous branches included heavy industry, passenger transportation, construction, and a stockbroking business. The nature of the South Korean chaebol system involves nepotism. A series of incidents involving Korean Air in the 2000s have "revealed an ugly side of the culture within chaebols, South Korea's giant family-run conglomerates".[^cite_note-53]

### Hotel ownership

Korean Air owns three hotels: a KAL hotel in Seogwipo, Jeju, the Hyatt in Incheon, and a hotel/office building called the [Wilshire Grand Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire_Grand_Tower) in Los Angeles. This building in downtown Los Angeles houses the largest [InterContinental Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterContinental_Hotel) in the Americas in what is the [tallest building in Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallest_building_in_Los_Angeles).[^cite_note-54]

### Korean Air Aerospace Division



Korean Air Aerospace logo

Korean Air is also involved in aerospace research and manufacturing. The division, known as the Korean Air Aerospace Division (KAL-ASD), has manufactured licensed versions of the [MD Helicopters MD 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Helicopters_MD_500) and [Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_UH-60_Black_Hawk) helicopters, as well as the [Northrop F-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5)E/F Tiger II fighter aircraft,[^cite_note-55] the aft fuselage and wings for the [KF-16](11642.md) fighter aircraft manufactured by [Korean Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Aerospace_Industries) and parts for various commercial aircraft including the Boeing 737, Boeing 747, Boeing 777, and Boeing 787 Dreamliner; and the Airbus A330 and Airbus A380.[^cite_note-56] In 1991, the division designed and flew the [Korean Air Chang-Gong 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Chang-Gong_91) light aircraft. KAA also provides aircraft maintenance support for the United States Department of Defense in Asia and maintains a research division with focuses on launch vehicles, satellites, commercial aircraft, military aircraft, helicopters, and simulation systems.[^cite_note-57]

In October 2012, a development deal between [Bombardier Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Aerospace) and a government-led South Korean consortium was announced, aiming to develop a 90-seat [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) [regional airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airliner), targeting a 2019 launch date. The consortium would have included [Korea Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Aerospace_Industries) and Korean Air.[^cite_note-58] While this plan did not come to fruition, in 2019, Korean Aerospace Industries nevertheless decided to conduct a two-year study to assess the feasibility of taking the lead on building a turboprop airliner.[^cite_note-59]

### Fleet development

At the Association of Asia Pacific Airlines Assembly in 2018, Korean Air announced that it was considering a new large wide-body aircraft order to replace older [Airbus A330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330), [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400), [Boeing 777-200ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777-200ER), and [Boeing 777-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777-300) aircraft. Types under consideration for replacement of older wide-body aircraft in the fleet included the [Boeing 777X](41065722.md) and [Airbus A350 XWB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350_XWB).[^cite_note-109] At the [International Air Transport Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association) Annual General Meeting (IATA AGM) in Seoul, Chairman Walter Cho said Korean Air's wide-body order is imminent and it is considering an extra order of [Airbus A220](1567474.md) aircraft, including the developing version Airbus A220-500.[^cite_note-110]

In 2022, Korean Air was considering ordering a new freighter to continue to support the cargo demand worldwide. Chairman of Korean Air Walter Cho said KAL is considering two options.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Three years later, on 25 August 2025, the freighter choice was finalized with an order for eight [Boeing 777-8F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777-8F) aircraft.[^cite_note-\:8-113]

## Services

### Cabins

Korean Air currently offers two types of first class, three types of business (*Prestige*) class, and one standard economy class, with First Class seats installed on all of its Airbus A380-800s, Boeing 747-8Is, and Boeing 777-300ERs. The airline markets Business Class as "Prestige Class", with Prestige class seats in some aircraft equipped as doored suites.[^cite_note-123] The airline announced its introduction of Premium Economy in 2017.[^cite_note-124] The first aircraft equipped with premium economy marketed as "Economy Plus Class" was CS300 (Airbus A220-300).[^cite_note-125] The premium economy product was eliminated in 2019 due to discordance of service and profit loss, but is set to be reintroduced through a retrofit of Boeing 777-300ER aircraft with the older "Prestige Sleeper" business class product and marketed as "Premium Class" instead of "Economy Plus Class".[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-\:8-113] The airline also offers Economy Class.

### In-flight catering



A meal served in Korean Air's Economy Class

Korean Air offers a diverse in-flight catering service, providing passengers with a selection of both traditional [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_cuisine) dishes and [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_cuisine) meals. The airlines also provides [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine) cuisine on certain flights. The specific offerings can vary depending on the flight route and class of service. In Economy Class, passengers can enjoy Korean options like [bibimbap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibimbap), served with [gochujang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gochujang) and sesame oil, or bulgogi rice, while Western selections include grilled beef tenderloin and roasted cod. Chinese options include stir-fried snapper with chilli sauce. Prestige Class (Business Class) enhances these offerings with options such as tuna bibimbap, Korean spicy seafood stew and upgraded versions of Western meals with enhanced presentation and sides. Stir-fried ling fish with oyster sauce is available as one of the Chinese options.&#x20;



Bibimbap served onboard a Korean Air flight from Seattle to Seoul

First Class provides premium dishes such as Korean spicy pork, spicy beef short ribs with bulgogi sauce, and braised pork belly. Western options include veal chop with Madeira sauce, seared monkfish with orang teriyaki sauce, and sirloin steak. Previously, Korean Air offered instant cup noodles as a mid-flight snack in Economy Class. However, due to safety concerns related to turbulence, this option has been discontinued.[^cite_note-128] However, in Business and First Class cabins, the option to order instant noodles remains available. Alternative snacks such as pizza, sandwiches, corn dogs, and hot pockets are now available at a snack bar for Economy Class passengers.

In 2025, Korean Air collaborated with [Inha University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inha_University) and updated their special dietary meals by introducing new selections for low-fat, diabetic and low-calorie meals, bland meals, [gluten intolerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten_intolerant) meals, low-salt meals, and low-lactose meals.[^cite_note-129]

### In-flight entertainment

Korean Air's business class offers high-quality in-flight entertainment with large personal screens, varying by aircraft. The [Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) features 23.8-inch UHD monitors,[^cite_note-130] while the [Airbus A321neo](32104573.md) has 24-inch 4K screens with Bluetooth support. Other aircraft, like the [Boeing 777](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777) and 787-9, have 15.4-inch to 18-inch screens, depending on the seat type.



The In-Flight entertainment screen onboard a Korean Air Boeing 777-300ER

The entertainment system provides a diverse selection of movies, TV shows, dramas, music (including K-pop and classical), and interactive games. Some aircraft offer Bluetooth connectivity for wireless headphones and Wi-Fi for internet access.

## Incidents and accidents

Main article: [Korean Air incidents and accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_incidents_and_accidents)

Safety has significantly improved since the 1990s as the airline has made concerted efforts to improve standards in the early 21st century.[^cite_note-\:4-20] In 2001, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) upgraded Korea's air-safety rating while Korean Air passed an [International Air Transport Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association) audit in 2005.[^cite_note-\:7-23]

Between 1970 and 1999, many fatal incidents occurred. Since 1970, 17 Korean Air aircraft have been written off in serious incidents and accidents, with the loss of 700 lives. Two Korean Air aircraft were shot down by the Soviet Union for violating Soviet airspace, one operating as [Korean Air Lines Flight 902](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_902) and the other as [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007).

Korean Air's deadliest incident was [Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007), which was shot down by the Soviet Union on September 1, 1983. All 269 people on board were killed, including a sitting U.S. Congressman, [Larry McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_McDonald).

The last fatal passenger accident was the [Korean Air Flight 801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Flight_801) crash in 1997, which killed 229 people of the 254 people aboard including [Shin Ki-ha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Ki-ha), a South Korean parliamentarian.[^cite_note-GuamHome-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] The [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTSB) concluded that poor communication between the flight crew as the probable cause for the air crash, along with the captain's poor decision-making on the [non-precision approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_approach).[^cite_note-AAR-00-01_Final_Report-141][^cite_note-Seattletimes-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

The last crew fatalities were in the crash of [Korean Air Cargo Flight 8509](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Cargo_Flight_8509) in 1999 due to instrument malfunction and [pilot error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_error).[^cite_note-Aviation-safety.net-145][^cite_note-AAIB-146]

The last aircraft [write-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_loss) occurred in 2022, when [Korean Air Flight 631](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Flight_631) overran the runway at [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mactan%E2%80%93Cebu_International_Airport), [Philippines](23440.md) while attempting to land under poor weather conditions.[^cite_note-147]

In a 2023 "landmark decision", the state-affiliated Korea Worker's Compensation and Welfare Service ruled that the cancer death of a flight attendant was akin to an [industrial accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_accident). The plaintiff had flown for 25 years on routes to Europe and America, which exposed workers to more [cosmic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray) because [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field) is weaker over the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole). Korean Air said it monitors and limits individual radiation exposure to less than 6mSv a year. The plaintiff's attorney contends that the company uses an old measuring method. The ruling panel said that the method employed by Korean Air could have downplayed the extent of radiation exposure and that the flight attendant could have been exposed to over 100 mSv of radiation.[^cite_note-148]
