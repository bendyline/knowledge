**Derby** ([/ˈdɑːrbi/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/61/En-derby.ogg/En-derby.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/61/En-derby.ogg/En-derby.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*DAR-bee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [cathedral city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) and [unitary authority area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) on the [River Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Derwent,_Derbyshire) in [Derbyshire](71070.md), England. Derbyshire is named after Derby, which was its original [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town). As a unitary authority, Derby is administratively independent from [Derbyshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire_County_Council). The population of Derby is 275,344 (2025).[^cite_note-popstats-4]

The Romans established the town of [Derventio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derventio_Coritanorum), which was later captured by the Anglo-Saxons and then by the Vikings who made *Djúra-bý* one of the [Five Boroughs of the Danelaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Boroughs_of_the_Danelaw). Initially a [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town), Derby grew rapidly in the industrial era and was home to [Lombe's Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombe's_Mill), an early British factory and it contains the southern part of the [Derwent Valley Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derwent_Valley_Mills) World Heritage Site. With the arrival of the railways in the 19th century, Derby became a centre of the [British rail industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Great_Britain). Despite having a [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral) since 1927, Derby did not gain [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) until 1977.

Derby is a centre for advanced transport manufacturing. It is home to engine manufacturer [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Group_PLC) and [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) (formerly [Bombardier Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Transportation)) has a production facility at the [Derby Litchurch Lane Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Litchurch_Lane_Works); [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Manufacturing_UK)'s UK headquarters is located south-west of the city at [Burnaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaston).

## Toponymy

The Roman camp of [Derventio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derventio_\(Little_Chester\)) is considered to have been at [Little Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Chester)/Chester Green ([grid reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Survey_National_Grid) [SK353375](http://streetmap.co.uk/grid/435300_337500_115)), the site of the old Roman fort. Later, the town was one of the "[Five Boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Burghs)" (fortified towns) of the [Danelaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danelaw), until it was captured by [Lady Æthelflæd of Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%86thelfl%C3%A6d) in July 917, after which the town was annexed to the Kingdom of Mercia.[^cite_note-7]



The tower of [Derby Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral)

[^cite_note-8]

The [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) name *Djúra-bý*, recorded in [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) as *Deoraby*, means "village of the deer". However, the origin of the name Derby has had multiple influences: a variation of the original Roman name *Derventio* with pronunciation of the letter "v" as "b", becoming *Derbentio*, and later Derby, along with a link to the river Derwent – from the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) meaning "valley thick with oaks"[^cite_note-9] – which flows through the city, triggering a shortened version of *Derwent by*, meaning 'Derwent settlement'.

The town name appears as *Darbye* on early maps, such as that of [John Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Speed), 1610.[^cite_note-10]

Modern research (2004) into the history and [archaeology](18951655.md) of Derby has provided evidence that the Vikings and Anglo-Saxons would have co-existed, occupying two areas of land surrounded by water. The *[Anglo-Saxon Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle)* (c. 900) says that "Derby is divided by water". These areas of land were known as *Norþworþig* ("Northworthy"="north enclosure") and *Deoraby*, and were at the "Irongate" (north) side of Derby.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Derby)

### 16th–18th centuries

During the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War) of 1642–1646, Derby was garrisoned by [Parliamentary troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army) commanded by [Sir John Gell, 1st Baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Gell,_1st_Baronet), who was appointed Governor of Derby in 1643. These troops took part in the defence of nearby [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham), the siege of [Lichfield](159948.md), the [battle of Hopton Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hopton_Heath) and many other engagements in [Nottinghamshire](68049.md), Staffordshire and [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire), as well as successfully defending Derbyshire against [Royalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist) armies.

The first civic system of piped water in England was established in Derby in 1692, using wooden pipes,[^cite_note-13] which was common for several centuries.[^cite_note-14] The Derby Waterworks included waterwheel-powered pumps for raising water out of the [River Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Derwent,_Derbyshire) and storage tanks for distribution.[^cite_note-15] This was designed and built by local engineer [George Sorocold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sorocold).[^cite_note-16]



Statue of Charles Edward Stuart on Cathedral Green

During the [Jacobite rising of 1745](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_rising_of_1745), [Jacobite Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_Army_\(1745\)) troops led by Prince [Charles Edward Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Edward_Stuart) arrived in Derby on 4 December 1745, while on their way to [London](17867.md) to attempt to overthrow the reigning [House of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hanover). Stuart called at [The George Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Derbyshire_Blues#The_George_Inn) on Irongate (where the [Duke of Devonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cavendish,_3rd_Duke_of_Devonshire) had set up his headquarters in late November after raising [The Derbyshire Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Derbyshire_Blues)) and demanded [billets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billet) for the 9,000 troops under his command. The prince stayed at [Exeter House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_House) on Full Street, where he held a [council of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_war) on 5 December which decided to retreat. Stuart had received misleading information about a government army coming to meet him south of Derby. He abandoned his invasion at [Swarkestone Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarkestone_Bridge) on the [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), a few miles south of Derby. The prince, who on the march from Scotland had walked at the front of the column, made the return journey on horseback at the rear of the bedraggled and tired army.

[Shrovetide football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_football) was played at Derby every year, possibly from as early as the 12th century. The town was split into the [St Peter's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_Church,_Derby) and [All Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral) parishes, who fought to bring the ball from the Market Place to a goal within their own parishes.[^cite_note-17] There were several attempts to ban the game, described in 1846 as "the barbarous and disgusting play of Foot-Ball, which for a great number of years has annually disgraced our town". In that year the military were brought in and after the police cut the first ball to pieces, another ball was produced and the town's Mayor was "struck on the shoulder by a brick-bat, hurled by some ferocious ruffian, and severely bruised".[^cite_note-18] The Derby Football was banned in 1846, although it was played once more in 1870.[^cite_note-19]

### Industrial Revolution

Derby and Derbyshire were among the centres of Britain's [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution). In 1717, Derby was the site of the first [water-powered silk mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Industrial_Museum) in Britain, built by [John Lombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lombe) and [George Sorocold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sorocold), after Lombe had reputedly stolen the secrets of [silk-throwing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_throwing) from [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) in Italy (he is alleged to have been poisoned by the Piedmontese as revenge in 1722).[^cite_note-20]

In 1759, [Jedediah Strutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jedediah_Strutt) patented and built a machine called the Derby Rib Attachment[^cite_note-nationalarchives.gov.uk-21] that revolutionised the manufacture of hose. This attachment was used on the Rev. Lee's [Framework Knitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stocking_frame) Machine; it was placed in front of – and worked in unison with – Lee's Frame, to produce ribbed hose (stockings). The partners were Jedediah Strutt, William Woollatt (who had been joined in 1758 by John Bloodworth and Thomas Stafford, all leading [hosiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosier) in Derby). The patent was obtained in January 1759. After three years, Bloodworth and Stafford were paid off, and Samuel Need – a hosier of [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham) – joined the partnership. The firm was known as Need, Strutt and Woollatt. The patent expired in 1773[^cite_note-nationalarchives.gov.uk-21] though the partnership continued until 1781 when Need died.



[Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) visiting Derby in 1891 by [David Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Payne_\(artist\)), artist

Messrs Wright, the bankers of Nottingham, recommended that [Richard Arkwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Arkwright) apply to Strutt and Need for finance for his cotton [spinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_\(textiles\)) mill. The first mill opened in Nottingham in 1770 and was driven by horses. In 1771 Richard Arkwright, Samuel Need and Jedediah Strutt built the world's first commercially successful [water-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-power) cotton spinning mill at [Cromford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cromford), Derbyshire, developing a form of power that was to be a [catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalyst) for the Industrial Revolution.[^cite_note-Cromford_Mills-22][^cite_note-Eclectica-23][^cite_note-1st_water_powered_Cotton_Spinning_Mill_in_the_World-24]

This was followed in Derbyshire by Jedediah Strutt's cotton spinning mills at [Belper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belper). They were: South Mill, the first, 1775; North Mill, 1784, which was destroyed by fire on 12 January 1803 and then rebuilt, starting work again at the end of 1804; West Mill, 1792, commenced working 1796; Reeling Mill, 1897; Round Mill, which took 10 years to build, from 1803 to 1813, and commenced working in 1816; and Milford Mills, 1778. The Belper and [Milford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milford,_Derbyshire) mills were *not* built in partnership with Arkwright; they were all owned and financed by Strutt.

Other notable 18th-century figures with connections to Derby include the painter [Joseph Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Wright_of_Derby), known as Wright of Derby, who was known for his innovative use of light in his paintings and was an associate of the [Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy); and [John Whitehurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Whitehurst), a [clockmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clockmaker) and philosopher. [Erasmus Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Darwin), doctor, scientist, philosopher and grandfather of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin), moved to Derby in 1782 and founded the [Derby Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Philosophical_Society).

Derby's place in the country's philosophical and political life continued with Henry Hutchinson, an active member of the [Fabian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Society). On his death in 1894, he left the society an amount in his will which was instrumental in founding the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics).[^cite_note-25]

The beginning of 19th century saw Derby emerging as an engineering centre, with manufacturers such as [James Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Fox_\(engineer\)), who exported machine tools to Russia.



Bold Lane, 1894

In 1840, the [North Midland Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Midland_Railway) set up [its works in Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Works) and when it merged with the [Midland Counties Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Counties_Railway) and the [Birmingham and Derby Junction Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_and_Derby_Junction_Railway) to form the [Midland Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Railway), Derby became its headquarters. The connection with the railway encouraged others, notably [Andrew Handyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Handyside_and_Company), [Charles Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fox_\(civil_and_railway_engineer\)) and his son [Francis Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Fox_\(engineer\)).&#x20;

A permanent military presence was established in the city with the completion of [Normanton Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normanton_Barracks) in 1877.[^cite_note-26]

Derby was one of the boroughs reformed by the [Municipal Corporations Act 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Corporations_Act_1835), and it became a [county borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough) with the [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888). The borough expanded in 1877 to include [Little Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Chester) and [Litchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litchurch), and then in 1890 to include New Normanton and Rowditch. The borough did not increase substantially again until 1968, when under a recommendation of the Local Government Boundary Commission it was expanded into large parts of the rural district of [Belper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belper), [Repton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repton_Rural_District) and [South East Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Derbyshire_Rural_District). This vastly increased Derby's population from 132,408 in the 1961 census to 219,578 in the 1971 census.[^cite_note-27]



The Silk Mill in 1776 by Moses Griffiths

Despite being one of the areas of Britain [furthest from the sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_points_of_the_United_Kingdom), Derby holds a special place in the history of marine safety – it was as MP for Derby that [Samuel Plimsoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Plimsoll) introduced his bills for a "[Plimsoll line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plimsoll_line)" (and other marine safety measures). This failed on first introduction, but was successful in 1876 and contributed to Plimsoll's re-election as an MP.

### Railway engineering



Annual dinner of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers held in the carriage works of the Midland Railway at Derby in 1898. Samuel Johnson, the railway's Chief Mechanical Engineer, was the institution's president.

As a consequence of the Midland Railway having their headquarters in Derby, along with their [Locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Works) and [Carriage & Wagon Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Litchurch_Lane_Works), the railways had been a major influence on the development of the town during the Victorian period.

During the 20th century, railway manufacturing developed elsewhere, while in Derby the emphasis shifted to other industries. Even though it had pioneered the introduction of diesel locomotives, new production finished in 1966. Repair work gradually diminished until the locomotive works closed, the land being redeveloped as Pride Park. The only buildings remaining are those visible from Platform 6 of the station.

The Carriage and Wagon Works has been owned by [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) since 2021 and continues to build trains.[^cite_note-28] The [Railway Technical Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Technical_Centre) continues to house railway businesses; this formerly included the headquarters of [DeltaRail Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeltaRail_Group) (previously known as the [British Rail Research Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Research_Division)).

[Derby railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_railway_station) retains an important position in the railway network. [East Midlands Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Railway) operate [Derby Etches Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Etches_Park) depot while [Network Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_Rail) and [Rail Operations Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_Operations_Group) also maintain trains in Derby. On 21 March 2023, it was announced that the city was to be the headquarters of Great British Railways.[^cite_note-29]

Derby is also the headquarters of the Derby Railway Engineering Society,[^cite_note-30] founded in 1908 to promote railway engineering expertise both in the city and nationally.

### 20th century to present day

An industrial boom began in Derby when [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Limited) opened a car and aircraft factory in the town in 1907. In 1923, the [Midland Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Railway) became part of the [London, Midland & Scottish Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Midland_&_Scottish_Railway) with headquarters in London. However, Derby remained a major rail manufacturing centre, second only to [Crewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewe_Works) and [Wolverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverton_Works). Moreover, it remained a design and development centre and in the 1930s, on the direction of [Lord Stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Stamp), the [LMS Scientific Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMS_Scientific_Research_Laboratory) was opened on London Road.

In 1911, the Derby Wireless Club was formed by a group of local engineers and experimenters. It was to be the first radio or "wireless club" in the country.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The early activities of the club, (even through World Wars), pushed the boundaries of 'wireless' technologies at the time in England, and promoted it into becoming a hobby for many local folk. {Over later years, as radio technology progressed, the club transitioned to become the Derby & District Amateur Radio Society (DADARS), continuing to host meetings and events for radio hobbyists with all the new technologies, into the early 2020s.}

In World War I, Derby was targeted by German [Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin) air bombers, who killed five people in a 1916 raid on the town.[^cite_note-33]

All Saints Church was designated as a cathedral in 1927, signalling that the town was ready for city status.[^cite_note-34]

[Slum clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum_clearance_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the 1920s and 1930s saw the central area of Derby become less heavily populated as families were rehoused on new [council estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_housing) in the suburbs, where houses for private sale were also constructed. Rehousing, council house building and private housing developments continued on a large scale for some 30 years after the end of World War II in 1945.[^cite_note-lambert-35]

Production and repair work continued at the railway works. In December 1947 the [Locomotive Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Works) unveiled Britain's first mainline passenger [diesel-electric locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_locomotive) – ["Number 10000"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_D16/1). In 1958 production switched over to diesel locomotives completely. Meanwhile, the [Carriage & Wagon Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Litchurch_Lane_Works) were building the first of the [Diesel Multiple Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_Multiple_Units) that were to take over many of the services.

In 1964 the [British Rail Research Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Research_Division) opened to study all aspects of railway engineering from first principles. Its first success was in drastically improving the reliability and speed of goods trains, work which led to the development of the [Advanced Passenger Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Passenger_Train).

Derby was awarded city status on 7 June 1977 by [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) to mark the [25th anniversary of her ascension to the throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-36] The Queen presented the "charter scroll" or "letters patent" in person on 28 July 1977 on the steps of the [Council House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_House,_Derby) to the then Mayor Councillor Jeffrey Tillet (Conservative).[^cite_note-37] Until then, Derby had been one of the few towns in England with a [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral) but not city status.

Derby holds an important position in the history of the [Labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement) as one of two seats (the other being [Keir Hardie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keir_Hardie)'s in [Merthyr Tydfil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merthyr_Tydfil)) gained by the recently formed [Labour Representation Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Labour_Party#Labour_Representation_Committee_\(1900%E2%80%931906\)) at the [1900 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_United_Kingdom_general_election). The MP was [Richard Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bell_\(MP_for_Derby\)), General Secretary of the [Railway Servants Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Rail,_Maritime_and_Transport_Workers). Bell was succeeded in 1910 by [Jimmy Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Henry_Thomas) and he in turn by the distinguished [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) and [Nobel Laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate) [Philip Noel-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Noel-Baker) in 1936.

Despite its strategic industries (rail and [aero-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero-engine)), Derby suffered comparatively little damage in both [world wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_war) (contrast [Bristol](36741.md) and [Filton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filton)). This may in part have been because of jamming against the German radio-beam navigations systems (X-Verfahren and [Knickebein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knickebein), camouflage and decoy techniques ("[Starfish sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish_site)") were built, mainly south of the town, e.g. out in fields near [Foremark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foremark)[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]).

Derby has also become a significant [cultural centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_centre) for the [deaf community in Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafness_in_the_United_Kingdom). Many deaf people move to Derby because of its strong [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language)-using community.[^cite_note-40] It is estimated that the deaf population in Derby is at least three times higher than the national average, and that only London has a larger deaf population.[^cite_note-41] The Royal School for the Deaf on Ashbourne Road provides education in [British Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Sign_Language) and English.

To celebrate the city's heritage, mosaic and cast-iron stars are installed in the city's pavements. Names of significant people contributing to a variety of fields (arts, sport, science etc.) are etched into the stars as part of the [Derby City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_City_Council)'s program called Made in Derby. People who have made an impact include [Florence Nightingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Nightingale), [Joseph Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Wright_of_Derby), [Brian Clough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Clough), [Bess of Hardwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bess_of_Hardwick), [John Hurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hurt), [John Flamsteed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Flamsteed), [Philip Noel-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Noel-Baker), [Alice Wheeldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Wheeldon), and Rolls and Royce.[^cite_note-42] By using the Made in Derby app, the names can be scanned to learn more about them.[^cite_note-43]

### Local government

Main article: [Derby City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_City_Council)

By traditional definitions, Derby is the [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of [Derbyshire](71070.md); the county's [quarter sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_sessions) were held at Derby and [knights of the shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_the_shire) were elected there, with [County Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Hall,_Derby) on St Mary's Gate being built in 1660 to host such courts and meetings.[^cite_note-44] When elected county councils were established in 1889, [Derbyshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire_County_Council) initially used County Hall as its meeting place, but moved to [Smedley's Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smedley's_Hydro) in [Matlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matlock,_Derbyshire) in 1955.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

Between 1889 and 1974 Derby was a [county borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough), independent from the county council.[^cite_note-47] It then became a lower-tier district council with the county council providing county-level services between 1974 and 1997. On 1 April 1997, Derby City Council became a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority), regaining its independence from the county council.[^cite_note-48] On 7 July 2014, Derby's first ever Youth Mayor, Belal Butt (a student from Chellaston Academy), was elected by the [Mayor of Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Derby).[^cite_note-49]

Under [current local government restructuring plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England) it is planned that a Southern Derbyshire unitary authority, to exist from 2028, will include Derby as well as adjacent areas.[^cite_note-50]

### UK parliament

[Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) was a single United Kingdom Parliamentary constituency represented by two members of parliament until 1950, when it was divided into the single-member constituencies of [Derby North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_North) and [Derby South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_South). However, in 2010, the wards of Allestree, Oakwood and Spondon were moved to the new constituency of [Mid Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_Derbyshire), created for the 2010 general election. As of 2020, Derby is represented by three MPs.

## Geography

Derby is in a relatively low-lying area along the lower valley of the [River Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Derwent,_Derbyshire), where the south-east foothills of the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) adjoin the lowlands and valley of the [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent) to the south.[^cite_note-51] The city is bordered by four [national character areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_character_area), the Trent Valley Washlands to the south, the [Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire and Yorkshire Coalfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire_Coalfield) in the east, the South Derbyshire Claylands in the west, and the Derbyshire Peak Fringe in the north. Most of the flat plains surrounding Derby lie in the Trent Valley Washlands and South Derbyshire Claylands, while the hillier, northern parts of the city lie within the Derbyshire Peak Fringe and the Coalfields.[^cite_note-52]

The city is around 16 miles (26 km) from [Coton in the Elms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coton_in_the_Elms), the [farthest place from coastal waters in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_extreme_points_of_the_United_Kingdom).

### Derby urban area

The **Derby Built-up Area (BUA)** or **Derby Urban Area** is an area including Derby and adjoining built-up districts of Derbyshire,. The [Office for National Statistics](324029.md) defines an [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_United_Kingdom) as one which is built upon, with nearby areas linked if within 200 metres.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] It had a total population of 270,468 at the time of the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2011).[^cite_note-BUA-55] An increase of over 10% since the [2001 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2001) recorded population of 236,738;[^cite_note-56] comprising population increases since 2001 along with new minor residential areas, and larger sub-divisions. The Derby built-up area is considered to be most of the city, as well as outlying villages within the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_England) of [Amber Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Valley) and [Erewash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Erewash) which adjoin the city.

Because methods of measuring linked areas were redefined for the 2011 census, [Breadsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadsall), [Duffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffield,_Derbyshire) and [Little Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Eaton) were included. However, [Quarndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarndon) was not considered at the time to be a component as it was marginally too distant. It extended south to small adjoining estates in the [South Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Derbyshire) district, at [Boulton Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulton,_Derby#Boulton_Moor)/[Thulston Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thulston), [Stenson Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenson_Fields), and the Mickleover Country Park residential development (The Pastures)[^cite_note-57] within [Burnaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaston) parish. The urban area was bounded to the east by a narrow gap between [Borrowash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borrowash) and [Draycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draycott,_Derbyshire) (to the west of the [Breaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaston) urban area sub-division of the [Nottingham BUA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Urban_Area)). It was also close to other nearby urban areas to the north. The conurbation methodology was changed for the 2021 census to only amalgamate built up areas linked by a direct road connection,[^cite_note-58] this resulted in a number of the 2011 areas being dropped but Quarndon being added and an overall increase in population.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

### Green belt

Main article: [Nottingham and Derby Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_and_Derby_Green_Belt)

Derby has a green belt area defined to the north and east of the city, first drawn up in the 1950s, to prevent convergence with the surrounding towns and villages. It extends for several miles into the counties of Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire, covering much of the area up to Nottingham.

## Economy

Derby's two biggest employers,[^cite_note-83] [Rolls-Royce Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Holdings) and [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota), are engaged in engineering manufacturing. Other companies of note include [railway systems engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_systems_engineering) firm [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom), who manufacture railway [rolling stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock) at [Derby Litchurch Lane Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Litchurch_Lane_Works); First Source, who deal with much of [Sky's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_UK) telephone support; and Triton Equity, who took over Alstom's manufacturing plant for large power plant [boilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiler) and [heat exchangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_exchangers) in 2014.[^cite_note-84]



&#x20;[Derby Silk Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Silk_Mill) is part of the [Derwent Valley Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derwent_Valley_Mills) World Heritage Site, now the Museum of Making

[Derby power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_power_station) on Silkmill Lane supplied electricity to the town and the surrounding area from 1893 until its closure in 1969.[^cite_note-power_station-85]

From 1922, Sinfin Lane was the home of the 62-acre (250,000 m<sup>2</sup>) site of [International Combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Combustion), originally manufacturers of machinery for the automatic delivery of pulverised fuel to [furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_furnace) and [boilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boilers), and later producing steam-generating boilers for use in electrical generating plant such as used in power stations. In the 1990s the firm was bought by Rolls-Royce plc and then sold on again to [ABB Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABB_Group).[^cite_note-86]



[Derby Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Arena)

Derby was the home of [Core Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_Design) (originally based on Ashbourne Road), who developed the successful video game *[Tomb Raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_Raider)*. When Derby's inner ring road was completed in 2010, a section of it was named 'Lara Croft Way' after the game's heroine [Lara Croft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_Croft). Lara also has a Made in Derby star.[^cite_note-87]

One of Derby's longest-established businesses is [Royal Crown Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Crown_Derby), which has been producing porcelain since the 1750s.



Royal Crown Derby

The [Midlands Co-operative Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands_Co-operative_Society), a predecessor of [Central England Co-operative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_England_Co-operative), traced its origins to Derby Co-operative Provident Society which, in 1854, was one of the first co-operatives in the region.

Infinity Park Derby is a planned business park for aerospace, rail and automotive technology adjacent to the Rolls-Royce site in Sinfin. In December 2014, the government announced that the park would gain [enterprise zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_zone) status by being added to [Nottingham Enterprise Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Enterprise_Zone).[^cite_note-88]

### Retail and nightlife

Shopping in central Derby is divided into three main areas. These are the [Cathedral Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_Quarter,_Derby), the St Peters Quarter and the [Derbion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbion) shopping centre. The Cathedral Quarter was Derby's first BID ([Business Improvement District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Improvement_District)), and includes a large range of shops, boutiques, coffee shops and restaurants. It is focused around the cathedral and the area around Irongate and Sadler Gate. It includes the Market place, the Guildhall and Assembly Rooms along with the City Museum and the Silk Mill industrial museum.



Irongate, close to [Derby Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral)

The St Peters Quarter is Derby's second Business Improvement District, brought into effect in the summer of 2011. Its boundary with the Cathedral Quarter follows Victoria Street, beneath which flows the underground course of the [Markeaton Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markeaton_Brook). The quarter boasts a diverse range of retail shops, many of them, in Green Lane, Babington Lane, Osmaston Road and elsewhere, independent traders. St Peters Street, London Road and East Street also include a large choice of national retailers and pubs, restaurants, banks and offices. The quarter includes the historic [St Peters Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_Church,_Derby) and, on St Peter's Churchyard, the medieval [Derby School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_School) building. Nearby also is the Old Courthouse (former County Court).[^cite_note-89]



The [Derbion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbion) shopping centre

[Derbion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbion) is the city's main indoor shopping centre. It opened in 2007 as Westfield Derby after extension work costing £340 million, subsequently being sold to [Intu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intu) in March 2014.[^cite_note-90] It contains a food court and a 12-screen cinema ([Showcase – Cinema De Lux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showcase_Cinemas)) which was opened in May 2008. The development was controversial and local opponents accuse it of drawing trade away from the older parts of the city centre where independent shops are located. Some of these experienced a downturn in trade and some have ceased trading since the development opened leading to the "Lanes" project which eventually became the second BID and the formation of St Peters Quarter. In the centre itself, a combination of high rents and rising rates have made things difficult for smaller traders.[^cite_note-91]

The Friar Gate area contains clubs and bars, making it the centre of Derby's nightlife. Derby is also well provided with pubs and is renowned for its large number of [real ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_ale) outlets. The oldest pub is the [Grade II listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building) [Ye Olde Dolphin Inne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Olde_Dolphin_Inne), dating from the late 16th century.[^cite_note-EnglishHeritage-92]



Ye Olde Dolphin Inne

Out-of-town shopping areas include the Kingsway Retail Park, off the A38; the Wyvern Retail Park, near Pride Park; and the Meteor Centre, on Mansfield Road.

## Landmarks

[Derby Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral) tower is 212 ft (65 m) tall to the tip of the pinnacles. This has been home to a pair of breeding [peregrine falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon) since 2006,[^cite_note-93] monitored by four webcams.[^cite_note-94]



The interior of [Derby Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral)

[Derby Gaol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Gaol) is a visitor attraction based in the dungeons of the Derbyshire County Gaol, which dates back to 1756.

[Derby Museum of Making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Museum_of_Making) is housed in [Derby Silk Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Silk_Mill) and shows the industrial heritage and technological achievement of Derby, including [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Limited) [aero engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_engine), railways, mining, [quarrying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry) and [foundries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundries). The Silk Mill stands at the southern end of the 24 km (15 mi) stretch of the [River Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Derwent,_Derbyshire) designated a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2001. On 10 May 2022, the Museum of Making was short-listed for the 2022 Art Fund Museum of the Year award,[^cite_note-95] and in 2024 it was one of six museums to receive a special commendation in the European Museum of the Year Awards.[^cite_note-96]



[Florence Nightingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Nightingale) statue on London Road

[Pickford's House Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickford's_House_Museum) was built by architect [Joseph Pickford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Pickford) in 1770. It was his home and business headquarters. [Derby Museum and Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Museum_and_Art_Gallery) shows paintings by [Joseph Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Wright_of_Derby), as well as fine [Royal Crown Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Crown_Derby) porcelain, natural history, local regiments and archaeology. Pickford also designed [St Helen's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Helen's_House) in King Street.



[Pickford's House Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickford's_House_Museum)

The skyline of the inner city changed in 1968 when the inner ring road with its two new crossings of the [River Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Derwent,_Derbyshire) was built. The route of the ring road went through the [St Alkmund's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Alkmund's_Church,_Derby) and its [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_architecture) [churchyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchyard), the only Georgian square in Derby. Both were demolished to make way for the road, a move still criticised today. Thus the editor (Elizabeth Williamson) of the 2nd edition of [Pevsner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaus_Pevsner) for Derbyshire wrote: "...the character and cohesion of the centre has been completely altered by the replacement of a large number of C18 houses in the centre by a multi-lane road. As a traffic scheme this road is said to be a triumph; as townscape it is a disaster."

The [M1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway) passes about 10 miles (16 km) east of the city, linking Derby southwards to London and northwards to [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield) and Leeds. ### Air

[East Midlands Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Airport) is located about 15 miles (24 km) from Derby city centre. There was controversy concerning the airport's decision to prefix its name with *Nottingham* in 2004; this was due to its proximity to Derby, the fact that the airport is in [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire) and the traditional rivalry between its three nearby cities (Derby, Leicester and Nottingham).[^cite_note-101] In 2006, Nottingham East Midlands Airport reverted to its previous name.[^cite_note-102]

The airport is served by [budget airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_airline), including [Ryanair](262878.md) and [Jet2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet2), with services to domestic and European destinations.

[Derby Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Airfield), approximately 7 miles (11 km) south-west of the city centre, has grass runways targeted at [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation).

Derby is known by many locals as "Derbados"; a portmanteau of Derby and Barbados.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Also see [List of city nicknames in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_city_nicknames_in_the_United_Kingdom).

### Music

The [indie pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_pop) band [White Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Town), fronted by [Jyoti Mishra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyoti_Mishra), is from Derby, and his video "[Your Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_Woman)", features scenes from the city centre. "Your Woman" reached No. 1 on the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart) in January 1997.[^cite_note-British_Hit_Singles_&_Albums-106]

Founding member and songwriter of [Mercury Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Prize) nominated [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock) band [Maxïmo Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max%C3%AFmo_Park) and solo artist [Duncan Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Lloyd) is from Derby. His band hit the number 2 spot in the U.K. album charts in 2020, with the album *Nature Always Wins*.[^cite_note-107]

[Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) singer [Kelli Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelli_Young) (born 7 April 1982) is from Derby. She is best known as a member of the pop group [Liberty X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_X).

[Lucy Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Ward_\(musician\)) is an English singer-songwriter from Derby, England. She performs traditional [English folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_folk) songs as well as her own material. Three of her albums, *Adelphi Has to Fly*, *Single Flame* and *I Dreamt I Was a Bird*, have been critically acclaimed and have each received four-starred reviews in the British national press.

In rock music, the blues singer-songwriter [Kevin Coyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Coyne) came from Derby, as does the three-piece rock band [LostAlone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LostAlone), and indie/glam rock band [The Struts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Struts). The ska punk band [Lightyear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightyear_\(band\)) also hail from the city, naming their second album *Chris Gentlemens Hairdresser and Railway Book Shop* after a shop in Macklin Street.

The band [Anti-Pasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Pasti), whose debut 1981 album *The Last Call* reached the top 40 in the [UK album charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_album_charts), came from Derby. The band reformed in 2012 and again with an altered line up in 2014.

Derby band [The Beekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beekeepers) were signed to [Beggars Banquet Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beggars_Banquet_Records) between 1993 and 1998. Singer [Jamie East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_East) later went on to create entertainment website [Holy Moly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Moly) and present *[Big Brother's Bit on the Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother's_Bit_on_the_Side)*.

[Sinfonia Viva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinfonia_Viva) is a chamber orchestra based in Derby, presenting concerts and educational events in the city, across the East Midlands, and occasionally further afield.[^cite_note-108]

A full-scale programme of orchestral and other concerts was presented by Derby LIVE at the Assembly Rooms, though this is currently closed following fire damage in March 2014. The amateur classical music scene includes two choral societies, Derby Bach Choir and [Derby Choral Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Choral_Union); smaller choirs including the Derwent Singers and Sitwell Singers; and Derby Concert Orchestra. Derby Chamber Music presents an annual series of chamber music concerts at [Derby University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_University)'s [Multifaith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifaith) Centre. A series of organ recitals is presented every summer at [Derby Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Cathedral).[^cite_note-109]

The folk-music scene includes the annual Derby Folk Festival.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] Derby Jazz promotes a year-round series of performances and workshops.[^cite_note-112] Kaleidoscope Community Music includes Kaleidoscope Community Choir and Calidoscopio Carnival Drummers.[^cite_note-113]

Other music venues in the city include The Venue on Abbey Street, The Hairy Dog on Becket Street, Ryan's Bar in the St Peter's Quarter, The Flowerpot on King Street, and The Victoria Inn.

### Theatre and arts

Derby has had a number of theatres, including the [Grand Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theatre,_Derby) which was opened from 1886[^cite_note-114] until 1950. This replaced the earlier Theatre Royal.



[Derby Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Theatre)

After a lengthy period of financial uncertainty, [Derby Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Playhouse) closed in February 2008. It was resurrected in September of that year after a new financing package was put together but forced to close again just two months later because of further financial problems. The lease was later bought by [Derby University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_University) and the building was renamed [Derby Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Theatre). Along with the Assembly Rooms and Guildhall Theatre, it was operated by Derby LIVE, the cultural arm of [Derby City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_City_Council). In 2012 Derby University took over as sole operator of Derby Theatre; Sarah Brigham was appointed artistic director, and has been in post since January 2013.

[QUAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_QUAD) is a centre for art and film that opened in 2008. The building has two cinema screens showing independent and mainstream cinema, two gallery spaces housing contemporary [visual arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts), a digital studio, participation spaces, digital editing suites, artists studio and the [BFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI) Mediatheque. QUAD organises the annual Derby Film Festival, and the FORMAT international photography festival, held every two years at various venues throughout the city.

The Robert Ludlam Theatre, on the campus of [Saint Benedict Catholic School and Performing Arts College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Benedict_Catholic_School_and_Performing_Arts_College), is a 270-seat venue with a programme of entertainment including dance, drama, art, music, [theatre in the round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_in_the_round), comedy, films, family entertainment, rock and pop events and workshops. The theatre company Oddsocks is based in Derby and stages productions in the city and the surrounding area, as well as travelling the country.[^cite_note-115]

Déda, established in 1991, is the only dedicated dance house in the East Midlands region, acting as a local, regional and national resource for dance and aerial artists and contemporary circus. Déda houses a 124-capacity studio theatre, three dance studios, meeting room facilities and the CUBE café bar. It offers a weekly class programme and a year-round professional performance programme for children, young people and adults, and a community development programme. Déda now hosts a BA degree in Dance in partnership with the University of Derby.[^cite_note-116]

Derby Book Festival, first held in 2015, takes place in late spring/early summer, with events throughout the city.[^cite_note-117] An additional "Autumn edition" was first held in October 2019.



Derby Feste

Derby Festé[^cite_note-118] is a weekend [street arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art) festival held at the end of September every year. The first Six Streets Arts trail was in June 2012,[^cite_note-119] took place again in 2013 and will now be a biennial event. It includes strong input from the local History Network[^cite_note-120] which was awarded a Heritage Lottery grant to pursue its work on marking the 100th anniversary of World War 1.

The actor and [Bafta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafta) award winner [Jack O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_O'Connell_\(actor\)) is from Derby. [John Dexter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dexter) the theatre director and the actor [Alan Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Bates) were from Derby. [John Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Osborne) wrote his play *[Look Back in Anger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_Back_in_Anger)* in 1956 while living in Derby and working at Derby Playhouse.[^cite_note-osborne-121]

### Parks



The restored Grove Street Lodge and "Grand Entrance" at the northern end of the Arboretum

[Derby Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Arboretum), donated to the town by local philanthropist [Joseph Strutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Strutt_\(philanthropist\)) in 1840, was the first planned urban [public park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_park) in the country. Although it suffered from neglect in the 1990s, it has been renovated. It has been claimed to have been one of the inspirations for [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) in New York.[^cite_note-arboretum-122]

[Markeaton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markeaton_Park) is Derby's most used leisure facility.[^cite_note-123] Other major parks in the city include [Allestree Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allestree), [Darley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darley_Abbey), [Chaddesden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaddesden), [Alvaston Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvaston), [Normanton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normanton,_Derbyshire) and [Osmaston Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmaston_Park). Derby is believed to be one of the country's highest, if not the highest, ranking cities for parkland per capita. [Darley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darley_Abbey) and Derwent Parks lie immediately north of the city centre. Derby Rowing Club and Derwent Rowing Club are located on the banks of the river, where there is also a riverside walk and cycle path.

On 10 November 2021, Derby City Council approved plans for the UK's first large-scale [urban rewilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_rewilding) project, in Allestree Park.[^cite_note-124]

## Sport



[Pride Park Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Park_Stadium)

Derby gained a high profile in sport following the appointment of [Brian Clough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Clough) as manager of [Derby County F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.) in 1967. Promotion to the [Football League First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) was achieved in 1969, and County were champions of the English league three years later. Following Clough's resignation in 1973, his successor [Dave Mackay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Mackay) guided Derby County to another league title in 1975, but this remains to date the club's last major trophy; relegation followed in 1980 and top flight status was not regained until 1987, after which Derby spent a total of 11 seasons (1987–1991, 1996–2002, 2007–2008) in the top flight.[^cite_note-dcfc-125] Other former managers of the club include [Arthur Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Cox_\(footballer\)), [Jim Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Smith_\(footballer,_born_1940\)), [John Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gregory_\(footballer\)) and [George Burley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Burley). Former players include [Colin Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Todd), [Roy McFarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_McFarland) (who both later had brief and unsuccessful stints as manager at the club), Dave Mackay, [Peter Shilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Shilton), [Dean Saunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Saunders), [Craig Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Short), [Marco Gabbiadini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Gabbiadini), [Horacio Carbonari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Carbonari), [Fabrizio Ravanelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_Ravanelli), [Steve Bloomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bloomer) and [Tom Huddlestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Huddlestone). The club moved from its century-old [Baseball Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Ground) in 1997 to the new [Pride Park Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Park_Stadium).[^cite_note-dcfc-125] The club's most recent spell as a top-division ([FA Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Premier_League)) club ended in May 2008 after just [one season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007-08_in_English_football), during which the club won just one out of 38 league games and finished with just 11 points, the lowest in the history of the [Premier League](11250.md).[^cite_note-126]

There are three senior non-league football clubs based in the city. [Mickleover Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickleover_Sports) play at Station Road, [Mickleover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickleover), and are members of the [EvoStik Northern Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Premier_League_Premier_Division) (the seventh level of the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system)). [Graham Street Prims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Street_Prims_FC) and [Borrowash Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borrowash_Victoria_AFC) are both members of the [East Midlands Counties League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Counties_League) (level ten) and play on adjacent grounds at the Asterdale complex in [Spondon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spondon).



The [County Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cricket_Ground,_Derby)

[Derbyshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire_County_Cricket_Club) are based at the [County Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cricket_Ground,_Derby) in Derby and play almost all home matches there, although matches at [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield,_Derbyshire) were re-introduced in 2006. One of the designated [first class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_cricket) county sides, they have won the [County Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Championship) once, in 1936.

Derby has clubs in both codes of [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football). In [rugby union](25405.md), [Derby RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_RFC) play in [Midlands Division One East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands_1_East) (the sixth level of English rugby union) at their Haslams Lane ground. [Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) team [Derby City RLFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_City_RLFC) were formed in 1990 and compete in the Midlands Premier Division of the National Rugby League Conference. From 2008 they are ground-sharing with Derby RFC at Haslams Lane.

The city is represented in the [English Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Basketball_League) Division One by [Derby Trailblazers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Trailblazers), who play at the Moorways Sports Centre. They were formed in 2002 following the demise of [British Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Basketball_League) side [Derby Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Storm).



Derby-born [Melissa Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Reid)

Team Derby, based at [Derby Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Arena), won the inaugural [National Badminton League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Badminton_League) title in 2014–15. Local industrialist [Francis Ley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ley) introduced baseball to the town in the late 19th century, and built a [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Ground) near the town centre. Professional golfer [Melissa Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Reid) was born in Derby in 1987. She plays on the [Ladies European Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies_European_Tour),[^cite_note-letbio-127] and was a member of the victorious European Team in the [2011 Solheim Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Solheim_Cup).

[Arthur Keily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Keily) the [marathon](49132.md) [runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runner) and [Olympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympics) was born in Derbyshire in 1921 and has lived his whole life in Derby. ## Education

See also: [List of schools in Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Derby)

Like most of the UK, Derby operates a non-selective primary and secondary education system with no [middle schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school). Pupils attend infant and junior school (often in a combined primary school) before moving onto a secondary school. Many of the secondary schools have [sixth forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form), allowing pupils to optionally take [A Levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Level_\(UK\)) after the end of compulsory education. For those who want to stay in education but leave school, the large [Derby College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_College) provides post-16 courses for school leavers, apprentices and employer-related training. It has two main campuses: the [Joseph Wright Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Wright_of_Derby) in the centre of Derby, where its [A Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Level) courses are based, and the historical [Derby Roundhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Railway_Works), the college's vocational training hub, providing a centre for apprenticeships such as engineering, catering and hair and beauty. The college also works in partnership with schools across the county to provide vocational training opportunities for students aged 14 upwards. Training for companies is undertaken through its Corporate College.



[Derby College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_College)

Inside the state sector, there are 16 secondary schools. These are: [Allestree Woodlands School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allestree_Woodlands_School), [Alvaston Moor Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvaston_Moor_Academy), [Bemrose School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemrose_School), [Chellaston Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chellaston_Academy), [City of Derby Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Derby_Academy), [Da Vinci Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Vinci_Academy), Derby Cathedral School, [Derby Manufacturing UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Manufacturing_UTC), [Derby Moor Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Moor_Academy), [Derby Pride Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Pride_Academy), [Landau Forte College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_Forte_College), [Lees Brook Community School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lees_Brook_Community_School), [Littleover Community School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littleover_Community_School), [Merrill Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrill_Academy), [Murray Park School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Park_School), [Noel-Baker Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel-Baker_Academy), [Saint Benedict Catholic Voluntary Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Benedict_Catholic_Voluntary_Academy) and [West Park School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Park_School).

Outside the state sector, there are three fee-paying independent schools. [Derby Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Grammar_School) was founded in 1994 and was for boys only until 2007, when they accepted girls into the sixth form for the first time. They aim to continue the work and traditions of the former [Derby School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_School), which closed in 1989, one of the oldest schools in England. [Derby High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_High_School,_Derby) was girls-only for senior and sixth form and for girls and boys at primary level until 2019 where boys were accepted into Year 7 and Year 12.[^cite_note-130] As of 2023, the school is now fully co-educational, accepting both boys and girls in all years of school.

Derby has [special needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_needs) establishments including Ivy House School at the Derby Moor Community Sports College (which takes pupils from nursery to sixth form) and the Light House which is a [respite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respite_care) facility for children and parents. Allestree Woodlands School have a Hearing Impaired department, and Saint Benedict have an Enhanced Resource Base for pupils to access specialised support within mainstream schooling. There also a number of alternative provision schools, including [Derby Pride Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Pride_Academy).

The [University of Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Derby) has its main campus on Kedleston Road. There is another campus in north Derbyshire at [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton).



[University of Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Derby), main campus

In 2003 the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) opened a graduate entry medical school based at [Royal Derby Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Derby_Hospital). The university also has its School of Nursing and Midwifery there, having moved from its former home at the [London Road Community Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Road_Community_Hospital) in mid-2012.

## Media

The *[Derby Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Telegraph)* (formerly the *Derby Evening Telegraph*) is the city's daily newspaper. Crime writer Richard Cox set his first book around his own experience as a *Derby Telegraph* reporter in the 1970s.[^cite_note-131] The *[Derby Trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Trader)* was a free weekly newspaper that is no longer in print. [BBC Radio Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Derby), the BBC's local station for Derbyshire and [East Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Staffordshire), is based on St Helen's Street in the city and offers local, national and international news, features, music and sports commentaries. It is available on 104.5 FM and 1116 AM, on 95.3 FM in north and mid-Derbyshire and on 96.0 FM in the [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton) area, as well as being streamed on the internet. The [BBC](19344654.md) in Derby have their own local website for the area providing news, travel and weather information, as well as other features.

Local news and television programmes are provided by [BBC East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_East_Midlands) and [ITV Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Central). The city's television signals are received from the [Waltham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltham_transmitting_station) TV transmitter.

[Capital Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Midlands) (previously [Capital East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_East_Midlands) and [Ram FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_FM)) is the biggest commercial radio station in the city, broadcasting to Derby on 102.8 FM from the transmitter at Drum Hill, just outside the city. It broadcasts a Contemporary Hit Radio (CHR) format, with Top 40 chart hits aimed at the city's under-35s.

### Osnabrück partnership treaty



The Osnabrück Milestone,

[^cite_note-233]

&#x20;an obelisk erected in honour of the twinning agreement

Derby is [twinned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_towns_and_sister_cities) with [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck) in Germany. The partnership treaty between the two cities was signed on 17 February 1976.[^cite_note-234]

The twinning agreement with Derby was in the historical Hall of Peace in Osnabrück's Rathaus (town hall). Every year, Derby and Osnabrück each appoint an envoy who spends twelve months in the twin city. The envoy promotes the exchange of ideas between the two cities and acts as an educational and information officer to increase awareness of the twinning scheme. The envoy gives talks to local societies and schools, finds [pen friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_friend) and short-term host families during work placements, works to assist groups who want to get involved in twinning by identifying and approaching possible counterparts and plans the annual May Week trip.



Old steam crane, manufactured by "Henry J. Coles", in Derby. Villalba station, Spain.

There is an annual exchange between the wind bands of [John Port Spencer Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Port_Spencer_Academy), Etwall, and its twin school Gymnasium Melle in [Melle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melle,_Germany), Germany, [District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Germany) of [Osnabrücker Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCcker_Land). An exchange was established in 2009 between [Allestree Woodlands School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allestree_Woodlands_School) and the Gymnasium Angelaschule in Osnabrück. This exchange was originally based on a drama project by both schools in June 2009, which included performances in both cities with over 1600 visitors. It is now a language and culture exchange between the two schools, run by the German department at Allestree Woodlands School.

The exchange of envoys between two cities is very unusual. The envoy in Osnabrück changes every year and Osnabrück also sends envoys to Derby, [Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angers) and [Çanakkale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87anakkale). No other city in Germany participates in this exchange of envoys, and in Britain, only one other town, [Wigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigan), receives and sends an envoy.
