The **Laurence Olivier Awards**, also commonly known as the **Olivier Awards**, are presented annually by the [Society of London Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_London_Theatre) (SOLT) in recognition of the "world-class status of London theatre."[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The awards were originally known as the **Society of West End Theatre Awards**, but they were renamed in honour of the [English actor of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier) in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Laurence_Olivier_Awards).

The awards are given annually to individuals involved in [West End productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_theatre) and other leading [non-commercial theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_theatre#London's_non-commercial_theatres) based in London across a range of categories covering [plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_\(theatre\)), [musicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theatre), dance, opera and affiliate theatre. A discretionary non-competitive [Special Olivier Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_London_Theatre_Special_Award) is also given each year. The Olivier Awards are widely recognised as the highest honour in [British theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_drama), equivalent to the [BAFTA Awards](150200.md) for [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_United_Kingdom), and the [Brit Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Awards) for [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_United_Kingdom). The Olivier Awards are considered equivalent to [Broadway](725252.md)'s [Tony Awards](54741.md), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_France)'s [Molière Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moli%C3%A8re_Award), [Spain](26667.md)'s [Premios Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premios_Max), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Australia)'s [Helpmann Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helpmann_Awards), [Norway](21241.md)'s [Hedda Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedda_Award) and [Russia](25391.md)'s [Golden Mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Mask_\(Russian_award\)).&#x20;

Since inception, the awards have been held at various venues and theatres across London, from 2012 to 2016 at the [Royal Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Opera_House), before moving to the [Royal Albert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) in 2017. Television coverage was broadcast in [prime time](24973.md) on [BBC Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two) in 2026, following ITV, who in turn acquired the rights from 2013 onwards, with radio coverage by [Magic Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Radio_\(London_radio_station\)).

## History

The awards were established in 1976 by the Society of London Theatre as the Society of West End Theatre Awards and were designed by artist Tom Merrifield. The first ceremony was in December 1976 at the [Café Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Caf%C3%A9_Royal). In 1984, British actor [Laurence Olivier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier) gave his consent for the awards to be renamed in his honour and they became known as the Laurence Olivier Awards.[^cite_note-3]

## Judging

Every year, judging panels for theatre, opera, dance and affiliate shows are put together by the Society of London Theatre.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

For opera, dance and affiliates, each panel is made up of a mix of professional panellists (journalists, casting directors, arts administrators, publishers and other industry professionals chosen for their knowledge in the field) and members of the public who are passionate about London theatre. The panels first select the shows they consider most worthy of an Olivier Award nomination, then vote on a winner at the end of the judging period.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

For the theatre awards, a longlist is compiled by a panel made up of members of the public, and submitted to SOLT members to vote on. Members may still vote outside of the list at this stage, except for in the four Supporting Actor/Actress categories (as these each contain thousands of eligible performers). The members' votes are collated with those of the panellists to create the list of nominees. The nominees list is then voted on by both members and panellists to produce the winners.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Ceremony

### Hosts

Past hosts of the Olivier Awards ceremony include [Michael Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ball), [Imelda Staunton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Staunton), [Clive Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Anderson), [Gemma Arterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemma_Arterton), [Stephen Mangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Mangan), [Hugh Bonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Bonneville), [Sheridan Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan_Smith), [Lenny Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Henry), [Catherine Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Tate), [Jason Manford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Manford), [Hannah Waddingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham) and [Nick Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mohammed).

### Venues

The venue most associated with the Awards is [Grosvenor House Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosvenor_House_Hotel), which has housed the after-show reception nine times and hosted the whole event on four further occasions. As well as at the Grosvenor, the presentations have been held at the [Albery Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward_Theatre) (now Noël Coward), [Café Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Caf%C3%A9_Royal), [Dominion Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Theatre), [London Palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Palladium), [Lyceum Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyceum_Theatre,_London), [Park Lane Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Hilton_on_Park_Lane), [Piccadilly Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccadilly_Theatre), [Royal National Theatre Olivier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre), [Royalty Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalty_Theatre) (now Peacock), [Shaftesbury Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaftesbury_Theatre), [Theatre Royal Drury Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Royal_Drury_Lane) and [Victoria Palace Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Palace_Theatre).[^cite_note-playbill-5]

The awards ceremony was held at the [Royal Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Opera_House) from 2012 to 2016, moving to the [Royal Albert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) in 2017.[^cite_note-Who_Won_at_The_Olivier_Awards_2017-6][^cite_note-7]

## Broadcast

The first Laurence Olivier Awards to be broadcast on television was the 1981 ceremony, which was broadcast on [BBC1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_One). This continued until 1992, before a switch to [BBC2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two) until 2003.[^cite_note-8] The awards ceremony was then only broadcast on radio until 2011, when the BBC broadcast live interactive red-button coverage of the event,[^cite_note-9] while [Paul Gambaccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gambaccini) presented a programme on [BBC Radio 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_2) with live coverage and interviews.[^cite_note-playbill-5] The same coverage followed in 2012 before [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_\(TV_network\)) secured the broadcast rights, which saw the return of the Olivier Awards to mainstream television in 2013.[^cite_note-10] This has continued in recent years, and the ceremony has also been broadcast on [Magic Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_\(UK_radio_station\)). It returned to BBC Two and BBC Radio 2 in 2026.[^cite_note-11]

In 2019, the six cast members of *[Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_\(musical\))* were jointly nominated for Best Actress in a Supporting Role in a Musical for their performances as the six wives of Henry VIII.

- In 2018, [Billie Piper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Piper) became the first, and so far only, actor to have won all six of the currently available UK Theatre Best Actress awards for a single performance: [Standard Theatre Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Theatre_Awards), [WhatsOnStage Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsOnStage_Awards), [Critics' Circle Theatre Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Circle_Theatre_Award), Broadway UK Theatre Awards, [Glamour Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamour_Awards) and Laurence Olivier Theatre Awards. This accolade was achieved by her performance in *[Yerma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerma)*, which was hailed as "the performance of the decade", "shattering, exhausting, earthquaking" and "unbearably harrowing".
