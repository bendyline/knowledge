# Home port

For other uses, see [Home Port (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Port_\(disambiguation\)).



Port of registry [Port aux Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_aux_Fran%C3%A7ais) lettered beneath the ship name on the stern

A [vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_vessel)'s **home port** is the port at which it is based, which may not be the same as its **port of registry**[^cite_note-1] shown on its [registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_registration) documents[^cite_note-2] and lettered on the [stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern) of the ship's [hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_\(watercraft\)).[^cite_note-3] In the [cruise industry](314855.md) the term "home port" is also often used in reference to the port in which a ship will take on/change over most of its passengers while taking on stores, supplies and fuel.[^cite_note-4]

## Navy

In a [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy), a ship's *home port* is the port best suited to provide maintenance and restock weaponry particular to ships of that class and build. On conclusion of a [tour of duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_of_duty), a [combat vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_vessel) returning to port will usually return to its *home port*. A single home port also makes it easier for family to visit sailors [on leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_\(military\)).
