**Buggenhout** (Dutch pronunciation: [\[ˈbʏɣə(n)ɦʌut\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5a/Nl-Buggenhout.ogg/Nl-Buggenhout.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5a/Nl-Buggenhout.ogg/Nl-Buggenhout.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Belgium) in the Belgian province of [East Flanders](152405.md) in the [Denderstreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denderstreek). The municipality comprises the towns of Buggenhout proper, [Briel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briel_\(parish\)), [Opdorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opdorp), and [Opstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opstal?action=edit\&redlink=1). In 2021, Buggenhout had a total population of 14,689. The total area is 25.25 square kilometres.[^cite_note-stat-2]

Buggenhout is also known for its forest, the "Buggenhoutbos", which is the largest forest of East Flanders.[^cite_note-3]

The municipality is 20 km north-west of [Brussels](3708.md) city centre. The geographic centre of Flanders lies in Opdorp.[^cite_note-4]

Buggenhout has two breweries, [Bosteels Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosteels_Brewery) (known for the "Tripel Karmeliet", "Deus" (a champagne beer), "[Kwak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauwel_Kwak)") and De Landtsheer, now called [Brouwerij Malheur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brouwerij_Malheur) (known for "Malheur").

## Etymology

The [original meaning of the name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placename_etymology) is "[beechwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech)" or "beech forest" (in modern Dutch: *Beukenhout*). The name is derived from the [Middle Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Dutch) *Bucghenhout* from [Old Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dutch) *Bukenholt*. *Buken* comes from [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic) \**bokjon* (Modern Dutch: *beuken*, Old English: *becen* (beeches)), *holt* from \**hulta* (Modern Dutch: *hout (bos)*, Old English: *holt* (forest)). Buggenhout still contains a forested area called *Buggenhoutbos*.[^cite_note-invent-5]

## History

The village was first mention in 1125. It used to belong to the [Duchy of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant). Due to internal feuds, the area was confiscated. The forest was declared [crown land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_land), and the village of Buggenhout became two *[heerlijkheden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heerlijkheid)* (landed estates). After the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), Buggenhout was transferred to East Flanders.[^cite_note-invent-5]
