**Maria Osmarina Marina da Silva Vaz de Lima**[^cite_note-2] (born **Maria Osmarina da Silva**; 8 February 1958), known as **Marina Silva**, is a Brazilian politician and [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalist), currently serving as [Minister of the Environment and Climate Change](40599788.md), a position she previously held from 2003 to 2008. She is the founder and former spokeswoman of the [Sustainability Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability_Network) (REDE). A former [senator](6132308.md) for the state of [Acre](229497.md) between 1995 and 2011, she has been a [federal deputy](1589485.md) for the state of [São Paulo](229422.md) since 2023. She ran unsuccessfully for president in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Brazilian_general_election), [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Brazilian_general_election) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Brazilian_general_election).

Silva was a member of the [PT](224622.md) until 2009, and served as a senator before becoming Minister of the Environment in 2003. She ran for president in the [2010 Brazilian elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Brazilian_presidential_election) as the candidate for the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Brazil\)), coming in 3rd with 19% of the first-round vote.[^cite_note-3] In April 2014, [Eduardo Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Campos) announced his candidacy for the [2014 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Brazilian_general_election), naming Marina Silva as his vice presidential candidate.[^cite_note-CamposMarinaElec-4] After Campos's death in a [plane crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Santos_Cessna_Citation_crash) on August, she was selected to run as the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Socialist_Party)'s candidate for the presidency, winning 21% of the vote and coming in 3rd.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-bbc.com-6] She again ran for president in the [2018 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Brazilian_general_election), this time as the nominee for the [Sustainability Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability_Network), finishing in 8th place with 1% of the vote.

Silva has won a number of awards from US and international organizations in recognition of her environmental activism. In 2010, she, along with [Cécile Duflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9cile_Duflot), [Monica Frassoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Frassoni), [Elizabeth May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_May) and [Renate Künast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_K%C3%BCnast), were named by *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)* magazine to its list of top global thinkers[^cite_note-7] for taking [Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_politics) mainstream. She was one of eight people chosen to carry the Olympic flag for the opening ceremonies of the [2012 London Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-8]

## Early life



Marina Silva in [Xapuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xapuri), Acre



Silva in the [Amazon rainforest](48139.md), Acre, with a picture of [Chico Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_Mendes) in the background

Marina Silva was born **Maria Osmarina da Silva** in the small village of Breu Velho, 70 km outside [Rio Branco, Acre](818818.md). Silva is a descendant of [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) and [black African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) ancestors in both her maternal and paternal lines.[^cite_note-9] She was one of eleven children in a community of [rubber tappers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_tapping) on the *Bagaço* rubber tree plantation ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) *Seringal Bagaço*), in the western state of [Acre](229497.md). Growing up, she survived five bouts of malaria in addition to cases of hepatitis and metal poisoning.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

At age 16, young Marina moved to the state capital, Rio Branco, to study and receive treatment for hepatitis. She was taken in by nuns in a convent and received a Catholic education. There, she became the first person in her family to learn to read and write. After leaving the convent, she went to work as a housemaid in exchange for lodging.[^cite_note-12] She completed her undergraduate degree in history from the [Federal University of Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Acre) at 26 and became increasingly politically active. In 1984 Silva helped create Acre's first workers' union.[^cite_note-Phillips-13]

## Early career

She led demonstrations called *empates* with [Chico Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_Mendes) to warn against [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and the outplacement of forest communities from their traditional locations.[^cite_note-14]

She helped [Chico Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_Mendes) to lead the trade union movement, being elected as councillor of Rio Branco in 1988 for her first mandate in a public office.

### Senate

In 1994, Silva was the first rubber tapper ever elected to the [Federal Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Brazil). As a native Amazonian and a senator, she built support for environmental protection of the reserves as well as for social justice and [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development) in the [Amazon region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_region).[^cite_note-15]

## First Lula government

A member of the [Workers' Party](224622.md), Marina Silva was appointed Environment Minister by [Lula](99328.md) in his first term.

Also in 2005, Silva established her main lines of action for the next two years: social participation, [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development), creation of a National Environmental System, and an Integrated Environmental Policy. As she said, "Our ministry is new. It's only 13 years old, and it needs to be rebuilt".[^cite_note-IPS-16]

### Effect

Deforestation decreased by 59% from 2004 to 2007, during which she implemented an integrated government policy. The policy, also known as, "The Action Plan For The Prevention and Control of Deforestation in the Legal Amazon,"[^cite_note-17] It simultaneously fostered sustainable development, favored territorial zoning, and attached greater value to standing forests. It also incorporated elements from international conventions and documents.[^cite_note-Embassy_of_Brazil_in_London-18] "All of this demonstrates that, when there is integrated planning and effort, it is truly possible to change the picture," Silva said in a statement to the Embassy of Brazil in London.[^cite_note-Embassy_of_Brazil_in_London-18]

In 2005, Silva was confronted by Paulo Adário, coordinator of [Greenpeace](12233.md) Brazil, over her environmental actions during her tenure in the ministry. Since her tenure began, Silva, together with the [Federal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Police_\(Brazil\)), the [Brazilian Army](1047559.md) and the [Federal Highway Police](14512223.md), performed 32 operations against illegal [deforestation in the Amazon](19732090.md). However, Adário claims that his organization monitors the Amazon region and that only one such operation was conducted in October 2004, in the town of [Itaituba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaituba), Pará. According to him, even if the 32 operations had actually been carried out, they would represent only half of what was anticipated in the National Plan to Combat Deforestation.[^cite_note-IPS-16]

### Resignation

She remained in office until 2008 and received several criticisms from entrepreneurs (mainly related to [agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness)) on account of delays in granting permits for projects with large environmental impact. In early 2005, however, she stated that she would not give up upon facing challenges even if they were imposed by the government to which she belonged, like the controversy over the [São Francisco River Diversion Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Francisco_River),[^cite_note-19] and the building of the BR-163 highway through the [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest): "I don't admit defeat, just challenges that must be overcome".[^cite_note-IPS-16]

Silva resigned from the Lula government in May 2008. She was replaced by [Carlos Minc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Minc).[^cite_note-Barrionuevo-20] Silva cited "the growing resistance found by our team in important sectors of the government and society" as the reason for her resignation.[^cite_note-Phillips-13] Tension between her and the rest of the Lula administration increased when President [Lula da Silva](99328.md) chose Minister of Strategic Affairs [Roberto Mangabeira Unger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mangabeira_Unger) to coordinate a sustainable development plan for the Amazon, instead of her. She had become increasingly isolated in [Lula da Silva](99328.md)'s government due to her views against hydroelectric dams, [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuels), and genetically modified crops.[^cite_note-Barrionuevo-20]

## 2010 presidential election

Waist high portrait of three middle aged people in the library of what could be a boat or other confined space.

Silva (center) with [Thomas Lovejoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Lovejoy) and [Stephen Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Schneider_\(scientist\))

### Party switch

&#x20;On 19 August 2009, Silva announced her switch from the [Workers' Party](224622.md) to the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Brazil\)), primarily in protest against the environmental policies endorsed by the [PT](224622.md). Confirming the expectations,[^cite_note-21] Marina Silva launched her candidacy[^cite_note-22] to the [2010 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Brazilian_presidential_election) under the Green Party ticket on 16 May 2010 in the city of Nova Iguaçu, [state of Rio de Janeiro](229419.md). Silva said she wanted to be "the first black woman of poor origin" to become president of Brazil.[^cite_note-23]



Silva on [SBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Brasileiro_de_Televis%C3%A3o)

In her campaign, Silva defended the "exercise of citizen-based political principles and values", "education for the knowledge society", "economy applied to a sustainable society", "social protection, health, welfare and 3rd generation of social programs", "[quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) and safety for all Brazilians", and "strengthening of culture and diversity".[^cite_note-24]

With her speech against the endemic corruption in Brazil (see *[A Privataria Tucana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Privataria_Tucana)* and *[Mensalão scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mensal%C3%A3o_scandal)*), and in favor of sustainable development (with a due consideration to environmental issues), Silva managed to attract the middle class sectors disillusioned with the government of the [Fernando Henrique Cardoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso)'s [PSDB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_da_Social_Democracia_Brasileira) and dissatisfied with the compensatory social policies of Lula da Silva's administration. As a result, she came to be seen as an alternative.[^cite_note-Maciel-25]

Marina Silva received strong support among young and highly educated voters. Running on a small-party ticket, she had about 1/20 of the TV time compared to the other two biggest party coalitions. Opinion polls notwithstanding, she received 19.4% of the votes cast.[^cite_note-26] This number far exceeded earlier estimates (more than double), but not enough to join the runoff against [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) or [José Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Serra).[^cite_note-27]

## 2014 presidential election



With [Eduardo Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Campos).

In April 2014, [Eduardo Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Campos) announced his name for the [October 2014 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Brazilian_general_election), naming Marina Silva as his candidate for vice president.[^cite_note-CamposMarinaElec-4]

### Sustainability Network

On 16 February 2013 a new party, *Rede Sustentabilidade* ("Sustainability Network"), was officially launched in Brasilia.[^cite_note-28] According to its founders, the name to be used at the polls would be simply REDE ("NETWORK").[^cite_note-29]

On 4 October 2013, the [Superior Electoral Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Electoral_Court) blocked the party's creation, with there being insufficient signatures to register it.[^cite_note-30] The following day, Marina announced her affiliation to the [Brazilian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Socialist_Party).[^cite_note-31]

### Death of Campos

On Wednesday, 13 August 2014, Campos' private jet, with six others on board, [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Santos_Cessna_Citation_crash) in bad weather as it was preparing to land in the coastal city of [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos,_Brazil), just south of [São Paulo](390875.md). After his death, Silva[^cite_note-32] became the Brazilian Socialist Party's candidate for president of Brazil.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Campaign

Soon after taking the place of Campos in the bid, Marina polled 20% of the votes, 10% more than Campos was polling. She enjoyed strong support among young voters and evangelicals, but because of her pro-environmental stance she was largely distrusted by Brazil's powerful agribusiness sector.[^cite_note-35] As an [Evangelical Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Christian), she opposed [abortion](765.md).[^cite_note-36] On 30 August 2014, Silva generated considerable controversy when she renounced the party's support for [same-sex marriage](92656.md), which was supported by Campos and had been included in the party's manifesto, published a day earlier.[^cite_note-37]

On Sunday, 5 October 2014, Silva received 21% of the vote in the first round of the election, to Rousseff's 41% and Neves's 34%.[^cite_note-bbc.com-6] Although many observers had expected Silva to advance to a second round against Rousseff, Silva ultimately received a much lower share of the vote than most opinion polls had indicated in the lead-up to the election, and did not advance to the 26 October run-off.[^cite_note-bbc.com-6] Some days after the election she endorsed Aecio Neves in the run-off against Dilma Rousseff.[^cite_note-38]

## 2018 presidential election

On 4 August 2018, Marina Silva was officially nominated as the Sustainability Network's presidential candidate in the [2018 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Brazilian_general_election). Silva's running mate was [Eduardo Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Jorge) of the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Brazil\)).[^cite_note-39]

Until August 2018, Silva came in third in [opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_Brazilian_general_election,_2018) for the presidency, behind [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) (before his candidacy was barred) and [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md). However, she was later overtaken by [Ciro Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciro_Gomes), [Fernando Haddad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Haddad) (Lula's replacement on the PT ticket), and [Geraldo Alckmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldo_Alckmin), and was later polling fifth on average.[^cite_note-40]

In the last few days before the election, her poll numbers dropped significantly, and in the end she polled around a single percentage point. She came out eight with 1.0% and 1,066,893 votes.

## Second Lula government



Silva at [COP30](75227876.md), Belém, 2025

### 2022 presidential election

On 12 September 2022 in an event open to the press, Silva publicly endorsed former President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva for a third term as president ahead of the [October general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election), stating that it was necessary "to beat Bolsonaro and the evil seeds he is sowing in our society." Lula in turn vowed to enact a series of environmental proposals presented by Silva if he won the election. The turn of events has been described by commentators as a major step towards reconciliation between Silva and the Workers' Party.[^cite_note-41]

### Minister of the Environment (2023–present)

Following Lula's victory over Bolsonaro in the 31 October runoff, he announced Silva's return as Minister of the Environment on 29 December.[^cite_note-42] In addition, Silva was elected to the [Chamber of Deputies](1589485.md) as a member of REDE for [São Paulo](229422.md). During her second term, deforestation rates in the Amazon have fallen to their lowest in over a decade through strengthened enforcement, Amazon Fund restoration and expanded protected areas. Silva has also advocated for zero deforestation by 2030 and shaped Brazil's COP30 position on forest finance and indigenous inclusion.[^cite_note-43]

She stepped down from President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s cabinet to launch a campaign for Congress. During her tenure, she oversaw environmental enforcement that cut Amazon deforestation by more than 50 percent. [^cite_note-44] Time magazine quoted her as saying, “When we took office, we had deforestation on an ascending curve that was out of control. We had to rebuild institutions, command and control organizations, and increase public funding.”[^cite_note-45]

## Views

### Political

Marina Silva is generally considered to be a [centrist](39619035.md) and an [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalist).[^cite_note-Reuters_anti-graft-46] She has campaigned on an anti-corruption platform.[^cite_note-Reuters_anti-graft-46] She opposes Brazil's nuclear energy program, and wants to redistribute the nuclear energy funds toward solar and wind power. Furthermore, she wants to initiate a national plebiscite on investments in nuclear energy.[^cite_note-nuclear-47] She is in favor of imposing presidential [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits).[^cite_note-WTOP-48]

### Religious

Since 1996,[^cite_note-49] Silva has been a Pentecostal Christian[^cite_note-Equal_Times-50][^cite_note-CP_World-51] in the [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God), the second largest Christian denomination in Brazil after the declining but still mainstream [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] Nevertheless, during her 2010 election campaign, she was criticized by one of the main leaders of the Brazilian [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God), Pastor [Silas Malafaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silas_Malafaia), after having proposed a [referendum](51582.md) on abortion and the [decriminalization of marijuana](168917.md). According to Malafaia, Marina Silva should be "more courageous and consistent" in defense of her religious convictions.[^cite_note-54]

## Honors

In 1996, Silva won the [Goldman Environmental Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Environmental_Prize) for South & Central America.[^cite_note-55] In 2007, the United Nations Environment Program named her one of the [Champions of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champions_of_the_Earth)[^cite_note-56] and the 2009 [Sophie Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Prize).[^cite_note-57] In December 2014, Marina Silva was elected by the British *[Financial Times](136566.md)* newspaper as one of its *Women of the Year*.[^cite_note-58] Silva is also a member of [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)–based think tank, the [Inter-American Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-American_Dialogue).[^cite_note-59]

### 2012 Summer Olympics

The participation of Marina Silva as one of the eight invited [flag-bearers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag-bearer) to carry the [Olympic flag](731192.md) at the opening of the [2012 London Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) surprised the [Brazilian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_government) representatives present at the ceremony.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] In the Brazilian press, headlines like "Marina steals [Dilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff)'s attention" appeared.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Commenting on the event, [Aldo Rebelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Rebelo), Brazilian Sports Minister from the PT, said that Silva "always had good relations with the [European aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_aristocracy)" and that it was the responsibility of the [Royal House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Windsor) to choose who would participate in the event. The [Olympic Committee](15147.md) said it was aware of Silva's work as an activist in defense of the [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest), but denied any political motivations regarding the choice.[^cite_note-64] About her participation in the ceremony, Silva compared it to the feeling she got when passing, aged 16, her [literacy course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy): "it was the same kind of happiness."[^cite_note-65]
