The **Brazilian drug war** is an ongoing conflict fought mainly between [police militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_militias), [drug cartels](197015.md) and federal (or state) security forces in [Brazil](3383.md).

## Background



Panorama night image of [Rocinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocinha), the largest favela in Rio de Janeiro.

[Organized crime](22625.md) is heavily intertwined with the geography of the largest cities in Brazil, particularly in their poorest neighborhoods, commonly known as [favelas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela). Many Brazilian cities have their wealthiest and most tourist-driven communities located close to informal neighborhoods that face high rates of [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty), [drug addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_use_disorder), [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment), [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence) and [homelessness](19394651.md); This extreme [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) and lack of proper [social services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_services) in these areas facilitates the formation and survival of organized crime, which is present in every [Brazilian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federative_units_of_Brazil).[^cite_note-35] Residents of these neighborhoods frequently face [social stigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stigma), which is commonly intertwined with [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism), [social segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_segregation#Social_segregation_and_gentrification) and [class discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_discrimination); The lack of both proper education and formal job opportunities caused by this social discrimination also causes organized crime to grow even further.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

Criminal organizations often rely heavily on relationships with both political and state actors to advance their influence. These relations usually involve [large-scale corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Brazil), such as [bribery](260979.md) and the use of informal favours or personal connections to achieve control over many government institutions, such as the police and judiciary.[^cite_note-40] Members of some security forces, particularly of the [Civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Police_\(Brazil\)) and [Military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Military_Police) polices, have also formed their own criminal organizations, popularly known as "[militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_militias)".[^cite_note-Milícias-41] Large businesses, particularly in the [finance](1043650.md), [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) and [oil](994887.md) sectors, are also known to be heavily intertwined with organized crime in Brazil, in many cases using [shell companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_corporation) and other means to perform [money laundering](19390.md) for these organizations.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-so-43][^cite_note-44]

Despite these known links between state forces and organized crime, public opinion, especially among the [upper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_class) and [upper middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_middle_class) classes, is largely in favour of the state using armed force to combat these criminal organizations.[^cite_note-45] This is also exacerbated by many [sensationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensationalism) TV programs, which use explicit and violent imagery to amplify these sentiments throughout the country.[^cite_note-46] Despite their own links to criminal organizations, many far-right political candidates have also taken advantage of this situation to be elected into public office.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-so-43][^cite_note-Milícias-41][^cite_note-48]

### Rio de Janeiro

Beginning with rackets such as [Jogo do Bicho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogo_do_Bicho), Rio de Janeiro has had many incidents of collusion among criminal organizations and social and political institutions. Following the [military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_in_Brazil), political tools originally used by the regime, such as the Cândido Mendes prison complex, created the first prison gangs in Rio in the late 1970s-early 1980s.[^cite_note-49] With the subsequent cocaine boom in South America,[^cite_note-50] these gangs would go on to unite or incorporate smaller street gangs and other groups, while also dominating the drug market and capitalizing off it within favela communities. Heightened tensions for control of these neighborhoods contributed to turf wars and new factions of organized crime forming.

### Drug policy in Brazil

Laws prohibiting the recreational use of drugs, such as [cannabis](1481886.md), [coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca) and [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium), were introduced to the [Penal Code of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_Code_of_Brazil) in 1940, when certain narcotic drugs were criminalized within Brazilian territory through the 1940 Penal Code (Law No. 2,848/1940), as set forth in Article 281:

> To import or export, sell or offer for sale, supply—even gratuitously—transport, carry, keep in storage, store, administer, or in any way deliver for consumption any narcotic substance, without authorization or in violation of legal or regulatory provisions.

This article rendered these drugs illegal and thus authorized police powers and regulatory bodies to impose sanctions on users and traffickers of [illicit drugs](218449.md).[^cite_note-51] Even before their legal prohibition, narcotic drugs were already shrouded in prejudice—particularly racial prejudice, such as in the case of marijuana, which was nicknamed "[Angola](701.md) weed", because it was mainly used by adherents of [Afro-Brazilian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora_religions#Brazil) in their rituals.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In 1973, when Brazil acceded to the [Convention on Psychotropic Substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Psychotropic_Substances), the "war on drugs" shifted its approach; the new drug policies clearly defined the penalties and consequences associated with possessing, purchasing, or selling these substances. It was in the Brazilian state's interest to marginalize and prohibit narcotics, given that during the [1968 student marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_the_One_Hundred_Thousand) against the military dictatorship, drugs had been used as a symbol of resistance against the regime—and, due to the lack of clear penalties, users faced neither prosecution nor punishment.[^cite_note-54]

## Criminal organizations

### Drug cartels

#### Comando Vermelho

[Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) (*Red Command*), often abbreviated as "CV", is Brazil's oldest active [criminal organization](22625.md), which engages primarily in [drug trafficking](218449.md) and [arms smuggling](42210.md), as well as [truck hijacking](362652.md#Truck_carjacking), [loan sharking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_shark), [pimping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuring_\(prostitution\)), [racketeering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket) and bank robberies, among other criminal activities. Considered the biggest faction in [Greater Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Rio_de_Janeiro), it has also spread to the rest of the state, as well as nearby [Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo) and into the north/northeastern regions of Brazil.&#x20;

The formation of the organization dates back to the late 1970s (during the military dictatorship), on the *Cândido Mendes prison* (located on [Ilha Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilha_Grande)), this penitentiary complex housed both ideological enemies of the regime (such as opposition politicians, journalists, unionists and intellectuals), as well as armed guerrilla fighters (such as members of [MR-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_October_Revolutionary_Movement) and [ALN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Libertadora_Nacional)) and high-profile criminals, which, after a series of massacres and general mistreatment by the prison guards, formed an "self-protective" alliance.[^cite_note-rc-55]

Through the transfer of major gang leaders by the Brazilian government, the organization was able to gain control of other prisons and establish itself on the ground in most of the state of Rio, gaining much influence within the ever-growing favela complexes. Later, during the cocaine boom of the 1980s, the gang's focus shifted from petty crime to that of drug and arms trafficking, which proved to be less dangerous than bank robberies and also allowed for larger profits and control of many city municipalities.[^cite_note-56]

Ever since the appearance and spread of rival organizations during the late 80s to the early 90s (both in Rio and other states), CV has waged major turf wars against other cartels and street gangs. In recent years it has been involved in a series of prison riots, massacres and territorial conflicts thorough the entire country, which resulted in dozens (or sometimes even hundreds) of casualties, mostly involving police militias (which have grown considerably in the past decade) and rival gangs such as ADA, GDE and TCP.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

After negotiations for an "alliance" with [São Paulo](390875.md)–based PCC broke down in 2016 (mainly due to disputes between shared drugs and arms trafficking routes in Paraguay, Bolivia and Colombia), a series of retaliations (which included mass shootings, executions and invasions of rival favelas) broke out between both groups and their allies, which have resulted in severe casualties, including not only gang members but also many innocent civilians and security officials who have been caught in the crossfire.[^cite_note-59]

However, despite years of violent conflicts with the Brazilian police and other criminal organizations, as well as deterrent efforts such as the [Police Pacifying Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Pacifying_Unit) (UPP) and the occupation of several favelas, the CV maintains significant power and governance in many of Rio de Janeiro's favelas, and, like many other organizations, has increasingly been portrayed as a "[cultural icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_icon)" in some genres of Brazilian music (such as in the *[Proibidão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proibid%C3%A3o)*, *[Ostentação](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_ostenta%C3%A7%C3%A3o)* and *[Automotivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonk#Funk_automotivo)* sub-genres of [Brazilian funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_carioca), [Trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap_music) and [Hip-Hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-Hop)).[^cite_note-60] Current issues for the CV reside in outbreaks of violence with criminal organizations for control of drug routes and turf wars with [militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_militias) over territories, such as the [Complexo do Alemão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Alem%C3%A3o) (which is sometimes considered the "headquarters" of the organization).[^cite_note-rc-55]

#### Primeiro Comando da Capital

The [Primeiro Comando da Capital](4711820.md) (PCC) is one of [Latin America](18524.md)'s largest [drug cartels](197015.md). The organization primarily deals with drugs/arms trafficking, bank robberies and hijacking of armored trucks, as well as [illegal gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling) and smuggling (especially [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum-running), [tobacco](40593794.md), [gold](49640132.md) and [timber](465780.md)). It is the biggest faction in Brazil, while also having a significant presence through the rest of [South America](26769.md), [Portugal](23033.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) (mostly through the [Brazilian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Americans) community).[^cite_note-61]

In recent years, the organization has been reported to operate in many other countries as well, mostly through its alliances with foreign criminal groups (such as ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta) in Italy, [Group America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Mafia#Group_America) in Serbia, [Tren de Aragua](70859925.md) in Venezuela, the [Triads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_\(organised_crime\)) in China and the [Jalisco Cartel](35006872.md) in Mexico) and paramilitary groups (such as the [FARC](26100.md) in Colombia and [EPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_People's_Army) in Paraguay).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] In recent years, there has even been reported activities of the group in [West Africa](67393.md) (mostly aligned with local groups such as the [Nigerian Mafia](11734374.md)), [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) (through the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_mafia) and [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_mafia) mafias) and the Middle East (through the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_mafia) and [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_mafia) mafias).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] The group has also been alleged to have ties to the [Yakuza](44774.md) in Japan and [Hezbollah](13919.md) in Lebanon.[^cite_note-66]

PCC was originally founded on August 31, 1993, by eight prisoners at [Taubaté Penitentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taubat%C3%A9_Prison), called "Piranhão" ("Big [Piranha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piranha)"), in the state of [São Paulo](229422.md). The group initially got together during a [football](10568.md) game, where they founded a team called "Comando Capital" (Capital Command). The prisoners had been transferred from the city of [São Paulo](390875.md) to the Piranhão as punishment for bad behavior. The game famously ended in widespread fights and chaos, which led to the brutal killing and decapitation of both the deputy director and a prisoner with special privileges, with the head of the latter being put on a stake.[^cite_note-el_pais-67] The group has the slogan "Peace, Justice and Freedom",[^cite_note-68] and makes use of the Chinese *[taijitu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taijitu)* (the [yin and yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang) symbol) as their emblem, claiming that it represents a "way to balance good and evil with wisdom".[^cite_note-69]

In February 2001, Idemir "Sombra" Carlos Ambrósio became the most prominent leader of the organization when he coordinated, by cell phone, simultaneous rebellions in 29 São Paulo state prisons, in which 16 prisoners were killed. "Sombra", also referred to as "father", was beaten to death in the Piranhão five months later by five PCC members in an internal struggle for the general command of the group. The PCC was initially led by César "Césinha" Augusto Roris da Silva and José "Geleião" Márcio Felício, who were responsible for a short-lived alliance with the [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) (CV). At the time, the group adopted CV's far-left beliefs and began advocating for revolution and the destruction of Brazil's capitalist system.[^cite_note-PCCinsight-70]

Geleião and Cesinha, from the [Bangu Penitentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangu_Penitentiary) where they were held, went on to coordinate violent attacks against public buildings. Considered radicals by another moderate current of the PCC, they used terrorism to intimidate authorities of the prison system and were withdrawn from leadership in November 2002, when the leadership was taken over by the current leader of the organization, [Marcos "Marcola" Willians Herbas Camacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcola). Marcola would eventually order the deaths of Geleião and Cesinha for having testified to the police, as well as for allegedly leaving the faction and creating a splinter group called the "Terceiro Comando da Capital" (Third Capital Command, TCC), which is now considered to be defunct.[^cite_note-el_pais-67][^cite_note-PCCinsight-70]

#### Amigos dos Amigos



ADA graffiti on the walls of the Rocinha favela.

[Amigos dos Amigos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amigos_dos_Amigos) (Literally "Friends of Friends"), known as "ADA", is one of Rio de Janeiro's most prominent drug gangs, historically having strongholds in [Rocinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocinha) -- the largest of the favelas in greater Rio de Janeiro. The criminal organization formed in the late 1990s by ex-members of [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) -- who were expelled due to the killing of another member -- in order to compete for territorial dominance.[^cite_note-da-71] CV is still ADA's biggest rival, along with the [Terceiro Comando Puro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terceiro_Comando_Puro) (Third Pure Command, TCP).

in 2004, after the death of the previous "owner" of the Rocinha favela (Luciano Barbosa da Silva, known as "Lulu", one of the "bosses" of the CV faction), ADA drug lord [Antônio Bonfim Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nem_da_Rocinha) (also known as "Nem" or "Nem da Rocinha") took control of the region and became the biggest leader in the faction. Under his control, the favela experienced an era of relative calm and peace,[^cite_note-72] while continuously strengthening ADA's drug trafficking schemes and the consolidation of power over political networks and social projects.[^cite_note-jb-73] This included financial and social support for "friendly" political candidates, provision of lacking basic services and the distribution of [food baskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_basket#Food_basket) to families living in the slums.[^cite_note-jb-73]

Large portions of money were also destined to holding massive parties, "[bailes funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_carioca)" and celebrations, which, according to the police, have been used as important ways to generate not only local entertainment, but also income to these groups, mostly through the illegal commerce of drugs, tobacco and liquor, as well as prostitution, extortion and robberies.[^cite_note-da-71] Nem's eventual arrest in 2011 led to years of incessant turf wars for control of Rocinha among ADA and CV, which are still ongoing. Although unconfirmed, it is generally thought that Nem still leads ADA operations from within prison.[^cite_note-74]

#### Terceiro Comando Puro

[Terceiro Comando Puro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terceiro_Comando_Puro) (Pure Third Command) or TCP is yet another of Rio de Janeiro's most powerful organized crime groups, participating in the drug market and [extortion](162449.md). Originating in 2002, the group began as an off-shoot of the [Terceiro Comando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terceiro_Comando) (Third Command),[^cite_note-tc-75] which no longer operates and was originally a faction formed off of Comando Vermelho.

Although enemies now, Terceiro Comando and Amigos dos Amigos formed an alliance in the late 1990s to overtake territory held by their mutual enemy, CV.[^cite_note-tc-75] Several years later, this alliance crumbled and led to the degradation of the Terceiro Comando, until former leader Nei da Conceição Cruz, alias “Facão, reformed the group in 2002 under the new name Terceiro Comando Puro.

Since then, TCP has maintained territorial control of the northern and western zones of Rio de Janeiro, although they may be attempting to expand their territory through an alliance with São Paulo's PCC, especially following the breakdown of the alliance between PCC and CV in 2016.[^cite_note-tc-75] TCP also engages in continual turf wars with ADA and CV, but have recently gained allies in the form of [militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_militias), such as the [Liga da Justiça](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_da_Justi%C3%A7a) faction.

TCP has also been historically allied with a series of other criminal organizations in the northeast, such as the [Guardiões do Estado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardi%C3%B5es_do_Estado) and the [Bonde do Maluco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonde_do_Maluco), which has expanded the group's reach beyond Rio, into Espirito Santo, the Northeast and even Pará.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

The gang has [persecuted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narco-Pentecostalism) adherents of Afro-Brazilian religions and [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Brazil) in its dominated territories.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Drug packages belonging to the organization have been marked with the [Star of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_David), [Israeli flags](459004.md) were raised and religious symbols (such as the Star of David) were displayed on the walls in several locations alongside the faction's initials.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

### Illegal gambling groups

[Jogo do bicho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogo_do_bicho) (Animal game) is an illegal gambling game in Brazil, which is still incredibly popular and sought out despite its federal prohibition in 1946. The game is played all around the country, with lottery-style drawings in every state occurring daily in its headquarters in [Greater Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Rio_de_Janeiro).[^cite_note-om-82] The game is controlled by bosses, called *bicheiros* or *banqueiros* ("bankers").

Jogo do Bicho is a longstanding staple in [Brazilian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_culture), with thousands participating in the game daily. Because of its potential to circulate and raise large sums of money, control of Jogo do Bicho is lucrative and largely contributes to corruption, with politicians, judicial personnel, and members of the police force all monetarily and politically benefitting from it.[^cite_note-om-82] Bicheiros also contribute funding to social programs, such as [samba schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_school), which play a critical role in cultural festivities such as [Brazilian Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Carnival).[^cite_note-om-82] Because of this, Jogo do Bicho, and in turn its bicheiros, are deeply ingrained in Brazilian culture, especially in Rio de Janeiro, and contribute to the landscape of organized crime and corruption.

### Militias

[Brazilian milicias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_militias) (militias), or paramilitary groups, consisting of current and retired police officers, prison guards, and military members, control much of the western territory of Rio de Janeiro[^cite_note-wt-83] and continue to expand their territory through turf wars with ADA, TCP and CV, both in Rio and in other states, such as São Paulo and the northeast.[^cite_note-84] Militia groups have grown in size and power rapidly, originating in the early 2000s and growing to control highly populated areas since. Founded as vigilante groups, they've amassed enough power as to dominate and extort entire territories of Rio de Janeiro. Along with extortion methods, the groups have gained political influence and have even been previously supported by high-profile politicians, such as former [president](273638.md) [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md).[^cite_note-85]

Unlike the other criminal organizations such as CV and ADA, who have always highly invested in the drug market, militias positioned themselves against drug trafficking in order to gain community approval and trust, with some favela residents seeing them as the "lesser evil" of crime as compared to drug gangs.[^cite_note-wt-83] Instead of drug trafficking, the groups participate in extortion, controlling civilian access to gas, land, and cable television.[^cite_note-86] Recently, some groups have decided to expand to allowing drug dealing within their territories or are doing so themselves, leveraging profit and control of important territories from drug gangs.[^cite_note-87]

## Major events

### Rio drug war



Father cries over the body of his dead son, who was murdered while refueling his motorcycle at a gas station.

The [militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_militias) emerged in the mid-late 1990s,[^cite_note-uol-1] being composed mainly of off-duty police officers and ex-military, in many cases also receiving assistance from local businessmen and residents looking for protection from criminal groups.[^cite_note-88] The [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md), as a response, began carrying out terrorist attacks against civilian targets between 27 and 31 December 2006, during these attacks, 19 people died, being 10 civilians, 2 policemen and 7 gang members. As retaliation, the militias conducted several raids against the Comando Vermelho, killing more than 100 gang members.[^cite_note-sr-31][^cite_note-sc-33] These groups launched several attacks between January 2007 and March 2008 against Comando Vermelho, conquering the Cidade Alta favela on 4 February, three days later it was recaptured by the Comando Vermelho commanded by Gilberto Martins da Silva (known as "Mineiro da Cidade Alta"), with at least 20 gang members being killed in subsequent clashes.[^cite_note-nr-89] The militias obtained a "Caveirão" (an [armored vehicle](19278686.md) used by the [Battalion of Special Police Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion_of_Special_Police_Operations) (BOPE) for high-risk operations) in January of the same year.[^cite_note-nr-89]

According to some investigations, the militias mainly finance their armed struggle with illegal activities, such as [extortion](162449.md), [kidnapping](169376.md), [loan sharking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_shark), [racketeering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_rackets), [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling), [pimping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimping), [contract killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_killing) and [arms trafficking](42210.md).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] In May 2008, the militias, commanded by Coronel Jairo, kidnapped and tortured a group of journalists from the Brazilian newspaper [O Dia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Dia) who were reporting the criminal activities perpetrated by the militiamen. After 7 hours of torture, they were released.[^cite_note-93] In that same month, in clashes between the Comando Vermelho and the militias, 10 people were killed. Residents were threatened by the fighting groups and the president of an association for local residents was kidnapped and subsequently disappeared.[^cite_note-94] On 5 August, one of the leaders of the militia, Carlos Alexandre Silva Cavalcante, known as "Gaguinho", was killed.[^cite_note-95] On 20 August 2008, the militias carried out a massacre which resulted in the death of 7 people in the Carobinha favela in a [false flag operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_flag_operation) aiming to frame the Comando Vermelho for the massacre. There was also an attempt to enforce the political candidacy of Carminha Jerominho, daughter of Jerônimo Guimarães Filho, alias "Jerominho", the leader of a militia faction.[^cite_note-96]

On 5 October, "Mineiro da Cidade Alta" was killed by the militias for the murder of several militia members. From 2007 to 2008, three politicians were arrested for providing support to the militias: Josinaldo Francisco da Cruz (known as "Nadinho de [Rio das Pedras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_das_Pedras,_Rio_de_Janeiro)"), Natalino José Guimarães and his brother Jerônimo Guimarães.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] On 9 June 2009, Josinaldo Francisco da Cruz was killed.[^cite_note-99] Despite initially being opposed to drug trafficking, many militia groups (such as the "[Liga da Justiça](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_da_Justi%C3%A7a)" faction) have allied themselves with cartels like the [TCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terceiro_Comando_Puro), while also recruiting many former drug traffickers, [informants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informant) and ex-convicts into their ranks, sometimes through coercion.[^cite_note-100]

#### Police helicopter downed on Morro dos Macacos

The *Morro dos Macacos* is one of the most violent [favelas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favelas) in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), the community was invaded by [CV](5172177.md) on 17 October 2009, while it was still under the control of [ADA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amigos_dos_Amigos), who clashed with Comando Vermelho members to contend for territory, 5 gang members were killed in the clashes from 11 to 17 October. In order to stop the clashes between the two groups, about 150 assault troops of the Special Operations Department of the [Polícia Militar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol%C3%ADcia_Militar) were dispatched for security purposes. In an attempt to prevent the intervention of the police in the ongoing [feud](292136.md), criminals built makeshift barricades at several key points and subsequently set them on fire.[^cite_note-101]

On 17 October, in the morning, the rear propeller of a Fenix helicopter patrolling above the community was shot and severely damaged by drug traffickers of [Amigos dos Amigos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amigos_dos_Amigos).[^cite_note-102] According to some sources, the weapons used in the attack were a [bazooka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazooka) and several [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun). The helicopter crashed, killing 2 soldiers on impact, with another soldier later dying of his wounds.[^cite_note-103] This attack sparked widespread retaliations by police and militias against drug cartels and civilians in the region, with massacres occurring from 17 to 25 October 2009, resulting in at least 45 deaths. During these attacks, Brazilian authorities admitted to corruption and brutality among the police force, including the illegal release of two narcos perpetrated by some policemen; confirming also the existence of these armed militias who compete against drug traffickers and gangs for control of the drug market.[^cite_note-104]

#### Formation of the UPP

The [Unidade de Polícia Pacificadora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifying_Police_Unit) (Pacifying Police Units) or UPP was a security program in Rio de Janeiro that aimed to reclaim favelas from criminal organizations, such as CV, ADA, and TCP, through community policing. The strategy worked in two parts: deployment of the [Military Police of Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_of_Rio_de_Janeiro_State) (PMERJ) into the favelas, and then increasing the amount of police officers who were meant to interact with the community through units,[^cite_note-105] in hopes of creating a healthier relationship between favela residents and law enforcement than what existed in years prior. For decades, interactions with police in Rio de Janeiro were abrasive; violent conflicts between drug traffickers and the police led to widespread civilian casualties, and numerous instances of abuses of power by the police led to a general distrust by favela residents.[^cite_note-wp-106] The creation of the UPP aimed to both restructure the police force and its interactions with civilians, while also deterring criminal activity leading up to the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) and [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics). A secondary component of the UPP plan was the creation of UPP Social, which aimed to address the social welfare needs of the favelas by UPP units. This included improvements to sanitation, education, and healthcare, among other social issues.

In 2008, the first UPP was set up in the [Santa Marta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela_Santa_Marta) favela, with more quickly following -- coalescing in 32 units overall, spanning all of Rio de Janeiro's zones (though most were located in Zona Norte). Despite urges for increased financial support, the UPP units amassed praise in their early years for evident decreases in both visible crime and violence within the favelas they were operating in.[^cite_note-fv-107] Because military presence was a precursor to actual UPP units, many drug traffickers fled occupied favelas to avoid persecution or capture by the Military Police tactical unit, [BOPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batalh%C3%A3o_de_Opera%C3%A7%C3%B5es_Policiais_Especiais). Because of this, crime rates significantly dropped for a couple years. In interviews with residents, details of the freedoms they were allowed once UPP units were installed continued to highlight the successes of the project.[^cite_note-fv-107]

#### Amarildo Souza, UPP abuses and backsliding

Main article: [Case of Amarildo de Souza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_of_Amarildo_de_Souza)

Despite early success, some of the main hinderances of the UPP project were police abuses and disappearances that occurred. One of the most prominent cases of this was the disappearance of Amarildo Souza, a bricklayer from the Rocinha favela.[^cite_note-108] Souza, 42, had been fishing the morning of his disappearance and decided to go into the favela to get produce when he was confronted by UPP officers.[^cite_note-rr-109] Souza was taken for questioning and subsequently never returned home.[^cite_note-rr-109]

Souza was well known throughout Rocinha, leading to public outcry at his disappearance and a lack of response by the UPP unit. After more than two months of public outcry, an investigation was opened into his disappearance, where twenty-four UPP officers and the UPP commander were found accused of torture, concealing a body, procedural fraud, and conspiracy.[^cite_note-110] In the subsequent trial, twelve officers would be found guilty of torture, procedural fraud, and concealing a body.[^cite_note-111]

Despite the investigation and criminal case, the UPP suffered weakened support from favela residents, who felt both anger and fear at the actions of the local UPP unit. Coupled with insufficient training[^cite_note-112] and poorly strategized growth of the units, the UPP continued to fail as insignificant funding heavily derailed the project, which could not perform what it was intended to.[^cite_note-113] With the weakening of the UPP, criminal organizations could again attempt to reclaim their territories through shootouts and confrontations, but were this time more successful.[^cite_note-wp-106]

#### 2010–present



Brazilian army [M113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113) armored vehicle in Rio de Janeiro, 2010.

After the battle, there were other several clashes between militiamen and gang members. Throughout the 2010s there were several arrests and convictions against militiamen and drug traffickers involved in the massacres. The militias managed to conquer several territories under the control of the [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md), although some of them later returned to the gang's control after being reconquered during clashes. In November 2010, another [massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Rio_de_Janeiro_security_crisis) occurred between the military police and the CV, resulting in the deaths of 41 people.[^cite_note-114] On 1 February 2012, during a raid conducted by the Polícia Militar (PM), a "Caveirão" was destroyed by the [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) in the favela of Jacarezinho, no injuries were reported.[^cite_note-115] During the course of the massacre, the CV and the militias began recruiting [child soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_in_the_military).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] On 19 November 2016, during an operation of the PM, a military police helicopter was shot down by [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) in the favela of [Cidade de Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cidade_de_Deus,_Rio_de_Janeiro), in the crash four policemen died.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] On 9 February 2020, a prominent militia faction leader, Adriano Magalhães da Nóbrega, also known as "Capitão Adriano" or "Gordinho", was seriously injured in a police operation and later died in a hospital.[^cite_note-120] On 15 October, in one of the deadliest operations against militias, the PM clash with a convoy of the militias killing 12 paramilitaries on Itaguaí, also a policeman was injured.[^cite_note-121] In the Morro do Fubá favela, the residents were forced to pay a monthly fee of 50 [brazilian reals](34201323.md) as part of a [protection racket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket). On 17 May 2022, militias attacked a civilian helicopter that was flying over the Rio area with rifles. No injuries were recorded but the helicopter suffered slight damage.[^cite_note-122] On 20 August of the same year, Delson Lima Neto, brother of one of the leader of the militias, Tandera Danilo Dias Lima, was killed alongside 3 paramilitaries soldiers by the [Polícia Militar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_of_Rio_de_Janeiro_State) on Nova Iguaçu, in Baixada Fluminense, after his death the Comando Vermelho conquered the favela of Grão-Pará, in [Nova Iguaçu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Igua%C3%A7u).[^cite_note-123] After the raid, on 23 August, the police found and seized an [improvised fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvised_fighting_vehicle) (similar to a Caveirão) used by the militias to fight against Comando Vermelho in Nova Iguaçu.[^cite_note-124]

According to law student Carlos Gilberto Martins Junior of Brazil in 2023, with emphasis on the [State of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Rio_de_Janeiro), there has been an arbitrary use of these powers and attributions, conferred on police institutions, to satisfy the patrimonial aspirations of some of its agents, through territorial domination and violence, to the detriment of the peripheral communities and under the pretext of saving them from the "greater evil" represented by drug trafficking, corroborating the emergence of criminals organizations what conventionally called the "militia".[^cite_note-125]

On 6 May 2021, [at least 29 people were killed in a shootout between gangsters of Comando Vermelho and police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Rio_de_Janeiro_shootout). On May 24 2022, the BOPE [raided the Vila Cruzeiro favela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vila_Cruzeiro_shootout) in Penha Complex, killing 26 people between civilians and gangsters of Comando Vermelho. On March 23 2023, Brazilian police [raided the Salgueiro favela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Salgueiro_raid) in [São Gonçalo, Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Gon%C3%A7alo,_Rio_de_Janeiro), killing Leonardo Costa Araujo, the head of CV in Para, and other 12 gangsters.[^cite_note-\:1-126] On September 4 2025, eight members of the [Terceiro Comando Puro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terceiro_Comando_Puro) were killed in a shootout with police in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md).[^cite_note-127] Later, on October 28, 2025, against the backdrop of [COP30](75227876.md), Brazilian police launched [Operation Containment](81456361.md) on Comando Vermelho spots in Rio de Janeiro, arresting at least 81 suspected gang members while resulting in the deaths of more than 132 people, including four police officers and at least 17 residents without prior criminal records.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] On 14 January 2026, four people were killed in a police operation against the [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) in the Salgueiro [favela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela) of [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md).[^cite_note-130] On 18 March, eight people, including [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) commander Claudio Augusto dos Santos, were killed in police raids across [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md).[^cite_note-131]

### PCC-CV war



The Provisional Detention Center of Pinheiros in São Paulo after a prison riot.

In 2016, the breakdown of a 20-year truce between the PCC and the [Red Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Command) (CV) led to a massive uptick in violence across Brazil, with the PCC embarking on an aggressive expansion campaign by absorbing less organized gangs and financing local groups to operate as [proxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_war) against the CV across the country, such as the B13 gang in [Acre](229497.md) and the CV's rivals in [Rio de Janeiro](229419.md).[^cite_note-breakingout-breakdown-132][^cite_note-tussle-breakdown-133] Between 2016 and 2020, a [series of gruesome prison riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Brazil_prison_riots) made headlines worldwide as the PCC fought for control of the [North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) against the [Família do Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fam%C3%ADlia_do_Norte), allies with the Comando Vermelho. On January 1, dozens of PCC prisoners were massacred at the Anísio Jobim Penitentiary Complex in [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus) after a prison riot, with the PCC retaliating in prison riots in [Boa Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boa_Vista,_Roraima) and in [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal,_Rio_Grande_do_Norte) in the same week. Dismemberments, beheadings and prisoners being burned alive were commonplace during all three prison riots.[^cite_note-trail-134][^cite_note-ascensao-135] Between 2015 and 2018, the Familia do Norte and the [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) (CV) formed an alliance to prevent the advance of the [Primeiro Comando da Capital](4711820.md) (PCC) in Amazonas.[^cite_note-136] In 2018, the alliance dissolved, generating a confrontation between the CV and Familia do Norte, weakening the faction.[^cite_note-137] 33 prisoners were killed in the [Agricultural Penitentiary of Monte Cristo riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_Penitentiary_of_Monte_Cristo_riot), located in the rural area of Boa Vista, [Roraima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roraima), also in the North. According to [Folha de S.Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folha_de_S.Paulo), the massacre in Roraima was a response of the PCC to the rebellion commanded by the FDN in the Amazon.[^cite_note-138] Even more people were killed later on in the month.[^cite_note-139] More than 106 were the fatalities during those prison riots.[^cite_note-140] Additionally, a cartel battle for control of the Amazon region has intensified between rival gangs including the CV, seeking access to the valuable trafficking routes in the Colombia-Brazil-Peru [tri-border region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tres_Fronteras), the CV, PCC, Familia do Norte, and Colombian militia groups including the Border Command and the Carolina Ramirez Front have violently fought, contributing to a significant uptick in regional fatalities.[^cite_note-g1.globo.com-141] In 2023, sources consistently reported frequent violent clashes between the CV and the PCC as well as other rival gang groups.[^cite_note-g1.globo.com-141][^cite_note-142] The worst PCC linked-proxy war was between [Guardiões do Estado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardi%C3%B5es_do_Estado) and Comando Vermelho, with more than 10.000 killed in gang battles in [Ceará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cear%C3%A1) state.[^cite_note-143] During this proxy war, GDE displaced hundreds of civilians, burned houses, buses, perpetred massacres and mostly homicides in the state of [Ceará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cear%C3%A1),[^cite_note-Sisnando-144] the most notable being the [Cajazeiras massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajazeiras_massacre?action=edit\&redlink=1) with 14 killed and 9 injured.[^cite_note-Ceara-145] [^cite_note-Rizzo-146]

### Violence outbreak in Espírito Santo



[Brazilian soldiers](1047559.md) in the streets of [Vitória, Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vit%C3%B3ria,_Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo).

Due to the mutiny of the [Military Police of Espírito Santo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_of_Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo_State) in which the agents demanded the correction of the remuneration for inflation, along with retroactive payments, criminal gangs and drug traffickers took advantage of the situation and a crime wave of [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence), [carjacking](362652.md) and [looting](209811.md) spread across [Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo), with most public services and businesses being closed.[^cite_note-147] The Espírito Santo state government called in assistance from the [National Public Security Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Security_Force) and the [Brazilian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Armed_Forces) to [restore law and order in Vitória and other cities until military policemen began to return to their duties after two weeks of strike action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Military_Police_of_Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo_strike). By 25 February, all military policemen in Espírito Santo had ended their strikes. At least 215 people were killed in the violence.[^cite_note-homicidios2-148]
