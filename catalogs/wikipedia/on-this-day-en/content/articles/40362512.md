A **political faction** is a group of people with a common [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) purpose. More specifically, **political faction** denotes a subgroup of a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party), where the former may be distinguished by having one or more opinions or interests that differ from the latter's.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] [Intra-party conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intragroup_conflict) between factions can lead to [schism](6288753.md) of the party into two or more political parties. The *[ley de lemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ley_de_lemas)* electoral system allows voters to indicate on the [ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot) their support for a specific faction of their preference within a political party. Political factions can represent [voting blocs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_bloc). Political factions require a [weaker party discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_discipline#Weaker_party_discipline). Research indicates that factions can play an important role in moving their host party along the [ideological spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum).[^cite_note-3]

## George Washington's Farewell Address

&#x20;The first president of the United States, [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington), warned of political factions in his famous [farewell address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington's_Farewell_Address) from 1796. He warned of political parties generally, as according to Washington, political party loyalty when prioritized over [duty to the nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotism) and commitment to principles, was considered to be a major threat to the survival of a [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) [constitutional republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_republic):[^cite_note-History-Washington-4][^cite_note-Politico-Washington-5]

> Without looking forward to an extremity of this kind (which nevertheless ought not to be entirely out of sight), the common and continual mischiefs of the spirit of party are sufficient to make it the interest and duty of a wise people to discourage and restrain it \[the formation and loyalty to partisan interests, over loyalty to principles or one's country].[^cite_note-Politico-Washington-5][^cite_note-History-Washington-4]
