According to the 2022 revision of the [World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Population_Prospects)[^cite_note-UN_WPP-2][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-3] the total population was 27,478,249 in 2021, compared to only 2 630 000 in 1950. The proportion of children below the age of 15 in 2010 was 40.9%, 55.3% was between 15 and 65 years of age, while 3.8% was 65 years or older.[^cite_note-WPP_2010-4]

[Ivory Coast](57744.md) has more than 60 ethnic groups, usually classified into five principal divisions: [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_\(ethnic_group\)) (east and center, including Lagoon peoples of the southeast), [Krou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krou) (southwest), Southern [Mandé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mand%C3%A9_people) (west), Northern Mandé (northwest), Sénoufo/[Lobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobi_people) (north center and northeast). The Baoulés, in the Akan division, probably comprise the largest single subgroup with 15%-20% of the population. They are based in the central region around [Bouaké](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouak%C3%A9) and [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro). The Bétés in the Krou division, the Sénoufos in the north, and the Malinkés in the northwest and the cities are the next largest groups, with 10%-15% of the national population. Most of the principal divisions have a significant presence in neighboring countries.

## Migration

### Immigration

Of the more than 5 million non-Ivorian Africans living in Ivory Coast, one-third to one-half are from [Burkina Faso](3470.md); the rest are from [Ghana](12067.md), [Guinea](12176.md), [Mali](19127.md), [Niger](21373.md), [Nigeria](21383.md), [Benin](3459.md), [Senegal](27278.md), [Liberia](17791.md), and [Mauritania](18778516.md).

Non-Africans in the country include [French people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), [Lebanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people), [Vietnamese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people), [Spaniards](963152.md), [Americans](19792942.md) and [Canadians](19851291.md).[^cite_note-13]

## Languages

[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) is the official language, while there are 60 living indigenous languages spoken in Ivory Coast. The [Dioula dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioula_language) of [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language) is the most widely spoken one.[^cite_note-17] Other language groups include the [Gur languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur_languages), the [Kru languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kru_languages) (including the [Bété languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9t%C3%A9_languages), [Dida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dida_language), [Nyabwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyabwa_language), [Wè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%A8_language), and [Western Krahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Krahn)), the [Kwa languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwa_languages) (including [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_language), [Anyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyin_language), and [Baoulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baoul%C3%A9_language)), and the [Senufo languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senufo_languages).[^cite_note-18]

The [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Ivory_Coast) and relative prosperity of Ivory Coast fostered huge demographic shifts during the 20th century. "In 1922, an estimated 100,000 out of 1.6 million (or 6 percent) of people in Côte d'Ivoire were Muslims. By contrast, at independence (in 1960), their share of the population had increased rapidly, and Muslims were moving southward to the cocoa-producing areas and the southern cities. By 1998, \[...], Muslims constituted a majority in the north of the country, and approximately 38.6 percent of the total population. This was a significantly larger population than the next largest religious group, Christians, who constituted approximately 29.1 percent of the total."[^cite_note-19] In earlier decades, this shift was mainly due to large-scale immigration from neighboring countries of the interior, that has been going on since colonial times and continued to be promoted during the [Houphouet-Boigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houphouet-Boigny) era. Since the 1990s, the widening conversion gap between different religious groups has started to tilt the demographic balance in favor of Christians. According to the last census of 2021 Muslims make up 42.5% of population (42.9% in 2014) and Christians 39.8% (33.9% in 2014).[^cite_note-20]
