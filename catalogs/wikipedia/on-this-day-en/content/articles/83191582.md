The **2026 Ebola epidemic** is an outbreak of the [Ebola virus disease](40817590.md) (EVD) mainly in the eastern [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) (DRC) and neighbouring countries, including [Uganda](31816.md). Initial cases were reported on 14 May 2026 in the [Ituri Province](1545860.md).[^cite_note-WHOPHEIC-5] It is the 17th [Ebola outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_outbreak) in the DRC and began five months after the end of the [previous outbreak](80978995.md).[^cite_note-HAN-19-052026-6] Early infections are theorised to have occurred in January or February 2026 in the town of [Mongbwalu](34360729.md). By early August, it had become the fastest growing [Ebola](40817590.md) outbreak on record, as well as the second largest.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

It is caused by the [Bundibugyo ebolavirus](33039752.md) (BDBV) pathogen, which has complicated efforts as existing treatments and vaccines are for the [Zaire ebolavirus](40817806.md).[^cite_note-ArsTechnica-16-05-2026-11] The outbreak was declared a [public health emergency of international concern](42694003.md) (PHEIC) by the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) on 16 May 2026.[^cite_note-WHOPHEIC-5]

There have been 4,042 confirmed deaths in the DRC,[^cite_note-INSP-DRC-3][^cite_note-12] and another 2 in Uganda, as of the first week of October.[^cite_note-WHO-13][^cite_note-CDC-02-06-2026-14] Poor health infrastructure and [ongoing armed conflicts](14687290.md) in the region hinder detection, treatment, and prevention; it is considered likely that the true scale of the outbreak exceeds the confirmed numbers, [stressing a preexisting severe humanitarian crisis](#Armed_conflict_stresses_gendered_humanitarian_crisis_and_epidemic_response_efforts).[^cite_note-June5IRC-15][^cite_note-16] This added instability provided a situation ripe for rebel groups to [capitalise on](#Social_and_political_ramifications).[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:3-18]

The outbreak has spread beyond Ituri to six more provinces in the DRC, including [North Kivu](446958.md), [South Kivu](446959.md), [Haut-Uélé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-U%C3%A9l%C3%A9), [Tshopo](33835756.md), [Bas-Uélé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-U%C3%A9l%C3%A9) and [Sud-Ubangi](33835941.md). Cases were also reported in Uganda's capital, [Kampala](56689.md), and in [France](5843419.md) following the return of a health worker on a humanitarian mission.[^cite_note-France1st-19]

## Background

See also: [List of Ebola outbreaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ebola_outbreaks)

There have been 16 known previous outbreaks of [Ebola](40817590.md) in the DRC.[^cite_note-HAN-19-052026-6] There have been two previous outbreaks of Bundibugyo virus,[^cite_note-Kuhn-2010-20] one in the [Bundibugyo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundibugyo_District) of Uganda in 2007 and 2008, from which it got its name, and another in 2012 in [Isiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isiro), DRC.[^cite_note-21]

### Regional issues



A map of the armed groups operating in Ituri and Kivu provinces

The outbreak began in Ituri,[^cite_note-22] a province of eastern DRC with a long history of ethnic conflict.[^cite_note-23] The area is rich in valuable metals including gold, tin, tungsten and [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum), which are predominantly extracted by small scale [artisan mining.](26285065.md) Armed groups such as the [Islamic State](9087364.md)-aligned [Allied Democratic Forces](1258240.md) (ADF-[Baluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Baluku)), the [Cooperative for Development of the Congo](65758310.md) (CODECO), and the [Rwanda](25645.md)-backed [March 23 Movement](35878650.md) (M23)—the last of which belongs to the rebel group coalition, the [Congo River Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River_Alliance) (*Alliance Fleuve Congo*, AFC)—struggle for control of [mining areas](11617636.md).[^cite_note-\:18-24][^cite_note-colcon-25] Fighting and restrictions by armed groups restricts humanitarian access, obstructs aid operations, curtails civilian movement and limits access to essential services.[^cite_note-colcon-25]

Large scale refugee movements and cross-border travel, as well as mining-related travel, complicate [contact tracing](1724851.md). Ituri's status as a commercial and migratory hub greatly increases the risk of diseases spreading to the broader region.[^cite_note-WHODON602-26]

### Culture and misinformation

Traditional burial rituals in the region often span several days. They may involve washing and dressing the corpse, returning it to ancestral land, and touching it as part of the interment ceremony; these practices put mourners at risk of infection. In contrast, safe burial practices require that the body be immediately placed in a sealed bag and transported to a designated burial site. This potentially places health workers in conflict with cultural tradition.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

There have been several attacks on health workers, particularly those involved with burials, fuelled by [misinformation](2203174.md) about the disease and the motives driving the humanitarian response.[^cite_note-30] For example in late May 2026, a crowd at a hospital in [Mongbwalu](34360729.md) demanded the return of bodies for traditional burial and set fire to a tent used by [Médecins Sans Frontières](20498.md) (MSF), prompting the organisation to withdraw its staff; during the unrest, 18 suspected Ebola patients absconded the hospital.[^cite_note-\:1-31] A May 2026 survey by [ActionAid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ActionAid) in three health zones in Ituri found that only 64% of respondents believed Ebola was real and not caused by spiritual or mystical forces. Rumours included claims that the disease had been invented for financial gain, that health workers were spreading the virus, and that [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) could provide immunity.[^cite_note-32] Conspiracy theories also circulated on [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp).[^cite_note-\:1-31]

### Uganda

Uganda declared an outbreak of Ebola on 15 May.[^cite_note-BBC-01-06-2026-48]

On 27 May, Uganda closed its borders with DRC for at least four weeks after it reported seven cases and a death. Anyone entering Uganda from DRC was also to be isolated for 21 days.[^cite_note-49] Ugandan authorities implemented a preparedness plan and activated an Ebola treatment centre which was on standby at [Mulago hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulago_National_Specialised_Hospital). The first two cases were moved to the treatment centre; subsequent cases came direct to the hospital. An estimated 6,000 contacts were isolated for an incubation period of 21 days. On 16 July, Uganda discharged its last Ebola patient from hospital, leaving no confirmed active cases in the country.[^cite_note-50] On 28 July 2026 Uganda declared the end of the outbreak after completing a 42-day period with no new cases; the WHO certified the declaration on 26 August.[^cite_note-51] Overall, Uganda recorded 20 confirmed cases (fifteen of whom were people who had travelled from DRC) and two deaths.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### France

On 24 June, France confirmed its first case after a doctor who had been in the DRC on a humanitarian mission returned.[^cite_note-France1st-19]

### Germany

Two people were [medically evacuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medically_evacuated) to Germany; one arrived in July, while another one was discharged earlier (June), from a German hospital; both were American humanitarian workers with Ebola flown in from the DRC.[^cite_note-GermanyUSCase-54][^cite_note-55]

### Deaths

Early in the epidemic, the [case fatality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_fatality_rate) (CFR) was provisionally estimated as low as 10% to 20%. However with improved monitoring and reporting, the CFR is estimated with more confidence to be around 46%.[^cite_note-ie-56][^cite_note-57]

## Bundibugyo virus disease

Main article: [Ebola virus disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease)

### Symptoms

The most common symptoms are similar to EBOV disease due to Zaire ebolavirus; fever, diarrhoea and vomiting, headache, difficulty breathing and swallowing, and muscle and joint pain. However a study of BDBV patients in the current outbreak found that bleeding, a common feature of EBOV infection, occurred in relatively few (approximately 10%).[^cite_note-58] The lack of bleeding has made it difficult to diagnose BDBV from symptoms alone, and made it less likely that people will be isolated or taken to hospital, thus contributing to the spread of disease.[^cite_note-ed-59][^cite_note-vp-60]

### Cause

[Ebola viruses](956106.md) regularly spread in Central and West Africa. [Bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat) are often [reservoir hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir), although this has not been confirmed for this species of the virus. The virus can infect mammals that prey on bats and can be transmitted to humans through contact with infected bats, carcasses, or [bushmeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushmeat).[^cite_note-eb-61][^cite_note-62] The virus is transmitted between humans through close, direct contact with the bodily fluids of a sick or dead person; this places the close family of an infected person and health workers at particular risk.[^cite_note-63]

BDBV virus appears to replicate more slowly *in vitro* than the more common [Zaire ebolavirus](40817806.md) (EBOV).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] The strain of BDVB which is causing this outbreak is distinct from the strains which caused previous outbreaks;[^cite_note-66] a study was commissioned in August 2026 to investigate whether viral evolution, which may facilitate transmission or increase severity of the disease, may explain the unprecedented scale of this outbreak.[^cite_note-67]

### Diagnosis

Early symptoms are [non-specific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-specific), such as fever, aching, headache, weakness and fatigue, sore throat, loss of appetite.[^cite_note-68] Other diseases common in the affected area, such as [malaria](20423.md) and [typhoid fever](31596.md), can appear similar.[^cite_note-eb-61]

On 22 May 2026, [Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention](63909811.md) recommended molecular diagnostic testing (using [real-time PCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_polymerase_chain_reaction)) for identifying the [Bundibugyo ebolavirus](33039752.md) strain, because no antigen [rapid diagnostic test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_diagnostic_test) (RDT) met WHO TPP specifications for recommendation during the outbreak response.[^cite_note-69] As of 25 August 2026, a reliable test is only available in hospitals.[^cite_note-70]

### Vaccines

There is no approved vaccine or medicine specifically for Bundibugyo virus.[^cite_note-NPR16May-71][^cite_note-\:12-72] A study involving [macaques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaque) suggests that the vaccine for Zaire ebolavirus, [rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine](43779954.md) (Ervebo), may be partially effective;[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] though the WHO has judged this evidence insufficient and has recommended against its use in the current outbreak.[^cite_note-WHO-28-05-2026-75]

On 31 July, on consideration of new evidence, the WHO Technical Advisory Group authorised a Phase 3 [clinical trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial) of rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine to take place in the DRC.[^cite_note-76] As of 6 September 2026, that vaccine had been administered to 2,007 healthcare and frontline workers.[^cite_note-77]

For prevention, three potential vaccines were to be expedited; these were candidates under development by [IAVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_AIDS_Vaccine_Initiative), [Moderna](39743243.md), and the [University of Oxford](31797.md).[^cite_note-CEPI-01-06-2026-78] On 1 June 2026, the [Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations](51522180.md) (CEPI) announced funding to fast-track the three vaccine candidates. Funding allocated was IAVI (\$3.2 million), Moderna (\$50 million), and the University of Oxford (\$8.6 million).[^cite_note-CEPI-01-06-2026-78]

On 24 July 2026, the University of Oxford started a Phase 1 trial of a possible vaccine based on the [ChAdOx1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChAdOx1) vaccine technology.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

### Treatments

There is no approved treatment for Bundibugyo virus.[^cite_note-\:12-72]

On 28 May 2026, the WHO recommended several candidate treatments for clinical trials. The drugs to be trialled were the [antiviral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiviral_drug) [remdesivir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remdesivir) and the [monoclonal antibody therapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclonal_antibody_therapy) MBP-134 and [maftivimab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maftivimab).[^cite_note-WHO_experts_advise-81] The WHO-sponsored PARTNERS clinical trial protocol was deployed[^cite_note-PSI_spotlight-82] to test remdesivir and MBP-134 starting in the DRC in July.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

## Timeline of the outbreak

The outbreak may have started as early as January.[^cite_note-85] Based on models of the spread of Bundibugyo virus, it is estimated that the virus crossed over into humans in January or February, the most plausible time of a [spillover event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spillover_event) being in mid-to-late February.[^cite_note-86] The outbreak [may have started with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_case) the funeral of a pastor in [Mongbwalu](34360729.md) on 4 February. During the funeral, the coffin was broken and the corpse was handled by family members. Though the pastor was never diagnosed with Ebola, nearly 50 others were reported to have died with Ebola-like symptoms following the funeral, including several of his brothers.[^cite_note-87]

### April

The earliest suspected case in Ituri was a man who began experiencing [hemorrhagic symptoms](1719309.md) on 24 April 2026. Early household transmission occurred when a sibling developed symptoms on 26 April and died the following day. The initial case subsequently died on 28 April.[^cite_note-AfricaCDC-SitRep01-88][^cite_note-AP17May-89]

A potential outbreak signal was reported on 30 April, though initial testing in [Bunia](1925095.md) was negative for Zaire Ebola and samples were sent to the [INRB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_National_pour_la_Recherche_Biomedicale) laboratory in [Kinshasa](45773.md).[^cite_note-AfricaCDC-SitRep01-88]

### May

On 5 May, the [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) was alerted to the potential Ebola outbreak in Ituri and dispatched a response team.[^cite_note-Reuters15May-35] MSF was alerted on 9 and 10 May of suspected [viral hemorrhagic fever](1719309.md) (VHF) in Mongwalu health zone, similarly dispatching an assessment team; it found that 55 people had died since the beginning of April, receiving during their time there more reported cases in Bunia and Rwampara health zones.[^cite_note-\:12-72] On 14 May, the INRB confirmed eight cases of a non-Zaire [filovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filovirus) [aetiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetiology).[^cite_note-INSP-SitRep001-2][^cite_note-AfricaCDC-SitRep01-88] Genomic sequencing on 15 May classified them as the Bundibugyo ebolavirus species. That same day, Uganda declared an outbreak due to an imported case.[^cite_note-AfricaCDC-SitRep01-88][^cite_note-NYT15May-90]

On 15 May, the Congolese [Ministry of Public Health, Hygiene, and Social Welfare](19686492.md) publicly confirmed the outbreak of Ebola in Ituri Province, including 246 suspected cases.[^cite_note-Reuters15May2-91][^cite_note-92] By 16 May, the three health zones of Bunia, Mongwalu and Rwampara in Ituri had confirmed or suspected cases. Cases were confirmed in the Ugandan capital [Kampala](56689.md).[^cite_note-WHOPHEIC2-93] The next day, the WHO reported eight laboratory-confirmed cases in Ituri, one confirmed case in Kinshasa, and two confirmed cases in Kampala. Furthermore, given the unusual clusters of suspected cases appearing across multiple parts of the eastern DRC, the WHO was unable to ascertain the geographic spread of the epidemic or the true number of infections. Alongside the cases in Uganda confirming an international risk, this led the WHO to declare the epidemic a [public health emergency of international concern](42694003.md).[^cite_note-WHODON6022-94][^cite_note-WHOPHEIC2-93] That same day, Congolese authorities said a total of 246 people with suspected cases and over 80 deaths had been reported.[^cite_note-\:12-72]

Within days, cases were recorded to have spread to North Kivu—which was confirmed by the AFC/M23, who was primarily in control of the province.[^cite_note-95] The first cases officially recorded were in [Goma](1210483.md), North Kivu on 17 May;[^cite_note-96] [Butembo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butembo), North Kivu on 18 May;[^cite_note-97] and the Nyakunde health zone in Ituri the same day.[^cite_note-18MayWHORep1-98] The first fatalities were recorded in [Bukavu](448570.md), South Kivu on 21 May[^cite_note-99] and [Beni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), North Kivu on 29 May.[^cite_note-CIDRAP30Mar-100]

On 17 May, as hospitals in Ituri's capital [Bunia](1925095.md) became overwhelmed, health authorities in the [DRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DRC) began establishing [field hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hospital), which the WHO described as only a "first response". Authorities were working to bring supplies into the region.[^cite_note-BBC18May-101]

On 18 May, the WHO reported that the DRC had 516 suspected cases and 131 deaths, in addition to 33 confirmed cases and four confirmed deaths in four health zones in Ituri and three health zones in [North Kivu](446958.md). Uganda had two confirmed cases and one confirmed death in [Kampala](56689.md).[^cite_note-18MayWHORep1-98]

Around 18 May, an American physician and medical missionary, who had been treating patients at Nyankunde hospital in Ituri, tested positive for Bundibugyo Ebola and developed symptoms. He was evacuated to the [Charité Hospital](2559577.md) in [Berlin](3354.md), where he was isolated, received antiviral therapy, and recovered.[^cite_note-WashPost-26-05-2026-102]

On 18 May, a group of Ugandans attended a burial in eastern DRC before returning to Uganda. Some developed symptoms of Ebola and were taken to [Fort Portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Portal) for treatment.[^cite_note-103] On 19 May, the Ugandan government temporarily banned handshakes, hugs, and unnecessary physical contact.[^cite_note-104]

The [DR Congo national football team](1030243.md) cancelled parts of its pre-[2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) training camp that was originally scheduled to take place in May in [Kinshasa](45773.md), but the team still travelled to Europe and then [Texas](29810.md) before the World Cup.[^cite_note-105]

On 18 May, the [European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Centre_for_Disease_Prevention_and_Control) announced that it would send experts to the region to assist in managing the response to the epidemic.[^cite_note-106]

On 19 May, as no licensed specific treatment or vaccine existed for Bundibugyo virus disease, the WHO said, "Response strategies will rely heavily on comprehensive public health measures, including supportive care, early case detection, stringent infection prevention and control protocols, rigorous contact tracing, safe burial practices, and deep community engagement".[^cite_note-107]

On 21 May, *The Guardian* described the US as "notably absent in these efforts", due to the end of [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID), reductions in US health agencies, and cancellation of research by the [second Trump administration](78298108.md). The US withdrawal from the WHO had resulted in major reductions in the agency's capabilities.[^cite_note-108]

On 21 May, the UK announced up to £20 million to support communities affected by the Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC.[^cite_note-109] A week later, the US [State Department](31975.md) announced \$112 million of bilateral assistance aimed at supplying [PPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment), [screening](3211372.md), [contact tracing](1724851.md), and [diagnostics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnosis);[^cite_note-110] the European Union pledged €15 million to support Ebola response and preparedness efforts in the DRC and Uganda.[^cite_note-111]

On 21 May, residents protested in Rwampara, near Bunia, Ituri, after relatives tried to take the body of a young man whom they claimed had died of [typhoid fever](31596.md). [Police](51178598.md) officers fired warning shots and tear gas to disperse the crowd, and some of the protesters set two tents on fire, in which people were being treated for Ebola by a medical charity. Police and [military](8029.md) reinforcements later arrived and restored order.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

On 22 May, the WHO upgraded the Ebola risk level in the DRC to "very high", while the regional risk remained "high" and the global risk remained low.[^cite_note-115]

On 23 May, eighteen suspected people with Ebola escaped a treatment centre and returned to the community in [Mongbwalu](34360729.md), DRC, after local residents attacked and burned a tent.[^cite_note-116]

On 23 May, the Red Cross announced that three workers who died between 5 and 16 May were believed to have contracted Ebola on 27 March during dead body management activities in Mongbwalu before the outbreak had been identified.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

On 29 May the DRC's Ministry of Health updated its total suspect case count to remove suspected cases ruled out after investigation and suspected deaths pending the results of ongoing investigation.[^cite_note-CDCSituation2-119]

On 30 May, three people confirmed to have Ebola fled treatment centres in Beni, after 20 civilians were killed by [Allied Democratic Forces](40780074.md) (ADF), a militia affiliated with Islamic State.[^cite_note-120]

### June

On 1 June, five people who had recovered from Ebola were discharged from hospital in the DRC, according to health authorities.[^cite_note-BBC-01-06-2026-48] On 1 June, a burial team in Bunia were assaulted by residents, injuring four people.[^cite_note-ReutersJune5-121]

On 1 June, the medical charity MSF announced that it is building a 65-bed Ebola treatment centre in Ituri, as well as supporting health facilities in Bunia.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] By 16 June, MSF had three treatment centres in operation, in Bunia, Goma, and Mongbwalu.[^cite_note-124]

On 2 June, the WHO announced that only 116 cases out of 906 were still suspected following additional testing.[^cite_note-125]

On 2 June, the [International Rescue Committee](196452.md) warned that the outbreak was "likely far worse" than official figures suggested.[^cite_note-June5IRC-15] They estimated that only 20% of the contacts in the [contact tracing](1724851.md) process were being located.[^cite_note-June5IRC-15] They reported that people were avoiding health facilities, with many people with Ebola choosing to stay in the community.[^cite_note-June5IRC-15]

On 2 June, two people were killed in protests against a proposed Ebola quarantine facility for US citizens at [Laikipia Air Base](34558742.md) outside of [Nanyuki](2889522.md), [Kenya](188171.md).[^cite_note-126] [Kenya's High Court](36602435.md) had temporarily stopped construction of the facility, but the government vowed to continue. On 9 June, another protester was killed.[^cite_note-127]

On 4 June, an Ebola burial team was attacked in Katana, South Kivu province, by residents who took possession of the body.[^cite_note-ReutersJune5-121] Katana is located between the cities of Bukavu and Goma and is controlled by [Congo River Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River_Alliance)/[M23](35878650.md) rebels.[^cite_note-ReutersJune5-121]

On 4 June, eleven Ebola patients fled isolation facilities in Ituri.[^cite_note-128]

On 17 June, a burial team was attacked in the Mongbwalu health zone, Ituri.[^cite_note-ECDCJune17-129] Five health workers were taken hostage after being falsely accused of spreading Ebola.[^cite_note-ECDCJune17-129]

Fatalities were recorded to have occurred within the [Haut-Uélé Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-U%C3%A9l%C3%A9) on 29 June[^cite_note-CIDRAP30Mar-100] and [Kisangani](588207.md), [Tshopo](33835756.md) on 30 June (with the death occurring on 27 June following infection on 18 June).[^cite_note-130]

On 19 June, [World Food Programme](33582.md) staff reported that some patients had been fleeing treatment facilities in search of food.[^cite_note-StraitsTimesJune19-131] The DRC government reported more than 150 patient escapes from facilities since late May, for multiple reasons.[^cite_note-StraitsTimesJune19-131]

On 24 June, France confirmed its first case after a doctor returned from a humanitarian mission in the DRC.[^cite_note-France1st2-132]

27 September: Marie-Celestin Karondwa, senior member of the DRC’s ruling party, was beaten to death and his house burnt after he appeared a radio program in which he promoted preventative measures against the disease. The perpetrators and their motives are unknown.[^cite_note-133]

### July

On 11 July, the [United States](3434750.md) (US) confirmed a US citizen who had been working for a humanitarian organisation to combat the outbreak in DRC had tested positive for the disease.[^cite_note-134]

On 15 July, MSF described the epidemic: "*In two months, this outbreak has become the third largest, and fastest growing, Ebola disease outbreak on record*", adding that rural communities still lacked adequate support.[^cite_note-135]

On 15 July, staff in Bunia General Hospital and Rwampara General Hospital were on strike due to unpaid wages and difficult working conditions.[^cite_note-136]

### August

On 2 August, the WHO noted that this is the second largest outbreak of Ebola disease in history, exceeded only by the [Western Africa epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_African_Ebola_epidemic) of 2013–2016.[^cite_note-137]

As of 9 August 45 health workers had died, with 155 having been infected.[^cite_note-138]

A fatality occurred in [Buta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buta,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), [Bas-Uélé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-U%C3%A9l%C3%A9) on 13 August.[^cite_note-139]

On 17 August, it was reported that a mob set fire to an ambulance in the [Aru Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aru_Territory) near Bunia in a non-isolated incident. This came a day after a second ambulance was attacked in the area.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

On 23 August the outbreak had reached 5,000 confirmed cases in the first 100 days; by contrast the [Western Africa Ebola epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_African_Ebola_epidemic) (2013-2016) reached 1,000 cases in the same time.[^cite_note-ed-59][^cite_note-vp-60]

### September

On 11 September, a man died in the town of Gwaka, ⁠[Sud-Ubangi](33835941.md), in northwest DRC, having tested positive for Bundibugyo. This is far from the epidemic's epicentre in eastern DRC. He had travelled from South Kivu, passing through Rwanda and Uganda before reentering and transiting DRC. There are concerns that he may have had contact with and infected many people during this journey.[^cite_note-142]

## Social and political ramifications

### M23 capitalises on Ebola outbreak to exercise its governance capabilities

See also: [Kivu conflict](14687290.md) and [M23 campaign (2022–present)](71001703.md)



A view of [Goma](1210483.md), North Kivu—which M23 [captured on 30 January 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Goma_offensive), two weeks before it [took over Bukavu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bukavu_offensive) on 16 February; it has controlled both major cities during the epidemic

[^cite_note-\:10-143]

The outbreak of the Ebola epidemic presented an opportunity for the Rwandan proxy group, [M23](35878650.md)—the [Kivu conflict](14687290.md)'s largest belligerent aside from the [Congolese armed forces](8029.md)—to test its governing capabilities in territories it has captured.[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:3-18][^cite_note-\:10-143][^cite_note-\:11-144] Controlling a majority of the [North](446958.md) and [South Kivu](446959.md) provinces, including their respective capitals of [Goma](1210483.md) and [Bukavu](448570.md),[^cite_note-\:3-18] M23 had already been imposing taxes and fulfilling other administrative roles before the outbreak.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-\:4-147] Analysts warned that the presence of the conflict would further complicate containment of the epidemic,[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:3-18] including difficulties in reaching patients in remote locations fearing their safety,[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:15-148][^cite_note-\:17-149] thus deterring them from seeking medical care.[^cite_note-\:4-147] Indeed, mobilisation by "trusted community leaders, long-standing humanitarian actors, and UN partners supported by skilled, largely African experts with deep experience in past Ebola outbreaks" were the most significant tool which helped contain the 2018–2020 outbreak.[^cite_note-150] Despite this, M23 was believed to see the DRC's governance void as a chance to bolster public favour in their administrative capabilities.[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:3-18][^cite_note-\:4-147]

> AFC/M23 is keen to demonstrate its capacity to function as a state and manage a public health crisis better than the Congolese government, but with only four cases recorded, it has been a limited test so far.

— Reagan Miviri, [Kinshasa](45773.md)-based Ebuteli research institute, 13 July 2026[^cite_note-\:3-18]

To respond to the epidemic, AFC/M23 established an Ebola response team and administration.[^cite_note-\:22-151][^cite_note-\:23-152] These teams recorded cases, monitored them, and completed daily follow-ups with patients;[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-\:3-18][^cite_note-\:11-144] quarantined confirmed cases;[^cite_note-\:11-144][^cite_note-\:5-154] restricted movement for the local population, including barring, limiting, and running health and safety checks for [bus](4146.md), [minibus](1094925.md), [taxi](22026933.md), and [passenger boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_ship) services;[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:10-143][^cite_note-\:6-155][^cite_note-\:7-156] and released cartoons about hand washing.[^cite_note-\:2-17] These operations were supported by the [Rwandan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Rwanda) through thousands of [US dollars](18717338.md) worth of equipment and medicine from Rwandan medical facilities.[^cite_note-\:3-18] To support their popular support, the AFC/M23 produced promotional videos visiting laboratories and research centres,[^cite_note-\:2-17] some of which they helped run.[^cite_note-Kaniki-Interview-21July2026-145][^cite_note-\:23-152] Raimo Mukiza, a taxi-bus driver in Goma, said, however, that "some people follow the measures and others don’t." [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md)-[laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_Peace_Prize_laureates) [Denis Mukwege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Mukwege) corroborated that for those trapped in AFC/M23 territory, the risk is "to be contaminated by Ebola, or to [die by hunger](216361.md#Democratic_Republic_of_Congo) or [by violence](14687290.md#Human_rights_abuses)." Having appealed to support from the US, Mukwege said "the world closes its eyes on this situation."[^cite_note-\:2-17] M23 also appealed to an international audience, claiming in mid-March 2026 that it would work with international partners to contain the outbreak.[^cite_note-157]

### M23 falsely claims epidemic eradication, Rwandan support, and political tensions

Further information: [M23 campaign (2022–present) § Rwandan role](71001703.md#Rwandan_role)

See also: [Democratic Republic of the Congo–Rwanda conflict (2022–present)](71002044.md) and [DRC–Rwanda relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DRC%E2%80%93Rwanda_relations)

Following the outbreak on 14 May, AFC/M23 said it had recorded four cases within its territories.[^cite_note-\:3-18] In late June, following a 21-day monitoring period where it recorded no new cases, and in stark contrast to the rest of the country—AFC/M23 falsely claimed to have stopped the outbreak—hastily attempting to prove their administrative dominance over the Congolese government.[^cite_note-\:3-18][^cite_note-\:11-144][^cite_note-\:22-151] To bolster public sentiment of their capabilities, AFC/M23 deputy coordinator for the response to the outbreak, [Dr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_\(title\)) Freddy Kaniki[^cite_note-Kaniki-Interview-21July2026-145]—an [MRDP-Twirwaneho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twirwaneho)-aligned doctor who trained in the US and helped respond to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md)[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-\:24-159]—said the group had recorded a total of 400 contacts and led 207 test which *[Reuters](18998750.md)* had independently verified by 18 June.[^cite_note-\:3-18] Until that point, he said AFC/M23's efforts had been bolstered by aid sent into the DRC from the WHO and the [UN Children's Fund](19698110.md) (UNICEF), despite not yet having cooperated with the Congolese government.[^cite_note-\:22-151] Having spoken of the same concerns on 21 March,[^cite_note-\:24-159] Kaniki said on 22 May this aid "should not be political" and should "save lives first."[^cite_note-160] AFC/M23's claim to have eradicated the virus did not hold up, however, following reports on two new cases being imported from [Lubero](41967894.md) on shared transport services in mid-August. This prompted AFC/M23 to impose travel restrictions.[^cite_note-\:7-156][^cite_note-\:10-143][^cite_note-\:22-151] Another new case was recorded in the Kayna health zone, located near Lubero, in early September. The 21-year-old male patient, who had arrived from [Butembo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butembo), was said to have been transferred to a quarantine facility. AFC/M23 said it had begun tracing contacts connected with the patient and stepped up surveillance to prevent further spreading.[^cite_note-\:5-154][^cite_note-161]



M23 rebels in [Bunagana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunagana,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), North Kivu in 2012, where the group's insurgency, [aided by Rwanda](71001703.md#Rwandan_role), [resurged in 2022](71001703.md#Secondary_offensives_and_ceasefire)

Kaniki argued that AFC/M23's isolation and monitoring of cases and contacts displayed a greater "discipline" and "anticipation" than that of the central government, despite relying heavily on the aid of Rwanda. By that time, AFC/M23 had received thousands of dollars in materials, including US\$6,891 from the Rwanda Biomedical Center and US\$85,467 from the Gisenyi Hospital—which documents reveal left response teams still desperately underresourced.[^cite_note-\:3-18] Rwanda continued to deny backing the rebels,[^cite_note-\:5-154][^cite_note-\:6-155] with Rwandan government spokesperson Yolande Makolo saying Kigali's support was because infections diseases "do not respect borders."[^cite_note-\:3-18] AFC/M23 went so far as to accuse the Congolese government of showing "indifference" towards those in Ituri, prompting Congolese foreign minister, [Thérèse Kayikwamba Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Kayikwamba_Wagner), to responded with the belief that "both the M23 and Rwanda are ill-equipped to run an emergency response for a threat of this magnitude" and that they "must leave the DRC."[^cite_note-\:2-17] Despite political tension, some cooperation to halt the outbreak occurred. The AFC/M23 shared with the DRC limited information about data and laboratory testing, which could not be independently verified despite the DRC continuing to pay health workers in rebel-controlled territories.[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:3-18]

### Armed conflict stresses gendered humanitarian crisis and epidemic response efforts



A camp of [internally displaced persons](427408.md) (IDPs) around a [MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) instillation in [Kitchanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchanga), North Kivu—a historic camp for IDPs—in 2013; M23 [captured the town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Kitshanga) in 2023

In spite of the epidemic's outbreak, armed fighting continued between M23 and the Congolese armed forces in the eastern regions of the DRC.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-\:13-163][^cite_note-\:9-164] Deepening the severe humanitarian crisis, determined to be underway long before the epidemic's outbreak—including [consistent attacks and massacres](79359053.md), [prevalent food insecurity](216361.md#Democratic_Republic_of_Congo), one of the [largest internally displaced populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_persons_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), [rampant reports of sexual violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_violence_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), and numerous other insecurities—analysts,[^cite_note-\:7-156][^cite_note-\:13-163] [non-government organisations](46539.md) (NGOs),[^cite_note-\:12-72] [international organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organisations) (IOs),[^cite_note-\:8-165] and [civil societies](209443.md)[^cite_note-\:9-164] feared the conflict and resulting insecurity would impact the mitigation of the epidemic. International organisations and experts have highlighted the critical funding gap needed to alleviate the suffering of populations in the country's eastern provinces.[^cite_note-\:15-148][^cite_note-\:7-156][^cite_note-\:8-165][^cite_note-166] Furthermore, domestic and international actors alike have called on further cooperation to deliver, funding for, and supplying of medical equipment to avoid further loss of life.[^cite_note-\:9-164][^cite_note-AP-29May2026-167]

> People are displaced by the conflict. Hundreds of thousands of people were displaced and about 100,000 new displaced people were registered, I think, in the first few months. This makes it very difficult to control the epidemic.

— [Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus](39712205.md), [Director-General of the WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_the_WHO), 29 May 2026[^cite_note-\:9-164][^cite_note-AP-29May2026-167]

Echoing Tedros' concerns for coordination,[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-AP-29May2026-167] [European Commissioner for Preparedness and Crisis Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Preparedness_and_Crisis_Management), [Hadja Lahbib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadja_Lahbib), argued that civilians populations displaced by armed conflict should be a top priority for epidemic relief efforts. Reassuring that the EU was in discussion with belligerents, Lahbib called for both to "allow safe, rapid and unhindered humanitarian access." Rodriguez Munguakonkwa, President of the Civil Society of [Kabare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabare_Territory), South Kivu, said that the response should be a "close and well-structured collaboration" that is "visible."[^cite_note-\:9-164] Like other analysts emphasised,[^cite_note-\:11-144] Munguakonkwa added that "if, despite the armed conflict, everyone continues to consider themselves enemies even in such health circumstances, it is the population who will suffer."[^cite_note-\:9-164] [CARE International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARE_International),[^cite_note-\:15-148] the [International Rescue Committee](196452.md) (IRC),[^cite_note-169] [UN Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women),[^cite_note-\:16-170] and others have pointed out that [gendered dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_role) of [caregiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caregiver), [food preparation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_food_preparation), and [burial participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral) impact women more, accounting for approximately between 53% to 60% of cases in the outbreak by June 2026 according to studies,[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-\:16-170][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] as it had in previous epidemics in the DRC.[^cite_note-174]

### Humanitarian aid crisis, strikes by health workers, and health worker shortages

Further information: [Executive Order 14169](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14169) and [United Nations § 2020s: financial crisis](31769.md#2020s:_financial_crisis)

Decline in foreign aid to the DRC—originating from an internationalised [humanitarian aid funding crisis](434679.md#2020s) spurred on in earnest by [foreign aid assistance freezes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14169) and the [dismantling of USAID](82201872.md) throughout 2025 at the behest of the US [Donald Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Donald_Trump)—weakened local health and surveillance systems.[^cite_note-\:18-24][^cite_note-\:15-148][^cite_note-\:13-163] In addition, fears of siphoning out health funds were reported, prompting DRC's health ministry to begin verifying names on their payroll.[^cite_note-\:14-175][^cite_note-\:21-176] Both having played a large part in causing funding to cease or slow for health workers in eastern regions of the DRC for two or more months, numerous strikes by health workers—including those at treatment centres, epidemiologists, case investigators, drivers, and [gravediggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravedigger)—occurred throughout the epidemic.[^cite_note-\:14-175][^cite_note-\:25-177] Many workers continued to work unpaid, despite the risks of working at treatment, infection, and care centers. They report receiving promises from the Congolese government stating that they would receive their outstanding payments. One veteran of the 2018 Ebola epidemic and health worker in this epidemic, Wiza Bondele, said he didn't know "whether it is injustice or whether there simply isn’t enough money."[^cite_note-\:21-176]

Strikes were reported at Rwampara General Hospital in Ituri on 13 July;[^cite_note-\:14-175] at Bunia General Hospital in Ituri on 15 July;[^cite_note-\:17-149] at the Elikya Ebola Treatment Center in Bunia, Ituri on 25 July—despite the Operations Manager Dr. Adelard Lufungula saying the issue was "being resolved" as the workers were being paid via [mobile money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_payment);[^cite_note-\:21-176][^cite_note-\:25-177] and before the governor's office in Bunia, Ituri on 6 August.[^cite_note-178] More retaliation was reported in September, when some health workers violated [Ebola control protocols](40817590.md#Corpses,_burial) by allowing grieving families to touch bodies of relatives killed by the virus.[^cite_note-179]

Soon after the beginning of the epidemic, regions facing Ebola began running short on health workers.[^cite_note-180]

### Other armed insurgencies threaten epidemic containment

See also: [CODECO § History](65758310.md#History), and [Allied Democratic Forces insurgency](40780074.md)



[MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) forces responding to an attack on and burning of a civilian vehicle by CODECO forces in [Djugu territory](69530633.md), Ituri in 2022

A number of other armed rebels' insurgencies belonging to the broader Kivu conflict further hindered containment of the epidemic, including CODECO and the ADF,[^cite_note-\:19-181][^cite_note-\:20-182] the latter of infamously [carry out massacres of entire villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Allied_Democratic_Forces_attacks).[^cite_note-183] CODECO violence had estimated to have displaced 1 million people since their [resurgence in 2017](65758310.md#Return_as_armed_group), which have culminated in many victims of violence fleeing to IDP camps with poor conditions—fostering poor sanitation conditions ripe for the spread of Ebola.[^cite_note-\:20-182] Alarmingly, between April and June, ADF rebels [escalated their campaign](40780074.md) on civilians,[^cite_note-184] moving north into the Haut-Uélé, where they did not typically lead operations—having remained for many years resigned to killings in Ituri and North Kivu.[^cite_note-\:19-181] The ADF had carried out attacks against health workers before the outbreak of the Ebola epidemic, including the killing of 17 patients at a health centre in November 2025,[^cite_note-\:19-181][^cite_note-185] raising security measures around operations of health workers during the epidemic.[^cite_note-\:19-181][^cite_note-\:20-182] Analysts increasingly argued [Operation Shujaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shujaa)—the joint programme between the [DRC](8029.md) and [Uganda's armed forces](56928.md) fighting the group—could not adequately respond to the violence despite making marginal progress.[^cite_note-186]
