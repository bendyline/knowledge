**Cyprus** is an island in the Eastern Basin of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). It is the [third-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Mediterranean#By_area) in the Mediterranean, after the [Italian](14532.md) islands of [Sicily](27619.md) and [Sardinia](29376.md), and the [80th-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) in the world by area. It is located south of the [Anatolian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), and belongs to the [Cyprus Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Arc).[^cite_note-1] Geographically, Cyprus is located in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia),[^cite_note-2] but the country is considered a European country in [political geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography).[^cite_note-3] Cyprus also had lengthy periods of mainly [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) and intermittent [Anatolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_peoples), [Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Ottoman](22278.md), and [Western European](33800.md) influence.

The island is dominated by two mountain ranges, the [Troodos Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troodos_Mountains) and the [Kyrenia Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_Mountains) or Pentadaktylos, and the central plain, the [Mesaoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesaoria), between them.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The Troodos Mountains cover most of the southern and western portions of the island and account for roughly half its area.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The narrow [Kyrenia Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_Mountains) extends along the northern coastline.[^cite_note-\:0-4] It is not as high as the Troodos Mountains, and it occupies substantially less area.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The two mountain ranges run generally parallel to the [Taurus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains) on the Turkish mainland, the outlines of which are visible from northern Cyprus.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Coastal lowlands, varying in width, surround the island.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

[Geopolitically](163225.md), the island is divided into four segments. The [Republic of Cyprus](5593.md), the only internationally recognized government, governs the southern 60% of the island, and has been a [member state of the European Union](10890716.md) since 1 May 2004. The [Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus](271409.md), diplomatically recognized only by Turkey, occupies the northern third of the island, which is around 36% of the territory. The [United Nations](31769.md)-controlled [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Buffer_Zone_in_Cyprus) is a buffer zone that separates the two and it is about 4%. Lastly, two areas—[Akrotiri and Dhekelia](1025144.md)—remain under British sovereignty for military purposes, collectively forming the Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri and Dhekelia (SBA). The SBAs are located on the southern coast of the island and together encompass 254 km<sup>2</sup>, or 2.8% of the island.[^cite_note-5]

## Terrain

Topography



Detailed map of Cyprus

There are three distinct geologic regions on the island. In the central and western part of the island is the Troodos Massif, a mountain range whose surface layer is mostly basaltic lava rock, and whose maximum elevation is 1,952 m (6,404 ft). Running in a thin arc along the northeast margin of the island is Cyprus's second mountain range, a limestone formation called the Kyrenia Range. The space between these ranges is home to the capital [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia), visible as a grayish-brown patch near the image's centre.

The rugged [Troodos Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troodos_Mountains), whose principal range stretches from Pomos Point in the northwest almost to Larnaca Bay on the east, are the single most conspicuous feature of the landscape.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Intensive uplifting and folding in the formative period left the area highly fragmented, so that subordinate ranges and spurs veer off at many angles, their slopes incised by steep-sided valleys.[^cite_note-\:0-4] In the southwest, the mountains descend in a series of stepped foothills to the coastal plain.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

While the Troodos Mountains are a massif formed of molten [igneous rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock), the [Kyrenia Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_Range) is a narrow [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) ridge that rises suddenly from the plains.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Its easternmost extension becomes a series of foothills on the [Karpas Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karpas_Peninsula).[^cite_note-\:0-4] That peninsula points toward Asia Minor, to which Cyprus belongs geologically.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The Kyrenia Range is also known as the Pentadactylon Mountains, due to a summit resembling five fingers.

Even the highest peaks of the Kyrenia Range are hardly more than half the height of the great dome of the Troodos massif, [Mount Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus_\(Cyprus\)) (1,952 m or 6,404 ft),[^cite_note-Embassy_climate-6] but their seemingly inaccessible, jagged slopes make them considerably more spectacular.[^cite_note-\:0-4] [British](31717.md) writer [Lawrence Durrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Durrell), in [Bitter Lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitter_Lemons), wrote of the Troodos as "an unlovely jumble of crags and heavyweight rocks" and of the Kyrenia Range as belonging to "the world of [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art) [Europe](9239.md), its lofty crags studded with crusader castles."[^cite_note-\:0-4]

Rich copper deposits were discovered in antiquity on the slopes of the Troodos.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The massive sulphide deposits formed as a part of an [ophiolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiolite) complex at a [spreading centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-ocean_ridge) under the Mediterranean Sea which was [tectonically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) uplifted during the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) and emplaced in its current location.[^cite_note-7]

## Drainage

In much of the island, access to a year-round supply of water is difficult.[^cite_note-\:0-4] This is traditionally attributed to deforestation which damaged the island's drainage system through erosion,[^cite_note-\:0-4] but Grove and Rackham question this view.[^cite_note-8] A network of winter rivers rises in the Troodos Mountains and flows out from them in all directions.[^cite_note-\:0-4] The Yialias River and the [Pedhieos River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedieos) flow eastward across the Mesaoria into Famagusta Bay; the Serraghis River flows northwest through the [Morphou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCzelyurt_\(Northern_Cyprus\)) plain.[^cite_note-\:0-4] All of the island's rivers, however, are dry in the summer.[^cite_note-\:0-4] An extensive system of dams and waterways has been constructed to bring water to farming areas.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

The central Mesaoria plain is the agricultural heartland of the island, but its productiveness for wheat and barley depends very much on winter rainfall; other crops are grown under irrigation.[^cite_note-\:0-4]This broad, central plain, open to the sea at either end, was once covered with rich forests. However, the timber was needed by ancient conquerors for their sailing vessels, so little evidence of the woodland remains .[^cite_note-\:0-4] The now-divided capital of the island, [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia), lies in the middle of this central plain.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Natural vegetation



Cyprus countryside on the way to Troodos Mountains during the summer

Despite its small size, Cyprus has a variety of natural vegetation. This includes forests of conifers and broadleaved trees such as [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine) (*[Pinus brutia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_brutia)*), [cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus), [cypresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress), and [oaks](39696.md). Ancient authors write that most of Cyprus, even Messaoria, was heavily forested, and there are still considerable forests on the Troodos and Kyrenia ranges, and locally at lower altitudes. About 12% of the whole island is classified as woodland. Where there is no forest, tall shrub communities of golden oak (*[Quercus alnifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_alnifolia)*), strawberry tree (*[Arbutus andrachne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbutus_andrachne)*), terebinth (*[Pistacia terebinthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terebinth)*), olive (*[Olea europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive)*), kermes oak (*[Quercus coccifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_coccifera)*), and styrax (*[Styrax officinalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styrax_officinalis)*) are found, but such [maquis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquis_shrubland) is uncommon. Over most of the island untilled ground bears a grazed covering of [garrigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrigue), largely composed of low bushes of [Cistus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistus), *[Genista sphacelata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genista)*, *[Calicotome villosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calicotome_villosa)*, *Lithospermum hispidulum*, *[Phagnalon rupestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagnalon),* and, locally, *[Pistacia lentiscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistacia_lentiscus)*. Where grazing is excessive this covering is soon reduced, and an impoverished [batha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrigue) remains, consisting principally of *[Thymus capitatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus_capitatus)*, *[Sarcopoterium spinosum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcopoterium)*, and a few stunted herbs.

## Climate

Main article: [Climate of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Cyprus)



[Sandstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm) in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant), October 19, 2002

The intense [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), warm and rather dry, with rainfall mainly between November and March, favours agriculture.[^cite_note-\:0-4] In general, the island experiences extremely mild wet winters and dry hot summers.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Variations in temperature and rainfall are governed by altitude and, to a lesser extent, distance from the coast.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Hot, dry summers from mid-May to mid-September and rainy, rather changeable winters from November to mid-March are separated by short autumn and spring seasons.

## Environmental concerns

**Natural hazards:** moderate [earthquake](10106.md) activity; [droughts](37335.md)

**Environment – current issues:** [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water) resource problems (no natural [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir) catchments, seasonal disparity in rainfall, [sea water intrusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_intrusion) to island's largest [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer), increased [salination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_salinity) in the north); water pollution from sewage and industrial wastes; coastal degradation; loss of wildlife habitats from [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization).
