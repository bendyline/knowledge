The **Ministry of Justice of Sudan** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): وزارة العدل السودانية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Wizārat al-ʻAdl*) was created in 1956 by Mohammed Ahmed Abu Ranat and Ahmed Metwally al-Atabani (who became a judiciary head and deputy general respectively after the country's independence from [Anglo-Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan) rule). In 1983, the ministry's responsibilities were clearly defined to include representing the state in legal affairs, reviewing and reforming laws that promote justice, and other functions.[^cite_note-2]

Before 2019 and signing of the [Draft Constitutional Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)#Draft_Constitutional_Declaration), the Minister of Justice also served as the **Attorney General**, combining executive oversight of the justice system with prosecutorial authority. On 10 October 2019, [Tag el-Sir el-Hibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tag_el-Sir_el-Hibir) was appointed as the Attorney General but the office was abolished following the [2021 coup d'état](69110231.md).[^cite_note-3]
