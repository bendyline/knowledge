**Bremen** (German pronunciation: [\[ˈbʁeːmən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a1/De-Bremen.ogg/De-Bremen.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a1/De-Bremen.ogg/De-Bremen.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>),[^cite_note-4] officially the **City Municipality of Bremen**,[^cite_note-5] is the capital and most populous city of the [German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of the [Free Hanseatic City of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\)) (*Freie Hansestadt Bremen*), a two-city-state consisting of the city of Bremen itself, and [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven). An important port city with about 586,000 inhabitants, this [Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) city is the [11th-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population) of Germany and the second-largest city in [Northern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Germany) after [Hamburg](13467.md).[^cite_note-6]

Bremen is the largest city on the [River Weser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Weser), the longest river flowing entirely in Germany, lying some 60 km (37 mi) upstream from its [mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_mouth) into the [North Sea](21179.md) at [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven), and is completely surrounded by the state of [Lower Saxony](18435.md). Bremen is the centre of the [Northwest Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Metropolitan_Region), which also includes the cities of [Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldenburg_\(city\)) and Bremerhaven, and has a population of around 2.8 million people. Bremen is contiguous with the Lower Saxon towns of [Delmenhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmenhorst), [Stuhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuhr), [Achim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achim), [Weyhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyhe), [Schwanewede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwanewede) and [Lilienthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilienthal,_Lower_Saxony). There is an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of Bremen in Bremerhaven, the "City of Bremen Overseas Port Area Bremerhaven" (*Stadtbremisches Überseehafengebiet Bremerhaven*). Bremen is the third-largest city in the [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) dialect area after Hamburg and [Dortmund](149349.md).

The city traces its origins to a settlement in the early [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and became the seat of a [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Archbishopric_of_Bremen) in 787. During the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages), Bremen grew into a flourishing trading centre and joined the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) in the 13th century, establishing commercial links across [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe). Its autonomy as a [free imperial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city) was recognised within the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), and despite periods of conflict, Bremen retained a strong civic identity. In later centuries, the city developed into a key hub for shipping and overseas trade, contributing to Germany's [maritime economy](28769.md).

Bremen is a major cultural and economic hub of Northern Germany. The city hosts a variety of galleries and museums, ranging from historical sculptures to major art museums, such as the [Bremen Overseas Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Museum,_Bremen) or the [Weserburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weserburg).[^cite_note-7] The [Bremen City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_City_Hall) and the [Bremen Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Roland) form the [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) "Town Hall and Roland on the Marketplace of Bremen". In 2023, Bremen was named a [UNESCO City of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_City_of_Literature), joining UNESCO's [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network). The city is well-known through the [Brothers Grimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_Grimm)'s fairy tale "[Town Musicians of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Musicians_of_Bremen)", and there is a statue dedicated to it in front of the city hall. [Bremen's port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports_of_Bremen), together with the port of Bremerhaven at the mouth of the Weser, is the second-largest port in Germany after the [Port of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg). [Bremen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Airport) lies in the southern borough of Neustadt-Neuenland and is Germany's [12th-busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Germany).

## History

Main article: [History of Bremen (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bremen_\(city\))

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Bremen).



16th century view of the city

For most of its 1,200 year history, Bremen was an independent city within the confederal jurisdiction of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). Its governing merchants and [guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guild) were at the centre of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) that sought to monopolise the [North Sea](21179.md) and [Baltic Sea](3335.md) trade. To enlarge and confirm its independence, the city had, until the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation), to contend with the temporal power of [the Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Archbishopric_of_Bremen), and after the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) with [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire), the masters of the surrounding [Duchy of Bremen-Verden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen-Verden). [George I Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_I_of_Great_Britain), the [Elector of Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Hanover) (and from 1714, [King of Great Britain and Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland)) who in 1715 acquired Bremen-Verden, recognised Bremen as a free city in 1720.[^cite_note-eb-8] The city was captured in 1806 and then annexed by [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) in 1810, before it regained independence in 1815.[^cite_note-eb-8]

In the late nineteenth century, Bremen was drawn by [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) into the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). With its new sea anchorage and wharves at Bremerhaven, it was the principal port of embarkation for German and central European emigrants to the Americas, and an [entrepôt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrep%C3%B4t) for Germany's late developing colonial trade. The [Norddeutscher Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norddeutscher_Lloyd) (NDL), founded in Bremen in 1857, became one of the world's leading shipping companies.

After the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), Bremen, a broadly [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_\(Germany\)) and [social-democratic](60585.md) city, and briefly a [Soviet republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Soviet_Republic), lost its autonomy under the [Hitler regime](21212.md). After [World War II](32927.md), in which almost two thirds of the city's fabric was destroyed, the autonomy was restored. Bremen became one of the founding [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) (*Länder*) of the [Federal Republic (West Germany)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany).

## Geography



View from the [Stephanibrücke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanibr%C3%BCcke?action=edit\&redlink=1) towards the city centre and cathedral

Bremen lies on both sides of the [River Weser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Weser), about 60 kilometres (37 miles) upstream of its estuary on the [North Sea](21179.md) and its transition to the Outer Weser by Bremerhaven. Opposite Bremen's *Altstadt* is the point where the "Middle Weser" becomes the "Lower Weser" and, from the area of Bremen's port, the river has been made navigable to ocean-going vessels. The region on the left bank of the Lower Weser, through which the [Ochtum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochtum) flows, is the Weser Marshes, the landscape on its right bank is part of the [Elbe-Weser Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe-Weser_Triangle). The [Lesum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesum), and its tributaries, the [Wümme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCmme) and [Hamme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamme_\(river\)), the [Schönebecker Aue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%B6nebecker_Aue) and [Blumenthaler Aue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumenthaler_Aue?action=edit\&redlink=1), are the downstream tributaries of the Weser.

The city's municipal area is about 38 kilometres (24 miles) long and 16 kilometres (10 miles) wide. In terms of area, Bremen is the eleventh largest city in Germany; and in terms of population the second largest city in northwest Germany after Hamburg and the eleventh largest in the whole of Germany *(see: [List of cities in Germany by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population))*.

Bremen lies about 50 kilometres (31 miles) east of the city of [Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldenburg_\(city\)), 110 kilometres (68 miles) southwest of [Hamburg](13467.md), 120 kilometres (75 miles) northwest of [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), 100 kilometres (62 miles) north of [Minden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minden) and 105 kilometres (65 miles) northeast of [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck). Part of [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven)'s port territory forms an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of the City of Bremen.

### Hills of Bremen

The inner city lies on a Weser dune, which reaches a natural height of 10.5 metres (34 feet, 6 inches) above sea level at [Bremen Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Cathedral); its highest point, though, is 14.4 metres (47 feet, 3 inches) above sea level and lies to the east at the *[Polizeihaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polizeihaus)*, *Am Wall* 196. The highest natural feature in the city of Bremen is 32.5 metres (107 feet) above sea level and lies in [Friedehorst Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedehorst_Park) in the northwestern borough of Burglesum.[^cite_note-JB2016-9]<sup>: 25 </sup> As a result, Bremen has the lowest [high point of all the German states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_highest_points_of_the_German_states).[^cite_note-10]

### Climate

Bremen has a moderate [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfb*) due to its proximity to the North Sea coast and temperate maritime air masses that move in with the predominantly westerly winds from the Atlantic Ocean. However, periods in which continental air masses predominate may occur at any time of the year and can lead to heat waves in the summer and prolonged periods of frost in the winter. In general though, extremes are rare in Bremen and temperatures below −15 °C (5.0 °F) and above 35 °C (95.0 °F) occur only once every couple of years. The record high temperature was 37.6 °C (99.7 °F) on 9 August 1992, while the official record low temperature was −23.6 °C (−10.5 °F) on 13 February 1940. On 13 October 2018, Bremen recorded its warmest October day on record with 28.6 °C (83.4 °F).[^cite_note-11] However, the astronomer [Heinrich Wilhelm Matthias Olbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Wilhelm_Matthias_Olbers) reported to have measured −27.3 °C on 23 January 1823.[^cite_note-12] Being at some distance from the main North Sea, Bremen still has a somewhat wider temperature range than [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven) that is located on the mouth of the Weser.

Average temperatures have risen continually over the last decades, leading to a 1.0 °C (1.8 °F) rise in the mean annual temperature between 1961–90 and 1991–2020 reference periods. As in most parts of Germany, the year 2024 has been the warmest year on record averaging 11.4 °C (52.5 °F), making Bremen the second-warmest German state after Berlin.[^cite_note-13] While Bremen is located in the comparatively cloudy northwestern part of Germany, there has been a significant increase in average sunshine hours over the last decades, especially in the months of April, May, and July, causing the annual mean to rise by 121 hours between the reference periods of 1961–90 and 1991–2020.[^cite_note-14] This trend has continued over the last 10 years (2016–2025), which average ca. 1760 hours of sunshine, almost 300 hours more than in the international reference period of 1961–90.[^cite_note-15] Nevertheless, especially the winters remain extremely gloomy by international standards with December averaging hardly more than one hour of sunshine (out of 7 astronomically possible) per day, a feature that Bremen shares with most of Germany and its neighbouring countries, though.

Precipitation is distributed fairly evenly around the year with a small peak in summer mainly due to convective precipitation, i.e. showers and thunderstorms. Snowfall and the period of snow cover are variable; whereas in some years, hardly any snow accumulation occurs, there has recently been a series of unusually snowy winters, peaking in the record year 2010 counting 84 days with a snow cover.[^cite_note-16] Nevertheless, snow accumulation of more than 20 centimetres (8 in) remains exceptional, the record being 68 centimetres (26.8 in) of snow on 18 February 1979.

The warmest months in Bremen are June, July, and August, with average high temperatures of 20.2 to 22.6 °C (68.4 to 72.7 °F). The coldest are December, January, and February, with average low temperatures of −1.1 to 0.3 °C (30.0 to 32.5 °F). Typical of its maritime location, autumn tends to remain mild well into October, while spring arrives later than in the southwestern parts of the country.

Bremen's economy boomed in line with the West German *[Wirtschaftswunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftswunder)* of the 1950s and 60s. This saw the growth, and permanent settlement, of a large [migrant worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_worker) population, drawn largely from [Turkey](11125639.md) and southern Europe. A new wave of immigration occurred after the turn of new century, following the entry of [Poland](22936.md), [Bulgaria](3415.md) and other former [East Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries into the [European Union](9317.md), and after 2015 with the settlement of [refugees from Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_civil_war) and other war-torn regions. Today Bremen has a population of 567,000 and is the 11th largest city in Germany and 5th largest city by area with area of 318.21 km<sup>2</sup> (122.86 sq mi), which makes this city area bigger than [Munich](19058.md).

By the second decade of the century out of a population (including [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven)) of approximately 680,000, over 115,000 had foreign citizenship,[^cite_note-JB2016-9][^cite_note-21] and nearly twice that number, almost a third of the total population, could be classified as having non-German origin/ethnicity.[^cite_note-22]

## Politics

The *Stadtbürgerschaft* (municipal assembly) is currently made up of 72 of the 87 legislators of the state legislature, the *[Bremische Bürgerschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCrgerschaft_of_Bremen)* who reside in the city of Bremen. The legislature is elected by the citizens of Bremen every four years.



Parliament building at the Market square

Bremen has a reputation as a [left-wing](18499.md) city. The port, shipyards and related industries sustained a large and unionised working class. This translated into support for the [Social Democrats](60585.md), considered Bremen's natural governing party. However, in the 1980s [mechanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanization) of the port and closure of the city's leading shipbuilder induced an employment crisis and shook the confidence of the party's traditional voter base. The SPD, which had still polled 51% in 1987, lost its effective majority. The once dominant left-liberal vote split, and coalition government became the norm.[^cite_note-25] The state today is governed by a coalition of the Social Democratic Party, [The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens) and [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_\(Germany\)).

In November 2019 the right-wing group Phalanx 18 was banned by the city-state of Bremen.[^cite_note-26]

One of the two mayors (*Bürgermeister*) is elected [President of the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Bremen) (*Präsident des Senats*) and serves as head of the city and the state. The current mayor is [Andreas Bovenschulte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Bovenschulte).[^cite_note-27]

- Many of the sights in Bremen are found in the *Altstadt* (Old Town), an oval area surrounded by the [Weser River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weser), on the southwest, and the *Wallgraben*, the former moats of the medieval city walls, on the northeast. The oldest part of the Altstadt is the southeast half, starting with the *Marktplatz* and ending at the *[Schnoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnoor) quarter*.

- The *[Marktplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremer_Marktplatz)* (Market square) is dominated by the opulent façade of the [Town Hall of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hall_of_Bremen). The building was erected between 1405 and 1410 in [Gothic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture), but the façade was built two centuries later (1609–12) in [Renaissance style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture). The Town Hall is the seat of the president of the [Senate of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Bremen). Today, it hosts a restaurant in original decor with gigantic [wine barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_\(wine\)), the [Ratskeller in Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Ratskeller). In July 2004, along with the [Bremen Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Roland), the building was added to the list of [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](44940.md).

- Two statues stand to the west side of the Town Hall: one is the statue [Bremen Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Roland) (1404) of the city's protector, [Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland), with his view against the *Cathedral* and bearing Durendart, the "sword of justice" and a shield decorated with an [imperial eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). The other near the entrance to the *Ratskeller* is [Gerhard Marcks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Marcks)' [bronze sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_sculpture) (1953) *Die Stadtmusikanten* ([Town Musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Musicians_of_Bremen)), which portrays the donkey, dog, cat and rooster of the [Grimm Brothers'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_Grimm) fairy tale.

- Other interesting buildings in the vicinity of the Marktplatz are the *[Schütting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%BCtting_\(Bremen\))*, a sixteenth-century Flemish-inspired guild hall, [Rathscafé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rathscaf%C3%A9_\(Bremen\)), [Raths-Apotheke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raths-Apotheke_\(Bremen\)), [Haus der Stadtsparkasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haus_der_Stadtsparkasse_\(Bremen\)) and the *[Stadtwaage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtwaage_\(Bremen\))*, the former [weigh house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weigh_house) (built in 1588), with an ornate Renaissance façade, and the nearby [Essighaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essighaus), once a fine Renaissance town house. The façades and houses surrounding the market square were the first buildings in Bremen to be restored after [World War II](32927.md), by the citizens of Bremen themselves.

- St Peter's [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Cathedral) (13th century), to the east of the Marktplatz, with sculptures of [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses) and [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David), [Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter) and [Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus) and Charlemagne. The [Bismarck Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck_monument_\(Bremen\)) is also outside the cathedral, which is the only monument in Germany to depict Otto von Bismarck in an equestrian format.

- On Katherinenklosterhof to the northwest of the cathedral, a few remaining traces can be found of [St. Catherine's Monastery, Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catherine's_Monastery,_Bremen) dating back to the thirteenth century.

- The *[Liebfrauenkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_\(Bremen\))* (Our Lady's Church) is the oldest church of the town (11th century). Its crypt features several impressive murals from the fourteenth century.

- Off the south side of the Markplatz, the 110 m (120 yd) *[Böttcherstraße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6ttcherstra%C3%9Fe)* was transformed in 1923–1931 by the coffee magnate [Ludwig Roselius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Roselius), who commissioned local artists to convert the narrow street (in medieval times, the street of the barrel makers) into an inspired mixture of [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art) and [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau). It was considered "*entartete Kunst*" ([degenerate art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_art)) by the Nazis. Today, the street is one of Bremen's most popular attractions, with the [Glockenspiel House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glockenspiel_House) at No. 4 with its [carillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carillon) of [Meissen porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meissen_porcelain) bells.

## Economy

According to [OECD](33853117.md) data, Bremen had a GDP per capita of \$53,379 in 2013, higher than the average for Germany as a whole. For comparison, in 2013, the World Bank reported Germany had a GDP per capita of \$46,268, and the EU overall had a GDP per capita of \$35,408 in the same year.[^cite_note-37]

Bremen is the second development centre of the region, after [Hamburg](13467.md). It forms part of the production network of [Airbus](26220236.md) SAS and this is where equipping of the wing units for all widebody Airbus aircraft and the manufacture of small sheet metal parts takes place. Structural assembly, including that of metal landing flaps, is another focal point. Within the framework of [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) production, assembly of the landing flaps (high lift systems) is carried out here. The pre-final assembly of the fuselage section (excluding the cockpit) of the [A400M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A400M) military transport aircraft takes place before delivery on to Spain.[^cite_note-38]



MZH building, campus of the University of Bremen

More than 3,100 persons are employed at Airbus Bremen, the second largest Airbus site in Germany. As part of the Centre of Excellence – Wing/Pylon, Bremen is responsible for the design and manufacture of high-lift systems for the wings of Airbus aircraft. The entire process chain for the high-lift elements is established here, including the project office, technology engineering, flight physics, system engineering, structure development, verification tests, structural assembly, wing equipping and ultimate delivery to the final assembly line. In addition, Bremen manufactures sheet metal parts like clips and thrust crests for all Airbus aircraft as part of the Centre of Excellence – Fuselage and Cabin.[^cite_note-39]

In Bremen there is a plant of [EADS Astrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_Astrium) and the headquarters of [OHB-System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHB-System), respectively the first and the third space companies of European Union.

There is also a [Mercedes-Benz](18564164.md) factory in Bremen, building the [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_C-Class), [CLK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLK-Class), [SL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_SL-Class), [SLK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_SLK-Class), and [GLK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_GLK-Class) series of cars.[^cite_note-40]

[Beck & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck's_Brewery)'s headlining brew Beck's and [St Pauli Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Pauli_Girl) beers are brewed in Bremen. In past centuries when Bremen's port was the "key to Europe", the city also had a large number of wine importers, but the number is down to a precious few. Apart from that there is another link between Bremen and wine: about 800 years ago, quality wines were produced here. Bremen is not the place where the largest [wine cellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_cellar) in the world is located although it was once said to hold over 1 million bottles, but during WWII was raided by occupying forces.

A large number of food producing or trading companies are located in Bremen with their German or European headquarters: [Anheuser-Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anheuser-Busch) InBev (Beck's Brewery), [Kellogg's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_Company), [Kraft Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_Foods) (Kraft, Jacobs Coffee, Milka Chocolate, Milram, Miràcoli), Frosta (frosted food), Nordsee (chain of sea fast food), [Melitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melitta) Kaffee, Eduscho Kaffee, Azul Kaffee, Vitakraft (pet articles and food for cats, dogs, birds, fish, rodents and other pets), Atlanta AG (Chiquita banana), chocolatier [Hachez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachez) (fine chocolate and confiserie), feodora chocolatier.

[Bremer Woll-Kämmerei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremer_Woll-K%C3%A4mmerei) (BWK), a worldwide operating company for manufacturing wool and trading in wool and similar products, is headquartered in Bremen. [Gleistein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleistein) is a German cordage factory with head office in Bremen.

## Transport



[Bremen Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Central_Station)



Map of the Bremen S-Bahn

Bremen has an [international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Airport) situated 3 km (2 mi) south of the city centre.

[Trams in Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Bremen) and local [bus](4146.md) services are offered by the [Bremer Straßenbahn AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremer_Stra%C3%9Fenbahn_AG) (translates from German as *Bremen Tramways Corporation*), often abbreviated BSAG, the [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) provider for Bremen.[^cite_note-41]

The [Bremen S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_S-Bahn) covers the [Bremen/Oldenburg Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen/Oldenburg_Metropolitan_Region), from [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven) in the north to [Twistringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twistringen) in the south and from [Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldenburg_\(city\)) in the west, centred on [Bremen Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_Central_Station). It has been in operation since 2010.[^cite_note-42] This network unified existing regional transport in Bremen as well as surrounding cities, including [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven), [Delmenhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmenhorst), [Twistringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twistringen), [Nordenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordenham), [Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldenburg_\(city\)), and [Verden an der Aller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verden_an_der_Aller). The network lies completely within the area of the [Bremen-Lower Saxony Transport Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund_Bremen/Niedersachsen?action=edit\&redlink=1), whose tariff structure applies.

## Sports



Weserstadion is the home ground of Werder Bremen.

Bremen is home to the [football](10568.md) team [Werder Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SV_Werder_Bremen), who won the [German Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_football_champions) for the fourth time and the German Football Cup for the fifth time in 2004, making them only the fourth team in German football history to win the double; the club won the German Football Cup for the sixth time in 2009. The home stadium of SV Werder Bremen is the [Weserstadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weserstadion), a pure football stadium, almost completely surrounded by solar cells. It is one of the biggest buildings in Europe delivering renewable energy.

## Education

With 18,000 students,[^cite_note-45] the [University of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bremen) is the largest university in Bremen, and is also home to the international [Goethe-Institut](470336.md) and the [Fallturm Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallturm_Bremen). Additionally, Bremen has a [University of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Arts_Bremen) and the [Bremen University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_University_of_Applied_Sciences). In 2001, the private [Jacobs University Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobs_University_Bremen) was founded. All major German research foundations maintain institutes in Bremen, with a focus on marine sciences: The [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society) with the [Max Planck Institute for Marine Microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Marine_Microbiology), and the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Scientific Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Scientific_Community) with the Center for Tropical Marine Ecology (zmt).[^cite_note-46] The Bremerhaven-based [Alfred-Wegener-Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Wegener_Institute_for_Polar_and_Marine_Research) of the [Helmholtz Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_Association) closely cooperates with the aforementioned institutes, especially within the MARUM[^cite_note-47] a center for marine environmental sciences, affiliated to the University of Bremen. Furthermore, The [Fraunhofer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraunhofer_Society) is present in Bremen with centers for applied material research (IFAM[^cite_note-48]) and medical image computing (MEVIS[^cite_note-49]). The Centre for Economics Education in the Unterwesergebiet (BWU) is a vocational education and training institution based in Bremen. It specializes in providing business-related education and professional development programs for individuals and businesses.[^cite_note-50]

## Miscellaneous

- In December 1949, Bremen hosted the lecture cycle *_Einblick in das, was ist_* by the philosopher [Martin Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heidegger), in which Heidegger introduced his concept of a "fourfold" of earth and sky, gods and mortals. This was also Heidegger's first public-speaking engagement following his removal from his [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau) professorship by the [Denazification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazification) authorities.

- Bremen is connected with a fairy tale by the [Brothers Grimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_Grimm), the *[Town Musicians of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Musicians_of_Bremen)*, although they never actually reach Bremen in the tale.

- The UK Comedy podcast "Three Bean Salad" have great affection for Bremen, and regularly listeners to their podcast will claim to be from Bremen, and they have postulated potential world tours including Bremen.
