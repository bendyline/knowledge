**Warner Bros. Discovery, Inc.** is an American [multinational](214491.md) [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) and [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_entertainment) [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(company\)) headquartered in [New York City](645042.md). It was formed through the [spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off) of [WarnerMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia) by [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T), and its [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Discovery,_Inc._and_WarnerMedia) with [Discovery, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc.) on April 8, 2022.

Warner Bros. Discovery operated via two divisions: Streaming & Studios and Global Linear Networks. Streaming & Studios includes the flagship [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Entertainment) studios, [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO), [DC Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Entertainment), and streaming services such as [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max) and [Discovery+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery+). Global Linear Networks largely includes advertising-supported cable networks. Those networks were inherited from its predecessors Discovery (such as the [Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel) among others), [Scripps Networks Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Networks_Interactive) (such as [HGTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HGTV) among others), and [Turner Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Broadcasting_System) (however, it closed down in 2019) (such as [CNN](62028.md), [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(American_TV_channel\)), and [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_\(American_TV_network\)) among others). [Warner Bros. Discovery International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery_International) is also included in the division, which manages broadcasting operations outside of the United States.

In June 2025, WBD announced plans to spin off the less-profitable Global Linear Networks division and its debt as a separate company, leaving the more profitable Warner Bros. studio business, HBO, and HBO Max. In October 2025, [Paramount Skydance](79917576.md) made an offer to acquire the entirety of WBD, arguing that it would provide greater [shareholder value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shareholder_value) than the split. After taking bids from other companies, WBD accepted a competing offer by [Netflix, Inc.](62430075.md) to acquire the studios business only. However, Paramount continued to make [unsolicited bids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_takeover), stating that its offer provided better value, and would face less regulatory scrutiny than a sale to Netflix.

On February 27, 2026, after the company revised its offer, WBD [agreed to be acquired by Paramount Skydance](81768101.md) in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately \$110 billion; the acquisition is expected to be completed by the end of September 2026, subject to regulatory approval. The sale was approved by the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) in June 2026, but was temporarily held up by a California-led antitrust lawsuit. On September 21, 2026, the state of California reached an out-of-court settlement with Paramount, subject to a five-year [consent decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consent_decree) imposing conditions on the operations of the combined company's film studios and cable television businesses. The settlement was officially approved by a judge on September 30, 2026.

On October 2, 2026, [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison) announced that the merged corporate entity will be named "[Skydance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Corporation)" and its stock will be traded on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol "SKYD" upon the merger's forthcoming completion, which is scheduled to close on October 6, 2026.

## Background

Main articles: [WarnerMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia) and [Discovery, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc.)

### 1923–1979

[Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Entertainment), Turner Broadcasting System, Scripps Networks Interactive and [Discovery, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc.) have conjoined histories. Warner Bros. was founded on April 4, 1923, by four brothers, [Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Warner), [Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Warner), [Sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Warner), and [Jack Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_L._Warner) in Hollywood. Warner Bros. established itself as a leader in the American [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures) industry[^cite_note-4] before diversifying into [animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Animation), [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Television_Studios), and [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Games). It is one of the ["Big Five"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_film_studios) American [film studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_studio), as well as a member of the [Motion Picture Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association) (MPA). In 1965, Turner Broadcasting System was founded by [Ted Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Turner) in [Atlanta](3138.md), Georgia. A year later, [Kinney National Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinney_National_Company) came into existence. It reincorporated as [Warner Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Communications) in 1972.

### 1979–1996

In 1979, Warner Communications formed a joint venture with credit card company [American Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Express) called [Warner-Amex Satellite Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner-Amex_Satellite_Entertainment). American Express acquired a 50% stake in Warner Communications' cable television holdings for \$175 million.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] This company owned such cable channels as [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV), [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon), [The Movie Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Movie_Channel), and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1) (which was launched in 1985 on the channel space left by Turner's [Cable Music Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_Music_Channel)). Warner Communications bought American Express's half in 1984 and sold the venture a year later to the [original iteration of Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_\(1952%E2%80%932005\)), which renamed it [MTV Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks#Background) (now known as Paramount Media Networks).[^cite_note-7] In 1982, Warner Communications purchased [Popular Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Library) from [CBS Publications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Publications). Warner Communications merged with [Time Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Inc.) in 1990 to become [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia).

In 1982, [Cable Education Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc.) was founded, launching [The Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel) three years later. It was named Discovery Communications in 1994.

### 1996–2021

Time Warner acquired Turner Broadcasting System in 1996, allowing it to reenter the cable industry. In 2001, it merged with [America Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL) (AOL) to form [AOL Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL_Time_Warner), but the merger proved disastrous, and the company reverted to its former name, Time Warner, in 2003.[^cite_note-8] Time Warner spun off [its cable division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner_Cable) (later known as [Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_\(brand\)), owned by [Charter Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_Communications)) in 2009, AOL (later owned by [Yahoo! Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Inc._\(2017%E2%80%93present\))) in 2009, and Time Inc. in 2013, which was later acquired by [Meredith Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_Corporation) and became [Dotdash Meredith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotdash_Meredith).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]



2019–2022 [WarnerMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia) logo

In 2018, Discovery Communications acquired Scripps Networks Interactive (a 2008 spun off from [E. W. Scripps Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._W._Scripps_Company)'s cable division) and was renamed as Discovery, Inc.[^cite_note-11] [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T) acquired Time Warner, becoming [WarnerMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia). In 2019, AT\&T integrated its related assets into Warner's business divisions as part of its reorganization, effectively breaking up Turner Broadcasting System.[^cite_note-12]

## History

### Formation (2021–2022)

Main article: [Merger of Discovery, Inc. and WarnerMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Discovery,_Inc._and_WarnerMedia)



The company's initial wordmark logo

On May 17, 2021, AT\&T and Discovery announced that AT\&T would spin off WarnerMedia to its shareholders, which in turn would be merged with [Discovery Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc.) to form Warner Bros. Discovery. The merger would be structured as a [Reverse Morris Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_Morris_Trust), with AT\&T shareholders holding a 71% interest in the new company's stock and appointing seven board members, and Discovery shareholders holding a 29% interest and appointing six board members. AT\&T would receive US\$43 billion in cash and debt. The merger was expected to be completed in mid-2022.[^cite_note-bloomberg-05-21-13][^cite_note-cnbc-05-17-14][^cite_note-15]



Zaslav in February 2026

The merged company would be led by Discovery's current CEO, [David Zaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zaslav); WarnerMedia's CEO [Jason Kilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Kilar)'s position in the new company was uncertain.[^cite_note-bloomberg-05-21-13] Zaslav stated that the two companies would spend a combined US\$20 billion annually on content (outpacing [Netflix](62430075.md)). The company aimed to expand their streaming services, which included WarnerMedia's [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max), to reach 400 million global subscribers.[^cite_note-cnbc-05-17-14]

On June 1, 2021, it was announced that the merged company would be known as Warner Bros. Discovery, and an interim [wordmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordmark) was unveiled with the tagline "The stuff that dreams are made of"—a quote from the 1941 Warner Bros. film *[The Maltese Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maltese_Falcon_\(1941_film\))*, itself paraphrasing [Shakespeare's](32897.md) *[The Tempest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tempest)*.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In an [SEC Filing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEC_Filing) on November 18, 2021, Discovery revealed that talks with AT\&T had fallen through, in April 2021, due to disagreements over the ownership of the new company between AT\&T and Discovery shareholders, and the amount of debt transferred to Discovery when they merged with WarnerMedia, before talks resumed on May 17, 2021.[^cite_note-18]

In November 2021, during an earnings call, Discovery Streaming CEO JB Perrette discussed possible options for its [Discovery+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery+) streaming service post-merger, including bundling the service with HBO Max and eventually merging them under a single platform with a mixture of both companies' technologies. He noted that WBD may prioritize launching Discovery+ and HBO Max as a unified platform in markets where Discovery+ had yet to launch, such as other parts of Asia-Pacific.[^cite_note-19]

On December 22, 2021, the transaction was approved by the [European Commission](9974.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] On January 5, 2022, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that WarnerMedia and [Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global) (at the time named ViacomCBS) were exploring a possible sale of either a majority stake or all of [The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW), and that [Nexstar Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexstar_Media_Group) was considered a leading bidder.[^cite_note-22] The reports also indicated that WarnerMedia and ViacomCBS could include a contractual commitment that would require any new owner to buy new programming from those companies, allowing them to reap some continual revenue through the network.[^cite_note-23] The CW's then-president-and-CEO [Mark Pedowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Pedowitz) confirmed talks of a potential sale in a memo to CW staffers, but added that "It's too early to speculate what might happen."[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

On January 26, 2022, AT\&T CEO [John Stankey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stankey) stated that the merger was expected to close sometime during the second quarter of 2022.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] On February 1, 2022, it was reported that AT\&T had finalized the structure of the merger: WarnerMedia would be spun off [pro rata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_rata) to AT\&T's shareholders, and then merge into Discovery Inc. to form the new company.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The transaction was approved by the Brazilian antitrust regulator [Cade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_Council_for_Economic_Defense) on February 7,[^cite_note-30] followed by the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) on February 9.[^cite_note-31] On March 11, 2022, the merger was approved by Discovery's shareholders. Due to the structure of the merger, it did not require separate approval from AT\&T shareholders.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In an SEC filing on March 25, 2022, AT\&T stated that two-way trading of WBD stock with that of AT\&T would begin on April 4, 2022, and that a special dividend would be issued the next day to give AT\&T shareholders a 0.24 share in WBD for each share of AT\&T common stock they held.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The merger was officially completed on April 8, 2022. Trading began on [Nasdaq](21559.md) on April 11.[^cite_note-wbdlaunch-37] At this time the company unveiled its final logo, designed by [Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chermayeff_&_Geismar_&_Haviv), which features a rendition of Warner Bros.' long-time shield logo.[^cite_note-38]

The combined company retained several top executives from WarnerMedia, including film and television heads [Toby Emmerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Emmerich) and [Channing Dungey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channing_Dungey), and HBO and HBO Max [chief content officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_content_officer) (CCO) Casey Bloys. Most of the company's top executive roles are filled by their Discovery counterparts, including Gunnar Wiedenfels as Warner Bros. Discovery's [chief financial officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_financial_officer) (CFO), JB Perrette as president and CEO of global streaming and interactive, and Discovery's chief lifestyle brands officer Kathleen Finch—whose role expanded to cover most of the combined company's U.S. linear networks, besides [CNN](62028.md) (which was taken over by [Chris Licht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Licht), replacing the outgoing [Jeff Zucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Zucker)), [Magnolia Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Network) (which reported to Bloys, after reporting directly to Zaslav under Discovery), and the [Turner Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Sports) unit (which would be overseen by the newly formed Warner Bros. Discovery Sports division).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Looks-40][^cite_note-Executive-41]

In an introductory town hall hosted by [Oprah Winfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oprah_Winfrey), Zaslav stated that the combined company would need to have "one culture" that "starts with people feeling safe, people feeling valued for who they are", as opposed what he described as a culture of internal competition between WarnerMedia's businesses.[^cite_note-42] He expected that "investment avoidance" via the consolidation of redundant business units (such as streaming) and staff would be one of the main ways that the company would achieve its promised \$3 billion in cost savings.[^cite_note-43] On April 21, 2022, Licht and Perrette announced the shutdown of CNN's streaming service [CNN+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN+), which had launched only two weeks prior to the completion of the merger; the new leadership considered it to be incompatible with their goal of a unified streaming service for WBD properties.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-cnbc-2022-04-21-45][^cite_note-nytimes-2022-04-21-46]

In an investors' call on April 26 (concurrent with the first quarter earnings reports for Discovery Inc., its last prior to the merger), Zaslav contrasted the company's streaming businesses with Netflix (whose stock declined after a quarterly loss in subscribers), describing Warner Bros. Discovery as a "far more balanced and competitive company" that would "invest at scale smartly" and not "overspend" on growth and that its streaming businesses would complement its linear networks. He stated that HBO Max had "meaningful subscriber [churn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churn_rate)", and that the planned merger of it with Discovery+ would help to reduce churn by offering a broader content mix.[^cite_note-47] It was reported that the company had suspended scripted development at [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(American_TV_channel\)) and [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_\(American_TV_network\)), to evaluate their strategies.[^cite_note-48] The following day, Zaslav purchased approximately \$1 million worth of WBD stock.[^cite_note-49]

On May 11, 2022, Warner Bros. Discovery eliminated several executive positions carried over from WarnerMedia, including [Kids, Young Adults and Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Television_Studios#Warner_Bros._Kids,_Young_Adults_and_Classics) head [Tom Ascheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Ascheim), and general manager of TBS, TNT, and [TruTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TruTV) head Brett Weitz. These networks would be overseen by Finch as head of U.S. Networks, while the studios and one network under the Kids, Young Adults and Classics division ([Warner Bros. Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Animation), [Cartoon Network Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network_Studios), [Turner Classic Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Classic_Movies) and [Hanna-Barbera Studios Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna-Barbera_Studios_Europe)) was moved under [Warner Bros. Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Television_Studios).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] That day, it announced an agreement with British telecom company [BT Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BT_Group) for it to contribute its [BT Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BT_Sport) channels into a 50/50 joint venture with its UK [Eurosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosport) channels, and eventually merge them.[^cite_note-\:22-53][^cite_note-54]

On June 1, 2022, Warner Bros. Pictures head [Toby Emmerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Emmerich) announced his departure to establish a new studio, to be funded and distributed exclusively (for five-years) by Warner Bros. Pictures.[^cite_note-Emmerich-55] Warner Bros. Pictures was then divided into three business units with separate leadership: former [MGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) executives [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca) and [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy) became the co-chairs of [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures) and [New Line Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Line_Cinema), and temporarily oversaw the [DC Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Films) and [Warner Animation Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Animation_Group) units.[^cite_note-Emmerich-55][^cite_note-56] Eight days later, WBD named former Discovery and [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision) executive [Luis Silberwasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Silberwasser) as chairman of Sports.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] In July 2022, [Alan Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Horn) rejoined Warner Bros. as a consultant.[^cite_note-59]

WBD delivered its second-quarter earnings report on August 4, 2022. Ahead of the report, the company performed surgery on HBO Max, including cutting new programming development in much of Europe,[^cite_note-halt-60] live-action children's programming development,[^cite_note-61] and direct-to-streaming films—including notable August 3 cancellations of the nearly completed films *[Batgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batgirl_\(film\))* and *[Scoob! Holiday Haunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoob!_Holiday_Haunt)* as tax [write-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write-off), and the quiet removals of multiple HBO Max original films from the platform along with upcoming releases.[^cite_note-62]

In the second quarter of 2022, WBD revealed \$9.8 billion in revenue and a net loss of \$2.2 billion [pro forma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_forma), primarily from integration and restructuring expenses. The company took \$825 million in write-offs on "content impairments and development".[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] The company confirmed cuts to children's program development,[^cite_note-65] and abandoned the production of direct-to-streaming films for HBO Max—with Zaslav arguing that they lacked economic value and impact in comparison to theatrical releases. WBD renewed its contracts with Bloys and other key HBO executives; Zaslav praised Bloys' performance as chief content officer.[^cite_note-66] Zaslav stated that a "10-year plan" was in development for DC Films, modeled after those of [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios),[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] while Perrette stated that the planned merger of Discovery+ and HBO Max would occur by summer 2023 in the United States, followed by other markets.[^cite_note-69]

HBO subsequently reorganized on August 15 to dismantle most of HBO Max's autonomous units. HBO Max's head of comedy Suzanna Makkos began reporting to HBO's head of comedy Amy Gravitt. Layoffs hit HBO Max's non-scripted, live-action family entertainment, international originals, and casting units, as well as HBO's acquisitions unit.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] HBO Max also continued to remove and cancel some of its lesser-viewed original programming, particularly family-oriented and animated series.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

On August 15, 2022, Nexstar confirmed in June that it would buy a controlling 75% interest in The CW; WBD and Paramount would each retain a 12.5% ownership interest.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-NexstarAcquires-75] Nexstar stated that Mark Pedowitz would remain its chairman and CEO. WBD and Paramount would remain The network's main content suppliers, but Nexstar stated that the arrangement would be for the [2022–23 broadcast season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_United_States_network_television_schedule), and it retained the option to extend the partnership.[^cite_note-NexstarAcquires-75][^cite_note-76] As the transaction did not require regulatory approval (unlike the ["Big Four" networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(American_television\)), The CW does not own stations), Nexstar immediately took over the network's operations.[^cite_note-77]

In September 2022, WBD became the subject of a proposed [class-action lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class-action_lawsuit) by one of its shareholders, alleging that WarnerMedia was overinvesting in streaming content "without sufficient concern for return on investments", and had overstated the number of HBO Max subscribers by at least 10 million by counting inactivated subscriptions bundled with [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T) services—thus misleading investors in violation of the [Securities Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_Act). It also alleged that Discovery executives failed to warn investors that WarnerMedia's [prospectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospectus_\(finance\)) contained misleading statements.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

On September 28 during a company town hall, Zaslav addressed speculation that WBD was pursuing a possible sale as early as 2024, stating that it was "absolutely not for sale", and "have everything we need to be successful".[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] On October 11, Warner Bros. Television Group laid off 82 employees and eliminated 43 vacant positions as part of a restructuring that primarily impacted its unscripted and animation units. The restructuring saw the consolidation of Warner Horizon and [Telepictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepictures)' creative operations, and the consolidation of Cartoon Network Studios' and [Warner Bros. Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Animation)'s development and production teams (with the two studios retaining separate [labels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprint_\(trade_name\)) with distinct output).[^cite_note-82]

On October 3, 2022, Nexstar closed its deal to acquire a controlling interest in The CW. Mark Pedowitz resigned from his position as the network's chairman and CEO, replaced by Dennis Miller as president.[^cite_note-83] Later that month, it was announced that filmmaker [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn) and producer [Peter Safran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Safran) would serve as co-CEOs and co-chairs of DC Films which rebranded as DC Studios. The duo signed a four-year deal to oversee film, television, and animation production for DC. The pair reported directly to Zaslav, while working independently with other members of the studio. Gunn would oversee creative development on DC projects, while Safran took the business aspect.[^cite_note-DCStudios_THR-84] An earnings report in November 2022, announced that the launch of WBD's streaming service had been moved up to spring 2023.[^cite_note-85] [Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_\(streaming_service\)) was unveiled April 12, 2023.[^cite_note-86] In December 2022, CNN announced cutbacks and a reorganization to prioritize its "core" operations, resulting in sister channel [HLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HLN_\(TV_network\)) being brought under the auspices of [Investigation Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigation_Discovery) and abandoning its remaining original live news programming.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-thr-hln-cuts-88]

### Attempted separation and merger with Paramount Skydance (2025–present)

Further information: [Proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance](81768101.md)

A group of professionally dressed people seated around a round table in a modern, warmly lit lounge, engaged in conversation and smiling. David Zaslav sits at the center, gesturing while speaking, with a woman in a white sweater featuring an American flag seated to his left and a man in a dark blazer leaning in from his right. Drinks rest on the table, while other guests and soft ambient lighting fill the background.

Zaslav during a meeting with United States ambassador to Italy and San Marino [Tilman Fertitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilman_Fertitta) in February 2026

On June 9, 2025, WBD announced plans to split its two operational units into separate companies.[^cite_note-BusinessesAfterSplit-121][^cite_note-122] The two companies —which would later be referred to as "Warner Bros." and "Discovery Global"[^cite_note-123]—would be led by Zaslav and current WBD CFO Gunnar Wiedenfels respectively. The transactions were expected to be completed by mid-2026; the split was structured to be tax-free, with Discovery Global assuming Warner Bros.' debt.[^cite_note-BusinessesAfterSplit-121][^cite_note-ReutersJun9-124] On September 10, 2025, Zaslav stated that the split was on track for completion by April 2026.[^cite_note-125]

On September 11, 2025, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison)—which had recently [acquired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global) [Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global) via [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media)—was exploring a bid to acquire the entirety of WBD via [Paramount Skydance](79917576.md).[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] Such an acquisition would integrate overlapping assets between the two companies, including two of the five [major film studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_film_studios) (Warner Bros. and [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures)), streaming services HBO Max and [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+), TNT Sports and [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports), and CNN and [CBS News](359551.md).[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-SizingUp-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-SwallowWhole-131][^cite_note-132] An analyst suggested that the bid was meant to preempt potential interest in the studios business post-split, and take advantage of a "period of industry-wide instability".[^cite_note-SwallowWhole-131] WBD's share prices increased by 33% following the reports.[^cite_note-StockSurges-133]

Paramount made multiple unsolicited bids in October 2025,[^cite_note-134] insisting that its offers would "\[deliver] superior value" to shareholders in comparison to the previously-proposed split.[^cite_note-135] On October 31, [Netflix, Inc.](62430075.md) was reported to be actively exploring a bid for the studio and streaming unit; the company stressed that it remains "predominantly focused on growing organically", and was not interested in WBD's legacy linear television businesses.[^cite_note-136] On November 20, Paramount Skydance, Netflix, and Comcast (which was in the process of similarly [divesting its cable television assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versant)) formally submitted bids; Comcast and Netflix made cash-and-stock and mostly-cash bids for the studios company respectively, while Paramount Skydance made an all-cash offer for the entirety of WBD, backed by debt financing from [Apollo Global Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Global_Management) and Middle-Eastern [sovereign wealth funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_wealth_fund).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

While WBD initially accepted a \$72 billion offer by Netflix to acquire the streaming and studios division (valuing WBD as a whole at \$82.7 billion).[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] Paramount Skydance argued that its bid would face fewer regulatory obstacles than the Netflix offer, arguing that the company wanted to use WBD's properties to enrich its dominant position in streaming and cut back on theatrical releases.[^cite_note-141] Netflix co-CEO [Ted Sarandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Sarandos) stated that the company would have broke from its usual streaming-first strategy and continued to release films theatrically through Warner Bros. with a standard 45-day window, arguing that its films represented "billions of dollars" of potential revenue.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

Paramount Skydance subsequently made multiple hostile offers in December and February,[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] with the latter also offering to cover Netflix's [breakup fee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_fee), and pay WBD shareholders a "ticking fee" of approximately \$650 million per-quarter if the acquisition is not closed within 2026, as a display of confidence that regulatory approval would be achieved quickly.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] Ellison stated that a combined Paramount–WBD would commit to releasing 30 "high-quality" theatrical films per-year, maintaining theatrical and home video windows, continue acquiring third-party content, and continuing to allow HBO to operate independently.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] Paramount's chief legal officer [Makan Delrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makan_Delrahim) stated that the acquisition would give it the [scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale) needed to compete in the streaming market with [Disney](37398.md) and tech companies such as [Amazon](90451.md) and Netflix, and that they would continue respecting 45-day theatrical window to "create momentum behind films before they reach streaming services."[^cite_note-ParamountDefendsWarner-Deadline-152]

On February 26, 2026, Warner Bros. Discovery confirmed that it considered Paramount's updated bid to be superior to Netflix's current offer.[^cite_note-153] Netflix subsequently declined to increase its bid, stating that the deal was "no longer financially attractive."[^cite_note-154] On February 27, 2026, Paramount Skydance confirmed its deal to acquire all of Warner Bros. Discovery for \$110 billion. The deal was expected to be closed by September 30, 2026 at the earliest.[^cite_note-155] On April 23, 2026, WBD's shareholders approved the sale to Paramount Skydance.[^cite_note-156] However, an offer to have the sale include a [golden parachute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_parachute) for Zaslav and other WBD executives was rejected.[^cite_note-157]

The Paramount offer faced the possibility of antitrust issues due to its [horizontal integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_integration) of two legacy media conglomerates and film studios, and a resulting reduction in viewpoint diversity; such a merger would resemble the larger [Disney](37398.md) [acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney) of [21st Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Fox) in 2019.[^cite_note-StockSurges-133][^cite_note-SizingUp-129][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] Concerns were raised that Paramount would utilize its market position in box office and cable television revenue to increase its leverage over cinemas and television providers,[^cite_note-StatesBlock-Deadline-160][^cite_note-WGACinemaUnited-Deadline-161] and over the editorial independence of CNN, citing the changes made to [CBS News](359551.md) after the Paramount Skydance merger (including appointing right-wing commentator [Bari Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Weiss) as editor-in-chief).[^cite_note-IllegalSideDeal-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] Delrahim contrasted the purchase to the Disney–Fox merger, stating that the merged company would not consolidate studios or cut back on theatrical releases, with the promised slate of 30 films being split evenly between Paramount and Warner Bros., and both studios "\[maintaining] full staff to support production and distribution."[^cite_note-ParamountDefendsWarner-Deadline-152]

On June 12, 2026, the sale was approved by the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md)'s [Antitrust Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Antitrust_Division).[^cite_note-166] The deal was approved by the European Commission in July 2026, with Paramount being required to exit its [United International Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_International_Pictures) distribution venture with Universal, not enter into any distribution agreements with Universal in the [European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area) (EEA) for 10 years, and—in the 19 European territories that UIP served—not enter into distribution agreements for Warner Bros. films with companies that distribute Disney and/or Universal films in that territory.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] The United Kingdom's minister for culture imposed conditions on its television operations, requiring the merged company to not combine linear and on-demand services for five years, maintain editorial independence among its news (including CNN and *[5 News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_News)*) and children's divisions, and maintain its commitments to UK-produced programming for [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_\(British_TV_channel\)).[^cite_note-171]

On July 13, 2026, California and 11 other states filed an antitrust lawsuit in the [U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California](891692.md), seeking to halt the acquisition. The motion argued that the combined company's control of a quarter of U.S. box office and cable television revenue would give it undue leverage on film exhibitors and television providers respectively, and the company could still "harm competition by reducing investment and innovation, degrading quality, and raising the price of those 30 films they produce."[^cite_note-StatesBlock-Deadline-160][^cite_note-WGACinemaUnited-Deadline-161] The WGA filed its own lawsuit the next day, stating that it would give Paramount the incentive to reduce output and suppress writers' wages.[^cite_note-172] On July 20, Judge [Araceli Martínez-Olguín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araceli_Mart%C3%ADnez-Olgu%C3%ADn) granted a two-week temporary restraining order in the California lawsuit.[^cite_note-173] On July 23, Judge Martínez-Olguín extended the temporary restraining order through August 17, 2026, in order to provide more time for legal proceedings,[^cite_note-174] after which Paramount Skydance entered a joint stipulation promising to not close the WBD acquisition until June 1, 2027 or a ruling in the antitrust case, whichever comes first. A trial was scheduled for March 2027,[^cite_note-175] while Paramount would become subject to the aforementioned "ticking fee" beginning in October 2026.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178]

[AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres), [Cinemark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinemark_Theatres), [Regal Cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regal_Cinemas), and the trade association [Cinema United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_United) all called for Paramount and California attorney general [Rob Bonta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Bonta) to reach a settlement, stating that they were confident that Ellison would adhere to his commitments to theatrical releases, and believed that a prolonged antitrust trial could cause uncertainty within the film industry.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] California governor [Gavin Newsom](418947.md) also pushed for a settlement, arguing that blocking the acquisition would be detrimental to the state's job market; reports emerged that Ellison had considered beginning the process of relocating Paramount out of California to a different, Republican-led state (such as Georgia, Tennessee, or Texas) as early as October if it could not settle with Bonta.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]

In mid-August 2026, Paramount demanded that the plaintiffs pay a \$1.8 billion [bond](60737.md) on the antitrust case to cover legal and regulatory costs, including the cost of the "ticking fee" through to March 2027, citing obligations under the *[Clayton Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Antitrust_Act_of_1914)* and the [Federal Rules of Civil Procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Rules_of_Civil_Procedure); Bonta criticized the move as [blackmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackmail), arguing that Paramount had wilfully negotiated the "ticking fee" while being fully aware that the deal would face antitrust scrutiny.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] On August 26, 2026, the states of [Iowa](26810748.md) and [Montana](19978.md) filed a [motion for leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_\(legal\)) to file a bill of complaint in the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) seeking to halt the antitrust trial, considering it "politicized antitrust enforcement by a small handful of states seeking to enjoin a \$110 billion merger that the United States, most American states, and competition regulators worldwide have cleared."[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] Paramount made a court filing similarly arguing that the 12 states "lack\[ed] regulatory authority over the merger, which is vested in the U.S. Department of Justice" [^cite_note-191]

On September 17, 2026, the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) approved a waiver for Paramount's increased foreign ownership following the acquisition.[^cite_note-192] On September 21, 2026, California announced that it had reached an out-of-court settlement that would allow Paramount's acquisition of WBD to move forward.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] Paramount agreed to a five-year consent decree that requires Paramount to uphold its promised investments in film production (including the 30-film quota, as well as specific commitments to spending \$1.5 billion over five years on productions in the United States, and \$5 million per-year into acquiring independent films), restrictions on how the company negotiates with cinemas and television providers, and establishing an independent oversight board to ensure that CNN and CBS News adhere to editorial independence and ethical journalism, among other provisions. In a memo to staff, Ellison stated that the sale would be completed in "approximately two weeks".[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] However, on September 29, 2026, it was reported that Paramount Skydance dropped 3% and had 25% below its year-start price and it marketed \$44.4B in secured notes to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery.[^cite_note-199]

On September 30, 2026, it was reported that Paramount intends to complete the Warner Bros. acquisition on October 6, 2026. It is also reported that the new name and logo for the company will be announced before this date.[^cite_note-200]

On October 2, 2026, it was announced that co-chairs and CEOs of Warner Bros. Pictures [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy) and [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca) would be exiting after the close of the Paramount and Warner Bros. merger.[^cite_note-201] It was also announced that the newly-merged entity would be known as [Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Corporation).[^cite_note-202]
