**Jalen Marquis Brunson**[^cite_note-1] (born August 31, 1996), nicknamed "**Captain Clutch**"[^cite_note-2] and the "**King of New York**",[^cite_note-6] is an American professional [basketball](3921.md) player for the [New York Knicks](72855.md) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA). The son of former NBA guard [Rick Brunson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Brunson), he played [college basketball](768856.md) for the [Villanova Wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova_Wildcats_men's_basketball), earning [national player of the year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._men's_college_basketball_national_player_of_the_year_awards) honors as a [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_\(education_year\)) and winning two national championships. Brunson was selected 33rd overall in the [2018 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_draft) by the [Dallas Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) and played his first four NBA seasons with them. He signed with the Knicks as a free agent in 2022, subsequently leading the franchise to its [first NBA championship in 53 years](82427430.md).

Brunson participated in the [2015 McDonald's All-American Boys Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_McDonald's_All-American_Boys_Game), [Jordan Brand Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Brand_Classic), and [Nike Hoop Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Hoop_Summit). He led [Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevenson_High_School_\(Lincolnshire,_Illinois\)) to the [Illinois High School Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_High_School_Association) (IHSA) Class 4A championship. He won [Illinois Mr. Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Mr._Basketball) and [was named the MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Under-19_World_Cup_MVP) of the [2015 FIBA Under-19 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIBA_Under-19_World_Championship). Brunson was a role player for the [championship season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Championship_Game) of the [2015–16 Villanova Wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Villanova_Wildcats_men's_basketball_team) and named the [national player of the year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._men's_college_basketball_national_player_of_the_year_awards) and a consensus first-team [2018 All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NCAA_Men's_Basketball_All-Americans) for the [championship season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Championship_Game) of the [2017–18 Wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Villanova_Wildcats_men's_basketball_team). After being selected by the Mavericks, Brunson spent four seasons with Dallas, culminating in a run to the [Western Conference Finals](809525.md).

Since joining the Knicks in 2022, Brunson has been named an NBA All-Star three times ([2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NBA_All-Star_Game)–[2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NBA_All-Star_Game)) and received three [All-NBA Second Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA_Team) selections in the same period. He set the NBA record for most three-point shots made in a half without a miss (8), and tied the NBA record for most three-point shots made in a single game without a miss (9). In 2025, he led the Knicks to their first Eastern Conference Finals appearance in 25 years. In 2026, he led the franchise to its first [NBA Finals](890793.md) appearance since 1999 and its first NBA championship since 1973, earning both the [Eastern Conference Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_conference_finals_MVP_awards) and the [NBA Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player). He also tied [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan)’s record for most points scored in a title clinching road finals game (45) which Jordan set back in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_NBA_Finals) against the [Utah Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Jazz).

## Early life

Born in [New Brunswick, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick,_New_Jersey), Brunson was raised in southern New Jersey until sixth grade. He is the son of [Rick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Brunson) and Sandra Brunson.[^cite_note-TVoJBl-7] He has a younger sister, Erica.[^cite_note-JBUSAB-8] His parents met at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University), where Rick played for the basketball team and Sandra played volleyball.[^cite_note-TVoJBl-7] There, Sandra was volleyball teammates and roommates with [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant)'s sister, Sharia Bryant.[^cite_note-9] Rick spent nine seasons in the NBA.[^cite_note-JBUSAB-8] The family first settled in [Cherry Hill, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Hill,_New_Jersey), but moved seven times before settling in the [Chicago area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area) in 2010, where Jalen played his high school career for [Stevenson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevenson_High_School_\(Lincolnshire,_Illinois\)).[^cite_note-TVoJBl-7] As a youth, he spent time in NBA locker rooms while his father played for the Knicks and his future head coach [Tom Thibodeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Thibodeau) worked for the team as an assistant coach.[^cite_note-10] His after-school chores included basketball exercises. While Rick was director of basketball operations at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), Jalen practiced with the [Virginia Cavaliers women's basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Cavaliers_women's_basketball) team. As a teenager, he trained with [Derrick Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Rose) and [Joakim Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joakim_Noah).[^cite_note-11] At age 13, he began hiring a professional strength and conditioning coach.[^cite_note-12]

## High school career



Brunson shooting in the 2013 [IHSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_High_School_Association) Class 4A championship game

As a freshman, Brunson was an All–[Lake County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_County,_Illinois) honorable mention selection in 2012,[^cite_note-13] as Stevenson finished the season with a 17–11 record.[^cite_note-14] During his sophomore season, Stevenson started the season 10–4 before going on a 19-game winning streak.[^cite_note-15] That year, Brunson led Stevenson to the March 16, 2013, IHSA Class 4A championship game against [Jabari Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabari_Parker) and [Kendrick Nunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendrick_Nunn)'s three-time defending state champion [Simeon Career Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Career_Academy), where he got the Simeon backcourt in foul trouble in the first half before being held to one [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_\(basketball\)) in the second half of a 58–40 loss. Stevenson finished the season 29–5.[^cite_note-16] Following the season, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) named him to the Class 4A All-state second team as the only sophomore on the first or second team.[^cite_note-17] Brunson averaged 21.5 points that season.[^cite_note-Bbp-18]



Brunson in the 2014 [IHSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_High_School_Association) Class 4A consolation game

On February 21, 2014, junior Brunson scored 57 points in a double [overtime](633242.md) victory over [Lake Forest High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Forest_High_School_\(Illinois\)). The performance gave him both the school single-game and career scoring records.[^cite_note-TWC9AotM-19][^cite_note-20] On March 21, Brunson set the IHSA playoff single game scoring record against the [Jahlil Okafor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahlil_Okafor)–led [Whitney Young High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_M._Young_Magnet_High_School) by scoring 56 points in a 75–68 state playoff semifinal loss.[^cite_note-21] Brunson averaged 26.1 points, 5.4 [rebounds](3521142.md), 4.7 [assists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_\(basketball\)) and 2.9 [steals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_\(basketball\)) for a Stevenson team that finished the season with a 32–2 record.[^cite_note-Bbp-18][^cite_note-JBffRf-22] Brunson was named Gatorade Player of the Year for the state of Illinois as a junior.[^cite_note-23] Following the season, [Associated Press](18935732.md) named him as the only non-senior on the Class 4A All-state first team that also included Okafor, [Cliff Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Alexander), [Tyler Ulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Ulis) and Sean O'Mara.[^cite_note-24] He finished fourth in the 2014 [Illinois Mr. Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Mr._Basketball) voting.[^cite_note-25]

In April 2014, [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Fighting_Illini_men's_basketball), [Purdue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_Boilermakers_men's_basketball), [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Jayhawks_men's_basketball) and [Villanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova_Wildcats_men's_basketball) had in-home visits.[^cite_note-26] On May 3, 2014, he announced the eight schools that he was considering playing college basketball for: [UConn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Huskies_men's_basketball), [Michigan State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Spartans_men's_basketball), Illinois, Kansas, Purdue, Villanova, [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball) and Temple.[^cite_note-27] That summer, he was the No. 1-ranked point guard, according to ESPN,[^cite_note-TVoJBl-7] although the class had a notable shortage of elite pure point guards.[^cite_note-28] On June 25—the day after Jalen won a gold medal at the [2014 FIBA Americas Under-18 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_Americas_Under-18_Championship)—Rick Brunson received an offer as an on-bench assistant coach from Temple.[^cite_note-29] The Temple offer fell through when Rick Brunson was arrested on various charges on July 25, and Temple fell out of the running for Jalen's services.[^cite_note-TpBctV-30][^cite_note-BFJBctV-31] Brunson participated in the July 9–11 LeBron James Skills Academy.[^cite_note-32] On August 5, he announced official visits to Illinois on August 29–31, Villanova on September 4–6 and then Temple from September 11–13 and on August 9, he announced official visits to Michigan State from September 19–21 and Purdue on September 26–28.[^cite_note-33]

On September 8, Brunson announced that he would be curtailing his recruitment and making his decision between Illinois and Villanova on September 10.[^cite_note-SJBtpbIV-34] On that date, Brunson committed to Villanova.[^cite_note-BFJBctV-31][^cite_note-JBctV-35][^cite_note-ST-36] At the time of his decision, he was the No. 1-ranked point guard in the national class of 2015 by [Scout.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout.com).[^cite_note-TpBctV-30] Stevenson was a preseason top-10 team in the [MaxPreps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MaxPreps) national high school rankings.[^cite_note-37] Much was expected of Brunson's team after losing to teams led by Parker and Okafor in the IHSA tournament final four in 2013 and 2014 even though no team from Lake County had ever won a state basketball championship.[^cite_note-Bbp-18] The team rattled off 11 straight victories to start the season before losing 88–81 to [Chaminade Prep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaminade_College_Preparatory_School_\(Missouri\)) and its star [Jayson Tatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayson_Tatum) in the Cancer Research Classic in [Wheeling, WV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeling,_WV), despite 48 points from Brunson.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] On January 17, Brunson and Stevenson faced [Derryck Thornton, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derryck_Thornton,_Jr.?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Findlay Prep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findlay_Prep) at the [Hoophall Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoophall_Classic?action=edit\&redlink=1). At the time, Brunson was the number 3 ranked point guard in the class of 2015 and Thornton was the number 2 ranked point guard in the class of 2016 for the number 1 team in the country according to *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*.[^cite_note-41] Findlay, who was led by [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Wildcats_men's_basketball) signee [Allonzo Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allonzo_Trier)'s 27 points, held Brunson to 26 in the second loss of the season for Stevenson,[^cite_note-42] who had been 15–1.[^cite_note-43] On January 28, he was named to the [2015 McDonald's All-American Boys Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_McDonald's_All-American_Boys_Game) roster,[^cite_note-44] becoming the second player from Lake County to earn such recognition.[^cite_note-BbSJBwMBoI-45] On February 2, he was named to the 11-man Team USA for the [Nike Hoop Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Hoop_Summit).[^cite_note-46] On February 21, Stevenson lost to Simeon as Brunson posted 25 points on 9–24 shooting, ending a 22-game in-state streak in a game that featured the top two teams in the state.[^cite_note-47] The Simeon frontline featured three [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) signees: D. J. Williams (Illinois), Ed Morrow (Nebraska) and Isaiah Moss (Iowa).[^cite_note-48] On March 5, he was named to the [Jordan Brand Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Brand_Classic) roster.[^cite_note-49]



Brunson scoring the first 3 of his IHSA Class 4A title game record 30 points in 2015

On March 17, Brunson helped Stevenson qualify for a third consecutive appearance in the IHSA final four with a victory over [Riverside Brookfield High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Brookfield_High_School). In the game, Brunson became the Lake County career scoring record holder.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] On March 19, Brunson received the highest vote total for the Illinois Class 4A Associated Press All-state team.[^cite_note-52] On March 20, he repeated as Illinois Gatorade Player of the Year.[^cite_note-53] Brunson led Stevenson to the IHSA final four for a third consecutive time, but he led the team to the first state championship by a Lake County school with an IHSA Class 4A title-game record 30 points in a 57–40 victory over [Normal Community High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_Community_High_School). Brunson was 9-for-15 from the field and 9-for-9 from the [free throw](1098087.md) line.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] 2015 was Lake county's ninth consecutive year with a team reaching the IHSA final four; Stevenson became the first school in the large school division and the third school overall to win IHSA football and basketball state championships in the same year.[^cite_note-56] The win got him out of the shadow of IHSA final four losses to Parker- and Okafor-led teams.[^cite_note-57]

Following the season, he won [Illinois Mr. Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Mr._Basketball) with 552 points and 99 of the 132 first place votes, ahead of fellow Jordan Brand All-American selection [Charles Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Matthews_\(basketball\)), who had 157 points.[^cite_note-BbSJBwMBoI-45] Brunson finished his senior season with averages of 23.3 points, 5.2 assists and 4.7 rebounds per game and with shooting percentages of 38% on three-point shots and 83% on free throws.[^cite_note-BbSJBwMBoI-45] At the March 30 POWERADE Jam Fest associated with the McDonald's All-American game, Brunson won the skills competition over finalists [Carlton Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Bragg) and [Isaiah Briscoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Briscoe), and was a finalist for the three-point shooting contest.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Brunson was a first-team [*Parade* All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade_All-America_Boys_Basketball_Team),[^cite_note-60] as well as a third-team [USA Today All-USA high school basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today_All-USA_high_school_basketball_team) selection.[^cite_note-61] In the April 11 Nike Hoops Summit, Brunson had 12 points and seven assists in a 103–101 loss.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] In the April 17 Jordan Brand Classic, Brunson had a game-high three steals.[^cite_note-65] He finished his high school career ranked No. 16 in the ESPN 100 and as the #2-point guard behind Isaiah Briscoe.[^cite_note-66]

### Recruiting



Brunson in the [2015 McDonald's All-American Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_McDonald's_All-American_Boys_Game)

## College career

### Freshman season

Brunson was a selection to the 20-man [Bob Cousy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Cousy_Award) preseason watchlist.[^cite_note-69] He was also selected as the [2015–16 Big East Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Big_East_Conference_men's_basketball_season) Preseason Freshman of the Year by the conference coaches although [Henry Ellenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ellenson) was the only freshman selected to the 2015–16 Preseason All-Big East First or Second Team. ## Professional career

### Dallas Mavericks (2018–2022)

#### Sixth man role (2018–2021)



Brunson takes a [layup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layup) with the [Dallas Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) in a game against the [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md) in 2021

On June 21, 2018, the [Dallas Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) drafted Brunson with the 33rd overall pick. He was the fourth and final Villanova player to be selected in the [2018 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_draft).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Brunson did not sign until after he completed his [2018 NBA Summer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_Summer_League) play and was accorded a 4-year contract similar to a first round selection with 3-years guaranteed.[^cite_note-137] He made his NBA debut on October 17, 2018, recording three points, one rebound, and an assist in a 121–100 loss to the [Phoenix Suns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Suns).[^cite_note-138] After coming off the bench for his first four NBA games, Brunson started in place of the injured [Dennis Smith Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Smith_Jr.) on October 26, against the [Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Raptors), scoring eight points, four assists, and three rebounds.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] On November 8, Brunson posted 11 points against [Utah Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Jazz).[^cite_note-141] Brunson replaced Smith in the starting lineup and posted career highs of 14 points on December 8 against the [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets) and 17 points on December 10 against the [Orlando Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Magic).[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

On January 5, 2019, he was honored by the [Philadelphia Sports Writers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Sports_Writers_Association) as its Amateur Athlete of the Year.[^cite_note-144] That same night, with [J. J. Barea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Barea) and [Devin Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devin_Harris) sidelined, Brunson posted 13 points, a career-high 11 rebounds, and a then-career-high eight assists against the [Philadelphia 76ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers) for his first professional double-double.[^cite_note-145] Starting in place of [Luka Dončić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luka_Don%C4%8Di%C4%87) on February 22, Brunson posted a then career-high 22 points against the [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Nuggets).[^cite_note-146] On March 12, 2019, Brunson improved upon his career high with 34 points with five rebounds, four assists, and a steal in a 112–105 loss to the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md).[^cite_note-147] On April 7, Brunson posted a then career-high 10 assists along with 12 points against the [Memphis Grizzlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_Grizzlies) for his second career double-double.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

With Dončić out of the lineup on December 16, 2019, and Brunson starting, the [2019–20 Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Dallas_Mavericks_season) ended an 18-game win streak by the [Milwaukee Bucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks),[^cite_note-150] on a night when Brunson posted a career-high 11 assists and his first double-double of the season (13 points).[^cite_note-151] On March 13, 2020, Brunson was reported to have undergone surgery to fix a labrum injury in his right shoulder.[^cite_note-152] During the [2020–21 NBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NBA_season), Brunson averaged career highs in points, rebounds, and assists, finishing fourth in voting for the [NBA Sixth Man of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Sixth_Man_of_the_Year_Award).[^cite_note-153]

#### Playoff success (2021–2022)

In the [2021–22 NBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NBA_season), Brunson became a starter and again finished the season achieving career highs in points, rebounds, and assists. His breakout continued in the playoffs, where he led the Mavericks, who were without [Luka Dončić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luka_Don%C4%8Di%C4%87) for the first three games against the Utah Jazz. On April 16, 2022, during game 1 of the first round, Brunson logged 24 points, seven rebounds, and five assists in a 99–93 loss to the Jazz.[^cite_note-154] On April 18, during game 2, Brunson had the best game of his career up to that point, where he dropped 41 points, eight rebounds, and five assists in a 110–104 win over the Jazz.[^cite_note-155] On May 15, Brunson scored 24 points and grabbed six rebounds in a 123–90 game 7 win against the first-seeded Phoenix Suns, securing the Mavericks a place in the Western Conference finals.[^cite_note-156] On May 20, during game 2 of the Western Conference finals, Brunson recorded 31 points, 7 rebounds, and 5 assists in a 126–117 loss against the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors).[^cite_note-157]

### New York Knicks (2022–present)

#### Breakthrough (2022–2023)

On June 2, 2022, his father was hired by the New York Knicks as an assistant coach.[^cite_note-Knicks_Hire_Brunson_as_Assistant_Coach-158] On July 12, 2022, Brunson signed a four-year, \$104 million contract with the [New York Knicks](72855.md).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] The Knicks were later punished for engaging in conversations with Brunson before the free agency window opened.[^cite_note-161] On October 19, he made his Knicks debut in a 115–112 loss in overtime to the Memphis Grizzlies, scoring 15 points with nine assists and six rebounds.[^cite_note-162] On October 26, Brunson scored 27 points, recorded 13 assists, and grabbed seven rebounds during a 134–131 overtime victory against the [Charlotte Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets).[^cite_note-163]

On January 4, 2023, Brunson scored 38 points alongside seven rebounds and six assists in a 117–114 win over the San Antonio Spurs.[^cite_note-164] On January 9, Brunson put up a then-career-high 44 points in a 111–107 loss to the Milwaukee Bucks.[^cite_note-165] On January 16, Brunson was recognized as NBA Eastern Conference player of the Week for the week of January 9–15 after averaging 34.8 points, 5.8 rebounds, and 5.0 assists with 3 30-point games as the Knicks went 3–1 for the week. It was his first career [NBA Player of the Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Player_of_the_Week) award.[^cite_note-166] On January 18, Brunson scored 32 points in a 116–105 loss against the [Washington Wizards](72858.md). He scored 25-plus points in eight straight games, the longest streak by a Knicks guard since [Dick Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Barnett) in 1965. Barnett logged 25 or more points in 12 consecutive contests.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

On February 4, Brunson scored 41 points on 14-of-19 shooting, 5-of-7 from three, 8-of-10 from the free throw line, along with five rebounds and seven assists in a 134–128 overtime loss against the [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md).[^cite_note-169] On February 8, Villanova retired his jersey.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] On February 13, Brunson put up 40 points on 15-of-21 shooting from the field and added five assists in a 124–106 win over the [Brooklyn Nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets).[^cite_note-172] On March 1, Brunson scored 39 points (30 points in the first half) on 15-of-18 shooting, 5-of-6 from three, and 4-of-4 from the free throw line in a 142–118 win over the Brooklyn Nets.[^cite_note-173] On March 2, Brunson won his first [NBA Player of the Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Player_of_the_Month) award when he was named the NBA Eastern Conference Player of the Month for February. He finished the month with 27.3 points and 6.0 assists while shooting 42.6% from three-point range and 52.9% overall. The Knicks went 9–2, finishing out the month with six straight wins.[^cite_note-174] On March 31, Brunson scored a career-high 48 points (33 points in the first half) on 18-of-32 shooting from the floor and delivered nine assists in a 130–116 win over the [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md).[^cite_note-175] In game 4 of the Eastern Conference semifinals against the [Miami Heat](72850.md), Brunson put up 32 points and 11 assists in a 109–101 loss. He joined [Walt Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Frazier) and [Patrick Ewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Ewing) as the only players in Knicks history to put up at least 30 points and 10 assists in a playoff game.[^cite_note-176] New York would lose to Miami in six games despite Brunson tying his playoff career high of 41 points in a 96–92 game 6 loss.[^cite_note-177]

#### First All-Star and All-NBA appearances (2023–2024)

On November 20, 2023, Brunson earned his second NBA Player of the week award as the Knicks went 3–1 while averaging 28.5 points and 6.5 assists, including back-to-back 32-point performances.[^cite_note-178] On November 30, Brunson put up 42 points, six rebounds and eight assists in a 118–112 win over the [Detroit Pistons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Pistons).[^cite_note-179] He also tied the record for the most 40-point games by a point guard (5, Frazier, [Stephon Marbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephon_Marbury)) in Knicks history.[^cite_note-180]

March. He averaged 28.8 points and 5.8 assists per game during the month.[^cite_note-209] On April 7 and 9, Brunson posted 43 points against Milwaukee and 45 against Chicago, respectively, for another back-to-back 40+ pair of games.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] Then, Brunson scored 39 points despite sitting out the fourth quarter against the [Boston Celtics](43376.md) on April 11.[^cite_note-212] This effort made Brunson the third Knick to have posted 5 consecutive 35+-point games (King: 5, 1984–85; Anthony: 6, 2012–13).[^cite_note-20240501NBAsummary-213] By finishing the season with 7 consecutive 30-point games,[^cite_note-214] he tied the Knicks franchise single-season 30-point games record with his 36th ([Richie Guerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richie_Guerin), 1961–62; Ewing, 1989–90). The April 14 season finale 40-point performance against the Bulls also tied him with Ewing for second on the Knicks single-season 40-point game list with 11 (behind King, 13).[^cite_note-215] On April 15, Brunson earned Eastern Conference Player of the Week for averaging 38.5 points, 7.5 assists and 3.5 rebounds and shooting 50 percent on three-point shots, while leading the Knicks to a 4–0 record and a number two playoff seeding.[^cite_note-216] Brunson finished 5th in the [NBA Most Valuable Player Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) voting and was named to the [All-NBA Second Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA_Team) for the first time in his career.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

In game 4 of the [first round of the playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NBA_playoffs#\(2\)_New_York_Knicks_vs._\(7\)_Philadelphia_76ers) against the Philadelphia 76ers, Brunson scored 47 points in a 97–92 victory. The tally was a career single-game playoff-high and a Knicks single-game playoff-record. King had twice scored 46 during the [1984 NBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_NBA_playoffs). With 10 assists, he became the first Knick to score 40 points during an assist-points playoff double double.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] He also became the first Knick with consecutive 30-point assist-points playoff double-doubles and his 24-point second half established the Knick career record with seven 20+-point playoff halves.[^cite_note-20240501NBAsummary-213] In that series, he became the second NBA player (and the first since [Oscar Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Robertson) in the [1973 NBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_NBA_playoffs)) to have three 35+-point/10+-assist games in an NBA playoff series.[^cite_note-222] He became the first player to close out an NBA playoff series with 3 consecutive 40+-point games since [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan) did so with [The Shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shot_\(Bulls%E2%80%93Cavaliers\)) in the [1989 NBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NBA_playoffs).[^cite_note-223] Brunson opened the [second round of the playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NBA_playoffs#\(2\)_New_York_Knicks_vs._\(6\)_Indiana_Pacers) against the [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) with 43 points and 6 assists, becoming the 4th player in NBA history and first since Jordan in the [1993 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_NBA_Finals) to score 40+ in four consecutive playoff games and the first ever to do so with 5+ assists in each of the four playoff games. He also became the first Knick to post five consecutive 30+ point/5+ assist games.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] In game 5, he scored 44 points in the game, including 28 in the first half to set a Knicks record for points scored in a playoff half.[^cite_note-226] In game 7, Brunson fractured his left hand, as the Knicks were eliminated in seven games; shortly after, he underwent surgery to repair his broken hand.[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228]

#### Clutch Player of the Year and Eastern Conference finalist (2024–2025)

On July 12, 2024, two years to the day of his signing with New York, Brunson signed a four-year, \$156.5 million contract extension through the 2028–29 season. He would have been eligible for a five-year, \$256 million contract in 2025 offseason, but he opted to take less in order to help the team have more flexibity to make future moves.[^cite_note-229] A few months later, the Knicks traded for [Karl-Anthony Towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Anthony_Towns).[^cite_note-230]

On August 6, the Knicks named Brunson as the 36th captain in franchise history.[^cite_note-231] Brunson earned conference player of the week on December 2.[^cite_note-232] On December 28, Brunson posted 55 points, 42 of which came in the second half or later, including nine in overtime in a 136–132 overtime victory over the Washington Wizards.[^cite_note-233] On January 25, 2025, Brunson was named as an Eastern Conference starter for the [2025 NBA All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_All-Star_Game).[^cite_note-234] Brunson again earned conference player of the week honors on March 3 for leading the Knicks to a 3–0 record.[^cite_note-235] On April 23, Brunson was named the [NBA Clutch Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Clutch_Player_of_the_Year); during [clutch situations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutch_\(sports\))[^cite_note-236] in the regular season, he led the league in made field goals (52), and ranked second in total points (156) and third in total assists (28). In the same situations, he also shot 51.5% from the field, 84.0% from the free throw line and averaged a league-best 5.6 points per game.[^cite_note-237] Brunson finished 10th in [MVP Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) voting and was named to the [All-NBA Second Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA_Team) for the second-straight year.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]

On May 1, Brunson scored 40 points and hit the game-winning three-pointer to seal the Knicks' series-clinching 116–113 game 6 victory over the Detroit Pistons in the first round of the NBA playoffs.[^cite_note-240] On May 12, Brunson recorded 39 points, 5 rebounds, and 12 assists in a 121–113 game 4 victory,[^cite_note-241] helping the Knicks take a 3–1 series lead over the Boston Celtics in the Eastern Conference semifinals. He became the highest scorer in fourth quarters through the first 10 games of a playoffs since 1997, eclipsing [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) and [Stephen Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Curry).[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243] This marked the first time the Knicks franchise had reached the [Eastern Conference Finals](809525.md) since [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NBA_playoffs#Conference_finals) against the [1999–2000 Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Indiana_Pacers_season) after losing in the conference semi-finals the prior two seasons and 2013.[^cite_note-244]

On May 21, in game 1 of the Eastern Conference finals, Brunson scored 43 points in a 138–135 overtime loss to the [2024–25 Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Indiana_Pacers_season). With the performance, he recorded his 8th 40-point playoff game as a Knick, passing [Bernard King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_King) for the most in franchise history, and tied [Patrick Ewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Ewing) with his 18th 30-point playoff game as a Knick.[^cite_note-245] On May 23, In game 2 of the Eastern Conference finals, Brunson posted 36 points and 11 assists in a 114–109 loss against the Pacers. He passed Ewing for the most 30-point playoff game in Knicks history. Also, only [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan) and [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) have more 35-point, 10-assist playoff games in the Eastern Conference in NBA history.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] On May 29, in game 5 of the Eastern Conference finals, Brunson had 32 points, five rebounds, and five assists in the Knicks' 111–94 victory over the Pacers. It was his 10th game this postseason with at least 30 points and five assists, making him one of only four players in NBA history to reach that mark in a single playoff run. The other three players to achieve this are Michael Jordan, LeBron James and [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](54516984.md).[^cite_note-248] The Knicks would go on to lose to the Pacers in six games, falling 125–108 in the game 6 closeout.

#### NBA Champion and NBA Finals MVP (2025–2026)

Brunson won Eastern Conference Player of the Week on December 1, 2025, by averaging 28.8 points, 4.5 assists and 40.7% three point shooting as the Knicks went 4–0.[^cite_note-249] On December 13, 2025, Brunson scored a then season-high 40 points and delivered 8 assists to lead the [New York Knicks](72855.md) to a 132–120 victory over the Orlando Magic in the [NBA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Cup) semifinal, helping the team advance to the final.[^cite_note-250] On December 16, Brunson scored 25 points and recorded eight assists to lead the Knicks to a 124–113 victory over the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md) in the NBA Cup final, earning NBA Cup MVP honors for his performance and being recognized as a member of the All-Tournament Team.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] On December 19, Brunson had 25 points, seven rebounds, and seven assists, making a game-winning three-pointer with 4.4 seconds remaining in a 114–113 win over the Indiana Pacers.[^cite_note-254] On December 21, Brunson scored a season-high 47 points along with eight assists in a 132–125 victory over the Miami Heat; the 47 points also marked his career-high scoring performance at [Madison Square Garden](75129.md).[^cite_note-255] On December 22, Brunson earned Eastern Conference Player of the Week.[^cite_note-256] For his outstanding performance in December, during which Brunson averaged 30.6 points and 7.1 assists per game and led the Knicks to a 10–4 record, he was named the Eastern Conference [NBA Player of the Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Player_of_the_Month), marking the third time in his career he received the honor.[^cite_note-257]

On January 19, 2026, Brunson earned his third consecutive [NBA All-Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_All-Star) selection and was named a starter for the second time in a row.[^cite_note-258] On January 30, Brunson put up 26 points in a 127–97 win over the Portland Trail Blazers and achieved 10,000 career points.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260] On February 4, Brunson recorded 42 points, 8 rebounds, and 9 assists, leading the Knicks to a 134–127 overtime win over the Denver Nuggets.[^cite_note-261] On March 6, Brunson recorded his second straight 15-assist game in the Knicks' 142–103 win over the Denver Nuggets, becoming the first Knick to do so since [Mark Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Jackson) in 1989.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263] Brunson led the Knicks to a 53–29 record and the third seed in the Eastern Conference. He was named to the [All-NBA Second Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA_Team) for a third consecutive season.[^cite_note-264]

On May 5, Brunson scored 35 points in 31 minutes on 12-of-18 shooting in the Knicks' 137–98 win over the Philadelphia 76ers in game 1 of the Eastern Conference semifinals. He scored 27 points in the first half, including the Knicks' final 11 points before halftime. The game marked his fifth consecutive playoff game with at least 35 points against 76ers. He became only the third player since 2000 to record five straight postseason games with 35 or more points against a single opponent, joining [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) and [Stephen Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Curry).[^cite_note-265] In Game 1 of the [Eastern Conference Finals](79524481.md#\(3\)_New_York_Knicks_vs._\(4\)_Cleveland_Cavaliers), Brunson recorded 38 points, five rebounds, six assists, and three steals, including 17 points in the fourth quarter and overtime, as he led a late 44–11 comeback to help the Knicks overcome a 22-point deficit and defeat the Cleveland Cavaliers 115–104.[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267] In game 2, Brunson put up 19 points (17 of which came in the second half) and a playoff career-high 14 assists to contribute to a 109–93 win.[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] Brunson averaged 25.5 points, 3.3 rebounds, and 7.8 assists per game during the series, as he led the Knicks to sweep the Cavaliers en route to being named the [Eastern Conference finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_conference_finals_MVP_awards) and leading the Knicks to the [2026 NBA Finals](82427430.md) and their first finals appearance since [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271] Brunson and his father became the first [father and son duo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_second-generation_NBA_players) to reach the [NBA finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_finals) as players with the same NBA franchise.[^cite_note-272]

On June 3, in Game 1 of the [NBA Finals](82427430.md), Brunson scored 30 points in the Knicks' 105–95 victory over the San Antonio Spurs. He scored 13 of his 30 points in the fourth quarter, helping New York overcome a 14-point second-half deficit and take a 1–0 lead in the series.[^cite_note-273] With the performance, Brunson became only the second player in franchise history to score at least 30 points in his NBA Finals debut, joining [Willis Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Reed).[^cite_note-274] In Game 2, Brunson recorded 20 points, five rebounds, six assists, and five steals, and converted a game-winning free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining to give the Knicks a 105–104 lead over the Spurs. He helped New York secure a 2–0 series lead in the NBA Finals. The victory made the Knicks just the third team in NBA Finals history to win the first two games of a Finals series on the road, joining the 1993 [Chicago Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bulls) and 1995 [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets). The Knicks also achieved their 13th straight postseason victory in a single playoff run, the second-longest streak in NBA history behind the 2017 [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors).[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277] On June 10, Brunson scored a game-high 36 points with five rebounds, seven assists, and three steals in 44 minutes during Game 4 of the NBA Finals against the Spurs, helping the Knicks erase a 29-point deficit to complete the largest comeback in NBA Finals history and take a 3–1 lead in the series with a 107–106 victory.[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280] With 33 points by [OG Anunoby](52672338.md), it was the first time two Knicks scored at least 30 points in an NBA Finals game.[^cite_note-281] In Game 5, Brunson put up 45 points to lead the Knicks to a 94–90 win and close out the NBA Finals against the Spurs, 4–1, securing the Knicks' first NBA championship in 53 years. He averaged 32.6 points, 4.2 rebounds, 4.6 assists, and 2.0 steals per game over the five-game series while shooting 42.1% from the field and 38.9% from three-point range, and was named [NBA Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Finals_MVP). His 45 points set a franchise NBA Finals record and marked the highest-scoring performance of the series. He also joined [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan) as the only two guards to score at least 45 points in an NBA Finals closeout game in NBA history.[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285] By scoring 45 of the Knicks' 94 points, Brunson tallied 47.9% of his team's points in a title-clinching game, which is second only to Michael Jordan.[^cite_note-286]

Brunson became the eighth player to win NBA Finals MVP after winning an NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament.[^cite_note-KJBjMJKAaftarcf-287] He became one of only five players in basketball history to win an NCAA national championship, the Naismith College Player of the Year Award, an NBA championship, and the NBA Finals Most Valuable Player Award, joining [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar), [Magic Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Johnson), Michael Jordan, and [Bill Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Walton).[^cite_note-288] Brunson became the fourth player to win the NBA Finals MVP after winning an NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament and a state high school championship, joining Abdul-Jabbar, Walton, and Johnson.[^cite_note-289][^cite_note-KJBjMJKAaftarcf-287] He also become one of only four second round NBA draft picks to be named the NBA Finals MVP, joining [Willis Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Reed), [Dennis Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Johnson), and [Nikola Jokić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Joki%C4%87).[^cite_note-NKsuthtfei5cd-290]

Winning the championship on the road in Texas followed his [2016 NCAA Division I men's basketball championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_championship_game) victory in Houston, Texas and his [2018 NCAA Division I men's basketball championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_championship_game) victory also in San Antonio, Texas.[^cite_note-JBbohabotK-291] With "Nova Knicks" teammates [Josh Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hart) and [Mikal Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikal_Bridges), they became the first trio of players to win both an NCAA and NBA championship together.[^cite_note-NKsuthtfei5cd-290] The championship season marked the culmination of a turnaround during Brunson's time. The team had had 4 winning seasons in the 21 seasons before Brunson's 2022 arrival and 4 during Brunson's first four seasons.[^cite_note-JBbohabotK-291] The team had made the [NBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_playoffs) six times and won only one playoff series in the 22 seasons before Brunson's arrival,[^cite_note-292] but won 8 series during Brunson's first four seasons.[^cite_note-JBbohabotK-291] Brunson established an NBA record with a career playoff scoring average 6.8 points above his career regular season scoring average (min 50 playoff games).[^cite_note-293] Jalen and Rick Brunson became the first father-son duo to win an NBA championship together on the same team with one as a player and the other on the coaching staff.[^cite_note-294]

## Playing style

Brunson is known for having elite footwork, but not elite athleticism.[^cite_note-295] Brunson describes himself as a "hustle player".[^cite_note-296] Brunson draws a high number of offensive fouls. This includes charging fouls as well as non-charging fouls such as "illegal screens, pushoffs and other forms of induction". This is a result of a style of play that evolved out of necessity due to being equipped with strength and agility, but not the height, length or verticality to differentiate himself off the ground.[^cite_note-297]

## Personal life

On September 21, 2022, Brunson announced his engagement to physical therapist Ali Marks, his [high school sweetheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood_sweetheart).[^cite_note-324] The proposal took place at Stevenson High School's gymnasium following Brunson's induction into the school's athletic Hall of Fame.[^cite_note-325] Brunson and Marks were married on July 29, 2023, at [The Ritz-Carlton Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Tower_Place); Brunson's current and former teammates were in attendance.[^cite_note-326][^cite_note-327] The wedding had to be moved forward from [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day) weekend (September 2) to accommodate Brunson's selection to United States men's national basketball team for the [2023 FIBA Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup).[^cite_note-328] Brunson and Marks welcomed their first child, a daughter, on July 31, 2024.[^cite_note-329]

Brunson is close friends with [New York Knicks](72855.md) teammate [Josh Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hart), a former teammate at Villanova and both run a podcast called *Roommates* together.[^cite_note-Ganguli_2023_c309-330][^cite_note-331] Along with other former Villanova and Knicks teammates, Hart and [Donte DiVincenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donte_DiVincenzo) were in attendance at Brunson's wedding to Marks, and DiVincenzo served as a groomsman.[^cite_note-332]

On June 28, 2024, Brunson appeared in *[WWE SmackDown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE_SmackDown)* at [Madison Square Garden](75129.md), alongside [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) guard [Tyrese Haliburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Haliburton).[^cite_note-333]

Brunson is of [Jamaican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaicans) descent through his maternal grandparents who were both born in Jamaica.[^cite_note-334]
