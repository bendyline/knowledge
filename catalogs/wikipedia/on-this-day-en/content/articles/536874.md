# Flag desecration

"Flag burning" redirects here. For burning of flags as a method of disposal, see [Flag protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_protocol).

Not to be confused with [Defacement (flag)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defacement_\(flag\)).



Countries by legality of flag desecration as of 2026

**Flag desecration** is the [desecration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desecration) of a [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag), violation of [flag protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_protocol), or various acts that intentionally destroy, damage, or mutilate a flag in public. In the case of a [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_flag), such action is often intended to make a political point against a country or its policies.

Some countries have laws against methods of destruction, such as burning in public, or forbidding particular uses, such as for commercial purposes. Such laws may distinguish between the desecration of the country's own [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_flag) and the desecration of flags of other countries. Some countries have banned the desecration of all flags on their soil.[^cite_note-lloc-1]

## Background

Actions that may be treated as the desecration of a flag include [burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning) it,[^cite_note-Charney-2] [urinating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urination) or [defecating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defecation) on it, defacing it with [slogans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slogan),[^cite_note-Charney-2] stepping upon it, damaging it with stones; bullets; or any other projectile, cutting or ripping it,[^cite_note-Charney-2] improperly flying it, verbally [insulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insult) it, dragging it on the ground,[^cite_note-Welch-3] or eating it, among other things.[^cite_note-auto-4]

Flag desecration may be undertaken for a variety of reasons. It may be a protest against a country's [foreign policy](233404.md), including one's own, or the nature of the government in power there. It may be a protest against [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) or a deliberate and symbolic insult to the people of the country represented by the flag. It may also be a protest at the very laws prohibiting the act of desecrating a flag.

### Flag desecration laws

In some countries, desecrating a flag is illegal and punishable by a [prison sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison_sentence) or a [fine](146699.md). In countries where it is not, the act may still be prosecuted as [disorderly conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disorderly_conduct) or [arson](184695.md) or, if conducted on someone else's property, as [theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theft) or [vandalism](32676.md).

## By jurisdiction

### Algeria

In Algeria, flag desecration is a crime. According to article 160 [bis](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bis#Latin) of the Algerian penal code, the intentional and public shredding, distortion, or desecration of the national flag is punishable by five to ten years of imprisonment.

In 2010, an Algerian court convicted 18 people of flag desecration and punished them by up to six years of imprisonment and \$10,000 in fines after protests about jobs and housing.[^cite_note-12]

### Argentina

The Penal Code (Código Penal) on its Article 222 criminalizes the public desecration of the national flag, coat of arms, national anthem, or any provincial symbol, imposing one to four years of imprisonment.[^cite_note-13] In other words, in Argentina, flag desecration is a crime punishable by up to one year of imprisonment.

### Armenia

The Armenian criminal code punishes any insult to the flag (as well as to the coat of arms and the national anthem) with community service of up to two years or imprisonment of up to one year.[^cite_note-14]

### Australia

Main article: [Flag of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Australia)

#### Legality

Flag desecration is not, in itself, illegal in Australia.[^cite_note-15] However, flag desecration must be compliant with the law.

In *[Coleman v Kinbacher & Anor (Qld Police)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleman_v_Kinbacher_&_Anor_\(Qld_Police\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)*,[^cite_note-colemankinbacher-16] Coleman was successfully prosecuted for flag burning, not because of its political nature, but because given the size of the flag, the use of petrol as an accelerant, and the fact that it was in an open park area, many members of the public experienced "concern, fright and anger", and in these circumstances flag burning could be considered [disorderly conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disorderly_conduct).

#### Attempts to ban flag burning

There have been several attempts to pass bills making flag burning illegal in Australia, none of which have yet been successful. For four consecutive years between 1989 and 1992, [National Party](21927.md) MP [Michael Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Cobb) introduced bills making it an offence to desecrate, dishonour, burn, mutilate or destroy the Australian national flag. On each occasion, the bill failed.[^cite_note-17] As of May 2016, the most recent bill which attempted to ban flag burning was the *Flags Amendment (Protecting Australian Flags) Bill 2016*, which was introduced by [National Party](21927.md) MP [George Christensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Christensen) but lapsed in April 2016.[^cite_note-flagbill2016-18]

#### Historical occurrences

During the [2005 Cronulla riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Cronulla_riots), a Lebanese-Australian youth, whose name has been kept secret, climbed a [Returned and Services League (RSL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Returned_and_Services_League_of_Australia) club building and tore down its flag before setting it on fire. The youth was sentenced to 12 months [probation](406786.md) not for flag desecration but for the destruction of property of the RSL.[^cite_note-19] In October of that year the youth accepted an invitation from the RSL to carry the Australian flag along with war veterans in the [Anzac Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzac_Day) march the following year.[^cite_note-news.com.au-20] However, the RSL was forced to withdraw this invitation as it received phone calls from people threatening to pelt the youth with missiles on the day.[^cite_note-21] The head of the New South Wales RSL was quoted as saying that "the people who made these threats ought to be bloody ashamed of themselves".[^cite_note-news.com.au-20]

In 2006, Australian contemporary artist [Azlan McLennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azlan_McLennan) burnt an Australian flag and displayed it on a billboard outside the Trocadero artspace in [Footscray, Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footscray,_Victoria). He called the artpiece *Proudly UnAustralian*.[^cite_note-22]

The socialist youth group [Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_\(socialist_youth_organisation\)) marketed "flag-burning kits" – inspired by, and to protest, the censorship of Azlan McLennan's art – to university students.[^cite_note-23]

Tasmanian Aboriginal Centre worker Adam Thompson burned the Australian flag on the week of [Australia Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Day) (2008) celebrations in [Launceston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launceston,_Tasmania)'s City Park to the cheers of about 100 people, who were rallying against what they call "[Invasion Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_Day)".[^cite_note-24]

[Tent embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Tent_Embassy) activists burned the Australian flag on 27 January 2012 at the entrance to [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra)'s Parliament House as they called for Aboriginal sovereignty over Australia.[^cite_note-25]

### Austria

In Austria, sections 248 and 317 of the federal law criminal code state that desecration of national and international symbols in a manner that makes the act known to a broad public is illegal. Section 248 deals specifically with maliciously insulting, disparaging or degrading a flag, emblem or anthem of the republic or one of its federal states;[^cite_note-26] section 317 does the same for those of foreign states or international organizations.[^cite_note-27] Punishment in both cases is imprisonment for up to six months, or a fine.

### Azerbaijan

The Criminal Code of Azerbaijan, Article 324, penalizes desecration of the national flag or state emblem with imprisonment for up to one year.[^cite_note-28]

### Belgium

Flag desecration is not illegal in Belgium. [Flemish](10878.md) nationalists have burned Belgian flags on at least one occasion.[^cite_note-29]

### Brazil

Brazilian law number 5700, chapter V,[^cite_note-30] from 1971, concerns respect and the national flag:

Article 30 states that, when in the flag is being marched or paraded (for example, when the national anthem is being played), everyone present must take a respectful attitude, standing in silence. Males must remove any head coverings. Military personnel must salute or present arms according to their corps' internal regulations.

Article 31 states that people are prohibited from:

1. presenting or flying any national flag which is in a poor condition;

2. altering the proportions, colors, shape, or label of the national flag, or defacing it with numbers, words or symbols or any other inscriptions;

3. using the national flag as clothing, a mouth covering, curtain, tablecloth or napkin, table trim, a podium coating, or as a cover for objects that are to be inaugurated; and

4. using the national flag as a label or wrapping for products at a sale.

Article 32 states that flags in a bad condition must be sent to the nearest military unit for incineration on Flag Day according to ceremonial procedures.

Article 33 states that, except at diplomatic missions such as embassies and consulates, no foreign flag may be flown without a Brazilian flag of the same size in a prominent position alongside it.

Chapter VI of the law states, in article 35, that the act of a civilian breaking this law is considered a [misdemeanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdemeanor), punished with a fine of one to four times the highest reference value active in the country, doubled in repeated infringement cases. In the Brazilian Armed Forces' Military Penal Code,[^cite_note-31] article 161, a soldier, airman or seaman who disrespects any national symbol is punished with one to two years' detention; officers may be declared unsuitable for their rank. In other words, the desecration of a flag is illegal in Brazil and punishable by one month of imprisonment and a fine of up to ten reais.

### Bulgaria

The desecration of any national symbol, including the national flag, is a crime in Bulgaria, which is punishable by up to two years of imprisonment and a fine of up to €1,600.

#### Legality

Flag desecration is not, in itself, a crime in Canada. Acts of this nature are forms of expression protected by the [Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Charter_of_Rights_and_Freedoms).

In 1990, during heated political times around the [Meech Lake Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meech_Lake_Accord), the [flag of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Quebec) was desecrated by protestors in [Brockville, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brockville,_Ontario) opposed to [Quebec's language laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_the_French_Language) after the Canadian flag had been burnt in protests in Quebec. Televised images of individuals stepping on the Quebec flag were played in [Quebec](7954867.md) and contributed to the deterioration in relations between Quebec and [English Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canada). The incident, seen as a metaphor of Canada's perceived rejection of Quebec (and of Quebec's distinctiveness in the demise of the Meech Lake Accord) was invoked by [Quebec nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_nationalism) during the run-up to the [1995 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Quebec_referendum) on [Quebec independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_sovereignty_movement) and is still remembered today.

In 1999, members of the [Westboro Baptist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westboro_Baptist_Church) from the United States staged a burning of the Canadian Flag outside of the [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md) in [Ottawa, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa,_Ontario). This was to protest legalization of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Canada) which was being adjudicated by the Canadian court.

### Chile

The *Ley de Seguridad Interior del Estado*,[^cite_note-32] articles 6 and 7, defines as a crime the public desecration of the national flag, coat of arms, the name of the country or the national anthem, and imposes a period of imprisonment, relegation or estrangement for a period of up to one year.

### China

Flag desecration is a crime in China. Article 299 of the penal code[^cite_note-33] provides for up to three years of imprisonment criminal detention, public surveillance, or deprivation of political rights for "whoever desecrates the [National Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_China) or the [National Emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_emblem_of_China) of the People's Republic of China by intentionally burning, mutilating, scrawling on, defiling or trampling upon it in a public place".

#### Hong Kong

Wikisource logo

[Wikisource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikisource) has original text related to this article:&#x20;

***[National Flag and National Emblem Ordinance](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/National%20Flag%20and%20National%20Emblem%20Ordinance)***

***[Regional Flag and Regional Emblem Ordinance](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Regional%20Flag%20and%20Regional%20Emblem%20Ordinance)***

Chinese laws concerning flag desecration were incorporated into Hong Kong law as the [National Flag and National Emblem Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Flag_and_National_Emblem_Ordinance?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1997 as required by Annex III of [the territory's constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Basic_Law). The [Regional Flag and Regional Emblem Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Flag_and_Regional_Emblem_Ordinance?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the equivalent statute in respect of the Hong Kong flag. Both ordinances ban desecration of the Chinese flag and Hong Kong flag, respectively, through methods including "burning, mutilating, scrawling on, defiling or trampling".[^cite_note-lawmakerarrest-34]

In 1999, two individuals were convicted for desecration of the [Regional Flag of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Hong_Kong) and the [Chinese flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_China). They were found guilty by a magistrate, had the conviction overturned in the [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Hong_Kong)[^cite_note-35] but the convictions were restored by the [Court of Final Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Final_Appeal_\(Hong_Kong\)). They were [bound over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_over) to keep the peace on their own recognisance of \$2,000 for 12 months for each of the two charges. In the judgment, Chief Justice [Andrew Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Li) said although the [Basic Law of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_of_Hong_Kong) guarantees freedom of speech, flag desecration is not legal because there are other protest methods.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Social activist Koo Sze-yiu has been convicted several times of flag desecration. He was sentenced to a nine-month prison term in 2013 for the offence. However, the sentence was reduced to four months and two weeks after an appeal. In March 2016, he was sentenced to a six-week prison term for burning the regional flag in Wanchai on [HKSAR Establishment Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Special_Administrative_Region_Establishment_Day) in 2015. Koo responded that "he is happy to be punished as being jailed is part of the life of an activist, and he would continue to protest against the Beijing and Hong Kong governments and fight for democracy."[^cite_note-rthk1-38] In January 2021, Koo was again jailed, this time for four months, for displaying an inverted Chinese flag with slogans written on it in July 2020.[^cite_note-39]

In October 2016, some miniature Chinese and Hong Kong flags that had been placed by pro-Beijing legislators in the Legislative Council chamber were flipped upside down by lawmaker [Cheng Chung-tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_Chung-tai), who regarded them as "cheap patriotic acts". In April 2017 he was charged with flag desecration. He alleged that the arrest was part of a "general cleansing" of dissenting voices ahead of [Carrie Lam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Lam)'s inauguration as new chief executive.[^cite_note-lawmakerarrest-34] On 29 September 2017, the [Eastern Magistrates' Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrates'_Court_\(Hong_Kong\)) found Cheng guilty and fined him \$5,000.[^cite_note-guilty-40]

In December 2019, a 13-year-old girl was sentenced to 12 months' probation for flag desecration during the [2019–20 Hong Kong protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Hong_Kong_protests). She received a curfew as well as a criminal record; the act was described as "rash" by magistrate Kelly Shui. Government intervention was on the basis of "(maliciously) challenging the national sovereignty".[^cite_note-41]

### Croatia

Croatian history recalls the burning of the flag of the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary#Austria-Hungary_\(1867%E2%80%931918\)) during the [1895 visit of Emperor Franz Joseph to Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1895_visit_of_Emperor_Franz_Joseph_to_Zagreb). Two people involved in the incident, [Stjepan Radić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stjepan_Radi%C4%87) and [Vladimir Vidrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vidri%C4%87), later pursued notable careers in politics and literature, respectively. In modern Croatia, desecrating any national flag or treating any national flag in a disrespectful manner is a felony. Offenders can face up to one year of imprisonment.[^cite_note-42]

### Kingdom of Denmark

#### Denmark

In [Denmark](76972.md), it is legal to burn or desecrate the national flag, the [Dannebrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-DRFlag-43][^cite_note-debate-44] However, it is illegal to publicly burn or desecrate the flags of foreign countries, the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_Nations) or the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Europe) according to § 110e of the [Danish penal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_penal_code)[^cite_note-debate-44][^cite_note-45] because [Parliament](429743.md) has decided that burning or desecrating these is a matter of [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Denmark),[^cite_note-DRFlag-43] as it could be construed as a threat. This law is rarely enforced; the last conviction was in 1936.[^cite_note-DRFlag-43]

Although there are no laws regulating the disposal of a worn out Dannebrog, the tradition is to burn it privately.[^cite_note-46]

#### Faroe Islands

In the autonomous [Faroe Islands](17238607.md), the flag law states that the Faroese flag, *[Merkið](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merki%C3%B0)*, may not be desecrated, "neither by words nor by deeds".[^cite_note-47]

### Dominican Republic

The law of patriotic symbols[^cite_note-48] (Law 210-19) which among other things regulates the use of the [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Dominican_Republic), states that any person who desecrates the flag may be punished with 1 to 3 months of imprisonment and a fine ranging from 5 to 20 [minimum wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage).

### Egypt

Flag desecration has been illegal in Egypt since 2014. It is punishable by a fine of up to 30,000 Egyptian pounds (about \$4,300).[^cite_note-49]

### Estonia

According to section 245 of the Estonian criminal code, it desecrating the national flag or other national symbols is punishable by imprisonment up to one year or a fine. ### Ethiopia

In 2009, the [Parliament of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Ethiopia) passed Proclamation 654/2009 (The Federal Flag Proclamation), which prohibited firstly amongst 23 other provisions "use \[of] the Flag without its Emblem", as well as "to deface the Flag by writing or displaying signs,  \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*] symbols, emblems or picture  \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*]", or "to prepare or use the Flag without the proper order of its colors and size or its Emblem". While most offenses were punishable by a fine of "3000 birr or rigorous imprisonment up to one year", the first offense, mandating the usage of the emblem, received an increased penalty of "5000 birr or the rigorous imprisonment of up to three years".[^cite_note-FNG0908-51] This replaced the 1996 Flag Proclamation, which had made no mention of offenses or penalties.

### Finland

According to section 8 of the law on the [Finnish flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Finland), it is illegal to desecrate the flag, treat it in disrespecting manner, or remove it from a public place without permission.[^cite_note-52]

### France

According to French law,[^cite_note-53] a person outraging the French national anthem or the French flag during an event organized or regulated by public authorities is liable to a fine of up to €7,500 and a punishment of up to six months of imprisonment if performing in a gathering. The law targets "outrageous behaviour" during public ceremonies and major sports events.

This clause was added as an amendment to a large bill dealing with internal security, in reaction to a [football](10568.md) match during which there had been whistles against *[La Marseillaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Marseillaise)*, but also to similar actions during public ceremonies.[^cite_note-54] The amendment initially prohibited such behaviour regardless of the context, but a parliamentary commission later restricted its scope to events organized or regulated by public authorities,[^cite_note-55] which is to be understood, according to the ruling of the [Constitutional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Council_of_France), as events organized by public authorities, mass sport matches and other mass events taking place in enclosures, but not private speech, literary or artistic works, or speech during events not organized or regulated by public authorities.[^cite_note-56]

In 2006, a man who had publicly burnt a French flag stolen from the façade of the city hall of [Aurillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurillac) during a public festival, organized and regulated by public authorities, was fined €300.[^cite_note-57]

A July 2010 law makes it a crime to desecrate the French national flag in a public place, but also to distribute images of a flag desecration, even when done in a private setting, if the objective is to create trouble in public space.[^cite_note-58] On 22 December 2010, an Algerian national was the first person to be convicted under the new status, and ordered to pay a €750 fine after breaking the pole of a flag hung in the [Alpes-Maritimes](80989.md) prefecture a day prior.[^cite_note-59]

### Germany



German flags being burned in protest

Under the German criminal code (§90a *[Strafgesetzbuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strafgesetzbuch)* (StGB)), it is illegal to revile or damage the German federal flag as well as any flags of its states in public. Offenders can be fined or sentenced to a maximum of three years in prison, or fined or sentenced to a maximum of five years in prison if the act was intentionally used to support the eradication of the Federal Republic of Germany or to violate constitutional rights. Actual convictions because of a violation of the criminal code need to be balanced against the constitutional right of the freedom of expression, as ruled multiple times by Germany's constitutional court.[^cite_note-60]

The [original law from 1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Flag_Desecration_Law_\(Germany\)) was expanded in 1935 to include the [flag of Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Nazi_Germany).

As of 2020, it is also punishable by up to three years of imprisonment for damaging or reviling the flag of any foreign country (§104 StGB). Until then, only flags that were shown publicly by tradition, event, or routinely by representatives of an official foreign entity were protected. The legislative reform to include also unofficially or privately used flags was an explicit reaction to the repeated burning of Israeli flags during anti-Israeli protests.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

As part of that reform, a newly formed §90c StGB was introduced that extends the scope of protection to the flag and anthem of the [European Union](9317.md).

After the [fall of the Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Berlin_Wall) in November 1989, some [East Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) cut out the emblem from their [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_East_Germany) in support for a reunified, democratic Germany. This flag is now used by the [Federal Foundation for the Reappraisal of the SED Dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Foundation_for_the_Reappraisal_of_the_SED_Dictatorship).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

### Greece

Under article 188 of Greece's Penal code, flag desecration, along with the desecration of other Greek symbols, is illegal and punishable by up to two years of imprisonment.

### Hungary



1956 Revolution Flag flying in front of the [Hungarian Parliament Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament_Building)

Desecration of the national flag is illegal in Hungary.[^cite_note-65]

During a demonstration at the beginning of the [Hungarian revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_revolution_of_1956) someone in the crowd cut out the communist coat of arms from the Hungarian flag, leaving a distinctive hole, and others quickly followed suit. The "flag with a hole" became a symbol of the Hungarian resistance.[^cite_note-Heller-66][^cite_note-67] The practice of cutting out the communist coat of arms was also followed by other [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries, such as [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania), especially during the [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989).

### India

Main articles: [Flag of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_India) and [Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_Insults_to_National_Honour_Act,_1971)

The [Indian Flag Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Flag_Code) is a set of laws that govern the usage of the Flag of India. The [Bureau of Indian Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Indian_Standards) is in charge of the enforcement of the manufacture of the flag according to the guidelines.

Violation of the code may invite severe punishments and penalties. The code was written in 2002 and merged the following acts: provisions of the Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950 (No. 12 of 1950) and the [Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971 (No. 69 of 1971)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_Insults_to_National_Honour_Act,_1971).

The Indian Flag Code was often criticized for being too rigid and prevented ordinary citizens from displaying the flag on homes and other buildings. For many years, only government officials and other government buildings could unfurl the flag. That changed in 2001 when [Naveen Jindal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naveen_Jindal) won a court case in the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) to give Indians the right to unfurl the flag publicly. The Indian cricket batsman [Sachin Tendulkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachin_Tendulkar) was accused of sporting the flag on his cricket helmet below the [BCCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Control_for_Cricket_in_India) emblem. He later changed it and placed the flag above the emblem. The flag code was updated in 2005; some new provisions include that the flag cannot be worn under the waist or on undergarments.[^cite_note-68]

### Indonesia

Article 24 of Law No. 24/2009 on Flags, Language, National Symbols, and Anthems, states that people are prohibited from:[^cite_note-UU_No_24/2009-69]

1. destroying, tearing, trampling, burning, or performing other actions with the intention to tarnish, insult, or degrade the honour of the national flag;

2. using the national flag for billboards or commercials;

3. flying the national flag if it is damaged, torn, smudged, crumpled, or faded;

4. printing on, embroidering or adding letters, numbers, images or other signs, or adding badges or any objects to the national flag;

5. using the national flag to cover a ceiling or roof, or for wrapping or covering goods in a way that can degrade the honor of the national flag.

Article 66 and 67 of Law No. 24/2009 states that anyone who commits any of these prohibited acts may face up to ten years of imprisonment as punishment or a fine of up to a billion rupiah.

### Ireland

### Japan



Japanese flag being burned by protestors in front of the Consulate-General of Japan in Hong Kong

In Japan, under Chapter 4, Article 92 of the [Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_of_Japan), the desecration of a recognized foreign nation's national flag and symbol to dishonour that particular nation is illegal and results in fine or penal labour, but [only on complaint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkokuzai) by the foreign government. Additionally, the Criminal Code forbids publicly damaging, removing, or defiling a "tangible object recognized in common social understanding" as being used as the Japanese national flag, in a manner that "causes people to feel extreme discomfort or disgust".[^cite_note-\:02-84]

In May 1958, the [flag of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_China), the *Wǔ Xīng Hóngqí*, at a postage stamp convention was pulled down and damaged, but as Japan did not recognize the PRC at the time, the law was not applied. In February 2011, [Japanese ultra-rightists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyoku_dantai) held a protest over the [Kuril Islands dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuril_Islands_dispute) outside of the Russian embassy in Tokyo, during which they dragged a [Russian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Russia) on the ground; Russian foreign minister [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) stated that his ministry had asked the Japanese government to launch a criminal case over the incident. On 26 October 1987, an Okinawan supermarket owner burned the *Hinomaru*, before the start of the [National Sports Festival of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Festival_of_Japan).[^cite_note-wundunn1995-85] The flag burner, Shōichi Chibana, burned the national flag not only to show opposition to atrocities committed by the wartime Japanese army and the continued Japanese-requested presence of U.S. forces, but also to prevent it from being displayed in public.[^cite_note-86] Other incidents in Okinawa included the flag being torn down during school ceremonies and students refusing to honor the flag as it was being raised to the sounds of "[Kimigayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimigayo)".[^cite_note-87]

The desecration of the [Japanese flag](144954.md), the *Hinomaru*, was not a crime until 2026. Absent from such law, the act of desecration was implicitly protected by Article 21 covering [freedom of speech](21401843.md) in the [Constitution of Japan](62699.md). In late January 2021, a group inside the ruling [Liberal Democratic Party](164231.md) including [Sanae Takaichi](8125532.md) announced an intention to propose a law in the Diet to prohibit the desecration of the *Hinomaru*.[^cite_note-88] However, this attempt did not proceed past the internal screenings of the party.[^cite_note-89] On 20 October 2025, LDP and [Japan Innovation Party](48459506.md) formed [a coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party%E2%80%93Japan_Innovation_Party_coalition) government, with Takaichi as the Prime Minister. The coalition agreement includes a promise to criminalize the desecration of the national flag during the 2026 Ordinary Diet Session.[^cite_note-90] On 30 June 2026, the [House of Representatives](217778.md) passed the [Law Concerning the Punishment of Damage to the National Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Concerning_the_Punishment_of_Damage_to_the_National_Flag), which criminalizes the desecration of the [flag of Japan](144954.md).[^cite_note-91] On 17 July 2026, the [House of Councillors](217779.md) passed the act, enacting it into law.[^cite_note-\:02-84]

### Malaysia

While Malaysia does not have specific legislation regarding flag desecration, legal action can be taken against those who show disrespect towards the national flag *[Jalur Gemilang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalur_Gemilang)* under the [Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_Code_\(Malaysia\)), [Sedition Act 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedition_Act_1948) and the Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act 1963.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

### Mexico

### Morocco

In Morocco, desecrating the national flag is a criminal offense under Law No. 17-05, which amended the Penal Code in 2005. ### New Zealand

In New Zealand, under the [Flags, Emblems and Names Protection Act 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags,_Emblems,_and_Names_Protection_Act) it is illegal to destroy the [New Zealand flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_New_Zealand) with the intent of dishonouring it. In 2003, Paul Hopkinson, a [Wellington](33804.md) schoolteacher, burned the national flag of New Zealand as part of a protest in Parliament grounds at the New Zealand Government's hosting of the [Prime Minister of Australia](24117.md), against the background of [Australia's support of the United States in the Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_contribution_to_the_2003_invasion_of_Iraq). Hopkinson was initially convicted under Flags, Emblems and Names Protection Act 1981 of destroying a New Zealand flag with intent to dishonour it, but appealed his conviction. On appeal, his conviction was overturned on the grounds that the law had to be read consistently with the right to freedom of expression under the [Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Bill_of_Rights_Act_1990). This meant that his actions were not unlawful because the word dishonour in the Flags, Emblems and Names Protection Act had many shades of meaning, and when the least restrictive meaning of that word was adopted Hopkinson's actions did not meet that standard. This somewhat unusual result was due in part to the fact that the Bill of Rights does not overrule other laws (*[Hopkinson v Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkinson_v_Police)*).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

In 2007, activist Valerie Morse had burned the New Zealand flag during an [ANZAC Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZAC_Day) dawn service in [Wellington](33804.md). She was fined NZ\$500 by the Wellington District Court and her conviction was upheld by the High Court and the Court of Appeal. After Morse's lawyers appealed the conviction on the grounds that she was being punished for expressing ideas, the [New Zealand Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Supreme_Court) ruled in 2011 that the previous rulings had misinterpreted the meaning of "offensive behavior" in the Summary Offences Act.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-Stuff.co.nz_4971083-125]

### Nigeria

Any person who flies or exhibits the National Flag in a defaced or bad condition shall be guilty of an offence. It is punishable by a fine of [₦](178686.md)100 and in the case of a continuing offence to a fine of [₦](178686.md)10 for every day or part of a day during which the offence is continued after the day on which such person is first convicted.[^cite_note-Mudi-7][^cite_note-LawNigeria-8][^cite_note-Dirisu-9]

### North Macedonia

The desecration of the national flag, coat of arms or anthem and international flags, coat of arms or anthems is banned under Articles 178 and 181 in the Criminal Code of North Macedonia.[^cite_note-126]

It is questionable if these laws are enforced as there have been many instances where the national and foreign flags were set on fire.

### Norway

Desecration of foreign countries' flags or national coats of arms was previously banned according to the General Civil Penal Code §95. However, the ban had rarely been practiced, and was eventually lifted in 2008.

Comedian [Otto Jespersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Jespersen_\(comedian\)) burned a U.S. flag during a satirical TV show in 2003. During the [Jyllands-Posten Muhammad cartoons controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyllands-Posten_Muhammad_cartoons_controversy), Norwegian flags were burned in demonstrations in various Muslim countries.[^cite_note-127]

### Pakistan

Pakistan's flag comes under the strict protection of various constitutional clauses. However, the statutes governing the topic consist only of Pakistan Flag Protocols and are unclear as with regards to legal status of the offender and the punishment under the [Pakistan Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Penal_Code).

### Panama

On 9 January 1964, a dispute broke out between Panamanian students and Americans living in the [Panama Canal Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_Zone) over the right of the [flag of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Panama) to be raised next to the [flag of the United States](11447.md), as the Canal Zone was then a [disputed territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputed_territory) between the two nations. During the scuffle a Panamanian flag carried by Panamanian students was torn. This sparked four days of riots that ended with 22 Panamanians and four Americans dead and with Panama breaking diplomatic relations with the United States. This event is considered very important in the decision to negotiate and sign the [Torrijos-Carter Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrijos-Carter_Treaties), by whose terms the [Panama Canal](24849.md) administration was handed over to the Panamanian Government at the end of 1999. 9 January is known as [Martyrs' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs'_Day_\(Panama\)) and is commemorated in Panama as a day of mourning.

### Peru

The precise law in Peru is unclear, but such acts are clearly capable of causing outrage. In 2008, the dancer, model and actress [Leysi Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leysi_Su%C3%A1rez) appeared naked photographed using [Peru's flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Peru) as a saddle while mounted on a horse. The country's defence minister said she would face charges that could put her in jail for up to four years for "offending patriotic symbols".[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] However, the case was closed in 2010.[^cite_note-130]

### Philippines

Section 34a of the 1998 [Flag and Heraldic Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Philippines) of the Philippines declares that it is a prohibited act "to mutilate, deface, defile, trample on or cast contempt or commit any act or omission casting dishonor or ridicule upon [the flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Philippines) or over its surface".[^cite_note-Republic_Act_No._8491-131]

Section 50 meanwhile declares, "Any person or judicial entity which violates any of the provisions of this Act shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of not less than five thousand [pesos](227672.md) (₱5,000.00) not more than twenty thousand pesos (₱20,000.00), or by imprisonment for not more than one (1) year, or both such fine and imprisonment, at the discretion of the court: Provided, That for any second and additional offenses, both fine and imprisonment shall always be imposed: Provided, That in case the violation is committed by a [juridical person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juridical_person), its President or Chief Executive Officer thereof shall be liable."[^cite_note-Republic_Act_No._8491-131]

Flag burning is only permitted in the case of proper disposal of the flag.[^cite_note-132]

A crucial point of etiquette for the Philippine flag is that flying it upside down (i.e., red field over blue), or vertically hanging it with the red to the viewer's left, makes it the national [war standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_flag). Outside of an official [state of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_war), Filipinos consider this a major *faux pas* or a highly offensive act: several instances of this incorrect display (usually by foreigners) have attracted online backlash, prompting official apologies.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

### Poland

Polish Criminal Code (1997) declares:

> Article 137. § 1. Whoever publicly insults, destroys, damages or removes an emblem, banner, standard, flag, ensign or other symbol of the State shall be subject to a fine, the penalty of restriction of liberty or the penalty of deprivation of liberty for up to one year.
>
> § 2. The same punishment shall be imposed on anyone, who on the territory of the Republic of Poland publicly insults, destroys, damages or removes an emblem, banner, standard, flag, ensign or other symbol of another State, publicly displayed by a mission of this State or upon an order of a Polish authority.
>
> &#x20;Article 138. § 1. The provisions of Articles 136 and 137 § 2 shall apply, when the foreign country ensures reciprocity.[^cite_note-135]

### Portugal

Currently, according to article 332 of the [Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_Code_of_Portugal),[^cite_note-136] "Who publicly, by means of words, gestures or print publication, or by other means of public communication, insults the Republic, the Flag or the National Anthem, the coats of arms or the symbols of Portuguese sovereignty, or fails to show the respect they are entitled to, shall be punished with up to two years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 240 days". In the case of the regional symbols, the person shall be punished with up to one year's imprisonment or a fine of up to 120 days (fines are calculated based on the defendant's income).

The Portuguese Penal Code (article 323) also forbids the desecration of foreign symbols: "Who publicly, by means of words, gestures or print publication, or by other means of public communication, insults the official flag or other symbol of sovereignty of a foreign State or of an international organization of which Portugal is a member shall be punished with up to one year imprisonment or a fine of up to 120 days." This article applies under two conditions (article 324): that Portugal maintains diplomatic relations with the insulted country, and that there is reciprocity (i.e., that the insulted country would also punish any insult against Portuguese symbols of sovereignty, should they occur there).

### Romania

The Romanian Criminal Code[^cite_note-137] no longer prohibits flag desecration (as was the case with the previous criminal code).[^cite_note-138] Several laws attempting to reinstate punishments for manifestations which express contempt for the Romanian symbols (according to the constitution, these are the flag, national day, anthem and coat-of-arms) have not been approved.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

During the [Romanian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) of 1989, the Communist era flag was flown with [the coat of arms cut out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_flags_of_Romania#Flag_of_the_Romanian_Revolution_of_1989), leaving a hole.

### Russia

National flag burning is a crime in Russia, which is punishable by up to one year of imprisonment.[^cite_note-142]

In February 2011, [Japanese ultra-rightists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyoku_dantai) held a protest over the [Kuril Islands dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuril_Islands_dispute) outside of the Russian embassy in Tokyo, during which they dragged a [Russian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Russia) on the ground; Russian foreign minister [Sergei Lavrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Lavrov) stated that his ministry had asked the Japanese government to launch a criminal case over the incident.

In 2013, the U.S. rock band [Bloodhound Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodhound_Gang) desecrated a Russian flag during a concert in Ukraine. In response, Vladimir Markin of the [Investigative Committee of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) said that his department was prepared to file criminal charges if prosecutors thought they had a case.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

In 2025, a Russian [streamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_stream), Stepan Scoffy, was detained after desecrating his Russian flag over an online donation.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

### Samoa

The Official Flag and National Anthem of Samoa Act 1994 stipulates that any person "who by act, word or conduct wilfully offers an insult to the [Flag of Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Samoa)" may be fined or imprisoned for up to six months.[^cite_note-147]

### Saudi Arabia

The [flag of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Saudi_Arabia) bears the *[shahada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahada)* ([Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic) declaration of faith). Because the *shahada* is considered holy, even the slightest disrespect amounts to not only desecration but [blasphemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy). This has led to several incidents of controversy. In 1994, [McDonald's](2480627.md) printed carry-out bags bearing the flags of all nations participating in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) (with a green flag with [Saudi Arabia's coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Arms_of_Saudi_Arabia) superimposed, rather than the Saudi flag), while [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola) did the same on cans of soda. Because of Saudi Arabian objections, the companies stopped producing those items.[^cite_note-148] Also during the FIFA World Cup, in 2002, Saudi Arabian officials protested against printing the flag on a football on the belief that kicking the creed with the foot was unacceptable.

Because of the *shahada*, even flying this flag at [half-mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-mast) is considered desecration in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-Goldstein-149] This also applies to the flags of [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Afghanistan) and [Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Somaliland), which also bear the *shahada*, as well as the flag of [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Iraq), which bears the *[takbir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takbir)*.

### Serbia

In Serbia, flag desecration is illegal.

### Singapore

In Singapore, flag desecration is illegal under the statuory of National Symbols Act 2022[^cite_note-150] and National Symbols Regulations 2023.[^cite_note-151] Under current constitution, using the national symbol (such as Presidential symbols, state crests, national flag, public seals and national flowers) in a derogatory manner (including defacing, dirtying and obstructing some symbols like stars and crescents) also amounts to flag desecration,[^cite_note-152] which enforces a stop-work order or a maximum penalty of S\$30,000 or a six-month imprisonment or both (previously S\$1,000).[^cite_note-153]

### Slovenia

In Slovenia, desecrating national symbols is a criminal offence. Article 163 of the Criminal Code (*Kazenski zakonik*, KZ-1) punishes anyone who publicly insults the flag, coat of arms or anthem of the Republic of Slovenia with a fine or imprisonment of up to one year.[^cite_note-KZ-1-163-10] Article 164 extends the same penalty to public insult of the flag, coat of arms or anthem of a foreign state or an international organisation recognised by Slovenia.[^cite_note-154]

### South Africa

During the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) era, protesters would burn [the former South African flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_South_Africa_\(1928%E2%80%931994\)) in protest against the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) policies of the South African government. In one example, Americans opposed to apartheid burned the old South African flag at an anti-apartheid protest in the U.S. state of Massachusetts during the mid-1980s.[^cite_note-155] South Africans opposed to minority rule also burned the (now former) South African flag,[^cite_note-156] viewing it as a symbol of the country's government at the time. Under the [1983 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Constitution_of_1983) desecration of the flag was a criminal offense punishable by up to five years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-lloc-1]

In post-apartheid South Africa there is no law against flag desecration yet.[^cite_note-lloc-1] The current South African flag designed and adopted in 1994 has been the subject of desecration. In early 1994, white supremacists from the "[Afrikaner Volksfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaner_Volksfront)" organization burned the new South African flag in [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein) in protest against the country's pending democratization.[^cite_note-firstraising-157]

### South Korea

The South Korean *Criminal Act* punishes flag desecration, of both domestic and foreign, in various ways:[^cite_note-158]

- Article 105 imposes up to five years in prison, [disfranchisement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfranchisement) of up to ten years, or a fine up to seven million [won](298884.md) for damaging, removing, or staining a South Korean [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_South_Korea) or [emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_South_Korea) with intent to insult the South Korean state. Article 5 makes this crime punishable even if done by aliens outside South Korea.

- Article 106 imposes up to one year in prison, disfranchisement of up to five years, or a fine up to two million won for [defaming](28661.md) a South Korean flag or emblem with intent to insult the South Korean state. Article 5 makes this crime punishable even if done by aliens outside South Korea.

- Article 109 imposes up to two years in prison or a fine up to three million won for damaging, removing, or staining a foreign flag or emblem with intent to insult a foreign country. Article 110 forbids [prosecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antragsdelikt) without foreign governmental complaint.

The desecration of the national flag in South Korea by the country's own citizens is rare when compared to analogous instances in other countries as the flag is viewed more along the lines of an [ethnic flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_flag) rather than merely just the flag of a state.

### Soviet Union

The flag of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was burned many times by protestors against its government's policies, for instance in Brazil by those protesting the [Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia) of 1968,[^cite_note-159] and in New York City in 1985 by protesters against the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War).[^cite_note-160] The Soviet flag was burned or otherwise desecrated during the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) in [Ukraine](31750.md).

### Spain

The [Spanish Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Penal_Code) punishes insulting the [flag of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Spain) and the [flags of the autonomous communities of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_autonomous_communities_of_Spain) with a fine that can range from €420 to €144,000.[^cite_note-161] However, flag burning is often seen in nationalist demonstrations.[^cite_note-Beswick-162]

### Suriname

Article 176a of the [Surinamese criminal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_criminal_code?action=edit\&redlink=1) imposes up to 6 months of imprisonment to "He who intentionally expresses himself regarding the flag of Suriname in a manner offensive to national sentiments".[^cite_note-163]

### Sweden

In Swedish law, there is no explicit prohibition against burning the flag of any country.[^cite_note-164] The desecration of the national flag was decriminalized in 1971. However, publicly showing a Swedish flag modified with added marks, characters or symbols is forbidden according flag law number 1982\:269, and may be punishable under the provisions regarding disorderly conduct (*förargelseväckande beteende*) under the chapter 16 § 16 of the criminal code.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

In 1997 a teenager who identified as a [skinhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_power_skinhead) was fined 500[kr](16709.md) for waving a large, modified Swedish flag from the top of a hill during an event celebrating Sweden's [National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Day_of_Sweden). The motifs that had been added to the flag in question were those of a [sun wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sun_\(symbol\)), an axe, a viking warrior's head in profile and the word *"Valhalla"*. The royal family being present, the event being part of the city of [Lycksele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycksele)'s 50 year jubilee, and the act of standing on the hill to increase the number of people who could see the flag, were all seen as contributing factors in the sentence.[^cite_note-167]

### Switzerland

The destruction, removal, or desecration of national emblems installed by a public authority (i.e., the [Swiss flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_flag), the [Swiss coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_coat_of_arms), the [cantonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_cantons) or [municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Switzerland) flags and coats of arms) is punishable by fines or up to three years of imprisonment according to the Swiss federal penal code.[^cite_note-168] However, the destruction or desecration of privately owned flags or coats of arms is legal.[^cite_note-169]

### Taiwan

Under Articles 118 and 160 of the Criminal Law of the Republic of China (Taiwan), it is a criminal offence to insult either the national flag or the national emblem of any country. If it is a national flag or emblem of a foreign country being insulted, the name of the offence would be 'obstructing state diplomacy'; if it is the ones of the Republic of China (Taiwan), the offence would be 'disturbing the order'. Besides, insulting or damaging the portrait of Sun Yat-sen is also punishable as 'disturbing the order'. The penalty can be either incarceration for up to one year, or a fine of \$9,000 NTD or less.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

### Thailand

In October 2018, Prime Minister [Prayut Chan-o-cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayut_Chan-o-cha) ordered the [Foreign Ministry](20529358.md) to investigate fashion footwear in Paris that incorporates the colours of the [Thai national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Thailand). Photos of the shoes, shown on the *[Vogue Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_Paris)* Facebook page taken during [Paris Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Fashion_Week), outraged Thai social media users, some of whom demanded apologies and jail sentences for the perpetrators. As was pointed out in the Bangkok daily, *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation_\(Thailand\))*, "The combination of the Thai flag and human feet is a [contentious cultural cocktail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Thailand#Customs) for Thais."

The designers are immune from prosecution as the shoes were made and displayed outside Thailand. Were the offence committed in Thailand, those responsible could face a 2,000 baht fine or a year in jail.[^cite_note-172]

A spokesman at the Thai National Flag Museum[^cite_note-173] commented that no one has a copyright on the flag's colours or the order in which they are presented.[^cite_note-174]

### Turkey

### Ukraine

In Ukraine, the desecration of national symbols, including the national flag, is a crime punishable by up to one year of imprisonment.

### United Kingdom



[Ulster loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_loyalism) prepare to burn the [Irish flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ireland) on a bonfire on the eve of [The Twelfth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Twelfth) in [Belfast](5046.md).

The [law](85441.md) of [England and Wales](275187.md) and the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law) of [Scotland](26994.md) have no specific concept of "flag desecration".[^cite_note-176] Flag desecration is not illegal in the United Kingdom.

In May 1998, in a protest by 2,000 former prisoners of war, a [Burma Railway veteran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Railway_veteran) torched the [Rising Sun banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_Sun_banner) before both [Emperor Akihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Akihito) and [Queen Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II). On appeal by [case stated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_stated) the [Administrative Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Bench_Division#Administrative_Court) found that her conviction for "using threatening, abusive and insulting words or behaviour likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress" contrary to section 5 of the [Public Order Act 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Order_Act_1986) was overturned as incompatible with [Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_10_of_the_European_Convention_on_Human_Rights).[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

In 2011, a group of approximately 20–30 students at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge) influenced the burning of a large British flag, the centerpiece of the [Student Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_Union)'s decorations to celebrate the [wedding of Prince William and Catherine Middleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Prince_William_and_Catherine_Middleton). The Union Flag has also been burned by [Argentine nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_nationalism) protesting [British sovereignty of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_dispute).[^cite_note-182]

#### Northern Ireland

Unlike the rest of the United Kingdom, the law in [Northern Ireland](21265.md) has varied since its foundation in 1921. ### United States

Further information: [Flag of the United States § Display and use](11447.md#Display_and_use)



A 2025 map of flag desecration laws in the United States, unenforceable since *Texas v. Johnson*



Iranian protesters burning the [flag of the United States](11447.md) in Tehran, November 2018

Flag desecration is not a crime in the United States. The [flag of the United States](11447.md) is sometimes burned as a cultural or political statement, in protest of the policies of the U.S. government, or for other reasons, both within the U.S. and abroad. The [United States Supreme Court](31737.md) in *[Texas v. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_v._Johnson)*, [491](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_491) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) [397](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/491/397/) (1989), and reaffirmed in *[U.S. v. Eichman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Eichman)*, [496](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_496) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) [310](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/496/310/) (1990), has ruled that due to the [First Amendment to the United States Constitution](31653.md), it is unconstitutional for a government (whether federal, state, or municipal) to prohibit the desecration of a flag, due to its status as "[symbolic speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_speech)".

However, content-neutral restrictions may still be imposed to regulate the [time, place, and manner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time,_place,_and_manner) of such expression. If the flag that was burned was someone else's property (as it was in the *Johnson* case, since Johnson had stolen the flag from a Texas bank's flagpole), the offender could be charged with petty larceny (a flag usually sells at retail for less than US\$20), or with destruction of private property, or possibly both. Desecration of a flag representing a minority group may also be used in some jurisdictions to support the prosecution of a crime as a [hate crime](13547.md).[^cite_note-189]

In the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the U.S. flag was flown by the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) against the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America). [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) general [Benjamin Franklin Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Butler_\(politician\)) ordered the 1862 execution for treason of [William B. Mumford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_B._Mumford), who had removed a Union flag in [occupied New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_New_Orleans).[^cite_note-190] An apocryphal tale of [Barbara Fritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Fritchie) preventing Confederate soldiers dishonoring her Union flag was propagated by [John Greenleaf Whittier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Greenleaf_Whittier)'s 1863 poem "[Barbara Frietchie](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Barbara%20Frietchie)", which contains the famous lines:

> "Shoot, if you must, this old gray head,\
> &#x20;But spare your country's flag," she said.

During the United States' involvement in the [Vietnam War](32611.md), American flags were sometimes burned during war protest demonstrations.[^cite_note-191]

The [Flag Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Protection_Act) was passed, protecting flags from anyone who "mutilates, defaces, physically defiles, burns, maintains on the floor or ground, or tramples upon any flag".[^cite_note-192] This law was later struck down in the *Eichman* decision. After that case, several flag burning amendments to the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) were proposed.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-193]

In June 2005, a [Flag Desecration Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Desecration_Amendment) was passed by the [House](19468510.md) with the needed two-thirds majority. In June 2006, another attempt to pass a ban on flag burning was rejected by the [Senate](24909346.md), in a close vote of 66 in favor and 34 opposed, one vote short of the two-thirds majority needed to send the amendment to be voted on by the states.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-193] [Bob Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Bennett_\(politician\)) introduced the [Flag Protection Act of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Protection_Act_of_2005), cosponsored by [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton), [Barbara Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Boxer), [Tom Carper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Carper) and [Mark Pryor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Pryor).

#### Confederate flag

The [Battle Flag of the Army of Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America#Battle_flag), commonly referred to as the Confederate flag, has sometimes been burned in protest as well. In 2000, protesters from the [Jewish Defense League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Defense_League) burned Confederate and Nazi flags to protest an arson attempt against a synagogue in [Reno, Nevada](26388.md). This was criticized by a representative of the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League), who said that it was more effective to work with the police and other authorities rather than to engage in "tactics which inflame and exacerbate situations".[^cite_note-201]

Of the states which continue to have laws against flag burning, in spite of them being ruled unconstitutional, five afford this protection to the Virginian battle flag as well: Florida,[^cite_note-202] Georgia,[^cite_note-203] Louisiana,[^cite_note-204] Mississippi,[^cite_note-205] and South Carolina.[^cite_note-206]

### Uruguay

Desecration of foreign national flags is punished by the article 139 of the Penal Code:

> Article 139 (Disrespect of foreign emblems) Whoever, in the territory of the State, disrespects, in a public place or in a place open or exposed to the public, the flag or any other emblem of a foreign State, shall be punished with six months of imprisonment to three years of penitentiary.[^cite_note-207]

There is no restriction in place for the desecration of the Uruguayan national flag.

### Uzbekistan

In Uzbekistan, desecration of state symbols, including the national flag of Uzbekistan or [Karakalpakstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakalpakstan), emblem, or anthem, is punishable under Article 215 of the Criminal Code with fines, community service, or correctional labor for up to three years. Additionally, Article 203 of the Administrative Code imposes fines for violating legislation on state symbols, with stricter penalties for repeat offenses.[^cite_note-208]

### Venezuela

Since the demonstrations against the refusal by the government to renew the broadcasting license of [RCTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCTV) (a major TV network), the upside-down [flag of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Venezuela) has been adopted as a symbol of protest for this and other alleged threats to civil liberties. Demonstrators claim that it is a sign of distress and a call for help. However, government and ruling-party officials insist that these demonstrators are desecrating the flag. An official video sharply criticizing this practice as disrespectful was produced.[^cite_note-YouTube_19_June_2007-209] [Globovisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globovisi%C3%B3n) prepended to the video a statement denouncing the message as violative of the [Law on Social Responsibility on Radio and Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_on_Social_Responsibility_on_Radio_and_Television), "for constituting anonymous official propaganda".[^cite_note-YouTube_19_June_2007-209]
