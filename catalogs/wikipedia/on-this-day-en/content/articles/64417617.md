**Zahra Ghanbari** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): زهرا قنبری;[^cite_note-FD-1] born 4 March 1992) is an Iranian professional [footballer](191466.md) who plays as a [forward](428681.md) for the [Kowsar Women Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowsar_Women_Football_League) club [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_W.F.C.) and the [Iran national team](9896170.md). She is Iran's [all-time top goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_top_international_women's_football_goal_scorers_by_country).

## Early life and education

Zahra Ghanbari[^cite_note-afc-2] was born on 4 March 1992[^cite_note-FD-1] in [Kangavar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangavar), [Kermanshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermanshah_Province), Iran.[^cite_note-GSA-3][^cite_note-FD-1] Kermanshah is the largest [Kurdish](3979351.md)-populated city in Iran and one of the most significant urban centres of [Kurdish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_culture) in the region,[^cite_note-4] and Ghanbari is Kurdish.[^cite_note-isna-5]

## Club career

Ghanbari has played in Iraq for the [Ghaz Al-Shamal SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaz_Al-Shamal_SC) women's team (North Gas Sports Club) team in [Kirkuk](199292.md), which plays in the [Iraqi Women's Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Women's_Football_League).[^cite_note-FD-1]

In Iran, she has played for [Bam Khatoon F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bam_Khatoon_F.C.) (formerly known as Shahrdari Bam) since at least 2013. She played for [Vechan Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vechan_Kurdistan?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 2020/2021.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-isna-5]

Ghanbari played for Bam Khatoon in the [2023 AFC Women's Club Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Women's_Club_Championship) and the [2024–25 AFC Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_AFC_Women's_Champions_League), scoring one goal in the latter tournament.[^cite_note-afc-2] In November 2024, Ghanbari was briefly suspended by the [Iranian federation](1262861.md) and forced to apologise for "improper hijab" during a goal celebration at the 2024–25 AFC Women's Champions League. After being forced to apologise, she was allowed to return to the team.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-healy2026-8]

She moved to [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis_W.F.C.) in 2025.[^cite_note-healy2026-8]

## International career

Ghanbari is [Iran's national team](9896170.md)'s [all-time top goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_top_international_women's_football_goal_scorers_by_country).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-healy2026-8]

She played for the international team in the following tournaments (number of goals in parentheses):[^cite_note-afc-2]

- [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md) 2008 (1), 2018 (6), 2022 (0)

- [AFC U-19 Women's Championship 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_AFC_U-19_Women's_Championship) (2)

- [Women's Olympic Football Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Olympic_Football_Tournament) 2020 (6)

- [AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament) 2024 (0)

- [AFC Women's Asian Cup 2026](67991141.md)

On 9 March 2026, after the national team was eliminated from the [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) in Australia, Ghanbari (who played in all three games), along with four teammates from the Iranian women's national football team, [left the team's training camp](82634971.md) in Australia and sought asylum. This was due to concerns about possible retaliation from Iranian authorities after the team had refrained from singing the national anthem after the opening game, which was shortly after the start of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-10] The [Australian Government](1222540.md) granted humanitarian visas to the players.[^cite_note-healy2026-8] On 15 March, Iranian state media reported that Ghanbari had withdrawn her asylum application and would return to Iran via Malaysia.[^cite_note-11]

Ghanbari was one of a number of Iranians to have their assets seized by the [Iranian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Government), but they were released by a court decision around 12 April 2026, based on her "change in behaviour".[^cite_note-12]
