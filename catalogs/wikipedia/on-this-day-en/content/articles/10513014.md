**Tabqa** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ٱلطَّبْقَة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *aṭ-Ṭabaqah*),[^cite_note-1] formerly **Al-Thawrah**, is a city in [Raqqa Governorate](5317143.md), [Syria](7515849.md), approximately 55 kilometres (34 mi) west of [Raqqa](858965.md). Until the 1960s it had been a relatively small settlement.[^cite_note-2] The city had a population of 69,425 as of the [2004 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Syrian_census).[^cite_note-3]

## Etymology

Since the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md), the city has officially reverted to its original name, *Tabqa*. The name literally means "the layer" or "the stratum" in Arabic, and likely refers to the geographic or topographic features of the area, such as stepped terrain or layered rock formations along the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates), where the city and dam are located.

Prior to the fall of the Assad regime and after the [1963 Syrian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), on 8 March 1967, the city was renamed *al-Thawrah* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ٱلثَّوْرَة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ad-Ṭhawrah*), meaning *The Revolution*, in honor of the 1963 Syrian coup d'état and the [Ba'athist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athism) ideological vision.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-5]

## History

### Syrian civil war

On 26 November 2012, during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), a main route from [Raqqa](858965.md) to [Aleppo](159244.md) passing through Tabqa along the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) was dotted with both government and [Syrian rebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_rebel) checkpoints.[^cite_note-damaap-6] On 11 February 2013, [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) rebel groups including the [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) [al-Qaeda](1921.md)-linked [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) and [Liwa Owais al-Qorani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_Owais_al-Qorani) took over the city.[^cite_note-7] On 21 November, there was fierce fighting between government troops and rebels in the town,[^cite_note-8] but by 25 November, the rebels were back in control.&#x20;

In January 2014, The [Islamic State](9087364.md) took control of the city.[^cite_note-9] During ISIL rule, the town's [Catholic](606848.md), [Antiochian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church_of_Antioch) and [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East) churches were turned into a parking garage, a weapons factory and a barn, with [ISIL](9087364.md) militants destroying all Christian symbols on the three churches. The [Shia](26961.md) Al Zahraa' Mosque was destroyed and an [Ismaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma'ilism) place of worship was turned into a children's training centre.[^cite_note-10] In addition, high ranking IS members would reside in the city, to escape the bombardments on its capital Raqqa.&#x20;

On 22 March 2017, the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) began the [Battle of Tabqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tabqa_\(2017\)) to retake the city, as the [international coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJTF%E2%80%93OIR) assisted by conducting airstrikes. [SOHR](34029669.md) reported that the airstrikes killed or injured more than 40 people,[^cite_note-11] while the [BBC](19344654.md) reported 27 killed and 40 wounded.[^cite_note-12] On May 10, 2017, the SDF successfully recaptured the city,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-ArabiyaTabqa-14] during which an estimated 40% of the buildings were either damaged or destroyed.[^cite_note-\:1-15]

In early January 2026, the [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) launched the [2026 northeastern Syria offensive](82106178.md) to take control of territories under the control of the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) (SDF). In the early morning of 18 January 2026, the [Syrian Armed Forces](27479.md) announced the capture of the city from the SDF, who had held the city since 2017.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Demographics



[Euphrates Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Lake) beach.

Prior to the Syrian civil war, the majority of the city's inhabitants were [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Arabs](2185.md), with [Kurdish](3821855.md), Armenian, [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Syria) as well as Ismaili and Shiite Arab minorities.[^cite_note-18]

The Assyrian minority consisted of around 1,000 people, with about half belonging to the [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East), originating from the Khabour River villages, and the other half being [Syriac Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church) Christians, along with a few [Chaldean Catholic](698821.md), [Syriac Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic_Church) and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) families.[^cite_note-19] Since the civil war, the Christian population has been slowly declining.[^cite_note-20]

## Geography



Tabqa is the administrative centre of [Tabqa Subdistrict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabqa_Subdistrict) and the [Tabqa District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabqa_District).

The [Tabqa Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabqa_Dam) and [Euphrates Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Lake) on the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) river, an important energy source for Syria, are near the town.[^cite_note-\:1-15]
