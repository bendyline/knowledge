Parliamentary elections were held in [Somalia](27358.md) on 26 March 1969. A total of 64 parties ran in the election, many of which had been formed shortly beforehand.[^cite_note-1] The result was a victory for the [Somali Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Youth_League) (SYL), which won 73 of the 123 seats. In total, 27 parties won seats, but immediately after the elections, most of the MPs for the smaller parties joined the Somali Youth League. The SYL held 109 seats by the end of May, in addition to being in a coalition with the Somali National Congress. This gave the SYL control of 120 of the 123 seats.[^cite_note-2]

This was to be the last election in Somalia prior to a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Somali_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) on 21 October, when officers of the far-left [Supreme Revolutionary Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Revolutionary_Council_\(Somalia\)) led by [Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre) transformed Somalia into a [single-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-party_state) [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist-Leninist) state that would last until the fall of the regime in 1991.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Electoral system

The electoral system used was a mixture of [single-member plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting_system) and [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation). There were 48 electoral districts. In ten of them, only one member was elected, and thus the single-member plurality system was used. In the remaining districts, seats were distributed using party-list proportional representation and the [Hare quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare_quota). The average number of members elected in a multi-member district was approximately three.[^cite_note-6]
