**South Holland** ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Zuid-Holland* [\[ˌzœyt ˈɦɔlɑnt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f9/290_Zuid-Holland.ogg/290_Zuid-Holland.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f9/290_Zuid-Holland.ogg/290_Zuid-Holland.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Netherlands) of the [Netherlands](21148.md) with a population of over 3.8 million as of January 2023[^cite_note-auto-2] and a population density of about 1,410/km<sup>2</sup> (3,700/sq mi), making it the country's most populous province and one of the world's most densely populated areas. Situated on the [North Sea](21179.md) in the west of the Netherlands, South Holland covers an area of 3,308 km<sup>2</sup> (1,277 sq mi), of which 609 km<sup>2</sup> (235 sq mi) is water. It borders [North Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Holland) to the north, [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_\(province\)) and [Gelderland](57160.md) to the east, and [North Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Brabant) and [Zeeland](52229.md) to the south. The provincial capital is the Dutch seat of government [The Hague](30269.md), while its largest city is [Rotterdam](26049.md). The [Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt_delta) drains through South Holland into the North Sea. [Europe](9239.md)'s busiest [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port), the [Port of Rotterdam](4410985.md), is located in South Holland.

## History

### Early history

Archaeological discoveries in [Hardinxveld-Giessendam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardinxveld-Giessendam) indicate that the area of South Holland has been inhabited since at least c. 7,500 years before present, probably by nomadic hunter-gatherers. Agriculture and permanent settlements probably originated around 2,000 years later, based on excavations near [Vlaardingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlaardingen). In the classical antiquity, South Holland was part of the [Roman Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Province) of [Germania Inferior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_Inferior), and the border of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) ran along the [Old Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oude_Rijn_\(Utrecht_and_South_Holland\)) and reached the [North Sea](21179.md) near [Katwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katwijk). The Romans built fortresses along the border, such as [Praetorium Agrippinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorium_Agrippinae) near modern-day [Valkenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkenburg,_South_Holland), [Matilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilo) near modern-day [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden), and [Albaniana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albaniana_\(Roman_fort\)) near modern-day [Alphen aan den Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphen_aan_den_Rijn). A city was founded near modern-day [Voorburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voorburg), [Forum Hadriani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Hadriani). It was built according to the grid plan, and facilitated a square, a court, a bathhouse and several temples.

After the departure of the Romans, the area belonged to the [Frisian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisian_Kingdom), after which it was conquered by the [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francia) king [Dagobert I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagobert_I) in 636. In 690, the Anglo-Saxon monk [Willibrord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willibrord) arrived near Katwijk and was granted permission to spread [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) by the Frankish king [Pepin II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_of_Herstal). He accordingly founded a church in [Oegstgeest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oegstgeest), after which the entire area was gradually Christianised. The area was appointed to [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia) in the [Treaty of Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Verdun) in 843, after which the king granted lands to [Gerolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerolf_of_Holland), who had helped him claim the lands. This was the birth of the [County of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Holland). Gerolf was later succeeded by [Dirk I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_I,_Count_of_Holland), who continued to rule Holland under the Frankish king. In 1248, count [William II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_II_of_Holland) ordered the construction of the [Ridderzaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridderzaal), which was later finished by his son and successor [Floris V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floris_V_of_Holland).

The [Binnenhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binnenhof) by the [Hofvijver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofvijver) (1625)

The first city in South Holland to receive city rights was [Dordrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dordrecht), which did so in 1220. The city retained a dominant position in the area until it was struck by a series of floods in the late 14th century. The same century also saw a series of civil wars, the [Hook and Cod wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_and_Cod_wars), concerning the succession of count [William IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_IV_of_Holland). Both his daughter [Jacqueline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline,_Countess_of_Hainaut) and his brother [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_III,_Duke_of_Bavaria), the latter supported by [Philip the Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_the_Good), Duke of [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy), claimed the throne. The conflict ended in 1490, with John victorious.

Overall, the area of South Holland remained largely agrarian throughout the late Middle Ages. This changed around 1500, when Holland became Europe's most urbanised area. During the [Eighty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty_Years'_War), the area of South Holland was the scene of the [Capture of Brielle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Brielle), the [Siege of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leiden) and the assassination of [William the Silent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Silent).

The [United Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) declared their independence in 1581, and Holland quickly emerged as the country's dominant province, with important trading cities such as Leiden, [Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft), [Gouda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouda,_South_Holland), and Dordrecht. In 1575, the Netherlands' first university was founded in Leiden by William the Silent. [The Hague](30269.md), which had originated around the castle of the counts of Holland, became its new political centre. Both the [States of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Holland_and_West_Friesland) and the [States General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_General_of_the_Netherlands) seated in the [Binnenhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binnenhof). The [Dutch Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Golden_Age) blossomed in the 17th century. The south of Holland, back then often referred to as the *Zuiderkwartier* (literally "South Quarter"), was the birthplace and residence of scientists such as [Antoni van Leeuwenhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_van_Leeuwenhoek) and [Christiaan Huygens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Huygens), philosophers such as [Baruch Spinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Spinoza) and [Pierre Bayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Bayle), and painters such as [Johannes Vermeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Vermeer), [Rembrandt van Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembrandt_van_Rijn) and [Jan Steen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Steen).

### As a province

The province of South Holland as it is today has its origins in the period of French rule from 1795 to 1813. This was a time of bewildering changes to the Dutch system of provinces. In 1795, the [Batavian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Republic) was proclaimed and the old order was swept away by a series of constitutional changes in the following years. In the Constitution enacted on 23 April 1798, the old borders were radically changed. The republic was reorganised into eight departments with roughly equal populations. The south of Holland was split up into three departments. The islands in the south were merged with Zeeland and the west of North Brabant to form the Department of the [Scheldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheldt) and [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse). The north of the area became the Department of the [Delf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delf_\(waterway\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). A small region in the east of the area became part of the Department of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), which spanned much of Gelderland and Utrecht. In 1801, the old borders were restored when the department of Holland was created. The reorganisation had been short-lived, but it gave birth to the concept of a division of Holland, creating less dominant provinces. In 1807, Holland was reorganised once again. This time, the department was split in two. The south, what would later become South Holland, was called the Department of [Maasland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasland). This also did not last long. In 1810, all the Dutch provinces were integrated into the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire), and Maasland was renamed [Bouches-de-la-Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-de-la-Meuse).



The centre of [Rotterdam](26049.md) in ruins after the [Rotterdam Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Blitz) (1940)

After the defeat of the French in 1813, this organisation remained unchanged for a year or so. When the 1814 Constitution was introduced, most borders were restored to their situation before the [French period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_period). The north and south of Holland were reunited as the province of Holland. However, the division had not completely been undone. Since its re-establishment in 1814, Holland had always had two [King's Commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Commissioner), one for the north and one for the south. Even though the province had been reunited, the two areas were still treated differently in some ways and the idea of dividing Holland remained alive. In 1840, it was decided to once again split [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland) into [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Holland) and South Holland, because the province of Holland was deemed too dominant in area, population and wealth. In the late 20th and early 21st centuries, five municipalities were transferred from South Holland to Utrecht; [Oudewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudewater) in 1970, [Woerden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woerden) in 1989, [Vianen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vianen) in 2002, [Leerdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leerdam) and [Zederik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zederik) in 2019.

The construction of the [Nieuwe Waterweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Waterweg) in 1863 marked the start of the growth of the [Port of Rotterdam](4410985.md). On 14 May 1940, during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the centre of [Rotterdam](26049.md) was destroyed by a [German bombardment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Blitz). The subsequent German occupation of the Netherlands resulted in anti-Jewish measures, and many members of Dutch resistance were captured and executed on the [Waalsdorpervlakte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waalsdorpervlakte). At the same time, the [Atlantikwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantikwall) was constructed along the coast. After the Second World War, in 1953, the south of South Holland was heavily struck by the [North Sea flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1953), which took the lives of 677 South Hollanders. After this, the Dutch government decided for the construction of the [Delta Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Works), which came to an end with the completion of the [Maeslantkering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maeslantkering) in 1997.

## Geography

South Holland covers an area of 3,308 km<sup>2</sup> (1,277 sq mi), of which 609 km<sup>2</sup> (235 sq mi) is water. It borders the [North Sea](21179.md) to the west, [North Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Holland) to the north, [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_\(province\)) and [Gelderland](57160.md) to the east, and [North Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Brabant) and [Zeeland](52229.md) to the south. Beside the dunes along the North Sea coast, the province is almost entirely flat and mostly consists of [polders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polder). The centre of South Holland and the area along the coast in the west are largely urban, part of the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad) conurbation, while the east of the province is more agrarian and belongs to the [Groene Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groene_Hart), literally *Green Heart*. The south of the province is a collection of islands in the [Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Meuse%E2%80%93Scheldt_delta).

The [Duin- en Bollenstreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duin-_en_Bollenstreek) is a region in the northwest of South Holland, around [Katwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katwijk), that features coastal dunes and the cultivation of flower bulbs. To the south of the region lie mostly pastures on sand soil, that form the transition to more urban area. This urban area runs along the [Old Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oude_Rijn_\(Utrecht_and_South_Holland\)), from Katwijk via [Rijnsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijnsburg) to [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden) and [Zoeterwoude-Rijndijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoeterwoude-Rijndijk). South of Leiden and north of [The Hague](30269.md) lies an area where a landscape of dunes bordering the North Sea in the west transitions to one of peat more to the east. The easternmost tip of South Holland is part of [De Biesbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Biesbosch), one of the largest national parks of the Netherlands and one of the last freshwater tide areas in Europe. Other parks in South Holland, although no national parks, include [Delftse Hout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delftse_Hout) east of [Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft),[^cite_note-5] [Ackerdijkse Plassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ackerdijkse_Plassen), a [bird reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_reserve) north of Rotterdam,[^cite_note-6] and [Buytenpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buytenpark?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Westerpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerpark_\(Zoetermeer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) near Zoetermeer.[^cite_note-7] The [Kagerplassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagerplassen) are a collection of lakes north-east of Leiden, popular for boating, watersports, fishing, camping and walking.

The southern part of South Holland consists of a number of [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island) of the [Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Meuse%E2%80%93Scheldt_delta). Although technically islands in the sense that they are surrounded by rivers, canals or other bodies of water, most of these islands are well connected to the rest of the province via bridges, tunnels and dams. The southern islands, most notably [Goeree-Overflakkee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goeree-Overflakkee) (1 on the map), [Tiengemeten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiengemeten) (2), [Hoeksche Waard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoeksche_Waard) (6) and [Voorne-Putten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voorne-Putten) (4/5), are largely agrarian, while more to the north, the islands are more urban, such as [Dordrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dordrecht) (7), [IJsselmonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJsselmonde_\(island\)) (9) and [Rozenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozenburg) (10), while other islands, like [Rozenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozenburg_\(island\)) (10), are mostly used for petrochemical industry. Together, these cities form the [Rijnmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijnmond) conurbation, centred on the city of [Rotterdam](26049.md). Together with [Haaglanden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaglanden) (centred on [The Hague](30269.md)) and [Delfland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden-Delfland) (centred on [Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft)) in the north, and the [Drechtsteden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drechtsteden) to the south-east, this conurbation in turn forms the [South Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidvleugel) of the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad) conurbation, which spans across South Holland, North Holland and Utrecht.

Other regions in South Holland include [Alblasserwaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alblasserwaard), [Gouwestreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouwestreek) ("The [Gouwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouwe) Area"), [Hoeksche Waard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoeksche_Waard), [Krimpenerwaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krimpenerwaard), [Rijnland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijnland) ("Rhineland"), [Rijnstreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijnstreek) ("The [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) Area"), [Vijfheerenlanden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijfheerenlanden) and [Westland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_\(region\),_Netherlands) (roughly including [Hook of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_of_Holland) and the municipalities of [Westland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_\(region\),_Netherlands) and [Midden-Delfland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden-Delfland)).

### Climate

Like the rest of the Netherlands, South Holland experiences a temperate [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), a Cfb-class according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). The climate is influenced by the North Sea and the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), which results in relatively cooler summers and milder winters. Rain showers are common in the fall and winter seasons. Because of the flat surface, wind storms are also not uncommon in the winter. Although rain occurs all year long, the first six months of the year are relatively dry. Eastern winds can result in temporary continental climate conditions with relatively warm and dry summers and cold and stormy winters. The temperature varies between the 2 °C and 6 °C in the winter and 17 °C and 20 °C in the summer.

### Nature

In 1934 [Zuid-Hollands Landschap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuid-Hollands_Landschap) was founded as the nature conservation organisation in the province.[^cite_note-10] As of 2023 South Holland is covered for 12.7% in half-natural ecosystems.[^cite_note-11]

## Economy

The [Gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the region was 163.8 billion € in 2018. This contributes to the gross domestic product of the Netherlands by 21.2%. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 38,700 € or 128% of the EU27 average in the same year.[^cite_note-12] South Holland's labour force amounts to 1,661,000 people; 47.1% of the total population. The unemployment rate is 6.1% in 2013.[^cite_note-13]

The main sectors of economical activity in this province are among others:

- Horticulture is an important sector in South Holland. The province is home to around 2550 glasshouse companies, approximately half of the Netherlands' total.

  [^cite_note-14]

  &#x20;Specifically the [Westland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_\(region\),_Netherlands) is well known for its intensive horticulture. The glasshouses in this region cover an area of 2,750 hectares (10.6 sq mi),

  [^cite_note-15]

  &#x20;making it the world's largest contiguous greenhouse area.

  [^cite_note-16]

* [Flower bulb cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flower_bulb_cultivation_in_the_Netherlands);

* [Port of Rotterdam](4410985.md);

* [Petrochemical](67728.md) industry, particularly near Rotterdam neighbourhood [Pernis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernis,_Netherlands); (Shell's refinery there is the largest oil refinery in Europe, and one of the largest in the world);

* The service-oriented economy of The Hague, where many jobs are offered by the national government (of which the main administrative parts are settled in this town), international institutions and headquarters of several large international businesses;

* Tourism-related activities (among others in historic places like [Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft), and several seaside resorts);

* [Commercial fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_fishing); main South Holland fishing places are [Katwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katwijk) and [Scheveningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheveningen);

* [Stockbreeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry), of which in this province [dairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy) industry is the main sector.

## Religion

Religion in South Holland (2015)

[^cite_note-religieus-17]

1. Not religious (52.2%)

2. [Protestant Church in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_the_Netherlands) (18.2%)

3. [Catholicism](606848.md) (14.5%)

4. Islam (7.50%)

5. Hinduism (1.80%)

6. Other (5.70%)

In 2015, 18.2% of the population belonged to the [Protestant Church in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_the_Netherlands) while 16.5% were [Roman Catholic](606848.md), 7.5% [Muslim](6037917.md), 1.8% [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), and 5.7% belonged to other churches or faiths. Over half (50.2%) of the population identified as non-religious.

## Notable residents

Among the well-known scientists originating from South Holland are:

- [Christiaan Huygens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Huygens) (1629–1695), mathematician, physicist, astronomer and inventor

- [Antonie van Leeuwenhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonie_van_Leeuwenhoek) (1632–1723), father of [microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiology) and developer of the [microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope)

- [Desiderius Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desiderius_Erasmus) (1466–1536), philosopher and humanist

Several prominent painters originate from this province, including:

- [Rembrandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembrandt) (Leiden)

- [Jan Steen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Steen) (Leiden)

- [Johannes Vermeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Vermeer) (Delft)
