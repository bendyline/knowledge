**Luzon** ([/luːˈzɒn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*loo-ZON*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Tagalog: [\[luˈson\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)) is the largest and most populous [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Philippines) in the [Philippines](23440.md). Located in the northern portion of the [Philippine archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_Philippines), it is the economic and political center of the nation. The country's capital city, [Manila](184334.md), as well as [Quezon City](284223.md), the country's most populous city, are located on the island of Luzon. Luzon has a population of 64.3 million as of 2024;[^cite_note-PSA20–d-2] this constitutes more than half of the total population of the Philippines.[^cite_note-3] Luzon is the [4th most populous island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_population) in the world. It is the [15th largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) in the world by area of land.

*Luzon* may also refer to one of the three primary [island groups in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_groups_of_the_Philippines). In this usage, it includes the Luzon [Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland), the [Batanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batanes) and [Babuyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babuyan_Islands) groups of islands to the north, [Polillo Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polillo_Islands) to the east, and the outlying islands of [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes), [Marinduque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinduque) and [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro), among others, to the south.[^cite_note-unique-4] The islands and provinces of [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate), [Palawan](240395.md) and [Romblon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romblon) are also included, although these three are sometimes grouped with another of the island groups, the [Visayas](72418.md).

## Etymology

The name *Luzon* is thought to derive from ᜎᜓᜐᜓᜅ᜔ *lusong*, a [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) word referring to a particular kind of large wooden [mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle) used in dehusking [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_production_in_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Roberts1-6] A 2008 research paper by Eulito Bautista and Evelyn Javier provides an image of a lusong, explaining:

> Traditional milling was accomplished in the 1900s by pounding the palay with a wooden pestle in a stone or wooden mortar called lusong. The first pounding takes off the hull and further pounding removes the bran but also breaks most grains. Further winnowing with a bamboo tray (bilao) separates the hull from the rice grains. This traditional hand-pounding chore, although very laborious and resulted in a lot of broken rice, required two to three skilled men and women to work harmoniously and was actually a form of socializing among young folks in the villages.[^cite_note-EulitoandJavier2008-7]

In old Latin, Italian, and Portuguese maps, the island is often called *Luçonia* or *Luconia*.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

*Luções,* [\[luˈsõjʃ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese) (also *[Luzones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzones)* in [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Philippines)) was a [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym)[^cite_note-Alfonso2016-16] used by [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) sailors in Malaysia[^cite_note-Scott1994-17] during the early 1500s, referring to the [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people) and [Tagalog people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people) who lived in [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay), which was then called *[Lusong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Luzon#History_of_Luzon_during_the_Classical_Period)* ([Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language): *Lusung*, [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Luçon*), from which Luzon was also derived.[^cite_note-Pires-18][^cite_note-Lach-19][^cite_note-Reid-20][^cite_note-Scott1994-17] The term was also used for Tagalog settlers in [Southern Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tagalog) region, where they created intensive contact with the Kapampangans.[^cite_note-Zorc-21] Eventually, the term "*Luzones*" would refer to the settlers of Luzon island, and later on, would be exclusive to the peoples of [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon).

## History

### Before European colonization

Further information: [History of Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Luzon), [Tondo (historical polity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_\(historical_polity\)), [Cainta (historical polity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta_\(historical_polity\)), [Namayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan), [Maynila (historical polity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynila_\(historical_polity\)), [Ma-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma-i), and [Pangasinan (historical polity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_\(historical_polity\))

*[Homo luzonensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_luzonensis)* fossils, found in [Callao Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_Cave) on Luzon, represent a newly identified extinct archaic human species from at least 50,000 to 67,000 years ago.[^cite_note-22]

Before 1000 CE, the [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people), and [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people) peoples of south and central Luzon had established several major coastal [polities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity), notably [Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Maynila), [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_\(historical_polity\)) and [Namayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan). The oldest known Philippine document, written in 900, is the [Laguna Copperplate Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Copperplate_Inscription), which names places in and around [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay) and also mentions [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan), a place in Indonesia.[^cite_note-23] These coastal Philippine kingdoms were [thalassocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy), based on trade with neighboring Asian political entities and structured by leases between chiefs or lords (*[Datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu)*) and paramount lords (*[Lakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakan)*) or [Rajahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah), by whom tributes were extracted and taxes were levied. These kingdoms were under the competing influence of Hinduism, Animism, or Islam. Before that, from 2500 - 2000 BCE, Kapampangans along with [Sambal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people) and [Hatang Kayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatang_Kayi_language) settled south Luzon; subsequently, from 1200 - 1000 BCE, the migrating Tagalog settlers from [eastern Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Visayas) or [northeast Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga) stayed in south Luzon and they made contact with the Kapampangans, Sambal people and the Hatang Kayi, of which contact with the Kapampangans was most intensive;[^cite_note-Zorc-21] after this, the original settlers moved northward: Kapampangans moved to modern Tondo, Navotas, and Central Luzon (modern Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, Aurora, Pampanga, south Tarlac, and east Bataan),[^cite_note-Tantingco:_The_Kapampangan_in_Us-24][^cite_note-What_is_the_Kapampangan_Region-25][^cite_note-language-and-society_Ariel-49-61-26][^cite_note-mega-Pampanga-27] and Sambals to the modern province of Zambales,[^cite_note-28] in turn, displacing the Aetas.

There was also a Buddhist polity known as [Ma-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma-i) or Maidh, described in Chinese and [Bruneian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) records in the 10th century, although its location is still unknown and scholars are divided on whether it is in modern-day [Bay, Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay,_Laguna) or [Bulalacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulalacao), [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

According to sources at the time, the trade in large native [*Ruson-tsukuri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_tea_ceremony_equipment#Shimamono) (literally *Luzon-made*, Japanese:[呂](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%91%82) [宋](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%AE%8B) [製](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%A3%BD)) clay jars used for storing [green tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_tea) and [rice wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_wine) with Japan flourished in the 12th century, and local [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people) and [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people) potters had marked each jar with [Baybayin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baybayin) letters denoting the particular urn used and the kiln the jars were manufactured in. Certain [kilns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiln) were renowned over others; prices depended on the reputation of the kiln.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Of this flourishing trade, the *Burnay* jars of [Ilocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos) are the only large clay jar manufactured in Luzon today with origins from this time.

In the early 1300s the Chinese annals, *Nanhai zhi*, reported that Hindu Brunei invaded or administered [Sarawak](28258.md) and [Sabah](28678.md) as well as the Philippine kingdoms of [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Butuan), [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu), and in Luzon: [Ma-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma-i) (Mindoro) and Malilu 麻裏蘆 (present-day [Manila](184334.md)); [Shahuchong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanmalan) 沙胡重 (present-day Siocon or [Zamboanga](25167165.md)), Yachen 啞陳 [Oton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oton) (Part of the [Madja-as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madja-as) Kedatuan), and 文杜陵 Wenduling (present-day [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao)),[^cite_note-33] which would regain their independence at a later date.[^cite_note-34]

&#x20;In 1405, the [Yongle Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor) appointed a Chinese governor of Luzon, Ko Ch'a-lao, during [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He)'s [voyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_voyages).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHo2009\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidEwnzBiM0LmACpgPA33_33\]-35][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKarnow2010\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidVbwogbQ3l8UCpgPT84_84\]-36] China also had vassals among the leaders in the archipelago.[^cite_note-37] China attained ascendancy in trade with the area in Yongle's reign.[^cite_note-38]



*Bangkang pinawa*, an ancient Philippine [mortar and pestle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle)

Afterwards, some parts of Luzon were [Islamized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) when the former Majapahit province of Poni broke free, converted to [Islam](6037917.md), and imported [Sharif Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_Ali), a prince from [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharifate_of_Mecca) who became the Sultan of [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Empire), a nation that then expanded its realms from Borneo to the Philippines and set up the [Kingdom of Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Maynila) as its puppet-state.[^cite_note-39] The invasion of Brunei spread Chinese royal descent like [Ong Sum Ping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Sum_Ping)'s kin and Arab dynasties too into the Philippines like the clan of Sultan [Sharif Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_Ali). However, other Luzon kingdoms resisted Islam, like [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_\(historical_polity\)). It had remained a tributary state of China and was a largely [Sinified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinicization) kingdom, which maintained trade with Japan.[^cite_note-40] The [Polity of Cainta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta_\(historical_polity\)) also existed as a fortified city-state, armed with walls and cannons. As written in the book of Dong-Xiyang kao 東西洋考, the Chinese [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) recorded that there was a "Kingdom of Luzon" that existed over the current island of Luzon.[^cite_note-MingDynasty-41]

### Interactions with the Portuguese

The [Portuguese](23033.md) were the first European explorers who recorded it in their charts as *Luçonia* or *Luçon*, calling the inhabitants *[Luções](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%A7%C3%B5es)*.[^cite_note-42] [Edmund Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Roberts_\(diplomat\)), who visited Luzon in the early 19th century, wrote that Luzon was "discovered" in 1521.[^cite_note-Roberts1-6] The Portuguese encountered Luzones, people from Luzon, as far away as the [Malabar Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_Coast) in the Western part of India at [South Asia](21566765.md) serving as naval and mercenary muscle for the Portuguese armada in the wars against local Muslim kingdoms.[^cite_note-Luzam-43]

Portuguese and Spanish accounts from the early[^cite_note-Pigafetta1524b-44] to mid 1500s state that the [Kingdom of Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Maynila) was the same as the Kingdom of Luzon[^cite_note-pttoponymy-45] that was mentioned in Ming Dynasty Records[^cite_note-MingDynasty-41] (from the Tagalog or Malay name *Lusong* and Kapampangan name *Lusung*), and whose citizens had been called "*Luções*".[^cite_note-Pigafetta1524b-44]

Many people from Luzon were employed within Portuguese Malacca. For example, the [spice magnate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade) [Regimo de Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regimo_de_Raja), based in Malacca, was highly influential and was appointed as *Temenggong* (Sea Lord)—a governor and chief general responsible for overseeing of maritime trade—by the Portuguese. As *Temenggong*, de Raja was also the head of an [armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) which traded and protected commerce in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), the [South China Sea](74209.md),[^cite_note-46] and the [medieval maritime principalities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(900%E2%80%931521\)).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] His father and wife carried on his maritime trading business after his death. Another important Malacca trader was Curia de Raja who also hailed from Luzon. The "surname" of "de Raja" or "diraja" could indicate that Regimo and Curia, and their families, were of noble or royal descent as the term is an abbreviation of Sanskrit *adiraja*.[^cite_note-49]

[Fernão Mendes Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_Mendes_Pinto) noted that a number of Luções in the Islamic fleets went to battle with the Portuguese in the Philippines during the 16th century. The Sultan of Aceh gave one of them (Sapetu Diraja) the task of holding Aru (northeast Sumatra) in 1540. Pinto also says one was named leader of the Malays remaining in the Moluccas Islands after the Portuguese conquest in 1511.[^cite_note-Pinto-50] [Antonio Pigafetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Pigafetta) notes that one of them was in command of the Brunei fleet in 1521.[^cite_note-Pigafetta-51] However, the Luções did not only fight on the side of the Muslims. Pinto says they were also apparently among the natives of the Philippines who fought the Muslims in 1538.[^cite_note-Pinto-50]

On [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia), Lusung/Luções warriors aided the Burmese king in his invasion of Siam in 1547. At the same time, Lusong warriors fought alongside the Siamese king and faced the same elephant army of the Burmese king in the defence of the Siamese capital at Ayutthaya.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPigafetta1969195-52] The Luções were so successful in [Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom) that the Thai king rewarded them by having them [ennobled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennobled) and granted them land.[^cite_note-53] Luções military and trade activity reached as far as [Sri Lanka](26750.md) in [South Asia](21566765.md) where Lungshanoid pottery made in Luzon were discovered in burials.[^cite_note-54] Meanwhile in the nearby [Sultanate of Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Aceh) the Luções fighting men so impressed the Sultan, that they were assigned to become the Sultan's royal guard [^cite_note-55] and to be assigned as the Sultan's royal guard, is proof of Luçoes men's physical strength, martial prowess, and [masculine attractiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masculine_beauty_ideal); as during that time period, among medieval kingdoms, that office was delegated only to the most strong, intelligent, handsome, attractive, virile, aristocratic, and combat-worthy, of warriors.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

Scholars have thus suggested that they could be mercenaries valued by all sides.[^cite_note-Pires-18][^cite_note-Lach-19][^cite_note-Reid-20]

In 1569, a Spanish expedition dispatched by [Miguel Lopez de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi) led by Luis Enriquez de Guzman and Augustinian friar Alonso Jimenez first set foot in Albay. They arrived on the coastal settlement called [Ibalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibalon) in present-day [Magallanes, Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magallanes,_Sorsogon) after exploring the islands of [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate_Island), [Ticao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticao_Island) and [Burias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burias_\(island\)) and proceeded inland as far as present-day [Camalig, Albay](2063412.md).[^cite_note-Newson-58][^cite_note-59] The [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) arrival in the 16th century saw the incorporation of the Luções people and the breaking up of their kingdoms and the establishment of the [*Las Islas Filipinas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies) with its capital [Cebu](160278.md), which was moved to [Manila](184334.md) following the defeat of the local [Rajah Sulayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Sulayman) in 1570. [Martín de Goiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Goiti), having been dispatched by Legazpi to Luzon, conquered [Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynila_\(historical_polity\)). Legazpi followed with a larger fleet comprising both Spanish and a majority [Visayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans) force,[^cite_note-halili2004-60]<sup>: [79-80](https://books.google.com/books?id=gUt5v8ET4QYC\&pg=PA79) </sup> taking a month to bring these forces to bear due to slow speed of local ships.[^cite_note-61] This large force caused the surrender of neighboring [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_\(historical_polity\)). An attempt by some local leaders, known as the [Tondo Conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_Conspiracy), to defeat the Spanish was repelled. Soon, the Spanish and Mexican conquistadors from Mexico were given [encomiendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Encomederos_and_Nobilities) over several parts of Luzon. They are the following: Antonio de Carvajal, held an encomienda in early Luzon settlements; no exact figures preserved.[^cite_note-62] Juan de Morón, early encomendero; tributary numbers not specified.[^cite_note-63] Francisco de Herrera, received an encomienda in Luzon; details incomplete.[^cite_note-64] and Pedro de Chaves, Luzon Encomienda holder; tribute count unknown.[^cite_note-65]



Depiction of the Luzon people in 1700s from the Chinese book *[Huang Qing Zhigong Tu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huang_Qing_Zhigong_Tu)*. The Chinese called them Lu Song whom they recognized as a prosperous and powerful "kingdom" under the Spanish Empire.

Legazpi renamed Maynila *Nueva Castilla*, and declared it the capital of the Philippines,[^cite_note-halili2004-60]<sup>: [80](https://books.google.com/books?id=gUt5v8ET4QYC\&pg=PA80) </sup> and thus of the rest of the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies),[^cite_note-66] which also encompassed Spanish territories in [Asia](689.md) and the [Pacific](23070.md).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-Andrade-68] Legazpi became the country's first governor-general. Under Spain, Luzon also came to be known as the ***Nueva Castilla*** or the **New Castile**. The population of Luzon at the time of the first Spanish missions is estimated as between 1 and 1.5 million, overall density being low.[^cite_note-69] Moros from western Mindanao and the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago) also raided the coastal Christian areas of Luzon and the Visayas. Settlers had to fight off the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) pirates (who lay siege to Manila, the most famous of which was [Limahong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limahong) in 1573).



Man of the Island of Luzon 1820 by John Crawfurd

After the successful expedition and the exploration of the North, Juan de Salcedo founded "Villa Fernandina de Vigan" in honor of King Philip II's son, Prince Ferdinand, who died at the age of four. From Vigan, Salcedo rounded the tip of Luzón and proceeded to pacify [Camarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarines), [Albay](253167.md), and [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes). As a reward for his services to the [King of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Spain), Salcedo was awarded the [old province of Ilocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos), which consisted of the modern provinces of [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte), Ilocos Sur, [Abra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abra_\(province\)), [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union) and part of [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province) as his [hacienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda) (estate), and was accorded the title of *Justicia Mayor de esta Provincia de Ylocos* (Province Mayor of Ilocos).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

In Spanish times, Luzon became the focal point for trade between the Americas and Asia. The [Manila Galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Galleons) constructed in the [Bicol region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_region) brought [silver](27119.md) mined from [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru) and [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) to Manila. The silver was used to purchase Asian commercial goods like Chinese [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk), Indian [gems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gems) and Indonesian [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spices), which were then exported back to the Americas. The Chinese valued Luzon so much, in that when talking about Spain and the Spanish-Americas, they preferred to call it as "Dao Lusong" (Greater Luzon) while the original Luzon was referred to as "Xiao (Small) Lusong" to refer to not only Luzon but the whole Philippines.[^cite_note-72]



La Independencia staff from [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_\(province\))

Luzon also became a focal point for global migration. The walled city of [Intramuros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros) was initially founded by 1200 Spanish families.[^cite_note-73] The nearby district of [Binondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binondo) became the center of business and transformed into the world's oldest [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown).[^cite_note-74] There was also a smaller district [reserved for Japanese migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_diaspora) in [Dilao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilao). [Cavite City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite_City) also served as the main port for Luzon and [many Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration_from_Mexico) soldiers and sailors were stationed in the naval garrisons there.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] When the Spanish evacuated from [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ternate), Indonesia; they settled the [Papuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papuan_languages) refugees in [Ternate, Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate,_Cavite) which was named after their evacuated homeland. After the short [British Occupation of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Occupation_of_Manila), the Indian [Sepoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy) soldiers that mutinied against their British commanders and joined the Spanish, then settled in [Cainta, Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta,_Rizal).

Newcomers who were impoverished Mexicans and peninsulares were accused of undermining the submission of the natives. In Rafael Bernal's book: *Mexico En Filipinas*, Luzon island was considered full of Mexican descendants.[^cite_note-77] In 1774, authorities from Bulacan, Tondo, Laguna Bay, and other areas surrounding Manila reported with consternation that discharged soldiers and deserters (from Mexico, Spain and Peru) were providing Indios military training for the weapons that had been disseminated all over the territory during the British war.[^cite_note-78] There was also continuous immigration of [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils) and [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis) into the rural areas of Luzon: Spanish administrators, native nobles, and Chinese businessmen imported them as [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Asia) during this period.[^cite_note-79]

In the 1600s, Fr. Joaqin Martinez de Zuñiga, conducted a census of the Archdiocese of Manila which held most of Luzon under its spiritual care, and it had the following number of tributes, with each tribute representing a family of 6–7, and he reported 90,243 native Filipino tributes;[^cite_note-Estadismo1-80]<sup>: 539 </sup> 10,512 Chinese (Sangley) and mixed Chinese Filipino mestizo tributes;[^cite_note-Estadismo1-80]<sup>: 537 </sup> and 10,517 mixed [Spanish Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos) mestizo tributes.[^cite_note-Estadismo1-80]<sup>: 539 </sup> Pure Spaniards are not counted as they are exempt from tribute. Out of these, Fr. Joaqin Martinez de Zuñiga estimated a total population count exceeding half a million souls.[^cite_note-Estadismo1-80]<sup>: 537 </sup>

People from the Philippines, primarily from Luzon, were recruited by [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) (then in alliance with [Spain](26667.md)), first to defend Indo-Chinese [converts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity) to [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Asia) being persecuted by their native governments. Eventually, Filipino mercenaries helped the French [conquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina) Vietnam and Laos and to re-establish Cambodia as a French Protectorate. This process culminated in the establishment of [French Cochinchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cochinchina), centered in [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon).[^cite_note-nigelgooding.co.uk-81]

A great number of infrastructure projects were undertaken during the 19th century that put the Philippine economy and standard of living ahead of most of its Asian neighbors and even many European countries at that time. Among them were a [railway system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways) for Luzon, a tramcar network for Manila, and Asia's first steel suspension bridge Puente Claveria, later called [Puente Colgante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puente_Colgante_\(Manila\)).[^cite_note-Borja-82] In the meantime, Luzonians and Filipinos living abroad; were active in the [Mexican War of Independence, Argentine War of Independence, and the War of 1812 between the United States and the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_military_activities_in_the_Americas), at the Americas, while at the same time had fought in the [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Townsend_Ward) in China.

### After Spanish colonization

After many years of Spanish occupation and resistance to reform, the [Andres Novales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andres_Novales) uprising occurred and it was inspired by the [Latin American Wars of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Wars_of_Independence). Novales' uprising was primarily supported by Mexicans living in the Philippines[^cite_note-83] as well as immigrant Latinos from the now independent nations of [Colombia](5222.md), [Venezuela](32374.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Argentina](18951905.md) and [Costa Rica](5551.md).[^cite_note-84] Although the uprising failed it inspired the [Cavite Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite_Mutiny), the suppression of which, lead to the martyrdoms of Priests, [Gomburza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomburza) and the subsequent execution of the reformist and hero, [Jose Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Rizal). Reeling against this, the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) against Spain erupted in Cavite and spread all throughout Luzon and the Philippines. Consequently, the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) was established in [Malolos, Bulacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos,_Bulacan). In the meantime, [Spain](26667.md) sold the Philippines to the United States and the First Philippine Republic resisted the [United States](3434750.md) in the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War) which the Republic's forces lost due to its diplomatic isolation (no foreign nation recognized the First Republic) as well as due to the numerical superiority of the [American military](32212.md).[^cite_note-85] The Americans then set up the cool mountain city of [Baguio](162480.md) as a summer retreat for its officials. The Americans also rebuilt the capital, Manila, and established American military bases in [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo) and [Angeles](842728.md) cities mainly [Clark Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Airbase) and [Subic Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic_Naval_Base).[^cite_note-86]

During the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War), the Philippines were considered to be of great strategic importance because their capture by Japan would pose a significant threat to the US. As a result, 135,000 troops and 227 aircraft were stationed in the Philippines by October 1941. Luzon was captured by [Imperial Japanese forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) in 1942 during their [campaign to capture the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_\(1941%E2%80%931942\)). [General](2965165.md) [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur)—who was in charge of the defense of the Philippines at the time—was ordered to Australia, and the remaining US forces retreated to the [Bataan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Peninsula).[^cite_note-2ww-87]

A few months after this, MacArthur expressed his belief that an attempt to recapture the Philippines was necessary. The US Pacific Commander [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [Chester Nimitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_Nimitz) and [Chief of Naval Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations) Admiral [Ernest King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_King) both opposed this idea, arguing that it must wait until victory was certain. MacArthur had to wait two years for his wish; it was 1944 before a [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_Campaign_\(1944%E2%80%9345\)) to recapture the Philippines was launched. The island of [Leyte](194750.md) was the first objective of the campaign, which was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte) by the end of December 1944. This was followed by the [attack on Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mindoro) and later, Luzon.[^cite_note-2ww-87]

Further information: [Battle of Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Luzon)



US Navy ships under attack while entering [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf), January 1945

The end of the World War necessitated [decolonization](302084.md) due to rising nationalist movements across the world's many colonies. Subsequently, the Philippines gained independence from the United States. Luzon then arose to become the most developed island in the Philippines. However, the lingering poverty and inequality caused by the long dictatorship of US-supported dictator, [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), gave rise to the Philippine diaspora and many people from Luzon have migrated elsewhere and had established large overseas communities; mainly in the United States, Hong Kong, Singapore and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). Eventually, the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution) led by [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) and [Cardinal Jaime Sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Sin), removed Marcos and his cronies from power and they fled to [Hawaii](13270.md) where the US granted them asylum. The following administrations are subsequently managing the political and economic recovery of the Philippines with the particular aim of spreading development outside of Luzon and into the more isolated provinces of the [Visayas](72418.md) and [Mindanao](20546.md). During the administration of Ferdinand Marcos' son, [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md), Luzon became a destination of American and Japanese enterprises, plus investments from other [G7 Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G7_Nations), as well as other members of the [Pax Silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Silica) Initiative, it being the location of the [Luzon Economic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_Economic_Corridor).[^cite_note-88]

## Geography

Further information: [Geography of Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Philippines#Luzon)



Satellite image of Luzon

Luzon island alone has an area of 109,964.9 square kilometres (42,457.7 sq mi),[^cite_note-unep-1] making it the [world's 15th largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area). It is bordered on the west by the [South China Sea](74209.md) (*Luzon Sea* in Philippine territorial waters), on the east by the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea), and on the north by the [Luzon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_Strait) containing the [Babuyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babuyan_Islands) Channel and [Balintang Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balintang_Channel). The [mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland) is roughly rectangular in shape and has the long [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula) protruding to the southeast.

Luzon is roughly divided into four sections; Northern Luzon, Central Luzon, Southern Luzon, and Metro Manila.

### Physical

#### Northern Luzon



Northern Luzon.

Northern Luzon consists of 3 clusters in three regions, the Ilocos Region, the Cordillera Administrative Region and the Cagayan Valley.

The northwestern portion of the island, which encompasses most of the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region), is characterized by a flat terrain extending east from the [coastline](5236.md) toward the [Cordillera Central mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central,_Luzon).

The Cordillera mountain range, which feature the island's north-central section, is covered in a mixture of [tropical pine forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_tropical_pine_forests) and [montane rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_rainforest), and is the site of the island's highest mountain, [Mount Pulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pulag), rising at 2,922 metres. The range provides the upland headwaters of the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River), which stretches from the slopes of [Mount Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Data), and meanders along the southern Cordillera mountains before reaching the plains of [Pangasinan](1691800.md).

The northeastern section of Luzon is generally mountainous, with the [Sierra Madre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_\(Philippines\)), the longest mountain range in the country, abruptly rising a few miles from the coastline. Located in between the Sierra Madre and the Cordillera Central mountain ranges is the large [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley). This region, which is known for being the second largest producer of rice and the country's top corn-producer, serves as the [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) for the [Cagayan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_River), the longest in the Philippines.

Along the southern limits of the Cordillera Central lies the lesser-known [Caraballo Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraballo_Mountains). These mountains form a link between the Cordillera Central and the Sierra Madre mountain ranges, separating the Cagayan Valley from the [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) plains.[^cite_note-TriumphPhil-90]

#### Central Luzon



The [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) plain with [Mount Arayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Arayat) in the background

The central section of Luzon is characterized by a flat terrain, known as the Central Luzon [plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain), the largest in the island in terms of land area. The plain, approximately 11,000 square kilometres (4,200 sq mi) in size, is the country's largest producer of rice, and is irrigated by two major rivers; the [Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_River) to the north, and the [Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga_River) to the south. In the middle of the plain rises the solitary [Mount Arayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Arayat).

The western coasts of Central Luzon are typically flat extending east from the coastline to the [Zambales Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales_Mountains), the site of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo), made famous because of its enormous [1991 eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo). These mountains extend to the sea in the north, forming [Lingayen Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf), and to the south, forming the [Bataan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Peninsula). The peninsula encloses [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay), a natural [harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor) considered to be one of the best natural ports in East Asia, due to its size and strategic geographical location.

The [Sierra Madre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_\(Philippines\)) mountain range continues to stretch across the western section of Central Luzon, snaking southwards into the [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula).

Central Luzon is located between the regions of Northern Luzon, Southern Luzon and Metro Manila. These are the provinces of: Aurora, Bataan, Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, Pampanga, Tarlac and Zambales. The province of Aurora in the middle of the year 2002 was included in the Southern Tagalog Region. The regional center of Central Luzon is [San Fernando, Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga).

Southern Luzon is dominated by [Laguna de Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_Bay) ([Old Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Spanish), "*Lake of [Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay,_Laguna) town*"), the largest [lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake) in the country. The 949-square-kilometre (366 sq mi) lake is drained into [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay) by the [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River), one of the most important rivers in the country due to its historical significance and because it runs through the center of [Metro Manila](72340.md).

Located 20 kilometres (12 mi) southwest of Laguna de Bay is [Taal Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taal_Lake), a [crater lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_crater_lake) containing the [Taal Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taal_Volcano), the smallest in the country. The environs of the lake form the [upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland) [Tagaytay Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagaytay_Ridge), which was once part of a massive prehistoric volcano that covered the southern portion of the province of [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite) and the whole of [Batangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batangas) province.

South of Laguna Lake are two [solitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_prominence) mountains, [Mount Makiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Makiling) in [Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_\(province\)) and Batangas provinces, and [Mount Banahaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Banahaw), the highest in the region of [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon).

Mainland Southern Tagalog is the current region of Calabarzon which consists of Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal and Quezon, and Mimaropa is Region IV-B which is considered the Southwestern Tagalog Region is in the outland.&#x20;

The southeastern portion of Luzon is dominated by the [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula), a mountainous and narrow region extending approximately 150 kilometres (93 mi) southeast from the [Tayabas Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tayabas_Isthmus) in [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_\(province\)) province to the [San Bernardino Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_Strait) along the coasts of [Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon_\(province\)). The area is home to several [volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano), the most famous of which is the 2,460-metre (8,070 ft) high symmetrically-shaped [Mayon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon_Volcano) in [Albay](253167.md) province. The [Sierra Madre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_\(Philippines\)) range has its southern limits at Quezon province. [Ultra-prominent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_prominence) mountains dot the landscape, which include [Mount Isarog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Isarog) and [Mount Iriga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Iriga) in [Camarines Sur](255883.md), and [Mount Bulusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Bulusan) in [Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon).

The [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula)'s coastline features several smaller peninsulas, [gulfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_gulfs) and [bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay), which include [Lamon Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamon_Bay), [San Miguel Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_Bay), [Lagonoy Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagonoy_Gulf), [Ragay Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragay_Gulf), and [Sorsogon Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon_Bay).

It consists of Bicolandia or Region 5 which is the last region on the island of Luzon.

#### Metro Manila

[Metro Manila](72340.md) is the urban center and [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government) of the Philippines, with the country's capital [Manila](184334.md) is part of the metropolitan area which also serves as its regional center. It consists of 16 cities and one town. Metro Manila was formerly a province or province that included the name Tondo. In 1898, it included the districts and barangays of each city area. The metropolitan area also carved [Valenzuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valenzuela,_Metro_Manila) from [Bulacan](252408.md) in [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) as well as some towns in the province of [Rizal](232458.md) of Southern Luzon.

### Tectonics

Main article: [Philippine Mobile Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Mobile_Belt)



[Lake Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Pinatubo) in [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales)

Luzon is part of the [Philippine Mobile Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Mobile_Belt), a fast deforming plate boundary zone (Gervasio, 1967) hemmed in between two opposing subduction zones, the west-dipping [Philippine Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Trench)-[East Luzon Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Luzon_Trench) subduction zone, and the east-dipping north–south trending [Manila Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Trench)-[Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_\(island\)) Trench-[Cotabato Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_Trench).[^cite_note-95] The [Philippine Sea Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea_Plate) subducts under eastern Luzon along the East Luzon Trench and the Philippine Trench, while the [South China Sea](74209.md) basin, part of the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate), subducts under western Luzon along the Manila Trench.

The North-Southeastern trending braided left-lateral strike-slip [Philippine Fault System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Fault_System) traverses Luzon, from [Quezon province](284223.md) and [Bicol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region) to the northwestern part of the island. This fault system takes up part of the motion due to the subducting plates and produces large earthquakes. Southwest of Luzon is a collision zone where the Palawan micro-block collides with SW Luzon, producing a highly seismic zone near [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro) island. Southwest Luzon is characterized by a highly volcanic zone, called the Macolod Corridor, a region of crustal thinning and spreading.

Using geologic and structural data, seven principal blocks were identified in Luzon in 1989: the Sierra Madre Oriental, Angat, [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales), [Central Cordillera of Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Cordillera_of_Luzon), Bicol, and [Catanduanes Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes_Island) blocks.[^cite_note-96] Using seismic and geodetic data, Luzon was modeled by Galgana et al. (2007) as a series of six micro blocks or micro plates (separated by subduction zones and intra-arc faults), all translating and rotating in different directions, with maximum velocities \~100 mm/yr NW with respect to Sundaland/Eurasia.

### Ethnic groups

Further information: [Ethnic groups of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_of_the_Philippines)



An [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_people) warrior with some of his trophies, Cordillera Mountains, c. 1912

Seven major [Philippine](2298105.md) [ethnolinguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnolinguistics) groups predominate Luzon. [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) and [Pangasinenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people) dominate northern Luzon, particularly in the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region) to parts of the [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley), while [Kapampangans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people), [Tagalogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people), Ilocanos, Pangasinans and [Sambals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people) populate [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon). [Tagalogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people) dominate the [National Capital Region](72340.md), [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon) and the island provinces of [Marinduque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinduque), [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro) and extending to parts of [Palawan](240395.md) and northern [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula), while [Bicolanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicolano_people) populate the [Bicol peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region). [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans), such as [Masbateños](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate%C3%B1o_people), [Romblomanons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romblomanon_people), [Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_people) [Sorsogonons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Sorsogon_language), [Cuyunons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyunon_people), mainly populate in the southern [Bicol peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region) and island provinces of [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate), [Romblon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romblon), and [Palawan](240395.md).

Other ethnic groups lesser in population include the [Aetas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeta) of [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) and [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan), the [Ibanags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibanag_people) of [Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_province) and [Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabela_province), the [Gaddang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddang_people) of [Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Vizcaya), the [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot) of the [Cordilleras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)), the [Bugkalots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugkalot_people) of Nueva Vizcaya, [Quirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirino), [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) and [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)), [Umiray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umiray_Dumaget_language) and Tagabulós of Aurora and [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon), [Remontado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatang_Kayi_language) of Quezon, and the [Mangyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangyan) of [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro).

Due to historical centuries-old migrations, populations of ethnic [Chinese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino), [Spanish Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino), [Japanese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines), [Indian Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipino), and Muslim [Moros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) from [Mindanao](20546.md) have also been present in urban areas. Historical [mixed mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_mestizo) populations, particularly [Chinese mestizos (*mestizo de Sangley*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley) and [Spanish mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino), and more recent mixed mestizos of [Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_of_American_descent), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_of_Japanese_descent), [Koreans](3440060.md), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_of_Indian_descent) (mostly [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_people)), and [Arabs](2185.md) are also occasionally present.

The historical [Sangley Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley) and their [pure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) and [mixed-mestizo descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley) are spread all across Luzon of several generations across the centuries.&#x20;

According to old Spanish censuses, around 1/3rd of the population of Luzon are [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos) admixed with either or both [Southern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) (mostly from [Manila](72340.md) to [Pampanga](255602.md)) and/or [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic) ([Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicans_in_the_Philippines)) descent.[^cite_note-103]

Most ex-patriates from the United States have settled in Central Luzon's highly urbanized cities of [Angeles](842728.md) and [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo) due to the former presence of the US air and naval bases ([Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) & [Subic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Base_Subic_Bay)) there.

Most of the Koreans and Japanese which have moved to Luzon have mainly settled in places like the highly urbanized city [Baguio (in the Cordillera Administrative Region)](162480.md), [Subic (in Zambales)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic,_Zambales), and Koreatown in [Angeles City](842728.md).

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Philippines)



Dominant languages per administrative region



Major languages per province, showing their subdivisions.

Almost all of the languages of Luzon belong to the [Philippine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_languages) group of the [Malayo-Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian_languages) branch of the [Austronesian language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages). Major regional languages include: [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Central Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bikol), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), and [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language).

[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English) is spoken by many inhabitants. The use of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Philippines) as an [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) declined following the [American occupation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_occupation_of_the_Philippines). Almost inexistent among the general populace, Spanish is still used by the elderly of some families of great tradition (Rizal, Liboro...) and by upper and middle-class residents of Spanish blood.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Philippines)

Like most of the Philippines, the major religion in Luzon is [Christianity](8276776.md), with [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_the_Philippines) being the major [denomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination). Religions rooted in Christianity represented in Luzon include [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_Philippines), the [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church) (Aglipayans), the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (Mormons), and the [Iglesia Ni Cristo ("Church of Christ")](288343.md) religious movement.[^cite_note-104] Indigenous traditions and rituals, though rare, are also present.

There are also sizable communities of [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs), [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) (see [Hinduism in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_Philippines)), [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) (see [Buddhism in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Philippines)), and [Muslims](19541.md) (see [Islam in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines)) in [Metro Manila](72340.md) and in other areas (especially urban ones) due to the immigration of people from India (see [Indian Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipino)), Japan (see [Japanese in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines), Korea (see [Koreans in the Philippines](3440060.md)), and China (see [Chinese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipinos)), as well as other countries to the Philippines.&#x20;

The [Bangsamoro people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people), a [Muslim](19541.md) people of native Filipino descent ([Islam](6037917.md) was introduced to them from Borneo and Malaya in the 14th century) also have a presence on Luzon.[^cite_note-105]

## Economy

The economy of the island is centered in [Metro Manila](72340.md) with [Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati) serving as the main economic and financial hub. Major companies such as [Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayala_Corporation), [Jollibee Foods Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jollibee_Foods_Corporation), [SM Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_Investments), and [Metrobank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Bank_and_Trust_Company) are based in the business hubs of [Makati Central Business District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati_Central_Business_District), [Ortigas Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Center), and [Bonifacio Global City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio_Global_City). Industry is concentrated in and around the urban areas of Metro Manila while agriculture predominates in the other regions of the island producing crops such as rice, bananas, mangoes, coconuts, pineapple, and coffee.[^cite_note-106] Other sectors include livestock raising, tourism, mining, and fishing.
