**Energy development** is the field of activities focused on obtaining sources of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) from [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource). These activities include the production of [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel)-derived, [nuclear](22153.md), and [renewable](25784.md) sources of energy, and for the [recovery and reuse of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_heat) that would otherwise be wasted. [Energy conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) and [efficiency measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use) reduce the demand for energy development and can benefit society by improving [environmental issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues).

Societies use energy for [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication), [heating, ventilation, and air conditioning](57169.md), [illumination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighting), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), and [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport), for agricultural, commercial, domestic, and industrial purposes. Energy resources may be classified as primary resources, where the resource can be used in substantially its original form, or as secondary resources, where the energy source must be converted into a more conveniently usable form. [Non-renewable resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-renewable_resource) are significantly depleted by human use, whereas renewable resources are produced by ongoing processes that can sustain indefinite human exploitation.

Thousands of people are employed in the [energy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry). The conventional industry comprises the [petroleum industry](994887.md), the natural gas industry, the [electrical power industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_power_industry), and the [nuclear industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_industry). New energy industries include the [renewable energy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_industry), comprising alternative and sustainable manufacture, distribution, and sale of [alternative fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_fuel).

## Classification of resources

Further information: [World energy supply and consumption](67895571.md)

See also: [Energy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry), [Energy planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_planning), and [Energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy)



Open System Model (basics)

Energy resources may be classified as [primary resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_resources), suitable for end use without conversion to another form, or [secondary resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_resources?action=edit\&redlink=1), where the usable form of energy requires substantial [conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conversion) from a primary source. Examples of primary energy resources are [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [solar power](13690575.md), wood fuel, fossil fuels such as coal, oil and natural gas, and uranium. Secondary resources are those such as electricity, [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_economy), and other synthetic fuels.

Another important classification is based on the time required to regenerate an energy resource. "[Renewable resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_resources)" are those that recover their capacity in a time significant by human needs. Examples include [hydroelectric](381399.md) power or [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), when the natural phenomena that are the primary source of energy are ongoing and not depleted by human demand. Non-renewable resources are those that are significantly depleted by human usage and that will not recover their potential significantly during human lifetimes. An example of a non-renewable energy source is coal, which does not form naturally at a rate that would support human use.

## Fossil fuels



The [Moss Landing Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_Landing_Power_Plant) in California is a [fossil-fuel power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil-fuel_power_station) that [burns natural gas in a turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_power_plant) to produce electricity

Main articles: [Fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) and [Peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil)

Fossil fuel (*primary non-renewable fossil*) sources burn [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) or [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) fuels, which are the remains of the decomposition of plants and animals. There are three main types of fossil fuels: coal, [petroleum](23195.md), and [natural gas](22131.md). Another fossil fuel, [liquefied petroleum gas](361384.md) (LPG), is principally derived from the production of natural gas. Heat from burning fossil fuel is used either directly for space heating and process heating or converted to mechanical energy for vehicles, [industrial processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_process), or [electrical power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_power_generation). These fossil fuels are part of the [carbon cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_cycle) and allow solar energy stored in the fuel to be released.

The use of fossil fuels in the 18th and 19th centuries set the stage for the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).

Fossil fuels make up the bulk of the world's current [primary energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_energy) sources. In 2024, 86% of the world's energy needs were met from fossil fuels,[^cite_note-3] up from 81% in 2005.[^cite_note-4] The technology and infrastructure for the use of fossil fuels already exist. Liquid fuels derived from petroleum deliver much usable energy per unit of weight or volume, which is advantageous when compared with lower [energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density) sources such as [batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_\(electricity\)). Fossil fuels are currently economical for decentralized energy use.



A ([horizontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_drilling)) [drilling rig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drilling_rig) for [natural gas](22131.md) in Texas

[Energy dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_dependence) on imported fossil fuels creates [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security) risks for dependent countries.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Oil dependence in particular has led to war,[^cite_note-10] funding of radicals,[^cite_note-11] monopolization,[^cite_note-12] and socio-political instability.[^cite_note-13]

Fossil fuels are non-renewable resources, which will eventually decline in production [^cite_note-14] and become exhausted. While the processes that created fossil fuels are ongoing, fuels are consumed far more quickly than the natural replenishment rate. Extracting fuels becomes increasingly costly as society consumes the most accessible fuel deposits.[^cite_note-15] Extraction of fossil fuels results in [environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation), such as the [strip mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strip_mining) and [mountaintop removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaintop_removal) for coal.

[Fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency) is a form of [thermal efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_efficiency), meaning the efficiency of a process that converts chemical potential energy contained in a carrier [fuel](21188370.md) into [kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy) or [work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_work). The [fuel economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_economy_in_automobiles) is the energy efficiency of a particular vehicle and is given as a [ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratio) of distance travelled per unit of [fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_fuel) consumed. Weight-specific efficiency (efficiency per unit weight) may be stated for [freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight), and passenger-specific efficiency (vehicle efficiency) per passenger. The inefficient atmospheric [combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion) (burning) of fossil fuels in vehicles, buildings, and power plants contributes to [urban heat islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island).[^cite_note-16]

Conventional production of oil [peaked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil), conservatively, between 2007 and 2010. In 2010, it was estimated that an investment of \$8 trillion in non-renewable resources would be required to maintain current levels of production for 25 years.[^cite_note-17] In 2010, governments subsidized [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) by an estimated \$500 billion a year.[^cite_note-sciencedaily1-18] Fossil fuels are also a source of [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md), leading to concerns about [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) if consumption is not reduced.

The combustion of fossil fuels leads to the release of [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) into the atmosphere. The fossil fuels are mainly carbon compounds. During [combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion), [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) is released, and also [nitrogen oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_oxides), [soot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soot) and other fine [particulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulates). The carbon dioxide is the main contributor to recent [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-19] Other emissions from fossil fuel power station include [sulphur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide), [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide) (CO), [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbons), [volatile organic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_organic_compounds) (VOC), [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(element\)), [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic), [lead](17747.md), [cadmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium), and other [heavy metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metals) including traces of [uranium](31743.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

A typical [coal plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_plant) generates billions of [kilowatt-hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour) of electrical power per year.[^cite_note-22]

[Nuclear power](22153.md) is the use of [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission) to generate useful [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat) and [electricity](9550.md). Fission of uranium produces nearly all economically significant nuclear power. [Radioisotope thermoelectric generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioisotope_thermoelectric_generator) form a very small component of energy generation, mostly in specialized applications such as deep-space vehicles.

[Nuclear power plants](207347.md), excluding [naval reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion), provided about 5.7% of the world's energy and 13% of the world's electricity in 2012.[^cite_note-23]

In 2013, the [IAEA](14984.md) report that there are 437 operational nuclear power reactors,[^cite_note-iaea.org-24] in [31 countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_by_country),[^cite_note-UIC-25] although not every reactor is producing electricity.[^cite_note-26] In addition, there are approximately 140 naval vessels using [nuclear propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_propulsion) in operation, powered by some 180 reactors.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] As of 2013, attaining a [net energy gain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_European_Torus) from sustained nuclear fusion reactions, excluding natural fusion power sources such as the [Sun](26751.md), remains an ongoing area of international [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Thermonuclear_Experimental_Reactor) and [engineering research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fusion_Materials_Irradiation_Facility). More than 60 years after the first attempts, commercial fusion power production remains unlikely before 2050.[^cite_note-ITERorg-30]

There is an ongoing [debate about nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_debate).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-jstor.org-32][^cite_note-A_Reasonable_Bet_on_Nuclear_Power-33] Proponents, such as the [World Nuclear Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Nuclear_Association), the [IAEA](14984.md) and [Environmentalists for Nuclear Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalists_for_Nuclear_Energy) contend that nuclear power is a safe, [sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy) source that reduces [carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emissions).[^cite_note-bloomberg.com-34] [Opponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-nuclear_movement) contend that nuclear power poses many threats to [people and the environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_radioactivity).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-Sturgis-36]

[Nuclear power plant accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_and_radiation_accidents) include the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) (1986), [Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_disaster) (2011), and the [Three Mile Island accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mile_Island_accident) (1979).[^cite_note-timenuke-37] There have also been some nuclear submarine accidents.[^cite_note-timenuke-37][^cite_note-rad-38][^cite_note-johnston2007-39] In terms of lives lost per unit of energy generated, analysis has determined that nuclear power has caused less fatalities per unit of energy generated than the other major sources of energy generation. Energy production from [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [petroleum](23195.md), [natural gas](22131.md) and [hydropower](14073.md) has caused a greater number of fatalities per unit of energy generated due to [air pollution](10934212.md) and [energy accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_accidents) effects.[^cite_note-autogenerated2007-40][^cite_note-without_the_hot_air-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-theage2006-43][^cite_note-tandfonline1-44] However, the economic costs of nuclear power accidents is high, and meltdowns can take decades to clean up. The human costs of evacuations of affected populations and lost livelihoods is also significant.[^cite_note-Richard_Schiffman-45][^cite_note-Martin_Fackler-46]

Comparing Nuclear's *latent* cancer deaths, such as cancer with other energy sources' *immediate* deaths per unit of energy generated(GWeyr). This study does not include fossil fuel-related cancer and other indirect deaths created by the use of fossil fuel consumption in its "severe accident" classification, which would be an accident with more than 5 fatalities.

As of 2012, according to the [IAEA](14984.md), worldwide there were 68 civil nuclear power reactors under construction in 15 countries,[^cite_note-iaea.org-24] approximately 28 of which in the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC), with the most recent nuclear power reactor, as of May 2013, to be connected to the [electrical grid](20344155.md), occurring on February 17, 2013, in [Hongyanhe Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongyanhe_Nuclear_Power_Plant) in the PRC.[^cite_note-47] In the United States, two new [Generation III reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_III_reactor) are under construction at [Vogtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogtle). U.S. nuclear industry officials expect five new reactors to enter service by 2020, all at existing plants.[^cite_note-us12-48] In 2013, four aging, uncompetitive, reactors were permanently closed.[^cite_note-Mark_Cooper-49][^cite_note-mw11111-50]

Recent experiments in the extraction of uranium use polymer ropes that are coated with a substance that selectively absorbs uranium from seawater. This process could make the considerable volume of uranium dissolved in seawater exploitable for energy production. Since ongoing geologic processes carry uranium to the sea in amounts comparable to the amount that would be extracted by this process, in a sense the sea-borne uranium becomes a sustainable resource.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

Nuclear power is a [low carbon power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_carbon_power_generation) method of producing electricity, with an analysis of the literature on its [total life cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_cycle_assessment) [emission intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_intensity) finding that it is similar to renewable sources in a comparison of [greenhouse gas](21350772.md) (GHG) emissions per unit of energy generated.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-Wagner2021-54] Since the 1970s, nuclear fuel has displaced about 64 [gigatonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigaton) of [carbon dioxide equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_equivalent) (GtCO2-eq) [greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gases), that would have otherwise resulted from the burning of oil, coal or natural gas in [fossil-fuel power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil-fuel_power_station).[^cite_note-55]

#### Fission economics

Main article: [Economics of nuclear power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_of_nuclear_power_plants)



Low global public support for nuclear fission in the aftermath of Fukushima ([Ipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipsos)-survey, 2011)

[^cite_note-Ipsos-Fukushima-61]

The economics of new nuclear power plants is a controversial subject, since there are diverging views on this topic, and multibillion-dollar investments ride on the choice of an energy source. [Nuclear power plants](207347.md) typically have high capital costs for building the plant, but low direct fuel costs. In recent years there has been a slowdown in electricity demand growth and financing has become more difficult, which affects large projects such as nuclear reactors, with very high upfront costs and long project cycles that carry a large variety of risks.[^cite_note-kidd2011-62] In Eastern Europe, a number of long-established projects are struggling to find finance, notably Belene in Bulgaria and the additional reactors at Cernavoda in Romania, and some potential backers have pulled out.[^cite_note-kidd2011-62] Where cheap gas is available and its future supply relatively secure, this also poses a major problem for nuclear projects.[^cite_note-kidd2011-62]

Analysis of the economics of nuclear power must take into account who bears the risks of future uncertainties. To date all operating nuclear power plants were developed by [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalized) or [regulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulated_market) [utility monopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_utility)[^cite_note-ft-20100912-63][^cite_note-NERA-20120316-64] where many of the risks associated with construction costs, operating performance, fuel price, and other factors were borne by consumers rather than suppliers. Many countries have now liberalized the [electricity market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_market) where these risks, and the risk of cheaper competitors emerging before capital costs are recovered, are borne by plant suppliers and operators rather than consumers, which leads to a significantly different evaluation of the economics of new nuclear power plants.[^cite_note-MIT-2003-65]

#### Costs

Costs are likely to go up for currently operating and new nuclear power plants due to increased requirements for on-site spent fuel management and elevated design-basis threats.[^cite_note-Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology_2011_xv-66] While first of their kind designs, such as the EPRs under construction are behind schedule and over-budget, of the seven South Korean [APR-1400s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APR-1400) presently under construction worldwide, two are in S.Korea at the [Hanul Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanul_Nuclear_Power_Plant) and four are at the largest nuclear station construction project in the world as of 2016, in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) at the planned [Barakah nuclear power plant](39806131.md). The first reactor, Barakah-1 is 85% completed and on schedule for grid connection during 2017.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] Two of the four [EPRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Pressurized_Reactor) under construction (in [Finland](10577.md) and France) are significantly behind schedule and substantially over cost.[^cite_note-Patel-69]

## Renewable sources

Main article: [Renewable energy commercialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialization)



Renewable energy capacity has steadily grown, led by [solar photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_system) power.

[^cite_note-IEA_202306-70]



The countries most reliant on fossil fuels for electricity vary widely in how great a percentage of that electricity is generated from renewables, leaving wide variation in renewables' growth potential.

[^cite_note-BP-Ember_20211103-71]

[Renewable energy](25784.md) is generally defined as energy that comes from resources which are naturally replenished on a human timescale such as [sunlight](27979.md), [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind), [rain](19009110.md), [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power), [waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_power) and [geothermal heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy). Renewable energy replaces conventional fuels in four distinct areas: [electricity generation](9540.md), [hot water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_hot_water)/[space heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_heating), [motor fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_fuel), and [rural (off-grid)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-alone_power_system) energy services.

Including traditional biomass usage, about 19% of global energy consumption is accounted for by renewable resources.[^cite_note-72] Wind-powered energy production is being turned to as a prominent renewable energy source, increasing global wind power capacity by 12% in 2021.[^cite_note-73] While not the case for all countries, 58% of sample countries linked renewable energy consumption to have a positive impact on economic growth.[^cite_note-74] At the national level, at least 30 nations around the world already have renewable energy contributing more than 20% of energy supply. National renewable energy markets are projected to continue to grow strongly in the coming decade and beyond.\[76]

Unlike other energy sources, renewable energy sources are not as restricted by geography. Additionally deployment of renewable energy is resulting in economic benefits as well as combating climate change. Rural electrification[^cite_note-75] has been researched on multiple sites and has positive effects on commercial spending, appliance use, and general activities requiring electricity as energy.[^cite_note-76] Renewable energy growth in at least 38 countries has been driven by the high electricity usage rates.[^cite_note-77] International support for promoting renewable sources like solar and wind have continued to grow.

While many renewable energy projects are large-scale, renewable technologies are also suited to [rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural) and remote areas and [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_developing_countries), where energy is often crucial in [human development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_\(humanity\)). To ensure human development continues sustainably, governments around the world are beginning to research potential ways to implement renewable sources into their countries and economies. For example, the UK Government's Department for Energy and Climate Change 2050 Pathways created a mapping technique to educate the public on land competition between energy supply technologies.[^cite_note-78] This tool provides users the ability to understand what the limitations and potential of their surrounding land and country has in terms of energy production.

### Hydroelectricity



&#x20;The 22,500 [MW](21347693.md) [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) in China – the [world's largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_conventional_hydroelectric_power_stations#Hydroelectric_power_stations) hydroelectric power station

[Hydroelectricity](381399.md) is electric power generated by [hydropower](14073.md); the force of falling or flowing water. In 2015 hydropower generated 16.6% of the world's total electricity and 70% of all renewable electricity [^cite_note-79] and was expected to increase about 3.1% each year for the following 25 years.

Hydropower is produced in 150 countries, with the Asia-Pacific region generating 32 percent of global hydropower in 2010. China is the largest hydroelectricity producer, with 721 terawatt-hours of production in 2010, representing around 17 percent of domestic electricity use. There are now three hydroelectricity plants larger than 10 GW: the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) in China, [Itaipu Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Dam) across the Brazil/Paraguay border, and [Guri Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guri_Dam) in Venezuela.[^cite_note-wi2012-80]

The cost of hydroelectricity is relatively low, making it a competitive source of renewable electricity. The average cost of electricity from a hydro plant larger than 10 megawatts is 3 to 5 U.S. cents per kilowatt-hour.[^cite_note-wi2012-80] Hydro is also a flexible source of electricity since plants can be ramped up and down very quickly to adapt to changing energy demands. However, damming interrupts the flow of rivers and can harm local ecosystems, and building large dams and reservoirs often involves displacing people and wildlife.[^cite_note-wi2012-80] Once a hydroelectric complex is constructed, the project produces no direct waste and has a considerably lower output level of the [greenhouse gas](21350772.md) [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) than [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) powered energy plants.[^cite_note-REN21-2011-81]

### Wind

[Wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) harnesses the power of the wind to propel the blades of [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine). These turbines cause the rotation of [magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet), which creates electricity. Wind towers are usually built together on [wind farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm). There are [offshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_offshore_wind_farms) and [onshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_onshore_wind_farms) wind farms. [Global wind power capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_by_country) has expanded rapidly to 336 [GW](21347693.md) in June 2014, and wind energy production was around 4% of total worldwide electricity usage and growing rapidly.[^cite_note-wwea2014-halfyear-82]

Wind power is widely used in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_the_European_Union), [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_China), and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-Glob-83] Several countries have achieved relatively high levels of wind power penetration, such as 21% of stationary electricity production in [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Denmark),[^cite_note-wwea-84] 18% in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Portugal),[^cite_note-wwea-84] 16% in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Spain),[^cite_note-wwea-84] 14% in [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Ireland),[^cite_note-85] and 9% in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Germany) in 2010.[^cite_note-wwea-84][^cite_note-ren212011-86]<sup>: 11 </sup> By 2011, at times over 50% of electricity in Germany and Spain came from wind and solar power.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] As of 2011, 83 countries around the world are using wind power on a commercial basis.[^cite_note-ren212011-86]<sup>: 11 </sup>

Many of the [world's largest onshore wind farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_onshore_wind_farms#Largest_operational_onshore_wind_farms) are located in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_the_United_States), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_China), and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_India). Most of the [world's largest offshore wind farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_offshore_wind_farms) are located in [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Denmark), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Germany) and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_the_United_Kingdom). The two largest offshore wind farms are currently the 630 [MW](21347693.md) [London Array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Array) and [Gwynt y Môr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynt_y_M%C3%B4r).

### Solar

[Solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy) is the [radiant energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_energy) from the [Sun](26751.md)'s [light](27979.md) and [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat), which can be harnessed using a range of [technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technologies) such as [solar electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_electricity), [solar thermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_thermal_energy) (including [solar water heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_water_heating)) and [solar architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_architecture).[^cite_note-Solar_energy_ie11-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] It is an essential source of [renewable energy](25784.md), and its technologies are broadly characterized as either [passive solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_solar) or active solar depending on how they capture and distribute solar energy or convert it into solar power. Active solar techniques include the use of [photovoltaic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_system), [concentrated solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power), and solar water heating to harness the energy. Passive solar techniques include designing a building for better [daylighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylighting_\(architecture\)), selecting materials with favorable [thermal mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_mass) or light-dispersing properties, and organizing spaces that [naturally circulate air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventilation_\(architecture\)).

In 2011, the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) said that "the development of affordable, inexhaustible and clean solar energy technologies will have huge longer-term benefits. It will increase countries' [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security) through reliance on an indigenous, inexhaustible, and mostly import-independent resource, enhance [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability), reduce [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution), lower the costs of [mitigating global warming](2119179.md) .... these advantages are global".[^cite_note-Solar_energy_ie11-96][^cite_note-Solar_energy_rev-99]

### Biofuels

Main articles: [Biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) and [Sustainable biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_biofuel)



A bus fueled by [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel)



Information on pump regarding [ethanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) blend up to 10%, [California](5407.md)

A biofuel is a [fuel](21188370.md) that contains energy from geologically recent [carbon fixation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fixation). These fuels are produced from [living organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_organisms). Examples of this [carbon fixation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fixation) occur in [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants) and [microalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae). These fuels are made by a [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) conversion (biomass refers to recently living organisms, most often referring to [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants) or plant-derived materials). This biomass can be converted to convenient energy-containing substances in three different ways: thermal conversion, chemical conversion, and biochemical conversion. This biomass conversion can result in fuel in [solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid), [liquid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid), or [gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) form. This new biomass can be used for biofuels. Biofuels have increased in popularity because of rising [oil prices](5137675.md) and the need for [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security).

[Bioethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethanol) is an [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(chemistry\)) made by [fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fermentation), mostly from [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate) produced in [sugar](27712.md) or [starch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch) crops such as [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) or [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane). [Cellulosic biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose), derived from non-food sources, such as trees and grasses, is also being developed as a [feedstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedstock) for ethanol production. Ethanol can be used as a fuel for vehicles in its pure form, but it is usually used as a [gasoline](23639.md) [additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_additive) to increase octane and improve vehicle emissions. Bioethanol is widely used in the [USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel_in_the_United_States) and in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel_in_Brazil). Current plant design does not provide for converting the [lignin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignin) portion of plant raw materials to fuel components by fermentation.

[Biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel) is made from [vegetable oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil) and [animal fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_fat). Biodiesel can be used as a fuel for vehicles in its pure form, but it is usually used as a [diesel](81761.md) additive to reduce levels of particulates, [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide), and [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) from diesel-powered vehicles. Biodiesel is produced from oils or fats using [transesterification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transesterification) and is the most common biofuel in Europe. However, research is underway on producing renewable fuels from [decarboxylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_fuels_by_decarboxylation)[^cite_note-100]

In 2010, worldwide biofuel production reached 105 billion liters (28 billion gallons US), up 17% from 2009,[^cite_note-Biofuels2010-101] and biofuels provided 2.7% of the world's fuels for [road transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_transport), a contribution largely made up of ethanol and biodiesel. Global [ethanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) production reached 86 billion liters (23 billion gallons US) in 2010, with the United States and Brazil as the world's top producers, accounting together for 90% of global production. The world's largest biodiesel producer is the [European Union](9317.md), accounting for 53% of all biodiesel production in 2010.[^cite_note-Biofuels2010-101] As of 2011, mandates for blending biofuels exist in 31 countries at the national level and in 29 states or provinces.[^cite_note-ren212011-86]<sup>: 13–14 </sup> The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) has a goal for biofuels to meet more than a quarter of world demand for transportation fuels by 2050 to reduce dependence on petroleum and coal.[^cite_note-102]

### Geothermal

Main article: [Geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy)



Steam rising from the [Nesjavellir Geothermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesjavellir_Geothermal_Power_Station) in [Iceland](14531.md)

Geothermal energy is [thermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy) generated and stored in the Earth. Thermal energy is the energy that determines the [temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature) of matter. The geothermal energy of the Earth's [crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crust_\(geology\)) originates from the planet's original formation (20%) and from [radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay) of minerals (80%).[^cite_note-ucsusa-103] The [geothermal gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_gradient), which is the difference in temperature between the core of the planet and its surface, drives a continuous conduction of thermal energy in the form of [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat) from the core to the surface. The adjective *geothermal* originates from the Greek roots *γη (ge)*, meaning earth, and *θερμος (thermos)*, meaning hot.

[Earth's internal heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_internal_heat_budget) is thermal energy generated from [radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay) and continual heat loss from Earth's formation. Temperatures at the [core-mantle boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_mantle_boundary) may reach over 4000 °C (7,200 °F).[^cite_note-104] The high temperature and pressure in Earth's interior cause some rock to melt and solid [mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_\(geology\)) to behave plastically, resulting in portions of [mantle convecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_convection) upward since it is lighter than the surrounding rock. Rock and water are heated in the crust, sometimes up to 370 °C (700 °F).[^cite_note-105]

From [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_springs), geothermal energy has been used for bathing since [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) times and for [space heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_heating) since ancient Roman times, but it is now better known for [electricity generation](9540.md). Worldwide, 11,400 [megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts) (MW) of geothermal power was online in 24 countries in 2012.[^cite_note-106] An additional 28 gigawatts of direct [geothermal heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_heating) capacity is installed for district heating, space heating, spas, industrial processes, desalination and agricultural applications in 2010.[^cite_note-IPCC-107]

Geothermal power is cost-effective, reliable, sustainable, and environmentally friendly,[^cite_note-108] but has historically been limited to areas near [tectonic plate boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_plate_boundaries). Recent technological advances have dramatically expanded the range and size of viable resources, especially for applications such as home heating, opening a potential for widespread exploitation. Geothermal wells release greenhouse gases trapped deep within the earth, but these emissions are much lower per energy unit than those of fossil fuels. As a result, geothermal power has the potential to help mitigate [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) if widely deployed in place of fossil fuels.

The Earth's geothermal resources are theoretically more than adequate to supply humanity's energy needs, but only a very small fraction may be profitably exploited. Drilling and exploration for deep resources is very expensive. Forecasts for the future of geothermal power depend on assumptions about technology, energy prices, [subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_subsidies), and interest rates. Pilot programs like EWEB's customer opt-in Green Power Program [^cite_note-109] show that customers would be willing to pay a little more for a renewable energy source like geothermal. But as a result of government-assisted research and industry experience, the cost of generating geothermal power has decreased by 25% over the past two decades.[^cite_note-110] In 2001, geothermal energy cost between two and ten US cents per kWh.[^cite_note-111]

### Oceanic

Main article: [Marine energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_energy)

**Marine Renewable Energy (MRE)** or marine power (also sometimes referred to as ocean energy, ocean power, or marine and hydrokinetic energy) refers to the energy carried by the mechanical energy of [ocean waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_wave), currents, and [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide), shifts in [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) gradients, and [ocean temperature differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_thermal_energy). MRE has the potential to become a reliable and renewable energy source because of the cyclical nature of the ocean&#x73;**.**[^cite_note-112] The movement of water in the world's oceans creates a vast store of [kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy) or energy in motion. This energy can be harnessed to [generate](9540.md) electricity to power homes, transport, and industries.

The term marine energy encompasses both [wave power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_power), i.e. power from surface waves, and [tidal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power), i.e. obtained from the kinetic energy of large bodies of moving water. [Offshore wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_wind_power) is not a form of marine energy, as wind power is derived from the wind, even if the [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) are placed over water. The oceans have a tremendous amount of energy and are close to many if not most concentrated populations. Ocean energy has the potential to provide a substantial amount of new [renewable energy](25784.md) around the world.[^cite_note-113]

Marine energy technology is in its first stage of development. To be developed, MRE needs efficient methods of storing, transporting, and capturing ocean power, so it can be used where needed.[^cite_note-114] Over the past year, countries around the world have started implementing market strategies for MRE to commercialize. Canada and China introduced incentives, such as [feed-in tariffs (FiTs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feed-in_tariff), which are above-market prices for MRE that allow investors and project developers a stable income. Other financial strategies consist of subsidies, grants, and funding from [public-private partnerships (PPPs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership). China alone approved 100 ocean projects in 2019.[^cite_note-115] Portugal and Spain recognize the potential of MRE in accelerating [decarbonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decarbonization), which is fundamental to meeting the goals of the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md). Both countries are focusing on solar and offshore wind [auctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction) to attract private investment, ensure cost-effectiveness, and accelerate MRE growth.[^cite_note-116] Ireland sees MRE as a key component to reduce its carbon footprint. The Offshore Renewable Energy Development Plan (OREDP) supports the exploration and development of the country's significant offshore energy potential.[^cite_note-117] Additionally, Ireland has implemented the Renewable Electricity Support Scheme (RESS) which includes auctions designed to provide financial support for communities, increase technology diversity, and guarantee [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security).[^cite_note-118]

However, while research is increasing, there have been concerns associated with threats to marine mammals' habitats and potential changes to [ocean currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_current). MRE can be a renewable energy source for coastal communities helping their transition from fossil fuel, but researchers are calling for a better understanding of its environmental impacts.[^cite_note-119] Because ocean-energy areas are often isolated from both fishing and sea traffic, these zones may provide shelter from humans and predators for some marine species. MRE devices can be an ideal home for many [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [crayfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crayfish), [mollusks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusca), and [barnacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnacle); and may also indirectly affect [seabirds](147019.md) and [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal) because they feed on those species. Similarly, such areas may create an "[artificial reef effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_reef)" by boosting biodiversity nearby. [Noise pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution) generated from the technology is limited and also causes fish and mammals living in the area of the installation to return.[^cite_note-120] In the most recent State of Science Report about MRE, the authors claim that there is no evidence for fish, mammals, or seabirds to be injured by either collision, noise pollution, or the electromagnetic field. The uncertainty of its environmental impact comes from the low quantity of MRE devices in the ocean today where data is collected.[^cite_note-121]

### 100% renewable energy

Main article: [100% renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_renewable_energy)

The incentive to use 100% renewable energy for electricity, transport, or even total primary energy supply globally has been motivated by [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) and other ecological as well as economic concerns. [Renewable energy use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialization) has grown much faster than anyone anticipated.[^cite_note-pg11-122] The [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](15030.md) has said that there are few fundamental technological limits to integrating a portfolio of renewable energy technologies to meet most of total global energy demand.[^cite_note-IPCC_2011_17-123] At the national level, at least 30 nations around the world already have renewable energy contributing more than 20% of energy supply. Also, [Stephen W. Pacala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_W._Pacala) and [Robert H. Socolow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Socolow) have developed a series of "[stabilization wedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilization_wedges)" that can allow us to maintain our quality of life while avoiding catastrophic climate change, and "renewable energy sources," in aggregate, constitute the largest number of their "wedges."[^cite_note-Pacala-124]

[Mark Z. Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Z._Jacobson) says producing all new energy with [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [solar power](13690575.md), and [hydropower](14073.md) by 2030 is feasible and existing energy supply arrangements could be replaced by 2050. Barriers to implementing the renewable energy plan are seen to be "primarily social and political, not technological or economic". Jacobson says that energy costs with a wind, solar, water system should be similar to today's energy costs.[^cite_note-enpol2011-125]

Similarly, in the United States, the independent National Research Council has noted that "sufficient domestic renewable resources exist to allow renewable electricity to play a significant role in future electricity generation and thus help confront issues related to climate change, energy security, and the escalation of energy costs ... Renewable energy is an attractive option because renewable resources available in the United States, taken collectively, can supply significantly larger amounts of electricity than the total current or projected domestic demand." .[^cite_note-NRC-126]

Critics of the "100% renewable energy" approach include [Vaclav Smil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaclav_Smil) and [James E. Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Hansen). Smil and Hansen are concerned about the [variable output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_renewable_energy) of solar and wind power, but [Amory Lovins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amory_Lovins) argues that the [electricity grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_grid) can cope, just as it routinely backs up nonworking coal-fired and nuclear plants with working ones.[^cite_note-lovi12-127]

Google spent \$30 million on its "Renewable Energy Cheaper than Coal" project to develop renewable energy and stave off catastrophic climate change. The project was cancelled after concluding that a best-case scenario for rapid advances in renewable energy could only result in emissions 55 percent below the fossil fuel projections for 2050.[^cite_note-128]

## Increased energy efficiency



A spiral-type integrated [compact fluorescent lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_fluorescent_lamp), which has been popular among North American consumers since its introduction in the mid-1990s

[^cite_note-129]

Main article: [Efficient energy use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use)

Although increasing the efficiency of energy use is not energy development per se, it may be considered under the topic of energy development since it makes existing energy sources available to do work.[^cite_note-130]<sup>: 22 </sup>

Efficient energy use reduces the amount of energy required to provide products and services. For example, [insulating a home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_insulation) allows a building to use less heating and cooling energy to maintain a comfortable temperature. Installing [fluorescent lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescent_lamp) or natural [skylights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylight_\(window\)) reduces the amount of energy required for illumination compared to [incandescent light bulbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incandescent_light_bulbs). [Compact fluorescent lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_fluorescent_lights) use two-thirds less energy and may last 6 to 10 times longer than incandescent lights. Improvements in energy efficiency are most often achieved by adopting an efficient technology or production process.[^cite_note-131]

Reducing energy use may save consumers money if the energy savings offset the cost of an energy-efficient technology. Reducing energy use reduces emissions. According to the [International Energy Agency](285292.md), improved energy efficiency in [buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_efficient_buildings), industrial processes and [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transportation) could reduce the global energy demand in 2050 to around 8% smaller than today, but serving an economy more than twice as big and a population of about 2 billion more people.[^cite_note-132]

Energy efficiency and [renewable energy](25784.md) are said to be the *twin pillars* of sustainable energy policy.[^cite_note-133] In many countries energy efficiency is also seen to have a national security benefit because it can be used to reduce the level of energy imports from foreign countries and may slow down the rate at which domestic energy resources are depleted.

It's been discovered "that for OECD countries, wind, geothermal, hydro and nuclear have the lowest hazard rates among energy sources in production".[^cite_note-134]

## Transmission



An elevated section of the [Alaska Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Alaska_Pipeline_System)

While new sources of energy are only rarely discovered or made possible by new [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technologies), [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(business\)) technology continually evolves.[^cite_note-135] The use of [fuel cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell) in cars, for example, is an anticipated delivery technology.[^cite_note-136] This section presents the various delivery technologies that have been important to historic energy development. They all rely in some way on the energy sources listed in the previous section.

### Shipping and pipelines

See also: [Pipeline transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport)

[Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [petroleum](23195.md) and their derivatives are delivered by boat, [rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport), or road. Petroleum and natural gas may also be delivered by [pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport), and coal via a [Slurry pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry_pipeline). Fuels such as [gasoline](23639.md) and [LPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquified_petroleum_gas) may also be delivered via [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft). Natural gas pipelines must maintain a certain minimum pressure to function correctly. The higher costs of ethanol transportation and storage are often prohibitive.[^cite_note-137]

### Wired energy transfer



[Electrical grid](20344155.md) – pylons and cables distribute power

Main article: [Electrical grid](20344155.md)

Electricity grids are the [networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_network) used to [transmit](38824.md) and [distribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_distribution) [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) from a production source to an end user, when the two may be hundreds of kilometres away. Sources include electrical generation plants such as a [nuclear reactor](22151.md), coal-burning power plant, etc. A combination of sub-stations and transmission lines are used to maintain a constant flow of electricity. Grids may suffer from transient [blackouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_blackout) and [brownouts](296636.md), often due to weather damage. During certain extreme [space weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weather) events [solar wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_wind) can interfere with transmissions. Grids also have a predefined [carrying capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrying_capacity) or load that cannot safely be exceeded. When power requirements exceed what's available, failures are inevitable. To prevent problems, power is then rationed.

Industrialised countries such as Canada, the [US](3434750.md), and Australia are among the highest per capita consumers of electricity in the world, which is possible thanks to a widespread electrical distribution network. The US grid is one of the most advanced, although [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) maintenance is becoming a problem. [CurrentEnergy](https://web.archive.org/web/20041014214845/http://currentenergy.lbl.gov/) provides a realtime overview of the electricity supply and demand for [California](5407.md), [Texas](29810.md), and the Northeast of the US. African countries with small-scale electrical grids have a correspondingly low annual per capita usage of electricity. One of the most powerful power grids in the world supplies power to the state of [Queensland](59051.md), Australia.

### Wireless energy transfer

[Wireless power transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_power_transfer) is a process whereby electrical energy is transmitted from a power source to an electrical load that does not have a built-in power source, without the use of interconnecting wires. Currently available technology is limited to short distances and relatively low power level.

Orbiting solar power collectors would require wireless transmission of power to Earth. The proposed method involves creating a large beam of microwave-frequency radio waves, which would be aimed at a collector antenna site on the Earth. Formidable technical challenges exist to ensure the safety and profitability of such a scheme.

## Storage



The [Ffestiniog Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ffestiniog_Power_Station) in [Wales](69894.md), United Kingdom. [Pumped-storage hydroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydroelectricity) (PSH) is used for [grid energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_energy_storage).

Main articles: [Energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_storage) and [List of energy storage power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_energy_storage_power_plants)

Energy storage is accomplished by devices or physical media that store [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) to perform useful operations at a later time. A device that stores energy is sometimes called an [accumulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accumulator_\(energy\)).

All forms of energy are either [potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_energy) (e.g. [Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_energy), [gravitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation), [electrical energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_potential_energy), temperature differential, [latent heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_heat), etc.) or [kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy) (e.g. [momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum)). Some technologies provide only short-term energy storage, and others can be very long-term such as [power to gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_to_gas) using [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) or [methane](18582230.md) and the [storage of heat or cold between opposing seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_thermal_energy_storage) in deep aquifers or bedrock. A wind-up clock stores potential energy (in this case mechanical, in the spring tension), a [battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_\(electricity\)) stores readily convertible chemical energy to operate a mobile phone, and a [hydroelectric](381399.md) dam stores [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_power_industry) in a [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir) as gravitational [potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_energy). [Ice storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy_storage#Air_conditioning) tanks store ice ([thermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy) in the form of latent heat) at night to meet peak demand for cooling. [Fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) such as coal and gasoline store ancient energy derived from sunlight by organisms that later died, became buried and over time were then converted into these fuels. Even [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food) (which is made by the same process as fossil fuels) is a form of energy stored in [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical) form.

## History



Energy generators past and present at [Doel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doel), Belgium: 17th-century windmill *Scheldemolen* and 20th-century [Doel Nuclear Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doel_Nuclear_Power_Station)

Since prehistory, when humanity discovered fire to warm up and roast food, through the Middle Ages in which populations built windmills to grind the wheat, until the modern era in which nations can get electricity by splitting the atom. Man has sought endlessly for energy sources.

Except nuclear, geothermal and [tidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power), all other energy sources are from current solar radiation or from fossil remains of plant and animal life that relied upon sunlight. Ultimately, [solar energy](13690575.md) itself is the result of the [Sun](26751.md)'s nuclear fusion. [Geothermal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power) from hot, hardened [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_\(geology\)) above the [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) of the Earth's core is the result of the decay of radioactive materials present beneath the Earth's crust, and [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission) relies on man-made fission of heavy radioactive elements in the Earth's crust; in both cases these elements were produced in [supernova](27680.md) explosions before the formation of the [Solar System](26903.md).

Since the beginning of the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), the future of energy supplies has been of interest. In 1865, [William Stanley Jevons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Stanley_Jevons) published *The Coal Question* in which he saw that the reserves of coal were being depleted and that oil was an ineffective replacement. In 1914, the [U.S. Bureau of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bureau_of_Mines) stated that the total production was 5.7 billion barrels (910,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>). In 1956, Geophysicist [M. King Hubbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._King_Hubbert) deduced that U.S. oil production would [peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubbert_peak_theory) between 1965 and 1970 and that oil production would peak "within half a century" based on 1956 data. In 1989, predicted peak by [Colin Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Campbell_\(geologist\))[^cite_note-138] In 2004, OPEC estimated that, with substantial investments, it would nearly double oil output by 2025.[^cite_note-139]

### Sustainability



Energy consumption from 1989 to 1999

Main article: [Sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy)

The [environmental movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement) has emphasized [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) of energy use and development.[^cite_note-140] [Renewable energy](25784.md) is sustainable in its production; the available supply will not be diminished for the foreseeable future - millions or billions of years. "Sustainability" also refers to the ability of the environment to cope with waste products, especially [air pollution](10934212.md). Sources that have no direct waste products (such as wind, solar, and hydropower) are brought up on this point. With global demand for energy growing, the need to adopt various energy sources is growing. [Energy conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) is an alternative or complementary process to energy development. It reduces the demand for energy by using it efficiently.

Extrapolations from current knowledge to the future offer a choice of energy futures.[^cite_note-143] Predictions parallel the [Malthusian catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malthusian_catastrophe) hypothesis. Numerous are complex [models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_modeling) based on [scenarios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenario) as pioneered by *[Limits to Growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limits_to_Growth)*. Short-term [energy crises](261316.md) are also a concern of energy development. Extrapolations lack plausibility, particularly when they predict a continual increase in oil consumption.

Energy production usually requires an energy investment. Drilling for oil or building a wind power plant requires energy. The fossil fuel resources that are left are often increasingly difficult to extract and convert. They may thus require increasingly higher energy investments. If investment is greater than the value of the energy produced by the resource, it is no longer an effective energy source. These resources are no longer an energy source but may be exploited for value as raw materials. New technology may lower the energy investment required to extract and convert the resources, although ultimately basic physics sets limits that cannot be exceeded.

Between 1950 and 1984, as the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution) transformed [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) around the globe, world grain production increased by 250%. The energy for the Green Revolution was provided by [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels) in the form of [fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizers) (natural gas), [pesticides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticides) (oil), and [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) fueled [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation).[^cite_note-144] The peaking of world hydrocarbon production ([peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil)) may lead to significant changes, and require sustainable methods of production.[^cite_note-145] One vision of a sustainable energy future involves all human structures on the earth's surface (i.e., buildings, vehicles and roads) doing [artificial photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_photosynthesis) (using sunlight to split water as a source of hydrogen and absorbing carbon dioxide to make fertilizer) efficiently than plants.[^cite_note-146]

With contemporary [space industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_industry)'s economic activity[^cite_note-Bromberg2000-1-147][^cite_note-Schrogl2010-148] and the related [private spaceflight](752732.md), with the [manufacturing industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_industries), that go into Earth's orbit or beyond, delivering them to those regions will require further energy development.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] Researchers have contemplated [space-based solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_solar_power) for collecting solar power for use on Earth. Space-based solar power has been in research since the early 1970s. Space-based solar power would require construction of collector structures in space. The advantage over ground-based solar power is higher intensity of light, and no weather to interrupt power collection.

## Energy technology

Energy technology is an [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary) [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) having to do with the efficient, safe, [environmentally friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentally_friendly), and economical extraction, conversion, transportation, storage, and use of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy), targeted towards yielding high efficiency whilst skirting [side effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_effect) on humans, nature, and the environment.

For people, energy is an overwhelming need, and as a scarce [resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource), it has been an underlying cause of political conflicts and wars. The gathering and use of energy resources can be harmful to local ecosystems and may have global outcomes.

Energy is also the capacity to do work. We can get energy from food. Energy can be of different forms such as kinetic, potential, mechanical, heat, light etc. Energy is required for individuals and the whole society for lighting, heating, cooking, running industries, operating transportation and so forth. Basically there are two types of energy depending on the source s they are; 1. Renewable Energy Sources 2. Non-Renewable Energy Sources

### Renewable energy

Main article: [Renewable energy](25784.md)



Solar ([photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic)) panels at a military base in the US.

[Renewable energy](25784.md) has many branches.

#### Wind power



[Wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) on [Inner Mongolian](55064.md) grassland

Main article: [Wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power)

[Wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) convert wind energy into electricity by connecting a spinning rotor to a generator. [Wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) draw energy from atmospheric currents and are designed using [aerodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamics) along with knowledge taken from mechanical and electrical engineering. The wind passes across the aerodynamic rotor blades, creating an area of higher pressure and an area of lower pressure on either side of the blade. The forces of lift and drag are formed due to the difference in air pressure. The lift force is stronger than the drag force; therefore the rotor, which is connected to a generator, spins. The energy is then created due to the change from the aerodynamic force to the rotation of the generator.[^cite_note-152]

Being recognized as one of the most efficient renewable energy sources, wind power is becoming more and more relevant and used in the world.[^cite_note-153] Wind power does not use any water in the production of energy making it a good source of energy for areas without much water. Wind energy could also be produced even if the climate changes in line with current predictions, as it relies solely on wind.[^cite_note-154]

#### Geothermal

Main article: [Geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy)

Deep within the Earth is an extreme heat-producing layer of molten rock called magma.[^cite_note-155] The very high temperatures from the magma heat nearby groundwater. Various technologies have been developed in order to benefit from such heat, such as using different types of power plants (dry, flash or binary), heat pumps, or wells.[^cite_note-156] These processes of harnessing the heat incorporate an infrastructure that has in one form or another a turbine which is spun by either the hot water or the steam produced by it.[^cite_note-157] The spinning turbine, being connected to a generator, produces energy. A more recent innovation involves the use of shallow closed-loop systems that pump heat to and from structures by taking advantage of the constant temperature of soil around 10 feet deep.[^cite_note-158]

#### Hydropower



Building of [Pelton water turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelton_turbine) in [Germany](11867.md).

Main article: [Hydropower](14073.md)

Hydropower draws mechanical energy from rivers, [ocean waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_power_plant) and [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power). [Civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) is used to study and build [dams](51518.md), [tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel), [waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterways) and manage coastal resources through [hydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrology) and [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology). A low-speed [water turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_turbine) spun by flowing water can power an [electrical generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_generator) to produce electricity.

#### Bioenergy

Main article: [Bioenergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioenergy)

Bioenergy deals with the gathering, processing and use of biomass grown in biological manufacturing, [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) from which [power plants](212141.md) can draw to burn as fuel. [Ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol), [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol) (both controversial) or hydrogen for [fuel cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cells) can be had from these technologies and used to generate electricity.

#### Enabling technologies

[Heat pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pumps) and [Thermal energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy_storage) are classes of technologies that can enable the utilization of [renewable energy](25784.md) sources that would otherwise be inaccessible due to a temperature that is too low for utilization or a time lag between when the energy is available and when it is needed. While enhancing the temperature of available renewable thermal energy, heat pumps have the additional property of leveraging electrical power (or in some cases mechanical or thermal power) by using it to extract additional energy from a low-quality source (such as seawater, lake water, the ground, the air, or [waste heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_heat) from a process).

Thermal storage technologies allow heat or cold to be stored for periods of time ranging from hours or overnight to [interseasonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_thermal_energy_storage), and can involve storage of [sensible energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensible_heat) (i.e. by changing the temperature of a medium) or [latent energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_energy) (i.e. through phase changes of a medium, such between water and slush or ice). Short-term thermal storage can be used for peak-shaving in district heating or electrical distribution systems. Kinds of renewable or alternative energy sources that can be enabled include natural energy (e.g. collected via solar-thermal collectors, or dry cooling towers used to collect winter's cold), waste energy (e.g. from HVAC equipment, industrial processes or power plants), or surplus energy (e.g. as seasonally from hydropower projects or intermittently from wind farms). The [Drake Landing Solar Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Landing_Solar_Community) (Alberta, Canada) is illustrative. [borehole thermal energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_thermal_energy_storage) allows the community to get 97% of its year-round heat from solar collectors on the garage roofs, with most of the heat collected in summer.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] Types of storage for sensible energy include insulated tanks, borehole clusters in substrates ranging from gravel to bedrock, deep aquifers, or shallow lined pits that are insulated on top. Some types of storage are capable of storing heat or cold [between opposing seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_thermal_energy_storage) (particularly if very large), and some storage applications require inclusion of a [heat pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pumps). Latent heat is typically stored in ice tanks or what are called [phase-change materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-change_material) (PCMs).
