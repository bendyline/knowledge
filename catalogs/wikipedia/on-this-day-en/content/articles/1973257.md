**Rabih Alameddine** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ربيع علم الدين; born 1959) is a Lebanese painter and writer.[^cite_note-1] His 2021 novel *The Wrong End of the Telescope* won the 2022 [PEN/Faulkner Award for Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEN/Faulkner_Award_for_Fiction).[^cite_note-\:0-2] He also won the 2025 [National Book Award for Fiction](30511875.md) for his novel *[The True True Story of Raja the Gullible (and His Mother)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_True_True_Story_of_Raja_the_Gullible_\(and_His_Mother\)).*[^cite_note-3]

## Early life

Alameddine was born in [Amman](45007.md), Jordan to [Lebanese Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Druze)[^cite_note-4] parents. (Alameddine identifies as an atheist).[^cite_note-5]

He grew up in Kuwait and Lebanon, which he left at age 17 to live first in England and then in California to pursue higher education. He earned a degree in engineering from the [University of California at Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Los_Angeles) (UCLA) and a Master of Business in San Francisco. Alameddine is gay.[^cite_note-6]

## Career

Alameddine began his career as an engineer, then moved to writing and painting. His debut novel *Koolaids*, which touched on both the [AIDS epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_epidemic) in [San Francisco](49728.md) and the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), was published in 1998 by [Picador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picador_\(imprint\)).[^cite_note-7]

The author of six novels and a collection of short stories, Alameddine was the recipient of a Guggenheim Fellowship in 2002. His queer sensibility has added a different slant to narratives about immigrants within the context of what became known as [Orientalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalism).[^cite_note-8]

He has lived in San Francisco and [Beirut](37428.md).[^cite_note-9]

## Awards and honors

In 2014, Alameddine was a finalist for the [National Book Critics Circle Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Critics_Circle_Award) and he won the [California Book Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Book_Awards) Gold Medal Fiction for *[An Unnecessary Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Unnecessary_Woman)*.[^cite_note-nbcc2014-10][^cite_note-The_Commonwealth_Club-11]

Alameddine is best known for this novel, which tells the story of Aaliya, a Lebanese woman and translator living in war-torn [Lebanon](17771.md). The novel "manifests traumatic signposts of the \[Lebanese] civil war, which make it indelibly situational, and accordingly latches onto complex psychological issues."[^cite_note-12]

In 2017, Alameddine won the [Arab American Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_American_Book_Award) and the [Lambda Literary Award for Gay Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_Literary_Award_for_Gay_Fiction) for *The Angel of History.*[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In 2018 he was teaching in the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia)'s creative writing program, in [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia).[^cite_note-15]

He was shortlisted for the 2021 [Sunday Times Short Story Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Times_Short_Story_Award) for his story, "The July War".[^cite_note-16]

Alameddine's novel *[The Wrong End of the Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wrong_End_of_the_Telescope?action=edit\&redlink=1)* won the 2022 [PEN/Faulkner Award for Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEN/Faulkner_Award_for_Fiction).[^cite_note-\:0-2] His novel *The True True Story of Raja the Gullible (and His Mother)* won the 2025 National Book Award for Fiction.[^cite_note-\:17-17][^cite_note-18]
