The **Government of India** ([Devanagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari): भारत सरकार; [IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alphabet_of_Sanskrit_Transliteration): *Bhārata Sarakāra*; legally, the **Union Government**, the **Union of India**[^cite_note-2] or the **Central Government**) is the national authority of the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India), established in accordance with the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India). As outlined in [Part I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_I_of_the_Constitution_of_India), India is a "Union of States", a term often used interchangeably to refer to the "Union Government" or the Government of India, representing the central authority. India is a [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic) led by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India), who, as the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), holds formal executive power. Dictated by [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_elections_of_India), the president appoints the [prime minister](24452.md) as the head of government, along with other ministers.

India is divided into [28 states and 8 union territories](375986.md), making a total of 36 [subnational entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnational_entities). The governance of these entities is described in the Constitution as a quasi-federal system, blending elements of both [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism) and [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) governments. While the [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_and_union_territory_capitals_in_India) are self-governing administrative divisions with their own [state governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_India), the [union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) are directly governed by the Union Government, under the administration of a lieutenant governor or an appointed administrator, who is chosen by the president of India. India operates under a [dual federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_federalism) system, where the Constitution outlines the powers and limitations of both the states and union territories.

The government's formal seat is at [Parliament House](67019017.md) in [New Delhi](51585.md). It has three primary branches: the legislature, the executive, and the judiciary. The Constitution of India vests legislative power in the bicameral [Parliament of India](410238.md),[^cite_note-3] executive power in the [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers),[^cite_note-\:6-4] and judicial power in the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md),[^cite_note-5] with the president as head of state.[^cite_note-\:7-6] It is a derivation of the British [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system) and has a federal structure.[^cite_note-\:8-7] The government is formed by  the prime minister and ministers, who are [members of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)) and belong to the Union Council of Ministers, with the prime minister at its head. The Union Council of Ministers is the peak decision-making body within the executive branch responsible for advising the president on executive policy.[^cite_note-\:7-6][^cite_note-8]

The Union Council of Ministers is responsible to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) (lower house), as is the Cabinet, in accordance with the principles of [responsible government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsible_government).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-\:8-7] As is the case in most parliamentary systems, the government is [dependent on Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_of_powers) to legislate, and [general elections](1435365.md) are held every five years to elect a new Lok Sabha. The most recent election was in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_general_election). After an election, the president generally selects as prime minister the leader of the party or alliance most likely to command the confidence of the majority of the Lok Sabha. If the Prime Minister is not a member of either house of parliament upon appointment, they are given six months to be elected or appointed to Parliament.[^cite_note-10]

## History

### British colonial rule: (1857–1947)

Main article: [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj)



[Council House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Parliament_House,_New_Delhi) in [New Delhi](51585.md), the seat of the former [Imperial Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Legislative_Council) and home to the [Parliament of India](410238.md) till 2023

The first seeds of elected representative government were sown during [British colonial rule in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), in particular by the [Indian Councils Act 1909](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Councils_Act_1909), commonly known as the [Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Morley)-[Minto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Elliot-Murray-Kynynmound,_4th_Earl_of_Minto) reforms. The Act introduced elections to the [Imperial Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Legislative_Council) (then the unicameral Legislature for British India). Before that, governance was carried out by an all-European Legislative Council and the [Viceroy's Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy's_Executive_Council). As such, no Indians were represented in high levels of government before 1909.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

India's current bicameral [Parliament](410238.md) has its roots in the [Government of India Act 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1919), commonly known as the [Montagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Montagu)-[Chelmsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Thesiger,_1st_Viscount_Chelmsford) reforms, which introduced a greatly expanded [Imperial Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Legislative_Council) and the system of diarchy in provinces.[^cite_note-13] It comprised a lower house, the [Central Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Legislative_Assembly), with 145 members (of which 104 were elected and 41 nominated); and an upper house, the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(India\)), with 60 members (of which 34 were elected and 26 nominated).[^cite_note-14]

The next structural modification to the governance of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), the [Government of India Act 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935) (the longest ever [Act of Parliament of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament_\(United_Kingdom\)) until this record was surpassed by the [Greater London Authority Act 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_London_Authority_Act_1999)) introduced provincial autonomy and laid the foundation for a federal structure in India.[^cite_note-15] It also led to the establishment of important institutions such as the [Union Public Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Public_Service_Commission). However, it was unpopular among Indians as it did not grant full self-rule or [dominion status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion).[^cite_note-16]

### Dominion: (1947–1950)

Main article: [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India)



[Governor-General of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_India) [C. Rajagopalachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Rajagopalachari) declaring India a republic at [Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrapati_Bhavan,_New_Delhi) on [26 January 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day)

Between midnight on [15 August 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(India\)) and [26 January 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day_\(India\)), India was an independent, self-governing [dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) with a [Prime Minister](24452.md) and a [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_India) as the viceregal representative of the head of state, [George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI). Its unicameral legislature, the [Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India), was tasked with drafting the country's constitution.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

### Republic: (1950–present)

The [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) came into effect on [26 January 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day_\(India\)), making India a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) with a [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) as head of state, replacing the monarch and his viceregal representative, the [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_India). It established a parliamentary system of government and a federal structure with a strong centre.[^cite_note-19]

It was based on the [Government of India Act 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935), and also drew inspiration from several other constitutions including those of the United Kingdom, United States, Ireland, Canada, and France.[^cite_note-20]

India today is the world's largest [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), with a federal parliamentary system that has evolved significantly since independence.[^cite_note-21]

## Nomenclature

The full name of the country is the *Republic of India*. India and Bharat are equally official short [names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_for_India) for the Republic of India in the Constitution,[^cite_note-22] and both names appear on legal banknotes, in treaties and in legal cases. The terms "Union government", "central government" and "*bhārat sarkār*" are often used officially and unofficially to refer to the government of India. The term *New Delhi* is commonly used as a [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonym) for the Union government,[^cite_note-23] as the seat of the central government is in [New Delhi](51585.md).

## Basic structure

The Government of India is modelled after the Westminster system.[^cite_note-24] The Union government is mainly composed of the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), the [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), and the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), and powers are vested by the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) in the [council of ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_India), [parliament](410238.md), and the [supreme court](395852.md), respectively. The [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md), while the [elected](1435365.md) [prime minister](24452.md) acts as the head of the executive and is responsible for running the Union government.[^cite_note-25] Parliament is [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) in nature, with the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) being the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house), and the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house). The judiciary systematically contains an apex supreme court, 25 [high courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_India), and hundreds of [district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Courts_of_India), all subordinate to the supreme court.[^cite_note-26]

The basic [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(common_law\)) and criminal laws governing the citizens of India are set down in major parliamentary legislation, such as the [civil procedure code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_Civil_Procedure_\(India\)), the [penal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Penal_Code), and the [criminal procedure code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_Criminal_Procedure_\(India\)).[^cite_note-27] Similar to the Union government, individual state governments each consist of executive, legislative and judiciary branches. The legal system as applicable to the Union and individual state governments is based on the English [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) and [Statutory Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_Law).[^cite_note-28]

## Legislature

Main article: [Parliament of India](410238.md)



[Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Parliament_House,_New_Delhi) in New Delhi

The powers of the legislature in India are exercised by [the Parliament](410238.md), a bicameral legislature consisting of the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) and the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). Of the two houses of parliament, the Rajya Sabha (or the 'Council of States') is considered to be the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) and consists of members appointed by the president and [elected](652583.md) by the [state and territorial legislatures](375986.md). The Lok Sabha (or the 'House of the People') is considered the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house).[^cite_note-29]

The parliament does not have complete control and [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_sovereignty), as its laws are subject to [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review) by the [Supreme Court](395852.md).[^cite_note-30] However, it does exercise some control over the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)). The members of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers), including [the prime minister](24452.md), are either chosen from parliament or elected there within six months of assuming office.[^cite_note-31] The council as a whole is [responsible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsible_government) to the Lok Sabha.[^cite_note-32] The Lok Sabha is a temporary house and can be dissolved only when the party in power loses the support of the majority of the house. The Rajya Sabha is a permanent house and can never be dissolved. The members of the Rajya Sabha are elected for a six-year term.[^cite_note-33]

## Executive

The [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch) of government has authority and responsibility to implement laws enacted by the legislature, and for the daily administration of the state through the civil service (the bureaucracy). The separation of various types of government power, its constraint and its sharing among the separate branches of government is central to the democratic idea of the [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers).[^cite_note-34]

### President

Main article: [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India)



[President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) [Droupadi Murmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droupadi_Murmu) (center), [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_India) [C. P. Radhakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._P._Radhakrishnan) (left), and [Prime Minister](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md)

The executive power is vested mainly in the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India), as per Article 53(1) of the constitution. The president has all [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) powers and exercises them directly or through subordinate officers as per the aforesaid Article 53(1). The president is to act following aid and advice tendered by the prime minister, who leads the Council of Ministers as described in [Article 74 of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_74_\(Constitution_of_India\)).[^cite_note-35]

The council of ministers remains in power during the 'pleasure' of the president. However, in practice, the council of ministers must retain the support of the Lok Sabha. If a president were to dismiss the council of ministers on her or his own initiative (ie, without advice to do so from the prime minister), it might trigger a constitutional crisis. Thus, in practice, the Council of Ministers cannot be dismissed as long as it holds the support of a majority in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md).[^cite_note-36]

The president is formally responsible for appointing many high level government officials in India. These high officials include the [governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_of_states_of_India) of the 28 [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India); the [chief justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India); other judges of the [supreme court](395852.md) and [high courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_courts_in_India) (on the advice of other judges); the [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_India); the [comptroller and auditor general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptroller_and_Auditor_General_of_India); the [chief election commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_of_India) and other [election commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commissioner_of_India); the chairperson and members of the [Union Public Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Public_Service_Commission); the officers of the [All India Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Services) ([IAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service), [IFoS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Forest_Service) and [IPS](451884.md)) and [Central Civil Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Civil_Services) in group 'A'; officers of the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md); and [ambassadors](68651.md) and [high commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_commissioner_\(Commonwealth\)) to other countries; among many others.[^cite_note-Darpan2007-37][^cite_note-38] Such appointments are made 'on advice': that is, on the recommendation of ministers

The president, as the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), also receives the credentials of [ambassadors](68651.md) from other countries, while the [prime minister](24452.md), as [head of government](37570.md), receives credentials of high commissioners from other [members of the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), in line with historical tradition.[^cite_note-39]

The president is the *[de jure](43594.md)* [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md).[^cite_note-Oldenburg2010-40]

The president can pardon or reduce the sentence of a convicted person once, particularly in cases involving the punishment of death. Decisions involving pardons and certain other matters are made independently of the advice of the prime minister or the opinion of the Lok Sabha majority. In most other cases, however, the president exercises any executive power only on the advice of the prime minister.[^cite_note-Kumar-41]

### Vice president

Main article: [Vice President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_India)

The vice president is the second-highest constitutional position in India after the president. The vice president represents the nation in the absence of the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) and takes charge as acting president in the case of resignation, impeachment or removal of the president. The vice president also has the legislative function of acting as the chairperson of the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md).[^cite_note-42] The vice president is elected indirectly by members of an electoral college consisting of the members of both the houses of parliament.

### Prime minister

Main article: [Prime Minister of India](24452.md)



North and South Blocks of the [Central Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_Building,_New_Delhi) housing various [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ministries_and_departments_of_the_Government_of_India) of the Government of India, with the [Rashtrapati Bhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrapati_Bhavan,_New_Delhi) visible in the distance

The [prime minister of India](24452.md), as addressed in the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India), is the chief executive of the government and the leader of the majority party that holds a majority in the Lok Sabha. The prime minister leads the executive of the Government of India.[^cite_note-43]

The prime minister is the senior member of the cabinet in the executive government in a parliamentary system. The prime minister selects and can dismiss other members of the cabinet; allocates posts to members within the Government; is the presiding member and chairperson of the cabinet and is responsible for bringing a proposal of legislation. The resignation or death of the prime minister dissolves the cabinet.[^cite_note-44]

The prime minister is appointed by the president to assist the latter in the administration of the affairs of the executive.[^cite_note-45]

### Cabinet, ministries and agencies

Main articles: [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) and [List of central agencies in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_central_agencies_in_India)



The organizational structure of a department of the Government of India.

The [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) includes the prime minister, Cabinet Ministers and [Ministers of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State) (MoS).[^cite_note-46] Each minister must be a member of one of the houses of the parliament. The cabinet is headed by the [prime minister](24452.md), and is advised by the [cabinet secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_of_India), who also acts as the head of the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) and other civil services. Other members of the council are either union cabinet ministers, who are heads of various ministries; or ministers of state, who are junior members who report directly to one of the cabinet ministers, often overseeing a specific aspect of government; or ministers of state (independent charges), who do not report to a cabinet minister. As per article 88 of the constitution, every minister shall have the right to speak in, and to take part in the proceedings of, either house, any joint sitting of the houses, and any committee of parliament of which he may be named a member, but shall *not* be entitled to a vote in the house where he is not a member.

#### Secretaries

Main article: [Secretary to the Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_to_the_Government_of_India)

A [secretary to the Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_to_the_Government_of_India), a [civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service), generally an [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS) officer,[^cite_note-\:1-47][^cite_note-\:2-48][^cite_note-\:3-49][^cite_note-\:4-50] is the administrative head of the ministry or department, and is the principal adviser to the minister on all matters of policy and administration within the ministry/department.[^cite_note-Governance_India-51][^cite_note-CSMOP_India2-52] Secretaries to the Government of India rank 23rd on [Indian order of precedence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_order_of_precedence).[^cite_note-rajyasabha2-53][^cite_note-\:0-54][^cite_note-\:5-55][^cite_note-Maheshwari_2001_6662-56] Secretaries at the higher level are assisted by one or many [additional secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_secretary_to_Government_of_India), who are further assisted by [joint secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_secretary_to_Government_of_India).[^cite_note-CSMOP_India2-52] At the middle they are assisted by directors/deputy secretaries and under secretaries.[^cite_note-CSMOP_India2-52] At the lower level, there are section officers, assistant section officers, upper division clerks, lower division clerks and other secretarial staff.[^cite_note-CSMOP_India2-52]

### Civil services

Main article: [Civil Services of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Services_of_India)

The Civil Services of India are the civil services and the permanent [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy) of India. The [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) decisions are implemented by the Indian civil servants.[^cite_note-\:9-57]



[Cabinet Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_\(India\)) [Rajiv Gauba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gauba), an [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) officer meets with trainee officers of the [Indian Police Service](451884.md); both arms of the [All India Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Services)

In the [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) of India, the ultimate responsibility for running the administration rests with the elected representatives of the people which are the ministers. These ministers are accountable to the legislatures which are also elected by the people based on [universal adult suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage). The ministers are indirectly responsible to the people themselves. But the handful of ministers is not expected to deal personally with the various problems of modern administration. Thus the ministers lay down the policy and it is for the civil servants to enforce it.[^cite_note-\:9-57]

#### Cabinet secretary

Main article: [Cabinet Secretary of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_of_India)

The cabinet secretary ([IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alphabet_of_Sanskrit_Transliteration): *Maṃtrimaṇḍala Saciva*) is the top-most executive official and [senior-most civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Services_of_India) of the Government of India. The cabinet secretary is the *[ex-officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex-officio)* head of the Civil Services Board, the [Cabinet Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretariat_of_India), the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS) and the head of all civil services under the rules of business of the government.[^cite_note-58]

The cabinet secretary is generally the senior-most officer of the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service). The cabinet secretary ranks 11th on the [Indian order of precedence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_order_of_precedence).[^cite_note-rajyasabha2-53][^cite_note-\:0-54][^cite_note-\:5-55][^cite_note-Maheshwari_2001_6662-56] The cabinet secretary is under the direct charge of the [Prime Minister](24452.md). Presently, the [Cabinet Secretary of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_of_India) is V. Somanathan, IAS.[^cite_note-59]

## Judiciary

Main article: [Judiciary of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_India)

India's independent centralised judicial system began under the British, and its concepts and procedures resemble those of [Anglo-Saxon countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_countries). The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) consists of a [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India) and 37 associate justices, all appointed by the President on the recommendations of a [collegium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_system) of senior judges.[^cite_note-60] [Jury trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jury_trial) were abolished in the early 1960s, following *[KM Nanavati v. the State of Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KM_Nanavati_v_State_of_Maharashtra)*. However, the impact of the case on the abolition of jury trials is disputed.[^cite_note-61]

Unlike its United States counterpart, the Indian justice system consists of a unitary system at both state and union levels. The judiciary consists of the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md), [High Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_India) at the state level, and [District Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Courts_of_India) and [Sessions Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sessions_Court) at the [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_India) level, in addition to various [tribunals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribunals_in_India) and [special courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Courts_\(India\)).&#x20;

### Supreme Court

Main article: [Supreme Court of India](395852.md)

Complex of the Supreme Court of India in New Delhi

Complex of the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) in [New Delhi](51585.md)

The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) is situated in [New Delhi](51585.md), the capital region of [India](14533.md).

The Supreme Court is the highest judicial forum and final court of appeal under the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India), the highest constitutional court, with the power of [constitutional review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_review). Consisting of the [Chief Justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India) and 37 sanctioned other judges, it has extensive powers in the form of [original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction), [appellate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_jurisdiction) and [advisory jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_jurisdictions).[^cite_note-62]

As the [final court of appeal](643.md) of the country, it takes up appeals primarily against verdicts of the [High Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_courts_in_India) of various states and other courts and tribunals. It safeguards [fundamental rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_rights) of citizens and settles disputes between various governments in the country. As an advisory court, it hears matters that may specifically be referred to it under the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) by the President. It also may take cognisance of matters on its own (or '[suo moto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sua_sponte)'). The law declared by the Supreme Court becomes binding on all courts within India and also on the union and state governments.[^cite_note-History_PDF-63] Per [Article 142](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution%20of%20India/Part%20V), it is the duty of the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) to enforce the decrees of the Supreme Court.

In addition, Article 32 of the Constitution gives extensive original jurisdiction to the Supreme Court concerning enforcement of fundamental rights. It is empowered to issue directions, orders, or writs, including [writs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ) in the nature of *[habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus)*, *[mandamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandamus)*, *[prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition)*, *[quo warranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quo_warranto)* and *[certiorari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certiorari)* to enforce them. The Supreme Court has been conferred with the power to direct the transfer of any civil or criminal matter from one High Court to another, or from a court subordinate to itself or a High Court. Although proceedings in the Supreme Court arise out of judgments or orders of subordinate courts, since the early 1970s the Supreme Court has started adjudicating matters of national importance, or matters of public interest. This may be done by any individual or group of persons either by filing a *[writ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ) petition* at the filing counter of the court or by addressing a letter to the [Chief Justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India), highlighting the question of public importance for redress. These are known as [public interest litigations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_interest_litigation_in_India).[^cite_note-64]

## Elections and voting

Main articles: [Elections in India](1435365.md) and [Politics of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India)

See also: [Election Commission of India](47863083.md)

India has a [quasi-federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism) form of government, called "union" or "central" government,[^cite_note-65] with elected officials at the union, state and local levels. At the national level, the [head of government](37570.md), the [prime minister](24452.md), is appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) from the party or coalition that has the majority of seats in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). The members of the Lok Sabha are directly elected for a term of five years by [universal adult suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) through a [first-past-the-post](261709.md) voting system. Members of the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), which represents the [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_India), are elected by the members of State legislative assemblies by [proportional representation](23298.md), except for 12 members who are nominated by the president.

The [Election Commission of India](47863083.md) conducts [national](410238.md) and [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislatures_of_India) elections, while [State Election Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_election_commission_\(India\)) conduct [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_India) elections.

India is currently the largest democracy in the world, with around 900 million eligible voters, as of 2019.[^cite_note-66]

## State and local governments

Main articles: [State governments of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_India), [Union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory), and [Local government in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_India)

In India, power is divided between the governments of the union and the [states of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_India),[^cite_note-67] the latter being ruled by the [chiefs ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_\(India\)).[^cite_note-68] The state legislature is [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) in [six states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_of_India_by_type_of_legislature) and [unicameral](204304.md) in the rest.[^cite_note-69] The lower house is elected with a five-year term, while in the upper house one-third of the members in the house gets elected every two years with six-year terms.

[Union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) are administered directly by the union government through an [Administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_government) or [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_-_India), though some have legislatures and elected governments with limited powers.[^cite_note-70]

Local governments function at the basic level. It is the third level of government apart from union and state governments. It consists of [panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_self-government_in_India) in rural areas and [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_governance_in_India) in urban areas. They are elected directly or indirectly by the people.

Local governments, comprising [Panchayati Raj Institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj_in_India) in rural areas and [Urban Local Bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_governance_in_India) in urban areas, function under the [73rd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-third_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_India) and [74th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_India) Constitutional Amendments to promote democratic decentralization. Each state also enacts its own legislation to regulate the structure and functioning of local governments.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

## Finance

See also: [Reserve Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Bank_of_India)

### Taxation

Main article: [Taxation in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_India)



[Reserve Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Bank_of_India)'s headquarters in Mumbai, India's financial capital

India has a three-tier tax structure, wherein the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) empowers the union government to levy [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax), tax on capital transactions ([wealth tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_tax), [inheritance tax](5824627.md)), [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax), service tax, customs and [excise](11404033.md) duties and the [state governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India) to levy sales tax on intrastate sale of goods, taxon [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_tax) and [professions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profession), excise duties on manufacture of [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage), [stamp duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_duty) on transfer of property and collect land revenue (levy on land owned). The [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_governments) are empowered by the state government to levy [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) and charge users for [public utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utilities) like [water supply](684928.md), [sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage) etc.[^cite_note-angela-73] More than half of the revenues of the union and state governments come from taxes, of which 3/4 come from [direct taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_taxes). More than a quarter of the union government's tax revenues are shared with the state governments.[^cite_note-sharing-74]

The tax reforms, initiated in 1991, have sought to rationalise the tax structure and increase compliance by taking steps in the following directions:

- Reducing the rates of individual and corporate income taxes, excises, and customs and making it more progressive

- Reducing exemptions and concessions

- Simplification of laws and procedures

- Introduction of [permanent account number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_account_number) (PAN) to track monetary transactions

- 21 of the 29 states introduced [value added tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_added_tax) (VAT) on 1 April 2005 to replace the complex and multiple sales tax system

  [^cite_note-angela-73]

  [^cite_note-VAT-75]

The non-tax revenues of the central government come from [fiscal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) services, interest receipts, public sector dividends, etc., while the non-tax revenues of the States are grants from the central government, interest receipts, dividends and income from general, economic and social services.[^cite_note-Datt-3-76]

Inter-state share in the union tax pool is decided by the recommendations of the [Finance Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Commission) to the president.

Total tax receipts of Centre and State amount to approximately 18% of national GDP. This compares to a figure of 37–45% in the OECD.

### Union budget

Main article: [Union budget of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_budget_of_India)

The Finance minister of India usually presents the annual [union budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_budget_of_India) in the [parliament](410238.md) on the last [working day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_day) of February. However, for the F.Y. 2017–18, this tradition had been changed. Now the budget will be presented on the 1st day of February. The budget has to be passed by the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) before it can come into effect on 1 April, the start of India's [fiscal year](5752099.md). The Union budget is preceded by an [economic survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_survey_of_India) which outlines the broad direction of the budget and the economic performance of the country for the outgoing financial year[^cite_note-77]

India's non-development revenue expenditure had increased nearly five-fold in 2003–04 since 1990–91 and more than tenfold from 1985 to 1986. Interest payments are the single largest item of expenditure and accounted for more than 40% of the total non-development expenditure in the 2003–04 budget. Defence expenditure increased fourfold during the same period and has been increasing to defend from a difficult neighbourhood and external terror threats. In 2025–26, India's defence budget stood at ₹6.81 trillion (equivalent to ₹7.0 trillion or US\$73 billion in 2026).

## Issues

### Corruption

Main article: [Corruption in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_India)

In 2009, several ministers are accused of corruption and nearly a quarter of the 543 elected members of parliament had been charged with crimes, including murder.[^cite_note-78] Many of the biggest scandals since 2010 have involved high-level government officials, including cabinet ministers and chief ministers, such as the [2010 Commonwealth Games scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerns_and_controversies_at_the_2010_Commonwealth_Games) (₹700 billion (equivalent to ₹1.7 trillion or US\$18 billion in 2026)), the [Adarsh Housing Society scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adarsh_Housing_Society_scandal), the [Coal allocation scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_coal_allocation_scam) (₹1.86 trillion (equivalent to ₹4.6 trillion or US\$47 billion in 2026)), the [mining scandal in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_scams_in_India#Illegal_iron_ore_mining_in_Karnataka) and the [cash-for-votes scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash-for-votes_scandal).

### Administrative and bureaucratic delays

The Indian bureaucracy suffers from indecision and risk aversion.[^cite_note-79] In 2023, a [Moody's](1705862.md) report noted that a lack of certainty around the time needed for land-acquisition approvals, regulatory clearances, obtaining licences, and establishing businesses can "materially prolong" project timelines in India.[^cite_note-80]
