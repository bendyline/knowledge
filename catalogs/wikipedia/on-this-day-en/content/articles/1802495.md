The title of patriarchs of the East is used by [primates](24672.md) of several [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination) within [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity). Historically, the title originated as ecclesiastical designation for primates of the [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalker1985172:_"this_church_had_as_its_head_a_"catholicos"_who_came_to_be_styled_"Patriarch_of_the_East"_and_had_his_seat_originally_at_Seleucia-Ctesiphon_(after_775_it_was_shifted_to_Baghdad)"-1] It was, and still is, officially used by different branches of the historical Church of the East.

Besides this, the patriarchate of Antioch has a title of '*Patriarch of Antioch and All The East'*. In this context, '*All The East*' denotes the [Diocese of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_the_East), also called the Diocese of Orients of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), and not in the same perspective of the Church of the East. This title is held by the [Melkite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite), [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox), [Syriac Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox) and the [Syriac Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic) patriarchs of Antioch, and also by primates of some other sees, belonging to several Eastern Christian denominations.

## Catholic Patriarchs of the East

The [Catholic](606848.md) Patriarchs of the East are generally speaking the head [bishops](4092.md) of some of the autonomous [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md). Each [patriarch](175409.md) of the east has authority over all bishops of a [particular eastern rite church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church). These patriarchs are elected by their [synods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synod), and must extend [communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_communion) to and receive it from the other patriarchs, including the [pope](23056.md), before officially taking their office. In matters of discipline and practice, but not in matters of [dogma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma), they generally follow the customs and laws of their particular church. Perhaps the most striking example is that in most Eastern Catholic Churches, [ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Orders) of married men to the priesthood is routine (although no priest may marry *after* ordination, and only celibate priests may become bishops). Eastern churches that are not headed by patriarchs are instead headed by bishops who are titled [major archbishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_archbishop), [metropolitans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop), or in a few cases merely [eparchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eparch).
