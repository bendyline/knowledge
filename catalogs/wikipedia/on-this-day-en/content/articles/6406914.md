The **India–Pakistan cricket rivalry** is one of the most intense [sports rivalries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_rivalry) in the world.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Matches between the teams are considered some of the biggest in the world and are among the most-viewed in all of sports.

[Across all formats](#Results), India and Pakistan have played each other 213 times, with Pakistan winning 88 matches and India winning 82. In Tests and ODIs, Pakistan has won more matches than India, while in T20Is, India has won more matches than Pakistan. Across all [ICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) World Cups, the two teams have faced each other 17 times, with India winning 16 matches and Pakistan winning 1 match. Both teams have won the [ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup#Titles_summary), the [ICC T20 World Cup](8258172.md#Team_performance), and the [ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Champions_Trophy#Overview). In total, India has won 8 ICC titles, while Pakistan has won 3.

The [tense relations between the two nations](4500730.md), resulting from bitter diplomatic relations and conflict that originated during the [Partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India) into India and Pakistan in 1947, the [Indo-Pakistani Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_Wars), [Kashmir conflict](3030955.md), and [Pakistani state sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_state-sponsored_terrorism) laid the foundations for the emergence of a fierce sporting rivalry between the two nations who had shared a common cricketing heritage.[^cite_note-3]

The two teams first [played in 1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_1952%E2%80%9353), when [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_cricket_team) toured [India](407754.md). Since then numerous [Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) series and, later, [One Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) (ODI) series have been played, although a number of planned tours by both teams have been cancelled or aborted due to political factors. No cricket was played between the two countries from 1962 to 1977 due to two major wars in 1965 and 1971, and the [1999 Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) and the [2008 Mumbai terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_terrorist_attacks) also interrupted cricketing ties between the two nations.[^cite_note-gran14jun19-4][^cite_note-tory16jun19-5]

In the 1980s and 1990s, the growth of large [expatriate populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_diaspora) from both countries across the world led to [neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_country) venues to host bilateral and multilateral ODI series featuring the two teams. In addition, there has always been high demand for tickets for the matches between the two in global ICC competitions, with over 800,000 ticket applications made for their meeting in the [2019 ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_ICC_Cricket_World_Cup);[^cite_note-gran14jun19-4][^cite_note-tory16jun19-5] the television transmission of the match was watched by 273 million viewers.[^cite_note-6]

Players from both teams routinely face extreme pressure to win and are threatened by extreme reactions in defeat. Extreme fan reactions to defeats in key matches have been recorded, with a limited degree of [hooliganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooliganism).[^cite_note-gran14jun19-4] At the same time, India–Pakistan matches have also offered opportunities for [cricket diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_diplomacy) as a means to improve relations between the two countries, allowing heads of state and cricket followers from either country to travel to the other to watch the matches.[^cite_note-tory16jun19-5]

The last full bilateral tour between the teams was [Pakistan's tour of India in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_2007%E2%80%9308), where both Test and ODI series were played. However, following the [2008 terror attacks in Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks), orchestrated by Pakistan-based terror group [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md), India suspended the planned 2009 series and all future engagements with Pakistan.[^cite_note-Herman-7] The attack eventually led to detrimental consequences for both nations, in diplomacy and cricket.[^cite_note-timesofindia.indiatimes.com-8] Since then, the two teams have only met in ICC and ACC tournaments, with India winning most of their meetings. In 2025, following their Asia Cup match, Indian captain [Suryakumar Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suryakumar_Yadav) stated that the India–Pakistan matchup was "not a rivalry anymore".[^cite_note-9] Pakistan's last victory against India came in [2022 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asia_Cup#Super_Four).[^cite_note-10]

## History

See also: [India–Pakistan relations](4500730.md) and [Cricket in South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_South_Asia)

The [Partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947 that led to the creation of independent Indian and Pakistani states was characterised by bloody conflict between ethnic groups that left one million people dead and led to the mass-migration of an estimated ten million people between either nation. The legacy of Partition and subsequent territorial disputes has contributed to intense rivalries in [field hockey](10886.md), [association football](10568.md), and especially [cricket](25675557.md), which was introduced during [British colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) and is the most popular sport in both countries.[^cite_note-11]



Captains [Abdul Kardar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hafeez_Kardar) of Pakistan (left) and [Lala Amarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lala_Amarnath) of India (right) with Indian President [Rajendra Prasad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Prasad) (center) on the day of the first test, 16 October 1952

Pakistan became a member of the **Imperial Cricket Conference** (now the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council)) in 1948,[^cite_note-12] becoming a Full Member[^cite_note-13] in July 1952.[^cite_note-14] Their tour of India later the same year saw the team play their first Test matches. They lost the first Test in [Delhi](37756.md) to India, but won the second Test in [Lucknow](392095.md), which led to an angry reaction from the home crowd against the Indian players. India clinched the Test series after winning the third Test in [Bombay](19189.md), but the intense pressure affected the players of both teams to the point that they pursued mainly defensive tactics that led to drawn matches and whole series without a victory.[^cite_note-15] When India toured Pakistan in 1955, thousands of Indian fans were granted visas to go to the Pakistani city of [Lahore](125315.md) to watch the Test match, but both the 1955 series and Pakistan's tour of India in 1961 ended in drawn series, with neither team being able to win a single Test match. Complaints about the fairness of umpires became routine.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

The [Indo-Pakistani War of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1965) and subsequent [War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1971) put a hold on matches between the two teams that lasted till 1978, when [India toured Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_1978%E2%80%9379) and cricket between the two countries resumed for a brief period.[^cite_note-18] In the post-1971 period, politics became a direct factor in the holding of cricketing events. India has suspended cricketing ties with Pakistan several times following terrorist attacks or other hostilities. The resumption of cricketing ties in 1978 came with the emergence of heads of government in both India and Pakistan who were not directly connected with the 1971 war and coincided with their formal initiatives to normalise bilateral relations.[^cite_note-19]

In the late 1980s and for most of the 1990s, India and Pakistan played each other only at neutral venues such as [Sharjah](210589.md) in the United Arab Emirates and in the Canadian city of [Toronto](64646.md), where large audiences of expatriates regularly watched matches between the teams.[^cite_note-20] The series between the teams in Canada in the 1990s and early 2000s were officially known as the "Friendship Cup". Sharjah, even though a neutral venue, was considered as the "back yard of Pakistan" given the close proximity and the massive support the team generated.[^cite_note-21]

The rise of multinational competitions, such as the [ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup), [ICC T20 World Cup](8258172.md), [ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Champions_Trophy), and the [Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Cup) led to more regular, albeit briefer, contests between the two teams.[^cite_note-22]



[Anil Kumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Kumble) became the second bowler in cricket history to take ten wickets in an innings in a match against Pakistan in 1999

In 1999, immediately following [Indian Prime Minister](24452.md) [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee)'s historic visit to Pakistan, the Pakistani team toured India for a series of Test matches and One Day Internationals. The [2 match test series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_1998%E2%80%9399) was drawn 1-1, and during the 2nd innings of second test, [Anil Kumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Kumble) took all 10 wickets, becoming the first Indian bowler to achieve the feat.[^cite_note-23] The [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) later in the year caused tensions between the countries and cricket was again suspended.

### 21st century

Vajpayee's peace initiative of 2003 led to India touring Pakistan after a gap of almost 15 years. During the [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2003%E2%80%9304) tour to Pakistan, [Virender Sehwag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virender_Sehwag) scored his first triple century in the first Test, scoring 309 from 375 balls, and was awarded player of the match.[^cite_note-24] The 2004 series was won by India, 2–1 in Test matches and 3–2 in ODI matches.[^cite_note-25] Subsequent exchange tours were held from March to April 2005 in India, January to February 2006 in Pakistan, and November to December 2007 in India. The [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_2004%E2%80%9305) Test series ended in a 1–1 draw, while the ODI series was won by Pakistan 4–2.[^cite_note-26] The [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2005%E2%80%9306) Test series was won by Pakistan 1–0, with the first 2 Test matches ending in a draw, while India won the ODI series 4–1.[^cite_note-27] The [2007 series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_2007%E2%80%9308) was won by India in both Test and ODI formats, by margins of 1–0 and 3–2, respectively.[^cite_note-28] The 2007 series would remain as the last full bilateral series played between India and Pakistan.

The [2008 Mumbai terror attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks) on 26–29 November 2008 by the Pakistan-based terrorist group [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md) dealt a major blow to the diplomatic and cricketing ties between India and Pakistan. It led to the suspension of India's planned tour of Pakistan in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2008%E2%80%9309) and all future engagements in Pakistan.[^cite_note-Herman-7] Since then, India has refused to play any bilateral series against Pakistan, meeting only at Asian and ICC events.[^cite_note-timesofindia.indiatimes.com-8] Furthermore, Pakistani players were [excluded](13260340.md#Exclusion_of_Pakistani_and_Bangladeshi_players) from the [Indian Premier League](13260340.md), following which their [contracts were terminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2009_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes#Withdrawals), with the inaugural season being the only one where they participated.[^cite_note-29]



Play from the last Test Match meeting between India and Pakistan, Bangalore, [December 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_2007%E2%80%9308).

The [2009 attack on the Sri Lanka national cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_attack_on_the_Sri_Lanka_national_cricket_team) in Lahore led to the suspension of international tours of Pakistan, and no Test series were played in the country for a decade.[^cite_note-30] Pakistan was also removed as a co-host for the [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup) which had been due to be played across the Indian subcontinent.[^cite_note-31] India and Pakistan qualified for the first semi-final of the tournament and the Indian government invited the Pakistani Prime Minister [Yousuf Raza Gilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Raza_Gilani) to watch the match along with his Indian counterpart, [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh).[^cite_note-32]



The second match of the series, Kolkata, [January 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_2012%E2%80%9313).



India and Pakistan during the [2013 ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_ICC_Champions_Trophy).

Bilateral ties finally resumed when the [Board of Control for Cricket in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Control_for_Cricket_in_India) (BCCI) invited the Pakistan national team to tour India for three ODIs and two T20Is [in December 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_2012%E2%80%9313).[^cite_note-33] In June 2014, the [Pakistan Cricket Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Cricket_Board) (PCB) announced that an agreement to play six bilateral series across eight years between the two teams had reached.[^cite_note-34] After lengthy negotiations involving offers and counter-offers on the venues and scheduling of the first of these series in December 2015, the boards were unable to reach an agreement.[^cite_note-35] In May 2017, the BCCI accounted that it would need approval from the Indian government before a bilateral series could go ahead.[^cite_note-May17-01-36] There was no further progress, despite members of both boards meeting in Dubai to discuss the matter.[^cite_note-May17-02-37]

In October 2021, during the [T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Men's_T20_World_Cup), the teams played their 200th international match against each other.[^cite_note-38] Pakistan won the fixture by ten wickets, their first in 13 attempts against India in World Cup tournaments of either format.[^cite_note-39]

In October 2021, following a meeting with the ACC, [Ramiz Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramiz_Raja) confirmed that Pakistan would host the [Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Cup) in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Asia_Cup), with Sri Lanka hosting the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asia_Cup) edition.[^cite_note-40] In October 2022, the [Board of Control for Cricket in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Control_for_Cricket_in_India) (BCCI) secretary and ACC President [Jay Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Shah) announced that India would not travel to Pakistan, citing security concerns, and that the Asia Cup 2023 would take place in a neutral venue.[^cite_note-41] In December 2022, the then PCB chairman [Ramiz Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramiz_Raja) said that Pakistan might consider pulling out of the tournament if their hosting rights were withdrawn because of India's unwillingness to travel to Pakistan.[^cite_note-42] The PCB had threatened to boycott the [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup) in India after the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) refused to send a team to the Asia Cup.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] This issue was later resolved, and Pakistan eventually participated in the [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup) in India.



[Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli) is the only player to win [Player of the Match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_of_the_Match_awards_\(cricket\)) vs Pakistan in all ICC formats events

In January 2023, ACC confirmed the teams and groups of the Asia Cup, with both India and Pakistan taking part.[^cite_note-45] In March 2023, it was proposed that Pakistan remain as hosts and that all India matches - including at least two India-Pakistan contests - would be played at a neutral venue yet to be confirmed.[^cite_note-46] The hybrid model proposed by Pakistan was rejected by Sri Lanka and Bangladesh.[^cite_note-47] In response, PCB Chairman [Najam Sethi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najam_Sethi) proposed two options. The first option was that India play all their matches at a neutral venue with Pakistan hosting the rest of the teams. The second option was that four matches in the group stage take place in Pakistan whereas the second phase, in which matches played by the Indian team followed by the next stage matches including the final, be played at a neutral venue. Sri Lanka and Bangladesh agreed to the second option.[^cite_note-48] On 15 June 2023, the [Asian Cricket Council](584073.md) announced that the tournament would be organized in a hybrid model with four matches being held in Pakistan, and the remaining nine in Sri Lanka.[^cite_note-49]

India and Pakistan met twice in the 2023 Asia Cup. Though the first match in the Group stage yielded a no result due to rain, India defeated Pakistan by an enormous margin in the Super Four clash between the 2 teams, scoring 356 for the loss of only 2 wickets. India in this match not only set their highest-ever score in ODI cricket against Pakistan, but also defeated them with the highest ever run margin of 228 runs, bundling Pakistan out for 128. India would eventually go on to win this Asia Cup, while Pakistan would be knocked out in the super-fours round.[^cite_note-50] India won the match against Pakistan at the [2024 Men's T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Men's_T20_World_Cup) by 6 runs, which was also the first ICC tournament to be held in [United States of America](3434750.md).[^cite_note-51] Then in 2025, India again defeated Pakistan; this time in the [2025 ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ICC_Champions_Trophy) by 6 wickets with Virat Kohli scoring his 51st century in ODI cricket.[^cite_note-52]

The [2025 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup) was a heavily tense situation for both teams, mostly due to the [Pahalgam terror attack](79778862.md) by Pakistan-based terror group [The Resistance Front](74821509.md) and the [military conflict](79896622.md) in the aftermath of the attack. Before the tournament, there were calls for boycott, but eventually the Government of India gave a go-ahead.[^cite_note-53] During the group stage, the Indian team led by [Suryakumar Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suryakumar_Yadav) refused to shake hands after defeating Pakistan, with Yadav dedicating the victory to the victims of Pahalgam attack and Indian Army.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Adil_2025-55] In the super four stage, Pakistani cricketers [Haris Rauf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haris_Rauf) and [Sahibzada Farhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahibzada_Farhan) made insensitive gestures in the match: while Rauf made a "jet crashing" gesture and a "6-0" finger sign, interpreted by media as a symbolic reference to Pakistan's claim of shooting down six Indian aircraft during [Operation Sindoor](79896622.md), Farhan celebrated his half-century by mimicking firing a rifle with his bat.[^cite_note-harisfine-56] Yadav and Rauf were fined 30% of their match fees, while Farhan received a warning.[^cite_note-harisfine-56] Following the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup_final), in which India defeated Pakistan again, a major controversy erupted as the Indian team refused to accept the winning trophy from [ACC](584073.md) president [Mohsin Naqvi](72818268.md), who also serves as [Pakistan's Interior Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Minister_of_Pakistan) and Chairman of the [PCB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Cricket_Board), and this led to a delay in handing out other awards.[^cite_note-57] Yadav later said the team had been "denied" the opportunity to lift the trophy and criticized the handling of the ceremony.[^cite_note-58] Naqvi handed the trophy to the UAE cricket authorities when the BCCI threatened to impeach him from the ACC presidency for misconduct.[^cite_note-e707-59]

Following the men's [Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup) victory, the BCCI asked the women's team to avoid shaking hands with the Pakistani women's team during the [2025 Women's Cricket World Cup](70509671.md).[^cite_note-60]

During the [2026 Men's T20 World Cup](69284234.md), the Pakistani team was reportedly asked by their Government not to play the initial group stage match with India, citing the ICC's refusal to relocate Bangladesh's matches out of India to Sri Lanka, due to the removal of [Mustafizur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafizur_Rahman) from the [2026 Indian Premier League](79331486.md).[^cite_note-61] However, the ICC warned the PCB of possible sanctions for a potential boycott of the match against India, and eventually went ahead.[^cite_note-62] During the coin toss, there was no handshake between the Indian captain [Suryakumar Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suryakumar_Yadav) and the Pakistani captain [Salman Ali Agha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salman_Ali_Agha), which was also followed after India had defeated Pakistan.[^cite_note-63]

## ICC matches

See also: [India at the Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_at_the_Cricket_World_Cup) and [Pakistan at the Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_at_the_Cricket_World_Cup)

In [ICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) Cricket World Cups, India and Pakistan have met eight times, with India winning all eight matches to lead 8–0. In the [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup), India and Pakistan met in a World Cup semi-final for the first time, with India winning by 29 runs. Their most recent meeting was in Ahmedabad in the [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup), where India defeated Pakistan by 7 wickets.[^cite_note-auto-69]

In ICC T20 World Cups, the two teams have faced each other nine times, with India leading the head-to-head record 8–1. In 2007, India and Pakistan met in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Twenty20_final) of the [inaugural T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Twenty20), with India winning the match by 5 runs. This was the first time the two teams met in an ICC final. In the [2021 T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ICC_Men's_T20_World_Cup#Group_2), Pakistan defeated India by 10 wickets, marking its first win over India in a T20 World Cup match.[^cite_note-wc21-70]

In the ICC Champions Trophy, India and Pakistan have played each other six times, with each team winning three matches. In 2017, India and Pakistan met in the [ICC Champions Trophy final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Champions_Trophy_final), which Pakistan won by 180 runs. The margin of victory was the largest in any ICC ODI tournament final in terms of runs.[^cite_note-71]

## ACC matches

India and Pakistan have met 21 times in the [ACC](584073.md) [Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Cup) across formats. India has won 13 of those matches, while Pakistan has won six, with two matches ending without a result due to rain.[^cite_note-ac87a-72][^cite_note-ac87-74] The two teams also played a Test match in the [1999 Asian Test Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Asian_Test_Championship), with Pakistan winning by 46 runs.

In the [2025 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup), India and Pakistan met three times, with India winning all three matches, including the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup_final) by 5 wickets. It was the first time the two teams had met in an Asia Cup final since the tournament's inception in 1984.

## ICC titles won

The [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) (ICC) is the world's governing body for cricket, responsible for organising and administering major international competitions. India and Pakistan have competed in all major ICC competitions, namely the Cricket World Cup, T20 World Cup, Champions Trophy, and World Test Championship.



Match between India and Pakistan at [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup)

In terms of ICC titles, India has won the [ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Cricket_World_Cup) twice, while Pakistan has won it once. In addition, India has won the [ICC T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Men's_T20_World_Cup) three times, including the inaugural [2007 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Twenty20), in which it defeated Pakistan in the final. Meanwhile, Pakistan has won the T20 World Cup once. India has also won the [ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Champions_Trophy) three times, while Pakistan won the 2017 edition, [defeating India in the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_ICC_Champions_Trophy_final). Neither team has secured the ICC World Test Championship, although India finished as runners-up in the first two editions.[^cite_note-76]

## T20I records

*As of 15th February 2026*

The two teams have played each other seventeen times in Twenty20 Internationals (T20Is). Nine of these matches have taken place in T20 World Cups, including the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Twenty20_final) of the [2007 T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_ICC_World_Twenty20), and six have taken place in Asia Cups, including the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup_final) of the [2025 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup). There was also a two-match T20I series that was played in India in 2012.

The highest team score in a T20I between the two teams is India's 192/5 in [Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Patel_Gujarat_Stadium) in 2012. Pakistan's highest T20I score against India is 182/5, made during the [2022 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asia_Cup). The lowest total is Pakistan's 83 all out in [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher-e-Bangla_National_Cricket_Stadium) at the [2016 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Asia_Cup).[^cite_note-97]

In India–Pakistan T20Is, the highest individual score is [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli)'s unbeaten 82, scored at the [MCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCG) during the [2022 T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Men's_T20_World_Cup). He also holds the record for the most runs in T20Is between the two teams.[^cite_note-98]

The best bowling figures in India–Pakistan T20Is are 4/18 by [Mohammad Asif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Asif_\(cricketer\)), recorded in their group stage match at the [2007 T20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Twenty20) in South Africa. Another notable performance is 4/30 by [Kuldeep Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuldeep_Yadav) in the [2025 Asia Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Asia_Cup_final).[^cite_note-99] Meanwhile, India's [Hardik Pandya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardik_Pandya) has taken the most wickets in T20Is between the two teams.

## Players who have played for both teams

India had already played international cricket prior to the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, having first played as an international team in 1932. Following partition, Pakistan was created and began playing as an independent national team, making their Test match debut in 1952 [during a tour of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_1952%E2%80%9353).

Three players played for Pakistan after appearing for India. They are:[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

- [Amir Elahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Elahi) – One Test for India against Australia at Sydney in 1947; five Tests for Pakistan against India in 1952

- [Gul Mohammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gul_Mohammad) – Eight Tests for India between 1946 and 1955; one Test for Pakistan in 1956

- [Abdul Hafeez Kardar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hafeez_Kardar) – Three Tests for India in 1946; 23 Tests for Pakistan between 1948 and 1958

After partition, Gul Mohammad continued to play for India until 1955 and played against Pakistan in their first tour of India in 1951–52. Both Amir Elahi and Abdul Hafeez Kardar played for Pakistan on the tour. Abdul Hafeez Kardar went on to play for Pakistan against India during [India's first tour of Pakistan in 1954–55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_1954%E2%80%9355).

## Public and government reaction to the rivalry

Cricket is a significant sport in both countries and matches between them can provoke what has been described as a strong response.[^cite_note-bbc3nov21-107]

During [India's tour of Pakistan in 1989–90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_1989%E2%80%9390), the 3rd ODI in Karachi was abandoned due to crowd disturbance.[^cite_note-108] When Pakistan lost 3 wickets at the score of 28 runs, stone pelting started against Indian fielders, who then gathered near the pitch. Local cricketer [Javed Miandad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javed_Miandad) was unable to calm the crowd and the match was abandoned.[^cite_note-109] [Chandu Borde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandu_Borde) said that in the same match, [Mohammad Azharuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Azharuddin) was hit with a metal hook.[^cite_note-110] [Sanjay Manjrekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjay_Manjrekar) wrote in his book that in the first ODI, Indian captain [Krishnamachari Srikkanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnamachari_Srikkanth)'s shirt was torn by a Pakistani spectator.[^cite_note-111]

There have been examples where fans of the opposing team have experienced legal action. In 2016, a 22-year-old Pakistani fan of Indian captain [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli), was arrested and later sentenced to 10-years imprisonment for hoisting India's flag in Pakistan after a match between India and Australia.[^cite_note-112]

In the city of [Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), tensions between Indian and Pakistani communities turned into violence and a series of protests following the [2022 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asia_Cup) match between Pakistan and India on 28 August.[^cite_note-bbc16sep22-113][^cite_note-bbc18sep22_1-114]

## Cricket diplomacy between India and Pakistan





The [Prime Minister of India](24452.md), [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh) and the [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md), [Yousuf Raza Gilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousuf_Raza_Gilani) interacting with the [Indian Cricket Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_national_cricket_team) and [Pakistani Cricket Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_cricket_team) ahead of the [India vs Pakistan semi-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup_knockout_stage#India_vs_Pakistan) in the [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup).

Main articles: [Cricket diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_diplomacy) and [India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_and_Pakistan)

Following the [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), and Soviet pressure on India to deflect the tension they faced, in February 1987 Pakistan's president at the time, General [Zia ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia-ul-Haq), attended a test match between India and Pakistan in [Jaipur](166537.md) – a visit that apparently helped cool a flare-up in tensions since it led to a meeting with the [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] Furthermore, in 2004 after a break of fifteen years, [India toured Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2003%E2%80%9304) in the wake of diplomatic initiatives to bury half a century of mutual hostility. Both teams relaxed their tough visa regulations for each other, allowing thousands of fans to travel across the border.[^cite_note-117]

In an attempt to replicate the cricket diplomacy of the past General [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) came to India in 2005 ostensibly for a cricket match. The trip, however, quickly took on the air of a summit as the teams were urged "to seize a historic chance to end their dispute over Kashmir."[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] Often this rivalry has been tinged with a religious-political bent to it. In 1991, the workers of the [Indian political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_India) [Shiv Sena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiv_Sena) dug up the cricket pitch at the [Wankhede Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wankhede_Stadium) in [Mumbai](19189.md) on the eve of an India-Pakistan [Test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) which was to be held there, forcing the entire series between the two nations to be cancelled.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] The Shiv Sena once again used this unique means of protest at the [Feroz Shah Kotla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feroz_Shah_Kotla) in [New Delhi](51585.md) in 2000 to protest against the Pakistan cricket team's proposed visit.[^cite_note-update.unu.edu-122] Following the [Kargil conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War), and at various other times, there have also been calls to suspend cricketing ties between the two countries.

During the [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup), [the semi-final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup_knockout_stage#India_vs_Pakistan) between India and Pakistan seemed to ease the tensions between the two countries, after the polarising [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks). [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh) had used the opportunity to ask his Pakistani counterpart [Yousuf Raza Gilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Raza_Gilani) to watch the match with him in Mohali, India. Gilani had subsequently accepted the offer and agreed to travel to India to watch the match with Singh.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

## In popular culture

The cricket rivalry between India and Pakistan has been portrayed in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) series *[The Greatest Rivalry: India vs Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_Rivalry:_India_vs_Pakistan).*[^cite_note-125] The series features interviews with [Virender Sehwag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virender_Sehwag), [Sourav Ganguly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourav_Ganguly), [Sunil Gavaskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Gavaskar), [Shoaib Akhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoaib_Akhtar), [Waqar Younis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqar_Younis), and [Javed Miandad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javed_Miandad), among others. It focuses on [Pakistan's tour of India in 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_India_in_1998%E2%80%9399), [India's tour of Pakistan in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cricket_team_in_Pakistan_in_2003%E2%80%9304), and the [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Indian_Premier_League) of [Indian Premier League](13260340.md).[^cite_note-126]
