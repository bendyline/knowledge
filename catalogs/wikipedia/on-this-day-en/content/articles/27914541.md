# China–Netherlands relations

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC) and the [Netherlands](21148.md) commenced official diplomatic relations in November 1954.[^cite_note-ce-1] In May 1972, diplomatic mission was increased to [ambassadorial](68651.md) level.[^cite_note-ce-1] On 11 May 1981, the diplomatic relations was downgraded to the [charge d'affaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_d'affaires) level due to the Dutch government ratifying the construction of two submarines for Taiwan by Dutch companies. Relations remained muted till 1 February 1984, when China and the Netherlands restored [ambassadorial](68651.md) diplomatic relations.[^cite_note-ce-1]

## History



Dutch Prime Minister [Mark Rutte](4321056.md) with Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) in December 2013

China-Dutch relations began prior to the founding of the People's Republic of China in the 17th and 18th century when Dutch traders of the [Verenigde Oostindische Compagnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verenigde_Oostindische_Compagnie) (VOC) setup trading post in [Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) and also in the western coast of [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan).[^cite_note-2] [Johan Nieuhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Nieuhof)'s account of the first VOC embassy to [Beijing](18603746.md) in 1655–1657—and the objections against it raised by members of the [Jesuit China Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_China_Mission)—became the influential and much-translated 1665 *[Embassy from the East-India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_from_the_East-India_Company)*.

China-Dutch relations contributed to the development of China's first nationality law, *Da Qing guoji taoli* (Nationality regulations of the Great Qing).[^cite_note-\:02-3]<sup>: 55–56 </sup> In 1907, the Dutch colonial government in the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) planned to institute a nationality law based on the *[jus soli](54035.md)* principle.[^cite_note-\:02-3]<sup>: 55 </sup> Relevant to relations with Qing-era China, the Dutch law would mean that all Chinese born in Dutch territory would be Dutch subjects.[^cite_note-\:02-3]<sup>: 55–56 </sup> The Qing government stated to the Dutch government that it would not accept a Dutch decision to treat Chinese in its territory as Dutch citizens.[^cite_note-\:02-3]<sup>: 56 </sup> In 1909, the Qing government instituted its nationality law to define its citizenship policy.[^cite_note-\:02-3]<sup>: 56 </sup>

### Cold War

During the [Korean War](16772.md), the Netherlands' [Regiment van Heutsz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment_van_Heutsz) under the [United Nations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Command) fought the PRC's [People's Volunteer Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Volunteer_Army) in multiple battles.[^cite_note-kwn-4]

PRC–Netherlands began in 1954 to establish the diplomatic relations of the charge d'affaires level. On 18 May 1972, Sino-Dutch relations were upgraded to the [ambassadorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassadorial) level.[^cite_note-5] In the 1980s Taiwan ordered two submarines from a Dutch shipyard which were delivered despite tremendous Chinese pressure.[^cite_note-6] China accused the Netherlands of colluding with American President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) and downgraded relations to the [charge d'affaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_d'affaires) level with the Netherlands on 11 May 1981, while threatening to do the same with the US.[^cite_note-7] In 1984, the Netherlands agreed not to export additional military goods in order to restore relations to the [ambassadorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassadorial) level.[^cite_note-8]

### 21st century

In March 2014, [Chinese Communist Party general secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) made the first [state visit](327283.md) of China to the Netherlands.[^cite_note-9]

In July 2019, the UN ambassadors from 22 nations, including Netherlands, signed a joint letter to the [UNHRC](635790.md) condemning China's alleged mistreatment of the [Uyghurs](31783.md) and other minorities, urging the Chinese government to close the [Xinjiang internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_internment_camps).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The PRC continues to deny the allegations, with support from several Muslim countries like [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Burkina Faso](3470.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Egypt](8087628.md). Many Chinese allies such as [Russia](25391.md), [Belarus](3457.md), [DPRK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPRK), [Cuba](5042481.md), [Angola](701.md), [Vietnam](202354.md) and [Laos](17752.md) have strongly supported China on the issue.

In February 2021, the [Dutch House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_House_of_Representatives) voted to recognize the Chinese government's treatment of its Uyghur Muslim minority as [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China), becoming the first country in the European Union to do so.[^cite_note-12]

In February 2024, the [Netherlands Defence Intelligence and Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Defence_Intelligence_and_Security_Service) and the [General Intelligence and Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Intelligence_and_Security_Service) stated that [Chinese state hackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_and_China) penetrated a Dutch military network the prior year.[^cite_note-13] The same month, Dutch trade minister [Geoffrey van Leeuwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_van_Leeuwen) justified export controls on [ASML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASML_Holding) tools to China on the grounds that they would be used for "high-value weapons systems and [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction)."[^cite_note-14] In April 2024, the Dutch Military Intelligence and Security Service stated that [Chinese spies](2440214.md) were actively targeting the Netherlands' semiconductor, aerospace and maritime industries in order to strengthen China's armed forces.[^cite_note-15]

In June 2024, the Dutch [Ministry of Defence](9473113.md) accused China of creating an "unsafe situation" when its fighter jets encircled Dutch frigate [HNLMS Tromp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Tromp_\(F803\)) in international waters in the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] In October 2024, the Dutch [National Coordinator for Security and Counterterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coordinator_for_Security_and_Counterterrorism) stated that Chinese state-backed cyberattacks had increasingly moved from cyberespionage to also prepare for sabotage.[^cite_note-18]

In May 2025, Dutch defense minister [Ruben Brekelmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_Brekelmans) stated that Chinese espionage on the Netherlands' semiconductor sector is "intensifying."[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In October 2025, the Dutch [Ministry of Economic Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economic_Affairs_\(Netherlands\)) took control of Nexperia's governance using the powers of the [Goods Availability Act](81327283.md), citing national security and European economic security.[^cite_note-SCMP13-10-25-21][^cite_note-22] The Chinese government subsequently banned Nexperia from exporting products that it makes in China.[^cite_note-23] In April 2026, the Dutch head of military intelligence stated that Chinese espionage is increasingly targeting Western military industries.[^cite_note-24] In May 2026, China's [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) stated that it used [electronic warfare](82272.md) attacks on the Dutch frigate *[De Ruyter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_De_Ruyter_\(F804\))* to drive it out of [disputed areas](32005536.md) in the [South China Sea](74209.md) near the [Paracel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracel_Islands).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Trade

Netherlands export to China includes petrochemicals, machinery, transport equipment, food, high technology and fossil fuels.[^cite_note-cbs-27] China's export to the Netherlands includes computer and consumer electronics, toys and clothes.[^cite_note-cbs-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Resident diplomatic missions



Embassy of the Netherlands in Beijing

- China has an embassy in [The Hague](30269.md).

- the Netherlands has an embassy in [Beijing](18603746.md) and consulates-general in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Shanghai](27643.md).

## Public opinion

A survey published in 2026 by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) found that 34% of Dutch people had a favorable view of China, while 59% had an unfavorable view. It also found that 49% of Dutch people in the 18-35 age group had positive opinions of China.[^cite_note-30]
