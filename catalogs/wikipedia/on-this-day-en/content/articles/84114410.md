A [by-election](155434.md) for the [United Kingdom parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_parliament) constituency of [Holborn and St Pancras](1344871.md) in London is due to be held on 8 October 2026,[^cite_note-1] following the [resignation from the House of Commons](192296.md) of former prime minister Sir [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) on 1 September 2026.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-resignation-bbc-3]

## Background

The seat is located in [Inner London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_London), in the [London Borough of Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Camden) and includes [Camden Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_Town) itself. Keir Starmer was first elected for [Holborn and St Pancras](1344871.md) in the [2015 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_United_Kingdom_general_election). He became [leader of the Labour Party](19043368.md) in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)), and led the party to a [landslide victory](41053306.md) at the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election). Starmer resigned as prime minister in 2026 and was replaced by the former mayor of Greater Manchester, [Andy Burnham](414818.md). On 1 September 2026, Starmer announced to the *[Camden New Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_New_Journal)* his intention to stand down as a member of parliament.[^cite_note-resignation-bbc-3] The same day, he was formally appointed as [Steward of the Chiltern Hundreds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steward_of_the_Chiltern_Hundreds), in order to vacate the seat.[^cite_note-4]

*[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that the seat was likely to be targeted by the [Green Party](79787.md), which finished third there at the 2024 general election with 10.4 per cent of the vote, behind [independent](2172378.md) candidate [Andrew Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Feinstein) in second place.[^cite_note-guardian-starmer-mp-5] The party's leader, [Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski), had previously stated, while Starmer was still PM, that he was "tempted" to challenge him in the seat.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

In the [2026 Camden London Borough Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Camden_London_Borough_Council_election), the Labour Party won the most votes (32.8%), with the Green Party in second place (27.1%),[^cite_note-\:2-7] though the Greens stood down in two wards for the Camden People's Alliance, a local left-wing party founded by Feinstein.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### Green Party

On 3 September, following speculation,[^cite_note-13] Green Party leader [Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski) announced his intention to stand in the by-election. Polanski said that he will remain as the party leader regardless of the outcome of the by-election.[^cite_note-Gecsoyler-14] On 7 September, Hamza Chowdhury, a Camden councillor for [Holborn and Covent Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holborn_and_Covent_Garden) ward, announced he would put himself forward for selection to be the party's candidate. He was nominated by 6 of the 10 other Green councillors in Camden,[^cite_note-15] who did so to give party members a choice as opposed to favouring Chowdhury over Polanski.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Following a formal selection meeting on 10 September, it was announced on 11 September that Polanski had been voted to be the party's candidate by members of the Camden Green Party.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Labour Party

Applications for the Labour Party candidacy were due by 4 September,[^cite_note-\:3-21] and the party received 96 applications.[^cite_note-22] On 7 September, the party announced a shortlist of three candidates, all Labour councillors on [Camden Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_London_Borough_Council): Sagal Abdi-Wali (who is also the leader of the council), Camron Aref-Adib, and Nasrine Djemai.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-\:4-24] Abdi-Wali, an ally of [Keir Starmer](18681491.md),[^cite_note-25] was selected as the candidate at a hustings meeting on 9 September.[^cite_note-26] The [Middle East Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Eye) has reported that Abdi-Wali "is unpopular among local pro-Palestine activists because Camden council has resisted calls to divest from companies allegedly complicit in Israel's [Gaza genocide](75668427.md)".[^cite_note-MEE-27]

### Other candidates

On 14 September, the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) announced Ewan Cameron, who was a Camden councillor for [Belsize ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belsize_\(ward\)) from 1998 to 2002, as its candidate.[^cite_note-28]

On 14 September, the [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) announced Patrick Stillman, a Camden councillor for [West Hampstead ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hampstead_\(ward\)), as their candidate.[^cite_note-29]

On 14 September, [Reform UK](59757202.md) announced Pete Newman, a former Metropolitan Police detective, as their candidate.[^cite_note-30]

On 14 September, [Workers Party of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_Party_of_Britain) leader [George Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Galloway) stated on his podcast *[The Mother of All Talk Shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mother_of_All_Talk_Shows)* that he would stand "unless the [Greens](79787.md) make an offer we can't refuse". At the time of announcement, Galloway was still living in "self-imposed [exile](9855.md)" in [Moscow](19004.md), [Russia](25391.md),[^cite_note-\:8-31] following his detention by counter-terrorism police in late 2025.[^cite_note-32] He confirmed later that day that he would stand as the Workers Party candidate.[^cite_note-\:7-33][^cite_note-\:6-34] Galloway has previously served as an MP seven times for five different constituencies, most recently for [Rochdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochdale_\(constituency\)) for three months in 2024 following a [by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rochdale_by-election).[^cite_note-\:6-34] [Chris Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Williamson_\(politician\)), a former MP, resigned as deputy leader of the Workers Party in protest at Galloway's decision to stand, and called for him to support Polanski's candidacy.[^cite_note-\:7-33] [James Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Giles_\(British_politician\)), who was Galloway's Chief of Staff when he was the MP for Rochdale, also endorsed Polanski.[^cite_note-35] By 22 September, Galloway relocated from [Moscow](19004.md) to [South Africa](17416221.md) to start a political tour; he claimed he will return to the UK when the [Home Office](225695.md) confirms that he will not be detained upon arrival.[^cite_note-36]

[Restore Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restore_Britain) announced Tyler Johnson, a London-based lawyer, as its candidate.[^cite_note-\:6-34] He is legal counsel for CNOOC International, whose parent firm, [China National Offshore Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Offshore_Oil_Corporation),[^cite_note-auto-37] was prohibited from receiving any US investment in 2021.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-auto-37] On Johnson's nomination forms, the proposer was Patrick McGinnis, leader of the [National Housing Party UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Housing_Party_\(UK\)) and candidate for that party in a 2022 Camden Council by-election, who has said "Jewish ideas are destroying our country".[^cite_note-searchlight-39]

[Rejoin EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejoin_EU) announced Briony Kapoor, who previously served two terms as an independent town councillor in [Folkestone and Hythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkestone_and_Hythe_District), as its candidate.[^cite_note-\:6-34]

The [Official Monster Raving Loony Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Monster_Raving_Loony_Party) selected Barmy Brunch, who previously stood in the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election) in the [North East Somerset and Hanham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_Somerset_and_Hanham) constituency, as its candidate.[^cite_note-40]

Ketankumar Pipaliya, leader of UK Voice, is standing in the by-election, having previously stood in the last United Kingdom by-election, the [2026 Clacton by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Clacton_by-election). Another by-election candidate, Tom Darwood, also stood in the Clacton by-election, under the pseudonym Joseph 77.[^cite_note-41]

### Declined to stand

[Andrew Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Feinstein), who came second to Starmer standing as an independent in 2024, said in an interview with [Novara Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novara_Media) in July 2026 that he "didn't want it to be \[him]" replacing Starmer, but said whoever did replace Starmer should be "the antithesis of Starmer in every possible way".[^cite_note-42] When the by-election was announced, Feinstein declined to stand, stating that he was "not in a position" to contest the seat again.[^cite_note-43]

Former [foreign secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(United_Kingdom\)) [David Miliband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Miliband) was suggested as a possible Labour candidate, but declined to contest the seat.[^cite_note-44]

Novelty candidate [Count Binface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Binface) announced on social media that he had declined to stand in the by-election.[^cite_note-45]

## Campaign

The by-election is the first of Andy Burnham's premiership, with some Labour figures describing it as a test of how much he has "nullified the threat from the Green Party" in the event of calling an early general election.[^cite_note-46]

On 2 September, [Zia Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Yusuf), [Reform UK](59757202.md)'s spokesperson for home affairs, criticised the possible choice of Sagal Abdi-Wali as the Labour candidate (prior to her selection) as she was born in Somalia.[^cite_note-47] His comments were criticised as racist by political figures from multiple parties,[^cite_note-\:5-48][^cite_note-49] and criticised by Reform UK figure [Nadine Dorries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadine_Dorries) as "nasty".[^cite_note-\:5-48] Reform MP [Robert Jenrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jenrick) defended Yusuf's comments, saying they were not racist and suggesting that it was legitimate to question the loyalties of foreign-born candidates.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

*[The London Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_London_Standard)* reported on 5 September that the Green Party had "knocked on nearly 1,500 doors in the first three days" since Keir Starmer's resignation, despite having not yet selected a candidate at the time.[^cite_note-52]

Andrew Feinstein has endorsed Polanski in the by-election.[^cite_note-Gecsoyler-14] The Camden People's Alliance has also endorsed Polanski after he signed up to the group's key pledges, including support for social housing, opposition to [austerity](684037.md), anti-racism, backing trade unions, and campaigning against the [Gaza genocide](75668427.md).[^cite_note-\:8-31]

Polanski released campaign videos in [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), which were criticised by Conservative MP [Katie Lam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Lam), who said "When candidates campaign in a foreign language ... \[i]t makes it impossible to hold our would-be representatives to account. We must ban this practice".[^cite_note-53] Defending the videos, the Greens argued that "democracy does not discriminate by language spoken".[^cite_note-54] On 19 September, Polanski released another campaign video, this time in [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language).[^cite_note-55]

On 19 September, an altercation took place in the constituency between Restore Britain campaigners, including Patrick McGinnis, and [Stand Up to Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_to_Racism) campaigners.[^cite_note-searchlight-39] A Stand Up to Racism campaigner punched a Restore Britain campaigner who was [canvassing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canvassing). She was then arrested.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

On 22 September, the Camden Muslim Network organised a campaign hustings at an art gallery in the constituency.[^cite_note-MEE-27] The only candidates to attend were Polanski and Patrick Stillman of the Liberal Democrats.[^cite_note-MEE-27] Organisers of the event told *[Middle East Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Eye)* that the Labour Party's campaign team had said that Abdi-Wali could not attend because of another commitment.[^cite_note-MEE-27]
