The **Bahrain Grand Prix** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جائزة البحرين الكبرى), officially known as the **Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix** for sponsorship reasons or in 2026 **Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia**, is a [Formula One](10854.md) motor racing event held in [Bahrain](18933277.md).[^cite_note-1] The inaugural race took place at the [Bahrain International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_International_Circuit) on 4 April 2004. It was the first Formula One Grand Prix held in the Middle East and was awarded the [FIA](248019.md)’s prize for “Best Organised Grand Prix”.[^cite_note-BNA501343-2]

The event has variously been the second, third, or fourth round of the F1 calendar. In [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Formula_One_World_Championship), it replaced the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Grand_Prix) as the season opener, as the latter was delayed to avoid clashing with the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games). Bahrain held the opening race in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Formula_One_World_Championship) as well, when the longer 6.299 km (3.914 mi) “Endurance Circuit” was used to mark Formula One’s diamond jubilee. In [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship), the Grand Prix opened the season once more after the cancellation of the [2021 Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Grand_Prix) due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). In [2025](72768531.md), however, the Australian Grand Prix reclaimed the opening slot as part of Formula One’s regionalisation strategy, and Bahrain was scheduled as the fourth race of the season.

In 2014, to commemorate the tenth anniversary of the event, the Bahrain Grand Prix was held under floodlights at night.[^cite_note-3] It became F1’s second night race, after the [Singapore Grand Prix](9305401.md)’s debut in 2008. [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton) won the inaugural night edition, and the race has remained a night event ever since.

The [2011 Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bahrain_Grand_Prix), scheduled for 13 March, was cancelled on 21 February amid widespread protests during the [2011 Bahraini uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bahraini_uprising).[^cite_note-Bahrain_Grand_Prix_called_off_due_to_protests-4] Drivers, including [Damon Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Hill) and [Mark Webber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Webber_\(racing_driver\)), voiced opposition to the race.[^cite_note-online.wsj.com-5] In 2012, human rights activists again called for cancellation of [the race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Bahrain_Grand_Prix) over reports of abuses by the authorities.[^cite_note-Guardianjan/30/bahrain-grand-prix-6] Despite safety concerns raised by team personnel, the race went ahead on 22 April.[^cite_note-Guardian2012/apr/09/formula-one-bahrain-grand-prix-7] The [2026 Bahrain Grand Prix](80201648.md) was also originally cancelled, one month before it was due to take place, due to risks imposed by the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-autosportcancellation-8][^cite_note-f1cancellation-9] In July 2026, it was reinstated and relocated to the [Sepang International Circuit](944336.md) in [Malaysia](3607937.md) in the opening weekend of October.[^cite_note-pf1bah-10]

## History



The Bahrain International Circuit in 2010

Construction of the [Bahrain International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_International_Circuit) at [Sakhir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhir) began in 2002. Bahrain secured the rights to host Formula One after competing against [Egypt](8087628.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), all of which had also bid to host a race.



Endurance circuit, used in 2010

In [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Formula_One_World_Championship), the race utilized the extended “Endurance Circuit” layout, which increased the lap length to 6.299 km (3.914 mi).[^cite_note-2010_layout-11] Plans to revert to the original “Grand Prix Circuit” layout were confirmed for the later-cancelled [2011 Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bahrain_Grand_Prix), and it has been used since the event returned in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Formula_One_World_Championship).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-FIA2012-13]

### 2011 cancellation

Main article: [2011 Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bahrain_Grand_Prix)



A demonstrator protesting against the race

On 21 February 2011, it was announced that the [Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bahrain_Grand_Prix) scheduled for 13 March was cancelled due to the [2011 Bahraini protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bahraini_protests).[^cite_note-Bahrain_Grand_Prix_called_off_due_to_protests-4][^cite_note-14] On 3 June, the FIA rescheduled the race for 30 October. The former world champion [Damon Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Hill) urged Formula One not to reinstate the event, warning that if it went ahead, “we will forever have the blight of association with repressive methods to achieve order”.[^cite_note-Cary-15] Bernie Ecclestone told the BBC, “Hopefully there’ll be peace and quiet, and we can return in the future, but of course it’s not on. The schedule cannot be rescheduled without the agreement of the participants – they’re the facts”.[^cite_note-16] A week after announcing the rescheduling, Formula One cancelled the race altogether.[^cite_note-online.wsj.com-5]

### 2020 postponement

See also: [2020 Formula One World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Formula_One_World_Championship)

In response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), organisers announced that no spectators would be permitted at the race scheduled for 22 March 2020. Two weeks before the event, it was indefinitely postponed.[^cite_note-17] It was later rescheduled for 29 November and became one of two races hosted at the Bahrain International Circuit across consecutive weekends, with the second race held on the outer layout and named the [Sakhir Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhir_Grand_Prix).

In February 2022, the Bahrain Grand Prix contract was extended through to 2036.[^cite_note-18]

### 2026 postponement

The [2026 Bahrain Grand Prix](80201648.md) was originally cancelled due to the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), which rendered Bahrain and the neighbouring [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) unsafe to host Formula One Grands Prix.[^cite_note-f1cancellation-9] As both races were held early in the season, replacement Grands Prix could not be scheduled at that time. By July, arrangements were made with the [Malaysian](3607937.md) [Ministry of Youth and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Youth_and_Sports_\(Malaysia\)) to host the Bahrain Grand Prix at the [Sepang International Circuit](944336.md), which formerly hosted the [Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Grand_Prix) between 1999 and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Formula_One_World_Championship), in a slot following the [Azerbaijan Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Grand_Prix) and before the neighbouring [Singapore Grand Prix](9305401.md). The event is due to run under the **Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia** name as the Bahraini government remains the primary financial promoter. This remains subject to final agreements and official sign-off, including approval by the [World Motor Sport Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Motor_Sport_Council).[^cite_note-pf1bah-10]

## Characteristics

The [Bahrain International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_International_Circuit) is known for its large run-off areas. While these have been criticised for failing to penalise mistakes, they help prevent sand from blowing onto the track. The circuit is widely regarded as one of the safest in Formula One.[^cite_note-Pictures-19]

Although alcohol is legal in Bahrain, podium celebrations use a non-alcoholic [rosewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosewater) drink called Waard instead of champagne.[^cite_note-Pictures-19]

## Controversies

Since the global media attention in 2011 and 2012, human rights groups have continued to report abuses and arrests linked to F1 protests. Photographer Ahmed Humaidan was among about 30 people jailed for roles in the 2012 demonstrations, while activist Najah Ahmed Yousif was imprisoned and physically and sexually abused after criticising the Bahrain Grand Prix on social media.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Rights organisations have criticised [Formula One Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_Group) and [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile](248019.md) for failing to uphold their own Statement of Commitment to Respect for Human Rights, arguing that by ignoring political crackdowns, F1 is complicit in the suffering of dissidents.[^cite_note-23] In 2018, after sustained pressure, F1 said it was “concerned” for Yousif, but there has been no known follow-up.[^cite_note-24]

A coalition of human rights groups, led by the [Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_Institute_for_Rights_and_Democracy), claims that the Bahrain Grand Prix has become a focal point for protests and abuse committed by security forces. They also accuse F1 of providing invaluable PR for Bahrain’s government and of helping normalise human rights violations.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-25] In letters to [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton), three [political prisoners](37307.md) praised his stance on human rights and urged him to raise awareness of their plight. The event has been widely cited as an example of [sportswashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportswashing).[^cite_note-26]

Human rights activists called for the cancellation of the [2012 Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Bahrain_Grand_Prix), held on 22 April, citing ongoing excessive force by authorities and reports of torture in detention.[^cite_note-Guardianjan/30/bahrain-grand-prix-6][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The activist Salah Abbas Habib was killed during a demonstration on the eve of the Grand Prix, and the photojournalist Ahmed Ismael Hassan al-Samadi was fatally shot while covering an anti-Grand Prix protest.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

On 9 April 2012, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that a senior team member, speaking anonymously, said his view was representative, “The Formula One teams want the sport’s governing body to cancel – or at least postpone – the Bahrain Grand Prix … I feel very uncomfortable about going to Bahrain. If I’m brutally frank, the only way they can pull this race off without incident is to have a complete military lockdown there. And I think that would be unacceptable, both for F1 and for Bahrain. But I don’t see any other way they can do it”.[^cite_note-Guardian2012/apr/09/formula-one-bahrain-grand-prix-7]

On 21 April 2012, the group [Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_\(group\)) launched “Operation Bahrain,” threatening [cyberattacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberattacks) if the race continued. Hours later, hackers took down the website f1-racers.net with a [distributed denial-of-service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_denial-of-service) attack.[^cite_note-31] Despite the protests, the Grand Prix was held as scheduled.

Bahrain hosted two races in November 2020, but the decision drew criticism from human rights groups and from seven-time world champion Lewis Hamilton. Hamilton urged F1 to “face its responsibilities” and address human rights issues in host countries. A consortium of organisations led by the Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy wrote to the F1 CEO [Chase Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Carey), warning that the race had again become a focal point for protests and abuses by Bahraini security forces. The Bahraini government denied allegations of sportswashing.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-25]

On 27 October 2022, F1 was served with a legal complaint from the Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy and two Bahraini torture survivors, Najah Yusuf and Hajer Mansoor, alleging it ignored human rights violations when it announced in February that the Bahrain Grand Prix would remain on the calendar until 2036. The claim, filed through the UK National Contact Point (NCP), accused them of breaching guidelines set by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development.[^cite_note-32]
