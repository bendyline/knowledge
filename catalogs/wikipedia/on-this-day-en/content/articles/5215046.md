# Agriculture in Nigeria



Development of agricultural output of Nigeria in 2015 US\$ since 1961



[Oshodi Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshodi_Market) in [Lagos](85232.md)

**Agriculture** is a major sector of the [Nigerian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Nigeria),[^cite_note-roles-1] accounting for up to 35% of total [employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment) in 2020.[^cite_note-\:1-2] According to the [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization),[^cite_note-\:0-3] [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) remains the foundation of the Nigerian economy,[^cite_note-roles-1] providing livelihood for most Nigerians and generating millions of jobs.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-facts1-5][^cite_note-jlukmon-6] Along with crude oil, [Nigeria](21383.md) relies on the agricultural products it exports to generate most of its national revenue.[^cite_note-jlukmon-6] The agricultural sector in Nigeria comprises four sub-sectors: [crop production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_production), [livestock](25160767.md), [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), and [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing).[^cite_note-7]

Nigeria has a total agricultural area of 70.8 million hectares,[^cite_note-\:0-3] of which 34 million [hectares](24919513.md) are [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land),[^cite_note-8] 6.5 million hectares are used for permanent crops, and 30.3 million hectares are [meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadow) and [pastures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasture).[^cite_note-9]

[Maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) (corn), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [Guinea corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_corn) (sorghum), [groundnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) (peanut) and [yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(vegetable\)) are the major crops farmed in Nigeria, with 70% of the households engaged in [crop farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_farming). In the south, 7.3% of the households practice fishing, while 69.3% of the households own or raise livestock in [northwest Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(Nigeria\)).[^cite_note-10]

In the third quarter of 2019, before the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the sector grew by 14.88% year-on-year. [Crop production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) remains the largest part of the sector. During the third quarter of 2019, the agriculture sector contributed 29.25% to the overall real GDP.[^cite_note-11] Between January and March 2021, agriculture contributed 22.35% of the total gross domestic product.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

The sector is undergoing transformation through commercialization at the small, medium, and large enterprise levels.[^cite_note-same_name-16] However, there are several factors in the Nigerian agricultural sector that may prevent its growth, including a land tenure system that limits access to land, the country's level of [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) development, limited adoption of research findings and technologies, costs of farm inputs, the amount of access to credit allowed by the management of specialized institutions established for the development of the agricultural sector, the manners of [fertilizer](37401.md) procurement and distribution, storage facility effectiveness, and the amount of access to markets.

More recently, changes in average [temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperatures), [rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfall), [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) extremes, and the growing infestation of pests and related diseases precipitated by [climate change](5042951.md) pose a challenge to the integrity of the country's agriculture system.[^cite_note-17] This is coupled with a dependence on [rain-fed agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfed_agriculture), which has made the sector vulnerable to seasonal conditions.[^cite_note-18]

These all contribute to agricultural productivity and [post-harvest losses and waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_waste) in Nigeria.[^cite_note-\:0-3] Illiteracy is also one of the several factors preventing the progress and development of agriculture in Nigeria. Research has proven that most of the farmers in Nigeria have not acquired formal education.[^cite_note-19]

## Dynamics

At the time of Nigeria's independence (1960), food exports made up more than 70% of the country's [Gross National Product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_national_product) (GNP).[^cite_note-20] However, over the next 25 years, the situation reversed, with food items accounting for over 50% of imports. Despite having [fertile land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_land?action=edit\&redlink=1), food output in Nigeria declined, and per capita food production also decreased, leading to a more than sevenfold increase in grain imports.[^cite_note-21]

In the 1970s, the Nigerian government promoted the use of inorganic fertilizers to address the issue.[^cite_note-pasquini-22] By 1990, out of Nigeria's total land area of about 91 million hectares, 82 million hectares were found to be suitable for farming, but only 42% of the cultivable area was farmed. The "bush fallow system," which involves land being left idle for a period of time to allow natural regeneration of soil fertility, was commonly used. Additionally, 18 million hectares were classified as permanent [pasture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasture), but had the potential to support crops, and most of the 20 million hectares covered by [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forests) and woodlands were believed to have agricultural potential.[^cite_note-23]

Agricultural holdings are small and scattered, and [farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming) is carried out with simple tools. Large-scale agriculture is not common. [Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) contributed 32% to GDP in 2001.

Nigeria’s Agricultural Import Bill surged 30% to N920 billion in Q1 2024, underscoring the nation's growing reliance on imported agricultural products like wheat and essential foods to meet consumer demand, which domestic farming struggles to satisfy. Nigeria's imported [food inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_inflation) grew to over 36.38% in June 2024 according to reports.[^cite_note-24]

This dependence on imports presents challenges for Nigeria’s agricultural sector, potentially reducing local production due to cheaper, more accessible imported goods. This competitive market environment poses risks to the sector’s long-term sustainability and development.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

Palm oil is high in [carotenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotene), which are precursors to vitamin A, [tocopherols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocopherol), and [antioxidants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioxidant), palm oil is mostly used for [cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking) and [frying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frying) in Nigeria. It has led to interest in using palm oil as a [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural) source of vitamin A in [Africa](5334607.md).[^cite_note-60]

Although oil palm [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) and estates occupy over 118,264 [hectares](24919513.md), they account for merely 5% of Nigeria's total crude palm oil production. Although palm kernel cake is well-known in West plantations as a useful feed [ingredient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingredient), it was mostly utilized as a source of [fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber) and energy in rations for [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [fowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fowl), and [pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig) until recently.

The palm oil industry constitutes another sector of the Nigerian economy, providing food and raw materials for the food, cosmetics, [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals), plastics, and [bio-energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) industries. In Nigeria, the institute with information about oil palm is the [Nigeria Institute for Oil Palm Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_Institute_for_Oil_Palm_Research). The formal mandate of the institute is to conduct research into the production and products of oil palm and other palms of economic relevance and transfer its research findings to farmers.[^cite_note-61]

As of 2023, [Edo state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_State) holds the top position in palm oil production in Nigeria, thanks to significant contributions from [Flourmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour_Mills_of_Nigeria) Nigeria, Dufil, and Saro Africa, who have collectively cultivated thousands of hectares of oil palm plantations in the state.[^cite_note-62]

## Ministry of Agriculture



A rice paddy field in Nigeria

The government office responsible for agriculture development and transformation in Nigeria is currently the [Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development_\(Nigeria\)). Primarily funded by Nigeria's federal government, the Ministry currently supervises almost fifty [parastatals](204768.md) operating as either departments or agencies across the country. The Ministry has two major departments, namely the Technical and Service Departments:&#x20;

- Technical Department: Manages agriculture (trees and crops), [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries), livestock, land resources, [fertilizer](37401.md), food reserve and storage, and [rural development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Development).

- Service Department: Manages finance, human resources, procurement, PPAS (plan, policy, analysis and statistics), and co-operatives.

The ministry was headed by [Audu Ogbeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audu_Ogbeh), who was appointed by [President Muhammad Buhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadu_Buhari) on 12 November 2015, succeeding [Akinwumi Adesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinwumi_Adesina), who was elected to head [Africa Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank). Buhari had also appointed [Heineken Lokpobiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Lokpobiri) as the new Minister of State for Agriculture and [Shehu Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehu_Ahmadu_Musa) as the Permanent Secretary under the newly created Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development.[^cite_note-65]

In 2023, President [Bola Ahmed Tinubu](3034327.md) changed the name of the Ministry of Agriculture to the Ministry of Agriculture and Food security and appointed Senator [Abubakar Kyari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Kyari) as the Minister of Agriculture and Food Security.[^cite_note-66]

### Policies



Rice processing in south east Nigeria

Women working in the mill. The husk and bran of the parboiled rice are removed to give the white grain in south east Nigeria.

In 2011, President [Jonathan's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Jonathan) administration launched the Agricultural Transformation Agenda, which was overseen by the [Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development). The primary goal of the agenda was to position agriculture as a profitable business, integrate the [agricultural value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_value_chain), and establish agriculture as a primary driver of Nigeria's economic growth.[^cite_note-67] To achieve this agenda, the government implemented several measures:

- New fiscal incentives to encourage domestic [import substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import_substitution)

- Removal of restrictions on areas of investment and maximum equity ownership in investment by foreign investors

- [Currency exchange controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_controls) – free transfer of capital, profits, and dividends

- Constitutional guarantees against [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization)/expropriation of investments

- Zero percent (0%) duty on agricultural machinery and equipment imports

- Pioneer [Tax holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_holiday) for agricultural investments

- Duty waivers and other industry-related incentives, e.g., based on the use of local raw materials, export orientation

Recently, the [Central Bank of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Nigeria) began the Anchors-Borrow program[^cite_note-68] to encourage the cultivation of certain crops, especially rice.

The Federal Government of Nigeria has also closed its land borders in order to curtail rice imports and encourage local production.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

In 2024, the [Nigerian government](21387.md) temporarily banned [import duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import_duties) on essential agricultural commodities to combat food inflation and support local production. This policy aims to reduce food import costs and encourage investment in local farming and food processing industries.[^cite_note-71]

Additionally, the government introduced financial and logistical support programs for [farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer), including low-interest loans, subsidies for agricultural inputs, and [investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_\(economics\)) in rural infrastructure.[^cite_note-72]

The Nigerian government also introduced the Nigeria's Farmers Soil Health Card Scheme which will ensure the establishment of soil testing laboratories in various states and local government in Nigeria to boost farmers' output.[^cite_note-73]

In August 2025, Nigeria imposed a six‑month ban on exporting raw shea nuts to catalyze local economic development and boost value addition.[^cite_note-74]

### Sustainable Development Goals

The [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) in the agriculture sector in Nigeria have had an impact on the export sector responsible for the consumption and production of agricultural products in Nigeria. The exportation sector's monthly earnings have improved in four years. In January 2016, agricultural exports raked in N4.1 billion, which then rose to N25 billion by January 2017. From April 2019 to March 2020, total agriculture exports hit N289 billion for Nigeria.

Agriculture exports for the first six months of 2020 were N204.45 billion, which indicates that productivity is increasing in the sector to enable export growth.[^cite_note-75]

The [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals_and_Nigeria) have also led to the emergence of policies that have had an impact on the agriculture sector. Some of them include the Nigerian “Agricultural Promotion Policy—2016–2020”, which focuses on ensuring [food security](69127807.md) by reducing food imports. It manages institutional reforms and incentives for technological development at the local level. The Empowering Novel [Agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness)-Led Employment Program mobilizes finance for youth-led agribusiness development. Another policy is the Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme Act from 2016, which offers incentives to farmers and other professionals throughout the agricultural supply chain. Finally, the “Green Alternative: The Agriculture Promotion Policy” was launched in mid-2016. It was created to boost soybean and cowpea production, which were chosen for the policy's focus due to their nutritional value and export potential.[^cite_note-76]

## Drawback to Nigeria's Agricultural Sector

Since 2015, [nomadic cattle herders' attacks on farms across Nigeria](54964174.md) have led to abysmally low output as many farming communities have either abandoned farming or have curtailed their farming activities due to threats of crop destruction or bloody clashes. This has invariably led to skyrocketing food inflation and a rise in unemployment. <https://journals.aphriapub.com/index.php/EBR/article/download/1683/1582/3223#:~:text=The%20study%20concluded%20that%20the,pay%20tax%20to%20the%20state.&text=the%20people.>[Herders against Farmers: Nigeria's Expanding Deadly Conflict](https://www.crisisgroup.org/africa/west-africa/nigeria/252-herders-against-farmers-nigerias-expanding-deadly-conflict) Farmers accuse herders of intentionally lead their cattle to eat up their crops, which sometimes made farmers to attack the herders. The herders then usually regroup to burn whole rural farming communities in retaliation.

Though Nigeria is nearing self-sufficiency in [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) production <https://businesspost.ng/economy/nigeria-targeting-self-sufficiency-in-wheat-rice-production-shettima/>, experts warn that if the protracted conflict between nomads and farmers is not solved soon, it may erode the gains made so far. Several peace accords signed between the two have failed to curtail incidences of crop destructions or the resulting violence. <https://punchng.com/farmers-herders-sign-peace-pact-to-end-clashes-in-swest/> A recent report by the [United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) revealed that around 25.3 million Nigerians are at the risk of food insecurity due to insecurity and climate change issues in Nigeria.[^cite_note-77]
