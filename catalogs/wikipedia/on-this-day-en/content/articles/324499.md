A **mortar** today is usually a simple, lightweight, man-portable, [muzzle-loaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzleloader) [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) consisting of a [smooth-bore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoothbore) (although some models use a [rifled barrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifling)) metal tube fixed to a base plate (to spread out the [recoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoil)) with a lightweight [bipod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipod) mount and a [sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sight_\(device\)). Mortars are typically used as [indirect fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_fire) weapons for close [fire support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_support) with a variety of ammunition. Historically, mortars were heavy [siege artillery](26905.md). Mortars launch explosive [shells](337004.md) (technically called [bombs](47628.md))[^cite_note-1] in high arching [ballistic trajectories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectile_motion).

## History

Mortars have been used for hundreds of years. The earliest reported use of mortars occurred in Korea in a 1413 naval battle: Korean gunsmiths developed the *wan'gu* (gourd-shaped mortar) (완구, 碗口).[^cite_note-Turnbull2002-2] The earliest version of the *wan'gu* dates back to 1407.[^cite_note-3] [Ch'oe Hae-san](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'oe_Hae-san) (1380–1443), the son of [Ch'oe Mu-sŏn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'oe_Mu-s%C5%8Fn) (1325–1395), is generally credited with inventing the *wan'gu*.[^cite_note-4] During the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), general [Qi Jiguang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi_Jiguang) (1528-1588) recorded the use of a mini-[cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) called the *[hu dun pao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_dun_pao)* that resembled a mortar.[^cite_note-5]

The first use in [siege warfare](26905.md) was at the [1453 siege of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople) by [Mehmed the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_the_Conqueror). An Italian account by [Giovanni da Tagliacozzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Tagliacozzo?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [1456 siege of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1456\)) states that the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) used seven mortars that fired "stone shots one [Italian mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_mile) high".[^cite_note-Ágoston2005-6] The time of flight of these was apparently long enough that casualties could be avoided by posting observers to give warning of their trajectories.[^cite_note-Babinger1992-7]



Engraving depicting the Venetian siege of the [Acropolis of Athens](2076.md), September 1687. The trajectory of the shell that hit the Parthenon, causing its explosion, is marked.

Early mortars, such as the 15th-century [Pumhart von Steyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumhart_von_Steyr), were large and heavy and could not be easily transported. Simply made, these weapons originated as iron bowls reminiscent of the kitchen and apothecary [mortars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle) whence they drew their name. (Some early sources refer to mortars as *[ potguns](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/potgun)*.[^cite_note-8]) Mortars played a significant role in the [ Venetian conquest of Morea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morean_War) (1684 to 1699), and in the course of this campaign an [ammunition depot](28191446.md) in the [Parthenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon) was blown up in 1687. An early transportable mortar was invented by Baron [Menno van Coehoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menno_van_Coehoorn) in 1701.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] This mortar fired an exploding shell, which had a [fuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuse_\(explosives\)) that was lit by hot gases when fired. The [Coehorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coehorn) mortar quickly gained popularity and necessitated a new form of naval ship, the [bomb vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_vessel). An early use of more-mobile mortars as [field artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_artillery) (rather than as [siege artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_artillery)) was by British forces in the suppression of the [Jacobite rising of 1719](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_rising_of_1719) at the [Battle of Glen Shiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Glen_Shiel). High-angle trajectory mortars held a great advantage over standard [field guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_gun) in the rough terrain of the [West Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Highlands) of [Scotland](26994.md).



[US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) 13-inch mortar "Dictator" was a rail-mounted gun of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) of 1861 to 1865.

The mortar had fallen out of general use in Europe by the [Napoleonic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_era), although [Manby Mortars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manby_Mortar) were widely used on the coast to launch lines to ships in distress. European interest in mortar weapons was not revived until the beginning of the 20th century. Mortars were heavily used by both sides during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) of 1861 to 1865. At the [Siege of Vicksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Vicksburg) in 1863, General [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) reported making mortars "by taking logs of the toughest wood that could be found, boring them out for 3 or 5 kg (6 or 12 lb) shells and binding them with strong iron bands. These answered as [Coehorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coehorn), and shells were successfully thrown from them into the trenches of the enemy".[^cite_note-11]

During the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War) of 1904 to 1905, Lieutenant General [Leonid Gobyato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Gobyato) of the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) applied the principles of [indirect fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_fire) from closed firing-positions in the field; and with the collaboration of General [Roman Kondratenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Kondratenko), he designed the first mortar that fired navy shells.



German [7.5 cm *Minenwerfer*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7.58_cm_Minenwerfer)

***Trench mortar*** was a term used during [World War I](4764461.md) to designate various short-range artillery weapons of different sizes. Their ranges were so short that they had to be fired directly from the frontline [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare), hence the name.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith1918-12] The [ Imperial German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(German_Empire\)) studied the 1904-1905 [Siege of Port Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Port_Arthur), where heavy artillery had been unable to destroy defensive structures like [barbed wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbed_wire) and [bunkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunker). Consequently, the Germans developed a short-barrelled rifled [muzzle-loading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzle-loading) mortar called the *[Minenwerfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minenwerfer)* ("mine-thrower"). Heavily used during [World War I](4764461.md), the *Minenwerfer* were made in three sizes: 7.58, 17 and 25 cm (2.98, 6.69 and 9.84 in). The British invented the lightweight [Stokes mortar](#Stokes_mortar).

## Types

### Stokes mortar



[Wilfred Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Stokes) with example of his WWI mortar and bombs

It was not until the [Stokes mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stokes_mortar) was devised by Sir [Wilfred Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Stokes) in 1915 during the First World War that the modern mortar transportable by one person was born. In the conditions of [trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare), there was a great need for a versatile and easily portable weapon that could be manned by troops under cover in the trenches. Stokes' design was initially rejected in June 1915 because it was unable to use existing stocks of British mortar ammunition, and it took the intervention of [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George) (at that time [Minister of Munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Munitions)) and Lieutenant Colonel J. C. Matheson of the Trench Warfare Supply Department (who reported to Lloyd George) to expedite manufacture of the Stokes mortar. The weapon proved to be extremely useful in the muddy trenches of the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)), as a mortar round could be aimed to fall directly into [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench), where artillery shells, because of their low angle of flight, could not possibly go.[^cite_note-13]



Trench mortar used during the [Battle of Vimy Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vimy_Ridge).

The Stokes mortar was a simple muzzle-loaded weapon, consisting of a smoothbore metal tube fixed to a base plate (to absorb recoil) with a lightweight [bipod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipod) mount. When a mortar bomb was dropped into the tube, an impact sensitive [primer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_cap#Primers) in the base of the bomb would make contact with a [firing pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firing_pin) at the base of the tube and detonate, firing the bomb towards the target. The Stokes mortar could fire as many as 25 bombs per minute and had a maximum range of 730 m (800 yd), firing the original cylindrical unstabilised projectile.[^cite_note-14]

A modified version of the mortar, which fired a modern [fin-stabilised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_stabilizer) streamlined [projectile](144553.md) and had a [booster charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_booster) for longer range, was developed after World War I;[^cite_note-15] this was in effect a new weapon. By [World War II](32927.md), it could fire as many as 30 bombs per minute and had a range of over 2,300 m (2,500 yd) with some shell types.[^cite_note-16] The French developed an improved version of the Stokes mortar as the [Brandt Mle 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandt_Mle_27/31), further refined as the [Brandt Mle 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandt_Mle_27/31); this design was widely copied with and without license.[^cite_note-Bishop2002-17][^cite_note-Norris2002-18] These weapons were the prototypes for all subsequent light mortar developments around the world.

### Mortar carrier



The German 60 cm [Karl-Gerät](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ger%C3%A4t) heavy siege mortar in August 1944



Interior of an [Israeli Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defence_Force) M113 mortar carrier showing the placement of an 81 mm mortar

[Mortar carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_carrier) are vehicles which carry a mortar as a primary weapon. Numerous vehicles have been used to mount mortars, from [improvised civilian trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_\(vehicle\)) used by [insurgents](589841.md), to modified [infantry fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle), such as variants of the [M3 half-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3_half-track) and [M113 armored personnel carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113_armored_personnel_carrier), to vehicles specifically intended to carry a mortar. Simpler vehicles carry a standard infantry mortar while in more complex vehicles the mortar is fully integrated into the vehicle and cannot be dismounted from the vehicle. Mortar carriers cannot be fired while on the move, and some must be dismounted to fire.

There are numerous [armoured fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicle) and even [main battle tanks](26595097.md) that can be equipped with a mortar, either outside or inside of the cabin. The Israeli [Merkava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkava) tank uses a 60 mm (2.4 in) mortar as a secondary armament. The [Russian army](1094752.md) uses the *[2S4 Tyulpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S4_Tyulpan)* self-propelled 240 mm (9.4 in) heavy mortar which is one of the largest mortars in current use.

### Gun-mortars



[2B9 Vasilek 82 mm gun mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2B9_Vasilek)

Gun-mortars are breech-loaded mortars usually equipped with a [hydraulic recoil mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_recoil_mechanism), and sometimes equipped with an [autoloader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoloader). They are usually mounted on an armoured vehicle and are capable of both [direct fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_fire) and [indirect fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_fire). The archetypes are the [Brandt Mle CM60A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandt_Mle_CM60A1) and [Brandt 60 mm LR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandt_60_mm_LR_Gun-mortar), which combine features of modern infantry mortars together with those of modern cannon. Such weapons are most commonly smoothbore, firing fin-stabilised rounds, using relatively small propellant charges in comparison to projectile weight. While some have been fitted with rifled barrels, such as the [2S31 Vena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S31_Vena) and [2S9 Nona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S9_Nona). They have short barrels in comparison to guns and are much more lightly built than guns of a similar calibre – all characteristics of infantry mortars. This produces a hybrid weapon capable of engaging area targets with indirect high-angle fire, and also specific targets such as vehicles and bunkers with direct fire. Such hybrids are much heavier and more complicated than infantry mortars, superior to [rocket-propelled grenades](26286.md) in the anti-armour and [bunker-busting](167751.md) role, but have a reduced range compared to modern gun-[howitzers](14392.md) and inferior anti-tank capability compared to modern anti-tank [guided weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition). However, they do have a niche in, for example, providing a multi-role anti-personnel, anti-armour capability in light mobile formations. Such systems, like the Soviet 120 mm (4.7 in) [2S9 Nona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S9_Nona), are mostly self-propelled (although a towed variant exists). The [AMOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMOS) (Advanced Mortar System) is an example of an even more advanced gun mortar system. It uses a 120 mm automatic twin-barrelled, breech-loaded mortar turret, which can be mounted on a variety of armoured vehicles and attack boats. A modern example of a gun-mortar is the [2B9 Vasilek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2B9_Vasilek).

### Spigot mortar



A Blacker Bombard during training

A spigot mortar consists mainly of a solid rod or [spigot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ductile_iron_pipe#Spigot_and_socket), onto which a hollow tube in the projectile fits—inverting the normal tube-mortar arrangement. At the top of the tube in the projectile, a cavity contains [propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propellant), such as [cordite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordite). There is usually a trigger mechanism built into the base of the spigot, with a long firing pin running up the length of the spigot activating a [primer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_cap) inside the projectile and firing the propellant charge. The advantage of a spigot mortar is that the firing unit (baseplate and spigot) is smaller and lighter than a conventional tube mortar of equivalent payload and range. It is also somewhat simpler to manufacture. Further, most spigot mortars have no barrel in the conventional sense, which means ammunition of almost any weight and diameter can be fired from the same mortar.

The disadvantage is that while most mortar bombs have a streamlined shape towards the back that fits a spigot mortar application well, using that space for the spigot mortar tube takes volume and mass away from the payload of the projectile. If a soldier is carrying only a few projectiles, the projectile weight disadvantage is not significant. However, the weight of a large quantity of the heavier and more complex spigot projectiles offsets the weight saved.

A near-silent mortar can operate using the spigot principle. Each round has a close-fitting sliding plug in the tube that fits over the spigot. When the round is fired, the projectile is pushed off the spigot, but before the plug clears the spigot it is caught by a constriction at the base of the tube. This traps the gases from the propelling charge and hence the sound of the firing. After World War II the Belgium Fly-K silent spigot mortar was accepted into French service as the TN-8111.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

A 40 mm (1.6 in) caseless underbarrel spigot launcher, the Battelle Pogojet has been developed by Armor Development Group.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]



A [hedgehog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedgehog_\(weapon\)) launcher on display. Note the exposed spigot on the lower left launcher.

Spigot mortars generally fell out of favour after World War II and were replaced by smaller conventional mortars. Military applications of spigot mortars include:

- The 230 mm (9.1 in) petard mortar used on the [Churchill AVRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_tank#Churchill_AVRE_\(Assault_Vehicle_Royal_Engineers\)) by Britain in World War II.

  [^cite_note-26]

- The [320 mm (13 in) Type 98 mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/320_mm_Type_98_mortar) used by Japan in World War II to some psychological effect in the battles of [Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwo_Jima) and [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island)

- The [Blacker Bombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacker_Bombard) and [PIAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIAT) anti-tank launchers used by Britain in World War II.

- The [Hedgehog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedgehog_\(weapon\)) launcher, used from the deck of a ship, used 24 spigot mortars which fired a diamond pattern of anti-submarine projectiles into the sea ahead of the ship. A sinking projectile detonated if it struck a submarine, and the pattern was such that any submarine partly in the landing zone of the projectiles would be struck one or more times.

Non-military applications include the use of small-calibre spigot mortars to launch lightweight, low-velocity foam dummy targets used for training [retriever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retriever) dogs for bird hunting. Simple launchers use a separate small primer cap as the sole propellant (similar or identical to the cartridges used in industrial [nail guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nail_gun)).

### Improvised



Improvised mortars in Batey ha-Osef Museum, Tel Aviv, Israel

Insurgent groups often use improvised, or "homemade" mortars to attack fortified military installations or terrorise civilians. They are usually constructed from heavy steel piping mounted on a steel frame. These weapons may fire standard mortar rounds, purpose-made shells, repurposed gas cylinders filled with explosives and shrapnel, or any other type of improvised explosive, incendiary or chemical munitions. These were called "[barrack busters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrack_buster)" by the [Provisional Irish Republican Army (PIRA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army).&#x20;

#### Syrian civil war

Improvised mortars used by insurgents in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) are known as [*&#x20;hell cannons*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvised_artillery_in_the_Syrian_civil_war). Observers have noted that they are "wildly inaccurate" and responsible for thousands of civilian deaths.[^cite_note-27]

#### Sri Lankan civil war

Improvised mortars used in the [Sri Lankan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_civil_war) by the rebel [Tamil Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Tigers) are known as "Pasilan 2000", also known as a "rocket mortar" or "Arti-mortar" like the 122 mm (4.8 in) cannon, successor to the **Baba mortar** used by the LTTE for ground operations since the 1980s. As Baba mortar rounds contained tar, they caused a fire when they hit the ground.[^cite_note-28] The Baba, the prototype mortar, was crude. But with time the weapon has improved.

The Pasilan 2000, the improved version, has been developed with characteristics similar to a rocket launcher. The Pasilan 2000 was a heavy mortar fired from a mobile launcher mounted on a tractor. The shell does not emit constant muzzle flares like [artillery](2508.md) or MBRL. This is ideal for LTTE's camouflage and conceals attacking style. Once a round is fired, forward observers/spies/civilian spotters can correct the fire. The way the tube is installed is similar to the positioning of rocket pods. The length and calibre of the barrel indicate Pasilan 2000 system has common features to the Chinese made Type 82 130 mm (5.1 in) 30-tube MLRS (introduced by the Palestinian Liberation Army (PLA) in the early 1980s) rather than rail-guided Katyusha variants such as the Qassam Rocket. The warhead weight is 70 kg (150 lb) and it is filled with TNT. It had a range of 15 to 25 km (10 to 15 mi). The rocket has since then undergone some modifications. The Pasilan 2000 was more lethal than Baba mortar. But it was not heavily used for ground attacks during the [Eelam War IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eelam_War_IV).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Modern

### Design



L16 mortar consisting of barrel, base plate and bipod



Looking down an L16 mortar barrel. Note: fixed firing pin.

Most modern mortar systems consist of four main components: a barrel, a base plate, a bipod and a sight. Modern mortars normally range in [calibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliber) from 60 to 120 mm (2.4 to 4.7 in). However, both larger and smaller mortars have been produced. The modern mortar is a muzzle-loaded weapon and relatively simple to operate. It consists of a barrel into which the gunners drop a mortar round. When the round reaches the base of the barrel it hits a fixed [firing pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firing_pin) that fires the round. The barrel is generally set at an angle of between 45 and 85 degrees (800 to 1500 mils), with the higher angle producing a shorter horizontal trajectory. Some mortars have a moving firing pin, operated by a [lanyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanyard) or [trigger mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigger_\(firearms\)).

### Ammunition



Mortar round with propellant rings about to be fired

[Ammunition](18940621.md) for mortars generally comes in two main varieties: fin-stabilised and spin-stabilised. Examples of the former have short fins on their posterior portion, which control the path of the bomb in flight. Spin-stabilised mortar bombs rotate as they travel along and leave the mortar tube, which stabilises them in much the same way as a rifle bullet. Both types of rounds can be either [illumination](337004.md#Illumination) ([infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) or visible illumination), [smoke](337004.md#Smoke), [high explosive](337004.md#High-explosive), and training rounds. Mortar bombs are often referred to, incorrectly, as "mortars".[^cite_note-31]

Operators may fire spin-stabilised rounds from either a smoothbore or a rifled barrel. Rifled mortars are more accurate but slower to load. Since mortars are generally muzzle-loaded, mortar bombs for rifled barrels usually have a pre-engraved band, called an obturator, that engages with the rifling of the barrel. Exceptions to this are the US [M2 110 mm (4.2 in) mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_4.2-inch_mortar) and [M30 mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M30_mortar), whose ammunition has a sub-calibre expandable ring that enlarged when fired. This allows the projectile to slide down the barrel freely but grip the rifling when fired. The system resembles the [Minié ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini%C3%A9_ball) for muzzle-loading rifles. For extra range, propellant rings (augmentation charges) are attached to the bomb's fins. The rings are usually easy to remove, because they have a major influence on the speed and thus the range of the bomb. Some mortar rounds can be fired without any augmentation charges, e.g., the [81 mm L16 mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L16_81mm_mortar).

### Precision guided



Soldiers standing in front of an [M1064 mortar carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1064_mortar_carrier), holding a XM395 Precision Guided Mortar Munition prototype at Fort Benning, Georgia, February 2006

The [XM395 Precision Guided Mortar Munition (PGMM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM395_Precision_Guided_Mortar_Munition) is a 120 mm [guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) mortar round developed by [Alliant Techsystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliant_Techsystems).[^cite_note-DID-33] Based on Orbital ATK's Precision Guidance Kit for 155 mm artillery projectiles, XM395 combines [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) guidance and directional control surfaces into a package that replaces standard fuses, transforming existing 120 mm mortar bodies into precision-guided munitions.[^cite_note-34] The XM395 munition consists of a GPS-guided kit fitted to standard 120 mm smoothbore mortar rounds that includes the fitting of a nose and tail subsystem containing the maneuvering parts.[^cite_note-army29march11-35][^cite_note-36]

The [Strix mortar round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strix_mortar_round) is a Swedish endphase-guided projectile fired from a 120 mm mortar currently manufactured by [Saab Bofors Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_Bofors_Dynamics). STRIX is fired like a conventional mortar round. The round contains an [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) imaging sensor that it uses to guide itself onto any tank or armoured fighting vehicle in the vicinity where it lands. The seeker is designed to ignore targets that are already burning. Launched from any 120 mm mortar, STRIX has a normal range of up to 4.5 km (2.8 mi). The addition of a special sustainer motor increases the range to 7.5 km (4.7 mi).

The [GMM 120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMM_120) (*Guided Mortar Munition 120*; known as **Patzmi**; also referred to as **Morty**) is a GPS and/or [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser)-guided mortar munition, which was developed by [Israel Military Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMI_Systems).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Another Israeli guided mortar is [Iron Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Sting), developed by [Elbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit). The Russian [KM-8 Gran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KM-8_Gran) is also laser-guided.[^cite_note-39]

## Compared to long range artillery



Self-propelled mortar based on [K-4386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamaz_Typhoon#Variants) during the "Armiya 2020" exhibition

Modern mortars and their ammunition are generally much smaller and lighter than long range [artillery](2508.md), such as [field guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_gun) and [howitzers](14392.md), which allows light (60 mm or 2.4 in) and medium (81 and 82 mm or 3.19 and 3.23 in) mortars to be considered light weapons; i.e. capable of transport by personnel without vehicle assistance.&#x20;

Mortars are short-range weapons and often more effective than long range artillery for many purposes within their shorter range. In particular, because of its high, [parabolic trajectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolic_trajectory) with a near vertical descent, the mortar can land bombs on nearby targets, including those behind obstacles or in [fortifications](204118.md), such as light vehicles behind hills or structures, or infantry in [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench) or [spider holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider_hole). This also makes it possible to launch attacks from positions lower than the target of the attack. (For example, long-range artillery could not shell a target 1 km (0.6 mi) away and 30 m (100 ft) higher, a target easily accessible to a mortar.)



Mortar trajectory comparison

In [trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare), mortars can use [plunging fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plunging_fire) directly into the enemy [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench), which is very hard or impossible to accomplish with long range artillery because of its much flatter trajectory. Mortars are also highly effective when used from concealed positions, such as the natural escarpments on hillsides or from woods, especially if [forward observers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_observer) are being employed in strategic positions to direct fire, an arrangement where the mortar is in relatively close proximity both to its forward observer and its target, allowing for fire to be quickly and accurately delivered with lethal effect. Mortars suffer from instability when used on snow or soft ground, because the recoil pushes them into the ground or snow unevenly. A [Raschen bag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raschen_bag) addresses this problem.

Fin-stabilised mortar bombs do not have to withstand the [rotational forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torque) placed upon them by rifling or greater pressures, and can therefore carry a higher [payload](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload) in a thinner skin than rifled artillery ammunition. Because of the difference in available volume, a smooth-bore mortar of a given diameter will have a greater explosive yield than a similarly sized artillery shell of a gun or howitzer. For example, a 120 mm (4.7 in) mortar bomb has approximately the same explosive capability as a 152 or 155 mm (6.0 or 6.1 in) artillery shell. Also, fin-stabilised munitions fired from a smooth-bore, which do not rely on the spin imparted by a rifled bore for greater accuracy, do not have the drawback of veering in the direction of the spin.

## Largest mortars

For the largest mortars, see [list of the largest cannon by caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_cannon_by_caliber).

From the 17th to the mid-20th century, very heavy, relatively immobile siege mortars were used, of up to 1 m (39 in) calibre, often made of cast iron and with an outside barrel diameter many times that of the bore diameter. An early example was [Roaring Meg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Meg_\(cannon\)), with a 390 mm (15.5 in) barrel diameter and firing a 100 kg (220 lb) hollow ball filled with gunpowder and used during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War) in 1646.

The largest mortars ever developed were the Belgian "[Monster Mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Mortar)" (610 mm or 24 in) developed by [Henri-Joseph Paixhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri-Joseph_Paixhans) in 1832, [Mallet's Mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallet's_Mortar) (910 mm or 36 in) developed by [Robert Mallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mallet) in 1857, and the "[Little David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_David)" (910 mm) developed in the United States for use in World War II. Although the latter two had a calibre of 910 mm, only the "Monster Mortar" was used in combat (at the [Battle of Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Antwerp_\(1832\)) in 1832).[^cite_note-Guiness-40] The World War II German [Karl-Gerät](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ger%C3%A4t) was a 600 mm (24 in) mortar and the largest to see combat in modern warfare.
