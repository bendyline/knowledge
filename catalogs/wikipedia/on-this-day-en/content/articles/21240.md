**Nissan Motor Co., Ltd.**[^cite_note-8], also known as **Nissan Motor Corporation** or just **Nissan**, formerly known as **Jidosha-seizo**, and stylized as **NISSAN**, is a Japanese multinational [automobile manufacturer](163778.md) headquartered in [Yokohama, Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama,_Kanagawa), Japan. The company sells its vehicles under the Nissan and [Infiniti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiniti) brands, and formerly the [Datsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun) brand, with in-house performance tuning products (including cars) under the [Nismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nismo) and [Autech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autech) brands. The company can be traced back to the beginning of the 20th century with the Nissan *[zaibatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaibatsu)*, or [Nissan Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Group).

Since 1999, Nissan has been part of the [Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan%E2%80%93Mitsubishi_Alliance) (with Mitsubishi joining in 2016), a partnership between Nissan and [Mitsubishi Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors) of Japan, with [Renault](162292.md) of France. As of November 2023, Renault holds a 15% voting stake in Nissan, while Nissan holds another 15% stake in Renault.[^cite_note-9] Since October 2016, Nissan held a 34% controlling stake in Mitsubishi Motors.[^cite_note-10] In November 2024, Nissan reduced its stake in Mitsubishi Motors from 34% to 24%.[^cite_note-11]

In 2017, Nissan was the sixth largest automaker in the world, after [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota), [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group), [Hyundai Motor Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_Group), [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), and [Ford](30433662.md).[^cite_note-OICA_ranking-12] With a revenue of \$78 billion in 2022, Nissan was the ninth largest automobile maker in the world. After making a ¥671 billion loss in the 2024/2025 financial year, new CEO Ivan Espinosa announced that Nissan will by 2027 close seven factories with the loss of 20,000 jobs, and reduce car models from 56 to 45 to focus investment on more profitable models.[^cite_note-guardian-20250513-13][^cite_note-guardian-20260414-14]

## History

### Beginnings of Datsun brand name from 1914



[Datsun Type 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Type_11)

Masujiro Hashimoto ([橋本増治郎](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%A9%8B%E6%9C%AC%E5%A2%97%E6%B2%BB%E9%83%8E)) founded the **Kwaishinsha (Kaishinsha) Motor Car Works** (快進社自働車工場, *Kwaishinsha jidōsha kōjō*; *A Good Company Automobile Manufacturer*) on 1 July 1911 in [Azabu-Hiroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azabu) district of Tokyo. In 1914, the company produced its first car, called the [DAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun).[^cite_note-auto-1][^cite_note-auto1-2][^cite_note-auto2-3]

The new car's model name was an [acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym) of the company's investors' [surnames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname):

- Kenjiro **D**en (*_[Den Kenjirō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Kenjir%C5%8D)_*)

- Rokuro **A**oyama (*_Aoyama Rokurō_*)

- Meitaro **T**akeuchi (*_Takeuchi Meitarō_*)

It was renamed to **Kaishinsha Motorcar Co., Ltd.** in 1918, and again to **DAT Jidosha & Co., Ltd.** (DAT Motorcar Co.) in 1925. DAT Motors built trucks in addition to the DAT and Datsun passenger cars. The vast majority of its output were trucks, due to an almost non-existent consumer market for passenger cars at the time, and disaster recovery efforts as a result of the [1923 Great Kantō earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Great_Kant%C5%8D_earthquake). Beginning in 1918, the first DAT trucks were produced for the military market. At the same time, **Jitsuyo Jidosha Co., Ltd.** (jitsuyo means practical use or utility) produced small trucks using parts, and materials imported from the United States.[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15]

See also: [Historic vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Bluebird#Historic_vehicles)

Commercial operations were placed on hold during [Japan's participation in World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_during_World_War_I), and the company contributed to the war effort.

In 1926, the Tokyo-based DAT Motors merged with the [Osaka](52230.md)-based Jitsuyo Jidosha Co., Ltd (*Jitsuyō Jidōsha Seizō Kabushiki-Gaisha*) a.k.a. **Jitsuyo Jidosha Seizo** (established 1919 as a [Kubota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubota_Corporation) subsidiary) to become **DAT Jidosha Seizo Co., Ltd Automobile Manufacturing Co., Ltd.** (ダット自動車製造株式会社, *DAT Jidōsha Seizō Kabushiki-Gaisha*) in Osaka until 1932. From 1923 to 1925, the company produced light cars and trucks under the name of Lila.[^cite_note-16] In 1929, DAT Automobile Manufacturing Inc. merged with a separated part of the manufacturing business of [IHI Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHI_Corporation) to become Automobile Industries Co., Ltd.

In 1931, DAT came out with a new smaller car, called the [Datsun Type 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Type_11), the first "Datson", meaning "Son of DAT". Later in 1933, after [Nissan Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Group) *[zaibatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaibatsu)* took control of DAT Motors, the last syllable of Datson was changed to "sun", because "son" also means "loss" in Japanese, hence the name "[Datsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun)" (ダットサン, *Dattosan*).[^cite_note-17]

In 1933, the company name was [Nipponized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipponized_English_terms) to **Jidosha-Seizo Co., Ltd.** (*Jidōsha Seizō Kabushiki-Gaisha*; "Automobile Manufacturing Share Company") and was moved to [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama).

### Nissan name debut

In 1928, [Yoshisuke Aikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshisuke_Aikawa) (nickname: Gisuke/Guisuke Ayukawa) founded the [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company) **Nihon Sangyo** (日本産業 Japan Industries or Nihon Industries). The name 'Nissan' originated during the 1930s as an abbreviation[^cite_note-18] used on the [Tokyo Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Stock_Exchange) for **Ni**hon **San**gyo. This company was [Nissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Group) "[Zaibatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaibatsu)" which included Tobata Casting and [Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi). At this time Nissan controlled foundries and auto parts businesses, but Aikawa did not enter automobile manufacturing until 1933.[^cite_note-19]

The zaibatsu eventually grew to include 74 firms and became the fourth-largest in Japan during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-20] In 1931, DAT Jidosha Seizo became affiliated with **Tobata Casting** and was merged into Tobata Casting in 1933. As Tobata Casting was a Nissan company, this was the beginning of Nissan's automobile manufacturing.[^cite_note-21]

### Nissan Motor

In 1934, Aikawa separated the expanded automobile parts division of Tobata Casting and incorporated it as a new subsidiary, which he named **Nissan Motor Co., Ltd.** (日産自動車, *Nissan Jidōsha*).[^cite_note-Odin,_L.C._1939-22] The shareholders of the new company; however, were not enthusiastic about the prospects of the automobile in Japan, so Aikawa bought out all the Tobata Casting shareholders (using capital from Nihon Industries) in June 1934. At this time, Nissan Motor effectively became owned by [Nihon Sangyo and Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Group).[^cite_note-23]

In 1935, the construction of its Yokohama plant was completed. 44 Datsuns were shipped to Asia, Central and South America. In 1935, the first car manufactured by an [integrated assembly system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line) rolled off the line at the Yokohama plant.[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15] Nissan built trucks, airplanes, and engines for the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army). In November 1937 Nissan moved its headquarters to [Xinjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changchun), the capital of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo). In December the company changed its name to [Manchuria Heavy Industries Developing Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchurian_Industrial_Development_Company) (MHID).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

In 1940, the first knockdown kits were shipped to [Dowa Jidosha Kogyo (Dowa Automobile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowa_Automobile_Company), one of MHID's companies, for assembly.[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15] In 1944, the head office was moved to [Nihonbashi, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihonbashi), and the company name was changed to Nissan Heavy Industries, Ltd., which the company kept through 1949.[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15]

### American market and expansion



The Graham-Paige based Nissan Model 70 sedan

DAT had inherited [Kubota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubota_Corporation)'s chief designer, American engineer [William R. Gorham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gorham_\(engineer\)). This, along with Aikawa's 1908 visit to Detroit, was to greatly affect Nissan's future.[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15][^cite_note-NISSANHISTORY-26] Although it had always been Aikawa's intention to use cutting-edge auto making technology from America, it was Gorham that carried out the plan. Most of the machinery and processes originally came from the United States. When Nissan started to assemble larger vehicles under the "Nissan" brand in 1937, much of the design plans and plant facilities were supplied by the [Graham-Paige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham-Paige) Company.[^cite_note-Odin,_L.C._1939-22] Nissan also had a Graham license under which passenger cars, buses, and trucks were made.[^cite_note-NISSANHISTORY-26]

In his 1986 book *[The Reckoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reckoning_\(1986_book\))*, [David Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Halberstam) states "In terms of technology, Gorham was the founder of the Nissan Motor Company" and that "young Nissan engineers who had never met him spoke of him as a god and could describe in detail his years at the company and his many inventions."[^cite_note-Halberstam-27]

### Austin Motor Company relations (1937–1960s)



Austin Seven Ruby



1953 Nissan labor dispute

From 1934, Datsun began to build [Austin 7s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_7) under license. This operation became the greatest success of [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Motor_Company#1919%E2%80%931939:_Interwar_success)'s overseas licensing of its Seven and marked the beginning of Datsun's international success.[^cite_note-28]

In 1952, Nissan entered into a legal agreement with Austin,[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] for Nissan to assemble 2,000 Austins from imported partially assembled sets and sell them in Japan under the Austin trademark. The agreement called for Nissan to make all Austin parts locally within three years, a goal Nissan met. Nissan produced and marketed Austins for seven years. The agreement also gave Nissan the rights to use Austin patents, which Nissan used in developing its own engines for its [Datsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun) line of cars. In 1953, British-built Austins were assembled and sold, but by 1955, the [Austin A50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_A50) – completely built by Nissan and featuring a new 1489 cc engine — was on the market in Japan. Nissan produced 20,855 Austins from 1953 to 1959.[^cite_note-31]

Nissan leveraged the Austin patents to further develop its own modern engine designs beyond what Austin's [A-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMC_A-Series_engine) and [B-family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMC_B-Series_engine) designs offered. The apex of the Austin-derived engines was the new design [A series engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_A_engine) in 1966. In 1967, Nissan introduced its new highly advanced four-cylinder overhead cam (OHC) [Nissan L engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_L_engine), which while similar to [Mercedes-Benz](18564164.md) OHC designs was a totally new engine designed by Nissan. This engine powered the new [Datsun 510](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_510), which gained Nissan respect in the worldwide [sedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedan_\(car\)) market. Then, in 1969, Nissan introduced the [Datsun 240Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_240Z) sports car which used a six-cylinder variation of the [L series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_L_engine) engine, developed under Nissan Machinery (Nissan Koki Co., Ltd. [日産工機](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E7%94%A3%E5%B7%A5%E6%A9%9F)) in 1964, a former remnant of another auto manufacturer [Kurogane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyu_Kogyo_Kurogane). The [240Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_240Z) was an immediate sensation and lifted Nissan to world-class status in the automobile market.[^cite_note-32]

During the [Korean War](16772.md), Nissan was a major vehicle producer for the US Army.[^cite_note-MultinationMonitorOct1983-33] After the Korean War ended, significant levels of anti-communist sentiment existed in Japan. The union that organized Nissan's workforce was strong and militant.[^cite_note-MultinationMonitorOct1983-33][^cite_note-34] Nissan was in financial difficulties, and when wage negotiations came, the company took a hard line. Workers were locked out, and several hundred were fired. The Japanese government and the US occupation forces arrested several union leaders.[^cite_note-MultinationMonitorOct1983-33] The union ran out of strike funds and was defeated. A new labor union was formed,[^cite_note-35] with Shioji Ichiro one of its leaders. Ichiro had studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) on a US government scholarship. He advanced an idea to trade wage cuts against saving 2,000 jobs.[^cite_note-Lohr-36] Ichiro's idea was made part of a new union contract[^cite_note-Lohr-36] that prioritized productivity. Between 1955 and 1973, Nissan "expanded rapidly on the basis of technical advances supported – and often suggested – by the union." Ichiro became president of the [Confederation of Japan Automobile Workers' Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_Japan_Automobile_Workers'_Unions) and "the most influential figure in the right wing of the Japanese labor movement."[^cite_note-MultinationMonitorOct1983-33]

### Merger with Prince Motor Company



1966 [Prince R380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_R380) race car

In 1966, Nissan merged with the [Prince Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Motor_Company), bringing more upmarket cars, including the [Skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Skyline) and [Gloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Gloria), into its selection. The Prince name was eventually abandoned, and successive Skylines and Glorias bore the Nissan name. Prince also possessed the necessary expertise in [front wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_wheel_drive) that Nissan lacked - this gave way to the first generation [Cherry/100A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Cherry) model in 1971. "Prince" was used at the Japanese Nissan dealership "[Nissan Prince Shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motor_Company#Japan)" until 1999, when "Nissan Red Stage" replaced it. Nissan Red Stage itself has been replaced as of 2007. The Skyline lives on as the G Series of [Infiniti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiniti).

To capitalize on the renewed investment during [1964 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics), Nissan established the gallery on the second and third floors of the San-ai building, located in [Ginza, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginza,_Tokyo). To attract visitors, Nissan started using beautiful female showroom attendants where Nissan held a competition to choose five candidates as the first class of Nissan Miss Fairladys, modeled after "Datsun Demonstrators" from the 1930s who introduced cars. The Fairlady name was used as a link to the popular Broadway play *[My Fair Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Fair_Lady)* of the era. Miss Fairladys became the marketers of the [Datsun Fairlady 1500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_1500,_1600,_2000_Roadster).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

In April 2008, 14 more Miss Fairlady candidates were added, for a total of 45 Nissan Miss Fairlady pageants (22 in Ginza, 8 in Sapporo, 7 in Nagoya, 7 in Fukuoka).[^cite_note-40]

In April 2012, 7 more Miss Fairlady candidates were added, for a total of 48 Nissan Miss Fairlady pageants (26 in Ginza, 8 in Sapporo, 7 in Nagoya, 7 in Fukuoka).[^cite_note-41]

In April 2013, 6 more Miss Fairlady candidates were added to Ginza showroom, for a total of 27 48th Ginza Nissan Miss Fairlady pageants.[^cite_note-42]

### Expansion into foreign markets



1971 [Datsun 240Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_240Z) (US) in green metallic



1980 Datsun 720 2-door "King Cab" (US)

In the 1950s Nissan decided to expand into worldwide markets. Nissan management realized, that its Datsun small car line would fulfill an unmet need in markets such as Australia and the world's largest car market, the United States. Nissan first showcased the [Datsun Bluebird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Bluebird#310/311/312_series) at the 1958 [Los Angeles Auto Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Auto_Show).[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15][^cite_note-43] Nissan had entered the Middle East market in 1957 when it sold its first car in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-44] The company formed a US subsidiary, [Nissan Motor Corporation U.S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motor_Corporation_U.S.A.), in Gardena, California[^cite_note-45] in 1960 headed by [Yutaka Katayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutaka_Katayama).[^cite_note-NISSANCORPORATEHISTORY-15] Nissan continued to improve the Sedan series with the latest technological advancements and chic Italianate styling for sporty cars such as the [Datsun Fairlady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Fairlady) roadsters, the race-winning [411 series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Bluebird), the [Datsun 510](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_510) and the [Datsun 240Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_240Z). By 1970 Nissan had become one of the world's largest exporters of automobiles.[^cite_note-46] In 1970, Teocar was established, which was a Greek assembly plant created in cooperation with local Nissan distributor Theocharakis.[^cite_note-ΤΕΟΚΑΡ_ΑΒΕΕ-47]

Nissan Mexicana was established in the early-1960s and commenced manufacturing in 1966 at the [Cuernavaca assembly facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civac), making it Nissan's first North American assembly plant. In the wake of the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), consumers worldwide, especially in the lucrative US market, began turning to high-quality small economy cars. To meet the growing demand of the new [Nissan Sunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Sunny), the company built new factories in Mexico, Australia, New Zealand, Taiwan, United States, and South Africa. The "[Chicken Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_Tax)" of 1964 placed a 25 percent tax on commercial vans imported to the United States. Nissan Motor Manufacturing Corporation USA was established in 1980.[^cite_note-wsjtransit-48] Nissan, [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) and [Honda](13729.md) began establishing manufacturing plants in the US in the early-1980s.[^cite_note-wsjtransit-48] Nissan's initial assembly plant [Smyrna assembly plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Smyrna_Assembly_Plant) broke ground in 1980 and at first only built trucks such as the [Datsun 720](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_720) and the [Nissan Hardbody Truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Hardbody_Truck), but has since expanded to produce several car and SUV lines, including the [Nissan Altima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Altima), the [Nissan Maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Maxima), as well as the Rogue, Pathfinder, Infiniti QX60, and the [Nissan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf) [all-electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-electric_car). The addition of mass-market automobiles was in response to the 1981 Voluntary Export Restraints imposed by the US Government. An engine plant in [Decherd, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decherd,_Tennessee) followed. Most recently a second assembly plant was established in [Canton, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton,_Mississippi).[^cite_note-ΤΕΟΚΑΡ_ΑΒΕΕ-47]



(Alfa-Romeo built) 1985 Nissan Cherry Europe

By the early-1980s, Nissan Datsun had long been the best selling Japanese brand in Europe.[^cite_note-nouv-49] The Greek manufacturing plant itself was closed. A joint venture with Italy's then state-owned [Alfa Romeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo) was also entered in 1980, leading to Italian production of the [Nissan Cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Cherry) and an Alfa-badged and motorized version, the [Alfa Romeo Arna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_Arna).[^cite_note-Arna-50]

In 2001 Nissan established a manufacturing plant in Brazil. In 2005 Nissan added operations in India, through its subsidiary [Nissan Motor India Private Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motor_India_Private_Limited).[^cite_note-nmipl-corp-51] With its global alliance partner [Renault](162292.md), Nissan invested \$990 million to set up a manufacturing facility in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), catering to the Indian market as well as a base for exports of small cars to Europe.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-et-launch-53]

In the United States, Nissan has been increasing its reliance on sales to daily-rental companies like [Enterprise Rent-A-Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_Rent-A-Car) or [Hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hertz_Corporation). In 2016, Nissan's rental sales jumped 37% and in 2017 Nissan became the only major automaker to boost rental sales when the [Detroit Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Three) cut back less profitable deliveries to daily-rental companies, which traditionally are the biggest customers of domestic automakers.[^cite_note-56]

### Project 901

Project 901 was an initiative in the mid-1980s, and it represented a desire for Nissan to offer technologically advanced cars by 1990. Known by various names such as P901 Activity, 901 Activity, 901 Plan, 901 Operation, and Project 901, the project began under the leadership of Nissan President Yutaka Kume (1985–1992).

Project 901 was initiated in early 1985 in response to the growing demand for new, technically advanced cars from all Japanese manufacturers. With a market share of around 25% then, Nissan saw a decline to less than 20%, prompting the company to address its shortcomings. Under the banner of 'Aiming to be the world's best in technology by the 1990s,' the plan focused on the technological development of chassis, engines, suspensions, handling, design, and quality improvements for all car models to be introduced before 1990.[^cite_note-Project901-57][^cite_note-Nissan_History-58]

### Alliance with Renault

Further information: [Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan%E2%80%93Mitsubishi_Alliance)

In 1999, facing severe financial difficulties, Nissan entered an alliance with [Renault](162292.md) of France.[^cite_note-\:0-59] In June 2001, Renault executive [Carlos Ghosn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ghosn) was named chief executive officer of Nissan. In May 2005, Ghosn was named president of Nissan's partner company Renault. He was appointed president and CEO of Renault on 6 May 2009.[^cite_note-60]

Under CEO Ghosn's "Nissan Revival Plan" (NRP), the company has rebounded in what many leading economists consider to be one of the most spectacular corporate turnarounds in history, catapulting Nissan to record profits and a dramatic revitalization of both its Nissan and [Infiniti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiniti) model line-ups.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Ghosn has been recognized in Japan for the company's turnaround in the midst of an [ailing Japanese economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Decade_\(Japan\)). Ghosn and the Nissan turnaround were featured in Japanese [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga) and popular culture. His achievements in revitalizing Nissan were noted by the Japanese government, which awarded him the [Japan Medal with Blue Ribbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medals_of_Honour_\(Japan\)) in 2004.[^cite_note-63]

On 1 April 2017, Ghosn stepped down as CEO of Nissan, while remaining chairman of the company. He was replaced as CEO by his then-deputy [Hiroto Saikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroto_Saikawa).[^cite_note-64] On 19 November 2018, Ghosn was fired as chairman following his arrest for the alleged under-reporting of his income to Japanese financial authorities.[^cite_note-65] After 108 days in detention, Ghosn was released on bail, but after 29 days he was again detained on new charges (4 April 2019). He had been due to hold a news conference, but instead, his lawyers released a video of Ghosn alleging this 2018–19 Nissan scandal is itself evidence of value destruction and Nissan corporate mismanagement.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] In September 2019, Saikawa resigned as CEO, following allegations of improper payments received by him.[^cite_note-68] Yasuhiro Yamauchi was appointed as acting CEO.[^cite_note-69] In October 2019, the company appointed Makoto Uchida as its next CEO.[^cite_note-70] On 1 December 2019, Uchida became CEO.[^cite_note-71]

In January 2023, Renault said it intended to transfer almost 30% of its controlling stake in Nissan to a French trust (pending approval by both companies), reducing its shares with voting rights to a minority 15% and, in doing so, matching Nissan shares in Renault to gain equal voting rights. The shareholding and voting ratio of both companies is set to be fixed in the future. The agreement also included Nissan investing in Ampere (a proposed Renault subsidiary for electric cars) and projects in various markets.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] In February 2023, both companies approved the going-ahead for the shareholding changes. Final details and regulatory clearances for the transaction were set to be completed by the first quarter of 2023 and it would be done by the fourth quarter. The companies also approved joint projects and Nissan's Ampere investment.[^cite_note-74] The share transfer was completed in November 2023.[^cite_note-share-6]

### Financial difficulties

Starting in late-July 2019, Nissan laid off 12,500 employees over three years, citing a 95% year on year net income fall. Hiroto Saikawa, CEO at the time, confirmed the majority of those cuts would be plant workers.[^cite_note-75]

In May 2020, Nissan cut production capacity by 20% due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). It further announced that its chief operating officer [Ashwani Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswani_Gupta_\(Adani_Port\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) was to be in charge of rebuilding the Japanese carmaker's brand in the US, the company also decided on cutting its model line-up and closing plants around the globe.[^cite_note-76] In mid-2020, the company shut down factories in Indonesia and Spain, and exited the South Korean car market.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Nissan pulled out of South Korea by December due to worsening business environment amidst the pandemic and the [2019 boycott of Japanese products in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_boycott_of_Japanese_products_in_South_Korea).[^cite_note-79] Service centers were managed to provide after-sales services such as vehicle quality assurance and parts management for eight years.[^cite_note-80] In November 2020, Nissan reported a \$421 million loss in the last quarter due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the scandal concerning Ghosn.[^cite_note-81] According to a spokesperson of Nissan North America, the company had suffered from a strategy of "volume at any cost", which has been attributed by analysts to Ghosn.[^cite_note-82] Commentators note that the company's US sales, which had dropped by half in seven years between 2017 and 2024 were likely caused by well-publicized reliability issues stemming from the company's [Xtronic CVT transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_variable_transmission) and, to a lesser extent, problems with the [Rogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Rogue)'s four-wheel drive system.[^cite_note-83]

In 2023, news emerged about serious disagreements between COO Ashwani Gupta and CEO Makoto Uchida. This led to exit of Gupta and caused further turmoil in the company.[^cite_note-84]

In November 2024, Nissan created an emergency turnaround plan which saw it cut its annual operating profit forecast by 70% to 150 billion yen (\$975 million), marking its second downward revision after a 17% cut earlier in the year.[^cite_note-Inagaki-85] Nissan also planned to cut 9,000 jobs and cut global production capacity by 20%.[^cite_note-Inagaki-85] Around this time, a Nissan executive told the Financial Times that, unless there is a major turnaround, Nissan will cease to exist in "12 to 14 months".[^cite_note-86] Sales were down across the board, with US sales down by almost half since 2017, thanks in large part to unreliable CVT transmissions and weak rear differentials.[^cite_note-87]

### Attempted merger with Honda

On 23 December 2024, Nissan officially announced an MOU had been entered to merge with fellow Japanese automaker [Honda](13729.md) to become the 3rd largest auto company by sales. [Mitsubishi Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors), in which Nissan has 24% ownership, also agreed to join the talks of integration.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

In February 2025, Honda and Nissan announced that their boards had voted to end talks to merge. Nissan reportedly backed out of the talks with larger rival Honda after negotiations were complicated by growing differences, including Honda's proposal that Nissan become a subsidiary.[^cite_note-90]

In March 2025, Nissan announced that Chief Planning Officer [Ivan Espinosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iv%C3%A1n_Espinosa_\(businessman\)) would take over as chief executive officer from 1 April 2025, and Uchida would be stepping down amid mounting pressure due to the company's financial crisis and the failed merger with Honda.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

### Turnaround plans

After a ¥671 billion loss in the 2024/2025 financial year, CEO Ivan Espinosa announced that Nissan will by 2027 close seven factories globally, with the loss of 20,000 jobs, reducing the number of factories from 17 to 10.[^cite_note-guardian-20250513-13] The seven factories are Nissan's Oppama and Shatai Shonan Plants in [Kanagawa Prefecture](61757.md) in Japan, the Civac and COMPAS Plants in [Mexico](3966054.md), as well as three other factories in [South Africa](17416221.md), [India](14533.md), and [Argentina](18951905.md).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] In April 2026, Espinosa announced the number of car models would be reduced from 56 to 45, to focus investment on more profitable models. He reaffirmed the commitment to [hybrid cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_car), and announced plans to work toward providing [autonomous driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car) in 90% of vehicles.[^cite_note-guardian-20260414-14]

In June 2026, Nissan announced that it would reduce the development time for new vehicles to 26 months, down from 55 for previous generations, by expanding the use of artificial intelligence and digital engineering tools throughout its design, testing, and manufacturing processes. The company said the new development approach would be implemented across approximately 90% of its vehicle programs by the end of fiscal 2026, with the next generation [Skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Skyline) becoming the first model developed under the accelerated process.[^cite_note-96]

## Nissan technologies

In 1982, Nissan's first final assembly robots were installed in the Murayama plant, where the then-new [March/Micra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_March) was assembled. In 1984, the Zama plant began to be robotized; this automation process then continued throughout Nissan's factories.[^cite_note-DF87-97]

[Nissan electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_electric_vehicles) have been produced intermittently since 1946. In 2010, the [Nissan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf_\(first_generation\)) plug-in [battery electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_electric_vehicle) was introduced; it was the world's most sold [plug-in electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_electric_car) for nearly a decade. It was preceded by the [Altra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Altra) and the [Hypermini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Hypermini). Until surpassed by [Tesla](5533631.md), Nissan was the world's largest [electric vehicle (EV)](279350.md) manufacturer, with global sales of more than 320,000 all-electric vehicles as of April 2018.[^cite_note-Leaf3bi-98] In 2022, it was announced that Nissan was intending to create [solid-state batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_batteries) for electric vehicles.[^cite_note-99] In July 2026, amid the [Dieselgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieselgate) scandal, British courts dismissed the allegations against five manufacturers, including Nissan, ruling in their favor.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

Nissan main office

[LDV Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDV_Group) sold a badge-engineered light commercial vehicle version of the [Nissan Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Serena) as the [LDV Cub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDV_Cub) from 1996 to 2001. In March 2023, Nissan and [Honda](13729.md) signed a memorandum of understanding regarding a strategic partnership to collaborate on producing components for electric vehicles and [artificial intelligence](1164.md) in automotive software platforms.[^cite_note-111] The following year, talks between the two companies reportedly became deeper as a merger was being discussed.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] The merger talks collapsed, however, and in February 2025 they were officially cancelled.

### Alliance with Renault and Mitsubishi

Main article: [Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan%E2%80%93Mitsubishi_Alliance)



Former CEO [Carlos Ghosn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ghosn) has been credited with reviving Nissan.

In 1999, facing severe financial difficulties, Nissan entered an alliance with [Renault](162292.md) of France.[^cite_note-\:0-59] Signed on 27 March 1999, the *Renault-Nissan Alliance* was the first of its kind involving a Japanese and French car manufacturer, each with its own distinct corporate culture and brand identity. Renault initially acquired a 36.8 percent stake in Nissan for \$3.5 billion pending court approval and Nissan permanently vowed to buy into Renault when it was financially able. In 2001, after the company's turnaround from near-bankruptcy, Nissan acquired a 15 percent share of Renault, which in turn increased its stake in Nissan to 43.4 percent.[^cite_note-Renault–Nissan-114]

The Renault-Nissan Alliance has evolved over the years to Renault holding 43.4 percent of Nissan shares, while Nissan held 15 percent of Renault shares. The alliance itself was incorporated as the Renault-Nissan B.V., founded on 28 March 2002 under Dutch law. Renault-Nissan B.V. is equally owned by Renault and Nissan.[^cite_note-115]

On 7 April 2010, [Daimler AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_AG) exchanged a 3.1 percent share of its holdings for 3.1 percent from both Nissan and Renault. This triple alliance allows for the increased sharing of technology and development costs, encouraging global cooperation and mutual development.[^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-116]

On 12 December 2012, the Renault–Nissan Alliance formed a joint venture with Russian Technologies (Alliance Rostec Auto BV) with the aim of becoming the long-term controlling shareholder of [AvtoVAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AvtoVAZ), Russia's largest car company and owner of the country's biggest selling brand, [Lada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada).[^cite_note-alliance-renault-nissan1-117] The takeover was completed in June 2014, and the two companies of the Renault-Nissan Alliance took a combined 67.1 percent stake of Alliance Rostec, which in turn acquired a 74.5 percent of AvtoVAZ, thereby giving Renault and Nissan indirect control over the Russian manufacturer.[^cite_note-118] Ghosn was appointed chairman of the board of AvtoVAZ on 27 June 2013.[^cite_note-119] In September 2017, Nissan sold its AvtoVAZ stake to Renault for Euro 45 million.[^cite_note-120]

Taken together, in 2013 the Renault–Nissan Alliance sold one in ten cars worldwide, and would be the world's fourth largest automaker with sales of 8,266,098 units.[^cite_note-dailykanban1-121]

In March 2023, Renault and Nissan announced a change in stock share between two companies, becoming economically independent entities.[^cite_note-122]

### &#x20;Corporate identity



Carlos Ghosn in front of new CI at the 2013 earnings press conference in Yokohama

For many years, Nissan used a red wordmark for the company, and car badges for the "Nissan" and "Infiniti" brands.[^cite_note-134] At Nissan's 2013 earnings press conference in Yokohama, Nissan unveiled "a new steel-blue logo that spells out—literally—the distinction between Nissan the company and Nissan the brand."[^cite_note-135] Using a blue-gray color scheme, the new corporate logo did read NISSAN MOTOR COMPANY. Underneath were the "badge" logos for the Nissan, Infiniti and Datsun brands.

Later in 2013, the Nissan "Company" logo changed to the Nissan "Corporation" logo. The latter was the logo used by Nissan Motor Co., Ltd.[^cite_note-136] up to early 2020. In July 2020, Nissan introduced new corporate and brand logos, as part of an image revamp tied to the [Ariya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Ariya) launch.[^cite_note-137]

As of 2007 in Japan, Nissan sells its products with internationally recognized "Nissan" signage, using a chrome circle with "Nissan" across the front.

Nissan Red Stage dealership in Tokorozawa, Saitama Prefecture

Nissan Red Stage was the result of combining an older sales channel of dealerships under the names "Nissan Prince Store" ([ja\:日産・プリンス店](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E7%94%A3%E3%83%BB%E3%83%97%E3%83%AA%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E5%BA%97), *Nissan Purinsu-ten*), established in 1966 after the merger of [Prince Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Motors) by Nissan, which sold the [Nissan Skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Skyline). "Nissan Satio Store" (日産・サティオ店, *Nissan Satio-ten*) sold cars developed from the [Nissan Sunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Sunny) at its introduction in 1966. The word "[satio](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/satio)" is Latin, which means *ample* or *sufficient*. "Nissan Cherry Store" (日産・チェリー店, *Nissan Cherī-ten*) was briefly known previously as "Nissan Cony Store" when it assumed operations of a small *kei* manufacturer called [Aichi Machine Industry Co., Ltd. (愛知機械工業)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aichi_Kokuki) who manufactured the "[Cony](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B3%E3%83%8B%E3%83%BC%E3%83%BB360)", "[Guppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cony_Guppy)" and ["Giant"](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%82%E3%83%A3%E3%82%A4%E3%82%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88) brand of *[kei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_car)* cars and trucks until 1970, when the network was renamed for the [Nissan Cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Cherry).[^cite_note-138]

Nissan Blue Stage was the result of combining older sales channels, called "Nissan Store" ([ja\:日産店](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E7%94%A3%E5%BA%97), *Nissan Mise*) in 1955 selling Nissan's original post-war products called the [Datsun Bluebird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Bluebird), [Datsun Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Sports), [Datsun Truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Truck), [Datsun Cablight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyu_Kogyo_Kurogane), [Datsun Cabstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Cabstar), [Nissan Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Junior) and the [Nissan Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Patrol). Select "Nissan Store" locations were renamed "Nissan Cedric Store" when the [Nissan Cedric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Cedric) was introduced in 1960, then renamed "Nissan Store" in 1965 and offered luxury sedans like the [Nissan President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_President) and the former Prince Motor Company developed [Nissan Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Laurel). In 1970, Nissan also set up a separate sales chain which sold used cars including auctions, called Nissan U-Cars ([ja\:日産ユーズドカーセンター](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E7%94%A3%E3%83%A6%E3%83%BC%E3%82%BA%E3%83%89%E3%82%AB%E3%83%BC%E3%82%BB%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC), *Nissan Yūzudo Kā Sentā*), which it still maintains.

In the early days of Nissan's dealership network, Japanese consumers were directed towards specific Nissan stores for cars that were of a specific size and pricepoint. Over time as sales progressed and the Japanese automotive industry became more prolific, vehicles that were dedicated to particular stores were [badge engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badge_engineered), given different names, and shared within the existing networks thereby selling the same platforms at different locations. The networks allowed Nissan to better compete with the network established earlier by [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_dealerships_\(Japan\)) at Japanese locations. Starting in 1960, another sales distribution channel was established that sold diesel products for commercial use, called [Nissan Diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Diesel) until the diesel division was sold in 2007 to [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo). To encourage retail sales, Nissan passenger vehicles that were installed with diesel engines, like the Cedric, were available at Nissan Diesel locations.

### Automotive products

Nissan has produced an extensive range of mainstream cars and trucks, initially for domestic consumption but exported around the world since the 1950s. It also produced several memorable [sports cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_car), including the [Datsun Fairlady 1500, 1600 and 2000 Roadsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_1500,_1600,_2000_Roadster), the [Z-car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Z-car), an affordable sports car originally introduced in 1969; and the [GT-R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_GT-R), a powerful [all-wheel-drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-wheel-drive) sports coupe. In 1985, Nissan created a tuning division, *[Nismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nismo)*, for competition and performance development of such cars. One of Nismo's latest models is the [370Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_370Z) Nismo.

Nissan also sells a range of [kei cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_car), mainly as a joint venture with other Japanese manufacturers like [Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki) or [Mitsubishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors). Until 2013, Nissan rebadged kei cars built by other manufacturers. Beginning in 2013, Nissan and Mitsubishi shared the development of the [Nissan DAYZ / Mitsubishi eK Wagon series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_eK).[^cite_note-Mitsu-127] Nissan also has shared model development of Japanese domestic cars with other manufacturers, particularly [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda), [Subaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru), [Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki) and [Isuzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isuzu). In China, Nissan produces cars in association with the [Dongfeng Motor Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongfeng_Motor_Group) including the 2006 [Nissan Livina Geniss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Livina_Geniss), the first in a range of a new worldwide family of medium-sized cars. In 2010, Nissan created another tuning division, *[IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiniti_Performance_Line)*, this time for their premium/luxury brand Infiniti.

In 2011, after Nissan released the [Nissan NV-Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_NV_\(North_America\)) in the United States, Canada, and Mexico, Nissan created a commercial sub-brand called *Nissan Commercial Vehicles* which focuses on commercial vans, pickup trucks, and fleet vehicles for the US, Canadian, and Mexican Markets.[^cite_note-150] In 2013, Nissan launched the [Qashqai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Qashqai) SUV in South Africa, along with its new motorsport Qashqai Car Games. It is the same year when the Datsun brand was relaunched by Nissan after a 27-year hiatus.[^cite_note-151]

Nissan launched its **Nissan Intelligent Mobility** vision in 2016 by revealing the IDS Concept at the [2016 Geneva Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Motor_Show#2016).[^cite_note-152] Most Nissan vehicles like the [Dayz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_eK), [Rogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Rogue) and [Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf) are equipped with Nissan Intelligent Mobility technology. In 2018, Nissan launched the sixth-generation [Altima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Altima) at the [2018 New York Auto Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Auto_Show#2018).[^cite_note-153]

### Trucks



2019 Nissan Titan XD Pro-4X

Nissan Cabstar (日産・キャブスター *Nissan Kyabusutā*) is the name used in Japan for two lines of pickup trucks and light commercial vehicles sold by Nissan and built by UD Nissan Diesel, a Volvo AB company and by Renault-Nissan Alliance for the European market. The name originated with the 1968 Datsun Cabstar, but this was gradually changed over to "Nissan" badging in the early 1980s. The lighter range (1-1.5 tons) replaced the earlier Cabstar and Homer, while the heavier Caball and Clipper were replaced by the 2–4 ton range Atlas (日産・アトラス *Nissan Atorasu*). The nameplate was first introduced in December 1981. The Cabstar is known also as the Nissan Cabstar, Renault Maxity and Samsung SV110 depending on the location. The range has been sold across the world. It shares its platform with the Nissan Caravan.

The [Nissan Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Titan) was introduced in 2004, as a [full-size pickup truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-size_pickup_truck) produced for the North American market, the truck shares the stretched Nissan F-Alpha platform with the [Nissan Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Armada) and [Infiniti QX56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiniti_QX56) [SUVs](47696.md). It was listed by [Edmunds.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmunds.com) as the best full-size truck.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] The second-generation Titan was revealed at the [2015 North American International Auto Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_International_Auto_Show#2015) as a 2016 model year vehicle. The Titan was discontinued in 2024 due to poor sales.

### Electric vehicles

Main article: [Nissan electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_electric_vehicles)



2018 [Nissan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf) at an electric charging station

Nissan introduced its first [battery electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_electric_vehicle), the [Nissan Altra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Altra) at the [Los Angeles International Auto Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_International_Auto_Show#1997) on 29 December 1997.[^cite_note-AltraPR-156] Unveiled in 2009, the EV-11 [prototype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototype) [electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_car) was based on the [Nissan Tiida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Tiida) (Versa in North America), with the conventional gasoline engine replaced with an [all-electric drivetrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_electric_vehicle).[^cite_note-157]

In 2010, Nissan introduced the [first-generation LEAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf_\(first_generation\)) as the first series produced, all-electric vehicle launched globally.[^cite_note-158] As of 2014, the Nissan Leaf was the world's best selling highway-capable all-electric car ever.[^cite_note-Leaf100K-159][^cite_note-TopSellingEver-160] Global sales totaled 100,000 Leafs by mid January 2014, representing a 45 percent [market share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_share) of worldwide pure electric vehicles sold since 2010.[^cite_note-Leaf100K-159] Global Leaf sales passed the 200,000 unit milestone in December 2015, and the Leaf continued ranking as the all-time best selling all-electric car.[^cite_note-Leaf200K-161][^cite_note-Leaf200K_2-162]

Nissan's second all-electric vehicle, the [Nissan e-NV200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_e-NV200), was announced in November 2013.[^cite_note-163] Series production at the Nissan Plan in [Barcelona](4443.md), [Spain](26667.md), began on 7 May 2014.[^cite_note-164] The e-NV200 commercial van is based on the Nissan Leaf. Nissan plans to launch two additional battery electric vehicles by March 2017.[^cite_note-165]

In June 2016, Nissan announced it will introduce its first [range extender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_extender_\(vehicle\)) car in Japan before March 2017. The [series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_hybrid) [plug-in hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) will use a new hybrid system, dubbed e-Power, which debuted with the Nissan Gripz [concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_car) crossover showcased at the September 2015 [Frankfurt Auto Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Auto_Show#2015).[^cite_note-166] As of August 2016, Nissan electric vehicles were sold in 48 world markets.[^cite_note-RN350K-167] Nissan global electric vehicle sales passed 275,000 units in December 2016.[^cite_note-Leaf3bi-98]

In 2018 Nissan sold its battery unit [AESC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_Energy_Supply_Corporation) to [Envision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envision_Energy) in order to focus on the production of vehicles.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] The second-generation [Nissan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf) was launched by Nissan in Japan in 2018. By December 2020, 10 years after its introduction, cumulative global deliveries had reached 500,000 Leaf cars.[^cite_note-Leaf500K-171]

In February 2023, Nissan announced its intent to produce electric vehicles with solid-state batteries by 2028.[^cite_note-172] In November 2023, Nissan announced its intention to build electric versions of the Qashqai and Juke at its plant in Britain.[^cite_note-173]

### Autonomous cars



Nissan [autonomous car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_car) prototype technology was fitted on a [Nissan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf) [all-electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-electric_car).

In August 2013 Nissan announced its plans to launch several [driverless cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driverless_car) by 2020. The company built a dedicated autonomous driving proving ground in Japan, completed in 2014. Nissan installed its autonomous car technology in a [Nissan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Leaf) [all-electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-electric_car) for demonstration purposes and was demonstrated at Nissan 360 test drive event held in California in August 2013.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] In September 2013, the Leaf fitted with the prototype Advanced Driver Assistance System was granted a license plate that allows it to drive on Japanese public roads. The testing car was used by Nissan engineers to evaluate how its in-house autonomous driving software performs in the real world. Time spent on public roads will help refine the car's software for fully automated driving.[^cite_note-176] The autonomous Leaf was demonstrated on public roads for the first time at a media event held in Japan in November 2013. The Leaf drove on the Sagami Expressway in [Kanagawa Prefecture](61757.md), near Tokyo. Nissan vice chairman [Toshiyuki Shiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiyuki_Shiga) and the prefecture's governor, Yuji Kuroiwa, rode in the car during the test.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178]

In 2025, Nissan announced that it will be integrating AI-powered systems made by [Wayve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayve) into its vehicles.[^cite_note-179]

### Non-automotive products

Nissan has also had a number of ventures outside the automotive industry, most notably the Tu–Ka mobile phone service (est. 1994), which was sold to DDI and Japan Telecom (both now merged into [KDDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDDI)) in 1999. Nissan offers a subscription-based [telematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telematics) service in select vehicles to drivers in Japan, called [CarWings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CarWings). Nissan also owns [Nissan Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Marine), a joint venture with [Tohatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohatsu) Corp that produces motors for smaller boats and other maritime equipment.

Nissan also built solid rocket motors for orbital launch vehicles such as the [Lambda 4S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_4S) and [M-V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-V).[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181] The aerospace and defense division of Nissan was sold to [IHI Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHI_Corporation) in 2000.[^cite_note-182]

## Research and development

Nissan's central research[^cite_note-201] is inside the Oppama Plant site, [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka), which began its operation in 1961, at the former site of [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy)'s Airborne Squadron base. In 1982, Nissan's technical centers in [Suginami, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suginami,_Tokyo) and [Tsurumi, Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsurumi-ku,_Yokohama) were combined into one: Nissan Technical Center (NTC) in [Atsugi, Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsugi,_Kanagawa), at the foot of [Mount Ōyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_%C5%8Cyama_\(Kanagawa\)) of the [Tanzawa Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzawa_Mountains). At its 30th anniversary, in 2012, NTC employed 9,500 employees in product development, design, production engineering, and purchasing.[^cite_note-202]

Nissan Technical Center works closely with its overseas operations: Nissan Technical Center (NTC)/North America,[^cite_note-203] NTC/Mexico, Nissan Design America, Nissan [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) Office,[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] and Nissan Technical Centre Europe (NTCE).[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207]

In 2007, the company opened Nissan Advanced Technology Center (NATC), near the NTC site. It works in close contact with the central research, the Silicon Valley office, the technical office near the Nissan headquarters in central [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama), and the overseas offices in [Detroit](8687.md), Silicon Valley, and [Moscow](19004.md).[^cite_note-208]

Nissan's test courses are in [Tochigi](180960.md) (two courses), [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka) and [Hokkaido](58092.md).

### Nissan Digital Hubs

In mid-2018, Nissan launched its first of many planned software and information technology development centers in [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram), Kerala, India.[^cite_note-209]
