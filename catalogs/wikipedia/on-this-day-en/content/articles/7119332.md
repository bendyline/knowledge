**Dharmendra Pradhan** (born 26 June 1969) is an Indian politician who served as the 9th [Minister of Education](49127628.md) from 2021 until his resignation in 2026. He previously served as [Minister of Petroleum and Natural Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Petroleum_and_Natural_Gas_\(India\)) from 2014 to 2021, [Minister of Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Steel_\(India\)) from 2019 to 2021, and [Minister of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Skill_Development_and_Entrepreneurship) from 2017 to 2024. Pradhan is currently a member of the Indian parliament representing the [Sambalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur_Lok_Sabha_constituency) constituency in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) as a member of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md). Previously, he has also served as a member of [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) representing [Bihar](442482.md) and [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md).&#x20;

His tenure as the [union education minister](49127628.md) was marred with irregularities, including leakage of the scripts for [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NEET_controversy) and [2026 NEET (UG) exams](83200188.md). The government conducted a re-examination involving over 2.2 million students after the 2026 leakage.[^cite_note-2] The irregularities led to suicides of at least 12 students, and has served as a catalyst to the [2026 Jantar Mantar protests in Delhi](83705042.md) calling for Pradhan's resignation. The protests turned violent, with clashes occurring between protestors and law enforcement officials, followed by negotiations between government and protestors and resignation of Pradhan.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-5]

## Early life and education

Pradhan was born on 26 June 1969 in [Talcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talcher), [Odisha](250724.md), to Debendra Pradhan, who served as [Minister of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_\(India\)) in the [Vajpayee ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Vajpayee_ministry) (1998–2004), and Basanta Manjari Pradhan. His family hails from the [Chasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasa_\(caste\)) community.[^cite_note-6] He became active in student politics through the [Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhil_Bharatiya_Vidyarthi_Parishad) (ABVP) in 1983 while at Talcher College, serving as president and later secretary of the student union. He earned a postgraduate degree in [Anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) from [Utkal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utkal_University) in [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Political career

### Early political career

Pradhan was elected to the [Odisha Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha_Legislative_Assembly) from [Pallahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallahara_Assembly_constituency) in 2000. He entered the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) in 2004 representing [Deogarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deogarh_Lok_Sabha_constituency) until the constituency was abolished in 2009. He also served as National President of the [Bharatiya Janata Yuva Morcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Yuva_Morcha) (BJYM) from 2004 to 2006, and held various party positions including National Secretary and All India General Secretary of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### Ministerial tenures

#### Petroleum and Natural Gas (2014–2021)

Appointed as Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Petroleum and Natural Gas in 2014, Pradhan was elevated to Cabinet rank in 2017. During his tenure, he oversaw initiatives such as the [Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pradhan_Mantri_Ujjwala_Yojana), which aimed to expand [LPG](361384.md) access in rural India. The ministry also implemented the Hydrocarbon Exploration and Licensing Policy, the PAHAL subsidy reform, and campaigns encouraging voluntary subsidy relinquishment. Investments exceeding USD 60 billion were made to expand city gas distribution networks.[^cite_note-11] He also laid the foundation of [Urja Ganga Gas Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urja_Ganga_Gas_Pipeline_Project) network, provisioning free LPG connections and plugging cooking gas subsidy diversions.[^cite_note-12]

#### Steel (2019–2021)

As Minister of Steel, Pradhan launched initiatives including Ispati Irada and Mission Purvodaya to increase production capacity, strengthen the secondary steel sector, and promote job creation and regional industrial growth.[^cite_note-13]

#### Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (2017–2024)

Pradhan implemented programs aimed at reskilling and upskilling the workforce, expanding [ITI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_training_institute) infrastructure by over 40% and increasing enrollment by 28% until 2019. The [National Council for Vocational Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_for_Vocational_Education_and_Training) (NCVET) and the Skill Saathi platform were established to standardize training and support trainees.[^cite_note-14]

#### Education (2021–2026)

Pradhan assumed office as Minister of Education in July 2021. He oversaw reforms to the [National Testing Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Testing_Agency) (NTA) and the implementation of policies aimed at improving entrance examinations, including commissioning a committee led by former [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO) chief [Koppillil Radhakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koppillil_Radhakrishnan). In 2024, the ministry faced scrutiny following the [2024 NEET controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NEET_controversy). The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) found no evidence of large-scale malpractice, though isolated incidents led to over 100 arrests.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] On 24 June 2024, he took oath as a [Lok Sabha Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha_Member_of_parliament) from [Sambalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur_Lok_Sabha_constituency) amid the [2024 NEET controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NEET_controversy).[^cite_note-17]

### NEET paper leak (2026)

Further information: [2026 NEET controversy](83200188.md) and [2026 Delhi Jantar Mantar protests](83705042.md)

In 2026, NEET-UG again became the subject of controversy after the [National Testing Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Testing_Agency) (NTA) cancelled the 3 May examination amid paper-leak allegations and scheduled a re-examination for 21 June 2026.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] On 6 June 2026, supporters of the [Cockroach Janta Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Janta_Party), a satirical youth-led political movement, held a protest at [Jantar Mantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi) in New Delhi and demanded Pradhan's resignation over examination leaks and marking-related concerns.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] On 25 July 2026, Pradhan resigned his position as Education Minister.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## Personal life

Pradhan is married to Mridula Pradhan since 1998 and has two children. He resides in New Delhi.[^cite_note-24]
