# Beauty pageant

"Beauty contest" redirects here. For other uses, see [Beauty contest (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_contest_\(disambiguation\)).

For the song by Holly Humberstone, see [Beauty Pageant (song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_Pageant_\(song\)).



The winners of the [International Pageant of Pulchritude 1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Pageant_of_Pulchritude) competition

A **beauty pageant** is a [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition) in which the contestants are judged and ranked based on various physical and mental attributes. Per its name, beauty pageants traditionally focus on judging the contestants' [physical attractiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_attractiveness), sometimes solely so, but most modern beauty pageants have since expanded to also judge contestants based on "inner beauty"—their individual traits and characteristics, including [personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality), [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence), [aptitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptitude), [moral character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_character), and [charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_\(practice\)). Though typically perceived as a female-oriented competition, male beauty pageants also exist, as do [child beauty pageants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_beauty_pageant) for youth.

The term beauty pageant refers originally to the [Big Four beauty pageants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_beauty_pageants): [Miss Universe](150340.md), [Miss World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_World), [Miss International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_International), and [Miss Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Earth).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Thousands of beauty contests are held annually,[^cite_note-4] but the Big Four are considered the most prestigious,[^cite_note-5] and are widely covered and broadcast by [news media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-wallstreet-debris-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-gma-afp-11] The earliest formal beauty pageants were held in the 19th century, although similar informal events date back to at least the [post-classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-classical_history). Modern beauty pageants were first established in the early 20th century, with their popularity later boosted by the establishment of international pageants in the mid-20th century. Reforms of pageants in the 21st century marked a shift from primarily focusing on physical appearance to placing more weight on other characteristics.

Beauty pageants are generally multi-tiered, with local competitions feeding into the larger competitions; for example, the international pageants have hundreds or thousands of local competitions. The organizers of each pageant may determine the competition rules, including the age range of contestants. The rules may also require the contestants to be unmarried, and be virtuous, amateur, and available for promotions, besides other criteria. It may also set the clothing standards in which contestants will be judged, such as [formal wear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_wear), [swimsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimsuit), [sportswear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportswear), or [designer clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designer_clothing). Possible awards of beauty contests include titles, [tiaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiara), [crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown), [sashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sash), [bouquets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flower_bouquet), [scepters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sceptre), [savings bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savings_bond), [scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship), and [prize money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_money).[^cite_note-12] The winner of a beauty contest is generally called a **beauty queen** for female pageants and a **beauty king** for male pageants. Pageant titles are often subdivided into Miss, Mrs. or Ms., and Teen, to clearly identify the difference between pageant divisions. The rankings of the contestants are referred to as *placements*.[^cite_note-13]

## History

### Ancient history

*Callisteia* or *Kallisteia* (καλλιστεῖα) were ancient Greek beauty contests celebrated around Greece. One notable example was on the island of [Lesbos](2633994.md), where women gathered in the sanctuary of [Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera) and a prize was awarded to the fairest among them. Another occurred in [Arcadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia_\(region\)) during a festival honoring [Demeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demeter) Eleusinia, where women known as Chrysophoroi (Χρυσοφόροι; "gold-bearers") competed. [Athenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenaeus) mentions [Tenedos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenedos) alongside Lesbos, suggesting similar beauty festivals were held there as well. In addition, he mentions that a beauty contest was held in [Elis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elis) and involved only men, the most handsome received a suit of armor, dedicated it to [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena) and was ceremonially adorned and escorted to the temple by his companions.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In addition to these aesthetic contests, there were other competitions in ancient Greece that blended beauty with physical excellence. These were the Euandria (εὐανδρία) and Euexia (εὐεξία), both part of agonistic festivals. The Euandria was likely a display of masculine beauty where physical size and strength played a key role in judging. The Euexia, by contrast, resembled a modern physique or fitness competition. It emphasized symmetry, muscle tone, posture, and overall health and vitality, rather than sheer bulk.[^cite_note-18]

European festivals dating back to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) provide the most direct lineage for beauty pageants. For example, English [May Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day) celebrations always had the selection of a [May Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Queen). In the United States, the May Day tradition of selecting a woman to serve as a symbol of beauty and community ideals continued, as young, beautiful women participated in public celebrations.[^cite_note-20]

A beauty pageant was held during the [Eglinton Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglinton_Tournament) of 1839, organized by [Archibald Montgomerie, 13th Earl of Eglinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Montgomerie,_13th_Earl_of_Eglinton), as part of a re-enactment of a medieval joust that was held in Scotland. The pageant was won by [Georgiana Seymour, Duchess of Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgiana_Seymour,_Duchess_of_Somerset), the wife of [Edward Seymour, 12th Duke of Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Seymour,_12th_Duke_of_Somerset), and sister of [Caroline Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Norton), and she was proclaimed as the "Queen of Beauty".[^cite_note-21] Beauty contests became more popular in the 1880s. In 1888, the title of 'beauty queen' was awarded to an 18-year-old [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples) contestant at a pageant in [Spa, Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa,_Belgium). All participants had to supply a photograph and a short description of themselves to be eligible to enter and a final selection of 21 was judged by a formal panel.[^cite_note-22] Such events were not regarded as respectable. In 1880, [Rehoboth Beach, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehoboth_Beach,_Delaware), held the first recorded beauty pageant in the United States, searching for "the most beautiful unmarried woman in our nation" and awarding her the title of [Miss United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_United_States).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### National and international pageants

Beauty contests came to be considered more respectable with the first modern "[Miss America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_America)" contest held in 1921.[^cite_note-26] The oldest pageant still in operation today is the [Miss America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_America) pageant, which was organized in 1921 by a local businessman to entice tourists to [Atlantic City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-Almanac-27] The pageant hosted the winners of local newspaper beauty contests in the "Inter-City Beauty" Contest, attended by over one hundred thousand people. Sixteen-year-old Margaret Gorman of Washington, D.C., was crowned Miss America 1921, having won both the popularity and beauty contests, and was awarded \$100.[^cite_note-28]



Bathing beauty contest, USA, 1920

In May 1920, promoter C.E. Barfield of [Galveston, Texas](53840.md), organized a new event known as "Splash Day" on the island. The event featured a "Bathing Girl Revue" competition as the centerpiece of its attractions.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-Miss_United_States-31][^cite_note-GDN:_Pulchritude-32] The event was the kick-off of the summer tourist season in the city and was carried forward annually. The event quickly became known outside of Texas and, beginning in 1926, the world's first international contest was added, known as the [International Pageant of Pulchritude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Pageant_of_Pulchritude).[^cite_note-Miss_United_States-31] This contest is said to have served as a model for modern pageants.[^cite_note-GDN:_Pulchritude-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] It featured contestants from England, Russia, [Turkey](11125639.md), and many other nations and the title awarded at the time was known as "Miss Universe".[^cite_note-GDN:_Pulchritude-32][^cite_note-35] The event was discontinued in the United States in 1932 because of the Depression (the international competition was revived briefly in Belgium).[^cite_note-Jones_Durham_2013_p._120-36][^cite_note-Sudarshan_2022_p._20-37]

The popularity of the Miss America pageant prompted other organizations to establish similar contests in the 1950s and beyond. Some were significant while others were trivial, such as the National Donut Queen contest. The [Miss World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_World) contest started in 1951, [Miss Universe](150340.md) started in 1952, as did [Miss USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_USA). [Miss International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_International) started in 1960. [Miss Asia Pacific International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Asia_Pacific_International), which started in 1968, is the first and oldest beauty pageant in Asia.[^cite_note-IndiaTimes-38][^cite_note-Darling_of_press-39] The [Miss Black America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Black_America) contest started in 1968[^cite_note-timeline-40] in response to the exclusion of African American women from the Miss America pageant. The Miss Universe Organization started the [Miss Teen USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Teen_USA) in 1983 for the 14–19 age group. [Miss Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Earth) started in 2001, which channels the beauty pageant entertainment industry to actively promote the preservation of the environment.[^cite_note-channeling-beauty-41][^cite_note-missearth.tv/about.html-42] These contests continue to this day.

## Diversity

The diversity of contestants and winners has both increased since the inception of beauty pageants. In 1945, [Bess Myerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bess_Myerson), an American politician, model, and television actress, became the first Jewish person to win the Miss America title in Atlantic City and to this day remains the only Jewish person to have received the crown. Her success in winning the title was hugely symbolic and personal to Jewish people at that time because it was in the direct aftermath of the Holocaust.[^cite_note-87] In 1959, Japanese model [Akiko Kojima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiko_Kojima) became the first woman of color to win the [Miss Universe](150340.md) title. Her success marked the beginning of a shift away from white women as the global female beauty ideal.[^cite_note-Riding_the_Crest-88] The continued success of Asian women in American and international beauty pageants has signaled that white women are no longer considered the beauty ideal.[^cite_note-Hune_Nomura_2003_p.-89] In 1983, [Vanessa Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Williams), an American singer, actress, and fashion designer, gained recognition as being the first black woman to receive the Miss America title.[^cite_note-Brand_2000_p._157-90] In 1991, [Lupita Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Jones), a Mexican actress and television producer, became the first Mexican person to win Miss Universe.[^cite_note-Orme_1997-91]

Besides the international beauty pageants, numerous minor competitions exist throughout the world, displaying the different perceptions of beauty. Some examples of criteria to select beauty queens that are unique to a certain culture. The Miss India USA pageant uses [Indian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India) and traditional craft skills as its specialties,[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] while the Miss Howard University competition takes advantage of the principles of "black beauty".[^cite_note-auto2-94] The winner is often viewed as a model for the "ideal" community member.[^cite_note-95] Through the competitions, the contestants can learn how to present themselves in public and how to cultivate certain traits such as confidence or poise.[^cite_note-96] In some cases, the competitors are selected to act as a representative on behalf of the community. In the African American community of Howard University, the selected Miss Howard University served as advocates for the Civil Rights Movement in the decades following the 1960s.[^cite_note-auto2-94] Additionally, the Miss Landmine competition situated in [Angola](701.md) allow victims to serve as advocates on behalf of other victims of mining accidents.[^cite_note-97]

Researchers suggest that the emergence of beauty pageants in countries outside the United States is linked to an economic boom geared towards a more consumeristic lifestyle. "In India, the growth of the personal care industry coincided with increased participation in national beauty pageants.[^cite_note-98] Additionally, after China hosted about 6 international beauty pageants in 2004, the beauty industry increased in influence in the area. At the same time, the number of regional beauty pageants in the country increased.[^cite_note-99]

## Criticism



The panel of judges for the 1973 Miss Amsterdam pageant

Critics of beauty pageants argue that such contests reinforce the idea that girls and women should be valued primarily for their physical appearance, and that this puts tremendous pressure on women to conform to conventional beauty standards by spending time and money on fashion, cosmetics, [hair styling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_styling), and even [cosmetic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmetic_surgery). They say that this pursuit of physical beauty even encourages some women to [diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieting) to the point of harming themselves.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

The London Feminist Network argues that rather than being empowering, beauty pageants do the opposite: denying women's full humanity by subjecting them to [objectification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_objectification) and by maintaining that their primary purpose is to be attractive.[^cite_note-102] Beginning in 1981, the International Year of the Disabled Person, campaigners in Australia targeted beauty pageants in order to, in the words of activist Leslie Hall, "challenge the notion of beauty" and "reject the charity ethic." High-profile demonstrations led to some charities abandoning their use of such contests for fundraising and also saw some remove offensive language from their organisational titles.[^cite_note-103]

Another criticism is in the way beauty pageant is quantifiably scored as highlighted by the "Myth of the Perfect 10".[^cite_note-104] Beauty becomes a numerical coefficient in ranking contestants, and this type of scoring still remains followed as a system even in nationwide beauty pageants such as [Miss America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_America).[^cite_note-105]

Researchers suggest that these events strengthen skills, such as interpersonal communications, self-assurance, and public speaking, which prove to be useful in future career paths.[^cite_note-106]

### Swimsuit competition

Main article: [Swimsuit competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimsuit_competition)

The requirement for contestants to wear a swimsuit was a controversial aspect of the various competitions. The controversy was heightened with the increasing popularity of the bikini after its introduction in 1946. The bikini was banned for the Miss America contest in 1947 because of [Roman Catholic](606848.md) protesters.[^cite_note-DailyTelegraph-107] When the [Miss World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_World) contest started in 1951, there was an outcry when the winner was crowned in a bikini. [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII) condemned the crowning as sinful,[^cite_note-Selvedge-47][^cite_note-theweek-48] and countries with religious traditions threatened to withdraw delegates.[^cite_note-shin-46] The bikini was banned for future and other contests. It was not until the late 1990s that they became permitted again,[^cite_note-Almanac-27] but still generated controversy when finals were held in countries where bikinis (or swimsuits in general) were socially disapproved.[^cite_note-Almanac-27][^cite_note-au.ibtimes.com-50] For example, in 2003, [Vida Samadzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vida_Samadzai) from [Afghanistan](737.md) caused an uproar in her native country when she participated in the [Miss Earth 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Earth_2003) contest in a red bikini.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] She was condemned by the [Afghan Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Afghanistan), saying such a display of the body goes against [Islamic law](28840.md) and [Afghan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Afghanistan).[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] In 2013, the swimsuit round of the Miss World contest was dropped because of Islamist protests in [Bali](4147.md) ([Indonesia](14579.md)), where the contest took place.[^cite_note-au.ibtimes.com-50] In 2014, the Miss World contest eliminated the swimsuit competition from its pageant.[^cite_note-112] In 2018, Miss America eliminated the swimsuit competition after 97 years.[^cite_note-113]

In 2017, [Carousel Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carousel_Productions) was criticized for objectifying women during the [Miss Earth 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Earth_2017) competition where delegates wore swimsuits during the event with their faces concealed by a veil in the *Beauty of Figure and Form,* a segment first introduced in the [Miss Philippines Earth 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Philippines_Earth_2017) pageant.[^cite_note-auto-114][^cite_note-auto1-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] It was one of the three preliminary judging segments of the pageant that include *Poise and Beauty of Face* and *Environmental and Intelligence Competition*.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-auto-114] The organizers defended the "beauty of figure and form" segment and released a statement that the said round was intended to promote strict impartiality during pre-judging by focusing on the contestants' curves, execution and not beautiful face.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-auto1-115]

## Scandals

There have been numerous scandals in the beauty pageant industry and they continue to emerge as beauty pageants become more known to the public. In December 2017, *[HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)* published emails written by then-Miss America CEO Sam Haskell that disparaged former pageant contestants, making vulgar references to their weight and personal lives. Due to the release of these emails to the public, Haskell and several other board members resigned from their positions.[^cite_note-120]

[Laura Zúñiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Z%C3%BA%C3%B1iga), former Miss Hispanic America, was detained with her boyfriend and six other people, and charged with racketeering, drug trafficking, weapons violations, and money laundering. When apprehended, they had multiple handguns and roughly \$53,000 in cash.[^cite_note-\:0-121]

At the [Miss Teen USA 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Teen_USA_2007) pageant, [Caitlin Upton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Upton) gained international notoriety for her convoluted and nonsensical response to a question posed to her during the August 2007 national pageant.[^cite_note-TodayShow1-122][^cite_note-IHT-123] During the pageant, judge [Aimee Teegarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimee_Teegarden) asked: "Recent polls have shown a fifth of Americans can't locate the U.S. on a world map. Why do you think this is?". Upton responded:

> I personally believe that U.S. Americans are unable to do so because, uh, some, uh, people out there in our nation don't have maps and, uh, I believe that our education like such as in South Africa and, uh, the Iraq, everywhere like such as, and, I believe that they should, our education over here in the U.S. should help the U.S., uh, or, uh, should help South Africa and should help the Iraq and the Asian countries, so we will be able to build up our future. For our children.[^cite_note-Guardian-124][^cite_note-USAToday-125]

As a guest on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC)'s *[The Today Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_\(American_TV_program\))*, Upton told [Ann Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Curry) and [Matt Lauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lauer) that she was overwhelmed when asked the question and did not comprehend it correctly.[^cite_note-today.com-126][^cite_note-\:0-121]
