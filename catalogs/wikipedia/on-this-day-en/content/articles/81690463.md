The **2025 AFL Women's Grand Final** was an [Australian rules football](2403.md) match held on 29 November 2025 at [Ikon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Park_\(stadium\)) to determine the [premiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AFL_Women's_premiers) of the [tenth season](77057754.md) of the [AFL Women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's) (AFLW) competition. The match was played between [North Melbourne](22106.md#AFL_Women's_team) and [Brisbane](5026.md#AFL_Women's_team); the third successive [grand final](54494570.md) between the teams. North Melbourne won by 40 points to claim their second premiership, and in so doing became the first team to win back-to-back AFLW flags.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-ABC-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

[North Melbourne](22106.md#AFL_Women's_team) entered the 2025 season as the reigning premiers, having defeated Brisbane by 30 points in the [2024 grand final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_AFL_Women's_Grand_Final). The Kangaroos dominated throughout the 2025 season, going undefeated through the home-and-away season to finish with a 12–0 record and win the club's second [minor premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AFL_Women's_minor_premiers). The club became the first male or female VFL/AFL team to go undefeated for 24 consecutive games following their 49-point over [Hawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorn_Football_Club_\(AFL_Women's\)) in round 12.[^cite_note-4] They then held the Hawks goalless in a 39-point win in the first qualifying final before overcoming a narrow three-quarter time deficit to defeat [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Football_Club#AFL_Women's_team) by 10 points in the first preliminary final.[^cite_note-5] This was North's third successive grand final, having lost to the Lions in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFL_Women's_Grand_Final) and defeating them in 2024.[^cite_note-ABC-2] Kangaroos vice-captain [Ash Riddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Riddell) became the club's first [AFLW Best and Fairest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_best_and_fairest) winner with 23 votes.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

[Brisbane](5026.md#AFL_Women's_team) came into 2025 off the back of a top-four finish the [previous season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_AFL_Women's_season), which culminated in defeat to the Kangaroos in the grand final. Despite losing three matches at home this season, the Lions again comfortably qualified for a top-four position, finishing third on the ladder with a 9–3 record at the end of the home-and-away season. The club faced Melbourne as the away side in the second qualifying final and defeated the Demons by 13 points, with back-to-back majors from [Ally Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Anderson) and [Lily Postlethwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Postlethwaite) in the final quarter sealing the victory.[^cite_note-8] This guaranteed them the [McClelland Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McClelland_Trophy) as the best performed club across both the men's and women's competitions for the respective seasons.[^cite_note-9] They then hosted [Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Football_Club#AFL_Women's_team) in the second preliminary final and won by 35 points, with a six-goal third quarter from the home team proving to be the difference between the sides.[^cite_note-10]

North Melbourne was aiming for its second AFLW premiership following its victory in 2024 and maiden grand final in 2023, while Brisbane, making its seventh appearance in a grand final, was seeking to equal [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_Football_Club#AFL_Women's_team)'s league-leading three premierships following premierships in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_AFL_Women's_Grand_Final) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFL_Women's_Grand_Final). The two sides met once during the home-and-away season, with North Melbourne recording a comfortable 29-point victory at [Brighton Homes Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield_Central_Stadium).[^cite_note-11]

Bookmakers heavily favoured North Melbourne to win the grand final; on the Monday before the game the Kangaroos were \$1.13 favorites compared to Brisbane who were distant \$5.75 outsiders.[^cite_note-12] The league determined the match would be played at 7.45pm local time, which replicated the nighttime timeslot first used in 2024.[^cite_note-13] General manager of women's football Emma Moore confirmed [Ikon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Park_\(stadium\)) would host all Victorian-based finals and the grand final following a rebuffed request from Melbourne to play their home qualifying final at [Casey Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Fields).[^cite_note-14] As the highest-placed winners to reach the final, North Melbourne were classified as the home team and thus the game was held at Ikon Park, a 12,500-set venue. As in 2024, the game was sold out within 24 hours.[^cite_note-15]

## Entertainment

Electronic band [Peking Duk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_Duk) performed a 17-minute set for the Telstra Pre-Game Entertainment, featuring guest artists [Ben Woolner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safia_\(band\)), KYE and Lucy Lucy. Singer, songwriter and actress [Bonnie Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_Anderson_\(singer\)) sang the Australian national anthem.[^cite_note-16]

Peking Duk's setlist included "[Dancing2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing2)" performed by [Keli Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Hyde) (an alias of Peking Duk member Adam Hyde), "[Take Me Over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_Me_Over_\(Peking_Duk_song\))" and "[Say My Name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Say_My_Name_\(Peking_Duk_song\))" with Woolner, an unreleased song called "Thrills" with KYE, before finishing with "[Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_\(Peking_Duk_song\))" and "[High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_\(Peking_Duk_song\))" alongside Lucy Lucy. The performance received largely negative reviews from fans and viewers.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

### Medal and cup presenters

The 2025 AFLW Premiership Cup Ambassador was women's football pioneer and [Australian Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Football_Hall_of_Fame) member [Debbie Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Lee), who delivered the cup onto the ground pre-match.[^cite_note-21] Injured Kangaroos player [Nicole Bresnehan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Bresnehan) presented the cup to the premiership coach Darren Crocker and premiership captain Jasmine Garner.[^cite_note-22]

The [Best on ground medal](54494570.md#Best_on_ground) was awarded to [Eilish Sheerin](72243345.md) by [AFL Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Commission) [Simone Wilkie AO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Wilkie). The medal for premiership coach [Darren Crocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Crocker) was presented by inaugural AFLW Premiership coach [Bec Goddard OAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bec_Goddard). For the first time, a medal was awarded to premiership captain [Jasmine Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Garner), presented by three-time AFLW premiership star [Erin Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Phillips).[^cite_note-23]

## Match summary



Centre ruck contest during the first quarter

Brisbane got off to a flying start when Brisbane's [Charlotte Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Mullins) tackled North Melbourne's [ Jasmine Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Ferguson?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was penalised for [holding the ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_the_ball). Mullins converted from a 40-metre set shot. North Melbourne soon responded with a goal from [Eilish Sheerin](72243345.md); this was controversial, as North Melbourne's [Tahlia Randall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahlia_Randall) had dropped the ball and should have been penalised. A second goal followed shortly before quarter time after North Melbourne ruck [Emma King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_King_\(footballer\)) took a spectacular grab and then delivered the ball to [Bella Eddey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Eddey) 25 metres out but on a tight angle. Eddey made the shot, giving North Melbourne the lead at quarter time, 2.0 (12) to Brisbane's 1.1 (7).[^cite_note-Nine-26][^cite_note-SMH-27]

In the second quarter, Brisbane defender [Shannon Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_Campbell) pushed North Melbourne' [Kate Shierlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Shierlaw) on the ground after Shierlaw had taken a mark, resulting in a [50-metre penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50-metre_penalty). Campbell then compounded her error by not exiting the zone as Shierlaw ran ahead, resulting in a second 50-metre penalty. This put Shierlaw well within range for an easy goal. A few minutes later, North Melbourne's [Jasmine Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Garner) took an intercept mark when Brisbane tried to move the ball out of defence, and delivered the ball to [Jenna Bruton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenna_Bruton), who scored a fourth unanswered goal for her team from long range. A snap shot by [Eilish Sheerin](72243345.md) soon made it five. At the half time break, North Melbourne had extended its lead to 6.0 (12) to Brisbane's 1.2 (8). Sheerin had racked up 15 disposals and two goals.[^cite_note-Nine-26][^cite_note-SMH-27]

The third quarter opened with Talia Randall extended the lead with a strong mark and another goal. Brisbane finally scored one back, with [Ruby Svarc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Svarc) crumbing a chaos ball kicked out the back. This began a strong fight back by Brisbane, led by [Isabel Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_Dawes), but North Melbourne's defence, led by [Libby Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libby_Birch), remained well-organised and held firm. At three-quarter time, North Melbourne led by 22 points, 6.0 (36) to Brisbane's 2.2 (14). The final quarter opened with Jasmine garner kicking another one from an intercept mark. Jenna Bruton was pushed over by Brisbane's [Ellie Hampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellie_Hampson), resulting in a fourth 50-metre penalty being awarded against Brisbane and a second goal to Bruton. With a few minutes left to go, she was awarded a free kick and made it three. Her teammate [Ash Riddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Riddell) also played a great game, finishing with 39 disposals (a record for an AFLW Grand Final), 20 contested possessions and six clearances.[^cite_note-Nine-26][^cite_note-SMH-27]

Libby Birch became the first AFLW player to rack up four premierships, having previously won with the Western Bulldogs in 2018, Melbourne in 2022 and North Melbourne in 2024. It was a third premiership win for teammates [Emma Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Kearney_\(footballer\)), Jenna Bruton and [Kim Rennie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Rennie), and the first for [Blaithin Bogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaithin_Bogue), [Eliza Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliza_Shannon?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Best on Ground Eilish Sheerin.[^cite_note-ABC-2]

## Best on ground medal



[Eilish Sheerin](72243345.md) (North Melbourne) on stage accepting the best on ground medal

The [best on ground medal](54494570.md#Best_on_ground) was presented by [AFL Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Commission) [Simone Wilkie AO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Wilkie). The voting panel consisted of [Abbey Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Holmes) (chair, [Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Network)), Gemma Bastiani ([AFL Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Media), [ABC Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Sport)), Laura Spurway ([3AW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3AW)) and Lauren Wood ([Herald Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Sun), [Fox Footy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Footy)). North Melbourne's [Eilish Sheerin](72243345.md) was unanimously judged best afield with a maximum 12 votes after 28 disposals, 8 tackles, 9 clearances and 2 goals.[^cite_note-Full_votes-28]

## Media coverage

### Television

[Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Network)'s coverage, simulcast on streaming service [7+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7plus), began at 7 pm [AEDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEDT). [Abbey Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Holmes) hosted the broadcast from the ground, with analysis from [Kate McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_McCarthy) and [Erin Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Phillips). The match was commentated by Alister Nicholson, Jo Wotton, Kate McCarthy and Erin Phillips.[^cite_note-29]

[Fox Footy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Footy)'s coverage, also shown on streaming services [Kayo Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayo_Sports) and [BINGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binge_\(streaming_service\)), began at 6\:30 pm [AEDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEDT). [Kelli Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelli_Underwood) hosted the broadcast from Fox Footy's Studios in Southbank, with analysis coming from former Carlton AFLW coach [Daniel Harford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Harford) and Collingwood AFLW captain [Ruby Schleicher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Schleicher). Fox Footy host and Herald Sun journalist Lauren Wood provided interviews and updates from Ikon Park during the broadcast. Fox Footy simulcasted the Seven Network coverage of the match with Seven's commentators and graphics, being accompanied by their own out-of-play analysis.
