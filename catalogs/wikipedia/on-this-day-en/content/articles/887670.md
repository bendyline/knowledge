**PAOK FC** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): ΠΑΕ ΠΑΟΚ, [\['pa.ok\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek)),[^cite_note-3] short for Pan-Thessalonian Athletic Club of Constantinopolitans ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Πανθεσσαλονίκειος Αθλητικός Όμιλος Κωνσταντινουπολιτών "[P.A.O.K. (Pan-Thesalonian Athletic Club of Constantinopolitans)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.A.O.K.)"), is a Greek professional [football](10568.md) club based in [Thessaloniki](40471.md), [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(Greece\)), Greece. The club is commonly known internationally as **PAOK Thessaloniki** or **PAOK Salonica**, while in Greece it is referred to simply as **PAOK**. PAOK are one of the top domestic clubs and the most successful and widely supported in [Northern Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Greece).

Established on 20 April 1926 by [Greek refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_refugees) who fled to [Thessaloniki](40471.md) from [Constantinople](3391396.md) in the wake of the [Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)), they play their home games at [Toumba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium), a 29,000 seating capacity football ground. Their name, along with the club's emblem, the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire)-style [double-headed eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagle) with retracted wings, honours the memory of the people and places (mostly from the city of [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople)) that once belonged to the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-History-4][^cite_note-5] PAOK currently plays in the top-flight [Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_League_Greece), which they have won four times (in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Alpha_Ethniki), [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Alpha_Ethniki), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Super_League_Greece) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Super_League_Greece)). They are eight-time winners of the [Greek Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Football_Cup) (in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Greek_Football_Cup), [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Greek_Football_Cup), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_Greek_Football_Cup), [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Greek_Football_Cup), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Greek_Football_Cup), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Greek_Football_Cup), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Greek_Football_Cup) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Greek_Football_Cup)). The club is one of the three which have [never been relegated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unrelegated_association_football_clubs) from the top national division and the only team in Greece that have won the [Double](88184.md) (in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_PAOK_FC_season)) going [unbeaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unbeaten_football_club_seasons) (26–4–0 record) in a national [round-robin](939742.md) league tournament (league format since [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959%E2%80%9360_Alpha_Ethniki)).[^cite_note-2018/19_squad-6]

The team has appeared several times in the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md), but has yet to reach the group stage of the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md). PAOK have reached the quarter-finals of a European competition three times; once in the [1973–74 European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup) and twice in the [UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League), in the [2021–22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_UEFA_Europa_Conference_League) and [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Europa_Conference_League) seasons. PAOK is the only Greek team that has more wins than losses in their European record (99 wins, 68 draws and 87 defeats, as of August 2025) and the 0–7 away [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_UEFA_Cup) win over [Locomotive Tbilisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Locomotive_Tbilisi) on 16 September 1999 is the largest ever achieved by a Greek football club in all European competitions.

## History

Main article: [History of PAOK FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_PAOK_FC)

### Foundation and early years (1926–1939)

For the founding members and 1st Board of Directors of the major [multi-sport club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club), see [P.A.O.K.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.A.O.K.)



PAOK in 1926

PAOK FC is the oldest department of the major [multi-sport club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club) [AC P.A.O.K.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.A.O.K.), which is closely linked with *[Hermes Sports Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyo%C4%9Flu_S.K.)*, that was formed in 1875 by the Greek community of [Pera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyo%C4%9Flu_S.K.), a district of [Istanbul](3391396.md) ([Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople)).[^cite_note-History-4] The football club was founded in April 1926 after a dispute with [Enosis Konstantinoupoliton Thessalonikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enosis_Konstantinoupoliton_Thessalonikis) (E.K. Th.), a social and political organisation, by [Constantinopolitans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinopolitan) who fled to [Thessaloniki](40471.md) after the Greek defeat in the [Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)). PAOK's policy was to be open to every citizen of [Thessaloniki](40471.md), leading to a minor rivalry with [AEK Thessaloniki](https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%91.%CE%95.%CE%9A.%20%CE%98%CE%B5%CF%83%CF%83%CE%B1%CE%BB%CE%BF%CE%BD%CE%AF%CE%BA%CE%B7%CF%82), the other [Constantinopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinopolitan) club of the city, from which PAOK had split prior to its foundation and was attended exclusively by [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_refugees).[^cite_note-7]

PAOK played its first friendly match on 4 May 1926 at the stadium of [Thermaikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermaikos), defeating [Megas Alexandros Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megas_Alexandros_Thessaloniki) 2–1, coached by Kostas Andreadis, who spent five years on the team's bench without demanding payment. The first professional contract was signed by the club in September 1928 for [French](5843419.md) footballer Raymond Etienne of [Jewish](25955086.md) descent from [Pera Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pera_Club) (the club that PAOK's founding members played in), which ignited further controversy between the two refugee clubs.[^cite_note-8] In March 1929, AEK Thessaloniki was disbanded as a [sports club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club) and their members joined PAOK. PAOK thereupon changed its emblem, adopting the [Double-headed eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagle), as a symbol of the club's [Constantinopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) heritage. PAOK also got possession of AEK Thessaloniki's facilities located around Syntrivani and settled there, becoming the sole refugee club in the city.

Albeit PAOK's first years in existence were marred by strife and controversy with the [Macedonia Football Clubs Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_Football_Clubs_Association) and the [Hellenic Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Football_Federation), the team's ascension led to its debut in the [1930–31 Panhellenic Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930%E2%80%9331_Panhellenic_Championship), becoming a mainstay at the national stage, with the construction of the Syntrivani Stadium being completed in 1932.[^cite_note-9] The team would win its first regional title, the [Macedonia Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_Football_Clubs_Association), in 1937, beginning a period of dominance. PAOK would reach a [Greek Cup final in 1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Greek_Football_Cup_final) and the two-legged final of the [Panhellenic Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939%E2%80%9340_Panhellenic_Championship) in 1940, losing both times to AEK Athens. However, the team's success would be cut short by the [Greco–Italian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco%E2%80%93Italian_War), where the team was dismembered, with two players dying on duty.[^cite_note-10]

### Regional league dominance and youth academy (1946–1959)

After [World War II](32927.md), in the early 1950s, a state-of-the-art [youth academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_academy) named [PAOK Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK_Academy) was created by the Austrian manager Wilhelm (Willy) Sevcik, who played for the club in the 1930s. The academy was known as the "chicos of Willi". From the newly founded academy sprang some great football players of the period, such as [Leandros Symeonidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandros_Symeonidis), Giannelos Margaritis and Giorgos Havanidis.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

In 1948, PAOK won their second [Macedonia Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_Football_Clubs_Association), and then participated in the final phase of the [Panhellenic Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947%E2%80%9348_Panhellenic_Championship) where they were ranked third. PAOK footballers dedicated the title to the memory of team captain, Thrasyvoulos Panidis, who had lost his life (18 February 1948) in the [Greek Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Civil_War) a few days prior.[^cite_note-13] In 1950, they became [champions of Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_Football_Clubs_Association) for a third time, and the following year (1950–51), the team reached their second [Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Greek_Football_Cup_final), but lost to [Olympiacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiacos_F.C.).[^cite_note-14]

After a busy transfer period in 1953, PAOK's offensive play was significantly strengthened with an effective front three, consisting of Kouiroukidis, Papadakis and Yientzis and the club dominated the Macedonian championship for the rest of the decade, with four consecutive titles from 1954 to 1957, going unbeaten from 1954 to 1956, under the management of Nikos Pangalos, Erman Hoffman and [Walter Pfeiffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Pfeiffer_\(footballer\)), albeit results in the Panhellenic Championship were more mixed.[^cite_note-15] The team also reached a third [Cup final in 1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Greek_Football_Cup_final), losing to Panathinaikos.[^cite_note-16] The concluding seasons of the decade were less successful, but the team's performance in the 1958–59 Macedonia Championship secured a spot in the newly found nationwide Alpha Ethniki, which replaced the region–based Panhellenic Championship.

### Alpha Ethniki, domestic and international success, professionalism (1959–1989)

Having moved to the newly built and crowdfunded [Toumba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium) in September 1959,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] PAOK's first years in the nationwide division were unassuming, finishing no higher than sixth between 1960 and 1966, except for a fourth-place finish in 1963. Under the captaincy of Leandros Symeonidis the club slowly built a solid foundation through its youth academy, with emerging youngsters such as [Giorgos Koudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Koudas) and [Stavros Sarafis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavros_Sarafis).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In this period, PAOK became a frequent guest in the [Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup) and significantly increased its attacking form under [Ivor Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Powell) and [Jane Janevski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Janevski). In 1968, Koudas' controversial transfer from PAOK to Olympiacos was annulled, in a string of incidents that started a [heated rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiacos-PAOK_rivalry) between the two teams.[^cite_note-23]

The 1970s was a successful period for the club, with scouting on behalf of president [Giorgos Pantelakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Pantelakis) building a strong team, including Sarafis, [Terzanidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christos_Terzanidis), [Iosifidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostas_Iosifidis), [Gounaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giannis_Gounaris), [Paridis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Paridis), [Aslanidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilleas_Aslanidis), [Apostolidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koulis_Apostolidis), [Fountoukidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelis_Fountoukidis), [Kermanidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagiotis_Kermanidis), [Anastasiadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelos_Anastasiadis), [Furtula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mladen_Furtula), [Guerino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neto_Guerino) and captained by Koudas. From 1970 to 1974, PAOK reached five consecutive [Cup finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Greek_Cup_finals), defeating Panathinaikos in 1972[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] and Olympiacos on penalties in 1974,[^cite_note-26] under the management of Englishman [Les Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Shannon). The club came close to its first nationwide league title in the [1972–73 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972%E2%80%9373_PAOK_FC_season), finishing runners–up and Cup finalists in a controversial manner to Olympiacos. It would eventually clinch its first league title in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_PAOK_FC_season), under the management of former Hungarian [Golden Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Team) international [Gyula Lóránt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_L%C3%B3r%C3%A1nt).[^cite_note-27] Afterward, the club mounted a 62-game undefeated streak at home from 1976 to 1980.[^cite_note-28]



[Giorgos Koudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Koudas), the most capped PAOK player with 607 games

In European competitions, the club achieved major wins against clubs like [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) in 1973 and [Barcelona](68187.md) in 1975, reaching the quarter-finals of the [European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) in 1974, eventually being knocked out by [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan).[^cite_note-29] It then lost the 1977 and 1978 Cup finals and failed to defend the title in controversial fashion in [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_PAOK_FC_season), with PAOK coming at odds with the Hellenic Football Federation. Further complications ensued after the [1978 Thessaloniki earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Thessaloniki_earthquake), which damaged Toumba Stadium and eventually led to its Gate 8 collapsing in February 1980.

In the summer of 1979, football in Greece became fully professional, with PAOK restructuring into a privately owned football limited company, with major stockholder Giorgos Pantelakis as the club's president. With the rise of PAOK's [ultras group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultras) [Gate 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_4), PAOK would become entangled in ownership controversies that would go on via various means for over two decades. As the advent of [hooliganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooliganism) rose dramatically in Greece, PAOK would become infamous for various incidents, starting with a loss against Panathinaikos at Toumba in 1980.[^cite_note-30]

The early 1980s were trophyless for PAOK, with the club being a consistent challenger for silverware domestically, despite being struck by tragedy when Gyula Lóránt died of a heart attack in a league match against Olympiacos in 1981.[^cite_note-31] Inspired by promising new players, such as [Christos Dimopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christos_Dimopoulos), the club reached two Cup finals, in 1981 and 1983, ultimately losing both to Olympiacos and [AEK Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEK_Athens_F.C.) respectively, while also losing in Europe to [Eintracht Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt) and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich), both on penalties.[^cite_note-32] Koudas and Furtula retired from football in the summer of 1984.

In the [1984–85 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_PAOK_FC_season), under Austrian manager [Walter Skocik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Skocik) and leading players, such as [Skartados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Skartados), [Alavantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikos_Alavantas), [Singas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Singas), [Kostikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgios_Kostikos_\(footballer,_born_1958\)) and [Paprica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rade_Paprica), PAOK clinched their second league title, while falling short of the Cup in the final against [AEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlitiki_Enosi_Larissa_F.C.).[^cite_note-33] Captain Kostas Iosifidis retired at the end of the season and the rest of the 1980s were less fruitful for the club. In the [1987–88 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_PAOK_FC_season), the club fall short of the league title amidst increasing board controversies.

### Financial issues, ownership changes, continental success (1989–2012)

In 1989, Thomas Voulinos became the club's president, initiating a rise in form entering the 1990s, with players such as [Magdy Tolba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdy_Tolba) and academy signing [Giorgos Toursounidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Toursounidis).[^cite_note-34] The club slumped to a third place in the 1990 title race and entered a period of on and off–pitch instability, starting with Voulinos protesting on the pitch in a match against Panathinaikos in 1990.[^cite_note-35] After losing in the [double–legged Cup final in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Greek_Football_Cup_final) to Olympiacos, Gate 4 and PAOK's presidency became increasingly at odds, resulting in boycotts from the club's fans and a sharp decrease in attendances at the insistence of Gate 4 throughout the decade.&#x20;

In Europe, the club had notable encounters in the [UEFA Cup](232175.md) with [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC), losing on penalties and [K.V. Mechelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K.V._Mechelen), defeating them away from home, but increasing discontent between Gate 4 and Voulinos resulted in heavy rioting at a UEFA Cup home game against [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.) in October 1992,[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] resulting in a two-season ban from European competitions, later reduced to one year. With younger players such as [Theodoros Zagorakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoros_Zagorakis), the team achieved a more successful [1994–95 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_PAOK_FC_season) under Dutchman [Arie Haan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arie_Haan). The following season, the club was threatened with its first relegation, ultimately finishing four points clear with a points deduction, following heavy rioting and arson against Thomas Voulinos after a 1–3 loss to AEK Athens in November 1995.&#x20;



[Angelos Anastasiadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelos_Anastasiadis) won the Cup both as a player ([1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Greek_Football_Cup)) and manager ([2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Greek_Football_Cup))

In 1996, Thomas Voulinos handed over a debt-free PAOK to [Giorgos Batatoudis](https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%93%CE%B9%CF%8E%CF%81%CE%B3%CE%BF%CF%82%20%CE%9C%CF%80%CE%B1%CF%84%CE%B1%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%8D%CE%B4%CE%B7%CF%82). Numerous successful players such as [Zisis Vryzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zisis_Vryzas),[^cite_note-38] [Spyros Marangos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spyros_Marangos), free kick specialist [Kostas Frantzeskos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostas_Frantzeskos),[^cite_note-39] [Percy Olivares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Olivares)[^cite_note-40] and [Joe Nagbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Nagbe) were signed under the new administration. Under former player Angelos Anastasiadis, after a five-year absence from European competitions, PAOK qualified for the UEFA Cup.[^cite_note-41] The club's reappearance at European level was marked by a victory and qualification over eventual double winners [Arsenal](2174.md).[^cite_note-42] Under [Dušan Bajević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Bajevi%C4%87), PAOK won the domestic Cup in 2001, [defeating Olympiacos 4–2 in the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Greek_Football_Cup_final)[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] to claim the club's first trophy in 16 years.[^cite_note-45] [Bajević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Bajevi%C4%87) departed in 2002, to be replaced by Angelos Anastasiadis, who led PAOK to a 1–0 defeat of [local rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_of_Thessaloniki) [Aris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aris_Thessaloniki_F.C.) in the [2003 Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Greek_Football_Cup_final), becoming the first former player to win a trophy as manager.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Batatoudis's shares were transferred, he was removed from the presidency following the Cup win and was succeeded by Giannis Goumenos following a rise in the club's debt.[^cite_note-50]

This period would be overshadowed by the untimely death of 21 year old player [Panagiotis Katsouris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagiotis_Katsouris) in February 1998[^cite_note-51] and the Vale of Tempe disaster in 1999, which claimed the lives of six PAOK fans following an away match against Panathinaikos.[^cite_note-4.10.1999_Tempi-52][^cite_note-53] Ceremonies take place every year in remembrance of the tragedies, with Katsouris' jersey number (No. 17) being permanently retired and a bust being erected in his memory in 2000.[^cite_note-54]

After a successful [2003–04 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_PAOK_FC_season), in which the club qualified for the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) third qualifying round for the first time, the club failed to qualify to the group stage while fielding [Liasos Louka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liasos_Louka), who was an ineligible player.[^cite_note-55] The club's financial situation worsened, as managerial and boardroom crisis culminated in the emergence of the club's poor financial position; players declared they had been unpaid for months, which led to a decision by [UEFA](32332.md) to ban the club from European competitions,[^cite_note-56] bringing the club close to dissolution. The organized supporters' groups launched an all-out war, occupying the club's offices during the summer of 2006.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] After possible takeover bids failed and embezzlement allegations arose, especially after the transfer of [Dimitris Salpingidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Salpingidis) to Panathinaikos,[^cite_note-59] Goumenos resigned in November 2006, with a temporary administration assuming control, as debt had tripled in Goumenos' three year presidency.[^cite_note-60]



[Theodoros Zagorakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoros_Zagorakis), captain and former president of PAOK FC

In June 2007, former player and captain Theodoros Zagorakis retired[^cite_note-61] and assumed the presidency of the club.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] PAOK's finances gradually improved thanks to new sponsorship deals and the rising attendance from the club's fans, which contributed to the completion of the [PAOK FC Sport Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK_FC_Sport_Center) in 2009.[^cite_note-64] He was followed by Zisis Vryzas, who retired in 2008 to become director of football.[^cite_note-65]

In his first season, the early replacement of [Georgios Paraschos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgios_Paraschos) by [Fernando Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Santos_\(Portuguese_footballer\))[^cite_note-66] did little to prevent a ninth-place finish in the league. The next season was more successful, with new signings such as club favourites [Vieirinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieirinha), [Pablo Contreras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Contreras) and [Pablo García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Garc%C3%ADa_\(footballer,_born_1977\)),[^cite_note-67] qualifying for European football for the first time since 2005. After a successful [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_PAOK_FC_season), where the team narrowly missed out on the league title and qualified for the Champions League qualifying round, Santos left the club, with former player [Pavlos Dermitzakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlos_Dermitzakis) leading the club to qualification in the rebranded [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) against [Fenerbahçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenerbah%C3%A7e_S.K._\(football\)), before being sacked in October. Under [Makis Chavos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makis_Chavos), PAOK reached the knockout stages before being eliminated by [CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_CSKA_Moscow).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The next season, under Romanian [László Bölöni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_B%C3%B6l%C3%B6ni),[^cite_note-70] was highlighted by a 1–2 win against [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur) at [White Hart Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Hart_Lane). PAOK finished top of its Europa League group,[^cite_note-71] ultimately being defeated in the next round by [Udinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udinese_Calcio). In January 2012, Zagorakis resigned from the club's presidency and was replaced by director Zisis Vryzas, in a transitional effort brought forth by increasing fan discontent following the sale of Vieirinha to [VfL Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

### The Ivan Savvidis era (2012–present)



[Răzvan Lucescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%83zvan_Lucescu), most successful PAOK manager

On 10 August 2012, [Ivan Savvidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Savvidis)[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] acquired PAOK ownership by depositing a fee of €9,951,000 and thus becoming the major shareholder of the club.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Following the takeover, the club sought to eliminate its debts, successfully doing so over the course of three years, a fee of over 10 million euros, becoming the first Greek sports club to fully repay its loans.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

The first years under the new presidency were ambitious but lackluster, with the club remaining trophyless after losing the domestic Cup final in 2014 to Panathinaikos[^cite_note-80] and failing to third place after leading the table at Christmas the following season, under returning Angelos Anastasiadis. Whilst European form was more positive, highlighted by a surprise 1–0[^cite_note-81] away win over [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) at [Signal Iduna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Iduna_Park), domestic form was underwhelming, with [Igor Tudor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Tudor) being replaced in March 2016 by youth-team coach and former player [Vladimir Ivić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Ivi%C4%87),[^cite_note-82] who led the team to a return in the Champions League third qualifying round. The next season saw the club's successful return to the Europa League knockouts and a controversial 2–1 win over AEK Athens in the [Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Greek_Football_Cup_final),[^cite_note-83] which was maligned by off pitch incidents that resulted in a points deduction for the club.[^cite_note-84]



[Vieirinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieirinha), PAOK most decorated player

After Ivić departed in the summer of 2017, he was briefly succeeded by [Aleksandar Stanojević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Stanojevi%C4%87), who was let go after two Europa League matches and was succeeded by [Răzvan Lucescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%83zvan_Lucescu).[^cite_note-85] Captained by returning player [Vieirinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieirinha), PAOK came second in the [2017–18 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_PAOK_FC_season). It became a highly controversial season, most notably for the home league matches against Olympiacos and AEK Athens which were both interrupted and lost by court decision.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] A few months later, PAOK claimed its second consecutive domestic Cup, beating AEK Athens 2–0 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Greek_Football_Cup_Final) held at the [Olympic Stadium of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_of_Athens) (AEK home ground at the time). During the post-game press conference, manager Lucescu and captain Vieirinha (final MVP[^cite_note-89]) both stated that the [2018 league title was stolen from PAOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_PAOK_FC_season).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

Inspired by initial European success, the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_PAOK_FC_season) became the most successful in the club's history, claiming PAOK's third league title and finishing undefeated, becoming only the second Greek team to do so after Panathinaikos in the 1963–64 season.[^cite_note-92] PAOK also claimed their third consecutive Greek Cup, beating AEK Athens in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Greek_Football_Cup_Final) for the third year in a row to seal the club's first [double](88184.md).[^cite_note-93] Lucescu departed shortly after, and his successor [Abel Ferreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Ferreira)[^cite_note-94] brought the club close to Champions League qualification, defeating [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica) and [Beşiktaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be%C5%9Fikta%C5%9F_J.K.) before being elinimated by [Krasnodar](474128.md). Former player and captain Pablo García succeeded him in October 2020, leading the club to an eighth Greek Cup, [defeating Olympiacos 2–1 in the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Greek_Football_Cup_Final), in May 2021. Lucescu would return to the managerial post shortly after, with talented emerging players such as [Giannis Konstantelias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giannis_Konstantelias) and [Konstantinos Koulierakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantinos_Koulierakis)[^cite_note-95] leading the club to the quarter-finals in the newly found [UEFA Europa Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Europa_Conference_League) in 2022, a feat the club would repeat in 2024, and reaching successive Cup finals, losing to Panathinaikos[^cite_note-96] and AEK Athens in 2022 and 2023 respectively.

In [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_PAOK_FC_season), PAOK after finishing first in the regular season, managed to remain on top after the conclusion of the play-offs winning their fourth Greek Championship on a remarkable fashion.[^cite_note-97] PAOK defeated all their major rivals ([AEK Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEK_Athens_F.C.), [Olympiacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiacos_F.C.), [Panathinaikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathinaikos_F.C.) and [Aris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aris_Thessaloniki_F.C.)) in the last four matches, winning against the three league contenders from [Athens](72650858.md) at [home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium) and clinching the title with a 1–2 away victory over [city rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_of_Thessaloniki), Aris at [Kleanthis Vikelidis Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleanthis_Vikelidis_Stadium) on 19 May 2024.[^cite_note-98]

In January 2026, Christos Zafeiris joined PAOK after transferring over from [Slavia Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Slavia_Prague).[^cite_note-99]

## Crest and colours

### Crest

The first emblem of [PAOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK) depicted a four-leaf clover and a horseshoe. The leaves were green and above them were the initials of the word "PAOK". Kostas Koemtzopoulos, one of PAOK's founding members, came up with this idea, inspired by his favourite brand of cigarettes.[^cite_note-100]

On 20 March 1929, Enosis Konstantinoupoliton Thessalonikis (A.E.K.) was dissolved and absorbed by PAOK and a mournful version of the [double-headed eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagle) with the wings closed instead of stretched, indicating the grief for the [lost homelands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_refugees), was adopted as the club's new emblem.[^cite_note-101]

On 11 June 2013, under the presidency of [Ivan Savvidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Savvidis), a golden outline was added to the crest, as a symbol of the club's [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greeks) heritage.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

During the 2018–19 season, the first emblem was used on the third kit.

### Colours

The club's colours have always been black and white, black for the sorrow related to countless thousands of [Greek refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_refugees) who were forced to leave the land their ancestors had been living in for centuries ([Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor), [Eastern Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Thrace), [Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontus_\(region\)), [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus)) and white for the hope of a new beginning that came with settling in a new home.[^cite_note-104] PAOK's traditional kit features a black and white vertical striped shirt, combined with black or white shorts and socks. Various types of shirts were used throughout the club's history and the most common alternatives were those with thinner or wider stripes, the all-black one and the all-white one.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] Over the years, several other colours were used on the [3rd kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_jersey), such as grey, silver, blue, purple, orange and red.[^cite_note-108]

## Facilities

### Toumba Stadium

Main article: [Toumba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium)

With PAOK's ambitions growing as the creation of a unified national championship loomed large, the club acquired land in the borough of [Toumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba) from the [Hellenic National Defence General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_National_Defence_General_Staff) for 6,000,000 [drachmae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_drachma) in 1957, and began a crowdfunding effort the next year. Works began in late 1958, with significant fan efforts and patronage from institutions such as the General Secretariat of Sports (today part of the [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Greece\))), which donated ₯1,100,000 for the construction of the stadium. Toumba, officially known as PAOK Stadium, was opened in September 1959. It initially had a capacity of 20,000 and had grass pitch installed, a rarity among Greek stadiums at the time.[^cite_note-123] Its creation was a costly one, and PAOK only completed the pay off of a sum of ₯1,500,000 in 1969.[^cite_note-124]

Over the course of the 1960s, numerous expansions took place, first in 1962 and then in 1965, with 31,504 attending a match against Olympiacos in 1967.[^cite_note-125] [Floodlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodlights) were first installed for an exhibition match against [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) in 1970, and with another expansion in 1972, capacity was raised to over 45,000, becoming the second largest stadium in Greece at the time, behind [Kaftanzoglio Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaftanzoglio_Stadium). The stadium's record attendance was recorded on 19 December 1976, when a league match between PAOK and AEK Athens drew 45,252 spectators. The [1978 Thessaloniki earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Thessaloniki_earthquake) caused Toumba's Gate 8 to collapse in March 1980,[^cite_note-126] rendering the stadium inoperable for over a year, and with the [Karaiskakis Stadium disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaiskakis_Stadium_disaster) in 1981, official capacity was reduced to 41,073 in 1982. In 1985, a [cantilevered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilever) roof was installed in Gates 1, 2 and 3 and new press booths were created. PAOK's financial turbulence for much of the next decade halted renovation plans, but in 1998, Toumba was converted into an [all-seater stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-seater_stadium), with its capacity dropping to 32,000. In 2000, security zones were added, which further dropped capacity to 28,701.

Toumba underwent a major renovation for the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics) after being selected as one of the training grounds for the football tournament. The roof was demolished and replaced with a new one, while new press rooms and VIP lounges were created, along with new plastic seats and refurbishments of the pitch and concrete steel around the stadium. After Ivan Savvidis acquired ownership of PAOK in 2012, renovations continued with expansion of press rooms and miscellaneous offices and the replacement of the pitch in 2015, though the stadium's structure remained unchanged. Toumba has hosted the [Greece national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team) various times in its history, and is used by PAOK's [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK_FC_\(women\)) for its matches in [UEFA competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_competitions). For the 2024–25 season, PAOK had the third highest attendance in Greece, behind Olympiacos, AEK Athens, and Panathinaikos.[^cite_note-127]

### Planned new ground

PAOK administration have already presented to the Greek public authorities an architecture study of a new stadium on the location of Toumba Stadium. The [Greek](12108.md) Council of State (CoS), the country's supreme court, in April 2022 approved a proposal to set up the complete redevelopment of Toumba Stadium, with the CoS deeming legal a draft Presidential Decree concerning the approval of a Special Urban Plan for the district of [Toumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba), where the venue is located. On 21 June 2022, PAOK has formalized the beginning of a collaboration with a team consisting of domestic engineering and consulting firm SALFO and global architectural design company [Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_\(company\)) to deliver the project.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] It is estimated that PAOK will be granted a building permit in 2023 and the new stadium will be completed by 2026 and will have a capacity of 41,926 spectators.[^cite_note-130] PAOK will probably move to Kaftanzoglio Stadium until the new Stadium is built.

New [Toumba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium) project plans present some significant changes to the original stadium, notably a giant roof covering the entire stadium which counters a common criticism of the current ground regarding exposure to weather and elements outside of Gates 1 and 2. The existing stands would be demolished and reconstructed, moving closer to the pitch and eliminating the distance from the former running track. However, as of early 2024, the project has been in a continuous stalemate.

## Supporters

See also: [Gate 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_4)



PAOK fans in Gate 4



Big shirt in Toumba stadium

PAOK FC is the most widely supported football club in [Northern Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Greece)[^cite_note-133] and with the 3rd largest fanbase in the country, according to the latest polls and researches.[^cite_note-ALCO-134][^cite_note-Metron_Analysis-135][^cite_note-Palmos_Analysis-136][^cite_note-University_of_Macedonia-137][^cite_note-gazzetta.gr_poll-138]

PAOK's traditional fanbase comes from the city of [Thessaloniki](40471.md), where the club is based, as well as from the rest of [Macedonia region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(Greece\)) and Northern Greece. They also have fans all over the country and in the [Greek Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Diaspora) (Germany, Australia, USA, etc.). Research by [Marca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marca_\(newspaper\)) in August 2018 reported that PAOK are the most popular Greek football team on [social media](5897742.md).[^cite_note-Marca-139][^cite_note-Marca_PAOK-140][^cite_note-social_media-141]

[Toumba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium) is infamous for its hostile atmosphere, a factor that led to the attribution of the Stadium as "**The Black Hell**".[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] On high-profile encounters, when the players walk out of the tunnel, the song [Hells Bells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hells_Bells_\(song\)) by [AC/DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC/DC) is heard from the stadium's speakers.[^cite_note-144] The notorious Gate 4 is home to many PAOK organized supporters' groups from around the globe, with the homonymous *[Gate 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_4)* fan club which was founded in April 1976, being the most familiar everywhere.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] The supporters' group from [Neapoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapoli,_Thessaloniki) district of Thessaloniki that was founded in 1963 is the oldest one.[^cite_note-147] One of the biggest banners in the world was created by PAOK's fan club in [Michaniona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michaniona).[^cite_note-148]

**No 12** jersey is dedicated to the fans, the symbolic 12th man on the pitch. It was permanently retired by the club on 16 August 2000.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

### Vale of Tempe tragedy (1999)

3,000 PAOK fans descended to the [Olympic Stadium of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_of_Athens) for the game against [Panathinaikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathinaikos_F.C.) on 3 October 1999. A few hours later, on its way back to [Thessaloniki](40471.md), the double-decker bus of the [Kordelio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleftherio-Kordelio) fan club collided with a truck and fell into a ditch in the [Vale of Tempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_Tempe), [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly). The aftermath of the bus crash was devastating. Six [PAOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK) fans lost their lives (**Kyriakos Lazaridis**, **Christina Tziova**, **Anastasios Themelis**, **Charalampos Zapounidis**, **Georgios Ganatsios**, **Dimitris Andreadakis**) and many others were injured. A [roadside memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadside_memorial) was erected at the site of the crash bearing the following inscription: "*Their love for PAOK brought them here, left them here and went beyond*".[^cite_note-4.10.1999_Tempi-52]

### Romania minibus crash (2026)

Seven supporters of PAOK were killed and three others injured in a minibus crash in western [Romania](25445.md) while traveling to Lyon for a UEFA Europa League match. The accident occurred on [DN6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DN6) (Drumul Național 6), a [single-carriageway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_carriageway) connecting western Romania, during an overnight journey. Romanian and Greek media reported that the vehicle lost control while overtaking. A doctor treating one of the survivors stated that a lane-keeping assist system may have activated during the maneuver, after which the driver was unable to regain control. The group consisted of 10 supporters en route to the [Lyon v PAOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Europa_League_league_phase) match scheduled for Thursday evening.[^cite_note-151]

The injured were initially taken to a hospital in [Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj) and later transferred to the county hospital in [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara). One sustained minor injuries, another suffered non-serious fractures, and a third incurred multiple fractures, including injuries to the neck and spine, though medical staff reported that his life was not in danger. Following the crash, approximately 200 PAOK supporters who had traveled to Romania gathered outside the Timișoara hospital for information and subsequently canceled their trip to France. PAOK supporters held a vigil at [Toumba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumba_Stadium) in [Thessaloniki](40471.md), where flags were flown at half-mast.[^cite_note-152]

The [Greek government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Greece) confirmed the deaths and stated that it was coordinating with Romanian authorities through the Greek embassy to provide assistance to the victims’ families and the injured. PAOK officials traveled to Romania to support those affected, and messages of condolence were issued by the club, rival Greek teams, and [Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais). Lyon announced that a tribute would be held during the Europa League match, and the section reserved for visiting PAOK supporters was closed at the request of the club and supporters’ groups.

### Rivalries

Further information: [Olympiacos–PAOK rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiacos%E2%80%93PAOK_rivalry), [Derby of Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_of_Thessaloniki), [Panathinaikos–PAOK rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathinaikos%E2%80%93PAOK_rivalry), and [Double-headed eagles derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagles_derby)



PAOK 1–0 OSFP, 2009 Greek Cup quarter-final

The [rivalry between Olympiacos and PAOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiacos%E2%80%93PAOK_rivalry) is the fiercest intercity football rivalry in Greece and is long-standing, emerging in the 1960s, when [Olympiacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiacos_F.C.) unsuccessfully tried to acquire [Giorgos Koudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Koudas) from [PAOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK), approaching him directly without going into a negotiation with his club.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]The rivalry with AEK intensified significantly after the controversial 2017–18 title race, when disciplinary decisions following their match at Toumba ended PAOK's title challenge, with AEK subsequently winning the championship.[^cite_note-164] A longtime heated rivalry exists between [PAOK and local rivals Aris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_of_Thessaloniki).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] [Panathinaikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathinaikos%E2%80%93PAOK_rivalry) and [AEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagles_derby), [Athens](1216.md)' two big clubs, are also considered major rivals.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] There are also some less intense rivalries, like those with [Iraklis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraklis_F.C._\(Thessaloniki\)) (local conflict) and [AEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlitiki_Enosi_Larissa_F.C.).
