**Richard J. Leon** (born December 3, 1949) is an American jurist who serves as a [senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status) [United States district judge](834368.md) of the [U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia](1786384.md). He was appointed in 2002 by President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and assumed senior status in 2016.

## Early life and education

Leon was born in [South Natick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natick,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), on December 3, 1949.[^cite_note-1] His parents were Silvano B. Leon and Rita M. O'Rorke.[^cite_note-2] His paternal grandparents were immigrants from [Portugal](23033.md) who settled in [Framingham, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framingham,_Massachusetts).[^cite_note-3]

Leon graduated from the [College of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_the_Holy_Cross) with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in psychology in 1971. As an undergraduate at Holy Cross, he was a member of the school's student judicial board,[^cite_note-\:0-4] and was a classmate of [Clarence Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Thomas).[^cite_note-5] He then earned his [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) (J.D.), *[cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude)*, from the [Suffolk University Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_University_Law_School) in 1974 and earned a [Master of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Laws) (LL.M.) from [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School) in 1981.[^cite_note-6]

## Legal career

Leon served as a [law clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_clerk) to the justices of the [Massachusetts Superior Court](30702547.md) from 1974 to 1975 and to Thomas F. Kelleher of the [Rhode Island Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Supreme_Court) from 1975 to 1976. Leon was an attorney for the [Immigration and Naturalization Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Naturalization_Service) of the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) from 1976 to 1977 and a Special [Assistant United States Attorney](3598252.md) in the office of the United States Attorney for the Southern District of New York working in the [Civil Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Civil_Division) from 1977 to 1978.

Leon was an assistant professor of law at [St. John's University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_University_School_of_Law) from 1979 to 1983 and a senior trial attorney in the United States Department of Justice from 1983 to 1987. Leon served as deputy chief minority counsel on the Select Committee to Investigate Covert Arms Transactions with Iran of the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md), which investigated the [Iran-Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Contra_affair), from 1987 to 1988.[^cite_note-7]

Leon was appointed Deputy [Assistant Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Assistant_Attorney_General) and served from 1988 to 1989, when he entered [private practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practice_of_law) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), first with [Baker & Hostetler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Hostetler) from 1989 to 1999 and then with [Vorys, Sater, Seymour and Pease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorys,_Sater,_Seymour_and_Pease) from 1999 to 2002, when he was appointed to the district court.

Leon was a member of the [President's Commission on White House Fellowships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Fellows) from 1990 to 1993. Leon was appointed chief minority counsel on the [October Surprise Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_October_Surprise_Task_Force) of the [House Foreign Affairs Committee](1267657.md) from 1992 to 1993. He served as special counsel to the [House Financial Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Financial_Services) in 1994. He is an adjunct professor at [The George Washington University Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_George_Washington_University_Law_School) and the [Georgetown University Law Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University_Law_Center).

## Federal judicial service

Leon was nominated to the [United States District Court for the District of Columbia](1786384.md) by [George W. Bush](3414021.md) on September 10, 2001, to the seat vacated by [Norma Holloway Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norma_Holloway_Johnson). [Confirmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) by the [Senate](24909346.md) on February 14, 2002, he received his [judicial commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent) five days later. He assumed [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status) on December 31, 2016.

### *WilmerHale v. Executive Office of the President* (2025)

On May 27, 2025, Judge Leon ruled in *WilmerHale v. Executive Office of the President*, striking down Executive Order 14250 issued by President Donald J. Trump. The order targeted the law firm WilmerHale due to its association with former Special Counsel Robert S. Mueller III. It directed federal agencies to suspend the firm’s security clearances, restrict access to federal buildings, and review existing contracts. Judge Leon found the order to be retaliatory and unconstitutional, violating the First and Fifth Amendments. In a 73-page opinion, he wrote, “The cornerstone of the American system of justice is an independent judiciary and an independent bar willing to tackle unpopular cases, however daunting.”[^cite_note-NYT2025-8][^cite_note-CourtListener-9]

### Guantanamo Bay

Leon was responsible for adjudicating the [habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus) petitions of several dozen captives held at the [Guantanamo Bay detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp).[^cite_note-GuantanamoHabeasRespondentResponse-10][^cite_note-CCRGuantanamoCaptivesLeadCounsel-11][^cite_note-MotionToDismissExhibitB-12] *[Boumediene v. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boumediene_v._Bush)*, which was eventually considered by the [Supreme Court](31737.md), was first heard by Leon. By August 28, 2008, Leon had 24 cases assigned to him.[^cite_note-13]

The Associated Press reported Leon hoped to resolve those cases before the [presidential inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_inauguration) in 2009 and was concerned that the public and the detainees will be barred from observing the hearings: "If it can't be done, I have great concern that these hearings will be virtually or exclusively classified, closed to the public and, I might add, to the detainees."[^cite_note-14]

During a hearing on October 23, 2008, Leon commented on the ambiguity of the term "[enemy combatant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_combatant)" and criticized Congress and the Supreme Court: "We are here today, much to my dismay, I might add, to deal with a legal question that in my judgment should have been resolved a long time ago. I don't understand, I really don't, how the Supreme Court made the decision it made and left that question open... I don't understand how the Congress could let it go this long without resolving."[^cite_note-15] On November 20, Leon ordered [five detainees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Six) released from [Guantanamo Bay Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_Naval_Base) due to insufficient evidence.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In December 2008, Leon denied [Moath Hamza Ahmed al Alawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moath_Hamza_Ahmed_al_Alawi)'s habeas corpus petition, finding the enemy combatant's detention was lawful under the [Authorization for Use of Military Force of 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authorization_for_Use_of_Military_Force_of_2001).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In February 2017, Leon denied another habeas corpus petition from al Alawi, rejecting the detainee's claim that the war's hostilities had ceased.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### NSA metadata collection unconstitutional

On December 16, 2013, Leon ruled that the [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA)'s bulk collection of Americans' telephone records likely violated the [Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution), though he stayed enforcement of his injunction pending appeal to the [D.C. Circuit](575245.md).[^cite_note-22] Excerpts from his decision are as follows:[^cite_note-23]

> I cannot imagine a more "indiscriminate" and "arbitrary invasion" than this systematic and high-tech collection and retention of personal data on virtually every citizen \[...] the almost-Orwellian technology \[...] Records that once would have revealed a few scattered tiles of information about a person now reveal an entire mosaic – a vibrant constantly updating picture of a person's life. \[...] No court has ever recognized a special need sufficient to justify continuous, daily searches of virtually every American citizen without any particularized suspicion. The Government urges me to be the first non-FISC judge to sanction such a dragnet. \[...] The Government does not cite a single instance in which analysis of the NSA's bulk metadata collection actually stopped an imminent attack \[...] Because of the utter lack of evidence that a terrorist act has ever been prevented because searching the NSA database was faster than other investigative tactics – I have serious doubts about the efficacy of the metadata collection program \[...] I have little doubt that the author of our Constitution, James Madison \[...] would be aghast.
