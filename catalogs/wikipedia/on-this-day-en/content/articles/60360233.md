**Folarin Jerry Balogun** (born July 3, 2001) is a  professional [soccer](10568.md) player who plays as a [striker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striker_\(association_football\)) for [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) and the [United States national team](582488.md).

Balogun began his career at the [Arsenal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C._Under-21s_and_Academy), and was promoted to the first team in 2020. He appeared sparingly for the club, and went on loan to [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) club [Middlesbrough](298603.md) in 2022. In the following season, he made his breakthrough during a loan to [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims_FC), where he scored 22 goals in [his first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022-23_Stade_de_Reims_season), the highest ever for an American in a [top five European league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-4] In August 2023, Balogun left Arsenal and signed for [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) for €40 million (\$43 million), where he was named the club's [2025–26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_AS_Monaco_FC_season) Player of the Season.[^cite_note-5]

Balogun represented both the United States and England at the youth international level. In May 2023, he decided to play for the U.S. senior national team. He made his debut in June 2023, winning the [2023 CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_CONCACAF_Nations_League) and scoring his first goal in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final).[^cite_note-\:1-6]

## Early life

Balogun was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), New York, to [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) Nigerian parents.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] His parents were living in [London](17867.md), and visited New York when his mother was seven months pregnant with him. Airline staff did not allow her to board the family's return flight to London due to safety concerns over the advanced state of her pregnancy, and Balogun was subsequently born in New York.[^cite_note-9] He returned to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) with his parents when he was two months old and grew up in London.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-ESPN2018-11][^cite_note-12]

## Club career

### Arsenal

#### Early career

Balogun joined [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C._Under-23s_and_Academy) at the age of eight, after being scouted while playing for his previous Sunday League team, Aldersbrook.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-YG-14] Before trialling with Arsenal, he had trialled with the club's North London rivals [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) and nearly signed for them.[^cite_note-YG-14]

In 2017, Balogun was an integral part of [Trevor Bumstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Bumstead)'s [Arsenal U16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C._Under-23s_and_Academy) team that won the [Liam Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Brady) Cup, defeating [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayern_Munich_Junior_Team), [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C._Reserves_and_Academy), and [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC_Youth_Sector).[^cite_note-15] He signed a professional contract in February 2019.[^cite_note-16] In July 2020, after failing to agree to a new contract with Arsenal, he was linked with a transfer away from the club, including a proposed £8 million move to [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

#### 2020–22: Senior team debut and loan to Middlesbrough

On October 29, 2020, Balogun made his senior debut in the [Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Europa_League), coming on as a substitute in the 74th minute, in a group stage match against [Dundalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundalk_F.C.).[^cite_note-auto3-19][^cite_note-2020-21-20] On November 26, he scored his first senior goal, in a Europa League group stage match against [Molde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molde_FK).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-2020-21-20]

On April 26, 2021, Balogun signed a new long-term contract with the club.[^cite_note-22] On August 13, Balogun made his [Premier League](11250.md) debut in a 2–0 loss against newly promoted [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C).[^cite_note-23]

On January 12, 2022, he joined [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) club [Middlesbrough](298603.md) on a loan deal until the end of the season.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] On March 8, he scored his first goal for *Boro* in a 4–1 loss to [Sheffield United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.),[^cite_note-27] and later scored in back-to-back wins over [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C.) and [Peterborough United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_United_F.C.).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

#### 2022–23: Loan to Reims



Balogun (left) playing for [Arsenal](2174.md) at the [2023 MLS All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_MLS_All-Star_Game)

In August 2022, he moved on loan to French club [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_Reims).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Balogun scored on his debut for the club on August 7, a 4–1 defeat to [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille); fellow Arsenal loanee [Nuno Tavares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuno_Tavares), also on his debut, scored for Marseille in the match.[^cite_note-32] On January 29, 2023, Balogun scored a late goal in stoppage time to seal a 1–1 draw away to league leaders [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.).[^cite_note-PSGgoal-33] It was his eleventh goal in Ligue 1 during the [season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ligue_1), the most of any player aged 21 or under in [Europe's top five leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-PSGgoal-33] Three days later, Balogun scored his first senior hat-trick in a 4–2 win over [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient), his fourteenth goal of the season in Ligue 1.[^cite_note-34] This made him the top scorer in Ligue 1 at that time.[^cite_note-35] He finished the season with 21 goals, the fourth top scorer in the league.[^cite_note-\:0-36]

Upon his return to Arsenal for the 2023–24 season, Balogun stated he would not leave the club again on loan.[^cite_note-37] In August 2023, he was reported to have attracted interest from [Chelsea](7473.md), [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan), and [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC).[^cite_note-38]

### Monaco



Balogun with [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) in 2025

On August 30, 2023, Ligue 1 side [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) announced the signing of Balogun on a five-year contract,[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] for a reported fee of €30 million, which could rise to €40 million with add-ons.[^cite_note-41] On September 2, he played his first match for Monaco, coming on against [Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Lens). During the match, he picked up his first yellow card for simulation after trying to win a penalty.[^cite_note-42] On September 17, he scored his first league goal for Monaco, three minutes after coming off the bench in a 2–2 draw to [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient).[^cite_note-43] On September 22, he had his first start at the club, in which he missed two penalties in a 1–0 defeat against [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGC_Nice).[^cite_note-44]

In the 2024–25 season, Balogun suffered a series of injuries which sidelined him most of the season. On May 3, 2025, he scored on his first start back from injury, a 3–1 win over [Saint-Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Saint-%C3%89tienne).[^cite_note-45]

On November 4, 2025, he scored his first [Champions League](44220.md) goal, securing a 1–0 away win over [Bodø/Glimt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Bod%C3%B8/Glimt).[^cite_note-46] On February 17 in the Champions League, Balogun scored a first-half brace against [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md), though Monaco would ultimately lose 2–3.[^cite_note-47] One month later on March 6, he recorded another goal and assist against PSG, with Monaco winning 3–1 in the league matchup.[^cite_note-48] Dating back to February 21, he scored in eight consecutive league games (including 10 goal contributions in his last 10 club games). This tied with the 2025–26 [top five European league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_\(association_football\)) season record goalscoring streak shared only by [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md) at [Real Madrid](26413.md) (eight goals).[^cite_note-\:2-49] Balogun's streak was also one goal away from the 21st-century Ligue 1 goalscoring streak record.[^cite_note-50] Balogun finished the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_AS_Monaco_FC_season) as Monaco's top scorer with 19 goals and four assists in all competitions, and the league's fourth top scorer with 13 goals.[^cite_note-51] For his season performances, he was named the 2025–26 Monaco Player of the Season.[^cite_note-\:2-49]

In August 2026, [Premier League](11250.md) club [Everton](91155.md) made a transfer bid for Balogun.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] On August 31, Balogun agreed to the transfer for a reported fee of €45 million (£38.58m; \$52.3m) with €5 million (£4.3m; \$5.8m) in add-ons.[^cite_note-54] However, on transfer deadline day, complications arose with his medical.[^cite_note-55] Despite Everton renegotiating the terms and being granted a two-hour 'grace period', Balogun ultimately rejected the move and opted to remain with Monaco.[^cite_note-56]

## International career

Born in the United States to Nigerian parents,[^cite_note-57] and raised in the United Kingdom, Balogun was eligible to represent the [United States](582488.md), [England](9904.md), and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) at international level before he committed himself to the United States.[^cite_note-58] He qualified for the United States through [birthright citizenship](7545952.md).[^cite_note-59]

### Youth career

After playing for England at the [under-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_under-17_football_team) level, and appearing in the [2018 UEFA European Under-17 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_UEFA_European_Under-17_Championship),[^cite_note-60] he accepted a call up from the [United States under-18 national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_under-18_soccer_team) in August 2018 for a training camp and a tournament in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-ESPN2018-11][^cite_note-61] He played in all four of the United States' games in the [Václav Ježek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Je%C5%BEek) Youth Tournament and scored twice.[^cite_note-usmnt-18-62] He also expressed interest in playing for [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), although he stated that he enjoys England's "style of play" which is "similar to Arsenal's".[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

In 2019, he appeared and scored for the [England under-18 national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_under-18_football_team) in a tournament in Dubai.[^cite_note-65] In October 2020, he made an appearance for the [England under-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_under-20_football_team) against [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_under-20_football_team).[^cite_note-66]

On August 27, 2021, Balogun received his first call up to the [England under-21 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_under-21_football_team).[^cite_note-67] On September 7, 2021, he made his England U21 debut as a substitute during the 2–0 [2023 UEFA European Under-21 Championship qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship_qualification) win over [Kosovo under-21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_national_under-21_football_team) at [Stadium MK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_MK).[^cite_note-68]

In October 2022, he said he was open to playing for [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team).[^cite_note-69]

### Senior career



Balogun playing for the [United States](582488.md) in 2026

On May 16, 2023, [FIFA](11049.md) approved Balogun's application for a switch to represent the [United States national team](582488.md). He had been recruited by the United States through several meetings, which included watching the [Orlando Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Magic) and [New York Knicks](72855.md) play in Florida.[^cite_note-70] Although the meetings were initially kept secret, Balogun inadvertently fueled widespread speculation about his international future after posting an Instagram photo of a training pitch without disclosing its location, which U.S. supporters identified the logo of an Orlando bar in the background that was near the national team's training base. His Instagram posts were subsequently flooded with comments with U.S. flags and bald eagle emojis from American fans.[^cite_note-71]

Balogun received his first call up to the United States senior squad on June 1, 2023, featuring in the squad for the [2023 CONCACAF Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals).[^cite_note-72] On June 18, Balogun scored his first goal for the United States in their Nations League [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final) victory over [Canada](723688.md), winning his first career trophy.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

Balogun was selected to the USMNT's roster at the [2024 Copa America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_America). In the first match, Balogun scored the United States' second goal in a 2–0 win against [Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia_national_football_team),[^cite_note-73] and in the second, he scored the opener in the 2–1 defeat at the hands of [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team) when the U.S. was down to 10 players after a red card given to [Timothy Weah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Weah) in the 18th minute. Despite the loss, Balogun won the Man of the Match award.[^cite_note-74] Following the game, Balogun, along with teammates Weah, [Chris Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Richards_\(soccer\)) and [Weston McKennie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston_McKennie), suffered [racial abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_association_football) from online trolls. Balogun's club [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC), as well as [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF), [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) and the [U.S. Soccer Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation) condemned the actions and fully supported the players.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

#### 2026 World Cup



Balogun playing for the [United States](582488.md) at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)

On May 26, 2026, Balogun was selected in the 26-man squad for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-77] On June 12 in his World Cup debut and the team's opening group stage match against [Paraguay](804025.md), Balogun scored twice in the 4–1 opener, being named Player of the Match. The game marked the first time an American had a multi-goal World Cup match in 96 years, since the [1930 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) in Uruguay, when [Bert Patenaude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Patenaude) scored the [first hat-trick in World Cup history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_hat-tricks), coincidentally also against Paraguay.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] The following game against [Australia](4792249.md), he won a second back-to-back Player of the Match award as the United States secured top spot in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D).[^cite_note-80]

##### Red card controversy

Further information: [2026 Trump–FIFA red card controversy](83626869.md)

Balogun scored the opening goal in the 2–0 win against [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) in the [round of 32](81667041.md), matching [Landon Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Donovan) for the second-most goals scored by an American in a single FIFA World Cup, with three, trailing only Patenaude's four goals in 1930.[^cite_note-81] However, later in the game he received a red card from referee [Raphael Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Claus) for a contact foul on the ankle of [Tarik Muharemović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarik_Muharemovi%C4%87) in the 64th minute when the two players were challenging for the ball. This made Balogun only the fourth World Cup player to score a goal and receive a red card in the same match, most recently by [Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane) in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-ESPN-82][^cite_note-83] This initially forced him out of the United States' [round of 16](81667005.md) match against [Belgium](679716.md), which led to outrage by American fans and caused a media campaign of "Free Flo".[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-ESPN-82]

The ban was later suspended by [FIFA](11049.md) after a review, allowing him to play against Belgium, a decision that sparked controversy over the application of disciplinary rules and claims of political interference involving the [Trump administration](78298108.md).[^cite_note-85] The United States went on to lose 4–1 to Belgium, with Balogun featuring in the match.[^cite_note-86] Balogun later said he expected the decision to "cause a lot of controversy" but was grateful to return to the team.[^cite_note-87]

## Style of play

Balogun is known for his pace, technique, and [two-footedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footedness).[^cite_note-88] Labelled a pure "[number nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squad_number_\(association_football\))" or [center forward](428681.md#Centre-forward), Balogun has also played as a *[trequartista](548981.md#Attacking_midfielder)* in youth soccer, and was deployed by [Chris Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wilder) in a [second-striker](428681.md#Second_striker) role at [Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_F.C).[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] [Martin Keown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Keown) compared Balogun to Arsenal's second highest ever goalscorer [Ian Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Wright), due to his pace and intelligent movement.[^cite_note-91]
