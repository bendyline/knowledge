**Princess Astrid, Mrs. Ferner** (Astrid Maud Ingeborg; 12 February 1932 – 11 September 2026), was the second child and youngest daughter of [King Olav V of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Olav_V_of_Norway) and [Princess Märtha of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_M%C3%A4rtha_of_Sweden). Astrid was the older sister of King [Harald V](66069.md) and the aunt of King [Haakon VIII](98258.md). Following her mother's death in 1954, she served as Norway's [first lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_lady) during the reign of her grandfather, King [Haakon VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_VII), and continued in this role throughout her father's reign until her brother's marriage in 1968.

Astrid married [Johan Ferner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Ferner) in 1961 and had five children. In later life she undertook public engagements, received an honorary government pension, and remained active in commemorative events. She was the chair of Crown Princess Märtha's Memorial Fund and patron of several organisations, particularly those supporting children and young people with [dyslexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexia).

## Early life and education

Astrid was born on 12 February 1932 at Villa Solbakken to the future [King Olav V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Olav_V) and [Crown Princess Märtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_M%C3%A4rtha_of_Sweden). She was baptised in the [Palace Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slottskapellet_\(Oslo\)) on 31 March. Her godparents were her paternal grandparents, [King Haakon VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Haakon_VII) and [Queen Maud of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Maud_of_Norway); her maternal grandparents, [Prince Carl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Carl,_Duke_of_V%C3%A4sterg%C3%B6tland) and [Princess Ingeborg of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Ingeborg_of_Sweden); her maternal aunt, [Princess Astrid, Duchess of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrid_of_Sweden); [Elizabeth, Duchess of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth,_Duchess_of_York); her great aunt, [Princess Thyra of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Thyra_of_Denmark_\(1880%E2%80%931945\)); her great uncle, [Prince Eugen of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Eugen_of_Sweden); and [Prince George of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George,_Duke_of_Kent). She was named after her maternal aunt, her paternal grandmother, and her maternal grandmother.[^cite_note-royalhouse-1] Astrid was a great-granddaughter of King [Edward VII of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VII_of_the_United_Kingdom) and therefore a second cousin of [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II). At the time of her birth, she was 18th in the [line of succession to the British throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_to_the_British_throne).[^cite_note-2]

She grew up on the royal estate of [Skaugum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skaugum) in [Asker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asker) and was privately educated. During [World War II](32927.md), she joined her family in fleeing the [Nazi invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_invasion_of_Norway) and spent the war years in exile in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), with her mother, brother, and sister.[^cite_note-3] After the royal family returned to Norway, she attended [Nissen's Girls' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissen's_Girls'_School).[^cite_note-\:3-4]

Her [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation) took place on 9 May 1948 in the Palace Chapel. Astrid studied ceramics with Halvor Sandøs and maintained her own pottery studio at Skaugum. She was also an accomplished [skier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skier) and [yachtswoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yachtswoman).[^cite_note-5]

After completing the [examen artium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examen_artium) in 1950,[^cite_note-\:3-4] Astrid studied economics and political history for two years at [Lady Margaret Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Margaret_Hall), [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University).[^cite_note-newspapers-6]

## Public role

Astrid's mother died in 1954. From that year until her brother's [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Harald,_Crown_Prince_of_Norway,_and_Sonja_Haraldsen) to [Sonja Haraldsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonja_Haraldsen) in August 1968, she served as the senior lady of the court and acted as Norway's [first lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_lady),[^cite_note-\:2-7] undertaking representation duties alongside her father, including state visits.[^cite_note-newspapers-6][^cite_note-royalhouse-1] In September 1954, she took part in the ship tour organised by [Queen Frederica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederica_of_Hanover) and King [Paul of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_of_Greece), later known as the "[Cruise of the Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_of_the_Kings)", which was attended by more than 100 European royals.[^cite_note-8]

## Marriage and children



The baptism of Astrid and Ferner's fourth child, Elisabeth, 31 May 1969. The infant's godmother, [Crown Princess Sonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Sonja_of_Norway), looks on.

Astrid married [Johan Martin Ferner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Martin_Ferner) (1927–2015) on 12 January 1961 at Asker Church, [Asker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asker).[^cite_note-9] They met when she hired him to sail her yacht in a race.[^cite_note-10] The marriage faced opposition from the [Church of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Norway) because Ferner had been previously married and [divorced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_views_on_divorce#Lutheran_Churches). After the wedding, she became known as Princess Astrid, Mrs Ferner ([Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language): *Prinsesse Astrid, fru Ferner*),[^cite_note-newspapers-6] and ceased to receive the annual endowment of 50,000 [Norwegian crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_crown) that she had been granted.[^cite_note-11] They had five children, seven grandchildren and, as of 2023, three great-grandchildren.[^cite_note-12] The couple raised their family in [Vinderen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinderen) in a house given by her father.[^cite_note-\:4-13]

## Later life and death



Astrid pictured in 2018

After raising her children, she and her husband moved to a condominium in Oslo which bordered [Nordmarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordmarka).[^cite_note-\:4-13]

In 2002, the government granted Astrid an honorary pension in recognition of her service to Norway, both during and after her years as first lady.[^cite_note-18] In 2005, she took part in ceremonies marking the 60th anniversary of the end of the Second World War, including the unveiling of a plaque commemorating the Norwegian monarch's exile in London.[^cite_note-19]

In February 2012, Astrid celebrated her 80th birthday with a private dinner at the Royal Palace in Oslo.[^cite_note-20]

In March 2026, Astrid was hospitalised following a short illness and was therefore unable to attend the state visit to Norway by [King Philippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_of_Belgium) and [Queen Mathilde of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mathilde_of_Belgium). The Royal Palace stated that she required rest and recovery but did not initially release further details. According to media reports, her brother, [King Harald V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Harald_V), and [Queen Sonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Sonja) visited her in a hospital in Oslo on 27 March.[^cite_note-\:2-7] It was later confirmed that she was suffering from [pneumonia](52135.md).[^cite_note-21] In May 2026, she was hospitalised again after carrying out a public engagement.[^cite_note-22] She relied on the use of a wheelchair in her final months.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Astrid died on 11 September 2026, aged 94, two days after attending the [state funeral of her brother](84083326.md) King Harald V.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Her funeral took place on 23 September at [Ris Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ris_Church) in Oslo.[^cite_note-28] [Crown Princess Victoria of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Crown_Princess_of_Sweden), [Princess Astrid of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Astrid_of_Belgium) and her husband [Prince Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Lorenz_of_Belgium), and siblings [Prince Jean of Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Jean_of_Luxembourg) and [Princess Margaretha of Luxembourg and Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Margaretha_of_Liechtenstein) attended her funeral.[^cite_note-29]

## Patronages

Astrid was chair of the board of [Crown Princess Märtha's Memorial Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_M%C3%A4rtha_of_Sweden#Legacy),[^cite_note-\:2-7] which provides financial support to social and humanitarian initiatives carried out by non-governmental organisations.[^cite_note-30]

She was a patron of several organisations with a focus on children and young people with [dyslexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexia), having the condition herself.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]
