[Sayyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid#Oman) **Badr bin Hamad bin Hamood Al Busaidi** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بدر بن حمد بن حمود البوسعيدي; born 30 May 1960) is a member of the [Omani royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bu_Said_dynasty), a diplomat and politician who has been serving as the [foreign minister](36308453.md) of [Oman](22316.md) since 18 August 2020.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] He has represented Oman in regional and international meetings, which include the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-3]

## Personal life

### Early life

Badr was born in [Muscat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat,_Oman) on 30 May 1960. He is the second son of Hamad bin Hamood Al Busaidi, who worked under [Sultan Said bin Taimur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_bin_Taimur) as his private secretary in the 1960s, and under [Sultan Qaboos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaboos_bin_Said) as Minister of Diwan (Royal Court) Affairs, managing the relations between the Sultan and the citizens, upon his succession in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Omani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) until 1986.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] From 1986 until his death in 2002, Hamad was the personal adviser to Sultan Qaboos.

### Education

Al Busaidi received his early education in the Saideyya schools between Muscat and [Salalah](1518956.md). He left for the United Kingdom in 1977 to pursue further education, where he spent his first months in [Wales](69894.md) with a private [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor). He relocated to [London](17867.md) in spring 1978 and worked with a team of tutors for three years. This education enabled Al Busaidi to gain the necessary grades in the British Secondary School Examinations to get admitted to [University of Oxford](31797.md), where he was awarded an [M.Litt.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Litt.) in Politics, Philosophy and Economics (PPE) in 1986.[^cite_note-6]

### Family

In 1979, Al Busaidi Badr married Noora bint Abdullah bin Mahawish Al Daher, formerly a Saudi Arabian but now an Omani national. They divorced in 1993. Their children are:

- Nasr bin Badr bin Hamad Al Busaidi

- Asila bint Badr bin Hamad Al Busaidi

- Salsabeel bint Badr bin Hamad Al Busaidi

- Mazan bint Badr bin Hamad Al Busaidi

## Diplomatic career



Al Busaidi with US Secretary of State [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) in 2022

Following Badr’s return to Muscat in 1988, he joined the [Foreign Ministry](36308453.md) as a diplomat. In 1989, he was appointed as the first secretary, and he established the Office of Political Analysis to provide systematic assessment and policy analysis of key international and regional issues. In 1990, he was promoted to councillor, and in 1996, he was promoted to [ambassador](68651.md). Al Busaidi was appointed as the head of the Minister's Office Department in 1997.&#x20;

In 2000, he was promoted to undersecretary, and then became Secretary General of the Foreign Ministry in 2007. He was appointed as the Foreign Minister on 18 August 2020.[^cite_note-7]

He led the initial negotiation with the United States concerning labour law issues, which began in 1993 and subsequently led to Omani membership of the [World Trade Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organisation) in 2000, and to a US Omani Free Trade Agreement in 2006. He has also chaired the Omani side in many bilateral and multilateral meetings regionally and internationally.[^cite_note-8]

In December 2025, he met Prince [Faisal bin Farhan Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_bin_Farhan_Al_Saud) in [Riyadh](51554.md) to seek a political solution to the [crisis in Yemen](46215853.md) after a [Saudi-led strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi-led_airstrikes_on_Yemen) on Yemen's southern port of [Mukalla](2414800.md), which raised tensions between Riyadh and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In late February 2026, Al Busaidi served as a key mediator between the [United States](3434750.md) and [Iran](14653.md) in [discussions around the latter's nuclear program](79685207.md). He met with US Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md),[^cite_note-11] and gave an interview on [CBS News](359551.md) claiming that a peace deal was "within reach".[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Analysts characterised this as a last-minute, and ultimately unsuccessful, attempt to prevent the [2026 Israeli–United States strikes on Iran](82546969.md), which began several hours after Al Busaidi's comments.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] After the United States and [Israel](9282173.md) attacked Iran, Al-Busaidi said that he was dismayed and that "active and serious negotiations" had been undermined while urging the United States to "not get sucked in further" into the conflict, adding "this is not your war."[^cite_note-16] He also claimed that the United States had "lost control of its own foreign policy" and accused Israel of persuading the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_administration) to engage in a war with Iran, which he termed a "grave miscalculation" and a "catastrophe".[^cite_note-17]

Al Busaidi has lectured in Oman and internationally on topics such as cultural dialogue, modernisation and development, and the writing of [Omani history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Oman). He takes a keen personal interest in youth development, human rights and the advancement of [women in public life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Oman), and he promotes cultural diplomacy, tolerance and mutual understanding.
