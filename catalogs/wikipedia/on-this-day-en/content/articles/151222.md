**Midland** is a city in the U.S. state of [Texas](29810.md). It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Midland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_County,_Texas) in [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas),[^cite_note-GR6-6] with small portions extending into [Martin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_County,_Texas). The population was 132,524 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census). Located in the [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)), Midland is a major center for [American oil and natural gas production](19086358.md).

The [Midland metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_metropolitan_area,_Texas) includes all of Midland County and had 169,983 residents at the 2020 census.[^cite_note-7] The metropolitan area is part of the larger [Midland–Odessa combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland%E2%80%93Odessa), which had a population of 340,391 in the 2020 census.[^cite_note-8] Residents of Midland are referred to as "Midlanders".

Midland was founded as the midway point between [Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas) and [El Paso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas) on the [Texas and Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_and_Pacific_Railway) in 1881. The city has many connections to the [Bush family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_family); it was the one-time home of former U.S. presidents [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) and [George W. Bush](3414021.md), and the hometown of former First Lady [Laura Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bush). The [Bush Family Home State Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_Family_Home_State_Historic_Site) is located in Midland.

## History

See also: [Timeline of Midland, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Midland,_Texas)



Main Street in 1894

Midland was established in June 1881 as Midway Station, on the [Texas and Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_and_Pacific_Railway). Its name came from its central location between Fort Worth and El Paso, but because there were already other towns in Texas named Midway, the city changed its name to Midland in January 1884 when it was granted its first post office.

Midland became the county seat of Midland County in March 1885, when that county was first organized and separated from [Tom Green County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Green_County,_Texas). By 1890, it had become one of the state's most important cattle shipping centers. The city was incorporated in 1906, and by 1910 established its first fire department, along with a new water system.[^cite_note-9]

Midland was changed significantly by the discovery of [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_drilling) in the [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)) in 1923 when the Santa Rita No. 1 well began producing in [Reagan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_County), followed shortly by the [Yates Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yates_Oil_Field) in [Iraan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraan,_Texas). Midland became the West Texas oil fields' administrative center. During [World War II](32927.md), it had the nation's largest bombardier training base. A second boom began after the war, with the discovery and development of the [Spraberry Trend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spraberry_Trend), still the country's third-largest oil field by total reserves.[^cite_note-10] Yet another boom period took place during the 1970s, with the high oil prices associated with the oil and energy crises. Today, the Permian Basin produces one fifth of the nation's total petroleum and [natural gas](22131.md) output.

Midland's economy still relies heavily on petroleum, but the city also became a regional telecommunications and distribution center. By August 2006, a busy period of crude oil production had caused a significant workforce deficit. According to the Midland Chamber of Commerce, at that time there were almost 2,000 more jobs available in the [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)) than there were workers to fill them.

In 1959, [John Howard Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard_Griffin) wrote a history of Midland, *Land of the High Sky.*

### *Avery v. Midland County*

In 1967, the U.S. Supreme Court heard the case *[Avery v. Midland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avery_v._Midland_County)*. Midland mayor Hank Avery had sued Midland County, challenging the electoral-districting scheme in effect for elections to the County Commissioner's Court. The county districts geographically quartered the county, but Midland, in the northwestern quarter, had 97% of the county's population. A judge, elected on an at-large basis, provided a fifth vote, but the result was that the three rural commissioners, representing only 3% of the county's population, held a majority of the votes.

The Court held that the scheme violated the [Fourteenth Amendment](31666.md)'s [Equal Protection Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Protection_Clause). A dissenting minority held that this example of the [Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Warren) Court's policy of [incorporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporation_\(Bill_of_Rights\)) at the local-government level exceeded its [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Constitution) authority.

Midland is in the [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)) in the [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas) plains.

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has an area of 71.5 square miles (185.2 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 71.3 square miles (184.7 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 0.2 square mile (0.5 km<sup>2</sup>) (0.28%) is water.

### Climate

Midland has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*) bordering upon a [hot arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_arid_climate) (*BWh*) and a [cool semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cool_semi-arid_climate) (*BSk*) with hot to sweltering summers and cool to mild winters. It occasionally has cold waves during the winter, but rarely sees extended periods of below-freezing cold.

Midland receives approximately 13.5 inches (340 mm) of precipitation per year, much of which falls in the summer. Highs exceed 90 °F or 32.2 °C on 115 afternoons per year and 100 °F or 37.8 °C on 24 afternoons while lows fall to 32 °F or 0 °C or below on 58 mornings.[^cite_note-NWS-11][^cite_note-NOWData-12]

### Cityscape

See also: [Category\:Buildings and structures in Midland, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Buildings_and_structures_in_Midland,_Texas)

Nicknamed "The Tall City", Midland has long been known for its downtown skyline. Most of downtown Midland's major office buildings were built during a time of major [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)) oil and gas discoveries. The surge in energy prices in the mid-1980s sparked a building boom downtown. For many years, the 22-story [Wilco Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco_building) in downtown was the tallest building between [Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas) and [Phoenix](49121.md). Today, the tallest is the 24-story [Bank of America Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_America_Building_\(Midland\)), at 332 feet (101 m). Four buildings over 500 feet (150 m) tall were planned in the 1980s, including one designed by architect [I.M. Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.M._Pei).[^cite_note-16]

The great oil bust of the mid-1980s killed any plans for future skyscrapers. A private development group was planning to build [Energy Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Tower_\(Midland,_Texas\)) at City Center, which was proposed to be 870 feet tall, with 59 floors (six floors underground and 53 above). If it had been built, it would have been Texas's sixth-tallest building.[^cite_note-17]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), there were 132,524 people, 48,227 households, and 32,632 families residing in the city.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-23]

The population density was 1,558.9 inhabitants per square mile (601.9/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 55,424 housing units at an average density of 667.1 per square mile (257.6/km<sup>2</sup>); 13.0% of the units were vacant, the homeowner vacancy rate was 1.7%, and the rental vacancy rate was 21.3%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-23]

According to the same census, 98.4% of residents lived in urban areas, while 1.6% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-24]

Of the 48,227 households, 37.8% had children under the age of 18 living in them, 51.2% were married-couple households, 19.4% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 23.3% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. In the city, the population was distributed as 27.2% under the age of 18, 9.0% from 18 to 24, 28.2% from 25 to 44, 20.6% from 45 to 64, and 11.1% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 33.3 years. For every 100 females, there were 100.9 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 99.7 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-23]

The median household income in 2020 was \$90,448, with a per capita income of \$47,870. 10.5% of the population was below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line).[^cite_note-26]

### 2000 census

In 2000, the median income for a household in the city was \$39,320, and for a family was \$48,290. Males had a median income of \$37,566 versus \$24,794 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the city in 2007 was \$52,294.[^cite_note-27] In 2000, about 10.1% of families and 12.9% of the population were below the poverty line, including 16.4% of those under age 18 and 8.0% of those age 65 or over.

In 2014, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine ranked Midland the second fastest-growing small city in the United States.[^cite_note-28]

[Midland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_College) is home to the McCormick Gallery, inside the Allison Fine Arts Building on the main campus. Throughout the year, exhibits at the McCormick feature works of MC students and faculty, visiting artists, and juried exhibits. The Arts Council of Midland[^cite_note-31] serves as the promotional and public relations vehicle to promote the arts and stimulate community participation and support. The McCormick is also home to the Studio 3600 Series,[^cite_note-32] established in 2006 to "spotlight selected art students and provide them the opportunity to exhibit key works that identify the style they have crafted over a period of time."

### Performing arts

The Midland-Odessa Symphony and Chorale (MOSC) has performed in the Permian Basin for over 45 years, and is the region's largest orchestral organization, presenting both Pops and Masterworks concerts throughout the year. The MOSC also is home to three resident chamber ensembles, the Lone Star Brass, Permian Basin String Quartet and West Texas Winds. These ensembles are made up of principal musicians in the orchestra.

The Midland Community Theatre (MCT) originated in 1946 with musicals, comedies, dramas, mysteries, children's theatre and melodramas. MCT produces 15 shows each year in three performance spaces—Davis Theatre I and Mabee Theatre II, in the Cole Theatre, and the annual fundraiser [Summer Mummers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Mummers) in the Yucca Theatre. MCT is a member of the American Association of Community Theatre, and hosted the 2006 AACT International Theatrefest.

Twice each year, the Phyllis and Bob Cowan Performing Arts Series at Midland College presents free cultural and artistic performances.[^cite_note-33] The series was endowed in 1999.

### Tourism



[Permian Basin Petroleum Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_Petroleum_Museum)

Sitting on the southern edge of the [Llano Estacado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llano_Estacado) and near the center of the [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)) oil fields, Midland's economy has long been focused on petroleum exploration and extraction. Providing more information about this industry is the [Permian Basin Petroleum Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_Petroleum_Museum), on the outskirts of town near Interstate 20. The museum houses numerous displays on the history, science, and technology of oil and gas development. The Permian Basin Petroleum Museum houses a collection of race cars designed by [Jim Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Hall_\(race_car_driver\)), a longtime Midland resident who pioneered the use of aerodynamic downforce in Formula One car design.

Midland is also home to The Museum of the Southwest. The museum features a collection of paintings by various members of the [Taos Society of Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taos_Society_of_Artists) and [Karl Bodmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bodmer) as well as engravings by John J. and John W. Audubon. Within the same museum complex are the Children's Museum and the Marian W. Blakemore Planetarium. The Museum of the Southwest is in the Turner Mansion, the historic 1934 home of Fred and Juliette Turner.

On display at the Midland County Historical Museum are reproductions of the "Midland Man", the skeleton of a [Clovis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_Culture) female found near the city in 1953.[^cite_note-34] Analysis of the remains by Curtis R. McKinney using uranium-thorium analysis showed that the bones are 11,600 ± 800 years old. Presenting his findings at the annual meeting of the [Geological Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Society_of_America) in 1992, McKinney said, "\[T]he Midland Woman was related to the earliest ancestors of every Indian who lives today, and she is very likely the only representative of those who created the Clovis cultures."

## Sports

Midland is home to the [Midland RockHounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_RockHounds), a [Texas League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_League) [minor league baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league_baseball) team. It is the AA affiliate of the [Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_\(baseball\)). The Rockhounds have played their home games in [Momentum Bank Ballpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_Bank_Ballpark) since 2002.

[West Texas FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas_FC) is an American soccer team founded in 2008. The team is a member of [USL League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_Two), the fourth tier of the American Soccer Pyramid, in the Ranger Division of the Southern Conference. The team plays its home games at [Astound Broadband Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astound_Broadband_Stadium).

Midland is home to the West Texas Drillers (adult tackle football) of the Minor Professional Football League. They were established in 2009. They play their home games at Grande Communications Stadium.

[Midland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_College) is a member of the [Western Junior College Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Junior_College_Athletic_Conference), and fields teams in baseball, men's basketball, women's basketball, men's golf, softball and volleyball. Midland College has won 20 national championships in sports since 1975, as well as produced 192 All-Americans.

Plans have been made to develop a 35-court tennis facility named the Bush Tennis Center.

Midland is also home to the Midland Mad Dog Rugby Club, which competes in the Texas Rugby Union as a Division III team.

Midland brought arena football to the city with the [Midland Frac-Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Frac-Attack) of [American Indoor Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indoor_Football) that play at the [Midland County Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_County_Horseshoe?action=edit\&redlink=1) starting in 2026.

## Education

### Colleges and universities

Midland is the home of [Midland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_College) (MC), which offers over 50 programs of study for associate degrees and certificates to more than 6,000 students who enroll each semester. MC offers programs in health sciences, information technology, and aviation, including a professional pilot training program. MC is one of only three community colleges in Texas approved to offer a bachelor's degree in applied technology. Dr. Damon Kennedy is MC's president.

Midland is home to the [Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center Permian Basin Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Tech_University_Health_Sciences_Center_Permian_Basin_Campus)'s physician assistant program, on the MC campus. The entry-level graduate program awards a Master of Physician Assistant Studies following 27 months of intensive academic and clinical training.

All of Midland County except for the Greenwood community is in the service area of Midland College. All of Martin County is in the service area of [Howard County Junior College District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_County_Junior_College_District).[^cite_note-41]

### Primary and secondary schools

[Midland Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Independent_School_District) serves the portion in Midland County, as in the vast majority of Midland.[^cite_note-42] Midland is home to three public high schools: [Midland High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_High_School_\(Midland,_Texas\)), [Legacy High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_High_School_\(Midland,_Texas\)) and Early College High School (ECHS) at Midland College, all of which are part of MISD. Another school district just outside Midland, [Greenwood Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_Independent_School_District), serves approximately 3,000 students[^cite_note-43] and operates [Greenwood High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_High_School_\(Texas\)), James R. Brooks Middle School, Greenwood Intermediate, and Greenwood Elementary.

In July 2020 the Midland Independent School District voted to change the name of the former Robert E. Lee High School to Legacy High School in the wake of the [George Floyd protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests).[^cite_note-44]

ECHS welcomed its first freshman class on August 24, 2009. It aims to award students their associate degrees from [Midland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_College) by the time they receive their high school diplomas.[^cite_note-45]

The portion in Martin County is in the [Stanton Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanton_Independent_School_District).[^cite_note-46]

Midland has many private schools, including Hillcrest School, Hillander, Midland Classical Academy, Midland Christian School, Midland Montessori, St. Ann's School, and Trinity School of Midland. It is also home to four charter schools: Richard Milburn Academy, [Premier High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_High_School_of_Midland), Texas Leadership Charter Academy, and Midland Academy Charter School.

## Transportation

Midland is served by [Midland International Air and Space Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_International_Airport) (ICAO code: KMAF, IATA code: MAF), which is located between [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa,_Texas) and Midland. Midland Airpark (ICAO code: KMDD, IATA code: MDD) is a [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airport located on Midland's northeast side.[^cite_note-47] Midland was the site of the 2012 [Midland train crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_train_crash), in which a train collided with a parade float carrying wounded military veterans, killing four. Midland also has citywide public bus services provided for the [Midland-Odessa Urban Transit District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland-Odessa_Urban_Transit_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) by Midland-Odessa Transit Management, otherwise known as E-Z Rider.
