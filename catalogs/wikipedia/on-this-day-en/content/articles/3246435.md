**Lee Cheuk-yan** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 李卓人; born 12 February 1957 in Shanghai[^cite_note-rfa_20240213-1]) is a Hong Kong politician and social activist.[^cite_note-Lo2006-2] He was a member of the [Legislative Council of Hong Kong](273946.md) from 1995 to 2016, when he lost his seat. He represented the [Kowloon West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_West_\(1995_constituency\)) and the [Manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_\(constituency\)) constituencies briefly in 1995 and had been representing the [New Territories West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories_West) constituency from 1998 to 2016. He is a former trade union leader, former General Secretary of the [Hong Kong Confederation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Confederation_of_Trade_Unions), as well as former chairman of the [Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China](1974713.md). He has been jailed since April 2021 for his role in the [Hong Kong anti-extradition bill protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests), having been sentenced for organizing two unauthorized assemblies. In September 2026, he was sentenced for incitement to subversion to seven years in prison, alongside fellow activists [Chow Hang-tung](67868709.md) and [Albert Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ho). By that time he had already been in prison in relation to that case for about five years.[^cite_note-hkfp_20260911-3]

## Background

Lee's ancestral home is [Chaoyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaoyang_District,_Shantou), Guangdong.[^cite_note-4] Lee emigrated from [Mainland China](55079.md) to Hong Kong in 1959. He graduated from the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong) with a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in civil engineering in 1978.[^cite_note-lg1-5] His labour activism began with his first job after graduation, when he came in contact with workers with occupational injuries and diseases.[^cite_note-scmp_hk_magazine_2009-6]

During the student-led [Tiananmen Square protests of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989), he collected donations from the [Concert for Democracy in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_for_Democracy_in_China) in Hong Kong and travelled to Beijing to hand over the funds to student protesters in [Tiananmen Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square). He was detained by the authorities there and made to sign a confession letter before being allowed to return to Hong Kong.[^cite_note-scmp_hk_magazine_2009-6][^cite_note-7] Since the events of 1989, Lee has remained a standing committee member of the [Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China](1974713.md) which promotes democratic reform in China.

## Legislative Councillor

In 1995, Lee was elected unopposed in a by-election of the [Legislative Council](273946.md), replacing [Lau Chin-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lau_Chin-shek), who had resigned. He was re-elected four times, continuously serving as a lawmaker, except for a brief period during 1997 and 1998, when the sovereignty of Hong Kong was transferred to the People's Republic of China, and the Legislative Council temporarily became a [Provisional Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Legislative_Council), composed of Beijing nominees.

He received an unexpected loss in the [2016 Legislative Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Hong_Kong_legislative_election), departing the legislature after more than 20 years of service.

## Labour Party

In December 2011, he and three other Legislative Councillors [Fernando Cheung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Cheung), [Cyd Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyd_Ho) and [Cheung Kwok-che](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheung_Kwok-che) co-founded the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Hong_Kong\)), which became the third largest [pan-democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-democracy_camp_in_Hong_Kong) party in the legislature. He served as chairman until December 2015 when he stepped down to give way to the younger party members and took the vice-chairmanship.[^cite_note-8]

## Family

He is married to [Elizabeth Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Tang?action=edit\&redlink=1), who in 2005 was the chairperson of the [Hong Kong People's Alliance on WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_People's_Alliance_on_WTO),[^cite_note-lg1-5][^cite_note-9] and who as of March 2023 is the general secretary of the [International Domestic Workers Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Domestic_Workers_Federation).[^cite_note-10] They have one daughter.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Arrests and sentencings

On 28 February 2020, Lee was arrested for his involvement in a [pro-democracy](3291227.md) march on 31 August 2019, which was part of the [protests sparked by the extradition bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests) and had been classified by police as illegal assembly. A few hours later, he was released on bail, as were the other arrestees [Jimmy Lai](269231.md) and [Yeung Sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeung_Sum). The cases were scheduled to be heard at Eastern Law Court on 5 May 2020.[^cite_note-scmp_3052789-13][^cite_note-hongkongfp_20200228_hong-14]

On 18 April 2020, Lee was one of the 15 Hong Kong high-profile democracy figures arrested on suspicion of organizing, publicizing or taking part in several unauthorized assemblies between August and October 2019 during the anti-extradition bill protests.[^cite_note-nytimes_20200418_world-15][^cite_note-hongkongfp_20200418_8-16] On 1 April 2021, Lee, along with six other pro-democracy advocates, was found guilty of organizing an unauthorised rally on 18 August 2019.[^cite_note-npr_162543970097-17] On 16 April, Lee was sentenced to 14 months in jail for his role in this and another August 2019 rally.[^cite_note-theguardian_20210416_jimmy-18][^cite_note-courthousenews_20200416-19]

On 11 September 2026, Lee was sentenced to seven years in prison over an incitement of subversion case for which he, alongside fellow activists [Chow Hang-tung](67868709.md) and [Albert Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ho). Chow and Ho were sentenced the same day, as was the [Hong Kong Alliance](1974713.md) in which the three had leading roles.[^cite_note-hkfp_20260911-3]
