**Michel Henri Devoret** (French: [\[miʃɛl dəvɔʁɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); born 5 March 1953)[^cite_note-2] is a French–American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist). He is Professor of Physics at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara),[^cite_note-UCSBDevoret-3][^cite_note-4] and Professor Emeritus of Applied Physics at [Yale University](34273.md).[^cite_note-5] He serves as the Chief Scientist for Quantum Hardware at [Google Quantum AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Quantum_AI).[^cite_note-GoogleDevoret-6] He is known for the development of various [superconducting quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing) architectures, including the quantronium, the [transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon), and the [fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#Fluxonium).&#x20;

Devoret shared the 2025 [Nobel Prize in Physics](52497.md) with [John Clarke](17245653.md) and [John M. Martinis](62226822.md) for their joint work on [macroscopic quantum phenomena](35543722.md) in [superconducting circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_quantum_electrodynamics).[^cite_note-Nobel-7]&#x20;

## Early life and education

Michel Henri Devoret was born on 5 March 1953 in [Paris](22989.md).[^cite_note-CDF-8][^cite_note-9] He has stated that his parents were of Jewish background, even though they were not religious.[^cite_note-10]&#x20;

Devoret graduated with an [engineer's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer%27s_degree) in 1975 from École nationale supérieure des télécommunications (now [Télécom Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9l%C3%A9com_Paris)). He then obtained a DEA (*[Diplôme d'études approfondies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipl%C3%B4me_d%27%C3%A9tudes_approfondies)*) in Quantum Optics in 1976, followed by a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Quantum Condensed Matter Physics in 1982, both from the University of Orsay (now [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-CDF-8] He performed his doctoral research at [CEA Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Paris-Saclay) in the group of [Anatole Abragam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatole_Abragam),[^cite_note-Hassinger-12][^cite_note-\:3-13] under the supervision of [Neil S. Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_S._Sullivan).[^cite_note-\:3-13]&#x20;

## Career

Devoret worked as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) in [John Clarke](17245653.md)'s group at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), from 1982 to 1984.[^cite_note-CDF-8] Together, with [John M. Martinis](62226822.md), a graduate student at the time, they demonstrated for the first time the [quantized energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit#Quantized_energy_levels) of a [Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect) in 1985.[^cite_note-CDF-8][^cite_note-Hassinger-12]&#x20;

Devoret then returned to France and founded the Quantronics group at the Orme des Merisiers laboratory of CEA Saclay together with [Daniel Esteve](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Esteve\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and Cristian Urbina. The group measured the traversal time of tunnelling, invented an electron pump, observed the charge of Cooper pairs directly, and developed a type of [qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit) dubbed *quantronium*. They also observed the [Ramsey fringes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_interferometry) of quantronium.[^cite_note-CDF-8][^cite_note-\:2-14][^cite_note-15]&#x20;

In 1996, Devoret spent a research stay in the laboratory of [Hans Mooij](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hans_Mooij\&action=edit\&redlink=1) at [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology).[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

Devoret became a professor at [Yale University](34273.md) in 2002. At Yale University, [Steven Girvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Girvin), [Robert J. Schoelkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Schoelkopf), and Devoret devised a type of superconducting [charge qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_qubit) called the [transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] In 2009, Devoret also pioneered [fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#Fluxonium),[^cite_note-19] which can be understood as a special type of [flux qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_qubit). In 2010, he also developed a microwave [quantum limited amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_amplifier) for qubit readout and sensing.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]&#x20;

He also participated in 2018 in an experiment demonstrating the interruption and reversal of quantum jumps in a superconducting [artificial atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_atom), providing new insights into the dynamics of quantum measurement.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

From 2007 to 2012, Michel Devoret held the Chair of Mesoscopic Physics at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_de_France) where his inaugural lecture, "From the Atom to Quantum Machines," illustrated the connection between fundamental quantum phenomena and emerging quantum technologies.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] He resigned in 2013.[^cite_note-CDF-8][^cite_note-\:2-14]&#x20;

In 2023, he was named the Chief Scientist for Hardware at [Google](1092923.md) Quantum AI.[^cite_note-GoogleDevoret-6] In 2024, he moved to the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) to serve as Professor of Physics.[^cite_note-UCSBDevoret-3]&#x20;

## Recognition

In 1970, Michel Devoret received the Prix de la Couronne française, an award recognizing young French researchers.[^cite_note-25]&#x20;
