# Ural (Russian administrative region)

**Ural** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Урал, from [Bashkir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkir_language): Урал, romanized: *Ural*) is a geographical region located around the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains), between the [East European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_European_Plain) and [West Siberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Siberian_Plain) plains. It is considered a part of the [Eurasian Steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe), extending approximately from the North to the South; from the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean) to the end of the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(river\)) near [Orsk](1616356.md) city. The border between Europe and Asia runs along the Eastern side of the Ural Mountains.[^cite_note-bse-3] Ural mostly lies within [Russia](25391.md) but also includes a small part of Northwestern [Kazakhstan](16642.md). This is historical, not an official entity, with borders overlapping its Western [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) and Eastern [Siberia](29639.md) neighboring regions. At some point in the past, parts of the currently existing Ural region were considered a gateway to Siberia, or even Siberia itself, and were combined with the Volga administrative the divisions. Today, there are two official namesake entities: the [Ural Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Federal_District) and the [Ural economic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_economic_region). While the latter follows the historical borders, the former is a political product; the District omits Western Ural and includes Western Siberia instead.

The historical center of the Ural is [Cherdyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherdyn,_Perm_Krai), which is now a small town in [Perm Krai](1765550.md). Perm was an administrative center of the [gubernia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubernia) with the same name by 1797. Most of the territory of historical and modern Ural was included in Perm Gubernia. The administrative center of Urals was moved to Sverdlovsk (nowadays [Yekaterinburg](66524.md)) after the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) and [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War). In the present, the Ural economic region does not have an administrative and informal capital, whereas Yekaterinburg is the administrative center of the Ural Federal District.[^cite_note-ukaz-1]

## Etymology

See also: [Ural-batyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural-batyr)

From the 11th century, the region of the Ural Mountains was called *Kamyen'* (Камень, "the Stone") by Russians. In the mid-16th – early 17th century, the southern parts became known as Ural, which later spread to the entire area. The name probably originated from [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) "aral". This word literally means "island" and was used for any territory different from the surrounding terrain. In [Bashkortostan](735530.md) there is a 13th-century legend about a hero named [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural-batyr). He sacrificed his life for the sake of his people, and they poured a stone pile over his grave, which later turned into the Ural Mountains.[^cite_note-bse-3]

## Topography

See also: [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains)



[Manaraga mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaraga)



Iset River near [Kamensk-Uralsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamensk-Uralsky).

By topography and other natural features, Ural is divided, from north to south, into the Polar (or Arctic), Near-Polar (or Sub-Arctic), Northern, Central and Southern parts. The Polar Ural has an area of about 25,000 km<sup>2</sup> and a strongly dissected relief. The Near-Polar Ural is wider (up to 150 km) and higher than the Polar Ural. The Northern Ural consists of a series of parallel ridges with the height up to 1,000–1,300 m and longitudinal depressions, which are elongated from north to south. The Central Ural is the lowest part of Urals, with the highest mountain of 994 m (Basegi) and a smooth relief. The relief of Southern Ural is more complex, with numerous valleys and ridges directed south-west and meridionally.[^cite_note-bse-3][^cite_note-brit-4]

## Geology

Urals contains 48 species of economically valuable ores and minerals. Eastern regions are rich in [chalcopyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcopyrite), [nickel oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel_oxide), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite) and [magnetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetite) ores, as well as in coal ([Chelyabinsk Oblast](475230.md)), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [gold](12240.md) and [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum). Western Ural contains deposits of coal, oil, gas and [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium) salts. The speciality of Urals is precious and semi-precious stones, such as [emerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald), [amethyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amethyst), [aquamarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquamarine_\(gemstone\)), [jasper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper), [rhodonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodonite), [malachite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malachite) and [diamond](8082.md).[^cite_note-bse-3]

## Climate

The climate of Ural is continental. The ridges of the Ural Mountains are elongated from north to south, they effectively absorb sunlight thereby increasing the temperature. The areas west to the Ural Mountains are 1–2 °C warmer in winter than the eastern regions because the former are warmed by the Atlantic winds whereas the eastern slopes are chilled by the Siberian air masses. The average January temperatures increase in the western areas from –20 °C in the Polar to –15 °C in the Southern Urals and the corresponding temperatures in July are 10 °C and 20 °C. The western areas also received more rainfall than the eastern ones by 150–300 mm per year. The highest precipitation (1000 mm) is in the Northern Ural that causes the average height of snow up to 90 cm. The eastern parts receive from 500 to 600 mm on the north to 300–400 mm on the south.[^cite_note-bse-3]

## Rivers and lakes



Chusovaya River

Most Ural rivers belong to the basin of the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean). They include [Tobol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobol_River), [Iset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iset_River), [Tura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tura_River), [Pechora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechora_River), [Lozva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozva_River) and [Severnaya Sosva Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severnaya_Sosva_River). The southern rivers – [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River), [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River), [Belaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belaya_River_\(Kama\)) and [Chusovaya Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chusovaya_River) – belong to the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) basin. Western rivers, especially north-western ones, are more affluent. They have high and long (2–3 months) floods in May–June (June–July in the Polar Ural). The south-eastern rivers are much narrower and may dry up in summer. Rivers are fed by melting snow and precipitations. The largest lakes are on the east of the Central and Southern Urals. They include Tawatui, [Argazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argazi_Reservoir), Uvildy and [Turgoyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Turgoyak). The deepest lake (136 m) is Bolshoe Schuchye. Rivers and lakes are used as a water supply, transport routes and for waterlogging. Some rivers have dams forming the [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_Reservoir) and [Votkinsk Reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votkinsk_Reservoir).[^cite_note-bse-3][^cite_note-brit-4]

## Flora

The landscapes of Ural change both in the latitudinal and vertical directions and are dominated by steppes and forests. Steppes lie mostly in the southern and especially south-eastern Urals. Meadow steppes have developed in the lower parts of mountain slopes and are covered with various [clovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clover), [daisies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serratula), [filipendula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipendula), [meadow-grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poa_pratensis) and [foxtail millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxtail_millet), reaching the height of 60–80 cm. Many lands are cultivated. Moving to the south, the meadow steppes become more sparse, dry and low. The steep gravelly slopes of mountains and hills of eastern slopes of the Southern Ural are mostly covered with rocky steppes. Valleys of the rivers contain [willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow), [poplar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus) and [caragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caragana) shrubs.[^cite_note-bse-3]



Aurora in Northern Ural

Forest landscapes of Urals are diverse, especially the southern part. The western areas are dominated by dark coniferous taiga forests which change to mixed and deciduous forests on the south. The eastern mountain slopes have light coniferous taiga forests. Southern Ural is most diverse in the forest composition; here together with coniferous forests also abundant are other tree species such as [larch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larch), [oak](39696.md), [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch), [maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple) and [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm). The Northern Ural is dominated by Siberian species of [fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir), [cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus), [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce) and [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine). Forests are much more sparse in [Polar Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_Ural). Whereas in other Ural Mountains areas they grow up to the heights of 1 km, the forests cease at 250–400 m in the Polar Urals. The polar forests are low and are mixed with [swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp), [lichens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen), [bogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog) and shrubs. Abundant are [dwarf birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betula_nana), [mosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss) and berries ([blueberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blueberry), [cloudberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubus_chamaemorus), [black crowberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empetrum_nigrum), etc.).[^cite_note-bse-3]

## Fauna

Ural forests are inhabited by animals typical of [Siberia](29639.md), such as [elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk), brown [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear), [fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox), [wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf), [wolverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverine), [lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynx), [squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel) and [sable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sable) (north only). In the Central Ural, one can meet a rare mixture of [sable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sable) and pine [marten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marten) named [kidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidus). In the Southern Ural frequent are [badger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_badger) and [black polecat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_polecat). Reptiles and amphibians live mostly in the Southern and Central Ural and are represented by the [common viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_viper), lizards and [grass snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natrix). Bird species are represented by [capercaillie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_capercaillie), [black grouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_grouse), [hazel grouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazel_grouse), [spotted nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_nutcracker) and cuckoos. In the summer, South and Central Urals are visited by songbirds, such as [nightingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightingale) and [common redstart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_redstart).[^cite_note-bse-3][^cite_note-brit-4]

Steppes of the Southern Urals are dominated by [hares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare) and [rodents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodent) such as [gophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopher_\(animal\)), [susliks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermophilus) and [jerboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerboa). There are many birds of prey such as [lesser kestrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_kestrel) and [buzzards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzzard). The animals of the Polar Ural are characteristic of the [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) and include fox, [lemming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemming) and [reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer). The birds of those areas include [rough-legged buzzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough-legged_buzzard), [snowy owl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowy_owl) and [rock ptarmigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_ptarmigan).[^cite_note-bse-3][^cite_note-brit-4]

## Astronomical events

### 2013 Russian meteor event

At around 9\:20 a.m. on Friday, 15 February 2013, an astronomical incident occurred, known as the [2013 Russian meteor event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Russian_meteor_event). A fiery meteor, roughly 55 feet wide, weighing at least 10,000 tons and travelling at a speed of 30,000 miles per hour, burst through Earth's atmosphere over the Ural Mountains. The news media reported that the meteor's deafening shock wave packed the punch of a 500-kiloton explosion, and was so powerful that it shattered windows and collapsed walls, damaging 4,000 buildings and injuring 1,491 people, the majority of whom were caught in the shards of flying glass. Of the people injured, 311 were children who were at school at the time. Small rocky meteorite fragments were discovered near an eight-metre crater, believed to have been created by the impact, in an ice-covered reservoir near the town of Chebarkul. The overall resulting damage from the shock wave was estimated at \$33,000,000.

## History

The first people appeared on the Ural at the end of the Early Paleolithic period (about 75 thousand years ago). Several sites were discovered from the late Paleolithic (35–10 thousand years ago) including the [Kapova Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapova_Cave). In the Neolithic period, tribes have formed which became the basis of the [Uralic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralic_languages) community, and mixed (Mongoloid, europoid) anthropological type. Around the same time, farming and [animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry) develop in the Southern Ural. Manufacture of copper and bronze started in the early 2nd millennium BC and production of iron was developed around the 8th–7th centuries BC.[^cite_note-bse-3]



Dam of the copper smelter in [Miass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miass).

At the beginning of the 2nd millennium, the [primitive society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urgesellschaft) of Ural started transforming to feudal relations. Around the 14th century, the feudal state [Great Perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Perm) was created west to the Northern Ural. Russian started moving to Ural in the 11th century, mostly from the [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod) areas. They became active around the 14th century when a number of settlements were established in the Northern Ural and attributed to [Veliky Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliky_Novgorod). Their ownership went to the Moscow state in 1471, together with lands around the [Kama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River). [Ivan III of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_III_of_Russia) conducted a [series of military campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugra_campaigns) against the principalities of [Yugra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugra), pushing eastward. Following the second campaign in 1483, Yugra was included in the title of the grand prince and the princes of Yugra swore allegiance to Ivan. After the defeat of [Khanate of Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kazan) in 1552, the Russian influence expanded to the most parts of [Bashkortostan](735530.md) and [Udmurtia](447145.md).[^cite_note-bse-3]



Iset ironworks factory near [Yekaterinburg](66524.md), established in 1726 (photo of 1910).

In the 17th century, Ural became inhabited by [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) who brought advanced farming technique and craftsmanship. This changed the lifestyle of the area and quenches the past armed conflicts. Grain-based agriculture, small-scale industries (wood, leather, pottery, blacksmithing, etc.) and a network of markets spread over Ural. The salt industry was developed in [Solikamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solikamsk) by [Stroganov family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroganov_family) salt industrialists who acquired extensive territories. At the same time, the national and social oppression started of the indigenous people of the area, such as [Mansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansi_people), [Khanty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty_people) and [Bashkirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs).[^cite_note-bse-3]



Orenburg Cossacks with camels, 19th century.

In the 17th century rich and high-quality mineral deposits were discovered in the Ural region. First iron and copper smelters were founded by the mid-17th century. The area was recognized by the Russian government as a strategic source of raw materials. More than 60 factories were built in the first half of the 18th century and this number doubled in the 1750–60s. The industrial activity declined in the early 19th century due to the crisis of the feudal system in Russia, and the growth slowed in all areas except for the gold mining. The largest industrial and commercial centers were [Perm](389777.md), [Yekaterinburg](66524.md), [Orenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orenburg), [Ufa](321112.md), [Kungur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kungur) and [Irbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irbit). Irbit hosted the biggest fair of the Urals. In the 1840s, regular commercial navigation started on the Kama River.[^cite_note-bse-3]



[Magnitogorsk Iron and Steel Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk_Iron_and_Steel_Works) in the 1920s–30s.

As a result of the [Emancipation reform of 1861](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_reform_of_1861), most Ural farmers lost more than half of their personal lands. First joint-stock companies appeared on the Ural in the second half of the 19th century, including those with foreign capital. Many old ironworks were reconstructed and a number of new ones were built. The development accelerated not only in the traditional gold and platinum industries, but also in the coal mining and engineering. Mechanical factories were established in Yekaterinburg, Perm, [Izhevsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izhevsk) and others cities, and chemical industry was developed in Berezniki. Nevertheless, Ural lost its status of the main metallurgical area to the South of Russia. The end of 19th century saw a rapid growth of the Ural cities and of the anti-capitalist movements. The long-term exploitation of the factory workers resulted in establishment of Social Democratic committees demanding improvements of working conditions. Those activities peaked around the period of the political collapse of the 1910s in Russia. This brought a severe industrial crisis, fuel shortages, disruption of transport, reduction of agricultural production and the deterioration of the life conditions. The [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) brought numerous benefits to the workers, but was followed by the years of Civil Wars (1917–19). The situation improved only after 1920. In 1920–21, the industrial production was at the 12% level of that in 1913, but by 1925–26 it recovered to 93% and in 1937 reached 700%. New giant plants were raised in [Magnitogorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk) (1932, iron and steel production), Bereznikovskiy (1932, Chemistry), [Sverdlovsk](66524.md) (1933, Heavy Machinery), [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) (1933, tractors), Solikamsk (1934, [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium)), [Krasnokamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnokamsk) (1936, pulp and paper), [Novotagilsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novotagilsk?action=edit\&redlink=1) (metallurgy) and others. In 1929, oil was discovered in the Kama River basin and its production began in 1932 in Bashkortostan.[^cite_note-bse-3]

During [World War II](32927.md) (1941–45) Ural became the industrial backbone of Russia as most important industrial enterprises were evacuated there from the western Russia occupied by Germans. From the summer to winter of 1941, 667 factories were moved to Ural and by the end of 1941 Ural accounted for 62% of the Soviet iron production. The Ural's industrial production increased 3 times in 1943 relatively to 1941 and the military production increased 6 times. During the war, Ural accounted for 40% of the Soviet military production. Three Ural's plants gave 66% of Soviet tanks and mobile artillery units. The annual increase of production was about 50%. In 1946, most plants were converted into non-military directions.[^cite_note-bse-3]

## Ethnic composition

The Northern Urals is home to [Uralic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralic_languages) speaking people like [Udmurts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_people), [Mari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_people), [Komi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi_peoples), [Mansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansi_people) and [Khanty people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty_people). The indigenous population of the Polar Urals consists of the [Nenets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_people) and [Samoyedic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoyedic_peoples) who are widespread through the Siberia and have their own languages. [Bashkirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs) form the largest indigenous group of the region. They live in the Southern Ural and speak a language of the Turkic group. Kazakhs are another significant national fraction of the Southern Ural. Many northern tribals still continue their traditional activities, including fishing, hunting and animal husbandry (reindeer). The nomadic people of the south used to breed horses, but are mostly settled now and are involved in agriculture, especially growth of wheat, potato, melons and watermelons. The majority of the Urals population (about 80%) is however Russians, who are mostly concentrated in the cities of the Central and Southern Urals and are involved in the industry.[^cite_note-brit-4]
