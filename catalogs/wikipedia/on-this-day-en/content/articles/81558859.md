The **Battle of Lake Chad** was waged between 5 and 8 November 2025 on [Lake Chad](59314.md) in [Nigeria](21383.md)'s [Borno State](2227628.md) between rival [jihadist](7826589.md) groups of [Boko Haram](23769768.md) and the [Islamic State – West Africa Province](46529902.md) (ISWAP).[^cite_note-auto1-3] The clashes were described as a "[turf war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turf_war_\(conflict\))".[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Background

Since ISWAP's formal split as a [splinter group](6288753.md) from Boko Haram in 2016,[^cite_note-\:2-6][^cite_note-7]<sup>: 5 </sup> the two groups have been in a territorial intra-jihadist conflict over influence in the Lake Chad region.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:0-9] In years prior to the battle, the conflict in the [Chad Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Basin) was worsening,[^cite_note-10] with ISWAP holding control of the islands within the lake since 2021 as part of its [Camp Holocaust campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Holocaust_campaign).[^cite_note-auto-2][^cite_note-\:3-11] Their hold over these territories occurred around May 2021, when ISWAP attacked the [Sambisa Forest](42612125.md), a stronghold of Boko Haram.[^cite_note-\:2-6]

In mid-2025, numerous military bases and outposts belonging to [Cameroon](5447.md), [Chad](5488.md), [Niger](21373.md), and Nigeria's [Multinational Joint Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Joint_Task_Force) (MNJTF) located within the Chad Basin were described as "soft targets for attacks" by ISWAP. The understaffed and remote positions were vulnerable to the group's shifting tactics towards [nighttime raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_combat) with [light firearms](11966.md) and [drone bombings](38212231.md). Attacks by ISWAP were on the rise.[^cite_note-\:3-11] By September 2025, ISWAP's victories were being challenged by Boko Haram, whose resurgence was attributed to the MNJTF's focus on ISWAP and failures of [disarmament, demobilization, and reintegration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament,_demobilization_and_reintegration) (DDR) programs. Boko Haram's tactics in attacking military positions echoed those of ISWAP.[^cite_note-12]

## Battle

Between 5 and 8 November 2025, Boko Haram fighters launched a naval invasion against [ISWAP](46529902.md) bases across the various islands in Lake Chad. The attacks were led by Hassan Buduma and Mohd Hassan and used dozens of fighters armed with heavy weapons on motorised boats.[^cite_note-auto-2] Military forces reported that "most of the deceased are believed to be ISWAP members,"[^cite_note-\:2-6] with the clashes leaving at least 4 Boko Haram attackers dead and between 170 and 200 ISWAP members killed.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-\:1-13]

Boko Haram successfully seized several ISWAP bases and their weaponry,[^cite_note-14] surviving militants retreated to their mainland positions in the villages of Ali Jillimari, Metele, Kangarwa, and Gudumbali in northern [Borno State](2227628.md), Nigeria.[^cite_note-\:0-9] Only three of ISWAP’s boats managed to escape, seven others were captured alongside their arms and ammunition.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

## Aftermath

In the aftermath of the battle, the [Nigerian Air Force](538869.md) launched a series of airstrikes on ISWAP positions on 9 November, targeting the Lake Chad Basin, as well as several other states in northern Nigeria.[^cite_note-15]
