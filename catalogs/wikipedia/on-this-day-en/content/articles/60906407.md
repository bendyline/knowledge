The **Leagues Cup** is an annual [soccer](10568.md) competition between clubs from [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS), the main soccer league in the United States and Canada, and [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX), the main soccer league in Mexico. It is mainly hosted in Canada and the United States. It began in July 2019 with four teams from both leagues participating. The [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Leagues_Cup) was a [single-elimination tournament](939680.md) hosted in the United States with a final played in [Whitney, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney,_Nevada), near [Las Vegas](47737.md), on September 18, 2019.[^cite_note-ESPN-Marshall-1]

In 2023, the tournament was expanded to include all MLS clubs and all Liga MX clubs, and now functions as a regional cup for [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) between the top division leagues in Mexico and United States and includes MLS teams that are based in Canada. The top three Leagues Cup teams, regardless of nation, qualify for the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup), with the champions receiving a bye to the round of 16. In 2025, the tournament contracted to only include the 18 best MLS clubs from the previous season and all 18 clubs from Liga MX.

## History

Major League Soccer and Liga MX clubs had previously played in the [North American SuperLiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_SuperLiga), which ran from 2007 to 2010. Both leagues also send clubs to the CONCACAF Champions Cup, which has been dominated by Mexican clubs, and the [Campeones Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeones_Cup), a single match played between the winners of the [MLS Cup](483246.md) and the [Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones).[^cite_note-ESPN-Marshall-1] The two leagues began planning a bi-national, eight-team competition to complement the Champions Cup and provide Mexican clubs with matches to replace the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md) in their calendar as soon as 2018.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] MLS and Liga MX announced a new partnership in March 2018 to create the Campeones Cup and explore options for other bi-national competitions between their clubs.[^cite_note-4]

The Leagues Cup tournament was announced on May 29, 2019, featuring eight teams in its inaugural edition to be played during the summer.[^cite_note-Pro-5] The announcement of the tournament was panned by soccer critics in the United States, who called it a meaningless friendly and "cash-grab" for American clubs.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The [MLS Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Players_Association) also expressed concerns over the tournament's creation on the basis of schedule congestion during the summertime.[^cite_note-9] [Sam Boyd Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Boyd_Stadium) in Whitney, Nevada was later announced as the host venue for the final and a broadcasting contract for the tournament was awarded to [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) and [TUDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUDN) (formerly [Univision Deportes Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision_Deportes_Network)).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] This event was also televised on [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) and [TVA Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVA_Sports) in Canada and [Televisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisa) in Mexico.[^cite_note-12]

In July 2019, MLS and Liga MX announced that the second edition of the Leagues Cup in 2020 would feature 16 teams—eight from each league. The MLS participants would be drawn from the top four teams in each conference that do not qualify for the [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League); the Liga MX participants would include the 2019 Apertura champion, 2020 Clausura champion, the [2019–20 Copa MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Copa_MX) champion, and the next five best-placed teams in the 2019–20 season aggregate table the league.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The tournament was canceled on May 19, 2020, amid the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-MLS-2020-15] The eight-team format debuted in the [2021 Leagues Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Leagues_Cup), which was played in August and September.[^cite_note-16] In the final at [Allegiant Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegiant_Stadium) in Paradise, Nevada, Mexican club [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Le%C3%B3n) defeated [Seattle Sounders FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC), the first American finalist in the competition's history.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

On April 14, 2022, MLS and Liga MX announced the [2022 Leagues Cup Showcase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Leagues_Cup_Showcase), which was held starting August 3, 2022, at [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in [Inglewood, California](107640.md). The event included a doubleheader of matches: [LA Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Galaxy) against [C.D. Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Guadalajara) and [Los Angeles FC](44255607.md) against [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica).[^cite_note-19] On June 30, 2022, it was announced that the Leagues Cup Showcase was expanded to include three more matches—[FC Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Cincinnati) against C.D. Guadalajara at [TQL Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TQL_Stadium) in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati), [Ohio](22199.md); [Nashville SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_SC) against Club América at [Geodis Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodis_Park) in [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md), on September 21; and [Real Salt Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Salt_Lake) against [Atlas F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_F.C.) at [Rio Tinto Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_Stadium) in [Sandy, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy,_Utah), on September 22.[^cite_note-20] The events served as a one-time replacement of the previously planned 2022 Leagues Cup which was not held due to fixture congestion from the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) and other factors.[^cite_note-LC22-21]

The Leagues Cup was expanded in 2023 to include all MLS and Liga MX clubs, during a month-long pause in their respective seasons. It also became a qualification tournament for the [2024 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) with three berths for teams from [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Football_Union).[^cite_note-22] The Leagues Cup champion qualified directly to the round of 16, while the runner-up and third-place finisher earned round one berths.[^cite_note-23] [Inter Miami CF](41853326.md) won the first edition of the expanded tournament in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Leagues_Cup), led by top goalscorer [Lionel Messi](2150841.md).[^cite_note-24]

On January 28, 2025, Major League Soccer announced that they would only send 18 representatives to the Leagues Cup for 2025 as part of their new competition guidelines, which only allowed teams to play in at most two cup competitions. Most of the teams in the [2024 MLS Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_MLS_Cup_playoffs) qualified for Leagues Cup; the [Vancouver Whitecaps](22060729.md), as winners of the [2024 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Canadian_Championship), had their place forfeited and given to expansion club [San Diego FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_FC).[^cite_note-MLS25-25]

### Criticism

The addition of the Leagues Cup and subsequent schedule congestion led Major League Soccer to announce their intention not to field senior teams in the [U.S. Open Cup](253156.md), the domestic cup competition for the United States. The announcement was met with "widespread anger and condemnation" and the proposal was rejected by the [United States Soccer Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation). A hybrid plan with eight MLS participants and [MLS Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Next_Pro) reserve teams as replacements for the remaining teams was used for the [2024 U.S. Open Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_U.S._Open_Cup).[^cite_note-26] Several Major League Soccer [supporters' groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporters'_group) have announced boycotts of the Leagues Cup.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Format

The first two editions of Leagues Cup featured four clubs from each league in an eight-team [single-elimination knockout tournament](939680.md), with the first two rounds hosted by the MLS club. The finals were played at neutral venues in the [Las Vegas metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley).[^cite_note-30] The participating MLS teams in the [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Leagues_Cup) were invitees, but the [second edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Leagues_Cup) used league results for qualification; the four Liga MX participants were chosen based on their league results in both of these editions.[^cite_note-Pro-5]

For the 2022 season, an official tournament was not held due to fixture congestion from the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), among other factors.&#x20;

For the [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Leagues_Cup) and [2024 editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Leagues_Cup), the Leagues Cup included all MLS and Liga MX teams—47 teams in total with 77 matches hosted in Canada and the United States. The top 15 teams from each league were seeded into 15 groups based on their league standings from the previous season, while the remaining teams were drawn based on geographic proximity. The group stage had three matches in a [round-robin](939742.md) format and the top two teams qualified for the knockout stage. Two teams receive byes to the knockout stage: the reigning [MLS Cup](483246.md) champion and highest-ranked Liga MX champion from either the previous [Apertura and Clausura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apertura_and_Clausura). The knockout stage was single-elimination on a fixed bracket.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

The [2025 Leagues Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Leagues_Cup) format was altered to maximize the number of inter-league matches. This year included 18 out of the 30 MLS teams (those who qualified for the [MLS Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_playoffs) in most instances), as well as all 18 Liga MX teams, for a total of 36 participants. Each team plays three games against teams from the other league and the top four teams from each league advance to the knockout stage. The quarterfinal matches are guaranteed to be MLS vs Liga MX like in the group stage.[^cite_note-33] All games are again held in Canada and the United States, but unlike the past two years, the competition also takes place during the MLS and Liga MX seasons.[^cite_note-34]

Since 2023, the tournament uses a unique points-scoring system in which winning teams earn three points, while draws go straight to penalties, with the shootout-winning team earning two points, and the shootout runner-up earning one.

## Trophy

The Leagues Cup trophy was unveiled in September 2019 and consists of a 22-pound (10.0 kg) silver bowl atop a pedestal. It is 16.5 inches (42 cm) in height and 16.1 inches (41 cm) wide. A replica trophy will be gifted to the winners following 12 months with the original trophy.[^cite_note-35]

## Broadcasting

Since 2023, all Leagues Cup matches have been broadcast worldwide on [MLS Season Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Season_Pass), an online streaming platform operated by [Apple](856.md) under its [Apple TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV_\(streaming_service\)) streaming service. All matches have commentary in English and Spanish, while those involving Canadian teams also include French commentary.[^cite_note-36] A select group of matches are also set to be broadcast on television networks using their own crews, including [Fox Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_\(United_States\)) and [TUDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUDN) in the United States; and [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) and [RDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_des_sports) in Canada.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]
