**North Rhine-Westphalia** or **North-Rhine/Westphalia** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Nordrhein-Westfalen* [\[ˌnɔɐtʁaɪn vɛstˈfaːlən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/ab/De-Nordrhein-Westfalen2.ogg/De-Nordrhein-Westfalen2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/ab/De-Nordrhein-Westfalen2.ogg/De-Nordrhein-Westfalen2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>),[^cite_note-5] commonly shortened to **NRW**,[^cite_note-6] is a landlocked [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) (*Land*) in [Western Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_states_of_Germany). With more than 18 million inhabitants, it is the [most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_population) state in Germany. Apart from the city-states ([Berlin](3354.md), [Hamburg](13467.md) and [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\))), it is also the [most densely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_population_density) populated state in Germany. Covering an area of 34,084 km<sup>2</sup> (13,160 sq mi), it is the [fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_area) German state by size.

North Rhine-Westphalia features 30 of the 81 German municipalities with over 100,000 inhabitants, including [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) (over 1 million), the state capital [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) (630,000), [Dortmund](149349.md) and [Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen) (about 590,000 inhabitants each) and other cities predominantly located in the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr) [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), the largest urban area in Germany and the fourth-largest on the [European continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_continent). The location of the Rhine-Ruhr at the heart of the European [Blue Banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Banana) makes it well connected to other major European cities and metropolitan areas like the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad), the [Flemish Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_Diamond) and the [Frankfurt Rhine-Main Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhine-Main_Region).

North Rhine-Westphalia was established in 1946 after [World War II](32927.md) from the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) provinces of [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Westphalia) and the northern part of [Rhine Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Province) ([North Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Rhine)), and the [Free State of Lippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Lippe) by the [British military administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_zone_in_Germany) in [Allied-occupied Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany) and became a state of the [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in 1949. The city of [Bonn](3295.md) served as the federal capital until the [reunification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Germany) in 1990 and as the [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government) until 1999.

Culturally, North Rhine-Westphalia is not a uniform area; there are significant differences, especially in traditional customs, between the Rhineland region on the one hand and the regions of Westphalia and Lippe on the other. [Its economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia) is the largest among [German states by GRDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_GRDP), but the state ranks below the national average [in GRDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_GRDP_per_capita).

## History

Main article: [History of North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia)

### Creation

The state of North Rhine-Westphalia was established by the [British military administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany)'s "Operation Marriage" on 23 August 1946 by merging the [province of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Westphalia) and the northern parts of the [Rhine Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Province), both being political divisions of the former [state of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Prussia) within the [German Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Reich).[^cite_note-HistoryNRW-7][^cite_note-DWNRW-8] On 21 January 1947, the former state of [Lippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippe_\(state\)) was merged with North Rhine-Westphalia.[^cite_note-HistoryNRW-7] The [constitution of North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia) was then ratified through a [referendum](51582.md).

### Rhineland

Main article: [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland)

The first written account of the area was by its conqueror, [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar), the territories west of the Rhine were occupied by the [Eburones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eburones) and east of the Rhine he reported the [Ubii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubii) (across from Cologne) and the [Sugambri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugambri) to their north. The Ubii and some other Germanic tribes such as the [Cugerni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cugerni) were later settled on the west side of the Rhine in the Roman province of [Germania Inferior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_Inferior). Julius Caesar conquered the tribes on the left bank, and Augustus established numerous fortified posts on the Rhine, but the Romans never succeeded in gaining a firm footing on the right bank, where the Sugambri neighboured several other tribes including the [Tencteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencteri) and [Usipetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usipetes). North of the Sigambri and the Rhine region were the [Bructeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bructeri).

As the power of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) declined, many of these tribes came to be seen collectively as [Ripuarian Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripuarian_Franks) and they pushed forward along both banks of the Rhine, and by the end of the fifth century had conquered all the lands that had formerly been under Roman influence. By the eighth century, the Frankish dominion was firmly established in western Germany and northern Gaul, but at the same time, to the north, Westphalia was being taken over by [Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxons) pushing south.

The [Merovingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingians) and [Carolingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingians) Franks eventually built an empire which controlled first their Ripuarian kin, and then the Saxons. On the division of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire) at the [Treaty of Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Verdun), the part of the province to the east of the river fell to [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia), while that to the west remained with the kingdom of [Lotharingia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotharingia).[^cite_note-eb1911-9]

By the time of [Otto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_I) (d. 973), both banks of the Rhine had become part of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), and the Rhenish territory was divided between the duchies of [Upper Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Lorraine) on the [Moselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moselle) and [Lower Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Lorraine) on the Meuse. The [Ottonian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottonian_dynasty) had both Saxon and Frankish ancestry.



Map of the [Lower Rhenish–Westphalian Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Rhenish%E2%80%93Westphalian_Circle) in 1799 by [John Cary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cary)

As the central power of the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) weakened, the Rhineland split into numerous small, independent, separate vicissitudes and special chronicles. The old Lotharingian divisions became obsolete, although the name survives for example in [Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine_\(region\)) in France, and throughout the Middle Ages and even into modern times, the nobility of these areas often sought to preserve the idea of a preeminent [duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke) within Lotharingia, something claimed by the [Dukes of Limburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Limburg), and the [Dukes of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant). Such struggles as the [War of the Limburg Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Limburg_Succession) therefore continued to create military and political links between what is now Rhineland-Westphalia and neighbouring [Belgium](3343.md) and the [Netherlands](21148.md).

In spite of its dismembered condition and the sufferings it underwent at the hands of its French neighbours in various periods of warfare, the Rhenish territory prospered greatly and stood in the foremost rank of German culture and progress. [Aachen](1520.md) was the place of coronation of the German emperors, and the ecclesiastical principalities of the Rhine bulked largely in German history.[^cite_note-eb1911-9]

Prussia first set foot on the Rhine in 1609 by the occupation of the [Duchy of Cleves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Cleves) and about a century later [Upper Guelders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Guelders) and [Moers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moers) also became Prussian. At the [Peace of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Basel) in 1795, the whole of the [left bank of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_bank_of_the_Rhine) was resigned to France, and in 1806, the Rhenish princes all joined the [Confederation of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Rhine).

After the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), Prussia was awarded the entire [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland), which included the [Grand Duchy of Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Berg), the ecclesiastic electorates of [Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Trier) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Cologne), the free cities of [Aachen](1520.md) and Cologne, and nearly a hundred small lordships and abbeys. The Prussian [Rhine province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_province) was formed in 1822 and Prussia had the tact to leave the lower Rhenish districts in undisturbed possession of the liberal institutions to which they had become accustomed under the republican rule of the French.[^cite_note-eb1911-9] In 1920, the districts of [Eupen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eupen) and [Malmedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malmedy) were transferred to Belgium (see [German-speaking Community of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-speaking_Community_of_Belgium)).

### Westphalia

Main article: [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia)

Around AD 1, numerous incursions occurred through Westphalia and perhaps even some permanent [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) or Romanized settlements. The [Battle of Teutoburg Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Teutoburg_Forest) took place near Osnabrück and some of the [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_tribes) who fought at this battle came from the area of Westphalia. [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) is thought to have spent considerable time in [Paderborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn) and nearby parts. His [Saxon Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Wars) also partly took place in what is thought of as Westphalia today. Popular legends link his adversary [Widukind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widukind) to places near [Detmold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detmold), [Bielefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld), [Lemgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemgo), Osnabrück, and other places in Westphalia. Widukind was buried in [Enger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enger), which is also a subject of a legend.

Along with [Angria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angria), [Eastphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastphalia), and [Nordalbingia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordalbingia), Westphalia (*Westfalahi*) was originally a district of the [Duchy of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony). In 1180, Westphalia was elevated to the rank of a duchy by Emperor [Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). The [Duchy of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Westphalia) comprised only a small area south of the [Lippe River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippe_River).



Ratification of the [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia) of 1648 in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnster) by Gerard Terborch

Parts of Westphalia came under [Brandenburg-Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia) control during the 17th and 18th centuries, but most of it remained divided duchies and other feudal areas of power. The [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia) of 1648, signed in Münster and Osnabrück, ended the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War). The concept of nation-state sovereignty resulting from the treaty became known as "[Westphalian sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalian_sovereignty)".

As a result of the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), there is no dominant religion in Westphalia. Catholicism and [Lutheranism](23371382.md) are on relatively equal footing. Lutheranism is strong in the eastern and northern parts with numerous free churches. [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnster) and especially [Paderborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn) are thought of as Catholic. Osnabrück is divided almost equally between Catholicism and Protestantism.

After the defeat of the [Prussian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Army) at the [Battle of Jena-Auerstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jena-Auerstedt), the [Treaty of Tilsit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_Tilsit) in 1807 made the Westphalian territories part of the [Kingdom of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Westphalia) from 1807 to 1813. It was founded by [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I_of_France) and was a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state). This state only shared the name with the historical region; it contained only a relatively small part of Westphalia, consisting instead mostly of [Hessian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) and [Eastphalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastphalia) regions.

After the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) received a large amount of territory in the Westphalian region and created the [province of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Westphalia) in 1815. The northernmost portions of the former kingdom, including the town of [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck), had become part of the states of [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hanover) and [Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Oldenburg).

## Flags and coat of arms

The [flag of North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia) is green-white-red with the combined coats of arms of the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland) (white line before green background, symbolizing the river Rhine), [Westfalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalen) (the white horse) and [Lippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippe_\(district\)) (the red rose). After the establishment of North Rhine-Westphalia in 1946, the tricolor was first introduced in 1948, but was not formally adopted until 1953.[^cite_note-flag1953-10] The plain variant of the tricolor is considered the [civil flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_flag) and state ensign, while government authorities use the [state flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_flag) (*Landesdienstflagge*) which is defaced with the [state's coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia).[^cite_note-flag1953-10] The state ensign can easily be mistaken for a distressed [flag of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Hungary), as well as the former national [flag of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Iran) (1964–1980). The same flag was used by the [Rhenish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenish_Republic) (1923–1924) as a symbol of independence and freedom.

The horse on the coat of arms is a reference to the [Saxon Steed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Steed), a heraldic motif associated with both Westphalia and [Lower Saxony](18435.md). The horse first featured in the 15th century coat of arms of the Duchy of Westphalia, before being inherited by the Prussian province of Westphalia and finally the modern state of North Rhine-Westphalia.[^cite_note-11]

## Geography



Geographic map of North Rhine-Westphalia

North Rhine-Westphalia encompasses the plains of the [Lower Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Rhine) region and parts of the [Central Uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Uplands) (*[die Mittelgebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Mittelgebirge)*) up to the gorge of [Porta Westfalica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Westfalica_\(gorge\)). The state covers an area of 34,083 km<sup>2</sup> (13,160 sq mi) and shares borders with [Belgium](3343.md) ([Wallonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallonia)) in the southwest and the [Netherlands](21148.md) ([Limburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg_\(Netherlands\)), [Gelderland](57160.md) and [Overijssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overijssel)) in the west and northwest. It has borders with the German states of [Lower Saxony](18435.md) to the north and northeast, [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md) to the south and [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) to the southeast.

Approximately half of the state is located in the relative low-lying terrain of the [Westphalian Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalian_Lowland) and the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland), both extending broadly into the [North German Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_German_Plain). A few isolated hill ranges are located within these lowlands, among them the Hohe Mark, the [Beckum Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckum_Hills), the [Baumberge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baumberge) and the [Stemmer Berge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stemweder_Berg). The terrain rises towards the south and in the east of the state into parts of Germany's [Central Uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Uplands). These hill ranges are the [Weser Uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weser_Uplands) – including the [Egge Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egge_Hills), the [Wiehen Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiehen_Hills), the [Wesergebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesergebirge) and the [Teutoburg Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutoburg_Forest) in the east, the [Sauerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerland), the [Bergisches Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergisches_Land), the [Siegerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegerland) and the [Siebengebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebengebirge) in the south, as well as the left-Rhenish [Eifel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eifel) in the southwest of the state. The [Rothaargebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothaargebirge) in the border region with [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) rises to height of about 800 m above sea level. The highest of these mountains are the [Langenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langenberg_\(Rothaargebirge\)), at 843.2 m above sea level, the [Kahler Asten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahler_Asten) (840.7 m) and the Clemensberg (839.2 m).



The highest peaks in North Rhine-Westphalia are located in the Rothaar Mountains.

The planimetrically determined centre of North Rhine-Westphalia is located in the south of Dortmund-[Aplerbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aplerbeck) in the Aplerbecker Mark (51° 28' N, 7° 33' Ö). Its westernmost point is situated near [Selfkant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selfkant) close to the Dutch border, the easternmost near [Höxter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6xter) on the [Weser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weser). The southernmost point lies near [Hellenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenthal) in the [Eifel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eifel) region. The northernmost point is the NRW-Nordpunkt near [Rahden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahden) in the northeast of the state. The Nordpunkt has located the only 100 km to the south of the North Sea coast. The deepest natural dip is arranged in the district Zyfflich in the city of [Kranenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranenburg,_North_Rhine-Westphalia) with 9.2 m above sea level in the northwest of the state. Though, the deepest point overground results from mining. The open-pit Hambach reaches at [Niederzier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederzier) a deep of 293 m below sea level. At the same time, this is the deepest human-made dip in Germany.

The most important rivers flowing at least partially through North Rhine-Westphalia include: the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_\(river\)), the [Ems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ems_\(river\)), the [Lippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippe_River), and the [Weser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weser). The Rhine is by far the most important river in North Rhine-Westphalia: it enters the state as [Middle Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Rhine) near Bad Honnef, where still being part of the [Mittelrhein wine region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittelrhein_\(wine_region\)). It changes into the [Lower Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Rhine) near Bad Godesberg and leaves North Rhine-Westphalia near Emmerich at a width of 730 metres. Almost immediately after entering the Netherlands, the Rhine splits into many branches.

The [Pader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pader_\(river\)), which flows entirely within the city of [Paderborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn), is considered Germany's shortest river.

For many, North Rhine-Westphalia is synonymous with industrial areas and urban agglomerations. However, the largest part of the state is used for agriculture (almost 52%) and forests (25%).[^cite_note-12]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia)



[Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia) in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf)

The politics of North Rhine-Westphalia takes place within a framework of a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic) [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democratic) republic. The two main parties are, as on the federal level, the centre-right [Christian Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_\(Germany\)) and the centre-left [Social Democratic Party](60585.md). From 1966 to 2005, North Rhine-Westphalia was continuously governed by the Social Democrats or SPD-led governments.

The state's legislative body is the [Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia) ("state parliament house").[^cite_note-NRWPolitcs-21] It may pass laws within the competency of the state, e.g. cultural matters, the education system, matters of internal security, i.e. the police, building supervision, health supervision and the media; as opposed to matters that are reserved to Federal law.[^cite_note-NRWPolitcs-21]

North Rhine-Westphalia uses the same electoral system as the Federal level in Germany: "*[Personalized proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_member_proportional_representation)*". Every five years the citizens of North Rhine-Westphalia vote in a general election to elect at least 181 members of the Landtag. Only parties who win at least 5% of the votes cast may be represented in parliament.[^cite_note-NRWPolitcs-21]

The Landtag, the parliamentary parties and groups consisting of at least 7 members of parliament have the right to table legal proposals to the Landtag for deliberation.[^cite_note-NRWPolitcs-21] The law that is passed by the Landtag is delivered to the Minister-President, who, together with the ministers involved, is required to sign it and announce it in the Law and Ordinance Gazette.[^cite_note-NRWPolitcs-21]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_North_Rhine-Westphalia)



[ThyssenKrupp](944221.md) headquarters in [Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen)

NRW has consistently been Germany's economic powerhouse, with the largest economy among the [German states by GDP figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_GRDP).[^cite_note-30] In 2024, NRW was the first state to exceed a nominal GDP of [US\$](18717338.md)1.0 trillion.[^cite_note-destatis-3]

In the post-World War II recovery, the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) region was one of the most important industrial regions in [Western Europe](33800.md), and contributed to the German [Wirtschaftswunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftswunder). In 1958, a decade long [Kohlekrise](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohlekrise) began: the amount of hard coal mined exceeded demand. One reason was the low [oil price](5137675.md). Around 1967, also a [steel crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_crisis) began. Until then, NRW had been known as *Land von Kohle und Stahl* (*Land of Coal and Steel*).

Producing sectors, particularly in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) and metal and [iron working](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_working) industry, experienced substantial growth. Despite this [structural change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_change) and an economic growth which was under [national average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_West_Germany), the 2024 GDP of €872 billion (1/5 of the total German GDP) made NRW the economically strongest state of Germany by GDP figures, as well as one of the most important economical areas in the world.[^cite_note-destatis-3] On a per capita base, however, Rhine-Westphalia remained (as of 2007) one of the weaker among the [Western German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) states.[^cite_note-31] Of Germany’s top 100 corporations, 37 are based in North Rhine-Westphalia.

NRW has attracted companies from both Germany and abroad. In 2009, NRW had the most foreign direct investments ([FDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment)) anywhere in Germany.[^cite_note-32]

At the end of 2018, the last coal mine in NRW closed ([de\:Bergwerk Prosper-Haniel](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergwerk%20Prosper-Haniel)). There have been many changes in NRW's economy. I.e., employment in the creative industries has grown.[^cite_note-CultureNRW-27] [Industrial heritage sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_heritage) are now workplaces for designers, artists and the advertising industry.[^cite_note-CultureNRW-27][^cite_note-33] The [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) region has – since the 1960s – undergone a significant structural change away from coal mining and steel industry. Many rural parts of Eastern [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia), [Bergisches Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergisches_Land) and the [Lower Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Rhine_region_\(Germany\)) ground their economy on "[Hidden Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Champions)" in various [sectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sector).

As of June 2014, the unemployment rate was 8.2%, second highest among all western German states.[^cite_note-34] In October 2018 the unemployment rate stood at 6.4%, higher than the national average.[^cite_note-35]

## Transport

With its central location in the [most important European economic area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Banana), high population density, strong urbanization and numerous business locations, North Rhine-Westphalia has one of the densest transport networks in the world.

### Regional rail network



Rail transport infrastructure map of North Rhine-Westphalia



Stadtbahn in [Dortmund](149349.md)

The regional rail network is organised around the big cities on the Rhine and Ruhr such as [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) and [Dortmund](149349.md). The public transport companies in the Ruhr area and Düsseldorf are run under the umbrella of the [Verkehrsverbund Rhein-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund_Rhein-Ruhr), which provides a uniform ticket system valid for the area. The region of Cologne and Bonn is run under the [Verkehrsverbund Rhein-Sieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund_Rhein-Sieg). There is also a ticketing system called *NRW-Tarif* which offers tickets between all the regions of North Rhine-Westphalia. The state is well-integrated into the national rail system, the [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn), for both passenger and goods services, each city in the region has at least one or more train stations. The bigger central stations have frequent direct connections to most bigger German cities and European cities such as [Amsterdam](844.md), [Brussels](3708.md), [Paris](22989.md) and [Basel](4911.md). Some direct trains run to [Vienna](55866.md) and [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich).

The Rhein-Ruhr area also contains some of the longest [tram](30733.md) system in the world, with tram and [Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtbahn) services from [Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witten) to [Krefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld) in the VRR zone and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) to [Bad Honnef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Honnef) and [Siegburg](628297.md) via [Bonn](3295.md) within the VRS zone. Besides the local public transportation there is an interconnected commuter rail network, with the [Rhine-Ruhr S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr_S-Bahn) network serving the Ruhr area as well as Düsseldorf and the [S-Bahn Köln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn_K%C3%B6ln) serving the area around Cologne.

### Road



Autobahn A40 in Essen

North Rhine-Westphalia has the densest network of [Autobahns in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Autobahnen) and similar Schnellstraßen (expressways). The Autobahn network is built in a grid network, with six east–west ([A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_2), [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_4), [A40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_40), [A42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_42), [A44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_44), [A46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_46)) and eight north–south ([A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_1), [A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_3), [A43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_43), [A45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_45), [A52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_52), [A57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_57), [A59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_59), [A61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_61)) routes. The A1, A2, A3, A4 and A61 are mostly used by through traffic, while the other autobahns have a more regional function.

Both the A44 and the A52 have several missing links, in various stages of planning. Some missing sections are currently in construction or planned to be constructed in the near future.

Additional expressways serve as bypasses and local routes, especially around Dortmund and Bochum. Due to the density of the autobahns and expressways, [Bundesstraßen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesstra%C3%9Fe) are less important for intercity traffic. The first Autobahns in the Region opened during the mid-1930s. Due to the density of the network, and the number of alternative routes, traffic volumes are generally lower than other major metropolitan areas in Europe. Traffic congestion is an everyday occurrence, but far less so than in the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad) in the Netherlands, another polycentric urban area. Most important Autobahns have six lanes.

### Airports

The region benefits from the presence of several airport infrastructure. The main airport is [Düsseldorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport), world class, which hosted 25.5 million passengers in 2019 and offers flights to various international destinations. Düsseldorf is the fourth-largest airport in Germany after [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport), [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Brandenburg_Airport) and serves as a [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) for [Eurowings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurowings) and as focus city for several more airlines. The airport has three passenger terminals and two runways and can handle wide-body aircraft up to the [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380).[^cite_note-a380ready-40]

The second airport is [Cologne Bonn Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Bonn_Airport) serving [Germany](11867.md)'s fourth-largest city [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), and also serves [Bonn](3295.md), former capital of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). With around 10 million passengers passing through it in 2023, it is the sixth-largest passenger airport in Germany and the third-largest in terms of cargo operations. It is also a hub for Eurowings, but also for some cargo airlines. By traffic units, which combines cargo and passengers, the airport is in fifth position in Germany.[^cite_note-41] As of March 2015, Cologne Bonn Airport had services to 115 passenger destinations in 35 countries.[^cite_note-42] It is named after [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer), a Cologne native and the first post-war Chancellor of West Germany.

Third airport in the region, [Dortmund Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Airport) is a minor [international airport](1519705.md) located 10 km (6.2 mi) east of [Dortmund](149349.md). It serves the eastern [Rhine-Ruhr area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr), the largest [urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration) in [Germany](11867.md), and is mainly used for low-cost and leisure charter flights. In 2019 the airport served 2,719,563 passengers.

There are three more minor airports within the state: [Münster Osnabrück Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnster_Osnabr%C3%BCck_Airport), [Airport Weeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_Weeze) and [Paderborn Lippstadt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn_Lippstadt_Airport).

### Waterways

The [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) flows through North Rhine-Westphalia. Its banks are usually heavily populated and industrialized, in particular the [agglomerations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agglomerations) [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) and [Ruhr area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_area). Here the Rhine flows through the largest conurbation in Germany, the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr) region. [Duisburg Inner Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duisburg_Inner_Harbour) (Duisport) and [Dortmund Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund_Port) are large industrial inland ports and serve as hubs along the Rhine and the German inland water transport system. The country is crossed by many canals like [Rhine–Herne Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Herne_Canal) (RHK), der [Wesel-Datteln-Kanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesel-Datteln-Kanal) (WDK), der [Datteln-Hamm-Kanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datteln-Hamm-Kanal) (DHK) and [Dortmund-Ems-Kanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund-Ems-Kanal) (DEK) an important role for inland navigation.

## Education

[University of Duisburg-Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Duisburg-Essen) (Universität Duisburg-Essen), is also well known and is one of the largest universities in Germany.

Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3._Liga) including [Arminia Bielefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminia_Bielefeld), [MSV Duisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSV_Duisburg), [Rot-Weiß Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rot-Wei%C3%9F_Essen), [Preußen Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preu%C3%9Fen_M%C3%BCnster), [Rot-Weiß Oberhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rot-Wei%C3%9F_Oberhausen), [FC Viktoria Köln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Viktoria_K%C3%B6ln) and [SC Verl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Verl). Since the formal establishment of the German Bundesliga in 1963, [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) and [Borussia Mönchengladbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_M%C3%B6nchengladbach) have been the most successful teams in the state, with both winning five titles. [1. FC Köln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_K%C3%B6ln) won two titles, including the first in 1963. Before the league's establishment, North Rhine-Westfalian teams competed for the title of Deutscher Fußballmeister (German Football Champion). Here, [FC Schalke 04](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Schalke_04) brought home seven titles, while Dortmund and Köln won an additional three and one title(s), respectively. [Fortuna Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortuna_D%C3%BCsseldorf) and [Rot-Weiß Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rot-Wei%C3%9F_Essen) have each been German Champion once. North Rhine-Westphalia has been a very successful footballing state having a combined total of 25 championships, fewer only than [Bavaria](3764.md).

### Basketball

The state is also home to several professional basketball teams that currently either compete in the [Basketball Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_Bundesliga) or [ProA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2._Basketball_Bundesliga) or have competed there in the past. These teams include [Telekom Baskets Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telekom_Baskets_Bonn), [Bayer Giants Leverkusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_Giants_Leverkusen), [Paderborn Baskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn_Baskets), [Uni Baskets Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni_Baskets_M%C3%BCnster), [VfL SparkassenStars Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_SparkassenStars_Bochum), [Phoenix Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Hagen) and [Alemannia Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannia_Aachen).

### Ice hockey

North Rhine-Westphalia is home to current and former [DEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Eishockey_Liga) teams [Düsseldorfer EG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorfer_EG), [Kölner Haie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6lner_Haie), [Krefeld Pinguine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld_Pinguine), and [Iserlohn Roosters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iserlohn_Roosters).
