**Dame Sandra Prunella Mason** [FB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Freedom_of_Barbados) [GCMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George) [DA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_or_Dame_of_St_Andrew) [SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Counsel) (born 17 January 1949) is a Barbadian politician, lawyer, and diplomat who served as the first [president of Barbados](68909900.md) from 2021 to 2025. She was previously the eighth and final [governor-general of Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-general_of_Barbados) from 2018 to 2021, the second woman to hold the office. On 20 October 2021, Mason was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Barbadian_presidential_election) by the [Parliament of Barbados](2555282.md) to become the country's first president, and took office on 30 November 2021, when Barbados ceased to be a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Barbados) and [became a republic](14456864.md).&#x20;

Mason was a practising [attorney-at-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-at-law) who has served as a High Court [judge](44719.md) in [Saint Lucia](27208.md) and a Court of Appeal judge in Barbados, and was the first woman admitted to the [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(law\)) in Barbados. She served as chair of the [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM) commission to evaluate regional integration, was the first magistrate appointed an [ambassador](68651.md) from Barbados, and was the first woman to serve on the country's [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados_Supreme_Court). She was the first appointee from Barbados to the Commonwealth Secretariat Arbitral Tribunal. In 2017, she was appointed the 8th governor-general of Barbados, with a term beginning on 8 January 2018. Simultaneously with her appointment, Mason was awarded the Dame Grand Cross in the [Order of Saint Michael and Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Michael_and_Saint_George). On assumption of the office of Governor-General, she became the Chancellor of the [Order of National Heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_National_Heroes), [Order of Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Barbados) and the [Order of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Freedom_of_Barbados).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]&#x20;

## Early life and education

Sandra Prunella Mason was born on 17 January 1949[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] in [Saint Philip, Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Philip,_Barbados).[^cite_note-(CSAT)-4] After studying at St. Catherine's Primary School until age nine, she attended secondary school at [Queen's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_College_\(Barbados\)),[^cite_note-BarbadosGov-5] then began teaching at the Princess Margaret Secondary School in 1968.[^cite_note-6] The following year, she worked at [Barclays Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays_Bank) as a clerk. Mason enrolled in the [University of the West Indies at Cave Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_West_Indies_at_Cave_Hill), where she earned a [Bachelor of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws).[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] Mason was one of the first graduates of the Faculty of Law from UWI, Cave Hill, completing her education in 1973.&#x20;

In 1975, she obtained a [Legal Education Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_Education_Certificate) from [Hugh Wooding Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Wooding_Law_School) in [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), becoming the first woman attorney-at-law from Barbados to graduate from the school.[^cite_note-BarbadosGov-5] She was [admitted to the bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admitted_to_the_bar) on 10 November the same year,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrathwaite1999287-7] becoming the first woman member of the Barbados Bar Association.[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] She is a [Soroptimist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroptimist_International) and Patron of SI Barbados.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

## Early career and legal practice

Beginning in 1975, she worked in Trust Administration for Barclay's and transferred to several different posts within the Barclay's company until 1977.&#x20;

In 1978, Mason began working as the Magistrate of the Juvenile and Family Court and simultaneously tutoring in family law at UWI. She stopped tutoring in 1983 and continued working as a magistrate. In 1988, Mason completed the [Royal Institute of Public Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Public_Administration) in [London](17867.md)'s course on Judicial Administration.[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] She served on the UN [Committee on the Rights of the Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_the_Rights_of_the_Child) from its 1991 inception until 1999, holding the vice chair from 1993 to 1995 and chair from 1997 to 1999.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEricksonCohenHart2001231-9]&#x20;

Between 1991 and 1992, Mason served as chair[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] and was one of the two women appointed to the 13-member [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM) commission charged with evaluating regional integration.[^cite_note-(CSAT)-4] She left the family court in 1992[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] to serve as an ambassador to Venezuela, and was the first woman magistrate from Barbados to serve in that position. Between 1993 and 1994 she also served as ambassador to [Chile](5489.md), [Colombia](5222.md) and [Brazil](3383.md).[^cite_note-BarbadosGov-5] Upon her return to Barbados[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrathwaite1999287-7] in 1994, Mason was appointed Chief Magistrate for Barbados, and in 1997 became the Registrar of the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Barbados).[^cite_note-appellate_judge-10]&#x20;

In 2000, Mason completed studies on Alternative Dispute Resolution at the [University of Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Windsor_Faculty_of_Law) in [Windsor, Ontario](33596.md), Canada, and then completed a Fellowship with the [Commonwealth Judicial Education Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Commonwealth_Judicial_Education_Institute\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Halifax, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia), in 2001, as well as a course in Advanced Dispute Resolution at UWI.[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] She continued to serve as Registrar of the Supreme Court until 2005, when she was appointed as Queen's Counsel to the Inner Bar of Barbados.[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] In 2008, Mason was sworn in as an Appeals Judge[^cite_note-appellate_judge-10] becoming the first woman to serve on the Barbados Court of Appeals.[^cite_note-Caribbean_Elections_bio-3] For three days in 2012, she became the acting Governor-General of Barbados[^cite_note-Acting_GG-11] and the following year was the first Barbadian appointed to membership in the [Commonwealth Secretariat Arbitral Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Secretariat_Arbitral_Tribunal) (CSAT). The Tribunal operates among members of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) to resolve issues concerning contract disputes.[^cite_note-(CSAT)-4] With that appointment, *Loop News* named her one of the 10 most powerful women in Barbados.[^cite_note-10_most_powerful-12]&#x20;

## Governor-General of Barbados



Mason presenting National Awards during the 2020 Independence Day parade

In 2017, Mason was appointed as the eighth Governor-General of Barbados, with a term beginning on 8 January 2018. Simultaneously with her appointment, Mason was also appointed a Dame Grand Cross in the [Order of Saint Michael and Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Michael_and_Saint_George).[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

In 2020, Mason, in her official capacity announcing government policy in the [Throne Speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throne_Speech), written by the government of Prime Minister [Mia Mottley](15337866.md), stated that [Barbados would become a republic](14456864.md), abolishing the [Barbadian monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Barbados).[^cite_note-14] She was then expected to be nominated as a candidate to be the first president of Barbados, then to be elected by the two houses of parliament, and to assume office on 30 November 2021.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]&#x20;

## President of Barbados



Mason taking oath as the first president of Barbados, 2021

On 12 October 2021, Mason was nominated by Prime Minister [Mia Mottley](15337866.md) and Opposition Leader [Joseph Atherley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Atherley) to become the first [president of Barbados](68909900.md).[^cite_note-18] On 20 October she was [elected by both houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Barbadian_presidential_election) without opposition.[^cite_note-19] Mason took office on 30 November 2021,[^cite_note-\:0-20][^cite_note-21] the 55th anniversary of [Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados_Independence_Act_1966). While she is nominally chief executive and is the sole head of state in Barbados, in practice her role is mostly ceremonial, much like her previous role as Governor-General.[^cite_note-22] She made her first official visit to [Kenya](188171.md) in June 2022.[^cite_note-23]&#x20;

As president, Mason represented Barbados at the [state funeral of Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_Queen_Elizabeth_II) in 2022, and at the [coronation of King Charles III and Queen Camilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_King_Charles_III_and_Queen_Camilla) in 2023.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]&#x20;

## Personal life

Mason has kept her family life largely private. She is known to have a son named Matthew, who is an attorney-at-law.[^cite_note-28]&#x20;
