# List of anti-war songs

&#x20; Some [anti-war](39766716.md) songs lament aspects of [wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars), while others patronize war. Most promote peace in some form, while others sing out against specific armed conflicts. Still others depict the physical and psychological destruction that warfare causes to soldiers, innocent civilians, and humanity as a whole. Many of these songs are considered [protest songs](23477.md), and some have been embraced by [war-weary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War-weary) people, various [peace movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_movement), and [peace activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_peace_activists).

## Traditional music

Apart from the various genres of modern music, some [traditional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_music) and contemporary [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) songs reflect the futile efforts of war and the attitudes of objectors prior to the major wars of the 20th century. Some of these include:

- "[Ain't Gonna Study War No More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain't_Gonna_Study_War_No_More)" also known as "Down by the Riverside", has a similar tune to "Hand Me Down, My Walking Cane," – African-American traditional anti-war song recorded by [The Weavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weavers) and many others.

- "[Arthur McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_McBride)" – While first curated in the 19th century, this song likely came into existence during the 17th century in response to the [War of the Grand Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Grand_Alliance), or especially the [Williamite War in Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamite_War_in_Ireland), after which the Irish [Jacobite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobitism) army was sent to France as agreed in the [Treaty of Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Limerick) on October 3, 1691.

- "The Cruel War" – Made famous in its current form by Pete Seeger and [Peter Paul and Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_and_Mary), this anti-war song has roots at least as far back as the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), and can probably be traced to an older English song.

  [^cite_note-40]

- "Ich bin Soldat, doch bin ich es nicht gerne" [de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ich%20bin%20Soldat,%20doch%20bin%20ich%20es%20nicht%20gerne) – German song popular during the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War), tells of the futility of being a soldier, and ends with a call for reconciliation between nations and the overthrow of tyrants.

- "[Johnny I Hardly Knew Ye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_I_Hardly_Knew_Ye)" – Irish traditional anti-war and anti-recruiting song that was the basis for the song "[When Johnny Comes Marching Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Johnny_Comes_Marching_Home)", and recorded as "Fighting for Strangers" by [Steeleye Span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steeleye_Span).

- "[Join the British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_the_British_Army)" – Irish rebel song, recorded by [Ewan MacColl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewan_MacColl) and the Dubliners.

- "[Kannoneer Jabůrek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannoneer_Jab%C5%AFrek)" is a popular [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) song mocking war heroism, referring to the events of the 1866 [Austro-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War)

- "Lincoln's Army" - the [Irish Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rovers)

- "[Lowlands of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowlands_of_Holland)" — [Martin Carthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Carthy)

- "[Mrs. McGrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._McGrath)" is an Irish song describing a young man named Ted who enters the [British Army](4887.md) and returns seven years later having lost his legs to a cannonball while fighting against [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) presumably at the [Battle of Fuentes de Oñoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fuentes_de_O%C3%B1oro) (fought between 3 and 5 May 1811). Bruce Springsteen recorded a version of this song on his 2006 album *[We Shall Overcome: the Seeger Sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Shall_Overcome:_the_Seeger_Sessions?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, and it appears on the subsequent live 2007 album *[Bruce Springsteen with the Sessions Band: Live in Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen_with_the_Sessions_Band:_Live_in_Dublin?action=edit\&redlink=1)*
