**Mary**[^cite_note-13] was a first-century [Jewish](25955086.md) woman of [Nazareth](21702.md),[^cite_note-14] the wife of [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph) and the mother of [Jesus](1095706.md). She is an important figure of [Christianity](5211.md), venerated under [various titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titles_of_Mary,_mother_of_Jesus) such as [virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_virginity_of_Mary) or [queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Heaven), many of which are mentioned in the [Litany of Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litany_of_Loreto). The [Eastern](10186.md) and [Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches), [Catholic](606848.md), [Lutheran](23371382.md), [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Christianity), [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican), and [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist) churches believe that Mary, as mother of Jesus, is the [Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos). The [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East) historically regarded her as [Christotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christotokos), a term still used in the liturgy of the [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East).[^cite_note-15] She has the [highest position in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_in_Islam) among all women and is mentioned numerous times in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), including in a chapter (surah) [named after her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_\(surah\)).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Jestice-2004-18] She is also revered in the [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD) and [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) faiths.[^cite_note-Bowman-2012-19]

The [synoptic Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synoptic_Gospels) name Mary as the mother of Jesus. The gospels of [Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew) and [Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke) describe Mary as a virgin[^cite_note-23] who was chosen by [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Christianity) to [conceive Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation) through the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity). After [giving birth to Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus) in [Bethlehem](4312.md), she and her husband Joseph raised him in the city of Nazareth in [Galilee](12639.md), and she was in [Jerusalem](16043.md) at his [crucifixion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus) and with the [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament) after his [ascension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_of_Jesus). Although the end of her life is not described in the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible), Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and some Oriental Orthodox and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) traditions believe that her body [was raised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entering_heaven_alive) into [heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_in_Christianity) at the end of her earthly life, which is known in [Western Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity) as the [Assumption of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary) and in [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity) as the [Dormition of the Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Mother_of_God).

[Mary has been venerated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_devotions) since [early Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity),[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] and is often considered to be the holiest and greatest [saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint). There is a certain diversity in the [Mariology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariology) and devotional practices of major Christian traditions. The Catholic Church and some Oriental Orthodox Churches hold distinctive Marian [dogmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma), namely her [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception) and her bodily assumption into heaven, while other [denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination) hold Marian beliefs as [doctrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine) or [traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_tradition).[^cite_note-26] Many Protestants hold [various views of Mary's role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_views_on_Mary) that they perceive as being in accordance with the Scriptures.[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2004-27][^cite_note-Wengert-2017-28] The [Confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Concord) of the [Lutheran Churches](23371382.md) have taught the [virgin birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_birth_of_Jesus), [Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos), and [perpetual virginity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_virginity_of_Mary).[^cite_note-Smith-2010-29][^cite_note-Anderson-1992-30]

The multiple forms of Marian devotions include various [prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_prayer) and [hymns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymns_to_Mary), the celebration of several [Marian feast days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_feast_days) in [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_liturgy),[^cite_note-Wengert-2017-28] the veneration of [images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_image) and [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic), the construction of [churches dedicated to her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Marian_church_buildings) and [pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) to [Marian shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrines_to_the_Virgin_Mary). Many [Marian apparitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_apparition) and [miracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle) attributed to her [intercession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercession_of_saints) have been reported by believers over the centuries. She has been a traditional [subject in arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_\(art\)), notably in [Byzantine art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_art), [medieval art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_art) and [Renaissance art](311401.md).

Mary's name in the original manuscripts of the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament) was based on her original [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) name מרים (Miryam), transliterated as *[Maryam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_\(name\))* or *Mariam*.[^cite_note-31] The English name *[Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_\(name\))* comes from the Greek Μαρία, a shortened form of the name Μαριάμ. Both Μαρία and Μαριάμ appear in the New Testament.

### In Christianity

In Christianity, Mary is commonly referred to as the **Virgin Mary**, in accordance with the belief that the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity) impregnated her, thereby conceiving her first-born son Jesus [miraculously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraculous_births), without sexual relations with her betrothed Joseph, "until her son \[Jesus] was born".[^cite_note-32] The word "until" has inspired considerable analysis on whether Joseph and Mary produced [siblings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_of_Jesus) after the birth of Jesus or not.[^cite_note-34] Among her many other names and titles are the [Blessed Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_the_Catholic_Church) (often abbreviated to "BVM"),[^cite_note-35] [Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint) Mary (occasionally), the [Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos) (primarily in [Western Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity)), the *[Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos)* (primarily in [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity)), Our Lady (Medieval [Italian](14708.md): *Madonna*), and [Queen of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Heaven) (*Regina caeli*; see also [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Coeli_\(disambiguation\))).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2003-37] The title "[queen of heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_heaven_\(antiquity\))" had previously been used as an [epithet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithet) for a number of goddesses, such as [Isis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isis), or [Ishtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishtar).

Titles in use vary among [Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Marian_theology), [Lutherans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther's_Marian_theology) and other [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_views_on_Mary), as well as [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_views_on_Mary), [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Mariology), [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Marian_theology) and [other Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_meetings_and_documents_on_Mary).

The three main titles for Mary used by the Orthodox are *Theotokos* (Θεοτόκος or "God-bearer"), *[Aeiparthenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeiparthenos)* (ἀειπαρθένος) which means ever-virgin, as confirmed in the [Second Council of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Constantinople) in 553, and *[Panagia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagia)* (Παναγία) meaning "all-holy".[^cite_note-Fairbairn-2002-38] Catholics use a wide variety of titles for Mary, and these titles have in turn given rise to many artistic depictions.

The title *Theotokos*, which means "God-bearer", was recognized at the [Council of Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ephesus) in 431.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] The direct equivalents of title in Latin are *Deipara* and *Dei Genitrix*, although the phrase is more often loosely translated into Latin as *Mater Dei* ("Mother of God"), with similar patterns for other languages used in the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church). However, this same phrase in Greek (Μήτηρ Θεοῦ), in the abbreviated form ΜΡ ΘΥ, is an indication commonly attached to her image in [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity) [icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon). The Council stated that the [Church Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Fathers) "did not hesitate to speak of the holy Virgin as the Mother of God".[^cite_note-ccel.org-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Some Marian titles have a direct [scriptural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible) basis. For instance, the title "Queen Mother" has been given to Mary, as she was the mother of Jesus, sometimes referred to as the "King of Kings" due to his ancestral descent from [King David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] This is also based on the Hebrew tradition of the "Queen-Mother", the [Gebirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebirah) or "Great Lady".[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Other titles have arisen from [reported miracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle), special appeals, or occasions for calling on Mary.[^cite_note-55]

### In Islam

Main article: [Mary in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_in_Islam)



*Virgin Mary* depicted by Muslim painter [Hossein Nuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Nuri#Virgin_Mary_painting)

In [Islam](6037917.md), Mary is known as [Maryam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_in_Islam) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مريم, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Maryam*), mother of [Isa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_in_Islam) (عيسى بن مريم, *ʿĪsā ibn Maryām*, lit. 'Jesus, son of Mary'). She is often referred to by the honorific title *"Sayyidatuna"*, meaning "Our Lady"; this title is in parallel to *"Sayyiduna"* ("Our Lord"), used for the prophets.[^cite_note-56] A related term of endearment is *"Siddiqah"*,[^cite_note-57] meaning "she who confirms the truth" and "she who believes sincerely completely". Another title for Mary is *"Qānitah"*, which signifies both constant submission to God and absorption in prayer and invocation in Islam.[^cite_note-qref|66|12|b=y-58] She is also called *"Tahira"*, meaning "one who has been purified" and representing her status as one of two humans in creation to not be touched by [Satan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iblis) at any point, the other being Jesus.[^cite_note-qref|3|36|b=y-59] In the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), she is described both as "the daughter of Imran" and "the sister of Aaron" and Moses, alluding to [Miriam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam) from the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible).[^cite_note-60] However, the title of "the sister of Aaron" is confirmed to be metaphorical (which is a common [figure of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_of_speech) in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic)) as per a [Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith) from the [Islamic](6037917.md) prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) explaining Mary was indeed named after Miriam.[^cite_note-61]

### New Testament

The [canonical Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_Gospels) and the [Acts of the Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles) are the [primary sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_source) of historical information about Mary.[^cite_note-Hesemann-2016-62][^cite_note-Canales-2010-63] They are almost contemporary sources, as the [synoptic Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synoptic_Gospels) and the Acts of the Apostles are generally considered dating from around AD 66–90, while the [gospel of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_John) dates from AD 90–110. They provide limited information about Mary, as they primarily focus on the teaching of [Jesus](1095706.md) and on [his apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament).[^cite_note-Hesemann-2016-62] The [historical reliability of the Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_reliability_of_the_Gospels) and [historical reliability of the Acts of the Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_reliability_of_the_Acts_of_the_Apostles) are subject to debate, as it was common practice in early Christian writings to mix historical facts with legendary stories.[^cite_note-Hesemann-2016-62]

The earliest New Testament account of Mary is in the [epistle to the Galatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Galatians), which was written before the [gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospels). She is referred to as "a woman" and is not named: "But when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law" (Galatians 4\:4).[^cite_note-Canales-2010-63]

Mary is mentioned several times in the canonical Gospels and the Acts of the Apostles:

#### Genealogy

Further information: [Genealogy of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genealogy_of_Jesus)



The Virgin's first seven steps, mosaic from [Chora Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chora_Church), c. 12th century

The New Testament tells little of Mary's early history. The Gospel of Matthew gives a genealogy for Jesus by his father's paternal line, only identifying Mary as the wife of Joseph. John 19\:25[^cite_note-76] states that Mary had a sister; semantically it is unclear if this sister is the same as [Mary of Clopas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_of_Clopas), or if she is left unnamed. [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome) identifies Mary of Clopas as the sister of Mary, mother of Jesus.[^cite_note-77] According to the early 2nd century historian [Hegesippus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegesippus_\(chronicler\)), Mary of Clopas was likely Mary's sister-in-law, understanding Clopas (Cleophas) to have been Joseph's brother.[^cite_note-78]

According to the writer of Luke, Mary was a relative of [Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_\(biblical_person\)), wife of the priest [Zechariah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zechariah_\(priest\)) of the priestly division of [Abijah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abijah), who was herself part of the [lineage of Aaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughter_of_Aaron) and so of the [Tribe of Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Levi).[^cite_note-79] Some of those who believe that the relationship with Elizabeth was on the maternal side, believe that Mary, like Joseph, was of the royal [Davidic line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidic_line) and so of the [Tribe of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Judah), and that the [genealogy of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genealogy_of_Jesus) presented in [Luke 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_3) from [Nathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_\(son_of_David\)), is in fact the genealogy of Mary, while the genealogy from [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) given in [Matthew 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_1) is that of Joseph.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] (Aaron's wife [Elisheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisheba) was of the tribe of Judah, so all their descendants are from both Levi and Judah.)[^cite_note-83]

#### Annunciation

Main article: [Annunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation)

Mary resided in "her own house"[^cite_note-84] in [Nazareth](21702.md) in [Galilee](12639.md), possibly with her parents, and during her betrothal—the first stage of a [Jewish marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_view_of_marriage). Jewish girls were considered marriageable at the age of twelve years and six months, though the actual age of the bride varied with circumstances. The marriage was preceded by the betrothal, after which the bride legally belonged to the bridegroom, though she did not live with him till about a year later, when the marriage was celebrated.[^cite_note-85]

The [angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel) Gabriel announced to her that she was to be the mother of the promised [Messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messiah) by conceiving him through the Holy Spirit, and, after initially expressing incredulity at the announcement, she responded, "I am the handmaid of the Lord. Let it be done unto me according to your word."[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] Joseph planned to quietly divorce her, but was told her conception was by the Holy Spirit in a dream by "an angel of the Lord"; the angel told him to not hesitate to take her as his wife, which Joseph did, thereby formally completing the wedding rites.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

Since the angel Gabriel had told Mary that Elizabeth—having previously been barren—was then miraculously pregnant,[^cite_note-90] Mary hurried to see Elizabeth, who was living with her husband Zechariah in "the hill country..., \[in] a city of Juda". Mary arrived at the house and greeted Elizabeth who called Mary "the mother of my Lord", and Mary spoke the words of praise that later became known as the *[Magnificat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnificat)* from her first word in the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) version.[^cite_note-91] After about three months, Mary returned to her own house.[^cite_note-92]

#### Birth of Jesus

Main article: [Nativity of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus)



The adoration of the shepherds, a [nativity scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_scene) in France

According to the [gospel of Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke), a decree of the [Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Emperor) [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) required that Joseph return to his hometown of [Bethlehem](4312.md) to register for a [Roman census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_of_Quirinius).[^cite_note-94] While he was there with Mary, she gave birth to Jesus; but because there was no place for them in the inn, she used a [manger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manger) as a cradle.[^cite_note-95]<sup>: p.14 </sup>[^cite_note-96] It is not told how old Mary was at the time of the Nativity,[^cite_note-Levine-2018-97] but attempts have been made to infer it from the age of a typical Jewish mother of that time. Mary Joan Winn Leith represents the view that Jewish girls typically married soon after the onset of puberty,[^cite_note-98] while according to [Amram Tropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amram_Tropper?action=edit\&redlink=1), Jewish females generally married later in Palestine and the Western Diaspora than in Babylonia.[^cite_note-Tropper-2006-99] Some scholars hold the view that among them it typically happened between their mid and late teen years[^cite_note-100] or late teens and early twenties.[^cite_note-Levine-2018-97][^cite_note-Tropper-2006-99] After eight days, the boy was [circumcised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcision_of_Jesus) according to Jewish law and named "[Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_\(name\))" (ישוע, *[Yeshu'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshua_\(name\))*), which means "[Yahweh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahweh) is salvation".[^cite_note-101]

After Mary continued in the "[blood of her purifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumah_and_taharah)" another 33 days, for a total of 40 days, she brought her [burnt offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnt_offering_\(Judaism\)) and [sin offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin_offering) to the [Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple) in [Jerusalem](16043.md) (Luke 2\:22),[^cite_note-102] so the priest could make atonement for her.[^cite_note-103] They also presented Jesus – "As it is written in the law of the Lord, Every male that openeth the womb shall be called holy to the Lord" (Luke 2\:23; Exodus 13\:2; 23\:12–15; 22\:29; 34\:19–20; Numbers 3\:13; 18\:15).[^cite_note-104] After the prophecies of [Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_\(Gospel_of_Luke\)) and the prophetess [Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_\(Bible\)) in Luke 2\:25–38,[^cite_note-105] the family "returned into Galilee, to their own city Nazareth".[^cite_note-106]

According to the [gospel of Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew), [magi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Magi) coming from Eastern regions arrived at Bethlehem where Jesus and his family were living, and worshiped him. Joseph was then warned in a dream that [King Herod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great) wanted to murder the infant, and the [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Family) [fled by night to Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_into_Egypt) and stayed there for some time. After Herod's death in 4 BC, they returned to Nazareth in Galilee, rather than Bethlehem, because Herod's son [Archelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_Archelaus) was the ruler of Judaea.[^cite_note-107]

Mary is involved in the only event in Jesus' adolescent life that is recorded in the New Testament. At the age of 12, Jesus, having become separated from his parents on their return journey from the [Passover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover) celebration in Jerusalem, was [found in the Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finding_in_the_Temple) among the religious teachers.[^cite_note-108]<sup>: p.210 </sup>[^cite_note-109]

#### Ministry of Jesus



*[Stabat Mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabat_Mater_\(art\))* by [Gabriel Wuger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Wuger), 1868

Mary was present when, at her suggestion, Jesus worked his first miracle during a [wedding at Cana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_at_Cana) by turning water into wine.[^cite_note-110] Subsequently, there are events when Mary is mentioned along with the [Jesus' brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_of_Jesus).[^cite_note-111] According to [Epiphanius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis), [Origen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origen) and [Eusebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius_of_Cesarea), these "brothers" would be sons of Joseph from a previous marriage. This view is still the official position of the Eastern Orthodox churches. Following [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome), those would be actually Jesus' cousins, children of Mary's sister. This remains the official Roman Catholic position. For [Helvidius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvidius), those would be full siblings of Jesus, born to Mary and Joseph after the firstborn Jesus. This has been the most common Protestant position.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

The [hagiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagiography) of Mary and the [Holy Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Family) can be contrasted with other material in the Gospels. These references include an incident which can be interpreted as Jesus rejecting his family in the New Testament: "And his mother and his brothers arrived, and standing outside, they sent in a message asking for him ... And looking at those who sat in a circle around him, Jesus said, 'These are my mother and my brothers. Whoever does the will of God is my brother, and sister, and mother'."[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

Mary is also depicted as being present in a [group of women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_at_the_crucifixion) at the [crucifixion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus) standing near the [disciple whom Jesus loved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disciple_whom_Jesus_loved) along with [Mary of Clopas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_of_Clopas) and [Mary Magdalene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Magdalene),[^cite_note-John_19-70] to which list Matthew 27\:56[^cite_note-117] adds "the mother of the sons of Zebedee", presumably the [Salome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_\(disciple\)) mentioned in Mark 15\:40.[^cite_note-118]

#### After the Ascension of Jesus

In Acts 1\:12–26,[^cite_note-119] especially verse 14, Mary is the only one other than the [eleven apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_apostles) to be mentioned by name who abode in the [upper room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenacle), when they returned from [Mount Olivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_of_Olives). Her presence with the apostles during the [Pentecost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecost) is not explicit, although it has been held as a fact by Christian tradition.[^cite_note-120]

From this time, she disappears from the biblical accounts, although it is held by Catholics that she is again portrayed as the [heavenly woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman_of_the_Apocalypse) in the [Book of Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation).[^cite_note-121]

Her death is not recorded in the scriptures, but Orthodox tradition, tolerated also by Catholics, has her first dying a natural death, known as the [Dormition of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Mother_of_God),[^cite_note-122] and then, soon after, her body itself also being [assumed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary) (taken bodily) into [Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven#In_Roman_Catholicism). Belief in the corporeal assumption of Mary is a [dogma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma_in_the_Catholic_Church) of the [Catholic Church](606848.md), in the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) and [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md) alike, and is believed as well by the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md),[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] the [Oriental Orthodox Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches), and parts of the [Anglican Communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion) and [Continuing Anglican movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuing_Anglican_movement).[^cite_note-125]

Some [apocryphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocryphal) texts add further narrative elements to the story of Mary, although scholars generally consider such texts to be historically mainly unreliable.[^cite_note-126] According to the [Gospel of James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_James), Mary was the daughter of [Joachim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim) and [Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Anne). Before Mary's conception, Anne had been barren and was far advanced in years. Mary was given to service as a consecrated virgin in the Temple in Jerusalem when she was three years old.[^cite_note-127] This event is commemorated by some Christian churches as a [liturgical feast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_feast), the [Presentation of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_Mary), even though its premise conflicts with common understandings of [Jewish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_culture) and temple customs.[^cite_note-128] The Gospel of James also states that Mary was 12–14 years old at the time of her betrothal to Joseph,[^cite_note-129] and that she was 16 during her pregnancy,[^cite_note-130] according to most manuscripts.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-NewAdvent-2][^cite_note-133] According to ancient Jewish custom, Mary could have been betrothed at 12,[^cite_note-134] but some scholars hold the view that in [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea) betrothal typically happened later.[^cite_note-Levine-2018-97][^cite_note-Tropper-2006-99] The text underlines that the relationship between Mary and Joseph was a non-sexual social arrangement, and that Mary was dedicated to God. It emphasizes holiness of Mary and the virginal nature of her pregnancy.[^cite_note-135]

Although the canonical gospels contain no account of Mary's life after the resurrection, texts written centuries later provide some embellishment. The origin and authenticity of these details are unknown. The earliest extant biographical writing on Mary is *[Life of the Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_of_the_Virgin_\(Maximus\))*, attributed to the 7th-century saint [Maximus the Confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximus_the_Confessor), which portrays her as a key element of the [early Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity) after the death of Jesus.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] [Hyppolitus of Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyppolitus_of_Thebes), writing in the 7th or 8th century, says that Mary lived for 11 years after the death of her son, dying in 41 AD.[^cite_note-139]

### Christian

See also: [Mariology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariology), [Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos), and [Hymns to Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymns_to_Mary)

Christian Marian perspectives include a great deal of diversity. While some Christians such as Catholics and Eastern Orthodox have well established Marian traditions, Protestants at large pay scant attention to [Mariological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariology) themes. Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Lutherans and Anglicans [venerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration) the Virgin Mary. This veneration especially takes the form of [prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayer) for intercession with her Son, Jesus Christ. Additionally, it includes composing poems and songs in Mary's honor, painting [icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon) or carving statues of her, and [conferring titles on Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titles_of_Mary) that reflect her position among the saints.[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2003-37][^cite_note-Fairbairn-2002-38][^cite_note-Flinn-2007-142][^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143]

#### Catholic

Main article: [Veneration of Mary in the Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_the_Catholic_Church)

In the Catholic Church, Mary is accorded the title "Blessed" (*beata*, μακάρια, *makaria*) in recognition of her assumption to Heaven and her capacity to intercede on behalf of those who pray to her. There is a difference between the usage of the term "blessed" as pertaining to Mary and its usage as pertaining to a [beatified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatified) person. "Blessed" as a Marian title refers to her exalted state as being the greatest among the saints; for a person who has been declared beatified, on the other hand, "blessed" simply indicates that they may be venerated despite not being [canonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonized). Catholic teachings make clear that Mary is not considered divine and prayers to her are not answered by her, but rather by God through her intercession.[^cite_note-144] The [four Catholic dogmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Mariology) regarding Mary are: her status as *Theotokos*, or Mother of God; her perpetual virginity; the Immaculate Conception; and her bodily Assumption into Heaven.[^cite_note-Fahlbusch-2003-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

The [Blessed Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_Roman_Catholicism), the mother of Jesus has a more central role in Roman Catholic teachings and beliefs than in any other major Christian group. Not only do Roman Catholics have more theological doctrines and teachings that relate to Mary, but they have more feasts, prayers, devotional and venerative practices than any other group.[^cite_note-Flinn-2007-142] The *[Catechism of the Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church)* states: "The Church's devotion to the Blessed Virgin is intrinsic to Christian worship."[^cite_note-ccc971-148]

For centuries, Catholics have performed acts of [consecration and entrustment to Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecration_and_entrustment_to_Mary) at personal, societal and regional levels. These acts may be directed to the Virgin herself, to the [Immaculate Heart of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary) and to the [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception). In Catholic teachings, consecration to Mary does not diminish or substitute the love of God, but enhances it, for all consecration is ultimately made to God.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

Following the growth of Marian devotions in the 16th century, Catholic saints wrote books such as [*Glories of Mary*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glories_of_Mary) and *[True Devotion to Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Devotion_to_Mary)* that emphasized Marian veneration and taught that "the path to Jesus is through Mary".[^cite_note-151] Marian devotions are at times linked to [Christocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christology) devotions (such as the [Alliance of the Hearts of Jesus and Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_the_Hearts_of_Jesus_and_Mary)).[^cite_note-152]

Major Marian devotions include: [Seven Sorrows of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sorrows_of_Mary), [Rosary and scapular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary_and_scapular), [Miraculous Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraculous_Medal) and [Reparations to Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Reparation_to_the_Virgin_Mary).[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] The months of May and October are traditionally "Marian months" for Roman Catholics; the daily [rosary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary) is encouraged in October and in [May Marian devotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_devotions_to_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary) take place in many regions.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] Popes have issued a number of [Marian encyclicals and Apostolic Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_papal_encyclicals_and_Apostolic_Letters) to encourage devotions to and the veneration of the Virgin Mary.

Catholics place high emphasis on Mary's roles as protector and intercessor and the [*Catechism*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church) refers to Mary as "honored with the title 'Mother of God', to whose protection the faithful fly in all their dangers and needs".[^cite_note-ccc971-148][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] Key Marian prayers include: *[Ave Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail_Mary)*, *[Alma Redemptoris Mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_Redemptoris_Mater)*, *[Sub tuum praesidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_tuum_praesidium)*, *[Ave maris stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ave_maris_stella)*, *[Regina caeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_caeli)*, *[Ave Regina caelorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ave_Regina_caelorum)* and the *[Magnificat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnificat)*.[^cite_note-162]

Mary's participation in the processes of [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_\(Christianity\)) and redemption has also been emphasized in the Catholic tradition, but they are not doctrines.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] [Pope John Paul II](23805.md)'s 1987 encyclical *[Redemptoris Mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redemptoris_Mater)* began with the sentence: "The Mother of the Redeemer has a precise place in the plan of salvation."[^cite_note-167]

In the 20th century, both popes John Paul II and [Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XVI) emphasized the Marian focus of the Catholic Church. Cardinal [Joseph Ratzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ratzinger) (later Pope Benedict XVI) suggested a redirection of the whole church towards the program of Pope John Paul II in order to ensure an authentic approach to [Christology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christology) via a return to the "whole truth about Mary,"[^cite_note-Miravalle-Burke-2008-168] writing:

> "It is necessary to go back to Mary if we want to return to that 'truth about Jesus Christ,' 'truth about the Church' and 'truth about man.'"[^cite_note-Miravalle-Burke-2008-168]

There is significant diversity in the Marian doctrines attributed to her primarily by the Catholic Church. The key Marian doctrines held primarily in Catholicism can be briefly outlined as follows:

- [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception): Mary was conceived without [original sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_sin).

- [Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_of_God): Mary, as the mother of Jesus, is the *Theotokos* (God-bearer), or Mother of God.

- [Virgin birth of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_birth_of_Jesus): Mary conceived Jesus by action of the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_\(Christianity\)) while remaining a virgin.

- [Perpetual Virginity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_Virginity): Mary remained a virgin all her life, even after the act of giving birth to Jesus.

- [Dormition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Mother_of_God): commemorates Mary's "falling asleep" or natural death shortly before her Assumption. Dormition is part of accepted [Eastern Catholic](214903.md) theology, but not part of [Latin Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Catholic) doctrine.

  [^cite_note-169]

- [Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary): Mary was taken [bodily into heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entering_heaven_alive) either at, or before, her death.

The acceptance of these Marian doctrines by Roman Catholics and other Christians can be summarized as follows:[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2004-27][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

| Doctrine | Church action | Accepted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Virgin birth of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_birth_of_Jesus) | [First Council of Nicaea](11118.md), 325 | Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Assyrians, Lutherans, Anglicans, Baptists and other Protestants |
| [Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_of_God) | [First Council of Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Council_of_Ephesus), 431 | Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Lutherans, Anglicans, Methodists, some Evangelicals.[^cite_note-172] |
| [Perpetual Virginity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_Virginity) | [Second Ecumenical Council of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Constantinople), 553 | Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Assyrians, many Lutherans, some Anglicans[^cite_note-Smith-2010-29][^cite_note-Anderson-1992-30][^cite_note-173] |
| [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception) | *[Ineffabilis Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineffabilis_Deus)* encyclical[Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX), 1854 | Catholics, some Oriental Orthodox,[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] some Anglicans, some Lutherans (early Martin Luther) |
| [Assumption of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary) | *[Munificentissimus Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munificentissimus_Deus)* encyclical[Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII), 1950 | Catholics, Eastern and Oriental Orthodox (only following her natural death), some Lutherans, some Anglicans |

The title "Mother of God" (*Theotokos*) for Mary was confirmed by the [First Council of Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Council_of_Ephesus), held at the [Church of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Mary) in 431. The Council decreed that Mary is the Mother of God because her son Jesus is one person who is both God and man, divine and human.[^cite_note-ccel.org-41] This doctrine is widely accepted by Christians in general, and the term "Mother of God" had already been used within the oldest known prayer to Mary, the *[Sub tuum praesidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_tuum_praesidium)*, which dates to around 250 AD.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-Smith-2010-29][^cite_note-Anderson-1992-30]



Miraculous Icon of Our Lady of Tartaków in Blessed Virgin Mary Church in Łukawiec

The [Virgin birth of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_birth_of_Jesus) was an almost universally held belief among Christians from the 2nd until the 19th century.[^cite_note-177] It is included in the two most widely used Christian [creeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed), which state that Jesus "was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary" (the [Nicene Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Creed), in what is now its familiar form)[^cite_note-178] and the [Apostles' Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles'_Creed). The [Gospel of Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew) describes Mary as a virgin who fulfilled the prophecy of Isaiah 7\:14,[^cite_note-179] The authors of the Gospels of Matthew and Luke consider Jesus' conception not the result of intercourse, and assert that Mary had "no relations with man" before Jesus' birth.[^cite_note-180] This alludes to the belief that Mary conceived Jesus through the action of God the Holy Spirit, and not through [intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_reproduction#Copulation) with Joseph or anyone else.[^cite_note-Miravalle-1993-181]

The doctrines of the Assumption or Dormition of Mary relate to her death and bodily assumption to heaven. Roman Catholic Church has dogmatically defined the doctrine of the Assumption, which was done in 1950 by [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII) in *[Munificentissimus Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munificentissimus_Deus)*. Whether Mary died or not is not defined dogmatically, however, although a reference to the death of Mary is made in *Munificentissimus Deus*. In the Eastern Orthodox Church, the Assumption of the Virgin Mary is believed, and celebrated with her [Dormition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Mother_of_God), where they believe she died.

Catholics believe in the [Immaculate Conception of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception_of_Mary), as proclaimed *[ex cathedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_cathedra)* by Pope [Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_IX) in 1854, namely that she was filled with grace from the very moment of her conception in her mother's womb and preserved from the stain of [original sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_sin). The [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) has a liturgical [feast by that name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Immaculate_Conception), kept on 8 December.[^cite_note-182] Orthodox Christians reject the Immaculate Conception dogma principally because their understanding of ancestral sin (the Greek term corresponding to the Latin "original sin") differs from the [Augustinian](2030.md) interpretation and that of the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-183]

The Perpetual Virginity of Mary asserts Mary's real and perpetual [virginity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginity) even in the act of giving birth to the Son of God made Man. The term Ever-Virgin (Greek ἀειπάρθενος) is applied in this case, stating that Mary remained a virgin for the remainder of her life, making Jesus her biological and only son, whose [conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarnation_\(Christianity\)) and [birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus) are held to be miraculous.[^cite_note-Fahlbusch-2003-145][^cite_note-Miravalle-1993-181][^cite_note-184] The Orthodox Churches hold the position articulated in the [Protoevangelium of James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_James) that Jesus' brothers and sisters were Joseph's children from a marriage prior to that of Mary, which had left him widowed. Roman Catholic teaching follows the Latin father [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome) in considering them Jesus' cousins.

#### Eastern Orthodox



A [mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic) from the [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) of [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) (modern Istanbul), depicting Mary with Jesus, flanked by [John II Komnenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_Komnenos) (left) and his wife [Irene of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_of_Hungary) (right), c. 1118 AD



15th century [icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon) of the *Theotokos* ("God-bearer")

[Eastern Orthodox Christianity](10186.md) includes a large number of traditions regarding the Ever-Virgin Mary, the *Theotokos*.[^cite_note-185] The Orthodox believe that she was and remained a virgin before and after Christ's birth.[^cite_note-Fairbairn-2002-38] The *[Theotokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokia)* ([hymns to the Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymns_to_Mary)) are an essential part of the [Divine Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Services) in the [Eastern Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Church) and their positioning within the liturgical sequence effectively places the *Theotokos* in the most prominent place after Christ.[^cite_note-Dragas-2005-186] Within the Orthodox tradition, the order of the saints begins with: the *Theotokos*, Angels, Prophets, Apostles, Fathers and Martyrs, giving the Virgin Mary precedence over the angels. She is also proclaimed as the "Lady of the Angels".[^cite_note-Dragas-2005-186]

The views of the [Church Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Fathers) still play an important role in the shaping of Orthodox Marian perspective. However, the Orthodox views on Mary are mostly [doxological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doxology), rather than academic: they are expressed in hymns, praise, liturgical poetry, and the veneration of icons. One of the most loved Orthodox [Akathists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akathist) ([standing hymns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akathist_to_the_Theotokos)) is devoted to Mary and it is often simply called the *[Akathist Hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akathist_to_the_Theotokos)*.[^cite_note-187] Five of the twelve [Great Feasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Feasts) in Orthodoxy are dedicated to Mary.[^cite_note-Fairbairn-2002-38] The [Sunday of Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_of_Orthodoxy) directly links the Virgin Mary's identity as Mother of God with icon veneration.[^cite_note-188] A number of Orthodox feasts are connected with the miraculous icons of the *Theotokos*.[^cite_note-Dragas-2005-186]



*(Panagía tou Páthous) [Virgin of the Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_of_the_Passion)* by [Emmanuel Tzanfournaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Tzanfournaris), early 1600s

The Orthodox view Mary as "superior to all created beings", although not divine.[^cite_note-Bulgakov-1997-189] As such, the designation of Saint to Mary as Saint Mary is not appropriate.[^cite_note-190] The Orthodox does not venerate Mary as conceived immaculate. [Gregory of Nazianzus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus), [Archbishop of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Constantinople) in the 4th century AD, speaking on the Nativity of Jesus Christ argues that "Conceived by the Virgin, who first in body and soul was purified by the Holy Ghost, He came forth as God with that which He had assumed, One Person in two Natures, Flesh and Spirit, of which the latter defined the former."[^cite_note-191] The Orthodox celebrate the [Dormition of the Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Theotokos), rather than Assumption.[^cite_note-Fairbairn-2002-38]

The [Protoevangelium of James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protoevangelium_of_James), an [extra-canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_canon) book, has been the source of many Orthodox beliefs on Mary. The account of Mary's life presented includes her consecration as a virgin at the temple at age three. The [high priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohen) Zachariah blessed Mary and informed her that God had magnified her name among many generations. Zachariah placed Mary on the third step of the altar, whereby God gave her grace. While in the temple, Mary was miraculously fed by an angel, until she was 12 years old. At that point, an angel told Zachariah to betroth Mary to a widower in Israel, who would be indicated. This story provides the theme of many hymns for the Feast of [Presentation of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_Mary), and icons of the feast depict the story.[^cite_note-192] The Orthodox believe that Mary was instrumental in the growth of Christianity during the life of Jesus, and after his Crucifixion, and Orthodox theologian [Sergei Bulgakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Bulgakov) has written: "The Virgin Mary is the centre, invisible, but real, of the Apostolic Church."

Theologians from the Orthodox tradition have made prominent contributions to the development of Marian thought and devotion. [John Damascene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Damascene) (c. 650 – c. 750) was one of the greatest Orthodox theologians. Among other Marian writings, he proclaimed the essential nature of Mary's heavenly Assumption or Dormition and her meditative role.

> It was necessary that the body of the one who preserved her virginity intact in giving birth should also be kept incorrupt after death. It was necessary that she, who carried the Creator in her womb when he was a baby, should dwell among the tabernacles of heaven.[^cite_note-193]

> From her we have harvested the grape of life; from her we have cultivated the seed of immortality. For our sake she became Mediatrix of all blessings; in her God became man, and man became God.[^cite_note-194]

More recently, [Sergei Bulgakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Bulgakov) expressed the Orthodox sentiments towards Mary as follows:[^cite_note-Bulgakov-1997-189]

> Mary is not merely the instrument, but the direct positive condition of the Incarnation, its human aspect. Christ could not have been incarnate by some mechanical process, violating human nature. It was necessary for that nature itself to say for itself, by the mouth of the most pure human being: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord, be it unto me according to Thy word."

#### Protestant

Further information: [Protestant views on Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_views_on_Mary)



Stained glass window of [Jesus leaving his mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_taking_leave_of_his_Mother), in a [Lutheran church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Matthew's_German_Evangelical_Lutheran_Church) in South Carolina

Protestants in general reject the veneration and invocation of the Saints.[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2004-27]<sup>: 1174 </sup> They share the belief that Mary is the mother of Jesus and "blessed among women" (Luke 1\:42)[^cite_note-195] but they generally do not agree that Mary is to be venerated. She is considered to be an outstanding example of a life dedicated to God.[^cite_note-196] As such, they tend not to accept certain church doctrines such as her being preserved from sin.[^cite_note-197] Theologian [Karl Barth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Barth) wrote that "the heresy of the Catholic Church is its [Mariology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariology)".[^cite_note-198]

Early Protestantism had high regard for Mary. [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther) wrote that: "Mary is full of grace, proclaimed to be entirely without sin. God's grace fills her with everything good and makes her devoid of all evil."[^cite_note-199] [John Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Calvin) remarked, "It cannot be denied that God in choosing and destining Mary to be the Mother of his Son, granted her the highest honor."[^cite_note-202] However, Calvin firmly rejected the notion that Mary can intercede between Christ and man.[^cite_note-203]

Although Calvin and [Huldrych Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huldrych_Zwingli) honored Mary as the Mother of Christ in the 16th century, they did so less than Martin Luther.[^cite_note-204] Thus the idea of respect and high honor for Mary was not rejected by the first Protestants; however, they came to criticize the Roman Catholics for venerating Mary. Following the [Council of Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Trent) in the 16th century, as Marian veneration became associated with Catholics, Protestant interest in Mary decreased. During the Age of the Enlightenment, any residual interest in Mary within Protestant churches almost disappeared, although Anglicans and Lutherans continued to honor her.[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2004-27]

In the 20th century, some Protestants reacted in opposition to the Catholic dogma of the [Assumption of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary).[^cite_note-205] The tone of the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) began to mend the ecumenical differences, and Protestants began to show interest in Marian themes. In 1997 and 1998, ecumenical dialogues between Catholics and Protestants took place, but, to date, the majority of Protestants disagree with Marian veneration and some view it as a challenge to the [authority of Scripture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sola_Scriptura).[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2004-27]

##### Lutheran

Main article: [Lutheran Mariology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Mariology)



Mary with an inscription referencing Luke 1\:46–47 in St. Jürgen (Lutheran) church in [Gettorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettorf) (Schleswig-Holstein)

[Evangelical-Lutheranism](23371382.md) regards her as the *Blessed Virgin Mary*, with this codified in the Formula of Concord.[^cite_note-Anderson-1992-30][^cite_note-206] The Evangelical-Lutheran Churches celebrate several Marian feast days, including the [Feast of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Annunciation), [Feast of the Purification of the Blessed Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlemas), and the [Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visitation_\(Christianity\)).[^cite_note-Wengert-2017-28] The [Formula of Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_of_Concord) and the [Smalcald Articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smalcald_Articles), upheld by the Lutheran Churches, teach the [virgin birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_birth_of_Jesus), *[Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos)* and the [perpetual virginity of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_virginity_of_Mary).[^cite_note-Smith-2010-29][^cite_note-Anderson-1992-30] The [Apology of the Augsburg Confession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apology_of_the_Augsburg_Confession) teaches that "blessed Mary prays for the Church".[^cite_note-207]

Theologians agree that Martin Luther adhered to the Marian decrees of the [ecumenical councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_council) and dogmas of the church. He held fast to the belief that Mary was a perpetual virgin and Mother of God.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209] Special attention is given to the assertion that Luther, some 300 years before the dogmatization of the [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception) by [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX) in 1854, was a firm adherent of that view. Others maintain that Luther in later years changed his position on the Immaculate Conception, which, at that time was undefined in the church, maintaining however the [sinlessness of Mary throughout her life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinlessness_of_Mary).[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] For Luther, early in his life, the Assumption of Mary was an understood fact, although he later stated that the Bible did not say anything about it and stopped celebrating its feast. Important to him was the belief that Mary and the saints do live on after death.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] "Throughout his career as a priest-professor-reformer, Luther preached, taught, and argued about the veneration of Mary with a verbosity that ranged from childlike piety to sophisticated polemics. His views are intimately linked to his Christocentric theology and its consequences for liturgy and piety."[^cite_note-215] Luther, while revering Mary, came to criticize the "Papists" for blurring the line between high admiration of the grace of God wherever it is seen in a human being, and religious service given to another creature. He considered the Roman Catholic practice of celebrating [saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint)' days and making intercessory requests addressed especially to Mary and other departed saints to be [idolatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idolatry).[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] His final thoughts on Marian devotion and veneration are preserved in a sermon preached at Wittenberg only a month before his death:

> Therefore, when we preach faith, that we should worship nothing but God alone, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, as we say in the Creed: 'I believe in God the Father almighty and in Jesus Christ,' then we are remaining in the temple at Jerusalem. Again,'This is my beloved Son; listen to him.' 'You will find him in a manger'. He alone does it. But reason says the opposite: What, us? Are we to worship only Christ? Indeed, shouldn't we also honor the holy mother of Christ? She is the woman who bruised the head of the serpent. Hear us, Mary, for thy Son so honors thee that he can refuse thee nothing. Here [Bernard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux) went too far in his *Homilies on the Gospel: Missus est Angelus*.[^cite_note-218] God has commanded that we should honor the parents; therefore I will call upon Mary. She will intercede for me with the Son, and the Son with the Father, who will listen to the Son. So you have the picture of God as angry and Christ as judge; Mary shows to Christ her breast and Christ shows his wounds to the wrathful Father. That's the kind of thing this comely bride, the wisdom of reason cooks up: Mary is the mother of Christ, surely Christ will listen to her; Christ is a stern judge, therefore I will call upon St. George and St. Christopher. No, we have been by God's command baptized in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, just as the Jews were circumcised.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

Certain Lutheran churches such as the [Anglo-Lutheran Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Lutheran_Catholic_Church) venerate Mary and the saints in the same manner that Roman Catholics do, and hold all Marian dogmas as part of their faith.[^cite_note-221]

##### Anglican

Main article: [Anglican Marian theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Marian_theology)

The various churches that form the [Anglican Communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion) and the [Continuing Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuing_Anglican) movement have different views on Marian doctrines and venerative practices given that there is no single church with universal authority within the Communion and that the mother church (the [Church of England](5955.md)) understands itself to be both "Catholic" and "[Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Christianity)".[^cite_note-222] The Anglican Communion includes segments which still retain some veneration of Mary.[^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143]

Mary's special position within God's purpose of salvation as "God-bearer" is recognized in a number of ways by some Anglican Christians.[^cite_note-Braaten-2004-223] All the member churches of the Anglican Communion affirm in the historic creeds that Jesus was born of the Virgin Mary, and celebrates the feast days of the [Presentation of Christ in the Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_Christ_in_the_Temple). This feast is called in older [prayer books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Common_Prayer) the [Purification of the Blessed Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purification_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary) on 2 February. The [Annunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation) of our Lord to the Blessed Virgin on 25 March was from before the time of [Bede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bede) until the 18th century New Year's Day in England. The Annunciation is called the "Annunciation of our Lady" in the 1662 [Book of Common Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Common_Prayer). Anglicans also celebrate in the [Visitation of the Blessed Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visitation_\(Christian\)) on 31 May, though in some provinces the traditional date of 2 July is kept. The feast of the St. Mary the Virgin is observed on the traditional day of the Assumption, 15 August. The [Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary) of the Blessed Virgin is kept on 8 September.[^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143]

The Conception of the Blessed Virgin Mary is kept in the 1662 Book of Common Prayer, on 8 December. In certain Anglican parishes of [Anglo-Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Catholic) churchmanship, this feast is called the Immaculate Conception. Again, the Assumption of Mary is believed in by most Anglo-Catholics, but is considered a [pious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piety) opinion by moderate Anglicans. [Reformed Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Anglican) reject the celebration of these feasts.[^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143]

Prayers and venerative practices vary greatly. For instance, as of the 19th century, following the [Oxford Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Movement), [Anglo-Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Catholic) frequently pray the [Rosary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary), the *[Angelus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelus)*, *[Regina caeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_caeli)*, and other litanies and anthems of Mary reminiscent of Catholic practices.[^cite_note-224] Conversely, [low church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_church) Anglicans rarely invoke the Blessed Virgin except in certain hymns, such as the second stanza of *[Ye Watchers and Ye Holy Ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Watchers_and_Ye_Holy_Ones)*.[^cite_note-Braaten-2004-223][^cite_note-225]

The [Anglican Society of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Mary_\(Anglican\)) was formed in 1931 and maintains chapters in many countries. The purpose of the society is to foster devotion to Mary among Anglicans.[^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143][^cite_note-226] [High church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_church) Anglicans espouse doctrines that are closer to Roman Catholics, and retain veneration for Mary, such as [Anglican pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) to [Our Lady of Lourdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes), which have taken place since 1963, and [pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) to [Our Lady of Walsingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Walsingham), which have taken place for hundreds of years.[^cite_note-227]

Historically, there has been enough common ground between Roman Catholics and Anglicans on Marian issues that in 2005, a joint statement called *Mary: grace and hope in Christ* was produced through ecumenical meetings of Anglicans and Roman Catholic theologians. This document, informally known as the "Seattle Statement", is not formally endorsed by either the Catholic Church or the Anglican Communion, but is viewed by its authors as the beginning of a joint understanding of Mary.[^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143][^cite_note-228]

##### Methodist

Further information: [Saints in Methodism § Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_in_Methodism#Virgin_Mary)

Methodists do not have any additional teachings on the Virgin Mary except from what is mentioned in Scripture and the ecumenical Creeds. As such, Methodists generally accept the doctrine of the virgin birth, but reject the doctrine of the Immaculate Conception.[^cite_note-229] [John Wesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wesley), the principal founder of the Methodist movement within the Church of England, believed that Mary "continued a [pure and unspotted virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_virginity_of_Mary)", thus upholding the doctrine of the perpetual virginity of Mary.[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] Contemporary Methodism holds that Mary was a virgin before, during, and immediately after the birth of Christ.[^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233] In addition, some Methodists also hold the doctrine of the [Assumption of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary) as a pious opinion.[^cite_note-234]

#### Nontrinitarian

[Nontrinitarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nontrinitarians), such as [Unitarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism), [Christadelphians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christadelphians), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), and [Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement)[^cite_note-235] also acknowledge Mary as the biological mother of Jesus Christ, but most reject any immaculate conception and do not recognize Marian titles such as "Mother of God". The [Latter Day Saint movement's view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_views_on_Mary) affirms the virgin birth of Jesus[^cite_note-236] and Christ's divinity, but only as a separate being than [God the Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father). The [Book of Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Mormon) refers to Mary by name in prophecies and describes her as "most beautiful and fair above all other virgins"[^cite_note-237] and as a "precious and chosen vessel".[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]

In nontrinitarian groups that are also [Christian mortalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mortalism), Mary is not seen as an intercessor between humankind and Jesus, whom mortalists would consider "asleep", awaiting resurrection.[^cite_note-240]

### Jewish

The issue of the parentage of [Jesus in the Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_in_the_Talmud) also affects Jewish views of Mary. However, the Talmud does not mention Mary by name, and is considerate rather than only polemic.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242] The story about [Panthera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthera_\(Jesus's_father\)) is also found in the *[Toledot Yeshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledot_Yeshu)*, the literary origins of which can not be traced with any certainty, and given that it is unlikely to go before the 4th century, the time is too late to include authentic remembrances of Jesus.[^cite_note-243] *The Blackwell Companion to Jesus* states that the *Toledot Yeshu* has no historical facts and was perhaps created as a tool for warding off conversions to Christianity.[^cite_note-244] The tales from the *Toledot Yeshu* did impart a negative picture of Mary to ordinary Jewish readers.[^cite_note-Remensnyder-2014-245] The circulation of the *Toledot Yeshu* was widespread among European and Middle Eastern Jewish communities since the 9th century.[^cite_note-246] The name Panthera may be a distortion of the term *parthenos* ("virgin") and [Raymond E. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_E._Brown) considers the story of Panthera a fanciful explanation of the birth of Jesus that includes very little historical evidence.[^cite_note-247] [Robert Van Voorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Van_Voorst) states that because *Toledot Yeshu* is a medieval document with its lack of a fixed form and orientation towards a popular audience, it is "most unlikely" to have reliable historical information.[^cite_note-248] Stacks of the copies of the Talmud were burnt upon a court order after the [1240 Disputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputation_of_Paris) for allegedly containing material defaming the character of Mary.[^cite_note-Remensnyder-2014-245]

### Islamic

Main article: [Mary in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_in_Islam)



[Persian miniature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_miniature) of Mary and Jesus

The Virgin Mary holds a singularly exalted place in [Islam](6037917.md), and she is considered by the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) to have been the greatest woman in the history of humankind. The Islamic scripture recounts the Divine Promise given to Mary as being: "O Mary! Surely Allah has selected you, purified you, and chosen you over all women of the world." ([3\:42](https://quran.com/3?startingVerse=42))

Mary is often referred to by Muslims by the honorific title *Sayedetina* ("Our Lady"). She is mentioned in the Quran as the daughter of Imran.[^cite_note-Glasse-2003-249]

Moreover, Mary is the only woman named in the Quran and she is mentioned or referred to in the scripture a total of 50 times.[^cite_note-250] Mary holds a singularly distinguished and honored position among [women in the Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_figures_in_the_Quran). A *[sura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sura)* (chapter) in the Quran is titled "[Maryam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_\(sura\))" (Mary), the only *sura* in the Quran named after a woman, in which the story of Mary (Maryam) and Jesus (Isa) is recounted according to the view of [Jesus in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_in_Islam).[^cite_note-Jestice-2004-18]

#### Birth

In a narration of [hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith) from Imam [Ja'far al-Sadiq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja'far_al-Sadiq), he mentions that [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah) revealed to [Imran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim#In_Islamic_tradition), "I will grant you a boy, blessed, one who will cure the blind and the leper and one who will raise the dead by My permission. And I will send him as an apostle to the Children of Israel." Then Imran related the story to his wife, [Hannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_in_Islam), the mother of Mary. When she became pregnant, she conceived it was a boy, but when she gave birth to a girl, she stated "Oh my Lord! Verily I have delivered a female, and the male is not like the female, for a girl will not be a prophet," to which [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah) replies in the Quran, "Allah knows better what has been delivered" (3\:36). When Allah bestowed Jesus to Mary, he fulfilled his promise to Imran.[^cite_note-251]

#### Motherhood



Islamic [miniature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_miniature) of Maryam in labor shaking a date palm, with Isa and hidden voice below

Mary was declared (uniquely along with Jesus) to be a "Sign of God" to humanity;[^cite_note-252] as one who "guarded her chastity";[^cite_note-qref|66|12|b=y-58] an "obedient one";[^cite_note-qref|66|12|b=y-58] and dedicated by her mother to Allah whilst still in the womb;[^cite_note-qref|3|36|b=y-59] uniquely (amongst women) "Accepted into service by God";[^cite_note-qref|3|37|b=y-253] cared for by (one of the prophets according to Islam) [Zakariya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_view_of_Zechariah) (Zacharias);[^cite_note-qref|3|37|b=y-253] that in her childhood she resided in the Temple and uniquely had access to Al-[Mihrab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihrab) (understood to be the [Holy of Holies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_of_Holies)), and was provided with heavenly "provisions" by God.[^cite_note-qref|3|37|b=y-253][^cite_note-Glasse-2003-249]

Mary is also called a "Chosen One";[^cite_note-qref|3|42|b=y-254] a "Purified One";[^cite_note-qref|3|42|b=y-254] a "Truthful one";[^cite_note-255] her child conceived through "a Word from God";[^cite_note-256] and "chosen you above the women of the worlds (the material and heavenly worlds)".[^cite_note-qref|3|42|b=y-254]

The Quran relates detailed narrative accounts of Maryam (Mary) in two places, [3\:35-47](https://quran.com/3?startingVerse=35) and [19\:16-34](https://quran.com/19?startingVerse=16). These state beliefs in both the Immaculate Conception of Mary and the virgin birth of Jesus.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259] The account given in [Surah Maryam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_\(surah\)) [19](https://quran.com/19?startingVerse=) is nearly identical with that in the Gospel according to [Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_the_Evangelist), and both of these (Luke, Sura 19) begin with an account of the visitation of an angel upon Zakariya (Zecharias) and "Good News of the birth of Yahya (John)", followed by the account of the annunciation. It mentions how Mary was informed by an angel that she would become the mother of Jesus through the actions of God alone.[^cite_note-260]

In the Islamic tradition, Mary and Jesus were the only children who could not be touched by Satan at the moment of their birth, for God imposed a veil between them and Satan.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] According to the author [Shabbir Akhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabbir_Akhtar), the Islamic perspective on Mary's Immaculate Conception is compatible with the Catholic doctrine of the same topic.&#x20;

> "O [People of the Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_the_Book)! Do not go to extremes regarding your faith; say nothing about Allah except the truth. The Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger of Allah and the fulfilment of His Word through Mary and a spirit ˹created by a command˺ from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers and do not say, "Trinity." Stop!—for your own good. Allah is only One God. Glory be to Him! He is far above having a son! To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

— [Surah An-Nisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-Nisa) [4\:171](https://quran.com/4?startingVerse=171)[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264]

The Quran says that Jesus was the result of a virgin birth. The most detailed account of the annunciation and birth of Jesus is provided in Suras 3 and 19 of the Quran, where it is written that God sent an angel to announce that she could shortly expect to bear a son, despite being a virgin.[^cite_note-265]

### Druze faith



The icon of [Saidet et Tallé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saidet_et_Tall%C3%A9), also known as "the Virgin of the Druze", is venerated by both the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) and [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) communities in Lebanon.

[^cite_note-Bowman-2012-19]

The [Druze faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_faith) holds the Virgin Mary, known as *Sayyida Maryam*, in high regard.[^cite_note-Makdisi-2000-141] Although the [Druze religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_religion) is distinct from mainstream [Islam](6037917.md) and [Christianity](5211.md), it incorporates elements from both and honors many of their figures, including the Virgin Mary.[^cite_note-Makdisi-2000-141] The Druze revere Mary as a holy and pure figure, embodying virtue and piety.[^cite_note-Dana-2008-266][^cite_note-Makdisi-2000-141] She is respected not only for her role as the mother of Messiah Jesus but also for her spiritual purity and dedication to God.[^cite_note-Dana-2008-266][^cite_note-Makdisi-2000-141] In regions where [Druze and Christians coexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_Druze), such as parts of [Lebanon](17771.md), [Syria](7515849.md) and [Israel](9282173.md), the [veneration of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_devotions) often reflects a blend of traditions.[^cite_note-Munro-2019-267] Shared pilgrimage sites and mutual respect for places like the [Church of Saidet et Tallé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saidet_et_Tall%C3%A9) in [Deir el Qamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el_Qamar),[^cite_note-268] the [Our Lady of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lebanon) shrine in [Harrisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harissa-Daraoun), the [Our Lady of Saidnaya Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Saidnaya_Monastery) in [Saidnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saidnaya), and the [Stella Maris Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Maris_Monastery) in [Haifa](55606.md) exemplify this.[^cite_note-Munro-2019-267]

Historical records and writings by authors like Pierre-Marie Martin and Glenn Bowman show that Druze leaders and community members have historically shown deep reverence for [Marian sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrines_to_the_Virgin_Mary).[^cite_note-Bowman-2012-19] They often sought her intercession before battles or during times of need, demonstrating a cultural and spiritual integration of Marian veneration into their religious practices.[^cite_note-Bowman-2012-19]

### Bahá'í Faith

The [Bahá'í Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'%C3%AD_Faith) venerates Mary as the mother of Jesus. The *[Kitáb-i-Íqán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit%C3%A1b-i-%C3%8Dq%C3%A1n)*, the primary theological work of the Bahá'í religion, describes Mary as "that most beauteous countenance," and "that veiled and immortal Countenance." The Bahá'í writings claim Jesus Christ was "conceived of the Holy Ghost"[^cite_note-269] and assert that in the [Bahá'í Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'%C3%AD_Faith) "the reality of the mystery of the Immaculacy of the Virgin Mary is confessed."[^cite_note-270]

### Biblical scholars

The statement found in [Matthew 1\:25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_1\:25) that [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph) did not have sexual relations with Mary before she gave birth to Jesus has been debated among scholars, with some saying that she did not remain a virgin and some saying that she was a perpetual virgin.[^cite_note-271] Other scholars contend that the Greek word *heos* ("until") denotes a state up to a point, but does not mean that the state ended after that point, and that Matthew 1\:25 does not confirm or deny the virginity of Mary after the birth of Jesus.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273][^cite_note-274] According to Biblical scholar [Bart Ehrman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_Ehrman), the Hebrew word *[almah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almah)*, meaning young woman of childbearing age, was translated into Greek as *parthenos*, which often, though not always, refers to a young woman who has never had sexual intercourse. In Isaiah 7\:14, it is commonly believed by Christians to be the prophecy of the Virgin Mary referred to in Matthew 1\:23.[^cite_note-275] While Matthew and Luke give differing versions of the virgin birth, John quotes the uninitiated Philip and the disbelieving Jews gathered at Galilee referring to Joseph as Jesus' father.[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279]

Other biblical verses have also been debated; for example, the reference made by Paul the Apostle that Jesus was made "of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1\:3)[^cite_note-280] meaning that he was a descendant of David through Joseph.[^cite_note-281]

### Pre-Christian Rome

From the early stages of Christianity, belief in the virginity of Mary and the virgin conception of Jesus, as stated in the gospels, holy and supernatural, was used by detractors, both political and religious, as a topic for discussions, debates, and writings, specifically aimed to challenge the divinity of Jesus and thus Christians and Christianity alike.[^cite_note-282] In the 2nd century, as part of his [anti-Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Christian) polemic *[The True Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_True_Word)*, the pagan philosopher [Celsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsus) contended that Jesus was actually the illegitimate son of a Roman soldier named [Panthera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius_Julius_Abdes_Pantera).[^cite_note-283] The Church Father [Origen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origen) dismissed this assertion as a complete fabrication in his apologetic treatise *[Against Celsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contra_Celsum)*.[^cite_note-284] How far Celsus sourced his view from Jewish sources remains a subject of discussion.[^cite_note-285]

## Christian devotions

Main article: [Marian devotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_devotions)

### History

#### 2nd century

[Justin Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Martyr) was among the first to draw a [parallel between Eve and Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Eve). This derives from his comparison of Adam and Jesus. In his *[Dialogue with Trypho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue_with_Trypho)*, written sometime between 155 and 167,[^cite_note-286] he explains:

> He became man by the Virgin, in order that the disobedience which proceeded from the serpent might receive its destruction in the same manner in which it derived its origin. For Eve, who was a virgin and undefiled, having conceived the word of the serpent, brought forth disobedience and death. But the Virgin Mary received faith and joy, when the angel Gabriel announced the good tidings to her that the Spirit of the Lord would come upon her, and the power of the Highest would overshadow her: wherefore also the Holy Thing begotten of her is the Son of God; and she replied, 'Be it unto me according to thy word." And by her has He been born, to whom we have proved so many scriptures refer, and by whom God destroys both the serpent and those angels and men who are like him; but works deliverance from death to those who repent of their wickedness and believe upon Him.[^cite_note-287]

It is possible that the teaching of Mary as the New Eve was part of the apostolic tradition rather than merely Justin Martyr's own creation, according to [Serafim Seppälä](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serafim_Sepp%C3%A4l%C3%A4).[^cite_note-288] [Irenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus), [bishop of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Lyon), also takes up this parallel, in *Against Heresies*, written about the year 182:[^cite_note-289]

> In accordance with this design, Mary the Virgin is found obedient, saying, "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to your word." Luke 1\:38 But Eve was disobedient; for she did not obey when as yet she was a virgin. ... having become disobedient, was made the cause of death, both to herself and to the entire human race; so also did Mary, having a man betrothed \[to her], and being nevertheless a virgin, by yielding obedience, become the cause of salvation, both to herself and the whole human race. And on this account does the law term a woman betrothed to a man, the wife of him who had betrothed her, although she was as yet a virgin; thus indicating the back-reference from Mary to Eve,...For the Lord, having been born "the First-begotten of the dead," Revelation 1\:5 and receiving into His bosom the ancient fathers, has regenerated them into the life of God, He having been made Himself the beginning of those that live, as Adam became the beginning of those who die. 1 Corinthians 15\:20–22 Wherefore also Luke, commencing the genealogy with the Lord, carried it back to Adam, indicating that it was He who regenerated them into the Gospel of life, and not they Him. And thus also it was that the knot of Eve's disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary. For what the virgin Eve had bound fast through unbelief, this did the virgin Mary set free through faith.[^cite_note-290]

During the second century, the Gospel of James was also written. According to [Stephen J. Shoemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_J._Shoemaker), "its interest in Mary as a figure in her own right and its reverence for her sacred purity mark the beginnings of Marian piety within early Christianity".[^cite_note-291]

#### 3rd to 5th centuries

During the Age of Martyrs and at the latest in the fourth century, the majority of the most essential ideas of Marian devotion already appeared in some form – in the writings of the [Church Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Fathers), apocrypha and visual arts. The lack of sources makes it unclear whether the devotion to Mary played a role in liturgical use during the first centuries of Christianity.[^cite_note-292] In the 4th century, Marian devotion in a liturgical context becomes evident.[^cite_note-293]

The earliest known Marian prayer (the *[Sub tuum praesidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_tuum_praesidium)*, or *Beneath Thy Protection*) is from the 3rd century (perhaps 270), and its text was rediscovered in 1917 on a papyrus in Egypt.[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295] According to some sources, [Theonas of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Theonas_of_Alexandria) consecrated one of the first holy places dedicated to Mary during the late 3rd century. An even earlier place has been found in Nazareth, dated to the previous century by some scholars.[^cite_note-296] Following the [Edict of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Milan) in 313, by the 5th century artistic images of Mary began to appear in public and larger churches were being dedicated to Mary, such as the [Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_Maggiore) in Rome.[^cite_note-297][^cite_note-298][^cite_note-299] At the [Council of Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ephesus) in 431, Mary was officially declared the *[Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos)*, meaning "God-bearer"[^cite_note-300] or "Mother of God". The term had possibly been used for centuries[^cite_note-301] or at least since the early 300s, when it seems to have already been in established use.[^cite_note-302]

The Council of Ephesus was long thought to have been held at a church in Ephesus which had been dedicated to Mary about a hundred years before.[^cite_note-303][^cite_note-304][^cite_note-305] Though, recent archeological surveys indicate that St. Mary's Church in Ephesus did not exist at the time of the Council or, at least, the building was not dedicated to Mary before 500.[^cite_note-306] The [Church of the Seat of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Seat_of_Mary) in [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea) was built shortly after the introduction of Marian liturgy at the council of Ephesus, in 456, by a widow named Ikelia.[^cite_note-307]

According to the 4th-century heresiologist [Epiphanius of Salamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis), the Virgin Mary was worshipped as a [mother goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_goddess) in the Christian sect of [Collyridianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collyridianism), which was found throughout Arabia sometime during the 300s AD. Collyridianism had women performing priestly acts, and made bread offerings to the Virgin Mary. The group was condemned as heretical by the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) and was preached against by [Epiphanius of Salamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis), who wrote about the group in his writings titled [Panarion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panarion).[^cite_note-308]

#### Byzantium

During the era of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), Mary was venerated as the virginal Mother of God and as an intercessor.[^cite_note-309]

[Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephesus) is a cultic centre of Mary, the site of the first church dedicated to her and the rumoured place of her death. Ephesus was previously a centre for worship of [Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis), a virgin goddess; the [Temple of Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Artemis) there is regarded as one of the [Seven Wonders of the Ancient World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Wonders_of_the_Ancient_World). The cult of Mary was furthered by [Queen Theodora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodora_\(wife_of_Justinian_I\)) in the 6th century.[^cite_note-310][^cite_note-311] According to William E. Phipps, in the book *Survivals of Roman Religion*,[^cite_note-312] "Gordon Laing argues convincingly that the worship of Artemis as both virgin and mother at the grand Ephesian temple contributed to the veneration of Mary."[^cite_note-313]

#### Middle Ages



*[Madonna of humility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_of_humility)* by [Fra Angelico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Angelico), c. 1430. A traditional depiction of Mary wearing blue clothes.

The Middle Ages saw many legends about Mary, her parents, and even her grandparents.[^cite_note-314] Mary's popularity increased dramatically from the 12th century,[^cite_note-315] linked to the Roman Catholic Church's designation of Mary as [Mediatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediatrix).[^cite_note-316][^cite_note-317]

#### Post-Reformation



*Our Lady of Sorrows* by [Giovanni Battista Salvi da Sassoferrato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Salvi_da_Sassoferrato), 17th century

Over the centuries, devotion and veneration to Mary has varied greatly among Christian traditions. For instance, while Protestants show scant attention to Marian prayers or devotions, of all the saints whom the Orthodox venerate, the most honored is Mary, who is considered "more honorable than the [Cherubim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherubim) and more glorious than the [Seraphim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraphim)".[^cite_note-Fairbairn-2002-38]

Orthodox theologian [Sergei Bulgakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Bulgakov) wrote: "Love and veneration for the Virgin is the soul of Orthodox piety. A faith in Christ which does not include \[...] His mother is another faith, another Christianity from that of the Orthodox Church."[^cite_note-Bulgakov-1997-189]

Although the Catholics and the Orthodox may honor and venerate Mary, they do not view her as divine, nor do they worship her. Roman Catholics view Mary as subordinate to Christ, but uniquely so, in that she is seen as above all other creatures.[^cite_note-318] Similarly, Bulgakov wrote that the Orthodox view Mary as "superior to all created beings" and "ceaselessly pray for her intercession". However, she is not considered a "substitute for the One Mediator" who is Christ.[^cite_note-Bulgakov-1997-189] "Let Mary be in honor, but let worship be given to the Lord", he wrote.[^cite_note-319] Similarly, Catholics do not worship Mary as a divine being, but rather "hyper-venerate" her. In Roman Catholic theology, the term *[hyperdulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration)* is reserved for Marian veneration, *[latria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latria)* for the worship of God, and *[dulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration)* for the veneration of other saints and angels.[^cite_note-320] The definition of the three level hierarchy of *latria*, *hyperdulia* and *dulia* goes back to the [Second Council of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Nicaea) in 787.[^cite_note-321]

Devotions to artistic depictions of Mary vary among Christian traditions. There is a long tradition of [Catholic Marian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Marian_art) and no image permeates [Catholic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_art) as does the image of [Madonna and Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_Child).[^cite_note-322] The icon of the Virgin *Theotokos* with Christ is, without doubt, the most venerated icon in the Orthodox Church.[^cite_note-323] Both Roman Catholic and Orthodox Christians venerate images and icons of Mary, given that the [Second Council of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Nicaea) in 787 permitted their veneration with the understanding that those who venerate the image are venerating the reality of the person it represents,[^cite_note-324] and the 842 Synod of Constantinople confirming the same.[^cite_note-325] According to Orthodox piety and traditional practice, however, believers ought to pray before and venerate only flat, two-dimensional icons, and not three-dimensional statues.[^cite_note-326]

The Anglican position towards Mary is in general more conciliatory than that of Protestants at large and in a book he wrote about praying with the icons of Mary, [Rowan Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan_Williams), former [archbishop of Canterbury](2345.md), said: "It is not only that we cannot understand Mary without seeing her as pointing to Christ; we cannot understand Christ without seeing his attention to Mary."[^cite_note-Schroedel-2006-143][^cite_note-327]

On 4 September 1781, 11 families of *[pobladores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Pobladores)* arrived from the Gulf of California and established a city in the name of [King Carlos III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain). The small town was named *El Pueblo de Nuestra Señora de los Ángeles de la Porciúncula* (after our Lady of the Angels), a city that today is known simply as [Los Angeles](18110.md). In an attempt to revive the custom of religious processions within the [Archdiocese of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Los_Angeles), in September 2011 the [Queen of Angels Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Angels_Foundation), and founder Mark Anchor Albert, inaugurated an annual Grand Marian Procession in the heart of [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles)' historic core. This yearly procession, held on the last Saturday of August and intended to coincide with the anniversary of the founding of the City of Los Angeles, begins at the [Cathedral of Our Lady of the Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Angels) and concludes at the parish of [La Iglesia de Nuestra Señora Reina de los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Iglesia_de_Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_Reina_de_los_Angeles) which is part of the [Los Angeles Plaza Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Plaza_Historic_District), better known as "La Placita".[^cite_note-328][^cite_note-329]

### Feasts

Main article: [Marian feast days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_feast_days)

The earliest feasts that relate to Mary grew out of the cycle of feasts that celebrated the [Nativity of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus). Given that according to the Gospel of Luke (Luke 2\:22–40),[^cite_note-330] 40 days after the birth of Jesus, along with the [Presentation of Jesus at the Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_Jesus_at_the_Temple), Mary was purified according to Jewish customs. The Feast of the Purification began to be celebrated by the 5th century, and became the "Feast of [Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_\(Gospel_of_Luke\))" in [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium).[^cite_note-Clayton-2003-331]



Village decorations during the *[Feast of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Assumption)* in [Għaxaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7axaq), Malta

In the 7th and 8th centuries, four more Marian feasts were established in [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity). In the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity), a feast dedicated to Mary, just before Christmas was celebrated in the Churches of [Milan](36511.md) and [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna) in Italy in the 7th century. The four Roman Marian feasts of Purification, Annunciation, Assumption and Nativity of Mary were gradually and sporadically introduced into England by the 11th century.[^cite_note-Clayton-2003-331]

Over time, the number and nature of feasts (and the associated [Titles of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titles_of_Mary)) and the venerative practices that accompany them have varied a great deal among diverse Christian traditions. Overall, there are significantly more titles, feasts and venerative Marian practices among [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics) than any other Christians traditions.[^cite_note-Flinn-2007-142] Some such feasts relate to specific events, such as the Feast of [Our Lady of Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Victory), based on the 1571 victory of the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States) in the [Battle of Lepanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lepanto_\(1571\)).[^cite_note-332][^cite_note-333]

Differences in feasts may also originate from doctrinal issues—the [Feast of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Assumption) is such an example. Given that there is no agreement among all Christians on the circumstances of the death, [Dormition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Theotokos) or Assumption of Mary, the feast of assumption is celebrated among some denominations and not others.[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2003-37][^cite_note-334] While the Roman Catholic Church celebrates the Feast of the Assumption on 15 August, some [Eastern Catholics](214903.md) celebrate it as [Dormition of the *Theotokos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Mother_of_God), and may do so on 28 August, if they follow the [Julian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar). The [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) also celebrate it as the Dormition of the *Theotokos*, one of their 12 [Great Feasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Feasts_of_the_Orthodox_Church). Protestants do not celebrate this, or any other Marian feasts.[^cite_note-Hillerbrand-2003-37]

### Relics

The veneration of Marian [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relics) used to be common practice before the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation). It was later largely surpassed by the veneration of Marian images.

#### Bodily relics

As Mary's body is believed by most Christians to have been [taken up into the glory of heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary), her bodily relics have been limited to hair, nails and [breast milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing_Madonna).

According to [John Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Calvin)'s 1543 *[Treatise on Relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise_on_Relics)*, her hair was exposed for veneration in several churches, including in [Rome](25458.md), [Saint-Flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Flour), [Cluny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluny) and [Nevers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevers).[^cite_note-335]

In this book, Calvin criticized the veneration of the Holy Milk due to the lack of biblical references to it and the doubts about the veracity of such relics:

> With regard to the milk, there is not perhaps a town, a convent, or nunnery, where it is not shown in large or small quantities. Indeed, had the Virgin been a wet-nurse her whole life, or a dairy, she could not have produced more than is shown as hers in various parts. How they obtained all this milk they do not say, and it is superfluous here to remark that there is no foundation in the Gospels for these foolish and blasphemous extravagances.

Although the veneration of Marian bodily relics is no longer a common practice today, there are some remaining traces of it, such as the [Chapel of the Milk Grotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_of_the_Milk_Grotto) in [Bethlehem](4312.md), named after Mary's milk.

#### Clothes



Late His Eminence Meletios Barnabas, Former [Syriac Orthodox Arch Bishop of Homs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Archdiocese_of_Homs), with the [Holy Girdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Girdle) of the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary)

Clothes which are believed to have belonged to Mary include the [Cincture of the Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincture_of_the_Theotokos) kept in the [Vatopedi monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatopedi_monastery) and her [Holy Girdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Girdle) kept in [Holy Belt Church, Homs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary_Church_of_the_Holy_Belt).

Other relics are said to have been collected during later [Marian apparitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_apparitions), such as her robe, veil, and part of her belt which were kept in [Blachernae church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St._Mary_of_Blachernae_\(Istanbul\)) in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) after she appeared there during the 10th century. These relics, now lost, are celebrated by the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) and [Byzantine Catholic Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Catholic_Church) as the [Intercession of the Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercession_of_the_Theotokos).

Few other objects are said to have been touched or given by Mary during [apparitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_apparitions), notably a 1531 image printed on a *[tilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilm%C3%A0tli)*, known as [Our Lady of Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe), belonging to [Juan Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Diego).

#### Places



The chapel based on the claimed [House of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Mary) in Ephesus

Places where Mary is believed to have lived include the [Basilica della Santa Casa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_della_Santa_Casa) in [Loreto, Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loreto,_Marche), and the [House of the Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Virgin_Mary) in [Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephesus).

[Eastern Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christians) believe that she died and was put in the [Tomb of the Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Virgin_Mary) near [Jerusalem](16043.md) before the [Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary).

The belief that Mary's house was in [Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephesus) is recent, as it was claimed in the 19th century based on the visions of [Anne Catherine Emmerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Catherine_Emmerich), an [Augustinian nun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustinian_nun) in Germany.[^cite_note-336][^cite_note-337] It has since been named as the [House of the Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Virgin_Mary) by [Roman Catholic pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage) who consider it the place where Mary lived until her assumption.[^cite_note-338][^cite_note-339][^cite_note-340][^cite_note-341] The Gospel of John states that Mary went to live with the [Disciple whom Jesus loved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disciple_whom_Jesus_loved),[^cite_note-342] [traditionally identified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disciple_whom_Jesus_loved#Identity) as [John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Evangelist)[^cite_note-343] and [John the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Apostle). [Irenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus) and [Eusebius of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea) wrote in their histories that John later went to Ephesus, which may provide the basis for the early belief that Mary also lived in Ephesus with John.[^cite_note-344][^cite_note-345]

The apparition of [Our Lady of the Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Pillar) in the first century was believed to be a [bilocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilocation), as it occurred in [Spain](26667.md) while Mary was living in Ephesus or Jerusalem. The pillar on which she was standing during the apparition is believed to be kept in the [Cathedral-Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral-Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Pillar) in [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza) and is therefore venerated as a relic.

## In arts

### Iconography

See also: [Madonna (art)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_\(art\)) and [Marian art in the Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_art_in_the_Catholic_Church)

In paintings, Mary is traditionally portrayed in [blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_blue). This tradition can trace its origin to the Byzantine Empire, from c. 500 AD, where blue was "the colour of an empress". A more practical explanation for the use of this colour is that in Medieval and Renaissance Europe, the blue pigment was derived from the rock [lapis lazuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapis_lazuli), a stone of greater value than gold, which was imported from Afghanistan. Beyond a painter's retainer, patrons were expected to purchase any gold or lapis lazuli to be used in the painting. Hence, it was an expression of devotion and glorification to swathe the Virgin in gowns of blue. Transformations in visual depictions of Mary from the 13th to 15th centuries mirror her "social" standing within the Church and in society.[^cite_note-346]

Traditional representations of Mary include the [crucifixion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus) scene, called *[Stabat Mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabat_Mater_\(art\))*.[^cite_note-347][^cite_note-348] While not recorded in the Gospel accounts, Mary cradling the dead body of her son is a common motif in art, called a "[pietà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet%C3%A0)" or "pity".

In the Egyptian, Eritrean, and Ethiopian tradition, Mary has been portrayed in story and paint for centuries.[^cite_note-349] Beginning in the 1600s, however, highland Ethiopians began portraying Mary performing a variety of miracles for the faithful, including paintings of her giving water to a thirsty dog, healing monks with her breast milk, and saving a man eaten by a crocodile.[^cite_note-350] Over 1,000 such [stories](https://pemm.princeton.edu/stories) about her exist in this tradition, and about one hundred of those have hundreds of paintings each, in various manuscripts, adding up to thousands of [paintings](https://pemm.princeton.edu/paintings).[^cite_note-351]
