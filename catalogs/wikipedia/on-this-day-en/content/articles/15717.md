[Jordan](7515964.md) has a population of more than 11.1 million inhabitants as of 2023.[^cite_note-2] Jordanians ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): أردنيون) are the citizens of [Jordan](7515964.md). Approximately 98% of Jordanians are [Arabs](2185.md), many of whom are of Palestinian origin.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The remaining 2% belong to ethnic minorities, including [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Jordan), [Chechens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechens_in_Jordan), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Jordan), [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Jordan) and [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_Jordan).[^cite_note-autogenerated32-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] In early 2016 about 30% of the population were non-citizens, a figure including refugees, and legal and illegal immigrants.[^cite_note-2015census2-8] Jordan's annual population growth rate stands at 3.05% as of 2023, with an average birth rate of 2.8. There were 1,977,534 households in Jordan in 2015, with an average of 4.8 persons per household.[^cite_note-2015census2-8]

The [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) is [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language), while [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is the second most widely spoken language by Jordanians. It is also widely used in commerce and government. In 2016, about 84% of Jordan's population live in urban towns and cities.[^cite_note-autogenerated32-5] Many Jordanians and people of Jordanian descent live across the world, mainly in the Gulf Cooperation Council Countries, United States, Canada and Turkey.[^cite_note-9]

In 2016, Jordan was named as the largest refugee hosting country per capita in the world, followed by Turkey, Pakistan and Lebanon.[^cite_note-10] Jordan hosts refugees mainly from Palestine, Syria, and Iraq, as well as smaller communities from other nations. There are also hundreds of thousands of workers from Egypt, Indonesia and South Asia, who work as domestic and construction workers.

## Definition

The territory of Jordan can be defined by the history of its creation following events after the end of [World War I](4764461.md), the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) and redrawing of the borders of the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean) littoral. The ensuing decisions, most notably the [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement), which created the [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine). In September 1922, [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan) was formally identified as a subdivision of the Mandate Palestine after the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) approved the British [Transjordan memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_memorandum) which stated that the Mandate east of the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River) would be excluded from all the provisions dealing with Jewish settlement west of the Jordan River.[^cite_note-11]

### Refugees

Further information: [Iraqis in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqis_in_Jordan), [Syrians in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrians_in_Jordan), and [Palestinians in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians_in_Jordan)

Jordan is a home to 2,175,491 registered Palestinian refugees.[^cite_note-wwwj2-16] Out of those 2,175,491 refugees, 634,182 have not been given Jordanian citizenship.[^cite_note-17] Jordan also hosts around 1.4 million [Syrian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Refugees) who fled to the country due to the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md) since 2011. About 31,163 Yemenis and 22,700 Libyan refugees live in Jordan as of January 2015.[^cite_note-2015census2-8] There are thousands of [Lebanese](17771.md) refugees who came to [Jordan](7515964.md) when [civil strife and war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) and the [2006 war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) broke out in their native country. Up to 1 million [Iraqis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqis_in_Jordan) came to Jordan following the [Iraq War](5043324.md) in 2003.[^cite_note-18] In 2015, their number was 130,911. About 2,500 Iraqi [Mandaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans) refugees have been resettled in Jordan.

### Arab

Arab Jordanians are either descended from families and clans who were living in the cities and towns in [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_\(region\)) prior to Jordanian independence in 1946, most notably in the governorates of [Jerash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerash_Governorate), [Ajlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajloun_Governorate), [Balqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balqa_Governorate), [Irbid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irbid_Governorate), [Madaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaba), [Al Karak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Karak), [Aqaba](328399.md), [Amman](45007.md) and some other towns in the country, or from the [Palestinian](23267.md) families who settled in Jordan in different times in the 20th century, mostly during and after the wars of [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_exodus) and [1967](29328.md). Many Christians are natives especially in towns such as [Fuhies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuheis), [Madaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaba), [Al Karak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Karak), [Ajlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajloun), or have Bedouin origins, and a significant number came in 1948 and 1967 mainly from [Jerusalem](16043.md), [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa), [Lydda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod), [Bethlehem](4312.md), and other Palestinian cities.



Bedouin Arab men from Jordan

#### Druze

The Druze people are believed to constitute about 0.5% of the total population of Jordan, around 32,000 people.[^cite_note-32] The Druze, who refer to themselves as al-Muwahhideen, or "believers in one God," are concentrated in the rural, mountainous areas west and north of [Amman](45007.md). Even though the faith originally developed out of [Ismaili Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili_Islam), most [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) do not identify as [Muslims](19541.md),[^cite_note-38] and they do not accept the [five pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pillars_of_Islam).[^cite_note-39]

#### Bedouin Arabs

The other group of Jordanians is descended from [Bedouins](61770.md), of which less than 1% live a nomadic lifestyle. Bedouin settlements are concentrated in the south and east of the country.

### Afro-Jordanians

Main article: [Afro-Jordanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Jordanians)

An unknown but considerable number of Jordanians are of African descent.

### Armenians

Main article: [Armenians in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Jordan)

There were an estimated 5,000 [Armenians](387816.md) living within the country in 2009.[^cite_note-looklex-40] An estimated 4,500 of these are members of the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church),[^cite_note-looklex-40] and predominantly speak the [Western dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenian)[^cite_note-41] of the [Armenian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language). This population makes up the majority of non-[Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) Christians in the country.[^cite_note-42]

### Assyrians

Main article: [Assyrians in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Jordan)

There is an Assyrian refugee population in Jordan. Many Assyrians have arrived in Jordan as refugees since the invasion of Iraq, making up a large part of the Iraqi refugees.

### Turks

Main article: [Turks in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Jordan)

There are people of Turkish ancestry living in Jordan. These people have had a thriving presence in Jordan since the rule of the Ottoman Empire. Today, there is a minority of about 60,000 people in the country who are the descendants of the Ottoman-Turkish immigrants. As of 2009, there are also 8,262 Turkish citizens who are recent migrants to Jordan.

### Circassians

Main article: [Circassians in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Jordan)

By the end of the 19th century, the Ottoman Authorities directed the Circassian immigrants to settle in Jordan. The Circassians are Sunni Muslims and are estimated to number 100,000 to 170,000 people.

### Chechens

Main article: [Chechens in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechens_in_Jordan)

There are about 10,000 Chechens estimated to reside in Jordan.

### Doms

Main article: [Doms in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doms_in_Jordan)

There are 70,000 [Dom people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_people) in Jordan.[^cite_note-43]

### Genetics

Bahri et al. (2011) observed that the Jordanians have a genetic profile that is Arabian Semitic, despite the succession of several civilizations in Jordan. They have a common origin in Mesopotamia and are not too genetically dissimilar from the peoples of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) and [North Africa](21714.md), who respectively have a common origin in Arabia and North Africa.[^cite_note-44]

Zanetti et al. (2014) discovered significant genetic differentiation between general Jordanians and Bedouin Arabs. General Jordanians were more similar to other Middle Eastern populations whilst Bedouin Arabs were more similar to North Africans. However, the Bedouin Arabs played a significant role in the "peopling" of Jordan, both in the past and present.[^cite_note-45]

## Health and education

Main articles: [Health in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Jordan) and [Education in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Jordan)

Jordan prides itself on its health services, which is considered some of the best in the region.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-47] Qualified medics, favourable investment climate and Jordan's stability have contributed to the success of this sector.[^cite_note-48]

Jordan has a very advanced education system. The school education system comprises 2 years of pre-school education, 10 years of compulsory basic education, and two years of secondary academic or vocational education, after which the students sit for the General Certificate of Secondary Education Exam ([Tawjihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawjihi)).[^cite_note-49] Scholars may attend either private or public schools.

Access to higher education is open to holders of the General Secondary Education Certificate, who can then choose between private Community Colleges, public Community Colleges or universities (public and private). The credit-hour system, which entitles students to select courses according to a study plan, is implemented at universities. The number of public universities has reached (10), besides (17) universities that are private, and (51) community colleges. Numbers of universities accompanied by significant increase in number of students enrolled to study in these universities, where the number of enrolled students in both public and private universities is estimated at nearly (236) thousand; (28) thousand out of the total are from Arab or foreign nationalities.[^cite_note-50]
