The **Sikorsky SH-60/MH-60 Seahawk** (or **Sea Hawk**) is a twin [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) engine, multi-mission [United States Navy](20518076.md) [helicopter](8286923.md) based on the [United States Army](32087.md) [UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Black_Hawk) and a member of the [Sikorsky S-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-70) family. The most significant modifications are the folding main rotor blades and a hinged tail to reduce its footprint aboard ships.

The U.S. Navy acquired H-60 helicopters under the model designations **SH-60B**, **SH-60F**, **HH-60H**, **MH-60R**, and **MH-60S**. Able to deploy aboard any air-capable [frigate](11464.md), [destroyer](8779.md), [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser), [fast combat support ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_combat_support_ship), [expeditionary transfer dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_transfer_dock), [amphibious assault ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship), [littoral combat ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship) or [aircraft carrier](2219.md), the Seahawk can handle [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) (ASW), [anti-surface warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-surface_warfare) (ASUW), naval special warfare (NSW) insertion, [search and rescue](172599.md) (SAR), [combat search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_search_and_rescue) (CSAR), [vertical replenishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_replenishment) (VERTREP), and [medical evacuation](146346.md) (MEDEVAC). When entering service, the SH-60B was too large to operate from some of the smaller vessels in service, so it served along with the [Kaman SH-2F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_SH-2_Seasprite) and [SH-2G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_SH-2G_Super_Seasprite) models until 2001.

Early model Seahawks began to be retired in the 2010s and 2020s, with the last B model leaving U.S. Navy service in 2015, after over three decades, then the F and H models followed in 2016. These were replaced by the upgraded MH-60R and S models.

## Design and development

### Origins

In the 1970s, the U.S. Navy began looking for a new helicopter to replace the [Kaman SH-2 Seasprite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_SH-2_Seasprite).[^cite_note-Leoni_p203-3] The SH-2 Seasprite was used by the Navy as its platform for the [Light Airborne Multi-Purpose System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Airborne_Multi-Purpose_System) (LAMPS) Mark I avionics suite for maritime warfare and a secondary search and rescue capability. Advances in sensor and avionic technology lead to the LAMPS Mk II suite being developed by the Naval Air Development Center. In 1974, the Navy conducted a competition to develop the Lamps MK III concept, which would integrate both the aircraft and shipboard systems. The Navy selected IBM Federal Systems as the Prime systems integrator for the Lamps MK III concept.

Since the SH-2 was not large enough to carry the Navy's required equipment, a new airframe was required. In the mid-1970s, the Army evaluated the [Sikorsky YUH-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_UH-60_Black_Hawk) and [Boeing Vertol YUH-61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Vertol_YUH-61) for its Utility Tactical Transport Aircraft System (UTTAS) competition.[^cite_note-Vect_S-70B-4] The Navy based its requirements on the Army's UTTAS specification, to decrease costs from commonality as the new airframe to carry the Lamps MK III avionics.[^cite_note-Leoni_p203-3] In April 1977, Sikorsky and Boeing-Vertol submitted proposals for Navy variants of their Army UTTAS helicopters for review. The Navy also looked at helicopters being produced by [Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Helicopter), [Kaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_Aircraft), [Westland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Aircraft) and [MBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt-B%C3%B6lkow-Blohm), but these were too small for the mission. In early 1978, the Navy selected Sikorsky's S-70B design,[^cite_note-Leoni_p203-3] which was designated "SH-60B Seahawk".

### SH-60B Seahawk

IBM was the prime systems integrator for the Lamps MK III with Sikorsky as the airframe manufacturer. The SH-60B maintained 83% commonality with the UH-60A.[^cite_note-5] The main changes were corrosion protection, more powerful T700 engines, single-stage [oleo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleo_strut) main landing gear, removal of the left side door, adding two weapon pylons, and shifting the tail landing gear 13 feet (3.96 m) forward to reduce the footprint for shipboard landing. Other changes included larger fuel cells, an electric blade folding system, folding horizontal stabilators for storage, and adding a 25-tube pneumatic [sonobuoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonobuoy) launcher on the left side.[^cite_note-Leoni_p206-6]

An emergency flotation system was originally installed in the stub wing fairings of the main landing gear. It was found to be impractical and possibly impeded emergency egress, and was removed.[^cite_note-\:12-7] Five YSH-60B Seahawk LAMPS III prototypes were ordered. The first YSH-60B flight occurred on 12 December 1979. The first production SH-60B made its first flight on 11 February 1983. The SH-60B entered operational service in 1984, with its first operational deployment in 1985.[^cite_note-Vect_S-70B-4]

The SH-60B is deployed primarily aboard [frigates](11464.md), [destroyers](8779.md), and [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser). The primary missions of the SH-60B are surface warfare and anti-submarine warfare. It carries a complex system of sensors, including a towed [magnetic anomaly detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_anomaly_detector) (MAD) and air-launched sonobuoys. Other sensors include the APS-124 search radar, ALQ-142 ESM system and optional nose-mounted [forward looking infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_looking_infrared) (FLIR) turret. Munitions carried include the [Mk 46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_46_torpedo), [Mk 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_50_torpedo), or [Mark 54 Lightweight Torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_54_Lightweight_Torpedo), [AGM-114 Hellfire](38151.md) missile, and a single cabin-door-mounted [M60D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M60D)/[M240](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M240_machine_gun) 7.62 mm (0.30 in) machine gun, or [GAU-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAU-16) .50 in (12.7 mm) machine gun.

A standard crew for a SH-60B is one pilot, one ATO/Co-Pilot (Airborne Tactical Officer), and an enlisted aviation warfare systems operator (sensor operator). The U.S. Navy operated the SH-60B in Helicopter Anti-Submarine Squadron, Light (HSL) squadrons. All HSL squadrons were redesignated Helicopter Maritime Strike (HSM) squadrons, and transitioned to the MH-60R between 2006 and 2015.

The SH-60J is based on the SH-60B airframe for the [Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force](2370744.md) without IBM avionics. The SH-60K is a modified variant of the SH-60J. The [SH-60J and SH-60K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_H-60#SH-60J) are built under license by [Mitsubishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Heavy_Industries) in Japan.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### SH-60F



A Seahawk hovers during a simulated casualty evacuation as [MARSOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARSOC) operators carry a stretcher.

After the SH-60B entered service,[^cite_note-Leoni_p211-10] the Navy conducted a competition to replace the [Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_SH-3_Sea_King). The competitors were Sikorsky, Kaman and IBM (avionics only). Sikorsky began development of this variant in March 1985. In January 1986, seven SH-60Fs were ordered including two prototypes (BuNos 163282/3).[^cite_note-11] The first example flew on 19 March 1987.[^cite_note-Donald_p158-12] The SH-60F was based on the SH-60B airframe, but with upgraded SH-3H avionics.[^cite_note-\:12-7]

The SH-60F primarily served as the carrier battle group's primary [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) (ASW) aircraft. The helicopter hunted submarines with its [AQS-13F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/AQS-13) dipping [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar), and carried a six-tube sonobuoy launcher. The SH-60F is unofficially named "Oceanhawk".[^cite_note-Donald_p158-12] The SH-60F can carry [Mk 46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_46_torpedo), [Mk 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_50_torpedo), or [Mk 54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_54_lightweight_torpedo) [torpedoes](31424.md) for its offensive weapons, and it has a choice of [fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage)-mounted machine guns, including the M60D, M240D, and GAU-16 (.50 caliber or 12.7 mm) for self-defense. The standard aircrew is one pilot, one co-pilot, one tactical sensor operator (TSO), and one [acoustic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_signature) sensor operator (ASO). The SH-60F was operated by the U.S. Navy's Helicopter Antisubmarine (HS) squadrons, until they were redesignated Helicopter Sea Combat (HSC) squadrons and transitioned to the MH-60S. The last HS squadron completed its transition in 2016.

### HH-60H



A hovering HH-60H helicopter operated by HSC-84 during fast-rope training in Iraq, 2010

The HH-60H was developed in conjunction with the US Coast Guard's [HH-60J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HH-60_Jayhawk), beginning in September 1986 with a contract for the first five helicopters with Sikorsky as the prime contractor. The variant's first flight occurred on 17 August 1988. Deliveries of the HH-60H began in 1989. The variant earned initial operating capability in April 1990 and was deployed to Desert Storm with HCS-4 and HCS-5 in 1991.[^cite_note-Donald_p158-12] The HH-60H's official DoD and Sikorsky name is *Seahawk*, though it has been called "Rescue Hawk".[^cite_note-13]

Based on the SH-60F, the HH-60H is the primary combat search and rescue (CSAR), naval special warfare (NSW) and anti-surface warfare (ASUW) helicopter. It carries various defensive and offensive sensors. These include a [FLIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FLIR) turret with laser designator, and the Aircraft Survival Equipment (ASE) package including the [ALQ-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALQ-144) [Infrared Jammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRCM), AVR-2 Laser Detectors, APR-39(V)2 Radar Detectors, AAR-47 Missile Launch Detectors and [ALE-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALE-47) chaff/flare dispensers. Engine exhaust deflectors provide infrared thermal reduction, reducing the threat of heat-seeking missiles. The HH-60H can carry up to four AGM-114 Hellfire missiles on an extended wing using the M299 launcher, and a variety of mountable guns including M60D, M240, GAU-16 and [GAU-17/A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minigun) machine guns.

The HH-60H's standard crew is a pilot, a copilot, an enlisted crew chief, and two door gunners or one rescue swimmer. Originally operated by [HCS-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_Attack_Squadron_\(Light\)_Five) and [HCS-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSC-84) (later HSC-84), these two special USNR squadrons were established with the primary mission of Naval Special Warfare and Combat Search and Rescue (CSAR). Due to SOCOM budget issues, the squadrons were deactivated in 2006 and 2016 respectively. The HH-60H was also operated by Helicopter Antisubmarine (HS) squadrons, with a standard dispersal of six F-models and two or three H-models, before the transition of HS squadrons to HSC squadrons equipped with the MH-60S, the last of which completed its transition in 2016. The only squadron equipped with the HH-60H as of 2016 is [HSC-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSC-85), one of only two remaining USNR helicopter squadrons, the other being HSM-60 equipped with the MH-60R. In Iraq, HH-60Hs were used by the Navy, assisting the Army, for [MEDEVAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEDEVAC) purposes and special operations missions.

### MH-60R



An MH-60R Seahawk conducts sonar operations.

The MH-60R "Romeo" was originally known as "LAMPS Mark III Block II Upgrade" when development began in 1993 with Lockheed Martin, formerly IBM/Loral. Two SH-60Bs were converted by Sikorsky, the first of which made its maiden flight on 22 December 1999. Designated YSH-60R, they were delivered to [NAS Patuxent River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Patuxent_River) in 2001 for flight testing. The production variant was redesignated MH-60R to match its multi-mission capability.[^cite_note-14] The MH-60R was deployed by the US Navy in 2006.[^cite_note-15]

The MH-60R is designed to combine the features of the SH-60B and SH-60F.[^cite_note-16] Its avionics includes dual controls and instead of the complex array of dials and gauges in Bravo and Foxtrot aircraft, 4 fully integrated 8 in × 10 in (200 mm × 250 mm) [night vision goggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_vision_goggle)-compatible and [sunlight-readable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_brightness_monitor) color [multi-function displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-function_display), all part of the [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit) produced by Owego Helo Systems division of [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin).[^cite_note-avionics-17] Onboard sensors include: AAR-47 Missile Approach Warning System by ATK,[^cite_note-avionics-17] [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon) AAS-44 electro-optical system that integrates FLIR and [laser rangefinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_rangefinder),[^cite_note-avionics-17] ALE-39 decoy dispenser and ALQ-144 infrared jammer by [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems),[^cite_note-avionics-17] ALQ-210 electronic support measures system by Lockheed Martin,[^cite_note-avionics-17] APS-147 multi-mode radar/IFF interrogator, which during a mid-life technology insertion project is replaced by APS-153 Multi-Mode Radar with Automatic Radar Periscope Detection and Discrimination (ARPDD) capability.[^cite_note-18]



MH-60R of the Indian Navy dispensing counter-measure flares at Operational Demonstration 2025

Both radars were developed by [Telephonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephonics_Corporation),[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-avionics-17] a more advanced AN/AQS-22 advanced airborne low-frequency sonar (ALFS) jointly developed by Raytheon & [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group),[^cite_note-avionics-17] an ARC-210 voice radio by [Rockwell Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_Collins),[^cite_note-avionics-17] an advanced airborne fleet [data link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_link) SRQ-4 Hawklink with radio terminal set ARQ-59 radio terminal, both by [L3Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L3Harris_Technologies),[^cite_note-SRQ4a-20][^cite_note-SRQ4b-21][^cite_note-ARQ59-22] and LN-100G dual-embedded global positioning system and [inertial navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system) by [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman) Litton division.[^cite_note-avionics-17] Beginning in 2020, CAE's MAD-XR were fielded on MH-60Rs, providing it with a magnetic anomaly detector.[^cite_note-23]

Offensive capabilities are improved by the addition of new Mk-54 [air-launched torpedoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-launched_torpedo) and Hellfire missiles. All Helicopter Anti-Submarine Light (HSL) squadrons that receive the MH-60R are redesignated Helicopter Maritime Strike (HSM) squadrons.[^cite_note-Airscoop-24]

### MH-60S



An MH-60S lifting humanitarian supplies from the deck of [USNS *Comfort*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Comfort).

In 1997, the Navy decided to replace its venerable [CH-46 Sea Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Vertol_CH-46_Sea_Knight) helicopters. In 1998, the Navy awarded a production contract to Sikorsky for the CH-60S, after sea demonstrations with a converted UH-60. The variant first flew on 27 January 2000 and it began flight testing later that year. The CH-60S was redesignated MH-60S in February 2001 to reflect its planned multi-mission use.[^cite_note-Donald_p159-60-25] The MH-60S is based on the [UH-60L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_UH-60_Black_Hawk) and has many naval SH-60 features.[^cite_note-Donald_160-61-26] Unlike all other Navy H-60s, the MH-60S is not based on the original S-70B/SH-60B platform, with its forward-mounted twin tail-gear and single starboard sliding cabin door. Instead, the S-model is a hybrid, featuring the main fuselage of the S-70A/UH-60, with large sliding doors on both sides of the cabin and a single aft-mounted tail wheel, and the folding tail pylon, engines, drivetrain and rotors of the S-70B/SH-60.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Donald_160-61-26] It includes the integrated glass cockpit developed by Lockheed Martin for the MH-60R and shares some of the same avionics/weapons systems.

It is deployed aboard aircraft carriers, [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship), Maritime Sealift Command ships, and [fast combat support ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_combat_support_ship). Its missions include vertical replenishment, medical evacuation, combat search and rescue, anti-surface warfare, maritime interdiction, close air support, intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, and special warfare support. The MH-60S is to deploy with the [AQS-20A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AQS-20A) Mine Detection System and an Airborne Laser Mine Detection System (ALMDS) for identifying submerged objects in coastal waters.



A US Navy technician inside an MH-60S cockpit

It is the first US Navy helicopter to field a [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit), relaying flight information via four digital monitors. The primary means of defense is door-mounted machine guns such as the M60D, M240D, or GAU-17/A. A "batwing" Armed Helo Kit based on the Army's UH-60L was developed to accommodate Hellfire missiles, Hydra 70 mm (2.75 inch) rockets, or larger guns. The MH-60S can be equipped with a nose-mounted forward looking infrared (FLIR) turret to be used in conjunction with Hellfire missiles. It carries the ALQ-144 Infrared Jammer.

The MH-60S is unofficially known as the "Knighthawk", referring to the preceding Sea Knight, though "Seahawk" is its official DoD name.[^cite_note-navy-sh-60-28][^cite_note-29] A standard crew for the MH-60S is one pilot, one copilot and two tactical aircrewmen, depending on the mission. With the retirement of the Sea Knight, the squadron designation of Helicopter Combat Support Squadron (HC) was retired from the Navy. Operating MH-60S squadrons were re-designated Helicopter Sea Combat Squadron (HSC).[^cite_note-Airscoop-24] The MH-60S was to be used for mine clearing from [littoral combat ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship), but testing found it lacks the power to safely tow the detection equipment.[^cite_note-30]

In August 2014, the U.S. Navy forward deployed the Airborne Laser Mine Detection System (ALMDS) to the [U.S. 5th Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._5th_Fleet). The ALMDS is a sensor system designed to detect, classify, and localize floating and near-surface moored mines in littoral zones, straits, and choke points. The system is operated from an MH-60S, which gives it a countermine role traditionally handled by the [MH-53E Sea Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-53E_Sea_Dragon), allowing smaller ships that the MH-53E cannot operate from, to be used in the role. The ALMDS beams a laser into the water to pick up reflections from things it bounces off of, then uses that data to produce a video image for ground personnel to determine if the object is a mine.[^cite_note-31]

The MH-60S will utilize the [BAE Systems Archerfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archerfish_expendable_mine_neutraliser) [remotely operated vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remotely_operated_vehicle) (ROV) to seek out and destroy naval mines from the air. Selected as a concept in 2003 by the Navy as part of the Airborne Mine Neutralization System (AMNS) program and developed since 2007, the Archerfish is dropped into the water from its launch cradle, where its human operator remotely guides it down towards the mine using a [fiber optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_optics) communications cable that leads back up to the helicopter. Using sonar and low-light video, it locates the mine, and is then instructed to shoot a [shaped charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaped_charge) explosive to detonate it. In April 2016, BAE was awarded a contract to build and deliver the ROVs.[^cite_note-32]

## Operational history

### U.S. Navy

The Navy received the first production SH-60B in February 1983 and assigned it to squadron [HSL-41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSM-41).[^cite_note-Donald_p156-7-33][^cite_note-34] The helicopter entered service in 1984,[^cite_note-35] and began its first deployment in 1985.[^cite_note-Donald_p156-7-33]

During the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in 1991, Seahawks saw extensive service in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). SH-60Bs operating from frigates, destroyers, and cruisers conducted radar and electronic surveillance across the northern Gulf as part of the coalition's anti-surface warfare campaign, locating and tracking Iraqi naval vessels and monitoring activity around offshore oil platforms.[^cite_note-Proceedings1991-36] On 24 January 1991, an SH-60B from [USS *Curts* (FFG-38)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Curts) provided airborne cover as U.S. Navy SEALs and U.S. Army helicopter teams operating from [USS *Curts* (FFG-38)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Curts), [USS *Nicholas* (FFG-47)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Nicholas_\(FFG-47\)), and [USS *Leftwich* (DD-984)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Leftwich) seized Iraqi prisoners on and around [Qaruh Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaruh_Island), the first Kuwaiti territory recaptured during the war.[^cite_note-Marolda-37] During the [Battle of Bubiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bubiyan), SH-60Bs located and maintained surveillance on Iraqi vessels attempting to flee toward Iran and vectored British [Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Lynx) helicopters in for attacks.[^cite_note-Proceedings1991-36] In later engagements, an SH-60B from [USS *Paul F. Foster* (DD-964)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Paul_F._Foster) directed the Kuwaiti missile boat *Istiqlal* onto an Iraqi patrol boat on 16 February 1991, and two days later an SH-60B from [USS *Jarrett* (FFG-33)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Jarrett) directed two Army [OH-58Ds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OH-58_Kiowa) against a [Silkworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworm_\(missile\)) missile site on [Faylaka Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faylaka_Island).[^cite_note-DoDGulf-38]



A MH-60R prepares to land aboard [USS *John C. Stennis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_John_C._Stennis).

The SH-60F entered operational service on 22 June 1989 with Helicopter Antisubmarine Squadron 10 (HS-10) at NAS North Island.[^cite_note-Donald_p159-60-25] SH-60F squadrons planned to shift from the SH-60F to the MH-60S from 2005 to 2011 and were to be redesignated Helicopter Sea Combat (HSC).

As one of the two squadrons in the US Navy dedicated to Naval Special Warfare support and combat search and rescue, the HCS-5 Firehawks squadron deployed to Iraq for Operation Iraqi Freedom in March 2003. The squadron completed 900 combat air missions and over 1,700 combat flight hours. The majority of their flights in the Iraqi theater supported special operations ground forces missions.



An MH-60R firing a live [Hellfire missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellfire_missile)

A west coast Fleet Replacement Squadron (FRS), Helicopter Maritime Strike Squadron (HSM) 41, received the MH-60R aircraft in December 2005 and began training the first set of pilots. In 2007, the R-model successfully underwent final testing for incorporation into the fleet. In August 2008, the first 11 combat-ready Romeos arrived at [HSM-71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSM-71), a squadron assigned to the carrier [*John C. Stennis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_John_C._Stennis). The primary missions of the MH-60R are anti-surface and anti-submarine warfare. According to [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), "secondary missions include search and rescue, vertical replenishment, naval surface fire support, logistics support, personnel transport, medical evacuation and communications and data relay."[^cite_note-39]

HSL squadrons in the US have been incrementally transitioning to the MH-60R and have nearly completed the transition. The first MH-60Rs in Japan arrived in October 2012. The recipient was [HSM-51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSM-51), the Navy's forward–deployed LAMPS squadron, home based in Atsugi, Japan. The Warlords transitioned from the SH-60B throughout 2013, and shifted each detachment to the new aircraft as they returned from deployments. HSM-51 will have all MH-60R aircraft at the end of 2013. The Warlords are joined by the Saberhawks of HSM-77.



A MH-60S deploying search and rescue swimmers.

On 23 July 2013, Sikorsky delivered the 400th MH-60, an MH-60R, to the U.S. Navy. This included 166 MH-60R variants and 234 MH-60S variants. The MH-60S is in production until 2015 and will total a fleet of 275 aircraft, and the MH-60R is in production until 2017 and will total a fleet of 291 aircraft. The two models have flown 660,000 flight hours. Seahawk helicopters are to remain in Navy service into the 2030s.[^cite_note-40]

The SH-60B Seahawk completed its last active-duty deployment for the U.S. Navy in late April 2015 after a seven-month deployment aboard [USS *Gary*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Gary_\(FFG-51\)). After 32 years and over 3.6 million hours of service, the SH-60B was formally retired from U.S. Navy service during a ceremony on 11 May 2015 at [Naval Air Station North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_North_Island).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] In late November 2015 [USS *Theodore Roosevelt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Theodore_Roosevelt_\(CVN-71\)) returned from its deployment, ending the last active-duty operational deployment of both the SH-60F and HH-60H. The models are to be transferred to other squadrons or placed in storage.[^cite_note-43]

### Indian Navy

See also: [Sikorsky S-70 § India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-70#India)

On 17 February 2011, the [Indian Defence Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) rejected a US offer for the direct acquisition of 16 [MH-60R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-60R) Romeo helicopters through the [Foreign Military Sales](30857635.md) (FMS) route. Instead, the [Indian Navy](604522.md) continued with competitive bidding for its 16 Multi Role Helicopter (MRH) tender. This left behind only two contenders — [Sikorsky S-70B Seahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-70) and [NHIndustries NH90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHIndustries_NH90) — for the MRH [tender](36571608.md) launched in September 2008 which intended to replace the [Sea King Mk 42B/C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Sea_King#India) fleet. This required the helicopter for both [anti-submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) and [anti-surface warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-surface_warfare) missions. This was the second attempt for the MRH programme after the 2006 tender was cancelled.[^cite_note-44] Following trials that lasted until late 2011,[^cite_note-45] the S-70B was selected in the commercial bidding stage.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-janes.com-47] However, in June 2017, India's Ministry of Defence terminated the procurement programme, since the Cost Negotiation Committee (CNC) had been in a deadlock with Sikorsky since 2014 and even after [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) took over the firm.[^cite_note-India_scraps_planned_acquisition2-48]

Thereafter, in August 2018, India's [Defence Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) approved the purchase of 24 multirole helicopters.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-2018-08-27_DN-50] As of November 2018, [The Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Indian_Express) reported that India has sought the multirole MH-60R from the US and has sent a [letter of request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_Request) in that case. The procurement is expected to cost \$2 billion.[^cite_note-51] In April 2019, the US [Defense Security Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Security_Cooperation_Agency) (DSCA) approved the sale of 24 MH-60R anti-submarine helicopters through the FMS route to India for US\$2.6 billion and notified Congress of the proposed sale.[^cite_note-dsca.mil-52]

The [Cabinet Committee on Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security) (CCS), chaired by the [Prime Minister of India](24452.md), cleared the deal for 26 MH-60R Romeo helicopters on 19 February 2020. This was a week ahead of the visit of [Donald Trump](4848272.md), the then [POTUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POTUS), to India. The programme will act as a stopgap measure until the Navy's requirement of 123 helicopters under the [Naval Multi Role Helicopter (NMRH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_Indian_Navy#Aircraft) is fulfilled.[^cite_note-53] India signed a \$2.12 billion (₹15,157 crore) deal with the US on 26 February. Six of the aircraft will be delivered in 2021 with the rest delivered within two years.[^cite_note-54] These helicopters are to aid in detecting and destroying enemy submarines prowling in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR).[^cite_note-news18.com-55]

On 16 July 2021, the U.S. Navy transferred the first two MH-60Rs to the Indian Navy at [Naval Air Station North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_North_Island), [San Diego](28504.md). The US Navy will be responsible for capability upgrades and sustainment during Indian Navy operations.[^cite_note-indiatoday.in-56] In December 2023, it has reported that the 6th helicopter was delivered to the Navy.[^cite_note-\:3-57]

The MH-60Rs have been integrated with the Navy's aircraft carrier [INS *Vikrant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Vikrant_\(2013\)). The new helicopters can be used for rescue operations, especially in night search and rescue missions, as they have night vision goggles and forward-looking infrared facilities. They participated in the [Exercise Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_\(naval_exercise\))-2024 held by the Navy.[^cite_note-\:0-58][^cite_note-\:1-59][^cite_note-\:2-60]

On 6 March 2024, the Indian Defence Minister [Rajnath Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajnath_Singh) commissioned the [INAS 334 Naval Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INAS_334) at [INS Garuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Garuda), [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi) under commanding officer [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(Indian_Navy\)) M Abhishek Ram.[^cite_note-\:0-58][^cite_note-\:1-59][^cite_note-\:2-60][^cite_note-\:5-61]

On 24 August 2024, DSCA approved a possible order worth \$52.8 million for sonobuoys and related equipment. The deal includes the sale of 3 types of High Altitude Anti-Submarine Warfare (HAASW) sonobuoys including AN/SSQ-53G/O[^cite_note-62], AN/SSQ-62F[^cite_note-63], and AN/SSQ-36[^cite_note-64] sonobuoys may be a part of the deal. The sonobuoys are to be integrated onboard MH-60R for ASW operations.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The Indian Navy is planning to acquire over 500 anti-submarine sonobuoys for the MH-60 fleet.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] On 6 October 2024, DSCA further cleared the possible sale of 53 aircraft-launched [MK 54 MOD 0 Lightweight Torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_54_lightweight_torpedo) all up rounds for its MH-60R fleet and associated equipment and support, including “in-country torpedo training”.[^cite_note-70]

As per Ministry of Defence reports on 26 December 2024, the first nine helicopters were delivered and put into service on the fleet ships. The helicopters took part in exercises like [MALABAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_\(naval_exercise\)), SIMBEX and [MILAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_\(naval_exercise\)) 24 in the year.[^cite_note-\:4-71]

On 28 November 2025, India and the US signed the Letters of Order and Acceptance (LOA), worth ₹7,995 crore (equivalent to ₹83 billion or US\$860 million in 2026), for the sustainment support the Indian Navy's MH-60R fleet for five years.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Over 20 of the helicopters were expected to be in service as of then.[^cite_note-\:6-74] The rest would be delivered by the end of 2026.[^cite_note-\:7-75] The Indian Navy's second squadron of MH-60R, [INAS 335](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INAS_335), was commissioned on 17 December 2025.[^cite_note-\:8-76][^cite_note-77]

Three delivered in 2021.[^cite_note-\:9-78] 6th delivered in December 2023.[^cite_note-MH-60-79] 10 delivered as of March 2025. Rest to be delivered by 2026-end.[^cite_note-\:7-75] 13 and 15 had been delivered as of June and July 2025.[^cite_note-\:10-80]

As per an 11 June 2026 report, the Navy has received 21 MH-60R helicopters of which 15 are operational, three are being used for training personnel in the U.S. and three are undergoing scheduled India-specific upgradation and certification before deployment. The ones in service will also be sent to the U.S. in rotation for the same progressive upgrades which includes integration of an indigenous electronic warfare (EW) suite, secure communication systems and weapons on the platform.[^cite_note-\:11-81] The 22nd MH-60R was delivered in [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi) in the second week of July 2026. The final two are expected to arrive by 18 July.[^cite_note-\:13-82]

- **Length:** 64 ft 8 in (19.71 m)

- **Height:** 17 ft 2 in (5.23 m)

- **Empty weight:** 15,200 lb (6,895 kg)

- **Gross weight:** 17,758 lb (8,055 kg) for ASW mission

- **Max takeoff weight:** 21,884 lb (9,926 kg)

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [General Electric T700-GE-401C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_T700-GE-401C) [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) engines, 1,890 shp (1,410 kW) each for take-off

- **Main rotor diameter:** 53 ft 8 in (16.36 m)

- **Main rotor area:** 2,262.3 sq ft (210.17 m

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 146 kn (168 mph, 270 km/h)

- **Service ceiling:** 12,000 ft (3,700 m)

- **Rate of climb:** 1,650 ft/min (8.4 m/s)
