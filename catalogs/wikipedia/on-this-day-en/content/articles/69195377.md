**Flupyradifurone** is a [systemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_pesticide) [butenolide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butenolide) [insecticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticide) developed by [Bayer CropScience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer) under the name **Sivanto**.[^cite_note-1] Flupyradifurone protects crops from sap-feeding pests such as aphids and is safer for non-target [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism) compared to other insecticides. Sivanto was launched in 2014 since it obtained its first commercial registration in central America ([Guatemala](17238567.md) and [Honduras](13394.md)).[^cite_note-\:0-2] [Insecticide Resistance Action Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticide_Resistance_Action_Committee) (IRAC) classified Flupyradifurone as 4D subset ([butenolide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butenolide)) and it is the first pesticide in the butenolide category.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] It was approved by [European Union](9317.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-5]

## Characteristics



[Aphid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphid)

Flupyradifurone is used to control [sucking pests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemiptera) such as aphids, leafhoppers, psyllids, and whiteflies.[^cite_note-Umetsu2026-6] It is a systemic insecticide that is absorbed by plants and transported through the [xylem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylem). It can be applied on leaves, soil, or seed.[^cite_note-Satpathy2020-7]

Flupyradifurone is structurally related to the natural insecticide, [stemofoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stemofoline?action=edit\&redlink=1) which occurs in the plant species *[Stemona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stemona) [japonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stemona_japonica?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:0-2] Stemofoline has some advantageous features, such as being fast-acting and an effective antifeedant, but does not efficiently bind to insect nicotinic acetylcholine receptors.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Mode of action

Flupyradifurone is an [agonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agonist) of insect [nicotinic acetylcholine receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotinic_acetylcholine_receptor),[^cite_note-\:0-2] causing [depolarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depolarization) of [nerve cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_cell) membranes.[^cite_note-\:1-11] Insects cannot detoxify flupyradifurone according to the research of [CYP6CM1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYP6CM1?action=edit\&redlink=1)-mediated [metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism).[^cite_note-\:0-2] Since flupyradifurone cannot be inactivated by [acetylcholinesterase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylcholinesterase), it will lead to the failure of nerve system of insects and end up with the death of insects.[^cite_note-\:1-11]

## Toxicological and ecotoxicological safety

Using the [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) guidelines to classify [carcinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen) effects, flupyradifurone is categorized as "not likely to be carcinogenic to humans." It is not irritant to humans' [eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye) and [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin),[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-\:2-12] and the EPA is not concerned about [occupational exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_exposure).[^cite_note-\:2-12]

Flupyradifurone is considered a reduced risk insecticide for beneficial insects or other non-target organisms, especially compared to broad-spectrum insecticides such as [pyrethroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrethroids), [neonicotinoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonicotinoids), and [organophosates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organophosates?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-Cornell2021-14] Its toxicity to honey bees is categorized as very low with a contact [LD<sub>50</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_lethal_dose) of > 100µg/bee and generally does not have adverse effects on honey bees or bumble bees.[^cite_note-Umetsu2026-6] Flupyradifurone will bind to the nicotinic acetylcholine receptors of honey bees; however, it will only affect the taste and appetitive learning performance at the highest labeled concentration.[^cite_note-15] However, it is highly toxic to aquatic invertebrates.[^cite_note-16]
