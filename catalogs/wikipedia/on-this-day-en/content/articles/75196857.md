Early parliamentary elections will be held in [Serbia](29265.md) on 25 October 2026 to elect [members](98066.md) of the [National Assembly](619753.md). Originally scheduled to be held by December 2027, [Aleksandar Vučić](13349267.md), the president of Serbia, called the elections earlier in September 2026.

The [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md) (SNS) came to power in 2012 after forming a government with [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia) (SPS). In the [2023 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_parliamentary_election), SNS regained its [parliamentary majority](465640.md). [Protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_election_protests) were briefly organised after the elections due to alleged [electoral fraud](382165.md). [Miloš Vučević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Vu%C4%8Devi%C4%87) became the prime minister of Serbia in May 2024. In the aftermath of the [Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse](78262640.md) in November 2024, the [student-led anti-corruption protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Serbian_anti-corruption_protests) began, while in January 2025, Vučević announced his resignation from office. He was succeeded by [Đuro Macut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90uro_Macut) in April. In 2026, changes to several election laws were adopted by the National Assembly.

SNS nominated Vučić as candidate for Prime Minister and head of the [United Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Serbia_\(Serbia\)) electoral list prior to the elections. The students have announced their participation in the elections as the [Student List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_List), having been endorsed by some parliamentary and extra-parliamentary political parties. [Ilija Srdanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilija_Srdanovi%C4%87) is their ballot holder.

## Background

A [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist) coalition, led by the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md) (SNS), came to power after the [2012 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Serbian_parliamentary_election), along with the [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia) (SPS).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] [Aleksandar Vučić](13349267.md), who initially served as [deputy prime minister of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_prime_minister_of_Serbia) and later as [prime minister of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Serbia),[^cite_note-5] was elected [president of Serbia](774382.md) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Serbian_presidential_election) and re-elected in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Serbian_general_election).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Since he came to power, observers have assessed that Serbia has suffered from [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding) into [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism), followed by a decline in [media freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_freedom_in_Serbia) and [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The [V-Dem Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Institute) has categorised Serbia as an [electoral autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_autocracy) since 2014,[^cite_note-10] while [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) noted in 2024 that SNS "eroded political rights and civil liberties, put pressure on independent media, the opposition, and civil society organisations".[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] On the other hand, supporters highlight the pragmatism of SNS and Vučić, as well as economic growth.[^cite_note-13]

On 17 December 2023, a [snap](462915.md) [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_parliamentary_election) was held in Serbia.[^cite_note-14] The election resulted in a victory of the SNS-led [Serbia Must Not Stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_Must_Not_Stop) electoral alliance that won a majority of seats in the [National Assembly of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Serbia). The second place was obtained by [Serbia Against Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_Against_Violence_\(coalition\)) (SPN), a broad coalition of opposition parties, that won 65 seats in total. The SPS electoral alliance, however, collapsed in popularity and only won 18 seats, its worst result since the [2007 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Serbian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-\:50-15] Despite this, according to non-governmental and monitoring organisations, the election day was marked with [electoral fraud](382165.md) and irregularities such as the [Bulgarian train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_train) and [vote buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_buying).[^cite_note-\:48-16][^cite_note-17] After the election, [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_election_protests) were held up to 30 December, with its organisers, including SPN, calling for the results to be annulled.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-\:103-19] Amidst the protests, a [riot](146704.md) occurred after an unsuccessful attempt from opposition councillors to enter the building of the [City Assembly of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Assembly_of_Belgrade).[^cite_note-20] After several months of negotiations, SNS formed a [new government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Milo%C5%A1_Vu%C4%8Devi%C4%87) on 2 May, with [Miloš Vučević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Vu%C4%8Devi%C4%87), the president of SNS, as prime minister.[^cite_note-\:60-21]

### Student-led protests

Demonstrators on the Slavija Square in Belgrade

Since 2024, student-led protests have been organised in Serbia, with one of their demands being to call a snap parliamentary election

Further information: [2024–present Serbian anti-corruption protests](78597389.md)

In the aftermath of the [Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse](78262640.md) in November 2024, ministers [Goran Vesić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Vesi%C4%87) and [Tomislav Momirović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Momirovi%C4%87) announced their resignation from office.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Their resignation was acknowledged by the National Assembly on 25 November.[^cite_note-24] [Student-led anti-corruption protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Serbian_anti-corruption_protests) also began in November 2024, with one of the demands being the release of Novi Sad railway station canopy documents to the public.[^cite_note-25] At the protest on [Slavija Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavija_Square) on 22 December, it was estimated that 100,000 demonstrators were present at the protest.[^cite_note-26] A major series of [civil disobedience](37073.md) that were promoted as a "[general strike](41138909.md)" also occurred on 24 January 2025.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] At a 15 March protest, it was estimated that over 325,000 demonstrators were present.[^cite_note-29]

After a group of students were physically attacked in Novi Sad, Vučević announced his resignation from office on 28 January.[^cite_note-30] His resignation was acknowledged by the National Assembly on 19 March.[^cite_note-31] [Đuro Macut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90uro_Macut) succeeded Vučević as prime minister on 16 April.[^cite_note-\:9-32] Macut is the third [independent politician](2172378.md) to be nominated for the office of prime minister of Serbia.[^cite_note-33] After his inauguration, the student movement also demanded a snap parliamentary election to be held and announced that they would participate in the upcoming election.[^cite_note-34]

As of 2026, the protests are still being held.[^cite_note-35] The latest largest one was held on 23 May, attended by between 180,000 and 190,000 people, according to the non-governmental organisation Archive of Public Gatherings.[^cite_note-36]

## Electoral system

The 250 members of the National Assembly are elected by [closed-list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-list) [proportional representation](23298.md) from a single nationwide [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency).[^cite_note-\:02-37][^cite_note-\:0-38] Eligible voters vote for [electoral lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_list), on which the candidates of the accepted lists are present. A maximum of 250 candidates could be present on a single electoral list. An electoral list could be submitted by a registered political party, a coalition of political parties, or a citizens' group.[^cite_note-\:0-38] To submit an electoral list, at least 10,000 valid signatures must be collected, though [ethnic minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) parties only need to collect 5,000 signatures to qualify on the ballot.[^cite_note-\:0-38][^cite_note-\:5-39] At least 40% of candidates on electoral lists must be female.[^cite_note-40] The electoral list is submitted by its chosen representative or representatives. An electoral list could be declined, after which those who had submitted can fix the deficiencies in a span of 48 hours, or rejected, if the person is not authorised to nominate candidates. The name and date of the election, names of the electoral lists and its representatives, and information on how to vote are only present on the voting ballot.[^cite_note-\:3-41]

The [Republic Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Electoral_Commission?action=edit\&redlink=1) (RIK), local election commissions, and polling boards oversee the election.[^cite_note-\:3-41] Seats are allocated using the [d'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) with an electoral threshold of 3% of all votes cast, although the threshold is waived for ethnic minority parties.[^cite_note-\:02-37][^cite_note-42] The seats are distributed by dividing the total number of votes received by the electoral list participating in the distribution of seats by each number from one to 250. If two or more electoral lists receive the same quotients on the basis of which the seat is distributed, the electoral list that received the greater number of votes has priority.[^cite_note-\:3-41] Parliamentary seats are awarded to candidates from electoral lists according to their order, starting with the first candidate from an electoral list.[^cite_note-\:0-38]

A parliamentary election is called by the president of Serbia, who also has to announce its date and dissolve the National Assembly in the process.[^cite_note-\:0-38][^cite_note-\:3-41] To vote, a person has to be a citizen and resident of Serbia and at least 18 years old.[^cite_note-\:4-43] For those who live abroad, they are able to vote at diplomatic missions.[^cite_note-\:02-37] At least five days before the election, citizens are notified about the election; citizens receive information about the day and time of the election and the address of the polling station where they can vote.[^cite_note-\:0-38] [Election silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_silence) begins two days before the voting day, and it lasts until the closure of all polling stations.[^cite_note-\:30-44] During the election day, eligible voters could vote from 07\:00 ([UTC+01\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00)) to 20\:00, though if the polling station is opened later than 07\:00, voting is then extended by the amount of time for which the opening of the polling station was delayed.[^cite_note-\:3-41][^cite_note-\:33-45] Voters who are not able to vote at polling stations due to being sick, old, or invalid have to inform their election commission between 7\:00 and 11\:00 so that they could vote on the election day from their home after 11\:00.[^cite_note-\:33-45]

### Election date

By law, the next parliamentary election should take place by 31 December 2027.[^cite_note-\:4-43] The election could be scheduled earlier.[^cite_note-\:1-46] Vučić announced in 2024 that the next parliamentary election and presidential election would be held on the same date in 2027, thus making it a [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_election).[^cite_note-47] Vučević also echoed that elections would not be held prior 2027.[^cite_note-48] However, [Darko Glišić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darko_Gli%C5%A1i%C4%87_\(politician\)), the president of the executive board of SNS, said that there could still be a possibility of holding elections earlier despite the government's plan being to complete the entire four-year mandate.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Political analyst Dragomir Anđelković argued that the next parliamentary election would be only held after the completion of the [Expo 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2027) fair and the expansion of the [Belgrade Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Waterfront) project.[^cite_note-51]

During the student-led anti-corruption protests, Vučić and Vučević suggested holding [snap elections](462915.md) in response to the opposition's call for the formation of a [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_government).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In the aftermath of Vučević's resignation announcement, it was suggested that either snap elections would be called or a new government would be formed.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] In March 2025, Vučić suggested holding elections on 1 or 8 June if a new government was not to be formed by 18 April.[^cite_note-56] However, Vučić nominated [Đuro Macut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90uro_Macut) as Vučević's successor in early April,[^cite_note-57] who succeeded Vučević as prime minister on 16 April.[^cite_note-\:9-32] In December 2025, Vučić suggested that elections could be held in May or December 2026,[^cite_note-58] while beginning in 2026, he suggested that they could be held during [fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn).[^cite_note-59] In August 2026, Vučić said that the elections could be held on either 18 or 25 October;[^cite_note-60] Vučić has to call the elections by 3 or 10 September, respectively, for the elections to be held on those dates.[^cite_note-61] The [Radio Television of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Television_of_Serbia) subsequently announced that the elections will be held on 25 October.[^cite_note-62] Vučić dissolved the National Assembly and scheduled the elections on 9 September for the elections to be held on 25 October.[^cite_note-63]

### Election conditions

After the [election protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_election_protests), the [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted a resolution regarding the [2023 parliamentory election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_parliamentary_election) in February 2024, calling for it to be internationally investigated.[^cite_note-\:12-80] The [Office for Democratic Institutions and Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Democratic_Institutions_and_Human_Rights) (ODIHR) also published a report on elections, concluding that it was marked with numerous irregularities and the abuse of public institutions.[^cite_note-81] In response to ODIHR's recommendation of initiating further changes to improve election conditions, the National Assembly of Serbia formed a working body on improving election conditions in April 2024.[^cite_note-82] At its first session, Nemanja Nenadić, the director of [Transparency Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_Serbia), was elected its chairman.[^cite_note-83] Nenadić announced that the proposed changes would have to be voted by the National Assembly by July 2025.[^cite_note-84] However, in February 2025, opposition parties and non-government organisations, such as Transparency Serbia and CRTA, left the working body on improving election conditions due to obstructions from the government.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

In response to the [2024 Belgrade City Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Belgrade_City_Assembly_election), ODIHR published another report in which it concluded that the election was again marked by irregularities.[^cite_note-87] ODIHR representatives further met with minister [Jelena Žarić Kovačević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelena_%C5%BDari%C4%87_Kova%C4%8Devi%C4%87) and RIK representatives in September 2024.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The representatives of opposition parties boycotted the public hearing of the Committee for Constitutional Issues and Legislation in January 2025, where the amendments of the Law on the Unified Voter List were presented, citing obstruction from the government to improve election conditions and the [voter list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_list).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

In May 2026, the National Assembly adopted changes to several election laws, dubbed Petrašinović's Laws,[^cite_note-92] which were listed on ODIHR recommendations that were published after the 2023 parliamentary election. Despite this, the political opposition and non-governmental organisations criticised the changes. The European Commission welcomed the changes. ODIHR commented on the adoption of the laws, noting that major suggestions that were recommended several years ago still have not been addressed.[^cite_note-93] This was also repeated by the director of Transparency Serbia.[^cite_note-94] Brnabić, however, claims that the government adopted over 70% of ODIHR's recommendations.[^cite_note-95]

## Pre-election activities

### Government parties

Following the 2023 elections, Vučević announced that the idea of the [People's Movement for the State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Movement_for_the_State) (NPZD) would continue to be discussed for the next parliamentary election.[^cite_note-\:6-104] SNS formed a joint alliance, named "Belgrade Tomorrow", in April 2024 for the June 2024 Belgrade City Assembly election; in addition to its partners, the list also included the minor government coalition members [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia) (SPS) and [Alliance of Vojvodina Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Vojvodina_Hungarians) (VMSZ), as well far-right [Serbian Party Oathkeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Party_Oathkeepers) (SSZ) and [Serbian Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Radical_Party) (SRS).[^cite_note-\:16-105] Vučević said that with the formation of the list, NPZD was also effectively formalised.[^cite_note-106] However, Vučić announced in December 2024 that NPZD would be formed in March 2025.[^cite_note-\:19-107] A rally promoting the movement was held on 24 January 2025.[^cite_note-108] NPZD was registered on 28 March and it opened its first offices in September 2025.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] In the same month, in an interview with *[Insajder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insajder)*, minister [Demo Beriša](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_Beri%C5%A1a) suggested that NPZD could participate in the next parliamentary election.[^cite_note-111]

[Radoslav Milojičić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radoslav_Miloji%C4%8Di%C4%87), the president of the [Serbian Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Left_\(2022\)) party, switched his affiliation to SNS in June 2024.[^cite_note-112] [Dragan Marković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_Markovi%C4%87), the president of the JS and its parliamentary group, died on 22 November.[^cite_note-113] [Igor Braunović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Braunovi%C4%87?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was elected on the SPS list in 2023, left SPS and its parliamentary group in February 2025, citing difference on issues such as lithium mining and the anti-corruption protests.[^cite_note-114]

In October 2024, [Željko Veselinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDeljko_Veselinovi%C4%87) and [Đorđo Đorđić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90or%C4%91o_%C4%90or%C4%91i%C4%87) left the [Party of Freedom and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Freedom_and_Justice) (SSP) parliamentary group.[^cite_note-115] They were followed by Sonja Pernat and Irena Živković on 19 November.[^cite_note-\:8-116] Three days later, they formed the "[Movement Sloga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Trade_Unions_of_Serbia_%22Sloga%22) – [Experts Should Have A Say](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Party_\(Serbia\))" parliamentary group.[^cite_note-117] Sloga leader Veselinović also announced the formation of the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_\(Serbia\)) (RP);[^cite_note-\:8-116] they began collecting signatures to form the party in December 2024.[^cite_note-118] In response to the consultations that Sloga took part in April 2026, two MPs of the Sloga parliamentary group announced that they would leave the parliamentary group once the National Assembly is in session.[^cite_note-119] Veselinović went onto form a parliamentary group with the pro-government parties RS and USS soon after.[^cite_note-120] In May, the RP was registered,[^cite_note-121] announcing their participation in the parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-122] Dačić announced in August that the SPS would contest the elections with their traditional partners, JS and Zeleni.[^cite_note-danas_dacic-123]

At a gathering in June 2026, Vučić announced that the electoral list of SNS would bear the name United Serbia.[^cite_note-124] Dačić announced in August that the SPS would contest the elections with their traditional partners, JS and Zeleni.[^cite_note-danas_dacic-123] Subsequently, SPS and SDPS signed a cooperation agreement, stating that they would cooperate in the upcoming parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-125]

### Opposition parties

SPN ceased to exist in April 2024 due to a dispute between its parties on whether to boycott the [2024 Belgrade City Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Belgrade_City_Assembly_election).[^cite_note-126] SSP, [Serbia Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_Centre) (SRCE), and [Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_\(Serbia\)) opted to boycott the election, while the rest of SPN parties took part under the We Choose Belgrade banner.[^cite_note-127] A rift between the SSP and [Movement of Free Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Free_Citizens_\(Serbia\)) (PSG) occurred in the National Assembly in March 2024. The three PSG MPs decided to leave the SSP–PSG parliamentary group and continue as MPs without a parliamentary group.[^cite_note-\:7-128] SSP remained in the charge of their parliamentary group.[^cite_note-129] In the aftermath of the acknowledgment of Vučević's resignation, former members of SPN and NADA presented a plan on the formation of a transitional government. At a press conference on 20 March, [Biljana Đorđević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biljana_%C4%90or%C4%91evi%C4%87), the co-president of the [Green–Left Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green%E2%80%93Left_Front), said that according to their plan, the transitional government would fulfill student demands, organise free and fair elections, and revise the voter list.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

The [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Serbia\)) (DS) elected its new president, [Srđan Milivojević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sr%C4%91an_Milivojevi%C4%87), on 14 December 2024.[^cite_note-132] Milivojević subsequently launched a campaign on consolidating progressive parties; [Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_\(Serbia\)) merged into DS on 27 December.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] The MI–GIN also suffered a split in 2024, with [Branimir Nestorović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branimir_Nestorovi%C4%87) and his allies forming the [We – Power of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_%E2%80%93_Power_of_the_People).[^cite_note-135] Initially, the party acquired 7 MPs, but lost two in July and December 2024, respectively.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] In March 2026, another MP, [Aleksandar Pavić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Pavi%C4%87), left the party due to disagreements with Nestorović.[^cite_note-138]

The [National Democratic Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alternative_\(Serbia\)), led by [Miloš Jovanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Jovanovi%C4%87), has announced their participation in the election.[^cite_note-139] Jovanović suggested that three opposition lists should participate in the election. This includes nationalist and pro-European lists, as well as the student list.[^cite_note-140] In April 2026, [Miroslav Aleksić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Aleksi%C4%87_\(born_1978\)), the president of the [People's Movement of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Movement_of_Serbia), and [Miloš Parandilović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Parandilovi%C4%87), the president of the [New Face of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Face_of_Serbia), suggested that students and opposition parties should participate on a joint electoral list.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

In June 2026, SSP, SRCE, and PSG formed the [Platform for a European Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_for_a_European_Serbia) (PES) coalition.[^cite_note-143] [Dragan Đilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_%C4%90ilas), the president of SSP, announced their participation in the parliamentary elections,[^cite_note-144] but also said that they would cooperate with the [Student List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_List).[^cite_note-\:03-145] PSG left the coalition in September, announcing that it wouldn't participate in the elections and would support the Student List instead.[^cite_note-146]

### Students

Since the start of the student-led anti-corruption protests, some of the opposition parties such as DS have pledged to not participate in the next parliamentary election if the movement forms an electoral list.[^cite_note-147] According to *Danas*, other opposition parties refused to comment until the elections get officially scheduled.[^cite_note-148] Initially, the [Green–Left Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green%E2%80%93Left_Front) (ZLF) expressed support for the student list if the elections are to be held during summer of 2026.[^cite_note-149] Later, it shifted its stance, fully supporting the Student List in the parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-150]

Since 2025, the creation of the Student List has been speculated in the public.[^cite_note-151] In June 2025, a political programme was released by students, in which they presented their conditions for candidates on the Student List. The programme lists the fulfilment of protest demands as one of the goals.[^cite_note-152] In November 2025, the magazine *[Vreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vreme)* published a list of names that were allegedly proposed by the University of Novi Sad for the list.[^cite_note-153] [Vladan Đokić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladan_%C4%90oki%C4%87), the current rector of the University of Belgrade, in an interview with the magazine *[Radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_\(news_magazine\))*, said that his role in the parliamentary or [presidential elections](82928743.md) will be decided by students and the public.[^cite_note-154]

## Campaign

Critics of Vučić alleged that his government has orchestrated violence against political opponents, as well as accusing him of rampant corruption, ties with organised crime, and stifling media freedoms. Additionally, Vučić has faced pressure from the European Union over the homage to the convicted war criminal [Ratko Mladić](22742941.md), who was burried in Serbia.[^cite_note-174] Pavle Kosić of *[Euractiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euractiv)* reports that the campaign is likely to become not only a referendum on Vučić's rule, but also a test of Serbia's political direction and its increasingly strained relationship with the European Union.[^cite_note-175] The government has claimed that the student movement is funded by Western powers to destabilise the country and that it is part of a [colour revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour_revolution).[^cite_note-\:11-176]

Key issues during the campaign have included government accountability, corruption, education, healthcare, environment, and rule of law, according to *[Balkan Insight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Insight)*.[^cite_note-177]

The Bureau for Social Research (BIRODI) announced that it will monitor the campaign on six television channels, [RTS 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTS_1_\(Serbian_TV_channel\)), [Pink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_\(Serbia\)), [Prva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prva_Srpska_Televizija), [Happy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalna_Televizija_Happy), [B92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B92), and [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(TV_channel\)).[^cite_note-178]

### Party campaigns

#### Government

SNS submitted their electoral list on 12 September;[^cite_note-184] RIK proclaimed it a day later.[^cite_note-SNS_list-155] The ballot carrier is Vučić and is followed by Vučević and Brnabić. Their electoral list also features non-SNS members, such as [Aleksandar Šešelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_%C5%A0e%C5%A1elj) and [Vjerica Radeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vjerica_Radeta) from SRS, as well as the government-aligned former student [Miloš Pavlović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Pavlovi%C4%87_\(student\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), and actress [Lidija Vukićević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidija_Vuki%C4%87evi%C4%87).[^cite_note-185] During the campaign, Vučević visited several towns, saying that the SNS electoral list "fights for a free, decent, and safe Serbia" (*se bori za slobodnu, pristojnu i sigurnu Srbiju*).[^cite_note-186] SNS also intends to campaign on issues related to the life standard in Serbia.[^cite_note-187] In September, SNS had put a black container in Kragujevac, where they had showcased alleged violence of the student movement.[^cite_note-188]

SPS submitted their electoral list on the same day as SNS. Their list consists of SPS, JS, Zeleni, and SDPS. Dačić is their ballot carrier.[^cite_note-189] The list was confirmed on 14 September.[^cite_note-SPS_list-156] Besides Dačić, other candidates on the list include [Dalibor Marković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalibor_Markovi%C4%87?action=edit\&redlink=1), the son of late [Dragan Marković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_Markovi%C4%87), [Dušan Bajatović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Bajatovi%C4%87), [Vladan Zagrađanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladan_Zagra%C4%91anin), [Snežana Paunović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sne%C5%BEana_Paunovi%C4%87), [Aleksandar Antić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Anti%C4%87), and [Slavica Đukić Dejanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavica_%C4%90uki%C4%87_Dejanovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-190] Dačić said that his coalition will tour throughout Serbia and will fight for national interests, as well as the interests of the people.[^cite_note-191]

The [Strength of Serbia Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_of_Serbia_Movement), led by [Bogoljub Karić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogoljub_Kari%C4%87), submitted its electoral list on 23 September.[^cite_note-192]

#### Opposition

NADA submitted its electoral list on 18 September, with the slogan "Authentic Right". Jovanović is their ballot holder. In a press conference, Jovanović said that their coalition supports cooperation with the European Union, but opposes Serbia's membership in the organisation.[^cite_note-193] Jovanović also said that SNS voters submitted their signatures for NADA's electoral list.[^cite_note-194] [Aleksandar Pavić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Pavi%C4%87), a member of parliament formerly affiliated with MI–SN, is a candidate on the NADA electoral list.[^cite_note-195] The coalition received support from the academics [Kosta Čavoški](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosta_%C4%8Cavo%C5%A1ki) and [Matija Bećković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matija_Be%C4%87kovi%C4%87), as well as the political analyst Dragomir Anđelković[^cite_note-196] and historian [Bojan Dimitrijević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojan_Dimitrijevi%C4%87_\(historian\)).[^cite_note-197] RIK confirmed their list the same day.[^cite_note-\:13-198] During the campaign, Jovanović said that NADA opposes the legalisation of same-sex marriage,[^cite_note-199] while supporting a referendum on membership of Serbia in the European Union.[^cite_note-200] [Predrag Marsenić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predrag_Marseni%C4%87) of NDSS said that their coalition would, if they cross the electoral threshold, join the government led by the Student List.[^cite_note-201]

PES submitted its electoral list on 19 September under the name "European Serbia". While they had collected signatures, several officials, such as [Borko Stefanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borko_Stefanovi%C4%87), [Jelena Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelena_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Peđa Mitrović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C4%91a_Mitrovi%C4%87?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Mila Popović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mila_Popovi%C4%87?action=edit\&redlink=1), refused to appear on the electoral list, while two members of the SRCE presidency left the party in protest of the party's participation in the elections.[^cite_note-202] Several city boards of SRCE had insisted that their party should support the Student List instead, however, [Zdravko Ponoš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zdravko_Pono%C5%A1), the party's leader, refused to do it.[^cite_note-203] Ponoš said that the National Assembly would lose a pro-European voice without their participation.[^cite_note-204] RIK proclaimed their list on 20 September.[^cite_note-205] Đilas is featured first on the electoral list,[^cite_note-\:14-206] while [Marinika Tepić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinika_Tepi%C4%87) and Ponoš are the ballot holders.[^cite_note-207] Other candidates include [Tatjana Pašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatjana_Pa%C5%A1i%C4%87), [Slobodan Cvejić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Cveji%C4%87), [Vladimir Obradović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Obradovi%C4%87_\(politician\)), and former minister [Jasna Matić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasna_Mati%C4%87). [Goran Ješić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Je%C5%A1i%C4%87) is also featured on the 186th position.[^cite_note-\:14-206] Tepić said that their coalition would campaign on issues related to the European Union.[^cite_note-208] The representatives of PES, Đilas, Ponoš, Tepić, and Ješić, signed a declaration on 24 September, pledging to defeat Vučić and the SNS government from power.[^cite_note-209]

#### Student List



A recreation of *[Pobednik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pobednik)* during a Student List rally

On 9 September, the Student List was officially formalised as a citizens' group (*grupa građana*).[^cite_note-210] They held their first campaign event a day later in Novi Sad, where they presented some of their candidates;[^cite_note-\:10-211] they began collecting signatures on 11 September.[^cite_note-212] The Student List has said that they will campaign on issues related to education, healthcare, judiciary, crime and corruption, and equality.[^cite_note-\:10-211] During the campaign, the Student List reported that three associations with similar names to theirs were registered in the Business Registry Agency.[^cite_note-213] The Student List was submitted to RIK on 14 September;[^cite_note-\:15-214] their ballot holder is [Ilija Srdanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilija_Srdanovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] Other candidates include basketball player [Dejan Bodiroga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejan_Bodiroga), economist [Dejan Šoškić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejan_%C5%A0o%C5%A1ki%C4%87), and sociologist [Jovo Bakić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovo_Baki%C4%87).[^cite_note-\:15-214] RIK confirmed the electoral list on 15 September.[^cite_note-SL_list-157] The Student List received support from DS, ZLF, PSG, POKS, SPO, NPS, NLS, EU, [Kreni-Promeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreni-Promeni), [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Serbia\)), [League of Social Democrats of Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Social_Democrats_of_Vojvodina), [Dveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dveri), and [Enough is Enough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_is_Enough_\(party\)), as well as from various local associations from Serbia and Kosovo.[^cite_note-218]

Beginning on 15 September, the Student List presented its election programme until 19 September.[^cite_note-219] Their programme consists of 11 points, containing issues related to judiciary, lustration, transparency, media capture, departisation, education, housing, and healthcare.[^cite_note-220] The programme calls for an independent judiciary and prosecution service, free media, and government transparency. European Union accession is not central to the programme.[^cite_note-\:11-176] Between 17 and 26 September, students will take part in two bicycle tours across Serbia. The first tour encompasses Vojvodina and Central Serbia, while the second one encompasses Central, Western, and Southern Serbia.[^cite_note-221] During and after the tours, the Student List will organise gatherings, both smaller and larger ones, to promote their programme and the candidates.[^cite_note-222]

During the campaign, Srdanović was featured in negative campaigns conducted by pro-government tabloids, alleging that he was a supporter of Montenegrin independence and that he has close ties to former Montenegrin leader [Milo Đukanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_%C4%90ukanovi%C4%87). According to fact checkers *[Istinomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istinomer?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, *[Raskrikavanje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRIK_\(organisation\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, and *Fake News Tragač*, tabloids [digitally manipulated photographs with artifical intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_and_politics) to portray Srdanović in a negative light.[^cite_note-223] [Darko Glišić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darko_Gli%C5%A1i%C4%87_\(politician\)), the president of the executive board of the SNS, described the Student List as "a bunch of woman-haters" (*skupom mrzitelja žena*).[^cite_note-224]

#### Minority lists

The [Sandžak Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand%C5%BEak_Democratic_Party) (SDP) and Montenegrin Democratic Party (CDP) submitted a joint electoral list on 14 September.[^cite_note-225] A day later, VMSZ submitted their electoral list, with [Bálint Pásztor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1lint_P%C3%A1sztor) as their ballot carrier.[^cite_note-226] Both of the electoral lists were confirmed by RIK on 15 September.[^cite_note-227] In an interview with the FoNet news agency, [Rasim Ljajić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasim_Ljaji%C4%87) said that he is not a candidate for the SDP–CDP electoral list and that the electoral list only bears his name for better visibility.[^cite_note-228] He is, however, their ballot holder.[^cite_note-SDPCDP_list-158][^cite_note-229] SPP submitted their electoral list on 16 September.[^cite_note-230] The electoral lists of SPP and RS were accepted by RIK on 17 September, respectively.[^cite_note-231] The [Party of Democratic Action of Sandžak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Democratic_Action_of_Sand%C5%BEak) (SDA Sandžaka) submitted its electoral list on 20 September.[^cite_note-232] RIK accepted it a day later.[^cite_note-233]

#### Withdrawn lists

The [People's Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Serbia) coalition was formed on 10 September by NPS, NLS, and EU, with the intention to partake in the parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-234] However, on 15 September, NLS and EU announced their withdrawal, supporting the Student List.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] Later that day, the coalition held a press conference, announcing that they submitted the list, but that they will withdraw it, endorsing the Student List instead.[^cite_note-237] RIK confirmed their list on 18 September.[^cite_note-\:13-198] The coalition withdrew their list on 22 September.[^cite_note-238]
