**Wakayama Prefecture** ([和歌山](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%92%8C%E6%AD%8C%E5%B1%B1)[県](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%9C%8C), *Wakayama-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[wa.kaꜜ.ja.ma, wa.ka.ja.maꜜ.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Kansai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-3]<sup>: 1026 </sup> Wakayama Prefecture has a population of 876,030 (as of 1 February 2025) and a geographic area of 4,724 [square kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (1,824 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)). Wakayama Prefecture borders [Osaka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture) to the north, and [Mie Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_Prefecture) and [Nara Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Prefecture) to the northeast.

[Wakayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakayama_\(city\)) is the capital and largest city of Wakayama Prefecture, with other major cities including [Tanabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanabe,_Wakayama), [Hashimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashimoto,_Wakayama), and [Kinokawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinokawa,_Wakayama).[^cite_note-4]<sup>: 1025 </sup> Wakayama Prefecture is located on the southwestern coast of the [Kii Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kii_Peninsula) on the [Kii Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kii_Channel), connecting the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) and [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea), across from [Tokushima Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokushima_Prefecture) on the island of [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku).

### 1953 flood disaster

Main article: [1953 Wakayama flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Wakayama_flood)

On July 17–18, 1953, a torrential heavy rain occurred, followed by collapse of levees, river flooding and landslides in a wide area. Many bridges and houses were destroyed.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] According to an officially confirmed report by the [Government of Japan](30858300.md), 1,015 people died, with 5,709 injured and 7,115 houses lost.

## Demographics



Wakayama prefecture population pyramid in 2020

Since 1996, population of Wakayama Prefecture has kept declining, and since 2010, it has been the only prefecture in Kansai region with population below 1,000,000. In 2017, Wakayama is ranked 40th by population in Japan with a population of 944,320. In the 2020 census, close to 32% of the population was over 65 years of age - the highest percentage in Japan and one of the highest for national subdivisions worldwide.[^cite_note-10]

## Culture

[Mount Kōya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_K%C5%8Dya) (高野山, *Kōya-san*) in the [Ito District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ito_District) is the headquarters of the [Shingon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shingon) sect of [Japanese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Buddhism). It is home to one of the first Japanese style [Buddhist temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple) in Japan and remains a [pilgrimage](23763.md) site and an increasingly popular tourist destination as people flock to see its ancient temples set amidst the towering cedar trees at the top of the mountain. The [Sacred sites and pilgrimage routes in the Kii Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Sites_and_Pilgrimage_Routes_in_the_Kii_Mountain_Range) extend for miles throughout the prefecture and together have been recognized as Japan's 11th [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-13]

The [Kumano Shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumano_Shrine) are on the southern tip of the prefecture. [Tomogashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomogashima) (a cluster of four islands) is part of the prefecture.

## Agriculture

### Orange

Wakayama Prefecture ranks first in the production of oranges in Japan. Wakayama has its own brand of oranges, which is produced in Arida District and called 'Arida-Orange'. Arida District, where oranges have been produced for more than 400 years,[^cite_note-14] yields about half of the orange crops in Wakayama today.[^cite_note-15] Furthermore, the yield of Arida-Oranges accounts for about 10 percent of Japanese domestic production of oranges.[^cite_note-16]

### Chinese flowering plum (Ume)

According to the survey by the [Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries](1327960.md) of Japan, Wakayama stands first in the production of [Chinese flowering plum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prunus_mume), or *ume* in Japanese, in Japan. As of 2016, Wakayama made up about 70 percent of Japanese domestic production of *ume*.[^cite_note-17]
