***One Battle After Another*** is a 2025 American [action-comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_comedy) [thriller film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_film)[^cite_note-16] written, directed, and produced by [Paul Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson). Inspired by the 1990 novel *[Vineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineland)* by [Thomas Pynchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pynchon), the film's [ensemble cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast) includes [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio), [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn), [Benicio del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benicio_del_Toro), [Regina Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Hall), [Teyana Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teyana_Taylor), and [Chase Infiniti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Infiniti), in her film debut. The film follows a drug using washed-up former [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary) who is thrust back into the conflict when an amoral tenacious [counterterrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterterrorist) agent resurfaces after sixteen years to bring them to justice to cover his past sins.

Anderson had wanted to adapt *Vineland* since the early 2000s, and eventually incorporated his own stories into the narrative while writing the screenplay. [Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) took place in [California](5407.md) from January to June 2024 using [VistaVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VistaVision), becoming one of the first films to use the format since the 1960s. Following its premiere on September 8, 2025, *One Battle After Another* was released in the United States by [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures) on September 26. With a budget of \$130–175 million, it is the most expensive film of Anderson's career and the highest-grossing at over \$213 million worldwide.

*One Battle After Another* received acclaim and [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_One_Battle_After_Another). At the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md), it was nominated for 13 awards and won [Best Picture](61702.md), [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director), [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) (Penn), [Best Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay), [Best Film Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Film_Editing), and the inaugural [Best Casting](81860114.md) award. It also received one win from a record seven nominations at the [32nd Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Actor_Awards); three wins at the [31st Critics' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Critics'_Choice_Awards), including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Picture); four wins at the [83rd Golden Globes](81659305.md), including [Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy](186063.md); and six wins at the [79th British Academy Film Awards](82230921.md), including [Best Film](1039507.md). The film was listed by the [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute) as one of the top ten films of 2025 and won five awards from the [National Board of Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Review), including [Best Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Review_Award_for_Best_Film).

## Plot

"Ghetto" Pat Calhoun and Perfidia Beverly Hills are lovers and members of a [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics) militant revolutionary group, the French 75. While breaking out detained immigrants from [Otay Mesa Detention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa_Detention_Center), Perfidia sexually humiliates the commanding officer, Steven J. Lockjaw, who afterward becomes obsessed with her. Later, when Lockjaw catches Perfidia planting a bomb, he lets her go after she agrees to his demand to meet him for sex. Soon after, Perfidia becomes pregnant.

After Perfidia gives birth to a girl they name Charlene, Pat tries to persuade her to settle down, but she instead abandons Pat and Charlene to continue her revolutionary activities. Perfidia is arrested after murdering a security guard during a botched bank robbery. Lockjaw arranges for her to avoid prison in exchange for information on key French 75 members. Perfidia enters witness protection, while Lockjaw uses the information she provided to hunt down and [summarily execute](1227309.md) her comrades. French 75 member Howard Sommerville gives Pat and Charlene stolen identities as Bob and Willa Ferguson, while Perfidia flees witness protection for Mexico.

Sixteen years later, living off-the-grid in the [sanctuary city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_city) of Baktan Cross, California, Bob has become a paranoid stoner. He is protective of Willa, now a teenager who resents Bob's substance abuse, and told her that her mother was a hero who died when she was a baby. Through his anti-immigration efforts, Lockjaw has become a [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_States\)) and a prominent figure within the U.S. security agencies. When Lockjaw is invited to become a member of the Christmas Adventurers Club, a white supremacist secret society, he seeks to kill Willa to hide his past interracial relationship with Perfidia. Lockjaw sends [bounty hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_hunter) Avanti Q to capture Howard, who is able to transmit a distress signal to the French 75 before he is forced to reveal Bob and Willa's location.

Using an immigration and drug enforcement operation as cover, Lockjaw sends troops to Baktan Cross. French 75 member Deandra rescues Willa before her school dance is raided and takes her to a convent of revolutionary nuns, where she is told the truth about her mother's betrayal. Bob is warned by the French 75 of Lockjaw's imminent arrival. Escaping through a hidden tunnel, Bob is helped by Sergio St. Carlos, Willa's karate sensei and a community leader, who also evacuates immigrants via a hidden passage. After learning about the convent from another French 75 member, Bob flees with Sergio's students across rooftops but falls and is arrested without being recognized.

The Christmas Adventurers club find evidence of Lockjaw's relationship with Perfidia, and that Willa may be his child. They send member Tim Smith to kill Lockjaw and Willa. By [tracing her phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_tracking), Lockjaw locates Willa at the convent, where Deandra is arrested. Holding Willa hostage, Lockjaw [tests their DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_testing) in front of her, confirming she is his biological daughter. Sergio arranges Bob's escape and drives him to the convent. When they are pursued by the police, Sergio diverts the police so Bob can escape. Bob reaches the convent, and fails to snipe Lockjaw with Sergio's rifle. Lockjaw hires Avanti to kill Willa, but Avanti refuses due to her age; he agrees instead to deliver her to a far-right militia.&#x20;

Tim shoots Lockjaw as he is driving, causing Lockjaw to crash. While searching for Willa, Bob discovers Lockjaw's body at the crash site. Avanti brings Willa to the militia, but after a change of heart, frees her and is killed in a shootout with the militia. Willa escapes with Avanti's car and pistol with Tim in pursuit as Bob desperately tries to catch up. Willa lures Tim into a crash by exploiting a [blind summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_summit), shooting and killing him when he fails to respond with the revolutionary countersign. Bob finds Willa, who demands the countersign. Bob is able to talk her down, and they embrace.

Some time later, a severely scarred Lockjaw is seemingly welcomed into the Christmas Adventurers Club, but they fatally gas him instead. Returning home with Willa, Bob gives her a letter from Perfidia, in which she apologizes for her actions and vows to someday reunite with her family. Later, Bob gives Willa his blessing as she departs for a protest in [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California).

## Production

### Development



Writer and director [Paul Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson)

[Paul Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson) had considered adapting the 1990 novel *[Vineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineland)* by [Thomas Pynchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pynchon) since the early 2000s, but struggled, believing his love for the novel would get in the way of his ability to fairly rework it.[^cite_note-Vineland-17] Instead, Anderson set aside working on an adaptation and envisioned two ideas: one about an "action car-chase movie" and one about a "female revolutionary". *One Battle After Another* ultimately emerged as a combination of those two stories with some elements of *Vineland*, particularly the father-daughter dynamic.[^cite_note-Vineland-17][^cite_note-Vineland2-18][^cite_note-Vineland3-19]

### Casting

In June 2023, Anderson's next film, rumored to star [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio), [Regina Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Hall), [Viggo Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viggo_Mortensen) and [Joaquin Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix), found its home at [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures).[^cite_note-20] In January 2024, DiCaprio and Hall were confirmed to star, with [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn) joining the cast.[^cite_note-21] DiCaprio reportedly received his standard \$25 million fee for his involvement.[^cite_note-22] In February, [Alana Haim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alana_Haim), [Wood Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_Harris), [Chase Infiniti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Infiniti), [Shayna McHayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shayna_McHayle), and [Teyana Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teyana_Taylor) joined the cast.[^cite_note-23] The casting of [Benicio del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benicio_del_Toro) was confirmed in June.[^cite_note-24] [Jacob Batalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Batalon) revealed that he auditioned for the role of Comrade Josh.[^cite_note-25]

To land the role of Willa Ferguson, the teenage daughter of the film's central character, Infiniti went through a six-month audition process involving multiple callbacks and camera tests as well as chemistry reads with DiCaprio and Hall. The reads were designed to gauge the rapport between the then-newcomer and her potential castmates, but, said Infiniti, "They felt like the coolest kind of masterclass. I remember leaving the room thinking, 'I would love this part, but even if I don't get it, I'll take that with me forever.'" Infiniti had also been a kickboxing trainer, but she still spent months training in both karate and mixed martial arts that tested her readiness to do her own stunts and handle some intense scenes with Penn.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] "With Willia  \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*], it was anybody who's right", casting director [Cassandra Kulukundis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassandra_Kulukundis) said of the broad criteria Anderson gave her, adding, "Doesn't matter if they've ever acted before, if they're a star, if I found them in school, if I find them on the street. That was a huge net – if you were between 12 and 25, I was looking at you." Kulukundis was able to narrow the search when she realized how important the physical demands of the part were to the performance. "The moment I saw \[Infiniti] dancing, I don't know how to describe it. She's the most unique looking human being I've ever seen." For Kulukundis, Infiniti's ability to hold her own against DiCaprio was also vital. "We tried \[DiCaprio] with a bunch of girls and he was really thoughtful and helpful, and we could see the chemistry with Chase. She just kept passing all the tests."[^cite_note-29]

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) began in [California](5407.md) in January 2024.[^cite_note-LostCoastOutpost-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The film, under the [working title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title) *BC Project*,[^cite_note-CU-2] had filmed for eleven days across [Humboldt County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_County,_California) in [Arcata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcata,_California), [Cutten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutten,_California), [Eureka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka,_California), [Kneeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kneeland,_California), and [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad,_California).[^cite_note-38] Local experts, including the [Sisters of the Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Valley), on which the Sisters of the Brave Beaver in the film was based, were consulted.[^cite_note-Tapestry-39] To make observations about music and fashion trends, Anderson and his crew attended the prom at [Eureka High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_High_School_\(California\)); students were cast as [extras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_\(acting\)) for a key scene.[^cite_note-40]

On February 3, production moved to [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California), with filming at the Sacramento County Administration Building and [Sacramento County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_County_Superior_Court).[^cite_note-41] A [homeless encampment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tent_city) was cleared to allow for filming, sparking controversy.[^cite_note-42] The former Sacramento mansion of Governor [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) was used for exterior shots for the Christmas Adventurers Club's headquarters, tying the film back into the original novel, with Pynchon's focus on Reagan.[^cite_note-Tapestry-39]



The [DNA test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_testing) scene was shot in the church at [La Purísima Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pur%C3%ADsima_Mission) in [Lompoc, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lompoc,_California)

[^cite_note-RealCalifornia-37]

The production took a two-and-a-half-month break from filming because del Toro had a scheduling conflict with [Wes Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Anderson)'s *[The Phoenician Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phoenician_Scheme)* (shot between March and June 2024). When del Toro arrived fresh off the set of *The Phoenician Scheme*, with ten days between projects, he stepped into a fast-moving production.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-Benicio-44] "I had 10 days to unpack and pack", del Toro said of the 2024 shoot, adding, "Unpack my character in Wes' movie and then get dressed like Sensei and just go. You don't have time to adjust. If you want to jump on a carousel, you generally want time to run and catch up and then jump in. There was none of that."[^cite_note-Benicio-44]

On location filming also took place in [Anza-Borrego Desert State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anza-Borrego_Desert_State_Park) and [Borrego Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borrego_Springs,_California) in May 2024, and [El Paso, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas), including [El Paso Streetcars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso_Streetcar), in June 2024.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Other filming locations included [La Purísima Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pur%C3%ADsima_Mission),[^cite_note-RealCalifornia-37] the [Westgate Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westgate_Hotel_\(San_Diego\)),[^cite_note-48] and the city of [San Diego](28504.md) and [Otay Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa,_San_Diego) near the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border).[^cite_note-Tapestry-39][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### Cinematography

The film was shot by [Michael Bauman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bauman_\(cinematographer\)) on [35 mm movie film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_mm_movie_film) using [VistaVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VistaVision) cameras, marking his third collaboration with Anderson, following *[Phantom Thread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_Thread)* (2017) and *[Licorice Pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licorice_Pizza)* (2021).[^cite_note-57] Between 75 and 80% of the film was shot on VistaVision.[^cite_note-58] *One Battle After Another* is notable for utilizing VistaVision cameras; [Brady Corbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Corbet)'s *[The Brutalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brutalist)* (2024) seemed to revive the high-resolution widescreen 35 mm format from the 1950s.[^cite_note-DP-52][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] "The question was more, 'Is this going to be a reliable enough format for us?' When you see the shots from *The Brutalist*, \[DP] [Lol Crawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lol_Crawley) has got the camera on a [tripod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripod). It's just sitting there and we weren't going to do anything like that", Bauman said, adding, "*One Battle* was always going to be about movement. It had to be handheld; it had to be on [Steadicam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steadicam); it had to be strapped to cars; it had to bounce around."[^cite_note-DP-52] Bauman described his experience shooting the film as "being on the knife's edge", mostly because it was shot on location instead of a [sound stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_stage).[^cite_note-Cinematography-51]

Bauman revealed that Anderson wanted something in the "vein of '[70s cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_in_film)", pointing to films like *[The French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_French_Connection_\(film\))* (1971) and *[The Last Detail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Detail)* (1973), explaining that this "stylistic roughness" was "essential" for Anderson in telling the story.[^cite_note-Bauman-61] According to Bauman, VistaVision "was a response to television that some of the studios were developing. And instead of it being four-[perf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_perforations) vertical, like a normal 35 millimeter camera, this takes the film and puts it eight-perf horizontal, which allows for a much bigger negative space. Each frame is twice the size. And so it's a much richer image." The format was ideal for capturing some of the action sequences in the film, particularly the final car chase sequence.[^cite_note-Bauman-61] Bauman further admitted that it was a challenge to figure out how to "blend the richness of VistaVision" to make *One Battle After Another* "look like a '70s type of film that felt like a lot of the scenes out of *The French Connection*". He pushed VistaVision to its limits, while using and testing it, saying, "This camera system hadn't really been used at this quantity of film because we shot about 1.5 million feet \[460 kilometres] of film for the project \[and] hadn't had that kind of level of film run through it in a long time \[...] It really underscores the power of shooting on film and what VistaVision is as far as projecting it. It's really a super resting image that has a strong emotional feel to it. The ability to experience that by going to the theater is really unique."[^cite_note-Bauman-61]

Regarding the impact and location of the climactic car chase, Bauman said, "The sequence is really a testament to the location being the driving force. When Michael Glazier, the [location manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_manager), originally found it, he found it almost like by accident when he was driving after he found the location for the 1776 camp."[^cite_note-CBR-62] To figure out how to capture the scene's "dynamic energy", Bauman and his team worked with Allan Padelford, a veteran [stunt coordinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunt_coordinator) and [second unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_unit) director best known for designing high-octane car-chase action sequences, and his Padelford Camera Car.[^cite_note-CBR-62]

For the film's color and lighting, Bauman said both he and Anderson referred to the work of Hong Kong filmmaker [Wong Kar-wai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wong_Kar-wai) and *[The Silence of the Lambs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Silence_of_the_Lambs_\(film\))* (1991) as inspiration. Bauman also revealed that he and Anderson carried a print of *The French Connection* with them since they "were always screening film dailies", adding, "Sometimes it was just like, hey, it's time to have a little review, just to make sure your visual palette was in the same direction."[^cite_note-63] Actor and cinematographer collector [Giovanni Ribisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Ribisi) is thanked in the film's end credits for allowing production to use VistaVision cameras that he had personally restored.[^cite_note-Vineland-17][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Post-production

Editing was completed by [Andy Jurgensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Jurgensen), who had previously edited *Licorice Pizza*.[^cite_note-66] As a frequent collaborator of Anderson's, Jurgensen knew how to best be "fluid" in his work on the film to match his director's sensibilities. Discussing the final car chase sequence, Jurgensen said, "It was shot over a number of days and there really weren't [storyboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storyboard) for it, \[Anderson] just had ideas for the beginning, middle, and end. It was really a matter of shooting the cars from the different perspectives \[...] We just had to make sure we had all the pieces. I did a string out of all the different perspectives as we were shooting the scenes so that we made sure that we had good moments from all the angles, then it was finding the best bits and starting to assemble. It was just a long sequence going through all of Willa's shots of her eyes, looking at both the rear-view mirror and the side view mirror looking behind at the reflections. You're just slowly kind of whittling down the best fits."[^cite_note-67] Jurgensen further explained that "it evolved to be what the final \[edit] was" and that he was "still adding elements in the final mix", adding, "We went through many edits, but in the [test screenings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_screening), it was always people's favorite part."[^cite_note-68]

Additionally, Jurgensen was on location and got to view dailies with Anderson on a vintage VistaVision horizontal projector, which they borrowed from Warner Bros. "I was the only one traveling from editorial, and then I could supervise setting up the screening room and be there for all the screenings, which was really helpful", said Jurgensen. "And then while they were shooting, I also brought my computer system, getting digital dailies and making more notes, assembling stuff. You're with the crew and it's just so much better. You understand what everyone's going through and you're part of the process. But that's Paul's style."[^cite_note-69]

In February 2024, *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* reported that the film had been [greenlit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlight) with a \$115 million [production budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_budget).[^cite_note-70] In August 2024, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported the budget was "more than \$140 million", noting that Anderson's highest-grossing film, *[There Will Be Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Will_Be_Blood)* (2007), only made \$76 million, but that "Warner executives say DiCaprio's box-office track record justifies the budget for Anderson".[^cite_note-71] That month, sources indicated the film had been titled *The Battle of Baktan Cross* as rumors circulated that it was loosely inspired by Pynchon's *Vineland*; Anderson previously adapted Pynchon's *[Inherent Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inherent_Vice)* (2009) into a [feature film in 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inherent_Vice_\(film\)).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

By August 2025, *Variety* reported the film's final budget as \$175 million, while Warner Bros. said it cost \$130 million.[^cite_note-Budget-7] The film is the most costly of Anderson's career.[^cite_note-75] *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)* reported that [below-the-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Below-the-line_\(filmmaking\)), on location shooting in California cost \$101.6 million, with a [tax credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_credit) of \$8.4 million.[^cite_note-Opening-76] By September 2025, *Variety* reported that an additional \$70 million was spent on [marketing](59252.md).[^cite_note-BoxOffice-77]

Beginning in January 2025, the film had multiple test screenings, which reinforced the rumored connection to *Vineland*.[^cite_note-78] It marked the first time since *[Boogie Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogie_Nights)* (1997) that Anderson agreed to audience testing; based on the feedback, he cut eight to ten minutes.[^cite_note-DeLucaAbdy-79] There was also a report that Warner Bros. was working with Anderson to refine the film, but did not ask for radical changes.[^cite_note-Jurgensen-80] "We trimmed it down and that was just our normal process", Jurgensen said. According to Jurgensen, Anderson does not like to test his films, but it was something that Warner Bros. requested. Anderson partially agreed to the test screenings because he wanted to know if it played well as a comedy, and, as Jurgensen said, "There are some sensitive things in the movie, both racial things and political things. It was going to be interesting for us to test it in different parts of the country." Jurgensen also added that the tests confirmed that "the humor was working. And luckily, it did confirm, when you're kind of trying to figure out, 'How can we push it a little further? How can we do things?'"[^cite_note-Jurgensen-80] He further noted that the studio was not trying to enforce an ultimatum and did not pressure them to cut anything, saying, "They were supportive. I think the test screenings helped us because they were reacting so well to it."[^cite_note-Jurgensen-80]

The film's title was officially confirmed in March 2025, with Warner Bros. debuting a [teaser trailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaser_trailer).[^cite_note-Teaser-81] *One Battle After Another* marked Anderson's sixth feature film collaboration with composer and [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead) guitarist [Jonny Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Greenwood),[^cite_note-82] and the sixth with Anderson and first [assistant director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_director) and producer [Adam Somner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Somner), who died in November 2024; the film is dedicated to Somner.[^cite_note-89]

In February 2026, *Variety* reported that the awards campaign budget for the film was approximately \$14 million.[^cite_note-90]

## Reception

### Box office

*One Battle After Another* grossed \$72.9 million in the U.S. and Canada, and \$140.2 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of \$213.1 million.[^cite_note-BOM-8] The film ended up being a [box office failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-office_bomb), falling short of its estimated \$300 million break-even point and losing Warner Bros. an estimated \$100 million during its theatrical run.[^cite_note-136] It is also Anderson's highest-grossing release to date.[^cite_note-BoxOffice-77][^cite_note-\$200_Million-131][^cite_note-HighestGrossingFilm-137]

In the U.S. and Canada, the film opened in 3,634 theaters, the widest release for a film directed by Anderson and his first to debut in wide release.[^cite_note-HighestGrossingFilm-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-Opening_Weekend-139] It made \$8.8 million on its first day, including \$3.1 million from Thursday previews.[^cite_note-Opening_Weekend-139][^cite_note-140] The film earned \$22 million over the weekend, topping the box office, and handily marking the best weekend for a film directed by Anderson, topping the \$4.9 million earned by *There Will Be Blood* in its fifth weekend in 2008.[^cite_note-Opening-76][^cite_note-141] In its second weekend, *One Battle After Another* grossed \$11 million (a drop of 50%), finishing second behind then-newcomer *[Taylor Swift: The Official Release Party of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_Official_Release_Party_of_a_Showgirl)*.[^cite_note-142]

### Critical response

[Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio), [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn), [Benicio del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benicio_del_Toro), and [Teyana Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teyana_Taylor) garnered critical acclaim for their performances and earned [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award) nominations for [Best Actor](23245410.md) (DiCaprio), [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) (del Toro and Penn) and [Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress) (Taylor), respectively, with Penn winning.

The film received widespread acclaim, with multiple critics calling the film a "masterpiece".[^cite_note-149] On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), 94% of 444 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 8.9/10. The website's consensus reads: "An epic screwball adventure teeming with awe-inspiring action set pieces, *One Battle After Another* is Paul Thomas Anderson's most entertaining film yet while also one of his most thematically rich."[^cite_note-Rotten_Tomatoes-150] [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean), assigned the film a score of 95 out of 100, based on 63 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".[^cite_note-Metacritic-151] Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore) gave the film an average grade of "A" on its A+ to F scale, while those at [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak) rated the film four-and-a-half out of five stars, with 74% of audiences saying they would "definitely recommend" it.[^cite_note-Opening-76]

[Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman) of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* wrote that the film "taps into the fierce urgency of now; it gives you a chill that's also a wake-up call", adding that it "has the kind of twists and turns that feed the audience, giving us the childlike sensation that we have no idea what's coming next, and that that's the happiest way there is to watch a movie". Brian Tallerico of *[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com)* wrote that *One Battle After Another* "is a timeless story of resistance, one that playfully weaves together influences as broad-reaching as the true story of [Weather Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Underground) and cinematic depictions of rebellion, but it's also a remarkably propulsive, fun, and eventually moving piece of work about the human beings caught up in the chaotic machine".[^cite_note-RogerEbert.com-152] [Justin Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Chang) of *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* called the film "a father-daughter epic, with an unusually personal gush of feeling. You can count on one hand the number of scenes that Bob and Willa share, but their connection – a swirl of protectiveness, exasperation, and fiercely unconditional love – binds the movie and its madly whirling parts together".[^cite_note-153]

Katie Walsh of the *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune)* called the film a "searing indictment of this particular moment in American history" by which "Anderson balances the sprawling, conspiracy-minded aspects of this yarn with the intimate father-daughter story, which is the heart of the matter".[^cite_note-154] Writing for *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, [Manohla Dargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manohla_Dargis) called the film "a carnivalesque epic about good and evil, violence and power, inalienable rights and the fight against injustice; it's also a love story. The film speaks to the failures of the past and of the present but insists on the promise of the future".[^cite_note-155] [Richard Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lawson_\(writer\)) of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* wrote that *One Battle After Another* is "a furious film, a richly engaging and persuasive polemic" in which "Anderson shows a previously unseen aptitude for action and suspense; *One Battle After Another* is, essentially, a thriller, albeit one teeming with enormous ideas about the collapse and possible rescue of the country".[^cite_note-Review-156]

Alex Saveliev of *[Film Threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Threat)* opened his review by writing that the film "demands to be seen on the largest screen possible to fully absorb the 35 mm VistaVision experience with every cell of your body. Everything about it is grand: its characters, its action sequences, its timely sentiments, even the quieter moments".[^cite_note-157] [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw), awarding the film five stars and writing for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, was effusive in his praise. He celebrated the "riff on the now recognisable Anderson-Pynchonian idea of counterculture and counter-revolution", praised the score by Greenwood, and pondered if the "central paternity crisis triangle \[is] an image for an ownership dispute around the American melting-pot dream".[^cite_note-158]

The performances of the cast were also highly praised.[^cite_note-167] Gleiberman also added that "Anderson knows that the quality that liberates DiCaprio is comedy. By having him play Bob as a dissolute stoner addict, discombobulated by his loss of faith, he humanizes DiCaprio and coaxes a great performance out of him."[^cite_note-168] Tallerico of *RogerEbert.com* felt DiCaprio gives a "carefully modulated" performance, but he nevertheless singled out Sean Penn's performance as "his best work in years", writing he "flexes his muscles, grits his teeth, and growls his lines, but somehow threads the needle between truth and caricature".[^cite_note-RogerEbert.com-152] David Sims of *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)* opined, "Penn, giving a brilliant performance of cold villainy that could win him a third [Oscar](324.md), is unafraid of lancing the inherent goofiness of a [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism). DiCaprio plays Bob as a sweetheart rather than a buffoon; he's a tired, strung-out [antihero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antihero) made weary not by his yearslong efforts to fight back but by the relentlessness of the world."[^cite_note-169] [David Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ehrlich) of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire)* praised "magnetically self-possessed newcomer/instant movie star Chase Infiniti, whose performance inspires a strange kind of secondhand pride". He also singled out the performances of Penn and Taylor, calling the latter "eruptive \[...] steaming with revolutionary zeal" and the former "career-best".[^cite_note-170] *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)*'s Pete Hammond called Penn's performance "a riot to watch" while considering the performance of Taylor as "dazzling" and "unforgettable".[^cite_note-Hammond-85]

William Bibbiani of *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap)* was less enthusiastic, describing the plot as scattershot, unfocused, overly long, and the film's themes as shallow, writing that Anderson was "more interested in taking cheap potshots at the film's real-world analogues than actually exploring them".[^cite_note-TheWrap-171] *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*'s [Kyle Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Smith_\(critic\)) similarly noted the film is "big and brash. Rangy in tone, style and theme, it has so much going on that a single viewing hardly seems sufficient to absorb it all. Whether it's a masterpiece or a hodgepodge will be a matter of some discussion; the reach is evident but the grasp is a little shaky".[^cite_note-172] Israeli media-critic [Liel Leibovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liel_Leibovitz) of *[The Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Press_\(CBS_News\))* described the film as "irredeemable" and "just another sign that Hollywood has once again driven itself to total moral, artistic, and creative ruin".[^cite_note-173]

Author [Bret Easton Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bret_Easton_Ellis) dismissed the praise, arguing that the film, in which he found a "liberal mustiness," is a hit with critics only because it aligns with a "leftist sensibility."[^cite_note-174] Several right-wing commentators, such as [Ben Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shapiro) or film critic [Armond White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armond_White), criticized *One Battle* for being a "left-wing" movie "made for left-wing elites sitting in their left-wing bubble."[^cite_note-175]

During a post-screening panel discussion with Anderson, fellow filmmaker and moderator [Steven Spielberg](26940.md) highly praised the film, calling it "insane" and "really incredible", adding, "This is such a concoction of things that are so bizarre and at the same time so relevant, that I think have become increasingly more relevant than perhaps even when \[Anderson] finished the screenplay, and assembled \[the] cast and crew, and began production."[^cite_note-Spielberg-143][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] The film garnered significant praise by other prominent filmmakers, including [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola),[^cite_note-178] [Michael Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mann),[^cite_note-179] and [Paul Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schrader).[^cite_note-180]

Producer [Sara Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Murphy_\(film_producer\)) said she hoped the film's reception among audiences and critics would encourage more studios to invest in "risky" projects similar to it, adding, "Original films are not usually done at this level \[of] a big, good-time movie."[^cite_note-Timeliness-181]

### Critical analysis

According to Jesse Hassenger of *The Guardian*, the "film has irked some on the right and galvanized others on the left". Hassenger added that some on the right accused the film for being "an [apologia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apologia) for radical [left-wing terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_terrorism)".[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-Conservatives-183][^cite_note-Left-Wing-184]

#### Political views and controversies

*The New York Times*' [Michelle Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Goldberg) argued it was "an antifascist movie at a fascist moment", a film which she observed "was made in the America that existed before [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s return" to office eight months earlier; Goldberg alleged whether or not "if such a forthrightly antifascist film could be produced in Hollywood today".[^cite_note-185]

Author, podcaster, and screenwriter [Bret Easton Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bret_Easton_Ellis) alleged, "It's kind of shocking to see these kind  \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*] of accolades for – I'm sorry, it's not a very good movie – because of its political ideology, and it's so obvious that's what they're responding to \[...] Why it's considered a masterpiece, the greatest film of the decade, the greatest film ever made \[is] because it really aligns with this kind of leftist sensibility."[^cite_note-186] Ellis later added on his podcast allegations that, "You have the left and the entertainment press propping this movie up to such an absurd degree that it felt unnatural."[^cite_note-Left-Wing-184]

Responding to the political controversies surrounding the film, Gleiberman posited that "part of the power of *One Battle After Another* is that it is not, in fact, a left-wing movie, it's important to recognize how that misperception has been fed by a lot of people on the left \[...] I think a lot of the attitude of the entertainment media has been to reflexively treat *One Battle After Another* as if the film itself were raising a revolutionary fist – and as if there were something 'left-wing' about attacking authoritarianism. There is not."[^cite_note-Left-Wing-184]

#### Authoritarian immigration enforcement

The film broaches the subject of the [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) agency with the French 75's raid on [Otay Mesa Detention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa_Detention_Center), an ICE facility; the agency is not mentioned directly.[^cite_note-187] However, as authority is depicted in the film as unpredictable, militarized, and with the power to destabilize entire neighborhoods,[^cite_note-Timeliness-181] many critics and outlets agreed that this made the film's release incredibly "timely" amidst the [expansion of ICE operations](78945778.md) under the [second Trump administration](78298108.md).[^cite_note-196] Murphy said in February 2026 that the concurrent [ICE operations in Minneapolis](82105033.md), which [killed civilians](82105033.md#Shootings), made the film "devastatingly" timely:

> I don't think we could have anticipated just how close to home it would be. It's hitting an audience in their core. I think it is reflective of the world that we're living in today, but I think, hopefully, it is also an optimistic take on how we can continue to fight for the things that are important to us and find peace in community. I hope the takeaway is one of optimism.[^cite_note-Timeliness-181]

In January 2026, Daniel Boguslaw of *[The American Prospect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Prospect)* compared the character Steven J. Lockjaw to then-commander of the [U.S. Border Patrol](567453.md) [Gregory Bovino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Bovino), who promoted aggressive [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) tactics within the agency.[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199]

#### Perfidia's character and racial themes

Ellen E Jones of *The Guardian* opined that the film's handling of racial themes "raises questions about how white male directors depict Black women", particularly with regards to Perfidia Beverly Hills, and the ways in which she is [sexualized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexualization) by two white men (Pat/Bob and Lockjaw, the latter a white supremacist); Bibbiani argued that Perfidia is a [sex criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_and_the_law).[^cite_note-TheWrap-171][^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201] *The New York Times*' Maya Phillips called Perfidia "the most radical part" of the film, writing, "In one major sense, the film does send a nuanced political message embodied in the brief and mysterious arc of Perfidia Beverly Hills: that the success of a contemporary American political movement is inextricably tied to the sexual freedom and agency of Black women."[^cite_note-202] In November 2025, Taylor rejected these critiques, stating:

> Is that not what Black women go through? We are [fetishized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_sexuality#Black_women) \[...] and we are, unfortunately, the least protected people. Showing what Black women go through, that's a hard reality to accept. And this movie should spark debate, I always knew it would, because sometimes you just got to shake the table.[^cite_note-203]

In January 2026, Taylor explained further: "\[People feel] like she was overly horny. And I'm like, do you realize the first thing we see of Perfidia is her having a gun to a guy's head, and he calls her 'sweet thing'? Are you – are we watching the same film?" She said that Perfidia weaponizes her sexuality against her enemies: "Perfidia kind of dived into the, 'Oh, you think I'm hot? All right, bet. Cool if I get to still do what I'm doing'." Perfidia's decisions, Taylor said, are made in "survival mode":

> It will never be a moment of judgment, because a lot of her mistakes have come from her being in survival mode, dealing with [postpartum depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_depression) and the title of being a strong woman – a [strong Black woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_black_woman). We don't get the same amount of compassion as everybody else. We don't get the same amount of grace as everybody else. Everybody just assumes we're okay.[^cite_note-204]

In February 2026, Taylor stated that Perfidia "is so misunderstood, but most importantly, human, and so raw. And she is unapologetically herself", adding:

> \[People] just write her off as like, she's just horny \[...] Perfidia became a revolutionary because of the things that she believes in. You see her mom saying Perfidia comes from a long line of revolutionaries. That in itself, to any woman, any person, is also a pressure. So not only is she carrying it on, it is instilled in her, and now it's become a part of her identity.[^cite_note-205]

In March 2026, backstage at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md), Anderson addressed the critiques regarding Perfidia's character:

> \[Perfidia] was so flawed and unfortunately makes decisions that are detrimental to the revolution that she's trying to fight. It's complicated. We always knew that we were trying to make something complicated. We knew that we weren't making something that was heroic and we needed to lean into that. We need to own the fact that this woman was suffering not only from postpartum depression, but she had issues of her own that she hadn't really reconciled with. It's a very dangerous thing, when you start out and want to change the world, but you start to kind of become selfish; you read your own reviews. That was our hero in Perfidia, who becomes an antihero. The point of it is to set up a story of Willa, the next generation. What happens when your parents, who are damaged and have handed quite a difficult history to you – how do you manage that? That's our story. Our story is in Chase \[Infiniti] and her reaching, like I talked about in terms of the generational aspect, to try to do better.[^cite_note-206]

### Year-end lists

The [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute) and [National Board of Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Review) listed *One Battle After Another* as one of 2025's ten best films.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209] *[Film Comment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Comment)*, a *[New York Times](30680.md)*' readers poll, and a *[Sight and Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sight_and_Sound)* poll of over 100 international critics all ranked it as the year's best.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212] [*IndieWire*'s annual critics poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire_Critics_Poll) also ranked the film at #1, with a final result of 573 points; 101 of the 148 critics who voted had *One Battle After Another* somewhere on their Top 10 ballots, while 43 of those 101 mentions were #1 picks.[^cite_note-213] Additionally, *IndieWire* surveyed fifty filmmakers' favorite films of the year; twenty listed *One Battle After Another*. These included [Fede Álvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fede_%C3%81lvarez), [Olivier Assayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Assayas), [Clint Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Bentley), [Oliver Laxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Laxe), and [Michael Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mann); Laxe called the film "a testament to the excess of our times, on every level".[^cite_note-214] In his list of 2025's 20 best films, *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*'s David Fear called *One Battle After Another* a "thundering, dizzying epic", and "a timeless tale about revolutionaries taking care of their own while getting the next generation to pick up the flag".[^cite_note-RollingStone-215]

### Accolades

Main article: [List of accolades received by *One Battle After Another*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_One_Battle_After_Another)

At the [31st Critics' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Critics'_Choice_Awards), *One Battle After Another* won three awards, including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Picture) and [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Director) for Anderson.[^cite_note-252] It was also nominated for nine awards at the [83rd Golden Globes](81659305.md), receiving the most nominations of any film that year; del Toro, DiCaprio, Infiniti, Penn, and Taylor all received nominations for their performances. Ultimately, the film won four Golden Globes: [Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy](186063.md), [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director), [Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture) (Taylor), and [Best Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Screenplay).[^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255] It also became the most-nominated film in the history of the [Screen Actors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild) at the [32nd Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Actor_Awards), with a record-breaking seven nominations and Penn winning [Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Supporting Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role).[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258]

At the [79th British Academy Film Awards](82230921.md), the film led the nominations with fourteen overall, winning six, including [Best Film](1039507.md) and [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Direction).[^cite_note-259] Additionally, at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md), the film received thirteen nominations, including [Best Picture](61702.md), [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director), [Best Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay) and four acting nominations (for del Toro, DiCaprio, Penn, and Taylor), the second-most of any film that year.[^cite_note-260] The film went on to win six Oscars: Best Picture (Somner,[^cite_note-262] Murphy, and Anderson), Best Director (Anderson), [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) (Penn), Best Adapted Screenplay (Anderson), [Best Film Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Film_Editing) (Jurgensen), and [Best Casting](81860114.md) (Kulukundis), becoming the inaugural recipient for the latter.[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265]
