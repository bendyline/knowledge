# Stilt house

"Lake dwelling" redirects here. For other types of lakeside dwellings, see [Wetland settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland_settlement).



[Nyaungshwe Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaungshwe_Township) in the [Inle Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inle_Lake), [Myanmar](19457.md)



Modern stilt houses along the [Tisza River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza_River) with concrete foundation and stilts,[Csongrád](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csongr%C3%A1d), [Hungary](13275.md)

**Stilt houses** (also known as **pile** or **lake dwellings**) are freestanding residential buildings ([houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House)) whose main structure is held elevated above the ground or [water body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_body) like a [pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier) by thin [pillars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillar) known as [stilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilts_\(architecture\)). They are primarily built to protect against [flooding](50482.md);[^cite_note-Bush2004-1] and to keep out [vermin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermin) on the ground.[^cite_note-Experts-2] The [shaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shade_\(shadow\)) area beneath the house, when the ground js not inundated, is often used as an [undercroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undercroft) for [work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workshop), [storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storage_room) or [parking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking).[^cite_note-3] Such dwellings are common across [coastal](5236.md)/[riparian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riparian) zones of Southeast Asia, Oceania, Central America, the Caribbean, parts of coastal West Africa, northern parts of South America, the Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius, Seychelles, Sri Lanka, and the Maldives.

**Stilt apartments** are [low-rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-rise_building) [apartment buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartment_building) that are built on the same off-the-ground concept as stilt houses, although the undercroft area is typically designed to provide covered residential [parking spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_space) for the tenants rather than for [flood protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_protection).

## Types

- [Diaojiaolou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaojiaolou?action=edit\&redlink=1) – Stilt houses in southern China.

- [Heliotrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliotrope_\(building\)) – A concept house designed by [Rolf Disch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Disch) with a single stilt, optimized for harnessing solar power.

- [Kelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelong) – Built primarily for fishing, but often doubling up as offshore dwellings in the following countries: [Philippines](23440.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Indonesia](14579.md) and [Singapore](27318.md).

- [Bahay Kubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipa_hut) – The traditional house type prevalent in the [Philippines](23440.md).

- Palafito – Found throughout South America since Pre-Columbian times. In the late 19th century, numerous palafitos were built in [Chilean](5489.md) cities such as [Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castro,_Chile), [Chonchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonchi), and other towns in the [Chiloé Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilo%C3%A9_Archipelago), and are now considered a typical element of [Chilotan architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilotan_architecture).

- [Pang uk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pang_uk) – A special kind of house found in [Tai O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_O), [Lantau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantau), Hong Kong, mainly built by [Tankas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanka_\(ethnic_group\)).

- [Papua New Guinea stilt house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea_stilt_house?action=edit\&redlink=1) – A kind of stilt house constructed by [Motuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motuan_people), commonly found in the southern coastal area of PNG.

- [Queenslander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queenslander_\(architecture\)) – Stilt house common in [Queensland](59051.md) and northern [New South Wales](21654.md), Australia.

- Chaang Ghar – A type of stilt house built in [Assam](186162.md) state of [India](14533.md). It is mainly found in flood-prone areas of the [Brahmaputra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River) [valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley).

- [Thai stilt house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_stilt_house) – A kind of house often built on [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater), e.g., a [lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelumbo) pond.

- [Vietnamese stilt house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_stilt_house?action=edit\&redlink=1) – Similar to the Thai ones, except having a front door with a smaller height for religious reasons.

## Africa

Stilted [granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granary) are also a common feature in West Africa, e.g., in the [Malinke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people) language regions of [Mali](19127.md) and [Guinea](12176.md).

## Americas



Palafitos in [Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castro,_Chile), [Chiloé Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilo%C3%A9_Archipelago), Chile



Stilt houses on the banks of the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mississippi_River) in [Iowa](26810748.md)

Stilt houses were also built by [Amerindians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) in [pre-Columbian times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era). *Palafitos* are especially widespread along the banks of the tropical river valleys of South America, notably the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) and [Orinoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco) river systems. Stilt houses were such a prevalent feature along the shores of [Lake Maracaibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maracaibo) that [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci) was inspired to name the region "Venezuela" (little Venice). As the costs of hurricane damage increase, more and more houses along the [Gulf Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Coast) are being built as or converted to stilt houses.[^cite_note-4]

Stilt houses are still commonplace in parts of the [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) in northeastern [Nicaragua](21362.md), and in [northern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Brazil).[^cite_note-Robinson1996-5] They are also common in the [bayou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayou) parts of the [Southern United States](179553.md), the hurricane prone [Florida Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Keys), and the South Carolina [Lowcountry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowcountry).

## Arctic



Summer family dwellings of the natives of the [Kamchatka Peninsula](678671.md) (Russia) called [Itelmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itelmens) or Kamchadals. Their winter dwellings were earth-sheltered and communal.

Houses where [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost) is present, in the [Arctic](36971.md), are built on stilts to keep permafrost under them from melting. Permafrost can be up to 70% water. While frozen, it provides a stable foundation. However, if heat radiating from the bottom of a home melts the permafrost, the home goes out of level and starts sinking into the ground. Other means of keeping the permafrost from melting are available, but raising the home off the ground on stilts is one of the most effective ways.

### Austronesia

See also: [Austronesian peoples § Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples#Architecture)



The raised *bale* houses of the [Ifugao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao_people) with capped house posts are believed to be derived from the designs of traditional [granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granaries)

[^cite_note-Sato1991-6]

Raised rectangular houses are one of the cultural hallmarks of the [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) and are found throughout the regions in [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia), [Island Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Melanesia), [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia), and [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia) settled by Austronesians. The structures are raised on piles, usually with the space underneath also utilized for storage or [domestic animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesticated_animals_of_Austronesia). The raised design had multiple advantages, they mitigate damage during flooding and (in very tall examples) can act as defensive structures during conflicts. The house posts are also distinctively capped with larger-diameter discs at the top, to prevent vermin and pests from entering the structures by climbing them. Austronesian houses and other structures are usually built in wetlands and alongside bodies of water, but can also be built in the highlands or even directly on shallow water.[^cite_note-Rainbird2004-7][^cite_note-Sato1991-6][^cite_note-Arbi2013-8][^cite_note-bin_Tajudeen-9]



Reconstruction of [Latte period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latte_stone) [Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamorro_people) buildings raised on capped stone pillars called *haligi*

Building structures on pilings is believed to be derived from the design of raised rice [granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granaries) and storehouses, which are highly important status symbols among the ancestrally rice-cultivating Austronesians.[^cite_note-Sato1991-6][^cite_note-bin_Tajudeen-9] The rice granary shrine was also the archetypal religious building among Austronesian cultures and was used to store carvings of ancestor spirits and local deities.[^cite_note-bin_Tajudeen-9] While rice cultivation wasn't among the technologies carried into [Remote Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Oceania), raised storehouses still survived. The *pātaka* of the [Māori people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) is such an example. The largest *pātaka* are elaborately adorned with carvings and are often the tallest buildings in the Māori *[pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81)*. They were used to store implements, weapons, ships, and other valuables; while smaller *pātaka* were used to store provisions. A special type of *pātaka* supported by a single tall post also had ritual importance and were used to isolate high-born children during their training for leadership.[^cite_note-Sato1991-6]

The majority of Austronesian structures are not permanent. They are made from perishable materials like wood, bamboo, plant fiber, and leaves. Because of this, archaeological records of prehistoric Austronesian structures are usually limited to traces of house posts, with no way of determining the original building plans.[^cite_note-Lico2008-10] Indirect evidence of traditional Austronesian architecture, however, can be gleaned from their contemporary representations in art, like in [friezes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friezes) on the walls of later [Hindu-Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions) stone temples (like in reliefs in [Borobudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borobudur) and [Prambanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prambanan)). But these are limited to the recent centuries. They can also be reconstructed linguistically from shared terms for architectural elements, like ridge-poles, thatch, rafters, house posts, hearth, notched log ladders, storage racks, public buildings, and so on. Linguistic evidence also makes it clear that stilt houses were already present among Austronesian groups since at least the [Late Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Neolithic).[^cite_note-Arbi2013-8][^cite_note-bin_Tajudeen-9]

In the late 20th century, stilt houses in extremely calm ocean water became a popular form of tourist lodging known as [overwater bungalows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungalow#Overwater_bungalow); the trend began in [French Polynesia](10737.md) and quickly spread to other tourist locations, especially in tropical locales.

### East Asia and Mainland Southeast Asia



A [stilt house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B4ng_house?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Bahnar people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnar_people), an [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages)-speaking ethnic group.

Arbi *et al.* (2013) have also noted the striking similarities between Austronesian architecture and Japanese traditional raised architecture (*[shinmei-zukuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinmei-zukuri)*). Particularly the buildings of the [Ise Grand Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ise_Grand_Shrine), which contrast with the [pit-houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pit-houses) typical of the Neolithic [Yayoi period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period). They propose significant Neolithic contact between the people of southern Japan and Austronesians or pre-Austronesians that occurred prior to the spread of [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) cultural influence to the islands.[^cite_note-Arbi2013-8] Rice cultivation is also believed to have been introduced to Japan from a para-Austronesian group from coastal eastern China.[^cite_note-Robbeets2017-11] Waterson (2009) has also argued that the architectural tradition of stilt houses in eastern Asia and the Pacific is originally Austronesian, and that similar building traditions in Japan and mainland Asia (notably among [Kra-Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra-Dai) and [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic)-speaking groups) correspond to contacts with a prehistoric Austronesian network.[^cite_note-bin_Tajudeen-9][^cite_note-Waterson2009-12]

### South Asia



Traditional [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people) houses, [Majuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majuli)

In South Asia, stilt houses are very common in [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India), specifically the [Brahmaputra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley) regions of [Assam](186162.md), which is extremely prone to regional flooding from the Brahmaputra. These houses are known as *chang ghar* in [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language), and as *kare okum* in [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_language); chang ghar are traditionally built by the [Mising people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people), who live along the Brahmaputra. Unlike many forms of traditional architecture, including stilt architecture, in South and Southeast Asia, the construction of chang ghar is making a resurgence and increasing in popularity, as a result of climate change increasing regular flooding in Assam, and the stilts of the chang ghar is adapted to flooding in the first place.[^cite_note-13] The height of the stilts of the chang ghar is determined by the height of the water during the last major flood.[^cite_note-14]

Stilt houses are also popular in [Kerala](4349459.md) in the [Kerala backwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_backwaters), another regions with high rainfall and regular flooding from monsoons. Although stilt houses in the Kerala Backwaters have been a traditional method of house construction for many years, following the disastrous [2018 floods in Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Kerala_floods), many more stilt houses have been constructed recently and utilize concrete as well as timber for their pillars.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Europe



Reconstruction of Bronze Age stilt houses on [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfahlbau_Museum_Unteruhldingen), [Pfahlbaumuseum Unteruhldingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfahlbaumuseum_Unteruhldingen), Germany

In [Traunkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traunkirchen) at [Lake Traun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traunsee) in [Upper Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria), archaeologists from the [University of Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Innsbruck) are researching the only Iron Age lakeside settlement currently known in Austria.

In the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), the [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Age) and the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), stilt-house settlements were common in the [Alpine](981.md) and [Pianura Padana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pianura_Padana) ([Terramare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terramare)) regions.[^cite_note-Ertl2008-17] Remains have been found at the [Ljubljana Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marsh) in [Slovenia](27338.md) and at the [Mondsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondsee_\(lake\)) and [Attersee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attersee_\(lake\)) lakes in [Upper Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria), for example.

Early [archaeologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist) like [Ferdinand Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Keller_\(antiquity_scholar\)) thought they formed artificial islands, much like the Irish and Scottish [crannogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crannog), but today it is clear that the majority of settlements were located on the shores of lakes and were only inundated later on.[^cite_note-Menotti2004-18]

Reconstructed stilt houses are shown in [open-air museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-air_museum) in [Unteruhldingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfahlbau_Museum_Unteruhldingen) and [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich) (Pfahlbauland). In June 2011, the [prehistoric pile dwellings in six Alpine states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_pile_dwellings_around_the_Alps) were designated as [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). A single [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) pile dwelling, the [Alvastra stilt houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvastra_stilt_houses), has been excavated in Sweden. [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) has described in his *[Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_\(Herodotus\))* the dwellings of the "lake-dwellers" in [Paeonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paeonia_\(kingdom\)) and how those were constructed.[^cite_note-19]

In the Alps, similar buildings, known as [raccards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccard), are still in use as granaries. In England, granaries are placed on [staddle stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staddle_stones), similar to stilts, to prevent mice and rats getting to the grain.

In Italy there are several stilt-houses settlements, for example the one on the [Rocca di Manerba del Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocca_di_Manerba_del_Garda).

In [Scotland](26994.md) there used to be prehistoric stilt houses called [crannogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crannog).[^cite_note-20]
