**Yulia Anatoliivna Svyrydenko**[^cite_note-3] (born 25 December 1985) is a Ukrainian politician who served as the 19th [prime minister of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Ukraine) from 2025 to 2026.[^cite_note-voted_in-4] Before that, she was the [first deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Ukraine) and [Minister of Economic Development and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_\(Ukraine\)) from 2021,[^cite_note-Interfax_041121-5] until she replaced [Denys Shmyhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denys_Shmyhal) as prime minister as part of a government reshuffle proposed by President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md).[^cite_note-proposed-6]

## Early life

Yulia Anatoliivna Svyrydenko was born on 25 December 1985[^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2] in [Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv), then part of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-Svyridenkocq6mpm7vg90o-1][^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2] Her father was the head of the Chernihiv territorial branch of the [Antimonopoly Committee of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimonopoly_Committee_of_Ukraine), and her mother worked in the office of the [Chernihiv Oblast Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast_Council).[^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2]

## Education

In 2008, Svyrydenko graduated from [Kyiv National University of Trade and Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Trade_and_Economics) with a degree in antimonopoly management.[^cite_note-Interfax_041121-5]

## Career

Svyrydenko began her career in 2008 as an economist at a Ukrainian-Andorran joint venture JSC "AMP".[^cite_note-\:0-7]

In 2011, Svyrydenko became a permanent representative of [Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv) in the city of [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi), the only representative office of a Ukrainian city in China.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

In 2015, Svyrydenko began working as the head of the Department of Economic Development of the [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast).[^cite_note-\:0-7] In the [2015 Ukrainian local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Ukrainian_local_elections) she ran for the [Chernihiv City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_City_Council) for the party [Petro Poroshenko Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko_Bloc) (as an [independent candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_candidate)) in one of the city's majoritarian districts, but she was not elected.[^cite_note-Svyridenkocq6mpm7vg90o-1][^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2] Svyrydenko's husband, Serhiy Derlemenko, also ran for Chernihiv City Council in 2015.[^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2] He did so for the party [Self Reliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self_Reliance_\(political_party\)), but was also not elected.[^cite_note-Derlemenko62613-8]

From 30 July to 28 November 2018, she served as acting [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Chernihiv_Oblast) (head of the regional state administration) of the [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast).[^cite_note-\:0-7]

On 5 May 2020, [President of Ukraine](20688479.md) [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) appointed Svyrydenko as the representative of Ukraine in the subgroup on social and economic issues of the [Trilateral Contact Group on Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilateral_Contact_Group_on_Ukraine).[^cite_note-\:0-7]

On 22 December 2020, President Zelenskyy appointed Svyrydenko as Deputy Head of the [Office of the President](19362024.md) to replace [Yuliya Kovaliv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuliya_Kovaliv).[^cite_note-9]

On 4 November 2021, the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) (Ukraine's national parliament) appointed Svyrydenko as [First Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Ukraine) and [Minister of Economic Development and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_\(Ukraine\)). 256 MPs voted for her appointment.[^cite_note-10]

In August 2022, the [Shmyhal Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmyhal_Government) authorised Svyrydenko to head the Interagency Working Group on the Implementation of the State Sanctions Policy.[^cite_note-11] She negotiated with other countries to strengthen [sanctions against](70323386.md) Russia, in particular with representatives of the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-12]

## Prime Minister of Ukraine (2025–2026)

Main article: [Svyrydenko Government](80462641.md)

On 14 July 2025, President Zelenskyy announced a government reshuffle and nominated Svyrydenko as [Prime Minister of Ukraine](1256678.md).[^cite_note-13] Her appointment was approved by the Verkhovna Rada on 17 July.[^cite_note-voted_in-4] Svyrydenko is the second female prime minister of Ukraine since [Ukrainian independence in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), following in [Yulia Tymoshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Tymoshenko)'s footsteps.[^cite_note-Svyridenkocq6mpm7vg90o-1]

Exactly a year after being named prime minister, Svyrydenko submitted her resignation which was accepted by the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md). People's Deputy [Oleksiy Honcharenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksiy_Honcharenko) stated "No one can even explain exactly why" with *Reuters* suggesting it had to do with failing to clean house following a corruption scandal. [Serhii Koretskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serhii_Koretskyi) was appointed as her successor.[^cite_note-14]

## Controversy

In 2024, Svyrydenko reported earning [UAH](722696.md) 3,102,506 for her work at the [Kyiv School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_School_of_Economics) (KSE).[^cite_note-15] KSE president [Tymofiy Mylovanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tymofiy_Mylovanov) confirmed that she receives approximately \$6,000 per month for her teaching duties.[^cite_note-16] Mylovanov, in turn, is a member of the supervisory board of NNEGC [Energoatom](5712443.md), appointed by the Cabinet of Ministers when Svyrydenko was [Minister of Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_\(Ukraine\)).[^cite_note-17] Some media and MPs (notably [Volodymyr Ariev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Ariev)) have raised concerns about a potential conflict of interest, pointing out that KSE-affiliated structures implemented grant programmes in which the Ministry of Economy (led by Svyrydenko) was a beneficiary.[^cite_note-18]

According to an investigation piece by the Ukrainian online newspaper *[Ukrainska Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainska_Pravda)* published in July 2024 Svyrydenko only achieved minor achievements during her time at the Economy Ministry (including small-business grant programmes and work tied to EU "Ukraine Facility" support) and on the other hand had received major criticisms from unnamed officials about project outcomes and leadership style. The same reporting described her close working relationship with the [Office of the President](19362024.md) and the [first lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lady_of_Ukraine) ([Olena Zelenska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olena_Zelenska)) during official travel; the article presented these as part of the political context around her prospective promotion at the time.[^cite_note-Pravda2024-19] In the same article the authors claimed that mid-2024, President Zelenskyy repeatedly discussed with Svyrydenko the possibility of her succeeding [Denys Shmyhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denys_Shmyhal) as prime minister. The outlet reported mixed views within the governing camp about her readiness to lead the cabinet, noting both supporters who emphasised her proximity to the president's office and critics who questioned whether she could command the entire system.[^cite_note-Pravda2024-19]

## Family and personal life

Svyrydenko is married to Serhiy Derlemenko (born in 1979).[^cite_note-Derlemenko62613-8][^cite_note-Svyrydenko2872004-20][^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2] The couple has one daughter.[^cite_note-Svyridenko62456-2]

According to Ukrainian biographies, Svyrydenko speaks English, Russian, Chinese, and Japanese.[^cite_note-Svyrydenko2872004-20] Her hobby is playing piano.[^cite_note-Svyrydenko2872004-20]

## Awards

Then-US secretary of commerce Gina Raimondo included Svyrydenko's profile in *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine's *TIME100 Next*, where she was described as "emblematic of the Ukrainian people's resilience" (referring to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)).[^cite_note-21]
