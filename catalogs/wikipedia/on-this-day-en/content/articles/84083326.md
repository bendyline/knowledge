[Harald V](66069.md), [King of Norway](4154892.md), died at [Oslo University Hospital Rikshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_University_Hospital,_Rikshospitalet) on 28 August 2026, at the age of 89. Harald had been admitted to the hospital since 17 August and was being treated for [hemolytic anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemolytic_anemia) and a [bloodstream infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodstream_infection). He was succeeded by his son, Crown Prince Haakon, who became [King Haakon VIII](98258.md).&#x20;

Harald lay in state at the [Slottskapellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slottskapellet_\(Oslo\)) from 31 August to 8 September. His [state funeral](714370.md) took place on 9 September at [Oslo Cathedral](1255835.md), followed by a procession to [Akershus Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus_Fortress) for private interment in the [Royal Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mausoleum_\(Norway\)). The service was attended by members of the [Norwegian royal family](2578348.md), [Norwegian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_government), foreign royal families, and foreign heads of state, government and diplomatic missions.&#x20;

## Background



Harald in May 2026

In 1991, Harald became King of Norway at the age of 53 following the death of his father, [Olav V](66068.md). Since the early 2000s, he had experienced chronic health issues.[^cite_note-\:3-1] He was diagnosed with [bladder cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder_cancer) in 2003, and in 2005 he underwent heart surgery. In 2020, he underwent heart surgery again to replace the artificial heart valve he had received at his previous operation.[^cite_note-2]

He had a leg operation in January 2022.[^cite_note-3] Harald was hospitalised in August with a fever and again in December for an infection, and was admitted once more in May 2023 shortly before [Constitution Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Day_\(Norway\)). He tested positive for [coronavirus](63030231.md) in March 2022 and again in October 2023.[^cite_note-Health-4] In January 2024, the Royal House announced that he was on sick leave due to a respiratory infection.[^cite_note-5]

While on holiday in Malaysia in February 2024, Harald was hospitalised in [Langkawi](422472.md) with a low heart rate and had a temporary [pacemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker) implanted.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] He was discharged on 3 March and transported back to Norway on a medical evacuation flight, after which he was placed on two weeks' sick leave.[^cite_note-8] He received a permanent pacemaker on 12 March[^cite_note-9] and was discharged two days later.[^cite_note-10]

In February 2026, he was hospitalised while on holiday in [Tenerife](269223.md) with his wife, [Queen Sonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Sonja_of_Norway), and treated for a skin infection on his leg.[^cite_note-11]

Prior to his death, Harald had been scheduled to visit the municipalities of [Rindal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rindal_Municipality), [Høylandet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8ylandet_Municipality), [Siljan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siljan_Municipality), and [Nissedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissedal_Municipality) in September and October 2026. These were the only municipalities he had not yet visited in an official capacity during his reign.[^cite_note-12] He did not intend to [abdicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication), arguing that he had taken a lifelong constitutional oath before the [Storting](172897.md) when he acceded the throne in 1991.[^cite_note-13]

## Final hospitalisation and death



Harald died at [Oslo University Hospital Rikshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_University_Hospital,_Rikshospitalet), where he had been admitted ten days earlier.

On 17 August 2026, Harald was admitted to [Oslo University Hospital Rikshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_University_Hospital,_Rikshospitalet) with fluid retention caused by [cortisone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortisone) treatment for hemolytic anaemia and was placed on two weeks' sick leave.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:0-15] His son, Crown Prince Haakon, was designated [regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent) during his hospitalisation.[^cite_note-\:0-15] On 24 August, the Royal House said his condition had worsened and that he was receiving antibiotics for a bloodstream infection.[^cite_note-16]

On 27 August, the Royal House announced that his condition had become "extremely serious" and that Haakon and Queen Sonja had cancelled all of their engagements.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:9-18][^cite_note-\:10-19] It was also reported that members of the family were visiting the hospital to be at his bedside.[^cite_note-\:9-18][^cite_note-\:10-19][^cite_note-20] The Royal House later stated that his condition had remained unchanged.[^cite_note-21] In response to the news, several officials cancelled or curtailed their travels,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] including President of the Storting [Masud Gharahkhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masud_Gharahkhani),[^cite_note-24] Prime Minister [Jonas Gahr Støre](2926932.md),[^cite_note-25] President of the Sámi Parliament [Silje Karine Muotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silje_Karine_Muotka), and the country's bishops, who were on a study trip to [Rome](25458.md).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Harald died at the hospital at 06\:35 ([CEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Summer_Time)) on 28 August, aged 89.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The Royal House announced his death at 08\:35.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

## Succession



[Haakon VIII](98258.md) on the day he swore his oath to the Constitution before the [Storting](172897.md)

Upon Harald's death, his son Haakon immediately ascended the throne as King of Norway.[^cite_note-32] On the same day, he met his government ministers for an extraordinary Council of State where he announced Haakon VIII as his [regnal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regnal_name), and renewed the "Alt for Norge" motto as his [own royal motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_mottos_of_Norwegian_monarchs); all monarchs since Haakon VII have used the same motto.[^cite_note-33] He took the oath to the Constitution before the Storting in a ceremony on 1 September.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## National mourning

A period of [national mourning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_days_of_mourning_\(2020%E2%80%93present\)) was declared leading up to the day of his funeral.[^cite_note-36] King Haakon VIII declared a period of court mourning, during which most of the royal family's engagements were cancelled, lasting until 9 October, one month after the funeral.[^cite_note-37]

On 30 August, memorial services were held in churches throughout Norway. The royal family attended a service at Asker Church, near the royal estate of [Skaugum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skaugum).[^cite_note-38]

### Lying in state

On the evening of 28 August, Harald's coffin was taken from the hospital to the Royal Palace. More than an estimated 10,000 people lined the streets of Oslo with flowers, Norwegian flags, and cards.[^cite_note-39] Upon arrival at the palace, it was placed in the Red Salon.[^cite_note-AP1-40]

On 31 August, Harald's coffin, draped in the [royal standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_standard_of_Norway), was carried by his children and grandchildren in procession from the Red Salon to the [Slottskapellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slottskapellet_\(Oslo\)), the chapel of the Royal Palace.[^cite_note-AP1-40] The [crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Norway) and the royal sword were carried before the coffin while the insignia of the king's Norwegian, British, Danish and Swedish honours were carried on cushions in the procession.[^cite_note-NRK3-41][^cite_note-NRK4-42] At the end of the procession, the crown and sword were placed on the coffin and the insignia were placed at its foot.[^cite_note-NRK3-41] The bier was surrounded by candelabra and floral tributes from Harald's family.[^cite_note-NRK4-42]

Harald lay in state in the chapel from 31 August until the morning of the funeral.[^cite_note-NRK2-43] The coffin was guarded by cadets from the [Norwegian Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Military_Academy), [Royal Norwegian Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Air_Force_Academy) and [Royal Norwegian Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Naval_Academy).[^cite_note-44] The chapel opened to members of the public for viewing from 14\:00 on 1 September and remained open daily until 8 September.[^cite_note-NRK2-43]

## State funeral



View of mourners at Torggata near [Oslo Cathedral](1255835.md), some from the roofs.

### Procession to Oslo Cathedral

At 12\:00 (CEST) on 9 September, Harald's body was moved from the Slottskapellet in procession down [Karl Johans gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Johans_gate) to [Oslo Cathedral](1255835.md) on a gun carriage pulled by a [Mercedes-Benz G-Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_G-Class), flanked by members of the King's [adjutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjutant) staff.[^cite_note-45] The procession was led by mounted police, [His Majesty the King's Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty_the_King's_Guard), the Norwegian Army Band and the [Lord Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chamberlain_\(Norway\)). The King, Crown Princess, Prince Sverre Magnus, Princess Märtha Louise and foreign dignitaries walked behind. The Queen and Queen Sonja followed immediately behind the Norwegian royal family by car.[^cite_note-Procession-46][^cite_note-Henley-47] During the procession, a [21-gun salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21-gun_salute) was fired from all of the Norwegian Armed Forces' designated saluting locations across the country, including Akershus Fortress.[^cite_note-Procession-46][^cite_note-Henley-47][^cite_note-48]

### Funeral service

Harald's state funeral took place at [Oslo Cathedral](1255835.md) on 9 September 2026 at 13\:00. The service followed the funeral liturgy of the [Church of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Norway) and was presided over by the [Preses of the Bishops' Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preses_\(Church_of_Norway\)), [Olav Fykse Tveit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olav_Fykse_Tveit), assisted by the [Bishop of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Oslo), [Sunniva Gylver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunniva_Gylver).[^cite_note-49]

Harald was eulogised by Gharahkhani and Prime Minister Støre. A candle was lit by representatives of different faiths. The sermon was given by the Preses. The first lesson, taken from the [Gospel of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_John), was read in [Northern Sámi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_S%C3%A1mi) by the vice chair of the [Sami Church Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_Church_Council_\(Church_of_Norway\)), Mathis Eira. The second lesson, taken from the [First Epistle to the Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Epistle_to_the_Corinthians), was read by Norwegian actor Ameli Isungset Agbota. The [Prayer of Saint Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayer_of_Saint_Francis) was read in [Swedish](26689.md) by Harald's goddaughter [Crown Princess Victoria of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Princess_Victoria_of_Sweden).[^cite_note-OOS-50]

Music was performed throughout the service. It included the hymns "No stig vår song", composed for Harald and Sonja's 70th birthdays in 2007 by Norwegian lyricist [Edvard Hoem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Hoem) and composer [Håkon Berge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A5kon_Berge), "[Deilig er jorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deilig_er_jorden)", and the English hymn "[Abide with Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abide_with_Me)"; the Norwegian folk songs "[Eg veit i himmerik ei borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eg_veit_i_himmerik_ei_borg)" and "Jørgen Hattemaker", the latter by [Alf Prøysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Pr%C3%B8ysen) and [Bjarne Amdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjarne_Amdahl); and choral settings of [Psalm 121](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_121) by [Arild Sandvold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arild_Sandvold) and the [Nunc dimittis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunc_dimittis) by [Kjell Habbestad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kjell_Habbestad). Two additional pieces were composed for the service by cathedral cantor Marcus André Berg. The [royal anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongesangen) was also sung.[^cite_note-OOS-50]

After the service, Harald's coffin was transported in procession to [Akershus Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus_Fortress) for private burial in the [Royal Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mausoleum_\(Norway\)).[^cite_note-NRK2-43]

## Reactions

### Domestic



A digital billboard at [Oslo Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Central_Station) showing a picture of King Harald on 29 August

Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and other ministers signed a book of condolences at the Royal Palace. Støre said that the country was "united in grief and gratitude".[^cite_note-bbc_moench-80] Støre and Gharakhani both addressed the nation following Harald's death.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Oslo mayor [Anne Lindboe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Lindboe) postponed her wedding ceremony to Pål Sandvid, which had been scheduled for 28 August.[^cite_note-83]

The [Football Association of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_Norway) announced the postponement of matches in the top four men's divisions and the top three women's divisions for 48 hours.[^cite_note-84] Footballer [Erling Haaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erling_Haaland), who had met with the king following [Norway](203277.md)'s participation in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), paid tribute to him on social media.[^cite_note-ReutersR-85][^cite_note-86] Haaland and fellow Norwegian footballer [Jørgen Strand Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B8rgen_Strand_Larsen) later wore black mourning bands during a [Crystal Palace](385313.md) vs [Manchester City](165813.md) [Premier League](11250.md) fixture.[^cite_note-87]

On the day of Harald's death, members of the Norwegian cycling team [Uno-X Mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uno-X_Mobility_\(men's_team\)) wore black mourning bands at Stage 7 of the [2026 Vuelta a España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a); a minute of silence was held before the stage began.[^cite_note-\:1-88] Uno-X Mobility finished with a one-two result, with [Andreas Leknessund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Leknessund) winning the stage and [Tobias Halland Johannessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Halland_Johannessen) placing second.[^cite_note-89] After their victory, Leknessund said the day had been emotional and dedicated his win to the deceased king and to Norway.[^cite_note-\:1-88]

The advertising agency [JCDecaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JCDecaux)'s digital billboards in Norway replaced their regular advertisements with a tribute to Harald for 24 hours.[^cite_note-90]

No prohibitions on cultural events were issued in the aftermath of his death, and some concerts and festivals scheduled for that weekend proceeded mostly as planned.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

Acting Director of the [Peace Research Institute Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Research_Institute_Oslo) Haakon Gjerløw expressed his condolences and "wish\[ed] the Royal Family strength in the time ahead."[^cite_note-93] [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) Rector Ragnhild Hennum highlighted Harald's contributions to the university, including laying the stone of the University Library at Blindern and the annual awarding of His Majesty The King's Gold Medal.[^cite_note-94]

### International

#### Leaders

King [Frederik X](339155.md) and [Queen Mary of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_of_Denmark) sent a letter of condolence to King [Haakon VIII](98258.md), in which Frederik referred to Harald as "Uncle Harald" and recalled the close relationship between the Norwegian and [Danish royal families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_royal_family). They described Harald as a "strong unifying figure" during his reign and emphasised the longstanding [ties between both countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway_relations).[^cite_note-95] The King and Queen ordered flags at [Amalienborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg) to be flown at half-mast on the day of his death.[^cite_note-96] Prime Minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) said Harald was a "highly respected" figure who symbolised unity and continuity for Norway and the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries).[^cite_note-97]

King [Carl XVI Gustaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf) of Sweden announced a period of court mourning, with flags at Swedish royal palaces flown at half-mast.[^cite_note-98] Crown Princess [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Crown_Princess_of_Sweden), who was Harald's godchild, released a statement calling him "an extraordinarily nice person" and "a role model".[^cite_note-99] Swedish prime minister [Ulf Kristersson](3246360.md) described Harald as a "uniting force" among the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries).[^cite_note-bbc_moench-80] Swedish opposition leader [Magdalena Andersson](41178429.md) said Harald had been with Norwegians during their best and most difficult moments.[^cite_note-100]

Finland's president [Alexander Stubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stubb) said Harald would be remembered for his duties as head of state, his "ability to unite Norwegians", and his "commitment to maintaining hope and confidence".[^cite_note-101] President of Iceland [Halla Tomasdottir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halla_Tomasdottir) also expressed her condolences.[^cite_note-102]

King [Charles III](125248.md) expressed "profound sadness" at the death of his "dear cousin" and said Harald had served with dedication and guided Norway through periods of change with warmth and humility, and expressed condolences to Queen Sonja.[^cite_note-bbc_moench-80][^cite_note-103] The [Union Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Flag) was flown at half‑mast at major [royal residences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Residences_of_the_United_Kingdom) and UK Government buildings, and at [British Armed Forces](31730.md) installations in recognition of Harald's honorary appointments in the British Armed Forces.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] Flags at civic offices in [Epping Forest District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_District) were lowered,[^cite_note-107] and the government of the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man) recalled Harald and Sonja's 2002 visit.[^cite_note-108] In [Shetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shetland), Deputy Convener Catherine Hughson and Lord Lieutenant Lindsay Tulloch expressed condolences, and Norwegian flags were flown at half-mast outside Lerwick Town Hall.[^cite_note-109] Scotland's first minister [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) expressed condolences,[^cite_note-110] and the government of [Gibraltar](7607314.md) flew flags at half-mast.[^cite_note-111]
