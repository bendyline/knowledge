**Sheikh Radwan** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الشيخ رضوان) is a district[^cite_note-1] of [Gaza City](55387.md) located nearly 3 kilometers (1.9 mi) northwest of the city center. It borders [al-Shati camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shati_camp) to the southwest, [Rimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimal) to the south, and [Jabalia](453723.md) to the east. The Sheikh Radwan Cemetery is located in the district. It contains hundreds of graves for [Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) killed in the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md),[^cite_note-2] including [Hamas](13913.md) leaders [Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yassin), [Abdel Aziz al-Rantissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantissi), and [Said Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Siam).[^cite_note-3]

## History

The district is named after Sheikh Radwan whose *[mazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar_\(mausoleum\))* ("mausoleum") is situated on a hilltop in the district with an elevation of 65 meters (213 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-BA\&K75-4][^cite_note-5] The mausoleum formerly served as a [mosque](19894.md), but is currently inactive.[^cite_note-Sharon37-6] Its walls are constructed from ancient building stones and fragments of marble slabs in secondary use. In the 19th century, it was surrounded by ancient trees.[^cite_note-BA\&K75-4] The French explorer [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) who visited it in 1863, speculated that it could have been an ancient [convent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convent), and the gardens around it the remains of its garden.[^cite_note-7]

There are a number of traditions regarding the identity of Sheikh Radwan.[^cite_note-Sharon37-6] [Christian](5211.md) scholars identify him as [Peter the Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Iberian) and the tomb as Peter's [monastery](45856.md),[^cite_note-BA\&K75-4] One local tradition has it that the tomb belongs to the 14th-century *[wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali)* ([Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) saint) Radwan ibn Raslan,[^cite_note-8] a son of the prominent local [sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh), Muhammad al-Batahi.[^cite_note-Sharon37-6] Another tradition claims that Radwan was the brother of al-Batahi and [Sheikh Ijlin](24494793.md) who were all descendants of the second Muslim [caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph), [Umar ibn al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar).[^cite_note-9] Prior to the construction of the [Sayed al-Hashim Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayed_al-Hashim_Mosque) in the [al-Daraj Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraj_Quarter) and the modern biographies of the [Islamic](6037917.md) prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) in the 19th century, local Muslim tradition had it that the modern-day tomb of Sheikh Radwan was the burial place of [Hashim ibn Abd al-Manaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashim_ibn_Abd_al-Manaf), Muhammad's great-grandfather. Historian [Moshe Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Sharon) attributes the varying traditions to the "tendency of believers to hunt for saints' tombs."[^cite_note-Sharon17-10]

### Modern era

In 1971, the [Israeli](9282173.md) government pursued a large scale resettlement scheme in order to control the refugee population and prevent [separatist violence](2646043.md). The idea was to move refugees to better housing so camp areas could be redeveloped and improved.[^cite_note-SchulzHammer2003-11] The plan was part of 9 relocation schemes within the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), parts of nearby [al-Shati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shati) camp were bulldozed and a housing project in Sheikh Radwan was initiated.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Karmi-13][^cite_note-14] Initially, refugee migration to Sheikh Radwan was involuntary since Israeli authorities demolished over 2,000 shelters in the camps to widen the roads for "security reasons" and denied permits for the residents to rebuild their houses.[^cite_note-15] About 8,000 refugees moved into the housing projects of Sheikh Radwan.[^cite_note-16] The [United Nations Relief and Works Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Relief_and_Works_Agency) and the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) were vociferous in their opposition to the Israeli action, saying it was forced resettlement. After the start of the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), Sheikh Radwan was the most consistently active areas in Gaza.[^cite_note-Karmi-13]

[Hamas](13913.md) secretary-general [Abdel Aziz al-Rantisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi) was assassinated in Sheikh Radwan on 17 April 2004 after an [Israeli](177619.md) [Apache gunship](37746.md) fired on his vehicle. One of Rantisi's sons and two bodyguards were also killed.[^cite_note-17] On 3 October 2005, armed members of Hamas assaulted the [Palestinian police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preventive_Security_Service) station in Sheikh Radwan which ended in the death of Hayam Mohammed Nassar, 30, as she stood on the patio of an adjacent balcony.[^cite_note-18] Members of the [Fatah](11104.md) controlled [al-Aqsa Martyrs Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Martyrs_Brigades) arrived to defend the police station, and the Hamas militiamen subsequently withdrew.[^cite_note-19] Since the [take-over of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) by Hamas in 2007, Sheikh Radwan has been considered a stronghold for the organization. On 17 May 2007 Israel unsuccessfully targeted a senior Hamas member in the district, killing a militant and injuring eight others instead.[^cite_note-20] During the [2008–2009 Israel–Gaza conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932009_Israel%E2%80%93Gaza_conflict), an Israeli airstrike against a house in Sheikh Radwan left three civilians dead and 25 injured.[^cite_note-21] The cemetery was closed down during the conflict due to the lack of vacant burial places,[^cite_note-22] and thirty graves were destroyed after an Israeli missile [struck the cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_razing_of_cemeteries_and_necroviolence_against_Palestinians).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

District of Sheikh Radwan in Gaza City, destroyed by [Israeli bombing](76139149.md), February 2025

During the [Gaza war](74998519.md), the neighborhood was subjected to heavy [bombing by Israel](76139149.md), resulting in "unprecedented destruction", including the destruction of homes, mosques, and public areas.[^cite_note-25] In January 2024, [Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Mediterranean_Human_Rights_Monitor) documented [damage to several cemeteries in Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_razing_of_cemeteries_and_necroviolence_against_Palestinians) including the one at Sheikh Radwan.[^cite_note-26]



The ruins of the UNRWA el-Sheikh Radwan health centre in February 2024

In August 2024, it was reported that the Sheikh Radwan Lagoon had become polluted by sewage and [stagnant rainwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_stagnation) as the result of the area's sanitation systems being damaged by the [Gaza war](74998519.md). The lack of infrastructure had led to increase in water-born disease, including [polio](25107.md), [hepatitis A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_A), and [cholera](7591.md), and skin infections.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The [United Nations](31769.md) established [vaccination](32473.md) clinic in Sheikh Radwan in November 2024 to distribute the [polio vaccine](192198.md) to Palestinian children. The clinic was bombed a few hours after opening, injuring two adults and four children. The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) and [Hamas](13913.md) both denied responsibility for the attack.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]
