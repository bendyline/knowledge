**Caritas Internationalis** (Latin pronunciation: [\[kaː.ri.t̪as in̪.t̪er.nat̪.t̪͡s̪iˈɔː.na.lis\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin); lit. 'Charity International') is a confederation of 162 national [Catholic](606848.md) [relief](434679.md), [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_development), and [social service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_services) organisations operating in over 200 countries and territories worldwide.[^cite_note-Britannica-7] The name Caritas Internationalis refers to both the global network of Caritas organisations and to its general secretariat based in [Vatican City](32408.md).

Collectively and individually, their missions are "to serve the poor and to promote charity and justice throughout the world".[^cite_note-MissionStatement-8] Caritas Internationalis is the second-largest international humanitarian aid network in the world after the [International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement](15487.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Times23-10][^cite_note-VN23Interview-11][^cite_note-America23-12][^cite_note-Treccani-13]

## History

### The beginning: *Caritas Catholica*

In 1891, [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII)'s encyclical *[Rerum novarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rerum_novarum)* was published, addressing the condition of the working classes.[^cite_note-RerumNovarum-14] *Rerum Novarum* is considered a foundational text of modern [Catholic social teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_social_teaching) and provides the ideological background for the work of Caritas.[^cite_note-Britannica-7] In this context, the first Caritas organisations began to be established, with [Caritas Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Germany) being the very first, founded by [Lorenz Werthmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_Werthmann) in 1897 in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg).[^cite_note-DF-15] In the following years, national Caritas organisations were set up in other countries, including [Caritas Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Switzerland) (1901), [Caritas Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Austria) (1903), [Catholic Charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Charities) in the United States (1910) and [Caritas Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Czech_Republic) (1922).[^cite_note-merkur22-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The first Caritas at the diocesan level was set up in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), which is now part of France but was then part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), in 1903.[^cite_note-strasbourg-19][^cite_note-caritasmoselle-20] In 1916, in the context of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_1), Caritas was recognised as the single official umbrella organisation for the German Catholic Church's relief operations.[^cite_note-DF-15] By 1922, all German dioceses had established their own Caritas association.[^cite_note-HistoryCaritasDE-21]



The 1924 [Eucharistic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharistic_Congress) was a catalyst for the cross-border exchange and cooperation between Caritas organisations.

In July 1924, during the 27th International [Eucharistic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharistic_Congress) in [Amsterdam](844.md), an international conference on charity was held. This conference saw the participation of delegates from Austria, Belgium, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, Hungary, Italy, the Netherlands, Russia, and Switzerland. The primary objective of the conference was to discuss Catholic charitable activities in various countries. These discussions revealed a plethora of charitable activities occurring, yet without effective cooperation and coordination.[^cite_note-Witness16-22]

As a result, most participants advocated for the establishment of a secretariat for charitable activities within each [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese). This centralised management would aim to consolidate various charitable efforts within a diocese, allowing for national and international collaboration. This network of organisations was subsequently named *Caritas Catholica* and formally established in 1928, with its headquarters at Caritas Switzerland in [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucerne). Wilhelm Kissling, the director of the [Swiss Caritas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Switzerland), was appointed the first General Secretary, while Paul Müller-Sirnonis, the director of Caritas [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace), became the inaugural president.[^cite_note-LONSEA-23][^cite_note-MullerSirnonis-24] The secretariat was organised into six specialised sections: youth, knowledge and teaching of charity, aid to the poor and to families, immigration and housing, assistance to the sick, and the fight against [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism).[^cite_note-Witness16-22]

The establishment of Caritas Catholica was reported to the [Secretariat of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_of_State_\(Holy_See\)) of the [Holy See](13393.md) and the delegates of Caritas Catholica met every two years. Delegations were also present at some international conferences until the outbreak of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), when all activities came to a standstill.[^cite_note-Witness16-22]

A few national Caritas organisations were set up during or right after the war to provide and coordinate aid for the populations most in need, including the American [Catholic Relief Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Relief_Services) (1943), the French [Secours catholique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secours_catholique) (1946) and [Caritas Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Japan) (1946).[^cite_note-CRSRW-25][^cite_note-croixhistoir-26][^cite_note-27]

### Formal establishment in 1951

The work of Caritas Catholica resumed in 1947, with the approval of the [Secretariat of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_of_State_\(Holy_See\)). Two conferences were convened in Lucerne to help coordinate efforts and collaboration. Caritas was given a further endorsement when the Secretariat of State entrusted it with the official representation of all Catholic welfare organisations at the international level, especially at the [United Nations](31769.md). The [Holy Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Year) in 1950 saw the beginning of a union of Caritas organisations.[^cite_note-LetterJP04-28] Following a suggestion by Monsignor Giovanni Montini, then Substitute Secretary of State and later [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI), a study week, with participants from 22 countries, was held in [Rome](25458.md) to examine the problems of Christian Caritas work. As a result, the decision was made to set up an "International Conference of Catholic Charities".[^cite_note-UIA-29]

On 21 October 1951, the [Holy See](13393.md) approved the statutes of this international organisation *ad experimentum* and from 12 to 14 December 1951, the constitutive assembly of Caritas Internationalis took place. Founding members came from Caritas organisations in 13 countries: [Austria](26964606.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Denmark](76972.md), [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Luxembourg](17515.md), [Netherlands](21148.md), [Portugal](23033.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Switzerland](26748.md), and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-Witness16-22][^cite_note-Bucher-30][^cite_note-Caritasin-31]

The first elected officials of the organisation were the Italian Bishop [Ferdinando Baldelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinando_Baldelli), who served as President of Caritas Internationalis until 1962, and the German [Carlo Bayer](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo%20Bayer), who remained the Secretary General until 1970.[^cite_note-gcatholic-32] In 1957, the International Conference of Catholic Charities changed its name to Caritas Internationalis to reflect the international presence of Caritas members on every continent.[^cite_note-namechange1-33][^cite_note-Treccani-13][^cite_note-CorUnum-3]

### Internationalisation throughout the 1950s and 1960s



The [North Sea flood of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1953) were a major emergency of the 1950s in which Caritas Internationalis coordinated the Catholic relief efforts.

Throughout the rest of the 1950s, new national Caritas organisations were founded and joined Caritas Internationalis, including [Caritas Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Hong_Kong) in 1953,[^cite_note-34] [Caritas Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Syria) in 1954,[^cite_note-35] and [Caritas Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Brazil) and [Caritas Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Colombia) in 1956.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-Cadena-37] At the same time, the French [Secours catholique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secours_catholique) set up local sections in the French colonies in Africa, which would later become independent Caritas organisations, such as the current [Caritas Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Burkina_Faso), first established in 1956, or the current [Caritas Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Madagascar) in 1959.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-MigrantsRef-39] By 1957, Caritas Internationalis had 37 member organisations.[^cite_note-Witness30-40]

Caritas Internationalis coordinated the relief efforts of its members in response to emergencies in Europe, such as the [1953 floods in the Netherlands and Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1953), the [1953 Ionian earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Ionian_earthquake), and the displacement of 200,000 Hungarian refugees after the [1956 uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956). In February 1957, Caritas Internationalis established an Emergency Aid Commission. Two years later, the Confederation also took its first steps in [advocacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy). The economic prosperity that many [Western](21208200.md) countries experienced highlighted that other parts of the world were still facing hunger and misery. In 1959, at the initiative of Cardinal [Joseph Frings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Frings), the German bishops decided to launch a joint [Lenten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent) campaign to alleviate hunger, poverty and disease in what was then called the "[Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World)". The idea of Lenten campaigns and using the raised funds to implement charitable projects was adopted in many countries and remains a major fundraising tool for many Caritas organisations worldwide.[^cite_note-Witness27-41]



In 1962, the Chilean Cardinal [Raúl Silva Henríquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Silva_Henr%C3%ADquez) was elected as president of Caritas Internationalis.

Caritas collaborated with the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) on its five-year "Freedom from Hunger" campaign, launched in 1960.[^cite_note-42] During its fifth General Assembly in 1960, the confederation called for locally based [development aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid) in addition to emergency aid. The Confederation increasingly prioritized development as more Caritas organisations became development actors within their own countries. Consequently, a dedicated service for this purpose was established in the general secretariat in Rome.[^cite_note-Witness31-43] In 1962, the Chilean Cardinal [Raúl Silva Henríquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Silva_Henr%C3%ADquez) was elected as president of Caritas Internationalis.[^cite_note-44]

The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) (1962–1965) opened up the Church to the modern world. It emphasized the value of the work done by [laypeople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laity), called for dialogue with other religions, and urged Christians to promote the development of poorer regions and social justice among nations. In 1967, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) published *[Populorum progressio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populorum_progressio)*, an [encyclical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclical) on the topic of "the development of peoples" which would become a foundational text for the work of Caritas Internationalis and its members. It prompted Catholic charities such as Caritas to contemplate their role within the Church and the contemporary world, reinforcing the notion that social action should be grounded in serving those most in need and that initiatives should be developed collaboratively with the communities.[^cite_note-cidsepop-45]

That same year, [CIDSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIDSE) was established to coordinate the tasks identified by the Second Vatican Council as priorities for the Church, such as caring for the poor and oppressed and focusing on global justice. Since then, there has been close cooperation between Caritas Internationalis and CIDSE, with some Caritas member organisations also belonging to CIDSE.

Emergency relief efforts and coordination continued in the 1960s, including reconstruction after the [1962 Buin Zahra earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Buin_Zahra_earthquake) and the [1963 Skopje earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Skopje_earthquake), as well as hunger and relief campaign after repeated famines in India and Pakistan. Caritas was a major humanitarian actor during the [Biafran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biafran_War) (1967–1970)[^cite_note-Omenka2011-46][^cite_note-47] and [Caritas Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Jerusalem) was founded to coordinate the Catholic relief efforts after the [1967 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

In 1969, following the [International Year of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Year_of_Human_Rights) (1968), the General Assembly of Caritas Internationalis adopted a "Declaration on the Rights of Developing Nations". It supported the fundamental principled of the [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights) and the [International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Economic,_Social_and_Cultural_Rights).[^cite_note-Witness50-50] This marked the beginning of Caritas Internationalis adopting a more outward-facing advocacy approach, involving closer cooperation and engagement on the global stage through its UN delegations.

### Professionalisation and further expansion in the 1970s and 1980s

On 19 July 1976, at the initiative of [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI), Caritas Internationalis was granted [civil juridical personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juridical_person) by the Vatican City State.[^cite_note-CorUnum-3]

The seven regions that currently form the Caritas confederation were established in the 1970s to enhance its effectiveness and unity. Regional assemblies were created and held during the General Assemblies of the confederation. Their primary task was to develop regional work plans, elect regional presidents, and appoint commissions to support the overall work of the Caritas Internationalis general secretariat. This structure aimed to foster increased cooperation among organisations within the same region, ensuring a more cohesive and impactful approach to their humanitarian missions.[^cite_note-Witness63-51] Additionally, the first common manual on how to implement disaster aid was adopted in 1979.[^cite_note-Witness65-52]



In the 1980s, Caritas Internationalis responded among other to the [1985 Mexico City earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Mexico_City_earthquake).

In the 1970s and 1980s, the Confederation responded to a series of global humanitarian emergencies, including [droughts in the Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_in_the_Sahel),[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] the [influx of Vietnamese refugees arriving in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_refugee_detention_centres_in_Hong_Kong) starting in 1982,[^cite_note-55] the [1985 Mexico City earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Mexico_City_earthquake),[^cite_note-56] the humanitarian emergency during the [First Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) (1980–1988), the [1988 Bangladesh cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Bangladesh_cyclone), and the extreme winter conditions in Europe at the end of the 1980s.[^cite_note-Witness82-57]

[Caritas Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Lebanon) became a major humanitarian actor during the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) (1975–1990).[^cite_note-58] Together with the [Lutheran World Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_World_Federation) and the [World Council of Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Council_of_Churches), Caritas Internationalis formed the Churches Drought Action for Africa, later renamed Joint Relief Programme, to respond to the communities affected by the [1983–1985 famine in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%931985_famine_in_Ethiopia) and later the victims of violence and hunger during the [Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-EthiopiaUN83-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

In terms of advocacy, Caritas Internationalis focused on promoting the inclusion of persons with disabilities. Notably, the organisation participated in United Nations sessions aimed at assessing and implementing recommendations from the [International Year of Disabled Persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Year_of_Disabled_Persons) (1981). Another significant area of focus was the promotion of women's participation in programmes. Caritas Internationalis played an active role in the [1980 World Conference on Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Conference_on_Women,_1980) and, in 1983, [Caritas Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Latin_America_and_Caribbean) organised a dedicated event in Panama focusing on the role of women in development.[^cite_note-Witness87-63]

In 1983, the Angolan Cardinal [Alexandre do Nascimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_do_Nascimento) succeeded the German priest Georg Hüssler as President of Caritas Internationalis.

### The 1990s and 2000s marked by humanitarian emergencies

#### Humanitarian action

The 1990s and were marked by numerous humanitarian crises to which the Caritas confederation responded, including the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) (1991–1995),[^cite_note-64] the [famine in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_famine_in_Somalia) (1992,[^cite_note-vienot357-65] the [Rwandan Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Genocide) (1994) and the subsequent [Great Lakes refugee crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_refugee_crisis) (1994–1996) and [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War) (1996–1997),[^cite_note-66] as well as the [Kosovo War](16760.md) (1998–1999).[^cite_note-67]

When the [War in Darfur](5043637.md) began in 2003, the British Caritas member [CAFOD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAFOD) took the lead in coordinating the confederation's cooperation with [Action by Churches Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_Alliance). This joint initiative was known as the Darfur Emergency Response Operation (DERO).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Caritas Internationalis also coordinated the confederation's response after the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), with [Caritas Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Iraq?action=edit\&redlink=1) being one of the few humanitarian organisations operational in the country.[^cite_note-70]

In the wake of the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami), Caritas Internationalis raised around [US\$](18717338.md)450,000,000 for the initial emergency phase and the long-term recovery phase implemented by Caritas organisations in India, Indonesia, Sri Lanka, Thailand, and other countries. The latter phase included construction of earthquake-resistant houses and community infrastructure, job creation, and social assistance.[^cite_note-TsunamiRW-71] After the [2010 Haiti earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake), Caritas Internationalis and its member organisations provided humanitarian relief and rehabilitation support to over 1.5 million Haitians, using a budget of [US\$](18717338.md)176,000,000.[^cite_note-HaitiRW-72]

When the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) erupted in 2011, [Caritas Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Syria), [Caritas Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Lebanon), and [Caritas Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Jordan) were the three Caritas agencies providing the largest response in support of the internally-displaced persons and the refugees. Two years later, Caritas Internationalis member organisations responded to the [Western African Ebola virus epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_African_Ebola_virus_epidemic) in Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia,[^cite_note-73] and when [Typhoon Haiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Haiyan) devastated large parts of the country in November 2013, [Caritas Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Philippines) organised large-scale relief and reconstruction efforts and [Caritas Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Bangladesh) has been heavily involved in the support to the [Rohingya refugees arriving from Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide) in 2017. [Caritas Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Lebanon) was faced with an additional emergency when the [2020 Beirut explosion](64752725.md) destroyed large parts of the city, and [Caritas Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Congo) has been a major humanitarian actor in the [Democratic Republic of Congo](76762.md) for decades.

#### Other work



Caritas Internationalis was one of many organisations involved in the [World Social Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Social_Forum) (here pictured in 2003 in [Porto Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Alegre)).

Nonetheless, Caritas also continued its work as social service provider and development actor around the world. For example [Caritas Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Hong_Kong), greatly expanded its educational and healthcare services.[^cite_note-74] In Iraq, [Caritas Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Iraq?action=edit\&redlink=1) assisted more than 250,000 persons with food programmes alone and between 1995 and 2000.[^cite_note-75] [Caritas Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Austria) expanded its support to children, people with disabilities, refugees, the homeless, the elderly, young mothers, people suffering from addictions, and the long-term unemployed.[^cite_note-SalzburgHist-76]

Simultaneously, a new global crisis emerged with the spreading of [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS). In 1999, Caritas Internationalis signed a memorandum of understanding with the [Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_United_Nations_Programme_on_HIV/AIDS), agreeing to collaborating in raising awareness about the virus and caring for those affected.[^cite_note-StudyContr09-77]

In the late 1990s, Caritas also focused on [peacebuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacebuilding), notably in Colombia where [Caritas Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Colombia) started implementing peacebuilding projects and became involved in [truth and reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_reconciliation_in_Colombia) initiatives. In 2000, it convened the first National Congress of Reconciliation.[^cite_note-historia-78]

In the 2000s, Caritas became involved in the [World Social Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Social_Forum),[^cite_note-79] with 450 delegates from around 40 Caritas organisations participating in the [Porto Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Alegre) summit in 2003.[^cite_note-vienot170-80]

On 16 September 2004, [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) granted public, juridical, and [canonical personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person_\(Catholic_canon_law\)) to Caritas Internationalis, recognizing its formal status within the Church and its ability to operate with a greater degree of autonomy and authority.[^cite_note-CorUnum-3][^cite_note-LetterJP04-28] He also entrusted the task of "supervising and guiding the activity of Caritas Internationalis" to the [Pontifical Council Cor Unum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Council_Cor_Unum).[^cite_note-LetterJP04-28]

Caritas Internationalis launched its "[HAART](203312.md) for Children" advocacy campaign in 2009, calling on governments and pharmaceutical companies to develop and provide child-friendly HIV and [tuberculosis](30653.md) medicine.[^cite_note-StudyContr09-77][^cite_note-81] In November 2012, [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) published the [motu proprio](793396.md)&#x20;*&#x20;Intima Ecclesiae natura*, which focuses on the principles and guidelines for the charitable activities of the Catholic Church, which explicitly asks of the bishops to "encourage in every parish of his territory the creation of a local Caritas service or a similar body".[^cite_note-intimaeccl-82]

### Since 2019: Controversies and organisational changes

In May 2019, the [Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_Promoting_Integral_Human_Development) became the new Vatican body overseeing the work of the Caritas Internationalis, following the integration of the previous oversight body, the [Pontifical Council Cor Unum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Council_Cor_Unum), into the Dicastery in 2017.[^cite_note-Dicastery19-83]

In November 2019, CNN reported that the Director of [Caritas Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Central_African_Republic?action=edit\&redlink=1), a Belgian Salesian priest, had been convicted in Belgium in 2012 for child sexual abuse and possession of child pornography. CNN also identified two children in the Central African Republic who were allegedly abused by the priest.[^cite_note-CNNfullreport-84] Following this report, the UN temporarily suspended its work with Caritas Central African Republic. Caritas Internationalis issues an apology, stating that it was "saddened and outraged" by the allegations of abuse and said it was working to improve its [safeguarding](11209440.md) policies towards children.[^cite_note-BBC19-85][^cite_note-lacroix19-86]

After the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in early 2022, Caritas in Ukraine and the neighbouring countries implemented a large-scale response to support the internally displaced persons and refugees from Ukraine. In the first two years of the conflict, [Caritas Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Ukraine) and [Caritas-Spes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas-Spes) alone provided humanitarian aid to more than 3.8 million people in Ukraine.[^cite_note-CR24-87]



The Japanese Archbishop [Tarcisio Isao Kikuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarcisio_Isao_Kikuchi) was elected as president of Caritas Internationalis in 2023.

In November 2022, Pope Francis removed the secretary general and the entire leadership and governance team of Caritas Internationalis following an external investigation that uncovered significant management deficiencies impacting staff morale at the Caritas secretariat in Rome. An organisational consultant was appointed as a temporary administrator until elections were held for a new secretary-general in May 2023.[^cite_note-katholisch22-88][^cite_note-domradio22-89][^cite_note-AP22-90] These elections resulted in Alistair Dutton, previously the executive director of [SCIAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCIAF) (Caritas Scotland), winning the position of Secretary-General, and [Archbishop of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Tokyo) Msgr. [Tarcisio Isao Kikuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarcisio_Isao_Kikuchi) being elected as President.[^cite_note-Crux23-91][^cite_note-NCR23-92][^cite_note-tablet23-93]

## Structure

Caritas Internationalis is a global confederation composed of national Caritas organisations from each country. As of 2024, the confederation has 162 members working in over 200 countries and territories. It is coordinated by a general secretariat located in the [Palazzo San Callisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_San_Callisto) in [Rome](25458.md). The members are local [faith-based organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith-based_organisation), usually established by the [episcopal conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_conference) in each country, fulfilling the social mission of the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-TsunamiRW-71] While all member organisations are fully autonomous, they share the same mission and values. Therefore, the Caritas Internationalis secretariat does not wield direct authority over its members, such as the power to appoint or dismiss staff or impose programmes.[^cite_note-AP231119-94] Caritas operates according to the principle of [subsidiarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiarity).[^cite_note-UIA-29] This also explains why the member organisations of Caritas Internationalis are only the national Caritas organisations. In most countries, the national Caritas is itself a federation of multiple Caritas organisations at [diocesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) level, which in turn can be further subdivided into different [parish](23623.md) Caritas organisations.[^cite_note-intimaeccl-82]

The confederation is divided into seven regions:[^cite_note-CorUnum-3] [Caritas Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Africa) (with a secretariat based in [Lomé, Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9,_Togo)), [Caritas Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Asia) ([Bangkok, Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok,_Thailand)), [Caritas Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Europa) ([Brussels, Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels,_Belgium)), [Caritas Middle East and North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Middle_East_and_North_Africa) ([Beirut, Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut,_Lebanon)), Caritas North America (with a decentralised secretriat), [Caritas Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Oceania) (with a decentralised secretariat), and [Caritas Latin America and Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Latin_America_and_Caribbean) ([San José, Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9,_Costa_Rica)).

Some member organisations of Caritas Internationalis focus solely on domestic social welfare, such as [Caritas Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Moldova)[^cite_note-95] or Caritas Singapore, while others, particularly in the [Western world](21208200.md), operate internationally, like [Cordaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordaid) from the Netherlands or [Trócaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%B3caire) from Ireland, which specialize in international development and humanitarian aid. [Caritas Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Spain), [Caritas Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Poland), and [Caritas Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Luxembourg) engage in both domestic and international activities. [Caritas Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Australia) primarily operates as an international humanitarian and development actor abroad but also supports Indigenous Australians locally,[^cite_note-96] while [Caritas Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Japan) focuses mainly on domestic operations while financially assisting relief efforts of other confederation members globally.[^cite_note-AnnualReport23-97]

The size of Caritas organisations varies widely from small volunteer-run entities to some of the largest civil society organisations in their respective countries. For instance, American [Catholic Relief Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Relief_Services) (CRS) is one of the largest international humanitarian NGOs globally, reporting a revenue of US\$923 million in 2020.[^cite_note-CRS2020-98] [Caritas Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Germany) is the largest welfare organisation and private employer in Germany, with almost 700,000 employees.[^cite_note-DF-15] In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, [Caritas Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Congo) fills gaps in essential services where the State is absent and provides 41% of all education services.[^cite_note-educationaaboveall-99]

Many Caritas organisations engaged in international cooperation and humanitarian relief outside their home countries operate as partnership organisations, practising [localisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localisation_\(humanitarian_practice\)). This approach entails supporting local partners, typically local Caritas organisations, in implementing programs. Examples include [Caritas Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Portugal), [Development and Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_and_Peace_\(Canada\)) (Caritas Canada), and [Caritas Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Denmark).[^cite_note-100] Others, such as CRS, [Caritas Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Czech_Republic), and [Caritas Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Switzerland), establish offices in third countries to directly manage humanitarian and development projects.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]
