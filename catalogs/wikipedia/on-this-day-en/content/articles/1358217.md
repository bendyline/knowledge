**Faroese people** or **Faroe Islanders** ([Faroese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_language): *føroyingar*; [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *færinger*) are an [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) [native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) to the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md).[^cite_note-4] The Faroese are of mixed [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) and [British](19097669.md) origins.[^cite_note-\:0-5] About 21,000 Faroese live in neighbouring countries, particularly in [Denmark](76972.md), [Iceland](14531.md) and [Norway](21241.md). Most Faroese are citizens of the [Kingdom of Denmark](11746442.md), in which the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) are a constituent nation. The [Faroese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_language) is one of the [North Germanic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_languages) and is closely related to [Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_language) and to western [Norwegian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language).

## Origins

Faroese politicians, priests and choir in front of the [Løgting](535570.md) (Parliament), [Ólavsøka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93lavs%C3%B8ka) 2012

The first known settlers of the Faroe Islands were [Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaels) [hermits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermit) and [monks](419369.md) who arrived in the 6th century.[^cite_note-6]

The [Norse-Gaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse-Gaels) started going to the island in the ninth century; they brought [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) culture and language to the islands with them. Little is known about this period, thus giving room for speculation. A single source mentions early settlement, the Icelandic [Færeyinga saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A6reyinga_saga). It was written sometime around 1200 and explains events taking place approximately 300 years prior. According to the saga, many [Norsemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) objected to the Norwegian king, Harald Fairhair's unification politics and thus fled to other countries, including the newfound places in the west.[^cite_note-7]

Historians have understood since the time of the Færeyinga saga that the Viking [Grímur Kamban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%ADmur_Kamban) was the first settler in the Faroes. The [Norwegians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians) must have known about the isles before leaving Norway. If Grímur Kamban had settled sometime earlier, this could explain the Norwegians' knowledge of them. Another, more logical explanation might be that the [Gaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaels) of Scotland and Ireland told the Norwegians of the islands.

While Grímur is an [Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse) first name, Kamban indicates a Celtic origin. Thus, he could have been a man from Ireland, Scotland or [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man), where the Vikings already had settlements. Some place names from the oldest settlements on the Faroes suggest that some of the settlers perhaps came from the Scottish islands and the British coast.

Y chromosomes, tracing male descent, are 87% [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia),[^cite_note-8] but [mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA), tracing female descent, is 84% British.[^cite_note-\:0-5]
