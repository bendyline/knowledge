The **North Jutland Region**[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Region Nordjylland*), or in some official sources, the **North Denmark Region**,[^cite_note-6] is an [administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Denmark) of [Denmark](76972.md) established on 1 January 2007 as part of the [2007 Danish municipal reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Danish_Municipal_Reform), which abolished the traditional [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Denmark) (*amter*) and set up five larger regions. At the same time, smaller [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Denmark) were merged into larger units, cutting the number of municipalities from 271 before 1 January 2006, when [Ærø Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%86r%C3%B8_Municipality) was created, to 98. North Jutland Region has 11 municipalities. The reform diminished the power of the regional level dramatically in favor of the local level and the central government in [Copenhagen](5166.md).

## Geography

The North Jutland Region consists of the former [North Jutland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jutland_County) combined with parts of the former [Viborg County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viborg_County) (the former municipalities of [Aalestrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalestrup_Municipality), [Hanstholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanstholm_Municipality), [Morsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mors%C3%B8_Municipality), [Sydthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydthy_Municipality), and [Thisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thisted_Municipality)), and the western half of [Mariager Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariager_Municipality) (in the former [Aarhus County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_County)). It includes islands of [Mors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mors_\(island\)), [Læsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A6s%C3%B8), and [North Jutlandic Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jutlandic_Island).

Geologically, the region lies in the northern part of Denmark, which is rising because of [post-glacial rebound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-glacial_rebound).

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the region was €26.1 billion in 2018, accounting for 8.7% of Denmark's economic output and is the lowest of any region. The GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was €33,200 or 110% of the EU27 average in the same year.[^cite_note-8]

## Transport

[Aalborg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_Airport) is the region's main airport, it is the third-busiest airport in Denmark behind [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) and [Billund Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billund_Airport). The airport provides direct routes to destinations in parts of Denmark, The Netherlands, Norway, Spain, and Turkey, along with seasonal flights to additional Spanish destinations, Greece and Greenland. It handed more than 1.4 million passengers a year.[^cite_note-9]

## Culture and education

One of Denmark's five universities, [Aalborg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_Universitet), is situated in the region. Most of the region's museums are situated in Aalborg such as [the Historical Museum of Northern Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Historical_Museum_of_Northern_Jutland), [KUNSTEN Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KUNSTEN_Museum_of_Modern_Art_Aalborg), [Musikkens Hus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musikkens_Hus), [Utzon Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utzon_Center) and [Aalborg Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_Zoo).

## Regional Council

Each of the five regions of Denmark has a regional council of 41 members. These are elected every four years, during the local elections.
