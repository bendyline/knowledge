**Takhar** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto),[^cite_note-5] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari):[^cite_note-6] تخار) is one of the 34 [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md), located in the northeast of the country next to [Tajikistan](30108.md). It is surrounded by [Badakhshan](1282198.md) in the east, [Panjshir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjshir_Province) in the south, and [Baghlan](457216.md) and [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz_Province) in the west. The city of [Taloqan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taloqan) serves as its capital. The province contains 17 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Afghanistan), over 1,000 villages, and approximately 1,113,173 people,[^cite_note-nsia-4] which is [multi-ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiethnic_society) and mostly a rural society.[^cite_note-7]

### Early history

Main article: [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire)

The region of present-day [Takhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhar_University) Province was historically part of a broader cultural and political zone known as Takharistan, referenced in early [Iranian traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_traditional_medicine). It formed part of ancient [Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria), a major center of civilization in [Central Asia](6742.md). Archaeological evidence suggests that the area was inhabited by [Indo-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Iranians) populations and later came under the influence of [Hellenistic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) following the campaigns of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in the [4th century BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_century_BC).[^cite_note-\:0-8]

One of the most significant archaeological sites in the province is [Ai-Khanoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai-Khanoum), located near the [Amu Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amu_Darya) and the [Kokcha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokcha_River). Established during the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), the city is widely believed to have been a major urban center of the [Greco-Bacterian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Bactrian_Kingdom). Excavations have revealed a well-planned city layout, including administrative buildings, temples, and residential quarters, along with artifacts such as coins, sculptures, and ceramics that reflect a fusion of Greek and local traditions.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

During the early centuries CE, the region became part of the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire), which played a key role in facilitating trade and cultural exchange along the Silk Road. This period saw the spread the spread of Buddhism and the development of [Greco-Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Buddhism) artistic traditions in the wider region. The historical city of [Taloqan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taloqan), which later became the provincial capital, also emerged as an important settlement during antiquity.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

### 7th to 16th centuries

Main article: [Muslim conquests of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquests_of_Afghanistan)

From the [7th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_century) onward, the region of Takhar was gradually incorporated into the expanding Islamic world following [Arab conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_conquests). It became part of successive Islamic dynasties that ruled over [Transoxiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana) and Greater [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan), contributing to the region’s integration into broader political and cultural networks of the [medieval Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Islamic_world).[^cite_note-\:0-8]

During this period, Taliqan developed as a regional center of administration and trade. The population largely adopted Islam, while elements of earlier cultural traditions persisted in local customs and practices.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

In the 13th century, the region experienced significant disruption during the [Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_and_conquests) led by [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan). The city of Ai-Khanoum and other settlements were destroyed or abandoned during this time.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

In the centuries that followed, Takhar came under the control of various regional powers, including the [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire) and later Central Asian [khanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanates_of_Azerbaijan).[^cite_note-\:2-10]

By the late medieval period, Takhar formed part of the wider historical region of [Qataghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qataghan_Province), maintaining its role as a link between Central Asia and northern Afghanistan.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-\:2-10]

### 16th to 20th centuries

Between the early 16th century and the mid-18th century, the territory was ruled by the [Khanate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Bukhara).

It was given to [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) by [Murad Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Murad_Beg) of [Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara) after a treaty of friendship was reached in or about 1750, and became part of the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire). It was ruled by the [Durranis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_dynasty) followed by the [Barakzai dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barakzai_dynasty) and was untouched by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) during the three [Anglo-Afghan wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Afghan_War) that were fought in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

### 1964–2001

It was established in 1964 when [Qataghan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qataghan_Province) was divided into three provinces: Baghlan, Kunduz and Takhar. During the 1980s [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the area fell under the influence of [Rabbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burhanuddin_Rabbani) and [Ahmad Shah Massoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Massoud). It was controlled by the [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance) in the 1990s. It experienced some fighting between the Northern Alliance and the [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan) forces. Takhar holds notoriety as the location where [Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) Commander Ahmad Shah Massoud was assassinated on 9 September 2001 by suspected [al-Qaeda](1921.md) agents.

### 2001–2021

Following the September 11 attacks, commonly known as 9/11, and amidst the United States' intervention, General Mohamad Daud and General Shajahan Noori, both widely recognized figures, played instrumental roles in leading the Afghanistan government forces to reclaim control of the northeastern province from the Taliban.

[International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (ISAF) took over security responsibility of the area in the early 2000s, which was led by [Germany](11867.md). The province also began to see some developments and the establishment of [Afghan National Security Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Security_Forces) (ANSF). In a minor incident in July 2008, the [Afghan National Police](13327465.md) killed Mullah Usman when several armed Taliban militants under his command raided a police checkpoint in the [Kalafgan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalafgan_District). This was the first time since the fall of [Taliban regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)) in 2001 that the Taliban insurgents engaged police in this province. Mullah Usman was the most senior Taliban commander in the northeast region of Afghanistan, according to the [Afghan Interior Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Afghanistan\)).[^cite_note-11]

In May 2009, Taliban insurgents fighting Afghan government attacked the [Baharak district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baharak_District,_Takhar) in Takhar province.[^cite_note-12] A bomb attack on 28 May 2011 killed [General Shahjahan Noori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahjahan_Noori), [Mohammed Daud Daud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Daud_Daud) and injured Governor Taqwa. Several German soldiers and Afghans were also killed.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In April 2012, the water supply at the [Rostaq district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustaq_District)'s school for girls was poisoned by unknown insurgents, sickening at least 140 Afghan schoolgirls and teachers ranging in age from 14 to 30, causing them to be hospitalized and some to partially lose consciousness, though there were no deaths as a result of the incident.[^cite_note-15]

During the 2021 Taliban offensive, Takhar Province experienced attacks as the withdrawal of US troops took place. In response, Mohibullah Noori emerged as a prominent figure, leading the second resistance to support security forces and prevent the Taliban from taking control of Taluqan city. On 8 August 2021, the Taliban eventually gained control of the province but despite this, the Resistance Forces have remained present in the area.

### 2015 earthquake

On 26 October, the 7.5 [Mw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) [Hindu Kush earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2015_Hindu_Kush_earthquake) shook northern [Afghanistan](737.md) with a maximum [Mercalli intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercalli_intensity_scale) of VIII (*Severe*). This earthquake destroyed almost 30,000 homes, left several hundred dead, and more than 1,700 injured.[^cite_note-16]

## Economy

Further information: [Economy of Afghanistan](9895.md)

[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and [mining](17967521.md) are the main industries of the province. Takhar has [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) reserves which are being exploited by hand in some villages and sold in the region. The local population considers [gold](12240.md) the most relevant resource for the Province. Gold is being washed in Takhar River, and about 2 kg are being transported to the specific weekly markets in the city of Taloqan. Also the city is a main source of construction materials like: loam, sand, and different types of stones. Takhar province is known for its salt mountains and you can find large deposits of fine salt in the region. The Takcha Khanna salt mine is one of the growing number of salt supplier, for the population of Takhar and northern Afghanistan. While the mines offer economic opportunities in the region, the availability of iodized salt considerably reduces the prevalence of health problems related to iodine deficiency.

### Population

As of 2021, the total population of the province is about 1,113,173.[^cite_note-nsia-4]

### Ethnicity, languages and religion

Takhar is mostly tribal and a rural society. The main inhabitants of Takhar province are majority [Tajiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks) and [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_people) with aboriginal [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) minority. Other smaller ethnic groups include [Hazaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_people), [Gujars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujar_people) and [Balochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people).
