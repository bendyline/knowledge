**Women's association football**, more commonly known as **women's football** or **women's soccer**,[^cite_note-6] is the [team sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_sport) of [association football](10568.md) played by [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman). It is played at the professional level in [multiple countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_women's_association_football), and about 200 national teams participate [internationally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_competitions_in_women's_association_football).[^cite_note-fifa_wr-7] The same rules, known as the [Laws of the Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_the_Game_\(association_football\)), are used for both women's and men's football.[^cite_note-LotG-8] The United States is the most successful nation in women's football, having won 4 Women's World Cups and 5 Olympic gold medals. The second most successful nation in women's football is Germany, with 2 Women's World Cups and 1 Olympic gold medal.&#x20;

After the "first golden age" of women's football occurred in the United Kingdom in the 1920s, with one match attracting over 50,000 spectators,[^cite_note-9] [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) instituted a ban from 1921 to 1970 in England that disallowed women's football on the grounds used by its member clubs.[^cite_note-BBCFAC21-10] In many other nations, female footballers faced similarly hostile treatment and [bans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bans_of_women's_association_football) by male-dominated organisations.[^cite_note-Drum19-11]

In the 1970s, international women's football tournaments were extremely popular,[^cite_note-Wilson71-12][^cite_note-JJHeritage70-13] and the oldest surviving continental championship was founded, the [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md). However, a woman did not speak at the [FIFA Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Congress) until 1986 ([Ellen Wille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Wille)).[^cite_note-FR24-Gorman-14] The [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) was first held in China in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and has since become a major television event in many countries.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## History

Women may have been playing football for as long as the game has existed. Evidence shows that a similar game (*[cuju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuju)*, also known as *tsu chu*) was played by women during the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (25–220 CE), as female figures are depicted in frescoes of the period playing *tsu chu*.[^cite_note-globalgame-17][^cite_note-footballnetwork-18] Annual matches being played in [Midlothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlothian), Scotland, are reported as early as the 1790s.[^cite_note-SFA-19][^cite_note-emagazine.credit-suisse.com-20]

In 1863, football governing bodies introduced standardised rules to prohibit violence on the pitch, so the sport was considered safe for women to play.[^cite_note-FA-21][^cite_note-Nettie_Honeyball-22] The first match of an international character took place in 1881 at [Hibernian Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibernian_Park) in [Edinburgh](9602.md),[^cite_note-23] part of a tour by Scotland and England teams.[^cite_note-Pink2018-4][^cite_note-Donmouth1881-5] The [Scottish Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Football_Association) recorded a women's match in 1892.[^cite_note-SFA-19]



North team of the British Ladies' Football Club, 1895

The [British Ladies' Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ladies'_Football_Club) was founded by activist [Nettie Honeyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettie_Honeyball) in England in 1894. They played the first women's match officially recorded in England in March 1895.[^cite_note-FA-21] Honeyball is quoted as saying, "I founded the association late last year \[1894], with the fixed resolve of proving to the world that women are not the 'ornamental and useless' creatures men have pictured. I must confess, my convictions on all matters where the sexes are so widely divided are all on the side of [emancipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism), and I look forward to the time when ladies may sit in [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) and have a voice in the direction of affairs, especially those which concern them most".[^cite_note-Nettie_Honeyball-22] Honeyball and those like her paved the way for women's football.[^cite_note-honeyb-24] However, the women's game was frowned upon by the British football associations, and continued without their support. It has been suggested that this was motivated by a perceived threat to the "masculinity" of the game.[^cite_note-25]

### The Munitionettes' Cup



Japanese high-school girls playing football in their traditional hakama with one team wearing sashes (c. 1920)

In August 1917, a tournament was launched for [female munition workers'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munitionettes) teams in north-east England. Officially titled the "Tyne Wear & Tees Alfred Wood Munition Girls Cup", it was also known as "The Munitionettes' Cup".[^cite_note-26] The first winners of the trophy were [Blyth Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blyth_Spartans), who defeated [Bolckow Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolckow_Vaughan) 5–0 in a replayed final tie at [Middlesbrough](334775.md) on 18 May 1918 in front of a crowd of 22,000.[^cite_note-27] The tournament ran for a second year in season 1918–19, the winners being the ladies of Palmer's shipyard in [Jarrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrow), who defeated Christopher Brown's of [Hartlepool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartlepool) 1–0 at [St James' Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James'_Park) in [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne) on 22 March 1919.[^cite_note-28]

At the time of the [First World War](4764461.md), female employment in heavy industry spurred the growth of the game, much as it had done for men fifty years earlier. A team from England played a team from Ireland on [Boxing Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_Day) 1917 in front of a crowd of 20,000 spectators.[^cite_note-\:0-29] The Irish side of this match was dramatised in the play *Rough Girls* in 2021.[^cite_note-30] [Dick, Kerr Ladies F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick,_Kerr_Ladies_F.C.) of [Preston, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston,_Lancashire) played in one of the first women's international matches against a French XI team in 1920,[^cite_note-\:0-29][^cite_note-31] and also made up most of the England team against a [Scottish Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_Scotland) XI in the same year, winning 22–0.[^cite_note-SFA-19]

### FA ban (1921–1970)

Despite being more popular than some men's football events (one match saw a 53,000 strong crowd),[^cite_note-Leighton08-32] women's football in England was halted in December 1921 when [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) outlawed the playing of the game on association members' pitches, the FA stating that "the game of football is quite unsuitable for females and ought not to be encouraged."[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-Wrack2022-34]



A Welsh women's football team pose for a photograph in 1959

Players and football writers have argued that this ban was due to envy of the large crowds that women's matches attracted,[^cite_note-BBC3jun05-35] and because the FA had no control over the money made from the women's game.[^cite_note-Wrack2022-34] Dick, Kerr Ladies player Alice Barlow said, "we could only put it down to jealousy. We were more popular than the men and our bigger gates were for charity".[^cite_note-BBC3jun05-35]

Despite the ban, some women's teams continued to play. The [Northern Rugby Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_League) did not follow the FA ban,[^cite_note-36] so the short-lived [English Ladies Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Ladies_Football_Association) (1921–1922) played some of its matches at [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football) grounds.[^cite_note-37]

In other countries, women's football was further debilitated by nationwide bans which often resembled the English FA's measures.[^cite_note-Drum19-11] The [German Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Association) banned women's football from 1955 until 1970.[^cite_note-38] Women's football was also banned in France from 1941 to 1970.[^cite_note-39] In Brazil, the [Vargas regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vargas_Era) and the [military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_in_Brazil) legally prohibited women and girls from playing football from 1941 to 1979.[^cite_note-CostaBRZ-40]

### Tournaments

The English Ladies' Football Association Challenge Cup

See also: [English Ladies Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Ladies_Football_Association)

Following the FA ban on women's teams on 5 December 1921, the [English Ladies' Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Ladies'_Football_Association) was formed, with 58 affiliated clubs.[^cite_note-DonmouthELFA-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] A silver cup was donated by the first president of the association, Len Bridgett. A total of 23 teams entered the first competition in the spring of 1922. The winners were Stoke Ladies who beat Doncaster and Bentley Ladies 3–1 on 24 June 1922.[^cite_note-DonmouthStoke-44]

The Championship of Great Britain and the World

In 1937, the [Dick, Kerr Ladies F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick,_Kerr_Ladies_F.C.), who had lost to Scotland's [Rutherglen Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherglen_Ladies_F.C.) in 1923 but continued to be proclaimed as "world champions",[^cite_note-lll-45][^cite_note-tribute-46] played the Edinburgh City Girls in the "Championship of Great Britain and the World". Dick, Kerr won the competition with a 5–1 scoreline. The 1939 competition was a more organised affair and the Edinburgh City Girls beat Dick, Kerr 5–2 in [Edinburgh](9602.md), following this up with a 7–1 demolition of Glasgow Ladies in [Falkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkirk) to take the title.[^cite_note-47]

### The revival of the women's game

The English *Women's FA* was formed in 1969 as a result of the increased interest generated by the [1966 World Cup](61629.md).[^cite_note-Leicester07-48]

The ban in England was maintained by the FA for nearly fifty years, until January 1970.[^cite_note-BBCFAC21-10] The next year, UEFA recommended that the national associations in each country should manage the women's game.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-50][^cite_note-Leicester07-48] In 2002, [Lily Parr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Parr) of Dick Kerr's Ladies was the first woman to be inducted into the [National Football Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Museum) Hall of Fame. She was later honoured with a statue in front of the museum.[^cite_note-51] It was not until 2008 (87 years later), that the FA issued an apology for banning women from the game of football.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-Leighton08-32]

### Women's World Championships, 1970 and 1971

In 1970, the Torino-based *[Federation of Independent European Female Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIEFF)* (FIEFF) ran the [1970 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Women's_World_Cup) in Italy, supported by the [Martini & Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martini_&_Rossi) strong wine manufacturers,[^cite_note-JJHeritage70-13] and entirely without the involvement of [FIFA](11049.md).[^cite_note-53] This event was at least partly played by clubs and won by Denmark.[^cite_note-54] A second edition, the [1971 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Women's_World_Cup), was hosted by Mexico the following year. The final, also won by Denmark, was played at [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md), the largest stadium in North America at the time, in front of crowds estimated at 110,000 or 112,500 attendees.[^cite_note-Wilson71-12][^cite_note-55]

### Professionalism

See also: [Professionalism in women's association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professionalism_in_women's_association_football) and [List of women's association football clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women's_association_football_clubs)

Two sitting women in a press conference with a ball and a trophy

Striker [Cristiana Girelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiana_Girelli) and coach [Rita Guarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Guarino) of Juventus in 2019. Juventus is one of the most storied and successful football clubs in Italy.

During the 1970s, Italy became the first country to have professional women's football players on a part-time basis. Italy was also the first country to import foreign footballers from other European countries, which raised the profile of the league. Players during that era included [Susanne Augustesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Augustesen) (Denmark), [Rose Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Reilly) and [Edna Neillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_Neillis) (Scotland), [Anne O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_O'Brien_\(footballer\)) (Ireland) and [Concepcion Sánchez Freire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchi_S%C3%A1nchez) (Spain).[^cite_note-56] Sweden was the first to introduce a professional women's domestic league in 1988, the [Damallsvenskan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damallsvenskan).[^cite_note-57]

### Asia and Oceania

In 1989, Japan became the first country to have a semi-professional women's football league, the L. League – still in existence today as Division 1 of the [Nadeshiko League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadeshiko_League).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-Edwards11-59] In 2020, Japan established the first-ever women's professional league in Asia, the [WE League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WE_League), which started on fall 2021.[^cite_note-60]



The [Urawa Red Diamonds Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urawa_Red_Diamonds_Ladies)

In Indonesia, the first recorded "national" women's football event, known as the "Kartini Cup", took place in 1981.[^cite_note-indonesia-comp-61] The competition was held on an amateur level. Later competitions were also held in an amateur and semi-professional level, including the 1982 appearance of the first women's league, Galanita.[^cite_note-62] The [Pertiwi Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertiwi_Cup), which drew contestants from throughout all of Indonesia, was first played in 2006. The first professional league was held in 2019 under the name [Liga 1 Putri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_1_Putri).[^cite_note-indonesia-comp-61]

In Australia, the W-League, now known as [A-League Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-League_Women), was formed in 2008.[^cite_note-63]

In 2015, the [Chinese Women's Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Women's_Super_League) (CWSL) was launched with an affiliated second division, CWFL.[^cite_note-64] Previously, The Chinese Women's Premier Football League was initiated in 1997 and evolved to the Women's Super League in 2004. From 2011 to 2014, the league was named the Women's National Football League.

The [Indian Women's League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Women's_League) was launched in 2016. The country has held the top-tier tournament, [Indian Women's Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajmata_Jijabai_Trophy), since 1991.[^cite_note-65]

### North America



[Portland Thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns) traveling supporters at Seattle's Memorial Stadium

In 1985, the [United States women's national soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_United_States_women's_national_soccer_team) was formed.[^cite_note-66] Following the [1999 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), the first professional women's soccer league in the United States, the [WUSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_United_Soccer_Association), was launched and lasted three years. The league was spearheaded by members of the World Cup-winning American team and featured players like [Mia Hamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Hamm), [Julie Foudy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Foudy) and [Brandi Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandi_Chastain),[^cite_note-67] as well as top-tier international players like Germany's [Birgit Prinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgit_Prinz) and China's [Sun Wen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Wen_\(footballer\)).[^cite_note-68] A second attempt towards a sustainable professional league, the [Women's Professional Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Professional_Soccer) (WPS), was launched in 2009 and folded in late 2011.[^cite_note-69] The following year, the [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League) (NWSL) was launched with initial support from the soccer federations of the United States, Canada, and Mexico.[^cite_note-70]

In 2017, [Liga MX Femenil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX_Femenil) was launched in Mexico and broke several attendance records. The league is composed of women's teams of the men's counterpart teams in [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX).[^cite_note-71] On 20 March 2024, the league in collaboration with the [NWSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League), announced a new international competition named [Summer Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_x_Liga_MX_Femenil_Summer_Cup). This competition will feature six teams from Liga MX Femenil that will compete against teams from the NWSL. The inaugural edition is scheduled to kick-off in July 2024.[^cite_note-\:8-72]

Unlike other countries, where a single club has both men's and women's squads (which obviously compete in different tournaments), such as [FC Barcelona](68187.md) and [FC Barcelona Femení](24763114.md), in the United States there are usually exclusively men's and women's teams in the same city, with no relation to each other, competing in the country's major soccer leagues (such as [MLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS) and the [NWSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL)). However, there are cases of a men's team and a women's team coincidentally having the same owner, creating a situation that is somewhat similar to what is seen in other countries, but with the two teams having different names and logos. Some examples: [Utah Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Royals) (NWSL) and [Real Salt Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Salt_Lake) (MLS); [Houston Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Dash) (NWSL) and [Houston Dynamo FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Dynamo_FC) (MLS); [Orlando Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Pride) (NWSL) and [Orlando City SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_City_SC) (MLS).

### 21st century



[Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) vs [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md)

A 2014 FIFA report stated that at the beginning of the 21st century, women's football was growing in both popularity and participation, and more professional leagues were being launched worldwide.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] From the inaugural [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) tournament held in 1991 to the 1,194,221 tickets sold for the [1999 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), visibility and support of women's professional football had increased around the globe.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

However, as in some other sports, women's pay and opportunities are lower in comparison with professional male football players.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Both national and international women's football have far less television and media coverage than the men's equivalent,[^cite_note-80] but also generally have far lower average attendances. This discrepancy is on-going, while research indicates some viewers are not even able to distinguish between professional women's and men's football.[^cite_note-81]

[Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) main rivalry is with [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F%C3%A9minine), with matches between the two teams sometimes referred as the "Classique féminin". Paris is OL's main contender for national titles, as they finished in second place of [D1 Féminine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_1_F%C3%A9minine) seven times. Lyon had never lost the D1 title to PSG until 2021 when PSG finished ahead of Lyon, and won five [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France_F%C3%A9minine) finals against Paris. In 2017 both teams reached the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League) final, with Lyon beating Paris after a penalty shoot-out and winning its fourth title in the competition.[^cite_note-82]



The official record attendance for a women's football match was set at Camp Nou on 22 April 2022, with 91,648 people watching Barcelona defeat [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg_\(women\)) 5–1 (pictured).

[^cite_note-83]

While a number of features continue to improve, this is not the case for female coaches. They continue to be under-represented in a number of European women's leagues.[^cite_note-84] However, the popularity and participation in women's football continues to grow.[^cite_note-85]

In 2022, [FC Barcelona](24763114.md) had the two largest reported attendances for women's football since the [1971 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Women's_World_Cup) final between [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_women's_national_football_team) and [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_women's_national_football_team) (110,000) at the [Azteca Stadium](1834902.md), when they played [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_Femenino) (91,553) and [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg_\(women\)) (91,648) at Camp Nou for the [2021–22 UEFA Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_knockout_phase).[^cite_note-GOAL.com/SPOX.com-86][^cite_note-ESPN-UWCLSF-87][^cite_note-WahlSub-88][^cite_note-Gulino-89]

In April 2024, the [2023–24 A-League Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_A-League_Women) season set the record for the most attended season of any women's sport in Australian history, with the season recording a total attendance of 284,551 on 15 April 2024,[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] and finishing with a final total attendance of 312,199.[^cite_note-94]

## International competitions

### Global

#### Women's World Cup



[Mia Hamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Hamm) (left) battles with [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team) defender [Kerstin Stegemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerstin_Stegemann)

The first known World Cup tournaments for women's teams are the [1970 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Women's_World_Cup) in Italy and the [1971 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Women's_World_Cup) in Mexico, both of which hold attendance records and were organised by the international women's association [FIEFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIEFF).[^cite_note-Wilson71-12][^cite_note-JJHeritage70-13][^cite_note-ESPN-UWCLSF-87] Some other major tournaments were the [Women's World Invitational Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Invitational_Tournament) in Taiwan (1978–1987) and the [Women's Mundialito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundialito_\(women\)) in Japan and Italy (1981–1988). FIFA effectively ignored women's football prior to the 1988 [FIFA Women's Invitation Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_Invitation_Tournament) in China.[^cite_note-FR24-Gorman-14][^cite_note-95] FIFA's first officially-recognised women's international match is France–Netherlands (1971), albeit a retroactive recognition decided in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Rankings).[^cite_note-96]

The first [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) was held in China in November 1991 and won by the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team). The runners-up, [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_women's_national_football_team), became the [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) champions, beating Germany in that final, in Sweden. The United States won the [1999 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Final) on [penalties](511302.md) against [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_women's_national_football_team) (with a competition-record crowd of over 90,000 in [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\))). [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team) won consecutive world titles in 2003 and 2007, winning finals against Sweden and Brazil respectively. [Japan](3190583.md) became champions in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), the country's first senior football world championship.[^cite_note-Edwards11-59] The United States won the tournament again in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup). [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_football_team) won the tournament for the first time in [2023](45271353.md).

The [FIFA Women's Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_Club_World_Cup) is a planned international club competition, with the [inaugural edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_FIFA_Women's_Club_World_Cup) scheduled for early 2028. The tournament will be held every four years. In the years without a Women's Club World Cup, a smaller annual competition—the [FIFA Women's Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_Champions_Cup)—will take place, beginning in early [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_Women's_Champions_Cup).

#### Olympics

A soccer game between the United States and Canada, seen from behind one of the goals.

United States' [Abby Wambach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abby_Wambach) plays off a corner kick against [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_soccer_team).

Since [Football at the 1996 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament), a Women's Football Tournament has been staged at the [Olympic Games](22576.md). Unlike in the men's Olympic Football tournament (based on teams of mostly under-23 players), the Olympic women's teams do not have restrictions on professionalism or age.

The participation of Great Britain at the [2012 Olympic tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics) was a bone of contention because England and other British Home Nations are not eligible to compete as separate entities. Eventually, both the women's and men's Great Britain teams fielded some players from the other home nations, but without their associations' active support.[^cite_note-97]

Although there are women's teams of [blind football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_football) (5-a-side) and [cerebral palsy football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_football) (7-a-side),[^cite_note-98] women's football has never been a [Paralympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_Games) event.[^cite_note-99]

### North America

The [CONCACAF W Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_W_Championship) is a women's football competition organized by [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) that often serves as the qualifying competition to the Women's World Cup and the Olympics.

The [CONCACAF W Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_W_Gold_Cup) had its inaugural edition in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CONCACAF_W_Gold_Cup). It featured 12 national teams (8 from the CONCACAF region, and 4 invited from the CONMEBOL region) and was won by the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team).

The [CONCACAF W Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_W_Champions_Cup) is an annual club competition that determines the club champion for the region encompassing North America, Central America and the Caribbean.

### Europe



Players fighting for the ball during the match between [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team) and [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_women's_national_football_team) in UEFA Women's Euro 2009 in Tampere, Finland

European women's tournaments featuring national teams were held [in Italy in 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_European_Competition_for_Women's_Football) and [in 1979 as the European Competition for Women's Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_European_Competition_for_Women's_Football).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] They were not recognised as "official" by UEFA, which opposed women's football until the 1970s.[^cite_note-Leicester07-48] The [UEFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Championship) began in 1984 under the name "European Competition For Representative Women's Teams". Now, it is also commonly referred to as the UEFA Women's Euro.

The [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_European_Competition_for_Women's_Football) tournament was won by [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_women's_national_football_team). [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_women's_national_football_team) won the [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_European_Competition_for_Women's_Football) edition. Between 1987 and 2013, the UEFA Women's Euro was dominated by [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team), which won eight titles, including six in a row from 1995 to 2013. As of 2022, the other teams that have also won the tournament are Norway in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_1993), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_women's_national_football_team) at home in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2017), and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_football_team) at home in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2022) when the tournament was delayed a year by the Covid-19 pandemic. In 2025, England successfully defended their title against Spain, winning a penalty shootout after extra time 3-1.

In addition to the quadrennial UEFA Women's Championship, the [UEFA Women's Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Nations_League) is held every two years for European women's national teams.

The [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) is an international competition that involves the top women's club teams from countries affiliated with the European governing body [UEFA](32332.md).

### South America



[Marta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_\(footballer\)) of [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_women's_national_football_team) is the all-time leading scorer of the senior FIFA World Cups.

The [Copa América Femenina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Femenina) is the main competition in women's football between national teams that are affiliated with [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL).

The [Copa Libertadores Femenina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_Femenina), formally the *CONMEBOL Libertadores Femenina*, is the international club competition for women's teams that play in the CONMEBOL region. The competition started in 2009[^cite_note-102] in response to the increased interest in women's football.

### Africa

The [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](47015413.md) is an international women's football competition held every two years and sanctioned by the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF). It was first contested in 1991, but was not held biennially until 1998. Nigeria is the most successful nation in the tournament's history with 11 titles.

The [CAF Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Women's_Champions_League) is an international competition that involves the top women's club teams from countries affiliated with the Confederation of African Football.

### Asia



[Sam Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Kerr) with [Australia](4689264.md) during the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) quarterfinal against [Japan](3190583.md) in Edmonton, 2015

The [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md) is a quadrennial competition in women's football for national teams which belong to the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC). It is the oldest women's international football competition and premier women's football competition in the AFC region for national teams.

The [SAFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAFF_Women's_Championship), also called the *South Asian Football Federation Women's Cup*, is a competition for women's national football teams governed by the [South Asian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Football_Federation) (SAFF). India won the first 5 editions, beating Nepal four times and Bangladesh once in the final.[^cite_note-auto-103][^cite_note-104] [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_women's_national_football_team) is the current champion having defeated [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_women's_national_football_team) by 3–1 goals on 19 September 2022 in the final.[^cite_note-105]

The [AFC Women's Champions League](64565584.md) is the top-tier women's football club competition in Asia. It involves the top clubs from countries affiliated with the Asian Football Confederation.

### Oceania

The [OFC Women's Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFC_Women's_Nations_Cup) is a women's football tournament for national teams that belong to the [Oceania Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) (OFC). The competition has served as a qualifying tournament for the FIFA Women's World Cup since 1991.

The [OFC Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFC_Women's_Champions_League) is the top-tier women's football club competition in Oceania. It involves the top clubs from countries affiliated with the Oceania Football Confederation.

### England

#### Women's FA Cup

Main article: [Women's FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_FA_Cup)



[Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) after winning the Women's FA Cup in 2007

After the lifting of the FA ban, the [Women's Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Football_Association) held its first national [knockout tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout_tournament), the [1970–71 WFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_WFA_Cup). [Southampton Women's F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Women's_F.C.) was the inaugural winner and became the Cup-winner eight times. From 1983 to 1994, [Doncaster Belles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Rovers_Belles_L.F.C.) reached ten out of eleven finals, winning six of them. As of 2023, [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_L.F.C.) are the title holders and [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) are the club with a record 14 wins.[^cite_note-106] Despite tournament sponsorship by some companies, entering the cup actually costs clubs more than they get in prize money. In 2015, it was reported that even if [Notts County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notts_County_Ladies_F.C.) were to win the tournament, the £8,600 winnings would leave them out of pocket.[^cite_note-107] The winners of the [Men's FA Cup](11237.md) in the same year received £1.8 million, with teams that did not even reach the first round proper getting more than the women's winners.[^cite_note-108]

## Youth tournaments



Iran vs Turkey in [2010 Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Summer_Youth_Olympics)

In 2002, FIFA inaugurated a women's youth championship, officially called the [FIFA U-19 Women's World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_U-20_Women's_World_Cup). The first event was hosted by Canada. The final was an all-[CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) affair, with the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_under-20_women's_national_soccer_team) defeating the host Canada 1–0 with an extra-time [golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal). The second event was held in [Thailand](30128.md) in 2004 and won by [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_under-20_football_team). The age limit was raised to 20, starting with the [2006 event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_U-20_Women's_World_Championship) held in Russia. Demonstrating the increasing global reach of the women's game, the winners of this event were [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_women's_national_under-20_football_team). The tournament was renamed the FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup since the [2008 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_FIFA_U-20_Women's_World_Cup), which was won by the US in [Chile](5489.md). [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_women's_national_under-20_football_team) won the tournament in France in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_U-20_Women's_World_Cup).[^cite_note-109]

In 2008, FIFA instituted an [under-17 world championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_U-17_Women's_World_Cup). [The inaugural event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_FIFA_U-17_Women's_World_Cup), held in New Zealand, was won by [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_women's_national_under-17_football_team). [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_under-17_football_team) won this tournament in [Uruguay](31843.md) in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_U-17_Women's_World_Cup).[^cite_note-110]

## Intercollegiate

### United States



[Florida State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Seminoles_women's_soccer) vs [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_women's_soccer), 2005

In the United States, the intercollegiate sport began from physical education programs. In the 1970s, women's club teams started to appear on college campuses, but it wasn't until the 1980s that they started to gain recognition and gained a [varsity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_team) status. Brown University was the first college to grant full varsity level status to their women's soccer team. The [Association for Intercollegiate Athletics for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Intercollegiate_Athletics_for_Women) (AIAW) sponsored the first regional women's soccer tournament at college in the US, which was held at [Brown University](4157.md). The first national level tournament was held at Colorado College, which gained official AIAW sponsorship in 1981. The 1990s saw greater participation mainly due to the [Title IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_IX) of 23 June 1972, which increased school's budgets and their addition of women's scholarships. Currently, there are over 700 intercollegiate women's soccer teams in the [NCAA](60706.md), NAIA and NJCAA.

Title IX massively increased the number of female students playing organized sports in American academic institutions.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] However, the college sports system and Title IX have also been criticized for promoting [systemic racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_sports#Views_in_the_United_States) and wealth inequality in women's soccer in the US.[^cite_note-USAT19-113][^cite_note-DBK2020-114][^cite_note-Time-3-19-115] Female [college soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_soccer) players are [70% white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States),[^cite_note-USAT19-113] with the sport also being "disproportionately white and upper-middle-class".[^cite_note-Yahoo-9-20-116] Participating in American youth soccer is substantially more expensive than in basketball or tackle football,[^cite_note-DBK2020-114] and academy soccer clubs are mainly located in suburbs and districts where black players are under-represented.[^cite_note-Yahoo-9-20-116] As a result, in the National Women's Soccer League in 2020, the coaches and executives were 98.9% white.[^cite_note-Yahoo-9-20-116] Three women's soccer coaches were implicated in the [2019 college admissions bribery scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_college_admissions_bribery_scandal).[^cite_note-USAT19-113][^cite_note-Time-3-19-115] NCAA Division I programs in money-losing sports, such as soccer, are extensively subsidized by the only two high-revenue college sports, basketball and American football, in which black players are greatly over-represented, but the players are paid no salaries and are "systematically denied the revenue they are responsible for generating".[^cite_note-Guardian-3-21-117]

### Canada

In Canada, the intercollegiate sport is governed by [U Sports women's soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Sports_women's_soccer) and was founded in 1987. As of the 2025 season, 52 teams from [Canadian universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Canada) are divided into four conferences, drawing from the four regional associations of U Sports: [Canada West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_West_Universities_Athletic_Association), [Ontario University Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_University_Athletics), [Réseau du sport étudiant du Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_du_sport_%C3%A9tudiant_du_Qu%C3%A9bec), and [Atlantic University Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_University_Sport).[^cite_note-118] The Season concludes with the [U Sports women's soccer championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Sports_women's_soccer_championship).

Prominent players at this level include [Sienna Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sienna_Gibson), [Megane Sauve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megane_Sauve), [Danielle Steer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Steer), [Miranda Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_Smith), and more.

## Controversies

### Misogynistic comments and decision-making around dress codes

Footballers around the world, both female and male, wear a [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) made up of a jersey, shorts, cleats (boots), and knee-length socks worn over [shin guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_guard).[^cite_note-LotG-8] In 2004, FIFA President [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter) suggested that women footballers should "wear tighter shorts and low cut shirts... to create a more female aesthetic" and attract more male fans. His comment was criticized as [misogynistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misogyny) by people involved with women's football and media outlets worldwide.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]



FC de Rakt DA1 (2008/2009)

In September 2008, the local amateur [FC de Rakt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_de_Rakt) women's team (FC de Rakt DA1) in the Netherlands made international headlines by swapping its old kit for a new one featuring skirts and fitted shirts.[^cite_note-122] This innovation, which had been requested by the team itself for physical comfort was initially vetoed by the [Royal Dutch Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Football_Association) on the grounds that, according to the laws of the game, shorts must be worn by all players, both male and female; but this decision was reversed when it was revealed that the FC de Rakt team were wearing tennis style short pants under their skirts, and were therefore technically in compliance of the "shorts rule". Denying that the kit change was merely a publicity stunt, club chairman Jan van den Elzen told [Reuters](18998750.md):

> *The girls asked us if they could make a team and asked specifically to play in skirts. We said we'd try but we didn't expect to get permission for that. We've seen reactions from Belgium and Germany already saying this could be something for them. Many girls would like to play in skirts but didn't think it was possible.*

21-year-old team captain Rinske Temming said:

> *We think they are far more elegant than the traditional shorts and furthermore they are more comfortable because the shorts are made for men. It's more about being elegant, not sexy. Female football is not so popular at the moment. In the Netherlands there's an image that it's more for men, but we hope that can change.*

### Women's football in the Middle East and North Africa



Goalkeeper [Zahra Khajavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahra_Khajavi) of the [Iran women's national football team](9896170.md)

Until 2020, only Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt, Algeria, Palestine, Turkey, Jordan, Iran, Lebanon, Syria, and Israel had large-scale women's competitions and national teams, which are still hindered due to discrimination against women in football.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

Since 2020, countries that have traditionally been stricter like Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, Somalia, Mauritania, and Sudan have begun to develop women's football in order to raise their international profiles.[^cite_note-125] [Sudan women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_women's_national_football_team) debuted in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Arab_Women's_Cup),[^cite_note-126] and the [Saudi Arabia women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_women's_national_football_team) was noticed internationally, due to Saudi Arabia's Islamic conservatism.[^cite_note-127]

#### Wearing of hijabs

In June 2011, [Iran](9896170.md) forfeited an [Olympic qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_Asian_Qualifiers) match in [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_women's_national_football_team), after trying to take to the field in [hijabs](68301.md) and full body suits. [FIFA](11049.md) awarded a default 3–0 win to Jordan, explaining that the Iranian kits were "an infringement of the Laws of the Game".[^cite_note-128] The decision provoked criticism from [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md),[^cite_note-129] while Iranian officials alleged that the actions of the [Bahraini](18933277.md) match delegate had been politically motivated.[^cite_note-130] In July 2012, FIFA approved the wearing of hijab in future matches.[^cite_note-131]

### Labour disputes

Main article: [Labour relations in women's association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_relations_in_women's_association_football)



[Megan Rapinoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Rapinoe) speaks on equal pay in March 2021.

Professional women's association football players have disputed several issues specific to the sport, such as [disparities in compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_pay_gap_in_sports) compared to men's teams;[^cite_note-si-canada-132] insufficient pay to compete with other women's teams;[^cite_note-m1-arg-133] unfair or exclusionary financial terms of federation business agreements involving the team;[^cite_note-si-canada-132] a lack of minimum standards in facilities and treatment, especially compared to men's teams in the same federation, league, or club;[^cite_note-abc-wleague-medical-134] reports of systemic gender-related abuse of players, including sexual abuse being ignored by league or federation officials;[^cite_note-ts-birarda-sentenced-135][^cite_note-espn-nwsl-136] and a lack of benefits specific to women, such as maternity leave and child care.

Disputes have been waged between national team players and federations,[^cite_note-eqz-arg-137] between club players and their teams and leagues,[^cite_note-eqz-arg-137][^cite_note-abc-wleague-medical-134][^cite_note-espn-nwsl-136] between referees of women's football and their governing organizations,[^cite_note-138] and between players and federations or laws that prevented women from playing or professionalizing the sport.[^cite_note-tg-arg-139]

### Sexual harassment and abuse

In the early 2020s, sexual harassment and abuse became hot-button issues in women's football. In 2021 and 2022, the NWSL was forced to deal with [widespread reports of abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NWSL_abuse_scandal), with some incidents dating back to the league's formation in 2013. An investigation led to four team managers receiving lifetime bans from NWSL employment, and lesser discipline for several other managers, coaches, and executives. This issue again came to the forefront with the [Rubiales affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubiales_affair), stemming from the behavior of [Spanish federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) president [Luis Rubiales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Rubiales) at the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_final).
