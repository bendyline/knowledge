**Chongqing**[^cite_note-13] is a provincial-level [direct-administered municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-administered_municipality) in [Southwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_China). It is one of the four direct-administered municipalities of China and the only one located inland.[^cite_note-14]

The municipality covers a large geographical area roughly the size of [Austria](26964606.md),[^cite_note-BBCHowdoyoumeasure-15] which includes several disjunct urban areas in addition to Chongqing proper. Due to its classification, the municipality of Chongqing is the [largest city proper](14649921.md) in the world by population, though it is not the most populous urban area.

The municipality of Chongqing is the only Chinese city with a resident population of over 30 million,[^cite_note-National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China-16] but that number includes its large rural population.[^cite_note-BBCHowdoyoumeasure-15] In 2020, Chongqing surpassed [Shanghai](27643.md) as China's largest municipality by urban population and, as of 2024, it had an urban population of 23.01 million.[^cite_note-National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China-16] The municipality contains 26 districts, eight [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), and four [autonomous counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_county).

The city served as the wartime capital of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China) (ROC) during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) (1937–1945). On 14 March 1997, the current municipality was separated from the surrounding province of [Sichuan](65185.md), with the goal of furthering development in the central and western parts of the country.[^cite_note-17]

Chongqing is one of China's [national central cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_central_city). It is a connection in the [Yangtze River Economic Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_Economic_Belt) and a base for the country's [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative).[^cite_note-18] [Chongqing Jiangbei International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Jiangbei_International_Airport) is the second-busiest airport in China, and is one of the top 50 [busiest airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) in the world.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] The city's monorail system is [the world's longest and busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monorail_systems), with 70 stations—the most of any system.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] It is the headquarters of [Changan Automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changan_Automobile), one of the "[Big Four](8518766.md)" car manufacturers in China.[^cite_note-23] As of 2023, the city hosts 12 foreign representations, the fifth-most in China, behind Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Chengdu.[^cite_note-24] It is one of the top 35 cities globally by [scientific research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index) output,[^cite_note-nature.com-25] and the municipality is home to several [notable universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Chongqing), including [Chongqing University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_University), [Southwest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_University), and [Chongqing University of Posts and Telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_University_of_Posts_and_Telecommunications).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## History

Main article: [History of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chongqing)

### Antiquity

During the Warring States Period, it was the gateway to the western border of the State of Chu, and its county territory belonged to the territory of [Wu Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Commandery). In the thirtieth year of [King Zhaoxiang of Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Zhaoxiang_of_Qin) (277 BC), Zhang Ruo, the governor of Qin Shu, attacked Chu and captured Wu Commandery. He also ordered Bai Qi to circle the territory of the State of Han, launch a surprise attack on the capital of the State of Chu, Ying (now Jiangling), and burn Yiling (now [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang)). After cutting off the rescue of Wu Commandery by the State of Chu, he seized Wu Commandery and the area south of the Yangtze River, establishing [Qianzhong Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianzhong_Commandery). Wu Commandery was under the jurisdiction of the county (with its administrative center in present-day Dachang Town) and belonged to the State of Qin. Later, it was transferred to the jurisdiction of [Nan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan_County). The county was named Wushan from then on.[^cite_note-29] Chongqing's location is historically associated with the [State of Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)). It was named Jiangzhou (江州) by a Qin general [Zhang Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Yi_\(Warring_States_period\)).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-chongqing_3gorges-31]

### Imperial era

Jiangzhou subsequently remained under [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang)'s rule during the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty), the successor of the Qin State, as well as the rule of [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) emperors. Jiangzhou was subsequently renamed during the [Northern and Southern dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_dynasties) to Chu Prefecture (楚州), then again in 581 AD ([Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty)) to [Yu Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C3%BA_Prefecture) (渝州), and later in 1102 during [Northern Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Song) to Gong Prefecture (恭州).[^cite_note-AboutChongqing06122007-32] The name Yu however survives to this day as an abbreviation for Chongqing, as well as for the city's historic center, where the old town once stood; its name is Yuzhong (渝中, Central Yu).[^cite_note-chongqing_3gorges-31] It received its current name in 1189, after Prince [Zhao Dun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Guangzong_of_Song) of the [Southern Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song_dynasty) described his crowning as king and then [Emperor Guangzong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Guangzong_of_Song) as a "double celebration" ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 双重喜庆; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 雙重喜慶; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *shuāngchóng xǐqìng*, or *chóngqìng* in short). To mark the occasion of his enthronement, Yu Prefecture was therefore converted to Chongqing [Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_\(administrative_division\)).

In 1362 (during the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty)), [Ming Yuzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Yuzhen), a peasant rebel leader, established the Daxia Kingdom (大夏) at Chongqing for a short time.[^cite_note-33] In 1621 (during the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty)), another short-lived kingdom of Daliang (大梁) was established by She Chongming (奢崇明) with Chongqing as its capital.[^cite_note-34] In 1644, after the fall of the Ming dynasty to a rebel army, Chongqing, together with the rest of [Sichuan](65185.md), was captured by [Zhang Xianzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Xianzhong), who was said to have massacred a large number of people in Sichuan and depopulated the province, in part by causing many people to flee to safety elsewhere. The [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchus) later conquered the province, and during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), immigration to Chongqing and Sichuan took place with the support of the [Qing emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_emperors_of_the_Qing_dynasty).[^cite_note-35]

In 1890, the British Consulate General was opened in Chongqing.[^cite_note-36] The following year, the city became the first inland commerce port open to foreigners, with the proviso that foreign ships should not be at liberty to trade there until Chinese-owned [steamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamer) had succeeded in ascending the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) river. This restriction was abolished by the [Treaty of Shimonoseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Shimonoseki) in 1895, which declared the city open on the same terms as other ports, although it was not until 1907 that a steamship made the journey without the help of manual haulers.[^cite_note-37] From 1896 to 1904, the American, German, French, and Japanese consulates were opened in Chongqing.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

### Provisional wartime capital of the Republic of China

See also: [Bombing of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing)



A street scene in Chongqing, c. 1944



View of Chongqing, circa 1900–1930, showing the city's characteristic hillside urban landscape along the Yangtze River.

During and after the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), from November 1937 to May 1946, it was Generalissimo [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek)'s provisional capital. After the General and remaining army had lived there for a time following their retreat in 1938 from the previous capital of [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), it was formally declared the [second capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_as_temporary_capital?action=edit\&redlink=1) (陪都; *péidū*; *p'ei<sup>2</sup>-tu<sup>1</sup>*) on 6 September 1940.[^cite_note-Revisiting_Chongqing:_China's_Secon-42] After Britain, the United States, and other Allies entered the war in Asia in December 1941, one of the Allies' deputy commanders of operations in Southeast Asia (Southeast Asia Command SEAC), [Joseph Stilwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stilwell), was based in the city.



Church buildings in Chongqing, 1941. The foreground structure is a Methodist Episcopal Mission church.

The city was also visited by Lord [Louis Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mountbatten,_1st_Earl_Mountbatten_of_Burma), the Supreme Commander of SEAC which was itself headquartered in Sri Lanka. Chiang Kai Shek as Supreme Commander in China worked closely with Stilwell.[^cite_note-43] From 1938 to 1943, the city suffered from continuous [massive bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing) campaigns of the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy_Air_Service) and [Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army_Air_Service); battles of which were fought entirely by the [Chinese Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_Air_Force) squadrons and anti-aircraft artillery units.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] Many lives were saved by the air-raid shelters which took advantage of the mountainous terrain. Chongqing was acclaimed to be the "City of Heroes" due to the indomitable spirits of its people as well as their contributions and sacrifices during the war. Many factories and universities were relocated from eastern China and ultimately to Chongqing during years of setbacks in the war, transforming this city from an inland port to a heavily industrialized city.

### Chinese Civil War

After World War II ended, Chongqing became one of the last refuges of the Nationalist Kuomintang government on the Chinese mainland during the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War). Following the [Yangtze River Crossing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_Crossing_campaign), the KMT capital of [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) was conquered by the Communists on 23 April. The KMT decamped first to [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), then Chongqing. Chongqing served as the KMT capital until late November 1949, when the Nationalist KMT government withdrew from the city by air.[^cite_note-46]

### Municipality status



A sunset view of [Jiefangbei CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiefangbei_CBD) and Hongya Cave, taken in 2017

On 14 March 1997, the Eighth [National People's Congress](38878740.md) decided to merge the [sub-provincial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-provincial_city) with adjacent [Fuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuling), [Wanxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanxian), and [Qianjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianjiang,_Chongqing) prefectures that it had governed on behalf of the province since September 1996, and grant it independence from Sichuan. The resulting single entity became Chongqing Municipality,[^cite_note-47]<sup>: 74 </sup> containing 30,020,000 people in forty-three former [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(People's_Republic_of_China\)) without intermediate political levels. The municipality became the spearhead of China's effort to develop its western regions and to coordinate the resettlement of residents from the reservoir areas of the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) project. Its first official ceremony took place on 18 June 1997.

On 8 February 2010, Chongqing became one of the nine [National Central Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Central_Cities), along with Beijing, Tianjin, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu, Wuhan, Xi'an, and Zhengzhou. According to Zhou Liqun, a consultant for the National Development and Reform Commission of China, Chongqing's status as a National Central City would encourage the development of [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China). Chongqing, which is located in central-western China, would complement the other National Central Cities, all of which are located along China's eastern coastline.[^cite_note-48] The same year on 18 June, the [Liangjiang New Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangjiang_New_Area) was established in Chongqing, which was the third state-level [new area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_areas) at the time of its establishment.[^cite_note-49]

### Physical geography and topography



Topography of Chongqing

Chongqing is located in the [subtropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics), situated in the transitional area between the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) and the plain on the middle and lower reaches of the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze). Its climate features frequent [monsoon](57630.md) conditions, often raining at night in late spring and early summer. The city's "night rain in the Ba Mountains" features in poems throughout Chinese history, including "Written on a Rainy Night—A Letter to the North" by [Li Shangyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Shangyin).[^cite_note-50] Its territory is 470 kilometers (290 mi) from east to west at its longest, and 450 kilometers (280 mi) from north to south at its widest.[^cite_note-51] It borders [Hubei](83319.md) and [Hunan](166409.md) to the east, [Sichuan](65185.md) and [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) to the north, and [Guizhou](173815.md) to the south.[^cite_note-52]



The Qutang Gorge on the Yangtze



[Wulong Karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wulong_Karst)

Chongqing covers a large area crisscrossed by rivers and mountains. The [Daba Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daba_Mountains) stand in the north, the [Wu Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Gorge) in the east, the [Wuling Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuling_Mountains) in the southeast, and the [Dalou Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalou_Mountains) in the south. The area slopes downward from north to south towards the Yangtze valley, and features a large [massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif) of mountains and hills, with steep sloping areas at different heights.[^cite_note-53] [Karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) landscape is common in this area, and stone forests, numerous collections of peaks, [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) caves and valleys can be found in many places. The *Longshuixia Gap* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 龙水峡地缝), with its [Three Natural Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Natural_Bridges), has made the region a popular tourist attraction. The Yangtze River runs through the whole area from west to east, covering a course of 665 kilometers (413 mi), cutting through the Wu Mountains at three places and forming the well-known [Three Gorges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges): the Qutang, Wuxia and Xiling gorges.[^cite_note-54] Coming from northwest and running through "the Jialing Lesser Three Gorges" of Libi, Wentang and Guanyin, the [Jialing River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jialing_River) joins the Yangtze in Chongqing.[^cite_note-55]

> Leaving at dawn the White Emperor crowned with cloud,\
> &#x20;I've sailed a thousand [li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_\(unit\)) through canyons in a day.\
> &#x20;With the monkeys' adieus the riverbanks are loud,\
> &#x20;My skiff has left ten thousand mountains far away.

— Li Bai's Poem of Chongqing's [Baidi Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidi_Cheng)

The central urban area of Chongqing, called Chongqing proper, is built on mountains and partially surrounded by the Yangtze and Jialing rivers. With its special [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography), Chongqing's topography includes mountains, rivers, forests, springs, waterfalls, gorges, and caves. The [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) poet [Li Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bai) was inspired by the natural scenery and wrote this [epigram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigram).[^cite_note-56]

The Zhongliang (中梁山) and Tongluo (铜锣山) mountains roughly form the eastern and western boundaries of Chongqing's urban area. Several high mountains are situated outside central Chongqing, including the 1,709.4-meter (5,608 ft)-high Wugong Ling Mountain in Jiangjin.

### Climate



In the spring and fall, downtown Chongqing is often enshrouded in fog.

Chongqing has a monsoonal [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwa*), bordering on a humid subtropical climate ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) and for most of the year experiences very high relative humidity, with all months above 75%. Known as one of the "[Three Furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Furnaces)" of the Yangtze River, along with [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan) and [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), its summers are long and among the hottest and most humid in China, with highs of 34 °C (93 °F) in July and August in the urban area.[^cite_note-57] Winters are short and somewhat mild, but damp and overcast. The city's location in the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) causes it to have one of the lowest annual sunshine totals nationally, at only 983 hours, lower than much of Northern Europe; the monthly percent possible sunshine in the city proper ranges from a mere 5% in January to 43% in August. Extremes since 1951 have ranged from −1.8 °C (29 °F) on 11 January 1955 to 43.7 °C (111 °F) on 18 and 19 August 2022[^cite_note-58]

Chongqing, with over 100 days of fog per year,[^cite_note-59] is known as the "Fog City" ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 雾都); this is because in the spring and fall, a thick layer of fog enshrouds it for 68 days per year.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] During the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) (1937–1945), this special weather possibly played a role in protecting the city from being overrun by the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army).

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Chongqing)

See also: [List of provincial leaders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_leaders_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)



The [Great Hall of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hall_of_the_People_\(Chongqing\)) serves as the venue for major political conferences in Chongqing.

Since 1997, Chongqing has been a [direct-controlled municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-controlled_municipality) in the Chinese administrative structure, making it a provincial-level division with commensurate political importance. The municipality's leader is [Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Chongqing) of the [Municipal Committee of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Municipal_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party), which, since 2007, has also held a seat on the [Politburo of the Chinese Communist Party](51851.md), the country's second highest governing council. Under the USSR-inspired [nomenklatura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenklatura) system of appointments, individuals are appointed to the position by the central leadership of the CCP and bestowed with an official position based on seniority and adherence to party orthodoxy, usually given to an individual with prior regional experience elsewhere in China and nearly never a native of Chongqing. Notable individuals who have held the municipal Party Secretary position include [He Guoqiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Guoqiang), [Wang Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Yang_\(politician\)), [Bo Xilai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo_Xilai), [Zhang Dejiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Dejiang), and [Sun Zhengcai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Zhengcai), the latter three were Politburo members during their term as party chief. The party chief heads the municipal [party standing committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_standing_committee), the *de facto* top governing council of the municipality. The standing committee is typically composed of 13 individuals, which includes the party chiefs of important subdivisions and other leading figures in the local party and government organization, as well as one military representative.

The [municipal People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Municipal_People's_Government) serves as the day-to-day administrative authority, and is headed by the [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Chongqing), who is assisted by numerous vice mayors and mayoral assistants. Each vice mayor is given jurisdiction over specific municipal departments. The mayor is the second-highest-ranking official in the municipality. The mayor usually represents the city when foreign guests visit.[^cite_note-73]

The municipality also has a [Municipal People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Municipal_People's_Congress), theoretically elected by lower level People's Congresses. The People's Congress nominally appoints the mayor and approves the nominations of other government officials. The People's Congress, like those of other provincial jurisdictions, is generally seen as a symbolic body. It convenes in full once a year to approve party-sponsored resolutions and local regulations and duly confirm party-approved appointments. On occasion, the People's Congress can be a venue of discussion on municipal issues, although this is dependent on the actions of individual delegates. The municipal People's Congress is headed by a former municipal official, usually in their late fifties or sixties, with a lengthy prior political career in Chongqing. The municipal Political Consultative Conference (*zhengxie*) meets at around the same time as the People's Congress. Its role is to advise on political issues. The *zhengxie* is headed by a leader who is typically a former municipal or regional official with a lengthy career in the party and government bureaucracy.

### Military

Chongqing was the wartime capital of China during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) (i.e., World War II), and from 1937 to 1945,[^cite_note-74] the seat of administration for the Republic of China's government before its departure to [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) and then [Taiwan](25734.md).[^cite_note-AboutChongqing06122007-32] After the eventual defeat at the [Battle of Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wuhan), General [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) and the army were forced to use it as their base of resistance from 1938 onwards.[^cite_note-Revisiting_Chongqing:_China's_Secon-42] It also contains a military museum named after the Chinese [Korean War](16772.md) hero [Qiu Shaoyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiu_Shaoyun).[^cite_note-75]

Chongqing used to be the headquarters of the [13th Group Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Group_Army) of the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md), one of the two group armies that formerly comprised the [Chengdu Military Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Military_Region), which was reorganized into the [Western Theater Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Theater_Command) in 2016.[^cite_note-76]

### Administrative divisions

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of administrative divisions of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_administrative_divisions_of_Chongqing) and [List of township-level divisions of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_township-level_divisions_of_Chongqing).

Chongqing is the largest of the four direct-controlled municipalities of the People's Republic of China. The municipality is divided into 37 subdivisions (3 were abolished in 1997. [Wansheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wansheng) and [Shuangqiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuangqiao_District,_Chongqing) districts were abolished in October 2011.[^cite_note-77] [Jiangbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangbei,_Chongqing) and [Yubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubei) districts were abolished in November 2025 along with the establishment of the formal [Liangjiang New Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangjiang_New_Area)[^cite_note-news.cctv.com-78]), consisting of 25 districts, 8 counties, and 4 autonomous counties. The boundaries of Chongqing municipality reach much farther into the city's hinterland than the boundaries of the other three provincial level municipalities (Beijing, Shanghai and [Tianjin](50821.md)), and much of its administrative area, which spans over 80,000 square kilometers (30,900 sq mi), is rural. At the end of 2018, the total population was 31.02 million. As of 2022, Chongqing is the largest Chinese city by urban population, with a population of 22.80 million.[^cite_note-National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China-16]

#### Central Chongqing



[Jiefangbei CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiefangbei_CBD), Yuzhong Peninsula of Chongqing at dusk



Central Chongqing in 1988.

The main urban area of Chongqing city (重庆主城区) spans approximately 5,473 square kilometers (2,113 sq mi), and includes the following nine districts:[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

- [Yuzhong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzhong_District) (渝中区, literally "Central Chongqing District"), the central and most densely populated district, where government and international business offices and the city's best shopping are located in the district's [Jiefangbei CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiefangbei_CBD) area. Yuzhong is located on the peninsula surrounded by Eling Hill, Yangtze River and Jialing River.

- ***Abolished** [Jiangbei District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangbei_District,_Chongqing) (江北区, literally "North of the River District"), located to the north of Jialing River.*

- [Shapingba District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapingba_District) (沙坪坝区), roughly located between Jialing River and Zhongliang Mountain.

- [Jiulongpo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiulongpo_District) (九龙坡区), roughly located between Yangtze River and Zhongliang Mountain.

- [Nan'an District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan'an_District) (南岸区, literally "Southern Bank District"), located on the south side of Yangtze River.

- [Dadukou District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadukou_District) (大渡口区)

- [Banan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banan_District,_Chongqing) (巴南区, literally "Southern of Ba District"). Previously called Ba County, and changed to the current name in 1994.

- ***Abolished** [Yubei District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubei_District) (渝北区, or "Northern Chongqing District"). Previously called Jiangbei County, and changed into the current name in 1994.*

- [Beibei District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beibei_District) (北碚区), a satellite district northwest of Chongqing.

- [Liangjiang New Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangjiang_New_Area) (两江新区, literally "Two Rivers New Area"), a state-level new area established on 5 May 2010. It became a formal district on 6 November 2025, consisting of the former [Jiangbei District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangbei_District,_Chongqing), part of the former [Yubei District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubei_District), and part of [Beibei District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beibei_District).

According to the [2020 national census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census), Chongqing has a population of 32,054,159, accounting for around 2.27% of the national total.[^cite_note-data2022-101] This makes it the [most populous city proper in the world](14649921.md).[^cite_note-102] As of 2010, the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) encompassing the central urban area was estimated by the [OECD](33853117.md) to have, a population of 17 million.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

In 2020, Chongqing surpassed Shanghai as China's largest municipality by urban population.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] As of 2021, 70.4% of Chongqing's population is estimated to be urban, and 29.6% rural.[^cite_note-data2022-101] As of 2024, Chongqing had an urban population of 23.01 million.[^cite_note-National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China-16]

The Census also lists the male percentage as 50.55% and the female percentage as 49.55%. In terms of age distribution, of the total population, 15.91% were age 0–14, 62.22% were 15–64, and 21.87% were 65 and over. Of the population's highest education level achieved, 15.41% were college, 15.96% were high school, 30.58% were middle school, and 29.89% were elementary school.[^cite_note-data2022-101]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Chongqing)

Chongqing was a major recipient of China's investment in industrial capacity during the [Third Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Front_\(China\)) campaign.[^cite_note-112]<sup>: 298 </sup>

There has been a massive government push to transform Chongqing into the region's economic, trade, and [financial center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_center) and use the municipality as a platform to open up the country's western interior to further development.[^cite_note-113] Chongqing is facing rapid [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization). For instance, statistics suggest that new construction added approximately 137,000 square meters (1,470,000 sq ft) daily of usable floor space to satisfy demands for residential, commercial and factory space.[^cite_note-114] Thus, Chongqing was separated from Sichuan province and made into a municipality in its own right on 14 March 1997[^cite_note-115] in order to accelerate its development and subsequently China's relatively poorer western areas (see [China Western Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Western_Development) strategy).[^cite_note-116] By the 2000s the city had become an important industrial area in western China.[^cite_note-117]

As of 2022, Chongqing's [nominal GDP](12594.md) was [US\$](18717338.md)433 billion ([CN¥](66958.md) 2.91 trilion), about 2.41% of the country's GDP and ranked [16th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) among [province-level administrative units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_China); the municipality's primary, secondary and tertiary industries were worth CN¥201.21 billion (US\$29.92 billion), CN¥1.169 trillion (US\$173.86 billion) and CN¥1.542 trillion (US\$229.3 billion) respectively. Its nominal GDP per capita was US\$13,479 (CN¥90,663) and ranked [10th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) in the country.[^cite_note-data2022-101]

Chongqing has been identified by the [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) in the November 2010 Access China White Paper as a member of the [CHAMPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHAMPS_\(China\)) (Chongqing, [Hefei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hefei), [Anshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshan_City), [Maanshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maanshan), [Pingdingshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingdingshan) and [Shenyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang)), an economic profile of the top 20 emerging cities in China.[^cite_note-118]

Traditionally, due to its geographic inaccessibility, Chongqing and [Sichuan](65185.md) have both been important military bases in weapons research and development.[^cite_note-119] Even though Chongqing's industries are diversified, unlike eastern China, its export sector is small due to its relatively disadvantageous inland location. Instead, factories producing local-oriented consumer goods such as processed food, cars, chemicals, textiles, machinery, sports equipment and electronics are common.

Chongqing is China's third largest motor vehicle production center and the largest for motorcycles. In 2007, it had an annual output capacity of 1 million cars and 8.6 million motorcycles.[^cite_note-China_Business_Review-120] Leading makers of cars and motorbikes includes China's fourth biggest automaker; [Changan Automotive Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'an_Motors) and [Lifan Hongda Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifan_Group), as well as [Ford Motor Company](30433662.md), with the US car giant having 3 plants in Chongqing. The municipality is also one of China's nine largest iron and steel producers in China as well as one of its three major aluminum producers. Important manufacturers include [Chongqing Iron and Steel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Iron_and_Steel_Company) (重庆钢铁股份有限公司) and Southwest Aluminum (西南鋁業), which is Asia's largest aluminum plant.[^cite_note-121]

Agriculture remains significant. Rice and fruits, especially oranges, are the area's main produce.&#x20;

Natural resources are also abundant with large deposits of coal, natural gas, and more than 40 kinds of minerals such as [strontium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strontium) and [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese). Coal reserves total approximately 4,800,000,000 metric tons (4.7×10<sup>9</sup> long tons; 5.3×10<sup>9</sup> short tons). Chuandong Natural Gas Field is China's largest inland gas field with deposits of around 270 billion m<sup>3</sup> – more than 1/5 of China's total. Has China's largest reserve of strontium (China has the world's 2nd biggest strontium deposit). Manganese is mined in the Xiushan area. Although the mining sector has been denounced as heavily polluting and unsafe.[^cite_note-122] Chongqing is also planned to be the site of a 10 million ton capacity refinery operated by [CNPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Petroleum_Corporation) (parent company of [PetroChina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PetroChina)) to process imported crude oil from the [Sino-Myanmar pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Myanmar_pipelines). The pipeline itself, though not yet finished, will eventually run from [Sittwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe) (in Myanmar's western coast) through [Kunming](197181.md) in Yunnan before reaching Chongqing[^cite_note-123] and it will provide China with fuels sourced from Myanmar, the Middle East and Africa. Recently, there has been a drive to move up the [value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_chain) by shifting towards high technology and knowledge intensive industries resulting in new development zones such as the Chongqing New North Zone (CNNZ).[^cite_note-124] Chongqing's local government is hoping through the promotion of favorable economic policies for the electronics and information technology sectors, that it can create a 400 billion RMB high technology manufacturing hub which will surpass its car industry and account for 25% of its exports.[^cite_note-125]

The city has also invested heavily in infrastructure to attract investment.[^cite_note-China_Business_Review-120][^cite_note-126] The network of roads and railways connecting Chongqing to the rest of China has been expanded and upgraded reducing [logistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) costs. Furthermore, the nearby [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam), which is the world's largest, supplies Chongqing with power and allows oceangoing ships to reach Chongqing's [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) port.[^cite_note-127] These infrastructure improvements have led to the arrivals of numerous foreign direct investors (FDI) in industries ranging from [car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) to finance and retailing; such as [Ford](30433662.md),[^cite_note-128] [Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda),[^cite_note-129] [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC),[^cite_note-130] [Standard Chartered Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chartered_Bank),[^cite_note-131] [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank),[^cite_note-132] [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank),[^cite_note-133] [ANZ Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZ_Bank),[^cite_note-134] [Scotiabank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotiabank),[^cite_note-135] [Wal-Mart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wal-Mart),[^cite_note-136] [Metro AG](305874.md)[^cite_note-137] and [Carrefour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrefour),[^cite_note-138] among other multinational corporations.

## Education and research

As of 2022, Chongqing hosts 70 institutions of higher education (excluding adult colleges), making it the fourth city with the most higher education institutions nationwide and the first city in [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China), which comprises Chongqing, six provinces ([Sichuan](65185.md), [Guizhou](173815.md), [Yunnan](166410.md), [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi), [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu), and [Qinghai](173818.md)), and three [autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_China) ([Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region), [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia), and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang)), with a combination of more than 290 million population.[^cite_note-147]

Chongqing is one of the top 35 cities in the world by scientific research outputs as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-nature.com-25]

## Transportation

Since its elevation to national-level municipality in 1997, the city has dramatically expanded its infrastructure. With the construction of railways and expressways to the east and southeast, Chongqing is a major transportation hub in southwestern China.

As of October 2014, the municipality had [31 bridges across the Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels) including over a dozen in the city's urban core.[^cite_note-150] Aside from the city's first two Yangtze River bridges, which were built, respectively, in 1960 and 1977, all of the other bridges were completed since 1995.

### Public transit

#### Chongqing Rail Transit



A train of Chongqing Rail Transit Line 2 coming through a residential building at [Liziba station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liziba_station)

Main article: [Chongqing Rail Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Rail_Transit)

Public transport in Chongqing consists of [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit), intercity railway, a ubiquitous bus system and the world's largest monorail network.

According to the Chongqing Municipal Government's ambitious plan in May 2007, Chongqing is investing 150 billion RMB over 13 years to finish a system that combines underground [metro lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) with heavy [monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorail).

As of 2017, four metro lines, the 14-kilometer (8.7 mi) long CRT [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1,_Chongqing_Rail_Transit), a conventional subway, and the 19-kilometer (12 mi) long heavy monorail CRT [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2,_Chongqing_Rail_Transit) (through Phase II), [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3,_Chongqing_Rail_Transit), a heavy monorail connects the airport and the southern part of downtown.[^cite_note-151] [Line 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_6,_Chongqing_Rail_Transit), runs between [Beibei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beibei_District), a district in the city's far north to downtown.[^cite_note-152] [Line 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_5,_Chongqing_Rail_Transit) opened in late 2017.

It was planned that by 2020, CRT would consist of 6 lines and 1 loop line, adding 363.5 kilometers (225.9 mi) of road and railway to the 2012 transportation infrastructure, and 93 new metro stations would be added to the 111 stations in place in 2012.[^cite_note-153] By 2050 Chongqing would have 18 lines.[^cite_note-154]

#### Aerial tramway



An aerial tramway across the Yangtze River in Chongqing CBD

Chongqing is the only Chinese city that has kept public [aerial tramways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_tramway). There were three aerial tramways in Chongqing: the Yangtze River Tramway, the Jialing River Tramway and the South Mountain Tramway, of which the Yangtze River Tramway is the only one still operating, now considered a Class 4A tourist attraction. The 1,160-meter (3,810 ft)-long tramway connects the southern and northern banks of the Yangtze River, carrying about 10,000 passengers a day.

### River port



[Hydrofoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofoil) on the Yangtze in the outer reaches of the municipality

Chongqing is one of the most important inland ports in China. There are numerous luxury cruise ships that terminate at Chongqing, cruising downstream along the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) to [Yichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichang), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) or even Shanghai.[^cite_note-155] In the recent past, this provided virtually the only transportation option along the river. However, improved rail, expressways and air travel have seen this ferry traffic reduced or cancelled altogether. Most of the river ferry traffic consists of leisure cruises for tourists rather than local needs. Improved access by larger cargo vessels has been made due to the construction of the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam). This allows bulk transport of goods along the Yangtze River. Coal, raw minerals and containerized goods provide the majority of traffic plying this section of the river. Several port handling facilities exist throughout the city, including many impromptu river bank sites.[^cite_note-156]

### Highways

Traditionally, the road network in Chongqing has been narrow, winding and limited to smaller vehicles because of the natural terrain, large rivers and the huge population demands on the area, especially in the [Yuzhong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzhong_District). In other places, such as [Jiangbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangbei_District,_Chongqing), large areas of homes and buildings have recently been cleared to improve the road network and create better urban planning; thus, several ring roads have also been constructed. This has seen many tunnels and large bridges needing to be built across the city. The construction of many expressways have connected Chongqing to its neighbors. The natural mountainous terrain that Chongqing is built on makes many road projects difficult to construct, including for example some of the world's highest road bridges.[^cite_note-157]

Unlike many other Chinese cities, it is rare for motorbikes, electric scooters or bicycles to be seen on Chongqing's Roads. This is due to the extremely hilly and mountainous nature of Chongqing's roads and streets. However, despite this, Chongqing is a manufacturing center for these types of vehicles.[^cite_note-158]

### Language

Main article: [Sichuanese Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_Mandarin)



Zhongshan Ancient Town, [Jiangjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangjin), Chongqing

The language native to Chongqing is [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin). More precisely, the great majority of the municipality, save for [Xiushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiushan), speak [Sichuanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_Mandarin), including the primary [Chengdu-Chongqing dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu-Chongqing_dialect) and [Minjiang dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minjiang_dialect) spoken in [Jiangjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangjin) and [Qijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qijiang).[^cite_note-162] There are also a few speakers of [Xiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Chinese) and [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) in the municipality, due to the great immigration wave to the Sichuan region (湖廣填川) during the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) and [Qing dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). In addition, in parts of southeastern Chongqing, the [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_languages) and [Tujia languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_language) are also used by some [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people) and [Tujia people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people).[^cite_note-163]

### Cuisine

Chongqing food is part of [Sichuan](65185.md) cuisine. Chongqing is known for its spicy food. Its food is normally considered numbing because of the use of [Sichuan pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_pepper), also known as Sichuan peppercorn, containing [hydroxy-alpha-sanshool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxy-alpha-sanshool). Chongqing's [city center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiefangbei_CBD) has many restaurants and food stalls where meals often cost less than [RMB](66958.md)10. Local specialties here include [dumplings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumpling) and [pickled vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickling), and, different from many other Chinese regional cuisines, Chongqing dishes are suitable for the solo diner as they are often served in small individually-sized portions.[^cite_note-180] Among the local specialties are:

- [Chongqing hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_hot_pot) – Chongqing's local culinary specialty which was originally from Northern China. Tables in [hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_pot) restaurants usually have a central pot, where food ordered by the customers is boiled in a spicy broth, items such as beef, pork, [tripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripe), [kidney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney) slices, pork aorta and goose intestine are often consumed.

  [^cite_note-181]

- [Chongqing *xiao mian*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_noodles) – a common [lamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamian) noodle dish tossed with [chili oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_oil) and mixtures of spices and ingredients

- Jiangtuan fish – since Chongqing is located along the Jialing River, visitors have a good opportunity to sample varieties of [aquatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture) products. Among them is a fish local to the region, Jiangtuan fish (*[Hypophthalmichthys nobilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypophthalmichthys_nobilis)*), more commonly known as bighead [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp).

  [^cite_note-182]

  &#x20;The fish is often served steamed or baked.

  [^cite_note-183]



Laziji is famous for its crispy texture.

- [Suan la fen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suan_la_fen) (sour and spicy [sweet potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato) noodles) – thick, transparent noodles of rubbery texture in a spicy vinegar soup.

  [^cite_note-184]

- [Laziji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laziji) (spicy chicken) – a stir-fried dish consisting of marinated and deep-fried pieces of chicken, dried Sichuan [chili peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_peppers), Sichuan peppercorns, garlic, and ginger,

  [^cite_note-185]

  &#x20;which originated near [Geleshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geleshan) in Chongqing.

  [^cite_note-186]

- Quanshui ji (spring water chicken) – cooked with the natural spring water in the [Southern Mountain of Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Mountain_of_Chongqing).

- Pork leg cooked with rock sugar – a common household dish of Chongqing, the tender, reddish dish has been described as having a strong and sweet aftertaste.

  [^cite_note-187]

- Qianzhang (skimmed soy bean cream) – the [cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream) skimmed from [soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean) milk. In order to create this, several steps must be followed very carefully. First, soybeans are soaked in water, ground, strained, boiled, restrained several times and spread over [gauze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauze) until delicate, snow-white cream is formed. The paste can also be hardened, cut into slivers and seasoned with [sesame oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_oil), garlic and [chili oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_oil). Another variation is to bake the cream and fry it with [bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon), which is described as soft and sweet.

  [^cite_note-188]

- [Fish with pickled mustard greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_with_pickled_mustard_greens) – a dish originating from Chongqing

### Media

The [Chongqing People's Broadcast Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_People's_Broadcast_Station) is Chongqing's largest radio station.[^cite_note-190] The only municipal-level TV network is [Chongqing TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_TV), claimed to be the fourth-largest television station in China.[^cite_note-191] Chongqing TV broadcasts many local-oriented channels, and can be viewed on many TV sets throughout China.

### Sports and recreation

#### Basketball

The [Chongqing Soaring Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Soaring_Dragons) became the 20th team playing in the [Chinese Basketball Association](939720.md) in 2013. They played at Datianwan Arena, in the same sporting complex as [Datianwan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datianwan_Stadium).[^cite_note-192] The team moved to Beijing in 2015 and is currently known as the [Beijing Royal Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Royal_Fighters).

#### Soccer

Professional soccer teams in Chongqing include:

- [Chongqing Liangjiang Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Liangjiang_Athletic_F.C.), *folded*

- [Chongqing F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_F.C.), *folded*

Chongqing Liangjiang Athletic was a professional Chinese soccer club that played in the [Chinese Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Super_League). They were owned by the Chongqing-based [Lifan Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifan_Group), which manufactures motorcycles, [cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car) and spare parts.[^cite_note-193] Originally called *Qianwei (Vanguard) Wuhan*, the club formed in 1995 to take part in the recently developed, fully professional [Chinese Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_football_league_system). They would quickly rise to top tier of the system and experience their greatest achievement in winning the 2000 [Chinese FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_FA_Cup),[^cite_note-194] and coming in fourth within the league. However, since then they have struggled to replicate the same success, and have twice been relegated from the top tier.[^cite_note-195]

Chongqing FC was a soccer club located in the city that competed in [China League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_League_One), the country's second-tier soccer division, before being relegated to the [China League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_League_Two), and dissolved due to a resultant lack of funds.[^cite_note-196]

#### Sport venues

Sport venues in Chongqing include:

- The [Chongqing Olympic Sports Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing_Olympic_Sports_Center) is a multipurpose stadium. It is currently used mostly for soccer matches, as it has a grass surface, and can hold 58,680. It was built in 2002 and was one of main venues for the [2004 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup).

  [^cite_note-197]

- [Yanghe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanghe_Stadium) is a multiuse stadium that is currently used mostly for soccer matches. The stadium holds 32,000 people, and is the home of Chongqing Lifan in the Chinese Super League. The stadium was purchased by the Lifan Group in 2001 for [RMB](66958.md)80 million and immediately replaced [Datianwan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datianwan_Stadium) as the home of Chongqing Lifan.

  [^cite_note-198]

- [Datianwan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datianwan_Stadium) is a multipurpose stadium that is currently used mostly for soccer matches. The stadium has a capacity 32,000 people, and up until 2001 was the home of Chongqing Lifan.

  [^cite_note-199]

### Cloud Valley

At the end of 2020, a collaboration between a Danish architecture firm and a Chinese tech company Terminus was announced, taking the form of an [AI](1164.md)-controlled campus. The project is named Cloud Valley and aims to use sensors and WiFi-controlled devices to collect data on the city's residents and atmosphere, including weather and eating and sleeping habits. The AI will adapt devices to work in a way that fits the gathered information and improves residents' lives.[^cite_note-200]

### "Backstreet" photography culture

In recent years, photographer-led groups have popularized "Houjie" (后街, Backstreet) photography walks in the older, non-touristed neighborhoods of Chongqing, such as in [Dadukou District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadukou_District). These walks aim to document the city's rapidly vanishing urban fabric and everyday life, creating a grassroots archival movement.[^cite_note-201]
