The **Long Island Rail Road** ([reporting mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporting_mark) **LI**), or **LIRR**, is a [railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) in the southeastern part of the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [New York](8210131.md), stretching from [Manhattan](45470.md) to the eastern tip of [Suffolk County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_County,_New_York) on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island). The railroad currently operates a public [commuter rail](6286.md) service, with its freight operations contracted to the [New York and Atlantic Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_and_Atlantic_Railway). With an average daily ridership of 328,300 (weekdays, Q2 2026)[^cite_note-APTAdaily-1], and annual ridership of 103,474,900 (2025)[^cite_note-APTA_Ridership_Report-2], it is the [busiest commuter railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_commuter_rail_systems_by_ridership) in North America. It is also one of the world's few commuter systems that run 24/7 year-round.[^cite_note-5] It is [publicly owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-owned_corporation) by the [Metropolitan Transportation Authority](384273.md), which refers to it as **MTA Long Island Rail Road**.

The LIRR logo combines the circular MTA logo with the text *Long Island Rail Road*, and appears on the sides of trains. The LIRR is one of two commuter rail systems owned by the MTA, the other being the [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad) in the northern suburbs of the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area). Established in 1834 (the first section between the Brooklyn waterfront and Jamaica opened on April 18, 1836) and having operated continuously since then, it is the oldest railroad in the United States still operating under its original name and charter.[^cite_note-LIRR175-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-abouttheLIRR-8][^cite_note-9] The railroad has been headquartered in the headhouse of [Jamaica Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Station), the second busiest station in the system, since 1913.[^cite_note-hq-10]

There are 126 stations and more than 700 miles (1,100 km) of track[^cite_note-abouttheLIRR-8] on its two main lines running the full length of the island and eight major branches, with the passenger railroad system totaling 319 [route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_length_\(transport\)#Route_length) miles (513 km).[^cite_note-mtabudget-11] As of 2018, the LIRR's budget for expenditures was \$1.6 billion plus \$450 million for debt service, which it supports through the collection of fares (which cover 43% of total expenses) along with dedicated taxes and other MTA revenue.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## History

Main article: [History of the Long Island Rail Road](9377403.md)



*Station, Bay Shore, Long Island, September 1879.*, a collodion silver glass wet plate negative by George Bradford Brainerd now on display at the [Brooklyn Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Museum)



LIRR (Montauk & NY) [RPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Post_Office) cover (TR27) for the railroad's 100th anniversary in April 1934

The **Long Island Rail Road Company** was chartered in 1834 to provide a daily service between [New York City](645042.md) and [Boston](24437894.md) via a ferry connection between its [Greenport, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenport,_Suffolk_County,_New_York), terminal on Long Island's North Fork and [Stonington, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonington,_Connecticut). This service was superseded in 1849 by the land route through Connecticut that became part of the [New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_New_Haven_and_Hartford_Railroad). The LIRR refocused its attentions towards serving Long Island, in competition with other railroads on the island. In the 1870s, railroad president [Conrad Poppenhusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Poppenhusen) and his successor, [Austin Corbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Corbin) acquired all the railroads and consolidated them into the LIRR.[^cite_note-14]

The LIRR was unprofitable for much of its history. In 1900, the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad) (PRR) bought a controlling interest as part of [its plan for direct access to Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Tunnel_Extension), which began on September 8, 1910. The wealthy PRR subsidized the LIRR during the first half of the new century, allowing expansion and modernization.[^cite_note-LIRR175-6] Electric operation began in 1905.[^cite_note-15]

After [World War II](32927.md), the railroad industry's downturn and dwindling profits caused the PRR to stop subsidizing the LIRR and the LIRR went into [receivership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receivership) in 1949. The [State of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_\(state\)), realizing how important the railroad was to Long Island's future, began to subsidize the railroad in the 1950s and continued doing so into the 1960s. In June 1965, the state finalized an agreement to buy the LIRR from the PRR for \$65 million.[^cite_note-16] The LIRR was placed under the control of a new [Metropolitan Commuter Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Commuter_Transit_Authority).[^cite_note-17] The MCTA was rebranded the [Metropolitan Transportation Authority](384273.md) in 1968 when it incorporated several other New York City-area transit agencies.[^cite_note-QnsGazette-QnsPubTrans-51stBDay-2015-18][^cite_note-19] With MTA subsidies the LIRR modernized further, continuing to be the busiest commuter railroad in the United States.[^cite_note-LIRR175-6] The company was officially converted to a [subsidiary public benefit corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_state_public-benefit_corporations) in 1980.[^cite_note-subsidiary-3]

The LIRR is one of the few railroads that have survived as intact companies from their original charters to the present.[^cite_note-LIRR175-6]

### 21st-century expansions

#### East Side Access

Main article: [East Side Access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Side_Access)

The East Side Access project built a LIRR spur to Grand Central Terminal that runs in part via the lower level of the existing 63rd Street Tunnel.[^cite_note-\:3-20] The East Side Access project added a new eight-track terminal called [Grand Central Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Madison) underneath the existing Grand Central Terminal.[^cite_note-\:3-20] The project was first proposed in the 1968 [Program for Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_for_Action), but due to various funding shortfalls, construction did not start until 2007.[^cite_note-21] As of April 2018, the project was expected to cost \$11.1 billion and was tentatively scheduled to start service in December 2022.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Siff_2018-23][^cite_note-Castillo_2018-24] It opened on January 25, 2023, with limited shuttle service between Jamaica and Grand Central.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Full service to Grand Central began on February 27, 2023.[^cite_note-27]

Several "readiness projects" were also completed to increase peak-hour capacity across the LIRR system in preparation for expanded peak-hour service after the completion of East Side Access.[^cite_note-TimesLedger_Readiness-28][^cite_note-Jan_2013-29][^cite_note-Nov_2017-30] The LIRR constructed a new platform for [Atlantic Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Terminal)-bound trains at [Jamaica station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_station),[^cite_note-31] converting most [Atlantic Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Branch) service between these two stations into a high-frequency shuttle.[^cite_note-MTA-LIRR-Hicksville-2016-32] The LIRR also installed a new storage track east of [Massapequa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massapequa_station) and extended one east of [Great Neck station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Neck_station),[^cite_note-Nov_2017-30][^cite_note-Great_Neck_Record-33] in addition to expanding the train yard at [Ronkonkoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronkonkoma_station).[^cite_note-Nov_2017-30][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-schedule-35] An expansion of the yard at [Port Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_station) was also proposed, but as of September 2022, the MTA has not come to an agreement with the [Town of North Hempstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_North_Hempstead),[^cite_note-Nov_2020-36]<sup>: 65 </sup> resulting in the project being postponed indefinitely.[^cite_note-senate092822-37][^cite_note-Nov_2021-38]<sup>: 61 </sup>

There are also plans to build [a new station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside_station_\(New_York\)) in the Queens neighborhood of [Sunnyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside,_Queens), in between the New York terminals and the [Woodside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodside_station_\(LIRR\)) station, serving as a rail hub for all LIRR branches and potentially some Amtrak, Metro North (New Haven Line) and [New Jersey Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) trains, as well.[^cite_note-\:4-39] The Sunnyside station is to be built after the completion of East Side Access, due to current capacity constraints.[^cite_note-\:4-39]

#### Main Line projects

The completed second track, as viewed from the reconstructed Wyandanch station.

The Ronkonkoma Branch's completed second track at the Wyandanch station

In 2012, the LIRR started adding a second track along the formerly single-tracked section of the Main Line between [Farmingdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmingdale_station) and [Ronkonkoma stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronkonkoma_station) to increase track capacity and allow for enhanced service options.[^cite_note-40] The project was completed in September 2018.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

As part of the preparations for East Side Access's opening,[^cite_note-MTA_2007_Third_Track_Presentation-43] the LIRR also widened the two-track sections of the Main Line between [Floral Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_Park_station) and [Hicksville stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hicksville_station) to three tracks, in addition to eliminating each of the grade crossings and rebuilding all of the stations along this stretch of the Main Line.[^cite_note-NYT_Fitzsimmons_01.05.2016-44][^cite_note-MTA_Third_Track_01.05.2016-45] Work on the third-track project started in September 2018.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] The project was completed in 2022, in time for the opening of East Side Access.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

The larger Belmont Park Redevelopment Project called for a new [Elmont station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont_station) between the [Queens Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Village_station) and [Bellerose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellerose_station) stations on the Main Line, to better serve the new [UBS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS_Arena) in the Nassau County neighborhood of [Elmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont,_New_York).[^cite_note-51] It was the first new station built by the LIRR in nearly 50 years; the last new station added was the former [Southampton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_College_\(LIRR_station\)) station on the Montauk Branch, which opened in 1976 and closed in 1998, due to low ridership and the high cost of installing [high-level platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-level_platform) for the then-new C3 railcars.[^cite_note-52] Elmont's eastbound platform officially opened in November 2021,[^cite_note-53] while the westbound platform opened in October 2022.[^cite_note-54]

- [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)), in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), is the busiest of the western terminals, serving almost 500 daily trains.

The New York City Subway's [34th Street–Penn Station (IRT Broadway–Seventh Avenue Line)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_Street%E2%80%93Penn_Station_\(IRT_Broadway%E2%80%93Seventh_Avenue_Line\)) ([1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), ​[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), and ​[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) trains) and [34th Street–Penn Station (IND Eighth Avenue Line)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_Street%E2%80%93Penn_Station_\(IND_Eighth_Avenue_Line\)) ([A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), ​[C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), and ​[E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) trains) stations are adjacent to the terminal. It also connects LIRR with Amtrak and [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) trains.

- [Grand Central Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Madison_station) is located under [Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal) and was built as part of the [East Side Access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Side_Access) project.

  [^cite_note-abouttheLIRR-8]

  &#x20;From Hunterspoint Avenue, the [Hunters Point Avenue subway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunters_Point_Avenue_\(IRT_Flushing_Line\)) ([7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) and [<7>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7d_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\))​ trains) can be reached. In addition, the [Jamaica station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_station) is a major [hub station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub) and transfer point in [Jamaica, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens). It has ten tracks and six platforms, plus [yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_yard) and bypass tracks. Passengers can transfer between trains on all LIRR lines except the [Port Washington Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_Branch).[^cite_note-abouttheLIRR-8] The sixth platform opened in February 2020, and exclusively serves [Atlantic Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Branch) shuttle trains to Brooklyn.[^cite_note-MTA-LIRR-Hicksville-2016-32] Transfer is also made to separate facilities for three subway services at the [Sutphin Boulevard–Archer Avenue–JFK Airport station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutphin_Boulevard%E2%80%93Archer_Avenue%E2%80%93JFK_Airport_station) ([E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\))​, ​[J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), and ​[Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) trains), a number of bus routes, and the [AirTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirTrain_JFK) automated [people mover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_mover) to [JFK Airport](97218.md).[^cite_note-60] The railroad's headquarters are next to the station.[^cite_note-61]

A schematic of the LIRR's routes and fare zones. This schematic is not to scale.

The Long Island Rail Road system has eleven passenger branches, three of which are main trunk lines:[^cite_note-\:5-62]

- [Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Line_\(Long_Island_Rail_Road\)), running along the middle of the island, between [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City_station) and [Greenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenport_station), via Jamaica.

  [^cite_note-\:5-62]

- [Montauk Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauk_Branch), running along the southern edge of the island, between Long Island City and [Montauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauk_station), via Jamaica.

  [^cite_note-\:5-62]

- [Atlantic Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Branch), running mostly in New York City to the south of both the Main Line and Montauk Branch, between Atlantic Terminal and [Valley Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Stream_station), via Jamaica.

  [^cite_note-\:5-62]

  [^cite_note-63]

There are eight minor branches.[^cite_note-\:5-62] For scheduling and advertising purposes some of these branches are divided into sections; this is the case with the Montauk Branch, which is known as the [Babylon Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_Branch) service in the electrified portion of the line between Jamaica and Babylon, while the diesel service beyond Babylon to Montauk is referred to as Montauk Branch service. All branches except the [Port Washington Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_Branch) pass through [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_\(LIRR_station\)); the trackage west of Jamaica (except the Port Washington Branch) is known as the [City Terminal Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Terminal_Zone). The City Terminal Zone includes portions of the Main Line, Atlantic, and Montauk Branches, as well as the [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)-owned [East River Tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River_Tunnels) to Penn Station.[^cite_note-64]

### Current branches



A map of diesel territory on the Long Island Rail Road

- The Main Line runs from [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City_station) east to [Greenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenport_station). It is electrified west of the [Ronkonkoma station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronkonkoma_station); limited diesel train service runs from this point to the [Yaphank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaphank_station), [Riverhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverhead_station), or Greenport stations. Trains using the East River Tunnels from Penn Station join the line at [Sunnyside Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside_Yard). The services that run along this line are named after the branches they use; trains beyond Hicksville, where the Port Jefferson Branch diverges, are known as [Ronkonkoma Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronkonkoma_Branch) and Greenport Branch trains.

- The Montauk Branch runs from Long Island City east to the [Montauk station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauk_station), with junctions with the Main Line at Long Island City and [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_station). It is electrified from Jamaica east to [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_station). Trains operating east of Babylon are listed as Montauk Branch service and are hauled by diesel locomotives, while trains using the line from Jamaica to Babylon are labeled as [Babylon Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_Branch) trains. The portion of the line between Long Island City and Jamaica, known as the Lower Montauk Branch, no longer carries passenger trains and is used only for freight service.

- The electrified Atlantic Branch runs from [Atlantic Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Terminal) in [Downtown Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Brooklyn) east to Jamaica, where it meets the Main Line and the Montauk Branch, and then heads southeast to become the Long Beach Branch east of [Valley Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Stream_station). East of Valley Stream, the [Far Rockaway Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_Branch) turns south, while the [West Hempstead Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hempstead_Branch) turns northward.

- The electrified [Port Washington Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_Branch), the only one that does not serve Jamaica, branches from the Main Line west of [Woodside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodside_station_\(LIRR\)) (running parallel to the Main Line until [Winfield Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfield_Junction_station), which is east of that station) and runs east to [Port Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_station). It only serves four stations in [Nassau County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York). It includes the [Manhasset Viaduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhasset_Viaduct), which is the highest bridge on the LIRR network.

  [^cite_note-65]

- The [Port Jefferson Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Jefferson_Branch) splits from the Main Line east of Hicksville, with electric service to [Huntington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_station_\(LIRR\)). The section from Huntington to [Port Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Jefferson_station_\(LIRR\)) is served only by diesel trains. Until 1938, it continued east to [Wading River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wading_River_station).

  [^cite_note-steel-66]

  : 258&#x20;

- The electrified [Hempstead Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hempstead_Branch) splits from the Main Line east of [Elmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont_station) (running parallel to the Main Line until just after [Floral Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_Park_station)) and runs east to [Hempstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Parks_Hempstead_Transit_Center). At [Garden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City_station_\(LIRR\)), the [Garden City–Mitchel Field Secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City%E2%80%93Mitchel_Field_Secondary) curves off and goes to [Mitchel Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchel_Field).

- The electrified [West Hempstead Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hempstead_Branch) splits from the Montauk Branch east of the Valley Stream station and runs northeast to [West Hempstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hempstead_station), originally continuing to junctions with the Hempstead Branch and the Oyster Bay Branch at the Main Line.

- The [Oyster Bay Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_Bay_Branch) splits from the Main Line east of [Mineola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineola_station_\(LIRR\)) and heads north and east to [Oyster Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_Bay_station). The first section to [East Williston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Williston_station) is electrified;

  [^cite_note-67]

  &#x20;only diesel trains run along the majority of the line to Oyster Bay.

  [^cite_note-LIRR_Stations_p._57-68]

- The diesel-only [Central Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Branch_\(Long_Island_Rail_Road\)) runs southeast from the Main Line east of [Bethpage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethpage_station) to the Montauk Branch west of the Babylon station, giving an alternate route to the Montauk Branch east of Babylon. The Central Branch used to continue west from Bethpage to include what is now the Garden City–Mitchel Field Secondary. It was proposed to be electrified as part of the 2020–2024 MTA Capital Program.

  [^cite_note-\:2-69]

- The electrified [Far Rockaway Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_Branch) splits from the Atlantic Branch east of the Valley Stream station and runs south and southwest to [Far Rockaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_station_\(LIRR\)). It used to connect westward to what is now the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway)'s [IND Rockaway Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IND_Rockaway_Line) to the [Hammels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammels,_Queens) and [Rockaway Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockaway_Park,_Queens) neighborhoods of Queens.

- The electrified [Long Beach Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Branch) begins where the Atlantic Branch ends east of the Valley Stream station (running parallel to the Montauk Branch until just after [Lynbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynbrook_station)) where it turns south to end at [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_station_\(LIRR\)).

### Former branches

The railroad has dropped a number of branches due to lack of ridership over the years. Part of the [Rockaway Beach Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockaway_Beach_Branch) became part of the [IND Rockaway Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IND_Rockaway_Line) of the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway), while others were downgraded to freight branches, and the rest [abandoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abandoned_railway) entirely. Additionally, the Long Island Rail Road operated trains over portions of the [Brooklyn Rapid Transit (BRT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Rapid_Transit_Company) elevated and subway lines until 1917.[^cite_note-70]

- The [Bethpage Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethpage_Branch) ran north from the Main Line and Central Branch at [Bethpage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethpage,_New_York).

  [^cite_note-71]

- The [Bushwick Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushwick_Branch), also called the Bushwick Lead Track, is a freight railroad branch that runs from [Bushwick, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushwick,_Brooklyn), to Fresh Pond Junction in Queens, where it connects with the Montauk Branch.

- The Camp Upton Branch was a short branch north from the Main Line to [Camp Upton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Upton).

  [^cite_note-72]

- The [Cedarhurst Cut-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedarhurst_Cut-off), officially known as the **New York and Rockaway Railroad**, was an extension of the [Montauk Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauk_Branch) from its merger with the [Atlantic Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Branch) at [Springfield Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield_Junction_\(Long_Island_Rail_Road\)) to [Cedarhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedarhurst,_New_York), where it would turn west and run parallel to the [Far Rockaway Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_Branch) until reaching Mott Avenue in [Far Rockaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway,_Queens).

- The [Central Extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Extension_\(Long_Island_Rail_Road\)) ran from Garden City eastward to Central Park (¾ mile south of current Bethpage station) and as far east as Bethpage Junction. The line was cut back to the point where it stopped at Island Trees. Today the western part of track still in use for freight and storage, and is officially known today as the [Garden City Secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City_Secondary).

- The Chestnut Street Incline (Brooklyn) between Atlantic Avenue and Fulton Street was opened in 1898 to allow for thru-operation over the Jamaica/Broadway Elevated Line to the East River ferry terminal. In 1909 thru passenger service to Manhattan via the [Williamsburg Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg_Bridge) was established in coordination with the [Brooklyn Rapid Transit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Rapid_Transit_Company) (BRT). LIRR Passenger service operated to Chambers Street between May 1909 and September 1917.

- The [Creedmoor Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creedmoor_Branch), a remnant of the [Central Railroad of Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Railroad_of_Long_Island) (CRRLI) of [Alexander Turney Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Turney_Stewart), was a short branch from the Main Line at [Floral Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_Park,_NY) northwest through [Creedmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creedmoor,_NY). It once went as far northwest as [Flushing](267693.md).

- The [Evergreen Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_Branch) connected the [Bushwick Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushwick_Branch) east of [Bushwick Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushwick_Terminal) with the [Bay Ridge Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Ridge_Branch) north of [East New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_New_York,_Brooklyn).

- The [Flushing Bay Freight Spur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Bay_Freight_Spur) extended north from the [Whitestone Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitestone_Branch), then across the [Woodside Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodside_Branch) and then the connecting line between both branches before terminating along the south coast of [Flushing Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Bay).

## Fare structure

Like [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad) and NJ Transit, the Long Island Rail Road fare system is based on the distance a passenger travels, as opposed to the New York City Subway and the area's bus systems, which charge a flat rate. The railroad is broken up into eight non-consecutively numbered fare zones. Zone 1, the [City Terminal Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Terminal_Zone), includes Penn Station, Grand Central, all stations in Brooklyn, all stations in Queens west of Jamaica on the Main Line, and Mets–Willets Point.[^cite_note-96]

Zone 3 includes Jamaica as well as all other stations in eastern Queens except Far Rockaway.[^cite_note-mta.info-97] Zones 4 and 7 include all stations in Nassau County, plus Far Rockaway and Belmont Park in Queens.[^cite_note-mta.info-97] Zones 9, 10, 12 and 14 include all stations in Suffolk County.[^cite_note-mta.info-97] Each zone contains many stations, and the same fare applies for travel between any station in the origin zone and any station in the destination zone.[^cite_note-mta.info-97]

LIRR ticket vending machines, as seen at the Bethpage station.

LIRR ticket vending machines at the Bethpage station

### Peak and off-peak fares

Peak fares are charged during the week on trains that arrive at western terminals between 6 AM and 10 AM, and for trains that depart from western terminals between 4 PM and 8 PM.[^cite_note-98] Any passenger holding an off-peak ticket on a peak train is required to pay a step up fee.[^cite_note-web.mta.info-99] Passengers can buy tickets from ticket agents or ticket vending machines (TVMs) or on the train from conductors, but will incur an on-board penalty fee for doing so.[^cite_note-web.mta.info-99] This fee is waived for customers boarding at a station without a ticket office or ticket machine, senior citizens, people with disabilities or Medicare customers.[^cite_note-web.mta.info-99]

There are several types of tickets: one way, round trip, peak, off-peak, AM peak or off-peak senior/disabled, peak child, and off-peak child. On off-peak trains, passengers can buy a family ticket for children who are accompanied by an 18-year-old for \$0.75 if bought from the station agent or TVM, \$1.00 on the train. Senior citizen/disabled passengers traveling during the morning peak hours are required to pay the AM peak senior citizen/disabled rate. This rate is not charged during PM peak hours.[^cite_note-100]

Commuters can also buy a peak or off-peak ten trip ride, a weekly unlimited or an unlimited monthly pass.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-101] Monthly passes are good on any train regardless of the time of day, within the fare zones specified on the pass.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-101]

The LIRR charged off-peak fares at all times during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-102] Peak fares were reinstated on March 1, 2022, and several new discounts and ticket options were introduced at the same time.[^cite_note-103]

### Special fares

During the summer the railroad offers special summer package ticket deals to places such as Long Beach, Jones Beach, the Hamptons, Montauk, and Greenport. Passengers traveling to the Hamptons and Montauk on the *Cannonball* can reserve a seat in the all-reserved Parlor Cars.[^cite_note-MTA_2015-104]

Prior to November 2021, passengers going to Belmont Park had to buy a special ticket to go from Jamaica to Belmont Park (or vice versa). Weekly and monthly passes were not accepted at Belmont Park.[^cite_note-mta.info1-105] With the opening of [Elmont station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont_station) in November 2021, Belmont Park and Elmont were placed into fare zone 4.[^cite_note-106]

### CityTicket

In 2003, the LIRR and Metro-North started a pilot program in which passengers traveling within New York City were allowed to buy one-way tickets for \$2.50.[^cite_note-Lisi_2003-107] The special reduced-fare CityTicket, proposed by the New York City Transit Riders Council,[^cite_note-Lisi_2003-107] was formally introduced in 2004.[^cite_note-108] The discounted fares were initially only available for travel on Saturdays and Sundays.[^cite_note-109] In March 2022, it was expanded to include all off-peak trains throughout the week for \$5.[^cite_note-110] The MTA announced plans in December 2022 to allow CityTickets to be used on peak trains as well;[^cite_note-Feldman_2022-111][^cite_note-Castillo_2022-112] governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul) confirmed these plans the next month.[^cite_note-Martinez_2023-113] The peak CityTickets, as announced in July 2023, would cost \$7 each.[^cite_note-Kaye_2023_g749-114] As part of a one-year pilot program starting in July 2024, monthly tickets for LIRR trips entirely within New York City would also receive a 10% discount.[^cite_note-CBS_New_York_2024_c370-115][^cite_note-Simko-Bednarski_2024_p168-116]

CityTicket is valid for travel within Zones 1 and 3 on the Long Island Rail Road. CityTickets can only be bought before boarding – except at [Mets–Willets Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mets%E2%80%93Willets_Point_station_\(LIRR\)), where they can be purchased on board; they must be used on the day of purchase.[^cite_note-mta.info1-105]

CityTicket is not valid for travel to the [Elmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont_station) station (located in Nassau County, just east of the Queens-Nassau border) – or the [Far Rockaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_station_\(LIRR\)) or special event-only [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park_\(LIRR_station\)) station (located in Queens, just west of the Queens-Nassau border) – and are all within Zone 4.[^cite_note-mta.info1-105]

### Freedom Ticket

In late 2017, the MTA was slated to launch a pilot that will allow LIRR, bus and subway service to use one ticket.[^cite_note-amny20170208-117] The proposal for the ticket, called the "Freedom Ticket," was initially put forth by the New York City Transit Riders Council (NYCTRC) in 2007.[^cite_note-pcac201512-118]<sup>: 1 </sup> The NYCTRC wrote a [proof of concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_concept) report in 2015.[^cite_note-amny20151202-119] At the time of the report, [express bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_express_bus_routes_in_New_York_City) riders from Southeast Queens had some of the longest commutes in the city, with their commutes being 96 minutes long, yet they paid a premium fare of \$6.50.[^cite_note-pcac201512-118]

Riders who take the [dollar van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar_vans_in_the_New_York_metropolitan_area) to the subway paid \$4.75 to get to Manhattan in 65 minutes; riders who only took the bus and subway paid \$2.75 to get to Manhattan in 86 minutes; and riders who took the LIRR paid \$10 to get to Manhattan in 35 minutes.[^cite_note-pcac201512-118]<sup>: iii </sup> Unlike the CityTicket, the Freedom Ticket would be valid for off-peak and multidirectional travel; have free transfers to the subway and bus system; and be capped at \$215 per month.[^cite_note-pcac201512-118]<sup>: 1–2 </sup> At the time, monthly CityTickets cost \$330 per month.[^cite_note-amny20151202-119]

The Freedom Ticket will initially be available for sale at the [Atlantic Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Terminal), [Nostrand Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostrand_Avenue_\(LIRR_station\)), and [East New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_New_York_\(LIRR_station\)) stations in Brooklyn and at the [Laurelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurelton_\(LIRR_station\)), [Locust Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locust_Manor_\(LIRR_station\)), [Rosedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosedale_\(LIRR_station\)), and [St. Albans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Albans_\(LIRR_station\)) stations in Queens.[^cite_note-amny20170208-117][^cite_note-\:0-120][^cite_note-\:1-121] Riders, under the pilot, would be able to purchase one-way, weekly, or monthly passes that will be valid on the LIRR, on buses, and the subway. The fare will be higher than the price of a ride on the [MetroCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroCard_\(New_York_City\)), but it will be lower than the combined price of an LIRR ticket and a MetroCard, and it will allow unlimited free transfers between the LIRR, buses, and subway.[^cite_note-\:1-121]

The former head of the MTA, Thomas Prendergast, announced at the January 2017 board meeting that the plan would be explored in a field study to determine fares and the impact on existing service.[^cite_note-amny20170208-117] The plan is intended to fill approximately 20,000 unused seats of existing trains to Atlantic Terminal and Penn Station (or about 50% to 60% of peak trains in each direction),[^cite_note-\:0-120] while at the same time providing affordable service to people with long commutes.[^cite_note-\:1-121] The details were to be announced in spring 2017, and the pilot would last six months.[^cite_note-amny20170208-117]

The MTA Board voted to approve a six-month pilot for a similar concept, the Atlantic Ticket, in May 2018. The Atlantic Ticket is similar in that it would allow LIRR riders in southeast Queens to purchase a one-way ticket to or from Atlantic Terminal for \$5. The Atlantic Ticket would start in June 2018.[^cite_note-122] The success of the pilot program has led the MTA to extend the program up to the summer of 2020 and renewed calls for the program to be implemented within New York City, where the fare for the Freedom Ticket—if approved—would cost US\$2.75 and include free transfers between the LIRR & Metro-North, bus, and subway.[^cite_note-123]

### Far Rockaway Ticket

In May 2023, the MTA announced that, as part of wider fare changes and in response to requests from [Far Rockaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway) residents, a discounted ticket option – the Far Rockaway Ticket – would be introduced for travelers traveling on the [Far Rockaway Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_Branch) between Far Rockaway and other stations within New York City (excluding Belmont Park). This new ticket would provide the same discounts as a regular CityTicket, while also having protections against fare evasion, given [the unique nature of the Far Rockaway Branch's route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_Branch#Stations).[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-\:12-125][^cite_note-\:22-126] Tickets can only be purchased at the Far Rockaway station or on the MTA's TrainTime app in the station's vicinity; geolocation restrictions on the TrainTime app only allow purchase of discounted tickets within the vicinity of the Far Rockaway station.[^cite_note-\:12-125][^cite_note-\:22-126]

The Far Rockaway Ticket became available for purchase on August 20, 2023.[^cite_note-\:22-126]

### OMNY

Main article: [OMNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMNY)

In 2017, it was announced that the [MetroCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroCard) fare payment system, used on New York City-area rapid transit and bus systems, would be phased out and replaced by [OMNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMNY), a [contactless fare payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contactless_payment) system. Fare payment would be made using [Apple Pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Pay), [Google Pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Pay_\(payment_method\)), debit/credit cards with [near-field communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-field_communication) enabled, or radio-frequency identification cards.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] As part of the implementation of OMNY, the MTA also plans to use the system in the Long Island Rail Road and Metro-North Railroad.[^cite_note-129]

### Combo Ticket

In December 2022, the MTA announced the launch of an additional fare for use on journeys that utilize both of its railroad systems via Grand Central. The fare is priced as \$8 more than an adult off-peak ticket from an origin station on one system to Grand Central. It is valid on both peak and off-peak trains.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

## Train operations



The interior of an [M7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M7_\(railcar\)) car

The LIRR is relatively isolated from the rest of the national rail system despite operating out of Penn Station, the nation's busiest rail terminal. It connects with other railroads in just two locations:

- West of [Harold Interlocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Interlocking) in [Sunnyside, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside,_Queens), LIRR trains enter the Amtrak-operated [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) leading to the [East River Tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River_Tunnels). When this track was owned by the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad), trains of the PRR connected to the LIRR at Penn Station. During the 1920s and 1930s a through sleeper was carried by PRR and LIRR trains from Pittsburgh to Montauk, called the 'Sunrise Special'.

  [^cite_note-arrts-arrchives.com-132]

  [^cite_note-arrts-arrchives1927-133]

- In [Glendale, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_Queens), the LIRR connects with CSX's [Fremont Secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Secondary), which leads to the [Hell Gate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_Gate_Bridge) and [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England); however, once trains leave the secondary, they enter LIRR trackage.

  [^cite_note-134]

All LIRR trains have an engineer (driver in non-US English) who operates the train, a conductor who is responsible for the safe movement of the train, fare collection and on-board customer service, as well as an assistant conductor, commonly referred to as the brakeman. In addition, trains may have one or more assistant conductors to assist with fare collection and other duties. The LIRR is one of the last railroads in the United States to use mechanical interlocking control towers to regulate rail traffic.[^cite_note-135]

As of 2016, the LIRR has 8 active control towers. All movements on the LIRR are under the control of the Movement Bureau in Jamaica, which gives orders to the towers that control a specific portion of the railroad. Movements in Amtrak territory are controlled by [Penn Station Control Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)#Tracks_and_surrounding_infrastructure) or PSCC, run jointly by the LIRR and Amtrak. The PSCC controls as far east as [Harold Interlocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Interlocking), in [Sunnyside, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside,_Queens). The PSCC replaced several towers.[^cite_note-136]

The Jamaica Control Center, operational since the third quarter of 2010, controls the area around Jamaica terminal by direct control of interlockings. This replaced several towers in Jamaica including Jay and Hall towers at the west and east ends of Jamaica station respectively. At additional locations, line side towers control the various switches and signals in accordance with the timetable and under the direction of the Movement Bureau in Jamaica.[^cite_note-137]

### Signal and safety systems

Today's LIRR signal system has evolved from its legacy [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad) (PRR)-based system, and the railroad utilizes a variety of wayside [railroad signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_signal) including position light, color light and dwarf signals.[^cite_note-138] In addition, much of the LIRR is equipped with a bi-directional [Pulse code cab signaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_code_cab_signaling) called [automatic speed control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_train_control) (ASC), though portions of the railway still retain single direction, wayside-only signaling. Unlike other railroads, which began using color-light signals in the 20th century, the LIRR did not begin using signals with color lights on its above-ground sections until 2006.

Some portions of the railway lack automatic signals and cab signals completely, instead train and track car movements are governed only by timetable and verbal/written [train orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_order_operation), although these areas are gradually receiving modern signals.[^cite_note-139] Many other signals and switching systems on the LIRR are being modernized and upgraded as part of the Main Line's Third Track Project, most notably at Mineola, where the system is being completely redone and modernized.[^cite_note-140]

A Long Island Rail Road M9, showing both over-running and under-running third rail shoes.

A Long Island Rail Road M9, showing the railcar's dual-type contact shoe

On portions of the railroad equipped with ASC, engineers consult the speed display unit, which is capable of displaying seven speed indications. As a result of a December 1, 2013, [train derailment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2013_Spuyten_Duyvil_derailment) in [the Bronx](3338.md) on the Metro-North Railroad, railroads with similar cab signal systems to Metro-North, such as the LIRR, were ordered to modify the systems to enforce certain speed limit changes, which has resulted in lower average speeds and actual speed limits across the LIRR.[^cite_note-141]

### Power transmission

The LIRR's electrified lines are powered via a [third rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_rail) at [750 volts DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_railway_electrification_systems#750_V_DC_conductor).[^cite_note-142]

### Electric fleet

The LIRR's electric fleet consists of 836 [M7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M7_\(railcar\)), 170 [M3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3_\(railcar\)), and 202 [M9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M9_\(railcar\))[^cite_note-143] [electric multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_multiple_unit) cars in married pairs, meaning each car needs the other one to operate, with each car containing its own engineer's cab. &#x20;On June 23, 2025, the MTA awarded the [M9A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M9_\(railcar\)) contract to [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom). 160 of the 316 cars being built will run on the LIRR.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]&#x20;

### Diesel and dual-mode fleets

The LIRR also uses 134 [C3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C3_\(railcar\)) [bilevel coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilevel_rail_car) powered by 24 [DE30AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMD_DE30AC_and_DM30AC) [diesel-electric locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel-electric_locomotive) and 20 DM30AC [dual-mode locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-mode_locomotive). They are used mostly on non-electrified branches, including the Port Jefferson, Oyster Bay, Montauk, Central, and Greenport Branches. There are also 23 [MP15AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP15AC) locomotives in use as work trains and yard switchers.[^cite_note-152]

## Freight service

Main article: [Rail freight transportation in New York City and Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_freight_transportation_in_New_York_City_and_Long_Island)

For more details on the franchisee that runs freight service for the LIRR, see [New York and Atlantic Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_and_Atlantic_Railway).

A New York and Atlantic freight train at Jamaica station.

A [NYAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYAR) freight train at Jamaica station

The LIRR and other railroads that became part of the system have always had freight service, though this has diminished. The process of shedding freight service accelerated with the acquisition of the railroad by New York State. In the 21st century, there has been some appreciation of the need for better [railroad freight service in New York City and on Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_freight_transportation_in_New_York_City_and_Long_Island).[^cite_note-\:6-160] Both areas are primarily served by trucking for freight haulage – an irony in a region with the most extensive rail transit service in the Americas, as well as the worst traffic conditions.[^cite_note-161]

Proposals for a [Cross-Harbor Rail Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-Harbor_Rail_Tunnel) for freight have existed for years to alleviate these issues, and, in recent years, there have been many new pushes for its construction by officials.[^cite_note-\:6-160][^cite_note-\:7-162] Financial issues, as well as bureaucracy, remain major hurdles in constructing it.[^cite_note-\:7-162] In May 1997, freight service was franchised on a 20-year term to the [New York and Atlantic Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_and_Atlantic_Railway) (NYAR), a short line railroad owned by the [Anacostia and Pacific Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anacostia_and_Pacific_Company).[^cite_note-163]

It has its own equipment and crews, but uses the rail facilities of the LIRR. To the east, freight service operates to the end of the West Hempstead Branch, to Huntington on the Port Jefferson Branch, to Bridgehampton on the Montauk Branch, and to Riverhead on the Main Line. On the western end it provides service on the surviving freight-only tracks of the LIRR: the Bay Ridge and Bushwick branches; the "Lower Montauk" between Jamaica and Long Island City; and to an interchange connection at [Fresh Pond Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Pond_Junction) in Queens with the [CSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSX), [Canadian Pacific Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Kansas_City), and [Providence and Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_and_Worcester) railroads.[^cite_note-164]

## Planned service expansions

### Electrification projects

As part of the MTA's 2020–2024 Capital Program, the Central Branch, shown here, will be electrified.

A diesel train running along the Central Branch in 2012

As part of the 2020–2024 MTA Capital Program, the MTA proposed electrifying the LIRR's [Central Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Branch_\(Long_Island_Rail_Road\)), which would allow for enhanced service options and capacity, and to mitigate service disruptions, should one arise. Although funding was initially allocated through the 2020–2024 MTA Capital Program, the project was ultimately put on hold.[^cite_note-\:2-69]

There have also been many pushes by residents and politicians over the past several decades – most recently by former New York State Senator [Jim Gaughran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Gaughran) – to electrify the remainder of the Port Jefferson Branch between the [Huntington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_station_\(LIRR\)) and [Port Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Jefferson_station_\(LIRR\)) stations, in addition to the remainder of the Oyster Bay Branch between the [East Williston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Williston_station) and [Oyster Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_Bay_station), to enhance service in the served areas and to upgrade service capacities along the lines; electrifying these lines could lead to more frequent direct service to and from Manhattan, as diesel trains are not allowed in Penn Station and dual-mode trains exceed the height clearance for the [63rd Street Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Street_Tunnel) into [Grand Central Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Madison).[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

## Criticism and controversy

### Passenger issues

The LIRR has a long history of tense relations with its passengers.[^cite_note-172] Daily commuters have long had complaints about the LIRR's service. According to a 1999 article in *[The New York Times](30680.md),* the LIRR's service woes were long considered part of the "unholy trinity of life on Long Island," along with the [Long Island Lighting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Lighting_Company)'s high rates and the [Long Island Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Expressway)'s traffic snarls.[^cite_note-173] Various commuter advocacy groups have been formed to try to represent those interests, in addition to the state mandated LIRR Commuters Council.[^cite_note-174]

The LIRR has been criticized for not providing additional service to the [East End of Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_\(Long_Island\)) as the twin forks continue to grow in popularity as a year-round tourist and residential destination. Demand is evidenced by flourishing for-profit bus services such as the [Hampton Jitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Jitney) and the [Hampton Luxury Liner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Luxury_Liner) and the early formative stages of a new East End Transportation Authority.[^cite_note-175] Local politicians have joined the public outcry for the LIRR to either improve the frequency of east end services, or turn the operation over to a local transportation authority.

Critics claim that the [on-time performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-time_performance) (OTP) calculated by the LIRR is manipulated to be artificially high. Because the LIRR does not release any raw timing data nor does it have independent (non-MTA) audits it is impossible to verify this claim, or the accuracy of the current On Time Performance measurement. The percentage measure is used by many other US passenger railroads but the criticism over accuracy is specific to the LIRR. As defined by the LIRR, a train is "on time" if it arrives at a station within 5 minutes and 59 seconds of the scheduled time.[^cite_note-176] The criterion was 4 minutes and 59 seconds until the LIRR changed it because of a bug in their computer systems.[^cite_note-\:10-177]

Critics[^cite_note-178] believe the OTP measure does not reflect what commuters experience on a daily basis. The LIRR publishes the current OTP in a monthly booklet called TrainTalk.[^cite_note-179] TrainTalk was previously known as "Keeping Track."[^cite_note-180] A more accurate way to measure delays and OTP has been proposed.[^cite_note-\:10-177] Called the "Passenger Hours Delayed" index, it can measure the total person-hours of a specific delay. This would be useful in comparing the performance of specific days or incidents, day-to-day (or week-to-week) periods, but has not been adopted.

Ridership has increased from 81 million passengers in 2011 to 89.3 million passengers in 2016, which is the railroad's highest ridership since 1949. The all-time highest ridership was in 1929, when 119 million passengers rode 1.89 billion passenger miles.[^cite_note-181] This increase in ridership has been attributed to the increased usage of the LIRR by millennials, and the increase of reverse-peak travel.[^cite_note-182]

### Pension and disability fraud scandal

A *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* investigation in 2008 showed that 25% of LIRR employees who had retired since 2000 filed for disability payments from the federal Railroad Retirement Board and 97% of them were approved to receive disability pension. The total collected was more than \$250,000,000 over eight years.[^cite_note-nyt-183] As a result, Railroad Retirement agents from Chicago inspected the [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island) office of the [Railroad Retirement Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_Retirement_Board) on September 23, 2008. New York [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_New_York) [David Paterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Paterson) issued a statement calling for [Congress](31756.md) to conduct a full review of the board's mission and daily activities. Officials at the board's headquarters responded to the investigation stating that all occupational disability annuities were issued in accordance with applicable laws.[^cite_note-nyt-183]

On November 17, 2008, a former LIRR pension manager was arrested and charged with official misconduct for performing outside work without permission. However, these charges were all dismissed for "no merit" by Supreme Court Judge Kase on December 11, 2009, on the grounds that the prosecution had misled the grand jury in the indictment.[^cite_note-184]

A report produced in September 2009 by the [Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) stated that the rate at which retirees were rewarded disability claims was above the norm for the industry in general and indicated "troubling" practices that may indicate fraud, such as the use of a very small group of physicians in making diagnoses.[^cite_note-185]

Another series of arrests on October 27, 2011, included two doctors and a former union official.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187]

According to court documents, from 1998 through 2011, 79% of LIRR retirees obtained federal disability when they retired. On August 6, 2013, a doctor and two consultants were found guilty in connection with the accusations and sentenced to prison.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]

### Overtime fraud scandals

In 2018, LIRR foreman Raymond Murphy was discovered at or near his home on 10 occasions whilst claiming overtime pay. Murphy earned \$405,021 in 2017, of which \$295,490 was overtime. According to reports, he was allowed to retire with a full public pension before being reprimanded or punished.[^cite_note-191]

In 2021, LIRR employee and track inspector Thomas Caputo and co-conspirators John Nugent and Joseph Balestra were federally convicted for large-scale overtime fraud.[^cite_note-192] Caputo was paid approximately \$461,000 in 2018, of which \$344,000 was supposed overtime. He claimed to have worked 3,864 overtime hours, an average of more than 10 hours of overtime for all 365 days the year. Phone, bank, email, and other records revealed many of these hours were fraudulent: Caputo was clocked in during vacation and while attending outside social events such as a bowling league.
