***Izvestia*** (Russian: Известия, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Izvestiya*, IPA: [\[ɪzˈvʲesʲtʲɪjə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian), "The News") is a daily [broadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet) newspaper in [Russia](25391.md). Founded in February 1917, *Izvestia*, which covered [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations), was the organ of the [Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_Soviet_Union), disseminating official state propaganda.[^cite_note-2] It is now described as a "national newspaper" of Russia.

The word *[izvestiya](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5#Russian)* in Russian means "bring news" or "tidings", "[herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald)" (an official messenger bringing news), derived from the verb *izveshchat* ("to inform", "to notify").

## History

### 1917–1991



Old *Izvestia* logo. It uses two letters that are no longer used in the Russian language (see [Reforms of Russian orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reforms_of_Russian_orthography)).

During the Soviet period, while *[Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravda)* served as the official mouthpiece of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union), *Izvestia* expressed the official views of the Soviet government as published by the [Presidium of the Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Supreme_Soviet) of the USSR.[^cite_note-3]

**Chronology of names**

- 1917&#x20;

  - February: *Izvestiya of the Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies*

  - March–August: *Izvestiya of the Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies*

  - August–September: *Izvestiya of the Central Executive Committee and the Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies*

  - September–October: *Izvestiya of the Central Executive Committee of the Soviets of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies*

- 1917–1918: *Izvestiya All-Russian Central Executive Committee of the Soviets of Peasants, Workers, Soldiers, and Cossack Deputies and the Petrograd Soviet of Workers and Red Army Deputies*

- 1918: *Bulletin of the All-Russian Central Executive Committee of the Soviets of Peasants, Workers, Soldiers, and Cossack Deputies and the Moscow Soviet of Workers and Red Army Deputies*

- 1918–1923: *Bulletin of the All-Russian Central Executive Committee and the Moscow Soviet of Workers and Soldiers’ Deputies*

- 1923–1938: *Bulletin of the Central Executive Committee of the USSR and the All-Russian Central Executive Committee of the Soviets of Workers, Peasants, Red Army and Cossack Deputies*

- 1938–1977: *Izvestia of the Soviets of Deputies of the Workers of the USSR*

- 1977–1991: *Izvestia of the Soviets of People's Deputies of the USSR*

- since 1991: *Izvestia*

The [Izvestia Trophy ice hockey tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_One_Cup_\(ice_hockey\)) was named after the newspaper between 1969 and 1996.

*Nedelya* was the weekend supplement of Izvestia.[^cite_note-NYT_1983-4][^cite_note-5]

### 1992–present



Izvestiia Newspaper Building in Moscow

Following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), *Izvestia* now describes itself as a "national newspaper" of Russia. The newspaper was owned by a vast holding company of [Vladimir Potanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Potanin) which had close ties with the government. A controlling stake in *Izvestia* was purchased by state-owned [Gazprom](320489.md) on 3 June 2005, and included in the [Gazprom Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazprom_Media) holding. According to the allegations of the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists), Raf Shakirov, editor-in-chief of *Izvestia*, was forced to resign because the government officials did not like the paper's coverage of the [Beslan school hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beslan_school_hostage_crisis).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Other sources informed that Potanin had asked him to leave for fear the Kremlin would be riled by the explicit photographs of the massacre published by *Izvestia*. As of 2005, the circulation of *Izvestia* was 240,967. Its 2007 circulation certified by TNS Gallup Media was 371,000 copies.[^cite_note-bbc8-8] Until his death on 1 October 2008, the chief artist was [Boris Yefimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yefimov), the centenarian illustrator who had worked as [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)'s political cartoonist.

In 2008, Gazprom Media sold *Izvestia* to [National Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Media_Group).[^cite_note-9]

In May 2024, the [European Union](9317.md) accused the newspaper of spreading [propaganda](45060755.md) and placed it on its sanctions list.[^cite_note-10]
