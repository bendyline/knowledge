**Junín** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[xuˈnin\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_language): *Sunin*) is a [department of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Peru). It is located in the central [highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_\(geography\)) and westernmost [Peruvian Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Amazon). It is governed by a [regional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Government_of_Jun%C3%ADn). Its capital is [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo).

The region has a very heterogeneous [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography). The western [range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera) located near the border with the [Lima Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Region), has snowy and ice-covered peaks. On the east, there are high glacier valleys which end up in high [plateaus](538005.md) (*[Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano)*). Among them is the [Junín Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun%C3%ADn_Plateau?action=edit\&redlink=1) that is located between the cities of [La Oroya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Oroya) and [Cerro de Pasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_de_Pasco).

The [Mantaro Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantaro_Valley) becomes wider before Jauja up to the limit with the [Huancavelica Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancavelica_Region). This area concentrates a large share of the region's population. Towards the east, near the [jungle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle), there is an abundance of narrow and deep [canyons](78478.md), with highly inclined hillsides, covered by woods under low-lying clouds.

The [Waytapallana mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waytapallana_mountain_range) is located in the south central area of the region. This range holds a great fault which is the reason earthquakes happen in the area. The upper [jungle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle), with valleys of great length, modelled by the Tulumayu, Pawqartampu, [Perené](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peren%C3%A9_River) and [Ene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ene_River) rivers, is located on the eastern side of the region.

[Lake Junin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Junin), the largest [lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake) entirely within Peru, is located in the region, except for its northernmost tip which belongs to the [Pasco Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasco_Region). Junín Region is also home to [Mount Toromocho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Toromocho).

It is rich in minerals, including silver, copper, mercury, bismuth, molybdenum, lead and coal.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

### Boundaries

The Junín Region borders the regions of [Pasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasco_Region) in the north, [Ucayali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ucayali_Region) in the northeast and [Cusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusco_Region) in the east. The [Mantaro River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantaro_River) marks the border of the region with the [Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayacucho_Region) and [Huancavelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancavelica_Region) regions in the south and in the west it is bordered by the [Lima Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Region).

### Climate

The Junín Region has an average annual temperature of 13.1 °C (56 °F), a maximum high of 17 °C (62 °F) and a minimum low of 0 °C (32 °F).

The [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_season) runs from November to April, and from December to March in [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) areas.

## History

Until the arrival of the [Incas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incas) the eastern plains of the Junín region, known in Quechua as "Pampas," were inhabited by the [Yanesha'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanesha'_people) and the [Asháninka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash%C3%A1ninka) people, who lived a hunter-gatherer lifestyle and tended to be hostile towards outsiders. Meanwhile, the southwestern Mantaro Valley was inhabited by the [Huancas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanca_people). [Sapa Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapa_Inca) [Pachacuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachacuti) conquered this region in 1460, bringing it under the domain of the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire). [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo) became the region's main highway rest stop on the Inca Trail.

Woolen mills (known in Spanish as "obrajes" or "mills") were created during the viceroyalty, where the tissue and its craft became a tradition that continues today. On September 13, 1825, [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) issued a decree creating what is now the Junín Region, to commemorate his victory in the [Battle of Junín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jun%C3%ADn), the last real cavalry charge in South America where no shot was fired. Major events of national importance occurred during this period: Huancayo hosted the Assembly that issued the 1839 Constitution and on December 3, 1854, [Ramón Castilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Castilla) signed a decree that granted freedom to [Afro-Peruvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Peruvian) slaves.
