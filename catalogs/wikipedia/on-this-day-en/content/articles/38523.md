In the [administrative divisions of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_France), the **department** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *département*, pronounced [\[depaʁtəmɑ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/42/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Poslovitch-d%C3%A9partement.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-Poslovitch-d%C3%A9partement.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/42/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Poslovitch-d%C3%A9partement.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-Poslovitch-d%C3%A9partement.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is one of the three levels of government under the national level ("[territorial collectivities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_collectivity)"), between the [administrative regions](58846.md) and the [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France). There are a total of 101 departments, consisting of ninety-six departments in [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France), and five [overseas departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_department_and_region) (which are also classified as overseas regions). Departments are further subdivided into 333 [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France) and 2,054 [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_France) (as of 2023).[^cite_note-1] These last two levels of government have no political autonomy, instead serving as the administrative basis for the local organisation of police, fire departments, and, in certain cases, elections.

Each department is administered by an elected body called a [departmental council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_council_\(France\)) (sing. *conseil départemental*, pl. *conseils départementaux*). From 1800 to April 2015, these were called general councils (sing. *conseil général*, pl. *conseils généraux*).[^cite_note-2] Each council has a president. Their main areas of responsibility include the management of a number of social and welfare allowances, of [junior high school (*collège*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_France#Coll%C3%A8ge) buildings and technical staff, and local roads and school and rural buses, and a contribution to municipal infrastructures.[^cite_note-3] Local services of the state administration are traditionally organised at departmental level, where the [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect_\(France\)) represents the government; however, regions have gained importance since the 2000s, with some department-level services merged into region-level services.

The departments were created in 1790 as a rational replacement of [Ancien Régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime) [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) with a view to strengthen national unity;[^cite_note-4] the title "department" is used to mean a part of a larger whole.[^cite_note-5] Almost all of them were named after physical geographical features (rivers, mountains, or coasts), rather than after historical or cultural territories, which could have their own loyalties, or after their own administrative seats. The division of France into departments was a project particularly identified with the French revolutionary leader the [Abbé Sieyès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abb%C3%A9_Siey%C3%A8s),[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] although it had already been frequently discussed and written about by many politicians and thinkers. The earliest known suggestion of it is from 1665 in the writings of [d'Argenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Louis_de_Voyer_de_Paulmy_d'Argenson).[^cite_note-8] They have inspired similar divisions in many countries, some of them former French colonies. The [1822 territorial division of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1822_territorial_division_of_Spain) (reverted due to the [1823 French intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Thousand_Sons_of_Saint_Louis) ending the [trienio liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trienio_liberal)) and the [1833 territorial division of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1833_territorial_division_of_Spain), which forms the basis of the present day [provinces of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain) with minor modifications, are also based on the French model of departments of roughly equal size.[^cite_note-9]

Most French departments are assigned a two-digit number, the Official Geographical Code, allocated by the *[Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_national_de_la_statistique_et_des_%C3%A9tudes_%C3%A9conomiques_\(France\))* (*Insée*).[^cite_note-10] Overseas departments have a three-digit number. The number is used, for example, in the [postal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_codes_in_France) and was, until the introduction of the [SIV scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_France#SIV) in 2009, part of the [vehicle registration plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_France) number. Residents commonly use the numbers to refer to their own department or a neighbouring one, for example inhabitants of [Loiret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loiret) may refer to their department as "the 45". More distant departments are generally referred to by their names, as few people know the numbers of all the departments.

In 2014, President [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande) proposed abolishing departmental councils by 2020, which would have maintained the departments as administrative divisions, and transferring their powers to other levels of governance.[^cite_note-11] This reform project has since been scrapped.

## History

Main article: [Territorial evolution of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_France)



Geometrical proposition rejected



French [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) before 1790 (color) and today's departments (black borders)

The first French territorial departments were proposed in 1665 by [Marc-René d'Argenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Ren%C3%A9_de_Voyer_de_Paulmy_d'Argenson_\(1623%E2%80%931700\)) to serve as administrative areas purely for the *[Ponts et Chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_des_ponts_et_chauss%C3%A9es)* (Bridges and Highways) infrastructure administration.[^cite_note-12]

Before the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), France gained territory gradually through the annexation of a mosaic of independent entities. By the end of the [Ancien Régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime) it was organised into [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France). During the Revolution they were dissolved, partly in order to weaken old loyalties. The [National Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Constituent_Assembly_\(France\)) decided to create a more uniform division into departments (*département*) and [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France#History) in late 1789.[^cite_note-Legay-13] The process began on 4 August 1789 with the elimination of provincial privileges, and a 22 December 1789 decree (with [letters patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent) in January 1790) provided for the termination of the provincial governments.[^cite_note-Legay-13]

The modern department system, as all-purpose units of the government, was decreed on 26 February 1790 (with letters patent on 4 March 1790) by the [National Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Constituent_Assembly_\(France\)).[^cite_note-Legay-13] Their boundaries served two purposes:

- Boundaries were chosen to break up France's historical regions in an attempt to erase cultural differences and build a more homogeneous nation.

- Boundaries were set so that every settlement in the country was within a day's ride of the capital of a department. This was a security measure, intended to keep the entire national territory under close control.



Departments at the maximum extent of the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) (1812)

The old nomenclature was carefully avoided in naming the new departments. Most were named after an area's principal river or other physical features. Even Paris was in the department of [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_\(department\)). [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy), during its temporary occupation, became the department of [Mont-Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Blanc_\(department\)).[^cite_note-14] The provinces continued to exist administratively until 21 September 1791.[^cite_note-Legay-13]

The number of departments, initially 83, had been increased to [130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/130_departments_of_the_First_French_Empire) by 1809 with the territorial gains of the Republic and of the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire).[^cite_note-15] Following the defeats of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) in 1814–1815 the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) returned France to its pre-war size and the number of departments was reduced to 86 (three of the original departments having been split). In 1860 France acquired the [County of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Nice) and [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy), which led to the creation of three new departments.[^cite_note-\:0-16] Two were added from the new Savoyard territory, while the department of [Alpes-Maritimes](80989.md) was created from Nice and a portion of the [Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_\(department\)) department.[^cite_note-\:0-16] The 89 departments were given numbers based on the alphabetical order of their names.[^cite_note-17]

The department of [Bas-Rhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-Rhin) and parts of [Meurthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meurthe_\(department\)), [Moselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moselle_\(department\)), [Vosges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vosges_\(department\)) and [Haut-Rhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-Rhin) were ceded to the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1871 following France's defeat in the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War). A small part of Haut-Rhin, however, remained French and became known as the [Territoire de Belfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territoire_de_Belfort); the remaining parts of Meurthe and Moselle were merged into a new [Meurthe-et-Moselle](90583.md) department. When France regained the ceded departments after [World War I](4764461.md), the Territoire de Belfort was not reintegrated into Haut-Rhin. In 1922 it became France's 90th department. Likewise the Lorraine departments were not changed back to their original boundaries, and a new Moselle department was created in the regained territory, with slightly different boundaries from the pre-war department of the same name.

The reorganisation of Île-de-France in 1968 and the division of [Corsica](5714828.md) in 1975 added six more departments, raising the total in Metropolitan France to 96. By 2011, when the [overseas collectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_collectivity) of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) became a department, joining the earlier [overseas departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_department_and_region) of the Republic (all created in 1946) – [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana), [Guadeloupe](12343.md), [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) and [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) – the total number of departments in the French Republic had become 101. In 2015 the [Urban Community of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Community_of_Lyon) was split from [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_\(department\)) to form the [Métropole de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metropolis), a *sui generis* entity, with the powers of both an intercommunality and those of a department on its territory, formally classified as a "territorial collectivity with particular status" ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *collectivité territoriale à statut particulier*) and as such not belonging to any department. As of 2019 [Corse-du-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corse-du-Sud) and [Haute-Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Corse) are still administrative departments, although they no longer have the status of departmental "[territorial collectivities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_collectivity)": region and department functions have been managed by a "[single territorial collectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_territorial_collectivity)" since 2018.

Despite the intention to avoid the old nomenclature, often the names of pre-1790 provinces remained in use. For example, the name of [Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berry,_France), though no longer having an official status, remains in widespread use in daily life.

## General characteristics

### Government and administration



Administrative divisions of France

The departmental seat of government is known as the [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) (*préfecture*) or *chef-lieu de département* and is generally a town of some importance roughly at the geographical centre of the department. This was determined according to the time taken to travel on horseback from the periphery of the department. The goal was for the prefecture to be accessible on horseback from any town in the department within 24 hours. The prefecture is not necessarily the largest city in the department: for instance, in [Saône-et-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne-et-Loire) department the capital is [Mâcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A2con), but the largest city is [Chalon-sur-Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalon-sur-Sa%C3%B4ne). Departments may be divided into *[arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France)*. The capital of an arrondissement is called a [subprefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefectures_in_France) (*sous-préfecture*) or *chef-lieu d'arrondissement*.

Each department is administered by a [departmental council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_council_\(France\)) (*conseil départemental*), an assembly elected for six years by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), with the [President of the Departmental Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_departmental_councils_\(France\)) as executive of the department. Before 1982, the chief executive of the department was the [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect_\(France\)) (*préfet*), who represents the [Government of France](3398091.md) in each department and is appointed by the [President of the French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_French_Republic). The prefect is assisted by one or more sub-prefects (*sous-préfet*) based in the subprefectures of the department. Since 1982, the prefect retains only the powers that are not delegated to the department councils. In practice, their role has been largely limited to preventing local policy from conflicting with national policy.

The departments are further divided into [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France), governed by [municipal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality). As of 2025, there were 34,746 communes in France. In the [overseas territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_territories_of_France), some communes play a role at departmental level. [Paris](22989.md), the country's capital city, is a commune as well as a department.



Population density in the departments (2007). The broken lines mark the approximate boundaries of the [empty diagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_diagonal). The solid line is the Le Havre-Marseille line, to the east of which lives 60% of the French population.

In mainland France ([metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France), excluding [Corsica](5714828.md)), the [median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median) land area of a department is 5,965 km<sup>2</sup> (2,303 sq mi), which is two-and-a-half times the median land area of the [ceremonial counties of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) and the [preserved counties of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preserved_counties_of_Wales) and slightly more than three-and-half times the median land area of a [county of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_the_United_States). At the 2001 census, the median population of a department in continental France was 511,000 inhabitants, which is 21 times the median population of a United States county, but less than two-thirds of the median population of a ceremonial county of England and Wales. Most of the departments have an area of between 4,000 and 8,000 km<sup>2</sup> (1500 to 3000 sq. mi), and a population between 320,000 and 1 million. The largest in area is [Gironde](64654.md) (10,000 km<sup>2</sup> (3,900 sq mi)), while the smallest is the city of Paris (105 km<sup>2</sup> (41 sq mi)). The most populous is [Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord_\(French_department\)) (2,550,000) and the least populous is [Lozère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loz%C3%A8re) (74,000).

### Numbering

The departments are numbered: their two-digit numbers appear in [postal codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_code), in [INSEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_national_de_la_statistique_et_des_%C3%A9tudes_%C3%A9conomiques) codes (including "social security numbers") and on [vehicle registration plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_France). Initially the numbers corresponded to the alphabetical order of the names of the departments, but several changed their names and some have been divided, so the correspondence became less exact. Alphanumeric codes 2A and 2B were used for [Corsica](5714828.md) while it was split but it has since reverted to 20. The two-digit code "98" is used by [Monaco](19261.md). Together with the [ISO 3166-1 alpha-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-1_alpha-2) country code FR, the numbers form the [ISO 3166-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-2) country subdivision codes for the metropolitan departments. The overseas departments have three digits.

### Relation to national government

Originally, the relationship between the departments and the central government was left somewhat ambiguous. While citizens in each department elected their own officials, the local governments were subordinated to the central government, becoming instruments of national integration. By 1793, however, the revolutionary government had turned the departments into transmission belts for policies enacted in Paris. With few exceptions, the departments had this role until the early 1960s.

These maps cannot be used as a useful resource of voter preferences, because Departmental Councils are elected on a two-round system, which drastically limits the chances of fringe parties, if they are not supported on one of the two rounds by a moderate party. After the 1992 election, the left had a majority in only 21 of the 100 departments; after the 2011 election, the left dominated 61 of the 100 departments. ## Future

The removal of one or more levels of local government has been discussed for some years; in particular, the option of removing the departmental level. [Frédéric Lefebvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Lefebvre), spokesman for the [UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement), said in December 2008 that the fusion of the departments with the regions was a matter to be dealt with soon. This was soon refuted by [Édouard Balladur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Balladur) and [Gérard Longuet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Longuet), members of the committee for the reform of local authorities, known as the Balladur Committee.[^cite_note-Lexpress-18]

In January 2008, the [Attali Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Attali#%22Attali_Commission%22) recommended that the departmental level of government should be eliminated within ten years.[^cite_note-19]

Nevertheless, the Balladur Committee has not retained this proposition and does not advocate the disappearance of the departments, but simply "favors the voluntary grouping of departments", which it suggests also for the regions, with the aim of reducing the number of regions to 15.[^cite_note-Balladur-20] This committee advocates, on the contrary, the suppression of the cantons.[^cite_note-Balladur-20]

1. Where a Napoleonic department was composed of parts from more than one country, the nation-state containing the prefecture is listed. Please expand this table to list all countries containing significant parts of the department.

2. Territories that were a part of [Austrian Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Netherlands) were also a part of [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire).

3. The [Bishopric of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric_of_Basel) was a German [Prince-Bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishop), not to be confused with the adjacent [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) [Canton of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_\(canton\)).

4. The [Ionian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Islands) were annexed by France after the [Fall of the Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Republic_of_Venice). They were lost to France, becoming the [Septinsular Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septinsular_Republic), a Russo-Ottoman vassal state, from 1800 to 1807, before reverting to France at the [Treaty of Tilsit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tilsit). The [second period of French rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_French_rule_in_the_Ionian_Islands) lasted until 1810/14, after which these territories became a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) protectorate, as the [United States of the Ionian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_the_Ionian_Islands)

5. [Maastricht](20125.md) was a [condominium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(international_law\)) of the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) and the [Prince-Bishopric of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Li%C3%A8ge).

6. On 6 June 1805, as a result of the annexation of the [Ligurian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Republic) (the [puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state) to the [Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa)), [Tanaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanaro_\(department\)) was abolished and its territory divided between the departments of [Marengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marengo_\(department\)), [Montenotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenotte_Department) and [Stura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stura).

7. Before becoming the department of [Apennins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennins), the [Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) was converted to a [puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state), the [Ligurian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Republic).

8. Before becoming the department of [Arno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arno_\(department\)), the [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany) was converted to a [puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state), the [Kingdom of Etruria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Etruria).

9. [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_\(department\)) was known as the *department du Tibre* until 1810.

10. Before becoming the departments of [Bouches-du-Rhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-du-Rhin), [Bouches-de-l'Escaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-de-l'Escaut), [Bouches-de-la-Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-de-la-Meuse), [Bouches-de-l'Yssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-de-l'Yssel), [Ems-Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ems-Occidental), [Frise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frise_\(department\)), [Yssel-Supérieur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yssel-Sup%C3%A9rieur) and [Zuyderzée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuyderz%C3%A9e), these territories of the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) were converted to a [puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state), the [Batavian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Republic) (1795–1806), then those territories that had not already been annexed (all except the first two departments here), along with the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) [County of East Frisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Frisia), were converted to another puppet state, the [Kingdom of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Holland).

11. Before becoming the department of [Simplon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_\(department\)), the *[République des Sept Dizains](198789.md)* was converted to a revolutionary *République du Valais* (16 March 1798) which was swiftly incorporated (1 May 1798) into the [puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic) until 1802 when it became the independent Rhodanic Republic.

12. In the months before [Lippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippe_\(department\)) was formed, the [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements) of [Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rees,_Germany) and [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnster) were part of [Yssel-Supérieur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yssel-Sup%C3%A9rieur), the arrondissement of [Steinfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinfurt) was part of [Bouches-de-l'Yssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-de-l'Yssel) and the arrondissement of [Neuenhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuenhaus) was part of [Ems-Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ems-Occidental).
