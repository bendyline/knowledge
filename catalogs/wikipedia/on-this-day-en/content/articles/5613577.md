The **Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Aerospace Force**, officially known as the **Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Air and Space Force**[^cite_note-2] (**IRGCASF**; [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): نیروی هوافضای سپاه پاسداران انقلاب اسلامی, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *niru-ye havâfazây-e sepâh-e pâsdârân-e enghelâb-e eslâmi*, acronymed in Persian as **NEHSA**), is the [strategic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_ballistic_missiles_program), [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force), and [space force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_force) of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC). It was renamed from the IRGC Air Force to the IRGC Aerospace Force in 2009.[^cite_note-3]

Besides operating [military aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft) alongside its counterparts in the [Islamic Republic of Iran Air Force](412792.md), the IRGC Aerospace Force is also responsible for [space warfare](956982.md) through its [military satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_satellite) program.[^cite_note-sl-4] Most importantly, it oversees the [ballistic missile program of Iran](79315120.md), which includes the deployment and operation of an arsenal of [intermediate-range](337326.md), and [medium-range ballistic missiles](478272.md) with ranges over 2,000 kilometers, considered one of the cornerstones of the Islamic Republic's [strategic deterrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_deterrence) and defense doctrine.[^cite_note-me-5]

Most American public sources disagree and argue on which aircraft are operated by the AFAGIR. The [Washington Institute for Near East Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Institute_for_Near_East_Policy) said in 2005 that "\[t]he backbone of the IRGCAF consists of ten Su-25 Frogfoot attack aircraft, including seven flown from Iraq to Iran during the 1991 Gulf War, kept airworthy with the help of Georgian technicians, although reports indicate that all of the IRGCAF aircraft have been sold to Iraq in July 2014, to increase the latter's for CAS and COIN for fighting against ISIS capabilities. All of them were replaced by [Su-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-22)", all of them flown from Iraq to Iran during 1991 Gulf War, and around forty [EMB-312 Tucanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_312_Tucano)".[^cite_note-6]

The Washington Institute said that the IRGCAF maintained thirty [Y-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Y-12) and [Dassault Falcon 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_20) light transports, as well as [MFI-17 Mushshak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MFI-17_Mushshak) and Super Mushshak trainers, and locally built Ababil and Mohajer reconnaissance unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs).[^cite_note-7]

The AFAGIR also operates a sizable rotary-wing force consisting of around twenty [Mi-171](1493374.md) helicopters for transport and armed assault roles, and a large transport force out of [Shiraz](39622.md), equipped with around fifteen ex-Iraqi Il-76s, originally operated by the IRIAF, and twelve An-74TK-200 transports.[^cite_note-8] *Scramble* backs up this picture in general, reporting [An-74s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-74), [An-14s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-14), and [Su-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-22) at [Tehran Mehrabad](2025041.md), [Chengdu F-7Ms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) at [Zahedan](1044089.md), while saying that MFI-17s were often reported at Zahedan incorrectly, and Il-76 AEW variants at [Shiraz Shahid Dastghaib International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_International_Airport), while saying that they might be based at Mehrabad.[^cite_note-9]

Other later writings make no mention of [Il-76s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-76). [Anthony Cordesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Cordesman) of the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies), writing in August 2007, said only the AFAGIR "may operate Iran's 10 EMB-312 Tucanos", and that it "seems to operate many of Iran's 45 PC-7 training aircraft" as well as Pakistani-built training aircraft at a school near [Mushshak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushshak), "but this school may be run by the regular air force". He said that reports of the Revolutionary Guards operating F-7s did not seem to be correct.[^cite_note-csis.org-10]

Cordesman noted claims of the AFAGIR building gliders for use in unconventional warfare, saying that they would be unsuitable delivery platforms, but could at least carry a small number of weapons. However the attached reference was a 1996 [Reuters](18998750.md) report, making the sources for such assertions extremely thin.[^cite_note-11] The [IISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IISS) Military Balance 2007 makes no mention of aircraft, referring only to the Shahab 1, 2, and 3 missiles.[^cite_note-12]

In October 2009, it was announced that its name has been changed from IRGC Air Force to IRGC Aerospace Force.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

In February 2014, [Jane's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Information_Group) announced that the Barani missile system had been tested.[^cite_note-binnie0214-16] This system is a laser-guided air-to-surface missile which releases submunitions: "new generation of long-range ballistic missiles carrying multiple re-entry vehicle [MIRV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIRV) payloads".[^cite_note-binnie0214-16] The UN Panel of Experts identified it as a variant of the Shahab (Ghadr 1) and questioned its alleged multiple re-entry vehicle capability, suggesting instead that it carried sub-munitions.[^cite_note-csis-prod.s3.amazonaws.com-17]

The [Bina missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bina_missile), which can be carried aloft and is able to be ground-launched from a rail car, was also revealed at the time.[^cite_note-binnie0214-16]

IRGC Aerospace Force missiles at an exhibition in Tehran, showing the [Shahab-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-1), [Shahab-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-2), [Zolfaghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolfaghar_\(missile\)) and [Dezful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezful_\(missile\)) missiles

The IRGC Aerospace Force is responsible for the operation of Iran's surface-to-surface (SSM) missile systems.[^cite_note-21] In 2006 it was mentioned by [John Negroponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Negroponte) that Iran held the largest inventory of ballistic missiles in the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-me-5] In August 2013, [Ahmad Vahidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Vahidi) former defense minister of Iran said that his country was ranked sixth in the world in missile production.[^cite_note-22] It is claimed to operate several thousand short- and medium-range mobile ballistic missiles, including the Shahab-3/3B with a range of up to 2,100 kilometers, which is the mainstay of Iran's strategic deterrent.&#x20;

This puts even NATO members Turkey, Greece, Bulgaria and Romania within striking range, if fired from Western Iran. If Iran ever produces nuclear weapons, they fall under the direct supervision of the Aerospace Force. Iran says that it has no intention of producing nuclear weapons. However, there is evidence which points otherwise. A stolen Iranian laptop, containing over 1000 pages of calculations, simulations and modifications required to make the Shahab-3 [nose cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nose_cone) capable of carrying a nuclear warhead, was obtained by U.S. intelligence in 2004 and shown to [IAEA](14984.md) officials.[^cite_note-23] Additionally, an interview with Alireza Assar, an Iranian physicist formerly tied with the Iranian ministry of defense, revealed the existence of a military nuclear program whose goal was "to produce enough HEU ([Highly Enriched Uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highly_Enriched_Uranium)) to enable the regime to produce nuclear weapons".[^cite_note-24] Iran's continuous lack of transparency regarding their nuclear program has resulted in much suspicion regarding its intentions.[^cite_note-25]

According to the testimony of John Negroponte in 2006, Iran's ballistic missile development, together with its enhancement of the [navy](2160969.md) is largely for the projection of its military power, with the goal of dominating the [Gulf](24761.md) region and maintaining its ability to deter and retaliate against adversaries, including the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-me-5] Additional reasons for the indigenous development of missiles may include the regime's intent to showcase its technological advancements, to intimidate and deter neighboring countries, and to reduce its reliance on precarious foreign supplies, particularly from [North Korea](21255.md).[^cite_note-tm-26]

The independent production of missiles in Iran can be traced back to at least 1997, when the country obtained missile plans and production components from [Russia](25391.md) and began constructing missile production facilities, including the two tunnels for housing [Scud missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile) at Kuh-e-Padri, located along the Persian Gulf between [Bandar Abbas](464348.md) and [Bushehr](205669.md).[^cite_note-tm-26][^cite_note-27] Additionally, Iran received assistance from [China](5405.md) in the construction of a ballistic missile plant and test range east of [Tehran](57654.md), a production facility near [Semnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semnan,_Iran),[^cite_note-ei-28][^cite_note-tm-26] and the acquisition guidance technology and precision machine tools.[^cite_note-ei-28] Iran's largest missile production facility, the complex near [Isfahan](223149.md), was built with North Korean and potentially Chinese assistance as the main assembly site for [Scud-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud-B) missile kits from [North Korea](106424.md).[^cite_note-ei-28]

In May 2013, Iran's Ministry of Defense and Logistics delivered a massive number of missile TELs to IRGC AF,[^cite_note-29] "Iranian television footage showed at least 26 TELs lined up in two rows for the event, which marked their purported delivery to the Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) Aerospace Force, which operates the country's ballistic missiles", according to the report by [IHS Jane's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHS_Jane's).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Any Iranian long-range [intermediate-range ballistic missile](337326.md) or [intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) would require an extraordinarily effective guidance system and level of reliability to have any real lethality with conventional warheads, even if it could be equipped with a functional GPS guidance platform. It would probably require nuclear warheads in order to compensate for critical problems in [accuracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_error_probable), reliability, and warhead lethality.[^cite_note-csis-prod.s3.amazonaws.com-17]

In June 2020, Iranian admiral [Hossein Khanzadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Khanzadi) said that Iran would start producing indigenous [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic) [cruise missiles](6590.md) equipped with [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) engines soon.

### Short range missiles

#### Solid fuel program

The foundations for this were laid with the [Oghab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghab) and [Shahin-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahin_\(rocket\)) missiles. These would lead the way for a number of other rocket artillery systems including [Fajr-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr-3_\(artillery_rocket\)), [Nazeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazeat) and [Zelzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelzal). The initial effort in this area relied heavily on technical help from the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) in the form of assembly and manufacturing contracts during 1991 and 1992. Iran was quick to surpass the Chinese level of assistance and became self-sufficient.

##### Bina

Main article: [Bina (missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bina_\(missile\))



A [Bina missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bina_\(missile\))

Bina is a [laser guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guided) dual-capability short-range [surface-to-surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-to-surface) and [air-to-surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-surface) [missile](19594.md). It appears to be an [AGM-65 Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick) air-to-ground missile with a semi-active laser (SAL) seeker fitted to its nose.[^cite_note-binnie0214-16] Brigadier General [Hossein Dehqan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Dehqan) said the ballistic missile had radar-evading capabilities. "The new generation of long-range ground-to-ground [ballistic missile](38025.md) with a [fragmentation warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragmentation_warhead) and the laser-guided air-to-surface and surface-to-surface missile dubbed Bina (Insightful) have been successfully test-fired. The Bina missile is capable of striking important targets such as bridges, tanks and enemy command centres with great precision."[^cite_note-45]

#### Liquid fuel program



Great Prophet II&#x20;

After the war, Iran's experience of liquid fuel missiles had purely focused on the [reverse engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) of [Scud-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-17_Elbrus) missiles. However, with the post war reorganisation the focus of the effort quickly changed and focused on assembly and maintenance. A domestic version of the Scud-B, known as [Shahab-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-1), was developed and manufactured. This led to its successor the [Shahab-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-2), a variant of the [Scud-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile#Scud-C) with a range of 500 to 700 km,[^cite_note-46] and finally the [Shahab-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3).

Since the end of the war, Iran has consistently attempted to recruit foreign help, as well as its expatriate population, into its missile program. Iranian expatriates who left with the revolution have been slow to return, but many are now doing so and thus heralding a new age for Iran's missile development programme.[^cite_note-47]

#### Other missile systems

Iran has an arsenal of short-range, liquid-fueled missiles including the [Scud-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-17_Elbrus) and [Scud-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile#Scud-C), and is now able to produce SCUD type missiles on its own, such as the R-17E, a variant of the Russian R-17 Elbrus (Scud-B). The Aerospace Industries Organization, a subsidiary of Iran's Ministry of Defense, supports the manufacturing process by engaging in SCUD missile restoration. Its short-range missile inventory also includes solid-fueled missiles, such as the [Tondar-69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondar-69) and the [Fateh-110](4603470.md).

Also, Iranian artillery rockets include the Samid, the [Shahin-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahin_\(rocket\)), the [Naze'at](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naze'at), and the Zelzal family ([Zelzal-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelzal-1), [Zelzal-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelzal-2), and [Zelzal-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelzal-3)).

### Longer range ballistic missiles (>1,000 km)

As of 2009, Iran has an active interest in developing, acquiring, and deploying a broad range of ballistic missiles, as well as developing a space launch capability. In mid-July 2008, Iran launched a number of ballistic missiles during military exercises, reportedly including the medium-range Shahab-3. Iran announced other missile and space launch tests in August and November 2008. In February 2009, Iran announced it launched a satellite into orbit and "officially achieved a presence in space".[^cite_note-CRS1-48]

#### Fajr-3 MIRV

Main article: [Fajr-3 (missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr-3_\(missile\))

The [Fajr-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr-3_\(missile\)) is currently Iran's most advanced [ballistic missile](38025.md). It is a domestically developed [liquid fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_fuel) missile with an unknown range. The Iranian government says it has [multiple independently targeted reentry vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_independently_targeted_reentry_vehicles) (MIRV) capabilities. Its MIRV capability may give it the ability of avoiding anti-missile [surface-to-air missiles](182664.md) (SAMs). The missile was last launched during Holy Prophet wargames, which was the IRGC's largest naval war games ever. The Fajr-3 and the [Fajr-3 artillery rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr-3_\(artillery_rocket\)) are different systems.

#### Shahab-3

Main article: [Shahab-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3)



[Shahab-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3)

The [Shahab-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3) is a [medium-range ballistic missile](478272.md) (MRBM) that was built by Iran's military. Its first model, also known as [Shahab-3A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3A) has a range of 1,300 km (810 mi). Soon after Iran came with a new model called [Shahab-3B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3B), which has a range of 2,000 km (1,200 mi), and can carry a heavier [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead). Making this missile was a major step in Iran's missile industry, and it opened the way to longer-range missiles. [Shahab-3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3D), which followed the [Shahab-3C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3C), is Iran's latest Shahab model. A 2,000-kilometre (1,200 mi) range including Russia (as far as [Moscow](19004.md)), Ukraine, parts of Hungary, Serbia, Greece, Egypt, Arabia, parts of India and China, as well as countries closer to Iran.

[Jane's Information Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Information_Group) said in 2006 that Iran had six operational Shahab-3 brigades, the first of which was established in July 2003. They said that the six brigades were mainly equipped with standard variants, but with others described as enhanced Shahab-3 variants, with ranges of 1,300, 1,500, and 2,000 km (810, 930, and 1,240 mi), respectively.[^cite_note-49] [Anthony Cordesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Cordesman) at the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies) however said only in August 2007 that 'the air force of the IRGC is believed to operate Iran's three Shahab-3 intermediate-range ballistic missiles units' while noting that their actual operational status remains uncertain.[^cite_note-csis.org-10]

#### Ghadr-110

Main article: [Ghadr-110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadr-110)



[Ghadr-110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadr-110)

The Ghadr-110 is a medium-range ballistic missile designed and developed by Iran. The missile has a range of 1,800[^cite_note-50] to 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi)[^cite_note-fars-51] and as such is the Iranian missile with the longest range.

It is believed to be an improved version of the [Shahab-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab-3), also known as the Ghadr-101. It has a liquid-fuel first stage and a solid-fuel second stage, which allows it to have a range of 2,000 km.[^cite_note-fars-51] It has a higher maneuverability than the Shahab-3 and a setup time of 30 minutes which is shorter than that of the Shahab-3.

#### Ashoura

Main article: [Ashoura missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoura_missile)

In November 2007, Iranian Defence Minister [Mostafa Mohammad-Najjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Mohammad-Najjar) announced that Iran had built a new missile with a range of 2,000 km (1,200 mi), the Ashoura missile. He did not say how the missile differed from the Shahab-3, which has a range of 2,100 km (1,300 mi).

He told the gathering [Basij](2892082.md) militia during the manoeuvers they were holding that same week that the "construction of the Ashoura missile, with the range of 2,000 km (1,200 mi), is among the accomplishments of the Defence Ministry".[^cite_note-52]

According to *[Jane's Defence Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Defence_Weekly)*,[^cite_note-53] the Ashoura represents a major breakthrough in Iranian missile technology. It is the first two-stage [MRBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRBM) using solid-fueled rocket motors instead of the existing liquid-fueled technology used on the [Shahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahab_\(missile\)). This would dramatically reduce the setup and deployment time for the missile and hence, shorten the amount of warning time for the enemy. Jane's noted that while the development parallels Pakistan's [Shaheen-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaheen-II) MRBM there is no evidence to suggest there had been any prior technology exchange or with its other known technology partners such as North Korea or China.

#### Sejjil

Main article: [Sejjil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejjil)



[Sejjil-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejjil) (right) and [Qiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiam_1) (left) missiles

The new two-stage solid-fuel missile has a range of nearly 2,500 km (1,600 mi), it was tested in November 2008. An improved version, the Sejjil-2, was tested in May 2009. Improvements include better navigation system, better targeting system, more payload, longer range, faster lift-off, longer storage time, quicker launch, and lower detection possibilities.

#### Simorgh

US Director of National Intelligence [James Clapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clapper) told the Senate Armed Services Committee in February 2014 that Iran was expected to test "a missile system that could potentially have ICBM-class range", a possible reference to the [Simorgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simorgh_\(rocket\)) satellite launch vehicle (SLV) on which Iran is working.[^cite_note-binnie0214-16]

#### Emad

Main article: [Emad (missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emad_\(missile\))



Emad missile

On October 10, 2015, Iran launched a new missile, the [Emad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emad_\(missile\)). The Emad is capable of delivering a nuclear weapon and has a range of 1,700 km (c. 1,000 miles), enough to reach all of Israel and Saudi Arabia. It is considered to represent a great advance in accuracy, with a guidance and control system in its nose cone that functions during reentry into the atmosphere.[^cite_note-54]

As a consequence of Iran's nuclear deal ([JCPOA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Comprehensive_Plan_of_Action)), on 20 July 2015 the [United Nations Security Council Resolution](31956.md) 2231 was endorsed,[^cite_note-55] replacing the [Resolution 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1929), which "*called upon*" Iran "*not to undertake any activity related to ballistic missiles designed to be capable of delivering nuclear weapons*".[^cite_note-56] It has been argued that the language is not a legal prohibition.[^cite_note-57] The U.S. ambassador to the UN [Samantha Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Power) said that the Emad missile was inherently capable of delivering a nuclear warhead which is therefore a violation. However, [Vitaly Churkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Churkin), Russia's ambassador disputed this interpretation: "a call is different from a ban, so legally you cannot violate a call, you can comply with a call or you can ignore the call, but you cannot violate a call".[^cite_note-58]

Iran's foreign minister, [Javad Zarif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javad_Zarif), responded by saying that since Iran does not possess nuclear weapons nor does it ever intends in having one, it does not design its missiles (Emad) to be capable of carrying something it does not have.[^cite_note-59] Nevertheless, the testing of the Emad missile took place before the adoption of the Resolution 2231. The US, France, Britain, Germany, Sweden, Turkey, and Australia asked the UN Security Council to investigate and take appropriate action.[^cite_note-60]

#### Khorramshahr

Main article: [Khorramshahr (missile)](54338983.md)



[A Khorramshahr](54338983.md) missile

The **Khorramshahr** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): خرمشهر), named after the city of [Khorramshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khorramshahr_\(1980\)) in [Iran](14653.md), is a [medium-range ballistic missile](478272.md) that was tested by Iran in January 2017. With a range between 1,000 and 2,000 km, it can carry a 1,800 kg warhead[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] and is 13 m in length.[^cite_note-68]

#### Hoveyzeh

Main article: [Hoveyzeh (Cruise Missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoveyzeh_\(Cruise_Missile\))



A Hoveyzeh cruise missile

The Hoveyzeh Cruise Missile is an all-weather, [surface-to-surface cruise missile](1483499.md).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] The Hoveyzeh is from the Soumar family of cruise missiles.[^cite_note-rt-71] The missile was unveiled and put on display on February 2, 2019, at an exhibition of defense achievements in Tehran during commemorations of the 40th anniversary of the [1979 Iranian Revolution.](347268.md) The surface-to-surface cruise missile is capable of low altitude flight and has a range of 1,350 km (840 mi),[^cite_note-rt-71] a maximum range has not yet been given.

It has the ability to strike ground targets with high precision and accuracy. Its motor utilizes a [turbojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbojet), it releases low heat signatures and the missile is equipped to deal with the most sophisticated types of electronic warfare.[^cite_note-rt-71][^cite_note-cm-72]

Discussing the capabilities of the missile, the Israeli military intelligence website [DEBKAfile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debkafile) states that there is "no military force in the world has so far found an effective means of intercepting cruise missiles before they strike, unless they are short range". The missile is essentially immune to any sort of radar and missile defense systems.[^cite_note-cm-72]

#### Dezful

Main article: [Dezful (missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezful_\(missile\))

Dezful Missile

Dezful missiles on a truck

**Dezful** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): دزفول) is a [medium range ballistic missile](478272.md) (MRBM) developed by [Iran](14653.md) and unveiled in February 2019 in an underground missile factory.[^cite_note-AP-73] The Iranian armed forces said that the missile has a range of over 1,000 kilometers (620 miles).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-AP-73] It carries a 600 or 700 kg warhead and has a CEP (circular error of probability) of 5 meters. The missile can attain the speed of Mach 7 (8,643 km/h).[^cite_note-75] Brigadier General [Amir Ali Hajizadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Ali_Hajizadeh) said this is an upgrade on the older Zolfaghar model, that had a range of 700 kilometers.[^cite_note-76]

#### Raad-500

National Aerospace Park opening ceremony

**Raad-500 missile** is an [Iranian](14653.md) [SRBM](1320069.md)[^cite_note-77]/[Tactical ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_ballistic_missile)[^cite_note-78] which is equipped with a progressive composite engine that is dubbed as "Zohair (Persian: زهیر)". Raad-500 means "Thunder 500", and it was designed by halving the weight of the previous Iranian missile ([Fateh-110](4603470.md)) whose body was made from metal; whereas the range of this new Iranian ballistic missile increased two hundred kilometers more than Fateh-110),[^cite_note-79] and its final range is 500 kilometers.[^cite_note-80]

#### Qassem Bassir

Main article: [Qassem Bassir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qassem_Bassir)

The Qassem Bassir missile is an [Iranian](14653.md) [medium-range ballistic missile](478272.md) (MRBM) unveiled in May 2025. With a [solid-fueled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_fuel), [two-stage system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stage-to-orbit), it is presented as an improved variant of Iran's [Haj Qassem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haj_Qasem_\(missile\)) series, named after [Quds Force](4028355.md) commander [Qassem Soleimani](16805217.md). Iranian officials state the Qassem Bassir has a range of about 1,200 km, and features enhanced guidance and countermeasure resistance. Iran claims the Qassem Bassir missile can penetrate advanced [missile defenses](1099196.md), and can strike selected targets (e.g. [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway), [aircraft hangars](1011776.md)) with "pinpoint" accuracy without [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS), though western analysts have cast doubt on these claims which are likely tinted with internal [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda).

## Space Command

Main articles: [Qased (rocket)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qased_\(rocket\)), [Qaem 100 (rocket)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaem_100_\(rocket\)), and [Noor (satellite)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_\(satellite\))



The first launch of the [Qased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qased_\(rocket\)) SLV

The IRGC Aerospace Force has been running its own space program, and on 22 April 2020, it made existence of its own 'Space Command' public.[^cite_note-Melman-91][^cite_note-sl-4] On that date it successfully launched its first [military satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_satellite), the [Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_\(satellite\)), into orbit.[^cite_note-92] This was acknowledged by Western experts, and marked joining the club of about a dozen countries to have carried out such a project.[^cite_note-Melman-91]

The [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force)'s [chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Space_Operations), [General](2965165.md) [John W. Raymond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Raymond), said it was unlikely that Iran's Noor satellite provided any information of value, describing it as "a tumbling webcam in space".[^cite_note-93] However, an Israeli security source told [Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz) that the satellite is "indeed an important accomplishment for the Iranian space program in general and its military in particular".[^cite_note-Melman-91] [Uzi Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzi_Rubin) commented that he "wouldn't be surprised" if an Iranian system of operational military space assets was soon operational.[^cite_note-Melman-91]

In July 2020, the Aerospace Force said that it had received detailed images of [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md) in Qatar, where [United States Central Command](338482.md)'s forward headquarters is hosted, captured by the satellite.[^cite_note-94]

In March 2022, a second launch of the [Qased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qased_\(rocket\)) [launch vehicle](1306157.md) from the [Shahroud Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahroud_Space_Center) put the [Noor-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_\(satellite\)) [military reconnaissance satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_satellite) into orbit.[^cite_note-95]

Commander of the Space Command, Brigadier General Ali Jafarabadi, has stated that the reconnaissance satellite is part of a larger project that will include satellites with communication and navigation capabilities, in addition to reconnaissance.[^cite_note-96]

In November 2022, the IRGC conducted a successful suborbital test launch of the newly unveiled [Qaem-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaem_100_\(rocket\)) launch vehicle.[^cite_note-97]

In September 2023, [Noor-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_\(satellite\)), also called Najm, was launched on a Qassed launcher to a 450 kilometer orbit. It weighs 24 kg, with a camera image resolution of 6 to 4.8 meters.[^cite_note-98]

In January 2024, the [Qaem 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaem_100) launch vehicle successfully performed its first orbital launch, placing the [Soraya satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soraya_\(satellite\)) into a 750 km orbit, setting a new altitude record for Iran.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]
