**Hunan**[^cite_note-6] is an inland [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China). Located in the middle reaches of the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) watershed, it borders the [province-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_China) of [Hubei](83319.md) to the north, [Jiangxi](173817.md) to the east, [Guangdong](65109.md) and [Guangxi](47836950.md) to the south, and [Guizhou](173815.md) and [Chongqing](171752.md) to the northwest. Its capital and largest city is [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha), which abuts the Xiang River. [Hengyang](1260021.md), [Zhuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuzhou), and [Yueyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yueyang) are among its most populous urban cities.

With a population of just over 66 million as of 2020 residing in an area of approximately 210,000 km<sup>2</sup> (81,000 sq mi), it is China's [7th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population) province, the third-most populous among landlocked provinces (after [Henan](166408.md) and [Sichuan](65185.md)), the third-most populous in [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) (after Guangdong and Henan), and the second-most populous province in [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China). It is the largest province in South Central China and the fourth-largest landlocked province.

Hunan's [nominal GDP](12594.md#Nominal_GDP_and_adjustments_to_GDP) was US\$790 billion (CN¥5.53 trillion) as of 2025, appearing in the world's top 20 [largest sub-national economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_GDP_over_200_billion_USD), with its [GDP (PPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) being over US\$1.62 trillion.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-\:3-8] Hunan is the [10th-largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) in China, the fourth-largest in South Central China, the third-largest in Central China, and the fourth-largest among landlocked provinces. Its nominal GDP per capita exceeded US\$11,900, making it the [third-richest province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) in South Central China, after Guangdong and Hubei.[^cite_note-\:0-7] As of 2020, Hunan's nominal GDP reached \$605 billion (CN¥4.18 trillion),[^cite_note-\:4-9][^cite_note-\:5-10] exceeding that of Poland, with a GDP of US\$596 billion, and Thailand, with a GDP of US\$501 billion, the [22nd- and 25th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) in the world, respectively.[^cite_note-\:6-11]

The name *Hunan* literally means "south of the lake".[^cite_note-12] The lake in question is [Dongting Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongting_Lake), in the northeast of the province. [Vehicle license plates from Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_China) are marked *Xiāng* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 湘), after the [Xiang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_River), which runs from south to north through Hunan and forms part of the province's largest drainage system. The area of Hunan was under Chinese rule as far back as 350 BC. Hunan was the birthplace of communist revolutionary [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong),[^cite_note-13] who became the [Chairman of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) and the [founding father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_of_the_Nation) of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md). Hunan today is home to some ethnic minorities, including the [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people) and [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), along with the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), who make up a majority of the population. Varieties of Chinese spoken include [Xiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Chinese), [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Chinese), and [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin).

[Wulingyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wulingyuan) was inscribed as a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1992.[^cite_note-14] [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha), the capital, is in the eastern part of the province and is an important commercial, manufacturing, and transportation center.[^cite_note-15] The [busiest airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_China) serve domestic and international flights for Hunan, including [Changsha Huanghua International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha_Huanghua_International_Airport), [Zhangjiajie Hehua International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangjiajie_Hehua_International_Airport), and [Changde Taohuayuan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changde_Taohuayuan_Airport).[^cite_note-\:8-16]

Hunan is the seat of the [Yuelu Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuelu_Academy) (later [Hunan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_University)), one of the four major academies over the last 1,000 years in ancient China.[^cite_note-17] As of 2023, Hunan hosted 137 [institutions of higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Hunan), ranking fifth among all province-level divisions of China,[^cite_note-全国普通高等学校名单_-_中华人民共和国教育部政府门户网站-18] and it houses five [Double First-Class Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_First-Class_Construction) of [Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_University), [Defense Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Defense_Technology), [Central South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_South_University), [Hunan Normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_Normal_University) and [Xiangtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan_University).[^cite_note-19] As of 2024, two major cities in Hunan (Changsha 23rd and [Xiangtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan) 199th) ranked in the world's top [200 cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output) by scientific research outputs.[^cite_note-\:7-20]

## History

Fenghuang, a traditional town of Hunan

[Hunan's primeval forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changjiang_Plain_evergreen_forests) were first occupied by the ancestors of the modern [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people), [Dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_people) and [Yao peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)). The province entered written [Chinese history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China) around 350 BC, when it became part of the [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty). After [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) conquered the Chu in 278 BC, the region came under the control of Qin, and then the [Changsha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha_Kingdom) during the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty). At this time, and for hundreds of years thereafter, the province was a magnet for settlement of [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) from the north, who displaced and assimilated the original indigenous inhabitants, cleared forests and began farming rice in the valleys and plains.[^cite_note-21] The agricultural colonization of the lowlands was carried out in part by the Han people, who managed river dikes to protect farmland from floods.[^cite_note-22] To this day, many of Hunan's small villages are named after Han families who settled there. Migration from the north was especially prevalent during the [Eastern Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Jin_dynasty), [Sixteen Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteen_Kingdoms) and the [Northern and Southern dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_dynasties) periods, when the north was mostly ruled by non-Han ethnic groups ([Five Barbarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Barbarians)) and in perpetual disorder.

During the [Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period), Hunan was home to its own independent regime, [Ma Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)). Han migration from the North continued under subsequent dynasties, with migrants fleeing from the invasions of the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) (Mongols). The [Red Turban Rebellions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Turban_Rebellions) against the Yuan dynasty first rose up in Hunan in the 14th century.

Hunan and [Hubei](83319.md) became a part of the province of [Huguang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguang) until the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). Hunan province was created in 1664 from Huguang and renamed in 1723. Hunan was regarded by early travelers as the "most conservative and virulently hostile to outsiders" yet was struck by the "keenness and stubborn deposition of the people". [Prince Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Gong), the Qing Prince Regent called the people of Hunan "turbulent and pugnacious". During [Griffith John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith_John)'s arrival at Hunan, he was stoned by a mob and as early as the 18th century, the [Jesuits in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits_in_China) regarded Hunan as the "most impenetrable part" of China, a place "where persecution is most to be feared".[^cite_note-23]

Hunan became an important communications center due to its position on the [Yangzi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzi_River). It was an important centre of scholarly activity and [Confucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucian) thought, particularly in the [Yuelu Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuelu_Academy) in [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha). It was also on the Imperial Highway between northern and southern China. The land produced grain so abundantly that it fed many parts of China with its surpluses. The population continued to climb until, by the 19th century, Hunan became overcrowded and prone to peasant uprisings. Some of the uprisings, such as the ten-year [Miao Rebellion of 1795–1806](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_Rebellion_1795-1806), were caused by ethnic tensions. The [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion) began in [Guangxi](47836950.md) Province in 1850, then spread into Hunan and further eastward along the Yangzi River valley. A Hunanese army ([Xiang Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Army)) under [Zeng Guofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeng_Guofan) marched into [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) to put down the uprising in 1864.&#x20;



Invading Japanese soldiers firing across the [Miluo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miluo_River) during the [Battle of Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_\(1939\)) in World War II

In 1920, a famine raged throughout Hunan and killed an estimated 2 million Hunanese civilians.[^cite_note-24] This sparked the [Autumn Harvest Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn_Harvest_Uprising) of 1927. It was led by Hunanese native [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong), and established a short-lived Hunan Soviet in 1927. The Communists maintained a guerrilla army in the mountains along the Hunan-[Jiangxi](173817.md) border until 1934. Under pressure from the Nationalist [Kuomintang](16903.md) (KMT) forces, they began the [Long March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March) to bases in [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) Province. After the Communists departed, the KMT fought the Japanese in the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War). It defended Changsha until it fell in 1944. Japan launched [Operation Ichi-Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ichi-Go), a plan to control the railroad from [Wuchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang,_Hubei) to [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) ([Yuehan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuehan_Railway)). Hunan was relatively unscathed by the civil war that followed the Japanese defeat in 1945. In 1949, the Communists returned as the Nationalists retreated southward.

As Mao's home province, Hunan supported the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) of 1966–1976, but it was slower than most provinces to adopt the reforms [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) implemented in the years after Mao's death in 1976.

In addition to Mao, several other first-generation communist leaders were from Hunan: [Chinese President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_China) [Liu Shaoqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Shaoqi); [CCP Secretaries-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-general_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Ren Bishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren_Bishi) and [Hu Yaobang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Yaobang); Marshals [Peng Dehuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peng_Dehuai), [He Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Long), and [Luo Ronghuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_Ronghuan); [Wang Zhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhen_\(general\)), one of the [Eight Elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Elders); [Xiang Jingyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Jingyu), the first female member of the CCP's central committee; Senior General [Huang Kecheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huang_Kecheng); and veteran diplomat [Lin Boqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Boqu). A more recent leader from Hunan is former [Chinese Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_China) [Zhu Rongji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Rongji).

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Hunan)

Hunan is on the south bank of the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), about halfway along its length, situated between 108° 47'–114° 16' east [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) and 24° 37'–30° 08' north [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude). Hunan covers an area of 211,800 square kilometres (81,800 square miles), making it the [10th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_People's_Republic_of_China_administrative_divisions_by_area) provincial-level division. The east, south, and west sides of the province are surrounded by mountains and hills, such as the [Wuling Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuling_Mountains) to the northwest, the [Xuefeng Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuefeng_Mountains) to the west, the [Nanling Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanling_Mountains) to the south, and the [Luoxiao Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoxiao_Mountains) to the east. Mountains and hills occupy more than 80% of the province, and plains less than 20%. At 2,115.2 meters above sea level, Hunan's highest point is Lingfeng (酃峰).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

The [Xiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_River), the [Zi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zi_River), the [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_River), and the [Lishui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lishui_River) Rivers converge on the Yangtze River at [Lake Dongting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Dongting) in the north of Hunan. The center and northern parts are somewhat low and a U-shaped basin, open in the north and with Lake Dongting as its center. Most of Hunan lies in the basins of four major tributaries of the Yangtze River.

[Lake Dongting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongting_Lake) is the largest lake in the province and the second largest freshwater lake of China.

The [Xiaoxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaoxiang) area and Lake Dongting figure prominently in Chinese poetry and paintings, particularly during the Song dynasty when they were associated with officials who had been unjustly dismissed.[^cite_note-28]

Changsha (which means "long sands") was an active ceramics district during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), its tea bowls, ewers and other products mass-produced and shipped to China's coastal cities for export abroad. An Arab dhow dated to the 830s and today known as the [Belitung Shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belitung_Shipwreck?action=edit\&redlink=1) was discovered off the small island of Belitung, Indonesia with more than 60,000 pieces in its cargo. The salvaged cargo is today housed in Singapore.

Hunan's climate is [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical); under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), it is classified as [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) (Köppen *Cfa*), with short, cool, damp winters, very hot and humid summers, and plenty of rain. January temperatures average 3 to 8 °C (37 to 46 °F), while July temperatures average around 27 to 30 °C (81 to 86 °F). Average annual precipitation is 1,200 to 1,700 millimetres (47 to 67 in). The [Furongian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furongian) Epoch in the [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian) Period of geological time is named for Hunan; Furong ([芙蓉](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%8A%99%E8%93%89)) means "[lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_\(genus\))" in Mandarin and refers to Hunan, which is known as the "lotus state".[^cite_note-29]

## Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Hunan), [List of provincial leaders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_leaders_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), [Government of Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Hunan), and [Hunan Provincial Committee of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_Provincial_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party)



[Young Mao Zedong statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Mao_Zedong_statue) in Changsha

The politics of Hunan is structured in a dual party-government system like all other governing institutions in [mainland China](55079.md).

The [Governor of Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Hunan) is the highest-ranking official in the People's Government of Hunan. However, in the province's dual party-government governing system, the Governor has less power than the Hunan [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) Provincial Committee Secretary, colloquially termed the "Hunan [CCP Party Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party_Committee_Secretary)".

## Economy

Hunan is the [10th-largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) of China, the third largest in the [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China) region after [Henan](166408.md) and [Hubei](83319.md), the fourth largest in the [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) region after [Guangdong](65109.md), Henan and Hubei and the fourth largest among inland provinces after [Sichuan](65185.md), Henan and Hubei.[^cite_note-38] As of 2025, Hunan's [nominal GDP](12594.md#Nominal_GDP_and_adjustments_to_GDP) was US\$790 billion (CN¥5.53 trillion), appearing in the world's top 20 [largest sub-national economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_GDP_over_200_billion_USD), with its [GDP (PPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) being over US\$1.62 trillion.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-\:3-8] Its nominal GDP per capita exceeded ¥84,583 (US\$11,900 or US\$24,804 in PPP), making it the [third-richest province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) in South Central China, after Guangdong and Hubei.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

As of 2020, Hunan's GDP (nominal) was US\$605 billion,[^cite_note-\:4-9][^cite_note-\:5-10] [making it larger than the economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) of Poland (the 22nd largest in the world), Thailand (the 2nd largest in ASEAN), and Nigeria (the largest in Africa).[^cite_note-\:6-11]

As of the mid-19th century, Hunan exported [rhubarb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhubarb), musk, honey, tobacco, [hemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemp), and birds.[^cite_note-Roberts1-39] The [Lake Dongting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Dongting) area is an important center of [ramie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramie) production, and Hunan is also an important center of tea cultivation. Hunan was a major recipient of China's investment in industrial capacity during the [Third Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Front_\(China\)) campaign.[^cite_note-\:62-40]<sup>: 298 </sup>

In recent years, Hunan has grown to become an important center for steel, machinery and electronics production, especially as China's manufacturing sector moves away from coastal provinces such as [Guangdong](65109.md) and [Zhejiang](96602.md).[^cite_note-41] The [Lengshuijiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lengshuijiang) area is noted for its [stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite) mines, and is one of the major centers of [antimony](898.md) extraction in China.[^cite_note-42]

Hunan is also well known for a few global makers of construction equipment such as [concrete pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_pump), [cranes](318378.md), etc. These companies include [Sany Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sany_Group), [Zoomlion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoomlion) and [Sunward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunward_Intelligent_Equipment?action=edit\&redlink=1). Sany is one of the world's major players. The city of [Liuyang](322068.md) is the world's top center for manufacturing [fireworks](59493.md).[^cite_note-43]

### Economic and technological development zones

- [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha) National Economic and Technical Development Zone

The Changsha National Economic and Technology Development Zone was founded in 1992. It is located east of Changsha. The total planned area is 38.6 km<sup>2</sup> (14.9 sq mi) and the current area is 14 km<sup>2</sup> (5.4 sq mi). Near the zone is National Highways G319 and G107 as well as Jingzhu Highway. Besides that, it is very close to the downtown and the railway station. The distance between the zone and the airport is 8 km (5.0 mi). The major industries in the zone include high-tech industry, biology project technology and new material industry.[^cite_note-46]

- Changsha National New & Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone

- Chenzhou Export Processing Zone

Approved by the [State Council](44498.md), Chenzhou Export processing Zone (CEPZ) was established in 2005 and is the only export processing zone in Hunan province. The scheduled production area of CEPZ covers 3km2. The industrial positioning of CEPZ is to concentrate on developing export-oriented hi-tech industries, including electronic information, precision machinery, and new-type materials. The zone has good infrastructure, and the enterprises inside could enjoy the preferential policies of tax-exemption, tax-guarantee and tax-refunding. By the end of the "[Eleventh Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Five-Year_Plan_\(People's_Republic_of_China\))", the CEPZ achieved a total export and import volume of over US\$1 billion and provided more than 50,000 jobs. It aimed to be one of the first-class export processing zones in China.[^cite_note-47]

- [Zhuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuzhou) National New & Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone

Zhuzhou Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone was founded in 1992. Its total planned area is 35 km<sup>2</sup> (14 sq mi). It is very close to National Highway G320. The major industries in the zone include biotechnology, food processing and heavy industry. In 2007, the park signed a cooperation contract with Beijing Automobile Industry, one of the largest auto makers in China, which will set up a manufacturing base in Zhuzhou HTP.[^cite_note-48]

### Hunanese Uyghurs

Around 5,000 Uyghurs live around [Taoyuan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoyuan_County,_Hunan) and other parts of [Changde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changde).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) and Uyghurs have intermarried in this area.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] In addition to eating pork, the Uygurs of Changde practice other Han Chinese customs, like [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_veneration_in_China) at graves. Some Uyghurs from [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) visit the Hunan Uyghurs out of curiosity or interest.[^cite_note-69] The Uyghurs of Hunan do not speak the [Uyghur language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_language), instead, Mandarin Chinese is spoken as their native language.[^cite_note-70]

## Notable people

See also: [Category\:People from Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:People_from_Hunan)

During the late [late Qing Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), Hunan emerged as the epicenter of revolution and reform in China, giving rise to many of the nation's most renowned scholars, politicians, and military leaders, including the most influential and controversial figure of China in the 20th century, [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong).

Hunanese were pioneers in embracing foreign ideas, as seen in the works of 19th-century thinkers like [Wei Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Yuan) (魏源). As the saying goes among Chinese historians: "Hunanese shaped half of modern Chinese history" (一部中国近代史，半部由湖南写就). Their profound and enduring influence on China's politics and society persisted well into recent times.

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Hunan)

As of 2022, Hunan's culture and related industries achieved an added value of CN¥250.65 billion (US\$37.27 billion), accounting for 5.27% of the province's GDP. Among them, the added value of cultural services was CN¥150.20 billion (US\$22.33 billion), the added value of cultural manufacturing was CN¥78.28 billion (US\$11.64 billion), the added value of cultural wholesale and retail was CN¥22.17 billion (US\$3.30 billion).[^cite_note-culture1-72]

In 2023, there are 655 art groups, 149 mass art galleries and cultural centers, 148 public libraries, 180 museums and memorial halls, 108 radio and television stations, 5.853 million cable TV users, and 27.441 million fiber optic Internet broadband users all connected to cable TV. The comprehensive population coverage rate of radio is 99.43%, and the comprehensive population coverage rate of television is 99.77%. There are 137 national intangible cultural heritage protection lists and 410 provincial [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage) protection lists. 12,078 books, 235 periodicals, and 44 newspapers have been published, with a total print run of 590 million books, 80 million periodicals, and 500 million newspapers.[^cite_note-GDPdata-3]

### Language

Hunan is a region with complex dialects. The dialects in the province include [Xiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Chinese), [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin), [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Chinese), [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese), and some local dialects whose identities have not yet been determined, such as [Xiangnan Tuhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangnan_Tuhua) and [Waxiang dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxiang_Chinese). In areas where ethnic minorities live, many people can speak their own ethnic language and communicate in Chinese.

### Cuisine

[Hunanese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_cuisine) is noted for its near-ubiquitous use of [chili peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper), [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic), and [shallots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shallot). These ingredients give rise to a distinctive dry-and-spicy (干辣; *gānlà*) taste,[^cite_note-Eats-73] with dishes such as smoked cured [ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ham) and stir-fried spicy beef being prime examples of the flavor.[^cite_note-Eats-73]

### Music

[Huaguxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaguxi) is a local form of [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera) that is very popular in Hunan province.

## Tourism

Located in the south central part of the Chinese mainland, Hunan has long been known for its natural environment. It is surrounded by mountains on the east, west, and south, and by the Yangtze River on the north. For thousands of years, the region has been a major center of agriculture, growing rice, tea, and oranges. China's first all glass suspension bridge was also opened in Hunan, in [Shiniuzhai National Geological Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiniuzhai_National_Geological_Park).[^cite_note-74]

- [Wulingyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wulingyuan) is a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) and a [5A Scenic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAAAA_Tourist_Attractions_of_China). Located in south-central Hunan, Wulingyuan is noted for its thousands of [quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzite) [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) pillars, caves, and waterfalls. The area also contains [Zhangjiajie National Forest Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangjiajie_National_Forest_Park).

- [Shaoshan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaoshan), known for being the [birthplace of Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong's_Former_Residence)

- [Yueyang Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yueyang_Tower), on the shores of [Lake Dongting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongting_Lake), was built in the [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) and [Jin dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(265%E2%80%93420\)), and has existed in its current state since the [Qing Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). Alongside the [Pavilion of Prince Teng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavilion_of_Prince_Teng) and [Yellow Crane Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Crane_Tower), it is one of the Three Great Towers of [Jiangnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnan).

- [Mount Heng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Heng_\(Hunan\)), in [Hengyang](1260021.md), is one of the [Five Great Mountains of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Mountains_of_China), and is home to the [largest temple in southern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Temple_of_Mount_Heng).

- [Fenghuang County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenghuang_County), in [Xiangxi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangxi_Tujia_and_Miao_Autonomous_Prefecture), has been placed on the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage) Tentative List for its ancient town. Fenghuang is known for its incorporation of mountain features and water flow into city design, and the ancient syncretism between the local [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) and [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people) cultures.

## Education and research

See also: [List of universities and colleges in Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Hunan)

As of 2023, Hunan hosts 137 institutions of higher education, ranking fifth together with [Sichuan](65185.md) (137) among all Chinese provinces after [Jiangsu](166412.md) (168), [Henan](166408.md) (168), [Guangdong](65109.md) (162), and [Shandong](105032.md) (156).[^cite_note-全国普通高等学校名单_-_中华人民共和国教育部政府门户网站-18][^cite_note-76] Hunan is also the seat of 12 adult higher education institutions.[^cite_note-\:1-77] Two major cities in Hunan (Changsha and [Xiangtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan)) were ranked in the top 200 [cities in the world by scientific research output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output), as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index) in 2024.[^cite_note-\:7-20] There are three [national key universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Key_Universities) under [Project 985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_985) ([Hunan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_University), [Central South University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_South_University) and the [National University of Defense Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Defense_Technology)) in Hunan, the third highest after [Beijing](18603746.md) and [Shanghai](27643.md). [Hunan Normal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_Normal_University) in Changsha is the key construction university of the national [211 Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_211), and [Xiangtan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan_University) in [Xiangtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan) is a key university jointly built by Hunan Province and the Ministry of Education and a member of national Project 111. These five national key universities are included in the [Double First-Class Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_First-Class_Construction) of Hunan Province. As of 2024, they are ranked among the top 500 globally by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index). Among them, Hunan University and Central South University are in the top 50.[^cite_note-78]

Hunan University and Central South University are the only two [Project 985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_985) universities in Changsha, Hunan to appear in the world's top 200 of the *[Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities) and the [U.S. News & World Report Best Global University Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report_Best_Global_University_Ranking)*.[^cite_note-\:2-79][^cite_note-shanghairanking.com-80] Hunan Normal University, the National University of Defense and Technology and [Changsha University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha_University_of_Science_and_Technology) located in Changsha, were ranked in the world's top 701 of the *[Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities)*.[^cite_note-shanghairanking.com-80]

[Hunan Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_Agricultural_University) in Changsha, the [University of South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_China) in [Hengyang](1260021.md), [Hunan University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_University_of_Technology) in [Zhuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuzhou) and [Xiangtan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan_University) in [Xiangtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtan) were ranked in the top 801–900 globally by the *[Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities).*[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] [Hunan University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_University_of_Science_and_Technology) in Xiangtan and [Central South University of Forestry and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_South_University_of_Forestry_and_Technology) in Changsha were ranked number 988 and number 1429 respectively in the 2022 Best Global Universities by *the [U.S. News & World Report Best Global University Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report_Best_Global_University_Ranking).[^cite_note-\:2-79][^cite_note-US_News-85]* As of 2023, [Hunan University of Chinese Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_University_of_Chinese_Medicine) in Changsha ranked the best in the [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China) region and 26th nationwide among Chinese Medical Universities.[^cite_note-86]

The [busiest airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_China) serve domestic and international flights for Hunan, including Changsha Huanghua International Airport, Zhangjiajie Hehua International Airport and Changde Taohuayuan Airport.[^cite_note-\:8-16] Notably, as of 2021, Changsha Huanghua International Airport was one of the 50 [busiest airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) in the world,[^cite_note-PANYNJ_2021_report-87] the [12th busiest civil airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_China#2016_preliminary_statistics) in China, the second busiest in [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) after [Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou_Baiyun_International_Airport) and the busiest in [Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China).[^cite_note-\:8-16]
