**Bajaur District** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): باجوړ ولسوالۍ, [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): ضلع باجوڑ), formerly **Bajaur Agency**, is a [district](639402.md) in the [Malakand Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakand_Division) of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) Province, [Pakistan](23235.md). Prior to 2018, Bajaur Agency was the northernmost component of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA), a semi-autonomous region along the Afghanistan–Pakistan border. In May 2018, FATA was merged into the larger Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Province (KPK) in an attempt to bring stability to the region, redesignating Bajaur Agency to Bajaur District.

The district lies on [Pakistan's western border](203570.md), sharing a 52 km border with [Afghanistan](737.md)'s [Kunar Province](2159600.md), and lies 35 mi (56 km) north of the [Torkham border crossing](15844053.md) linking [Jalalabad](16274.md) and [Peshawar](66230.md). 498 square kilometer miles in size, Bajaur occupies a small mountain basin and is into seven [tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) (subdistricts) with its district headquarters in the town of [Khar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khar,_Bajaur), in the district's center.

## Geography



Map of Bajaur District [tehsils](446017.md#Tehsil)

Before the 2018 incorporation of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas' (FATA) tribal agencies into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Province (KPK), Bajaur Agency was both the northernmost and smallest of the seven tribal agencies, bordering the slightly larger [Kurram Agency](5053207.md) to its south.

Bajaur is about 45 miles (72 km) long and 20 miles (32 km) wide. It lies at a high elevation to the east of the [Kunar Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_Valley) of [Afghanistan](737.md) from which it is separated by a continuous line of rugged frontier hills. The old road from Afghanistan's [Kabul](16826.md) to [Pakistan](23235.md) went through Bajaur before a new pass, [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass), was constructed. To the south of Bajaur is the district of [Mohmand](4557955.md). To the east, beyond the [Panjkora River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjkora_River), are the hills of [Swat District](1236283.md). On its east side, there is the district of Malakand, while on its northeast is an intervening watershed between Bajaur and [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir,_Pakistan).

[Nawagai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawagai,_Bajaur) is the chief town of Bajaur; the Khan of Nawagai was previously under the British protection for the purpose of safeguarding of the Chitral road.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoldich1911-5]&#x20;

The drainage of Bajaur flows eastwards, starting from the eastern slopes of the dividing ridge, which overlooks the [Kunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River) and terminating in the [Panjkora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjkora) river, so that the district lies on a slope tilting gradually downwards from the Kunar ridge to the [Panjkora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjkora).

## History

### Ancient period

The area was the site of the ancient [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara) kingdom of [Apraca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apraca) from the 1st century BCE to the 1st century CE, and a stronghold of the [Aspasioi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspasioi), a western branch of the [Ashvakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashvaka) (q.v) of the Sanskrit texts who had earlier offered stubborn resistance to the Macedonian invader [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 326 BCE. The whole region came under [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) control after the conquests of [Kujula Kadphises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kujula_Kadphises) during the first century CE.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

[Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) turned south from Aornus and continued march towards the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), but the greatest surprise during the march came when he neared the town of [Nysa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nysa_\(Alexander\)) (former name of Bajaur). The local people and even the flora seemed strangely out of place in these mountains. The Nysains placed their dead in cedar coffin in the trees - some of which Alexander accidentally set on fire - and made wine from grapes, unlike other tribes in the area.[^cite_note-8] The Acuphis, the chief man of the city, who has been sent to them along with other thirty leaders, begged him not to harm their towns as they were descendants of settlers that the god [Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus) placed their generation before. Their prolific ivy, a plant sacred to [Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus) that nowhere else in the mountain, was proof they were the people blessed by god. Then they were only commanded to give him 300 cavalry, after which he restored their freedom and allow them to live under their own laws, having made Acuphis governor of the city. Alexander took his son and grandson as hostages. He sacrificed there to [Bacchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus) under this god's others name of [Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus).[^cite_note-9]

The [Bajaur casket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaur_casket), also called the [Indravarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indravarma) [reliquary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliquary), year 63, or sometimes referred to as the Avaca inscription, is an ancient reliquary from the area of Bajaur in ancient [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara), in the present-day Federally Administered Tribal Areas of Pakistan. It is dated to around 5-6 CE. It proves the involvement of the [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) kings of the [Apraca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apracharajas), in particular King Indravarman, in Buddhism. The casket is made of schist.

### Mughal period

Main article: [Mughal-Afghan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal-Afghan_Wars)

#### Bajaur massacre

See also: [Bajaur massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaur_massacre)

In 1518, Babur had invested and conquered the fortress of Bajaur, The Gabar-Kot from Sultan Mir Haider Ali Gabari the Jahangirian Sultan and gone on to conquer [Bhera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhera) on the river [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum), a little beyond the salt ranges. [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) claimed these areas as his own, because they had been part of [Taimur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taimur)'s empire. Hence, "picturing as our own the countries once occupied by the [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people)",[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChandra22-10] he ordered that "there was to be no overrunning or plundering \[of the countryside]".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChandra22-10] It may be noted that this applied to areas which did not offer resistance, because earlier, at Bajaur, where the [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) tribesmen had resisted, he had ordered a general massacre, with their women and children being made captive.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChandra22-10]

Babur justifies this massacre by saying, "the Bajauris were rebels and at enmity with the people of [Islam](6037917.md), and as, by heathenish and hostile customs prevailing in their midst, the very name of Islam was rooted out...".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChandra23-11]

As the Bajauris were rebels and inimical to the people of Islam, the men were subjected to a general massacre and their wives and children were made captive. At a guess, more than 3,000 men met their death. We entered the fort and inspected it. On the walls, in houses, streets and alleys, the dead lay, in what numbers! Those walking around had to jump over the corpses.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBabur207-12][^cite_note-Holdich-1911-Gazetteers-13]

#### Battle of Malandari Pass

Main article: [Battle of Malandari Pass (1586)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Malandari_Pass_\(1586\))

From late 1585 into 1586, forces of the Mughal army led by [Zain Khan Koka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zain_Khan_Koka), at the direction of the Mughal emperor [Akbar the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar), waged a military campaign to subdue the Yusufzai tribes of Bajaur and Swat.[^cite_note-14] The Mughal operation, which culminated in the Battle of the Malandari Pass resulted in an Afghan victory and a military embarrassment for Akbar.

### Later period

Jandol, one of the northern valleys of Bajaur, has ceased to be of political importance since the 19th century, when a previous chief, Umra Khan, failed to appropriate himself Bajaur, Dir and a great part of the Kunar valley. It was the active hostility between the Amir of Kabul (who claimed sovereignty of the same districts) and Umra Khan that led, firstly to the demarcation agreement of 1893 which fixed the boundary of Afghanistan in Kunar; and, secondly, to the invasion of Chitral by Umra Khan (who was no party to the boundary settlement), and the siege of the Chitral fort in 1895.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoldich1911-5]

Bajaur was a princely state run by the Nawab of Khar. The last and most prominent Nawab was [Abdul Subhan Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Subhan_Khan), who ruled until 1990.[^cite_note-15]

During the [Soviet invasion in the 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the area was a critical staging ground for Afghan and local [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) to organise and conduct raids. It had hosted a large population of [Afghan refugees](3657017.md) sympathetic to [Gulbuddin Hekmatyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbuddin_Hekmatyar), a Mujahideen leader ideologically close to the Arab militants. During the [War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md), the United States believed that militants based in Bajaur, frequently launched attacks on American, [NATO](21133.md) (including the [ISAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISAF) and [RSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission)) and Afghan troops in Afghanistan.

## Counterterrorism

### Airstrikes

Main articles: [Damadola airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damadola_airstrike) and [Chenagai airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenagai_airstrike)

An aerial attack, executed by the United States targeting [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri), took place in a village in Bajaur Agency on January 13, 2006, killing 18 people.[^cite_note-16] Al-Zawahiri was not found among the dead and the incident led to severe outrage in the area.

On October 30, 2006, 80 people were killed in Bajaur when Pakistani forces attacked a religious school they said was being used as a militant training camp.[^cite_note-17] There are many unconfirmed reports that the October attack was also carried out by the [United States](3434750.md) or [NATO](21133.md) forces, but was claimed by Islamabad over fears of widespread protest similar to those after the US bombing in January 2006.[^cite_note-18] Maulana Liaqat, the head of the seminary, was killed in the attack. Liaqat was a senior leader of the pro-[Taliban](30635.md) movement [Tanzim Nifaz Shariat Mohammadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzim_Nifaz_Shariat_Mohammadi) (TNSM), that spearheaded a violent Islamic movement in Bajaur and the neighbouring Malakand areas in 1994. The TNSM had led some 5,000 men from the Pakistani areas of [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir,_Pakistan), [Swat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swat,_Pakistan) and Bajaur across the [Mamund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamund_Tehsil) border into Afghanistan in October 2001, to fight US-led troops. In what is thought to be a reprisal for the October strike in Bajaur, in November, a suicide bomber killed dozens in an attack on an army training school in Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa.[^cite_note-19]

That was the reason that this area was affected mostly. A military offensive by the military of Pakistan (FC and Leaves) was launched in early 8 August 2008 to retake the border crossing near the town of Loi-Sum, 12 km from [khar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khar_Bajaur_Subdivision)[^cite_note-20] from militants loyal to [Tehrik-e-Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-e-Taliban), the so-called Pakistani Taliban.[^cite_note-wsws-21] In the two weeks following [the initial battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bajaur), government forces pulled back to [Khar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khar_Bajaur_Subdivision) and initiated aerial bombing and artillery barrages on presumed militant positions, which reportedly has all but depopulated Bajaur and parts of neighbouring [Mohmand Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohmand_Agency), with an estimated 300,000 fleeing their homes.[^cite_note-wsws-21] The estimate of casualties ran into the hundreds.[^cite_note-wsws-21] The offensive was launched in the wake of Prime Minister [Yousuf Raza Gilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousuf_Raza_Gilani)'s visit to Washington in late July, and is believed by some to be in response to U.S. demands that Pakistan prevent the FATA being used as a safe haven by insurgents fighting American and NATO troops in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-wsws-21] However, the offensive was decided by the military, not the civilian government.[^cite_note-22] The bloody [bombing of Pakistan Ordnance Factories in Wah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Wah_bombing) on August 21, 2008, came according to [Maulvi Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulvi_Omar), a spokesman for the [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md), as a response to the Bajaur offensive.[^cite_note-reuters-23][^cite_note-CNN-24] after a few weeks, the Pak army came to battlefield. In an initial way toward the Loi sum Taliban did not resist and let them to come to middle position, when they reach to Rashakai, (3–4 km from Loi sum) Taliban started to attack them but the Army was far stronger than their expectation. For several weeks they stayed in Rashakai, then 1st attempt Army come to loi sum, stay for whole day and come back to Rashakai, In 2nd attempt was the same, and 3rd attempt they come to loi sum and took the control of the area. Army continues there journey, control the main road of Bajaur from Khar to Nawagi, and the peripheral areas were still in the hold of Taliban. After nine months of vigorous clashes between government security forces and Taliban, military forces have finally claimed to have forced militants out of Bajaur Agency, and advanced towards strongholds of Taliban in the region. According to figures provided by the Government of Pakistan, 1,600 militants were killed and more than 2,000 injured, while some 150 civilians also died and about 2,000 were injured in the fighting. The military operation forced more than 300,000 people to flee their homes and take shelter in IDP camps in settled districts of the province. To date, more than 180,000 IDPs have returned to their homes in Bajaur Agency, facing widespread destruction to their lives, livelihoods and massive unemployment. In August, 2012, the Pakistani Army de-listed Bajaur as conflict zone.[^cite_note-25]

### Media coverage

From 2008 through 2010, [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) produced multiple features of the ongoing conflict between Pakistani military forces and Taliban militants in the agency.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In early 2013, [VICE News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_News) founder [Shane Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Smith_\(journalist\)) accompanied and documented a raid on suspected Taliban fighters by the Pakistani [Frontier Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Corps)' [Bajaur Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaur_Scouts) in Bajaur Agency.[^cite_note-29]

### Islamic State

As of March 2024, the [Islamic State's Khorasan Province](48029263.md) (ISIS-K) maintains an operational presence in Bajaur, conducting 4 attacks in 2021, 21 attacks in 2022, and 18 in 2023. The majority of ISIS-K attacks in Bajaur occur in Mamond tehsil, followed by Inayat Kali, Salarzo, and Khar tehsils.[^cite_note-30] On 29 July 2025, Pakistan security forces had launched a [military operation in Bajaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sarbakaf) against the [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) and [Islamic State](9087364.md)'s [Khorasan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan_Province_\(Militant_Group\)).

### Language

The [mother tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of the majority of Bajauris are expectedly 99.85% [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto), reflective of the indigenous Pashtun (also 'Pakthtun') population that inhabits much of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KPK, for which the province derives its name) and eastern Afghanistan. Other residents are [first-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) speakers, the national language of Pakistan, while relatively small numbers are native [Balochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_language), [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language), [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_language), [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language), [Brahvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahui_language) (Brahui), and [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) speakers.[^cite_note-35]

## Governance and politics

Constituents of Bajaur District are politically represented locally through elected union councils, town governments, and tehsil governments. The district government includes a deputy commissioner, additional deputy commissioner, two assistant commissioners, tehsildars (heads of tehsil), district agricultural officer, district educational officer, medical superintendent, district coordination officer, assistant director for local government, and district population welfare officer.[^cite_note-37]

### Provincial Assembly Members

At the provincial level, constituents are represented by the [Provincial Assembly of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa), an elected unicameral legislature of 145 seats in the provincial capital of Peshawar, with 115 general seats, 26 reserved for women, and 4 reserved for non-Muslims.

#### 12th Provincial Assembly

The 8 February [2024 Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa_provincial_election), based on the results of a [2023 digital census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), granted Bajaur District a fourth seat in the Provincial Assembly.

## Tourism

Bajaur is located near [swat](1236283.md) and District [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir,_Pakistan), so the climates of these districts are comparatively same.

### Koh-i-Mor

Koh-i-Mor is the highest peak in Bajaur. It is also called three peak mountain. Its top is covered with snow in winter and clouds are touching its peak. Its history is found 2000 year back, here at the foot of the Koh-i-Mor mountain, that [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) founded the ancient city of Nysa and the Nysaean colony, traditionally said to have been founded by [Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus). The Koh-i-Mor has been identified as the Meros of Arrian's history — the three-peaked mountain from which the god issued.

### Chenarran (platane Orientalis)

At the base of Koh-i-Mor a lot of chenar trees along with spring. Locals people are coming here and enjoy the nature, making their own cooking, some have load speakers, music, etc. majority people come along with their families.

### Gabar Chenna

It is situated in Tehsil [Salarzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salarzai_Subdivision), it has snowy water, It was historically spring, when once here was a undefeated king rested.

### Charmang Hill

The Charmang hills in Bajaur are covered with pine trees and also the roads is made up to top of hill. The road goes on top of hill from bottom to top. In winter, the whole mountain is covered with snow for months.

### Raghagan DAM

Raghagan Dam is situated in Tehsil Salarzai. It a tourist spot nowadays. Boats are present here for tourists.

### Agriculture

Bajaur is a semi-independent in agriculture field, The soil is fertile but the no proper irrigation system. Harvest Crops; People grow wheat, maze and rices in some areas. All the crops is mainly dependent on rain. Vegetable and Fruits; The different types of vegetables are growing in Bajaur. Potato, tomato, onion, lady fingers, spinach, and orange parsimon, etc

### Rare earth minerals

[Marbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble) are found in various regions, mainly in Inzari and [Nawagai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawagai,_Bajaur). There are different types of [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble) supper white, Badle etc. In the local areas are marble factories, cut to into different sizes of the base of demand, and supply to all over the country and even abroad main found in the areas of Shaikh kali and Umary. The supply to the factories of marble mainly from the local mountains and they also bring the marble from ambar and Zairat. These different types and variety of marble then supply all over the country.

[Nephrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrite) ([jade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade)) is the precious stone, found in Inzari and some area in Utmankhail tehsil. It exports mainly to [China](5405.md), The [Chinese](372983.md) thought so too, and for thousands of years, nephrite articles had a special value and signature and skilled artisans carved increasingly intricate designs.

### Olives and olive oil

The [Government of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) has started olive production projects in the Bajaur district. Previously, many wild olive trees are present in the area having no such importance. They use agricultural techniques to convert these wild trees into more farmer friendly and productive plants. With new projects of planting olive trees on more than 150000 acres of land, the Bajaur district will be the olive hub of Pakistan.[^cite_note-43]
