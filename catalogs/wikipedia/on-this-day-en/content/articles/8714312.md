**Cheng Li-wun** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 鄭麗文; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Zhèng Lìwén*; born November 12, 1969) is a Taiwanese politician and lawyer. A member of the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (KMT), she has been [the party's chairperson](39105591.md) since November 2025. She is the second woman to hold the position, after [Hung Hsiu-chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hung_Hsiu-chu).

From 1996 to 2000, Cheng represented Taipei in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republic_of_China\)) as a member of the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party) (DPP). She resigned from the party in 2002, joined the Kuomintang in 2005, and was elected to the [Legislative Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Yuan) for the first time in 2008. She was elected chair of the KMT in the [2025 Kuomintang chairmanship election](79722128.md).

## Early life and education

Cheng was born in [Kouhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouhu), [Yunlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunlin_County), on November 12, 1969, to a middle-class family whose [ancestral home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_home_\(Chinese\)) is in [Yunnan](166410.md), China.[^cite_note-1] She was raised in [East District, Tainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_District,_Tainan),[^cite_note-2] and grew up in a [military dependents' village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dependents'_village).[^cite_note-3] Her father, a [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people) soldier in the [Chinese Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Expeditionary_Force), was from [Pu'er City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu'er_City), Yunnan, and her mother was a Taiwanese native of Kouhu.[^cite_note-4] Her maternal grandfather, a close friend of writer [Cheng Feng-hsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_Feng-hsi?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-5] worked as an irrigator. The family lived in a dormitory owned by the [Taiwan Sugar Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Sugar_Corporation).[^cite_note-6]

After graduating from [Taipei Municipal Zhongshan Girls High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Municipal_Zhongshan_Girls_High_School),[^cite_note-7] Cheng attended law school at [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University) (NTU), where she became the president of the university's debate society (台大論壇社), participated in the [Wild Lily student movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Lily_student_movement), and received her [Bachelor of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws) (LL.B.) degree in 1991. As a Wild Lily student activist, she campaigned for the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party) (DPP) and advocated for [Taiwanese independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_independence).[^cite_note-8]

Cheng then studied law in the United States at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) and earned a [Master of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Laws) (LL.M.) in [international law](8195726.md) from its [Beasley School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University_Beasley_School_of_Law) in 1993.[^cite_note-\:0-9] She completed further graduate studies in England, earning a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (M.Sc.) in [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) in 2000 from the [University of Cambridge](25978572.md),[^cite_note-10] where she was also a doctoral [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) candidate in international relations.[^cite_note-\:0-9] As a graduate student at Cambridge, Cheng studied under Dutch sinologist [Hans van de Ven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_van_de_Ven) and intended to begin an academic career as a historian.[^cite_note-11]

## Career

After graduating from law school, Cheng became a member of the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party) (DPP), serving in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republic_of_China\)) as a representative from Taipei from 1996 to 2000 and in the party's youth organization afterwards. In 2002, she publicly made disparaging comments about Acting [Minister of Health and Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Welfare_\(Taiwan\)) [Twu Shiing-jer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twu_Shiing-jer) after he was accused of sexual harassment by a [restaurant owner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restaurateur).[^cite_note-12] The accuser later apologised after realising that he had confused Twu with another man, the Health Ministry's Department of Personnel chief Tu Hau-lin.[^cite_note-13] Shortly after, Cheng was expelled from the party.[^cite_note-14]

Cheng left the Legislative Yuan following her departure from the DPP, but rumors that she now was aligned with the [Pan-Blue Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Blue_Coalition) were confirmed by her appearance at a March 2004 Kuomintang rally protesting the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Taiwanese_presidential_election). Following her failed campaign to be elected as an independent candidate for [Kaohsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaohsiung), she officially joined the Kuomintang (KMT) in 2005. She accompanied Kuomintang chairman [Lien Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lien_Chan)'s [visit to mainland China in that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Pan-Blue_visits_to_mainland_China).[^cite_note-\:3-15] She was quickly promoted within the party, and was elected as a party-list member of the Legislative Yuan in 2008. She was not reelected upon her run for [Taichungs 7th constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taichung_City_Constituency_7) in seat 2012, losing to [Ho Hsin-chun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Hsin-chun). After her defeat, she served as Spokesperson of the [Executive Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Yuan) for two years, being replaced by [Sun Lih-chyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Lih-chyun).[^cite_note-Post-16]

From her departure from the Executive Yuan to September 2015, she hosted a talk show on [TVBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVBS). She served as deputy secretary-general of the KMT between August and November 2018. She returned to the Legislative Yuan in 2020 and served as secretary-general of the KMT caucus in the Yuan in 2021. On October 12, 2021, she had a heated argument with DPP legislator [Su Tseng-chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Tseng-chang) regarding the 2002 "ear-licking" case.[^cite_note-17] She left the Yuan at the conclusion of her term in February 2024. On June 11, 2025, Cheng, [Julian Kuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Kuo), [Chen Pei-jer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Pei-jer), [Holger Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Chen), and others announced the foundation of the "Opposition Alliance", a party intended to "revive the spirit" of the [*tangwai* movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangwai_movement) and combat the DPP's so-called "[Green Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Terror_\(Taiwan\))".[^cite_note-18]

## Chairperson of the Kuomintang

On September 18, 2025, Cheng Li-wun registered to run for the KMT chairmanship at the KMT Central Party Headquarters.[^cite_note-19] Taiwanese security officials said that her campaign was promoted on social media in a coordinated fashion through accounts linked to the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP).[^cite_note-\:3-15] She was elected [Chairperson of the Kuomintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson_of_the_Kuomintang) in [October 2025](79722128.md) and entered office on November 1, 2025.[^cite_note-20] [CCP General Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) sent her a congratulatory message on her victory.[^cite_note-\:3-15] The same month, she attended a [White Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Terror_\(Taiwan\)) memorial event that honored CCP spy [Wu Shi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Shi).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

After President [Lai Ching-te](22609914.md) backed Japan amidst the [2025 China–Japan diplomatic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China%E2%80%93Japan_diplomatic_crisis), Cheng accused Lai of inflaming tensions and said leaders should instead show caution and restraint.[^cite_note-\:02-24] In December 2025, she opposed President Lai's proposal to increase military spending by \$40 billion over the next eight years.[^cite_note-\:3-15] She also said she would prioritize a meeting with Xi in 2026 with no preconditions beyond supporting the [1992 Consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Consensus) and opposing [Taiwan independence](31156.md), while also saying a Lai–Xi meeting would be possible if DPP dropped its pro-independence platform.[^cite_note-25]

### 2026 visit to mainland China

Main article: [Cheng Li-wun's visit to mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_Li-wun's_visit_to_mainland_China)



Cheng Li-wun visited the [Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen_Mausoleum) in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) on 8 April as her part of visit to mainland China

On 30 March 2026, General Secretary Xi Jinping formally invited Cheng to lead a delegation to visit [mainland China](55079.md), which Cheng accepted.[^cite_note-26] In April 2026, Cheng visited [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), [Shanghai](27643.md) and [Beijing](18603746.md). While in Beijing, she [met](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_Li-wun's_visit_to_mainland_China) with General Secretary Xi, marking the first meeting between the leaders of the Kuomintang and the Chinese Communist Party in nearly ten years.[^cite_note-27] Cheng led a 14-member delegation which included [Chiayu Tung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiayu_Tung).[^cite_note-28]

### 2026 visit to the United States

On 1 June 2026, Cheng Li-wun departed Taiwan for a 15-day visit to the United States. The itinerary Included stops in San Francisco, Boston, New York, Washington D.C, and Los Angeles to meet with American officials, think tank experts, academics, and overseas Chinese [hometown association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hometown_association) leaders with [united front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_front_\(China\)) links.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] She did not have an expected meeting with [Donald Trump](4848272.md) or high-level U.S. government officials,[^cite_note-31] and a scheduled meeting with the [United States National Security Council](44026.md) was cancelled.[^cite_note-32] Cheng claimed to have met with [Steve Daines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Daines), but only met with his staff.[^cite_note-33] During the trip, she was noted for perpetuating the [antisemitic trope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitic_trope) of Jewish control of the United States, having stated "If Jewish friends can have this much critical influence, there’s no reason why the wisdom, diligence and morals of we Huaren (華人) would be lesser than theirs".[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

In her younger days, Cheng supported the [Taiwan independence movement](31156.md), and described both the Kuomintang and the Chinese Communist Party as "tyranny". Her political positions switched during her days with the Democratic Progressive Party, when she questioned the DPP's reaction to [two-state theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_state-to-state_relations) and corruption scandals surrounding President [Chen Shui-bian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Shui-bian).[^cite_note-dw-36] She later became a strong opponent of Taiwanese independence, calling it "Taiwan independence [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_\(insult\))".[^cite_note-\:1-37] She said that while President [Lai Ching-te](22609914.md) had not declared formal independence, his statements "conceal a pro-independence historical perspective and ideology", and accused the DPP of "green terror", referencing the [White Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Terror_\(Taiwan\)), and also said the DPP was trying to "[de-Sinicize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-Sinicization) Taiwan".[^cite_note-\:2-38]

Cheng is also viewed as favoring closer relations with [mainland China](55079.md) and supporting the [1992 Consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Consensus).[^cite_note-\:1-37] During her campaign to become Kuomintang chairperson, she said that she wants "all Taiwanese people to be able to proudly and confidently say, 'I am Chinese.'".[^cite_note-39] She also stated that "Taiwan and the mainland should join forces to reach new heights in human civilization".[^cite_note-40] She said that "At least 90% of Taiwan's culture, history, and bloodline are Chinese'", continuing by saying "We speak [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), write [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters), eat [Chinese food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine), and worship [Chinese gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_gods_and_immortals). That's why I say it's a very natural thing to say 'we are Chinese".[^cite_note-\:2-38] Cheng supports easing cross-strait tensions with talks. She stated she is open to meeting Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) if "it could ease tensions and promote peaceful cooperation".[^cite_note-41] She supports resuming the [Cross-Strait Economic, Trade and Culture Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-Strait_Economic,_Trade_and_Culture_Forum) between the Kuomintang and the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md), which stopped in 2016.[^cite_note-\:2-38]

Cheng supports close relations with the United States, but has warned against Taiwan over-relying on the US, saying Taiwan "must not become a sacrifice or [Trump](4848272.md)'s bargaining chip" and that it "must not become another Ukraine". She also asked that "Could it be that the United States is treating Taiwan as a chess piece, a pawn, to strategically provoke the Chinese Communist Party at opportune times?"[^cite_note-\:3-15] She opposes raising Taiwan's defense budget to five per cent of the gross domestic product, calling it "too high and unreasonable for Taiwan",[^cite_note-\:1-37] and said it "cannot truly guarantee the security of the Taiwan Strait".[^cite_note-\:2-38] She opposed opening Taiwan's agricultural market, saying it would "have a very serious, even fatal, impact" on Taiwanese farmers.[^cite_note-\:2-38] In an interview held by [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle), Cheng stated her worry that Taiwan under the [presidency of Lai Ching-te](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Lai_Ching-te) would become like [Ukraine](31750.md) under the [Russian invasion](79400268.md). When the interviewer responded that "the [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_under_Vladimir_Putin) caused the war", she rejected the claim and described [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) as "democratically elected", blaming the invasion on [NATO enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO).[^cite_note-dw-36] Cheng later confirmed the statement and expressed her surprise that Putin was being labeled a dictator.[^cite_note-42] In December 2025, Cheng criticized the government's one-year suspension of [Xiaohongshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaohongshu) in the country due to fraud concerns as "[censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_Taiwan)."[^cite_note-43]

## Personal life

On October 18, 2011, Cheng married Luo Wu-chang.[^cite_note-44]
