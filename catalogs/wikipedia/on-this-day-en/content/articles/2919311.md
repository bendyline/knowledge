**Nuwakot District** ([Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language): [नुवाकोट जिल्ला](https://ne.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%9F%20%E0%A4%9C%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B2%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%BE)[Listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a6/Nuwakot.ogg/Nuwakot.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), a part of [Bagmati Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagmati_Province), is one of the [seventy-seven districts of Nepal](2918695.md). The district, with [Bidur](16194374.md) as its district headquarters, covers an area of 1,121 km<sup>2</sup> (433 sq mi) and had a population of 288,478 in 2001 and 277,471 in 2011.

The district contains places of historical significance such as the town of [Nuwakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuwakot,_Nuwakot), and the village of [Devighat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devighat) (the death place of [Prithvi Narayan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithvi_Narayan_Shah)) located at the confluence of the Tadi and Trishuli Rivers. [Kakani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakani) is popular among Nepalese people as a touristic place and picnic spot.

## Etymology

The name 'Nuwakot' is composed of two words 'nawa' and 'kot'. 'Nawa' means nine in Nepali and 'kot' refers to fortified or sacred hilltop sites. Accordingly, the district is traditionally associated with nine prominent hills on which various deities are believed to reside and protect the region. This has led to [Nuwakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuwakot,_Nuwakot) often being referred to as the "City of Nine Hills". The [Gorkhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) king [Prithivi Narayan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithvi_Narayan_Shah) invaded Nuwakot, which was then under the rule of [Jaya Prakash Malla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaya_Prakash_Malla), and made Nuwakot the capital of his expanding kingdom.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The present-day administrative boundaries of Nuwakot District differ from historical political divisions, as areas west of the [Trishuli River](27394714.md)—now part of present-day [Dhading District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhading_District)—were historically associated with the [Gorkha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_kingdom) rather than Nuwakot.[^cite_note-3]

## Economy

Nuwakot, being a hilly area with very less plains, most of the areas are still undeveloped. In last few years, huge changes are observable. The city areas are provided with schools, colleges, hospitals and the road infrastructure is also developed. Two hydro power stations are currently in operation, and from 2020 the first part of the biggest solar power station ([Nuwakot Solar Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuwakot_Solar_Power_Station)) was consented to the electric grid of Nepal. People are dependent on agriculture, teaching, foreign economy, livestock farming, business, hotels, Agro Tourism, Eco-Tourism and Khadya Bank, etc.

## Additional local insights

Source:[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Nuwakot retains a strong oral history tradition in which local elders pass down accounts of the unification period, including strategic movements and daily life under early Shah rule. These narratives often differ in detail from formal historical records, reflecting localized interpretations of national events.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Several hilltop settlements in Nuwakot still follow traditional settlement planning, where houses are aligned to maximize sunlight exposure and agricultural efficiency. This reflects adaptation to mid-hill geography and seasonal climate patterns.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

The district features a mix of ethnic communities, including Tamang, Brahmin, Chhetri, and Newar populations, contributing to a diverse cultural landscape. Each community maintains distinct rituals, dress styles, and festivals, often blending into shared regional practices.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Agriculture remains the dominant livelihood, with terrace farming widely practiced. Crops such as rice, maize, millet, and mustard are cultivated based on elevation and seasonal rainfall patterns. Livestock rearing is also integrated into household economies.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Traditional building techniques in rural areas still rely on locally sourced materials such as stone, mud mortar, and timber. These methods are adapted for insulation and structural stability in hilly terrain, although many were vulnerable during the 2015 earthquake.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Local markets (*haat bazaars*) play a significant role in the rural economy, operating on weekly cycles where agricultural produce, livestock, and household goods are exchanged. These markets also function as social gathering points.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Nuwakot's landscape includes a network of rivers and streams, such as the Trishuli River, which supports irrigation, fishing, and small-scale hydropower activities in nearby areas.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

Migration trends have influenced the district's demographic patterns, with many youths seeking employment in urban centers or abroad, while older generations maintain agricultural and cultural practices in the villages.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

## Special Economic Zone

Jiling is recognized as an SEZ Area where business flourishes and there is an intent to grow business and economy of Nuwakot. NEPAL KHADYA BANK LTD. has established at Kashitar to serve farmers in Food Security, Food Banking, Grain Storage, Supply and Distribution.
