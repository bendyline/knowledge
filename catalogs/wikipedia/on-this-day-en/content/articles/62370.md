The **Preakness Stakes** is an American [thoroughbred horse race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_horse_race) traditionally held annually on the third Saturday in May at [Pimlico Race Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimlico_Race_Course) in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), [Maryland](18858.md). The Preakness Stakes is a [Grade I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_stakes_race) race run over a distance of 1+3⁄16 miles (9.5 furlongs; 1.9 kilometers) on dirt. [Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_\(horseracing\)) and [geldings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelding) carry 126 pounds (57 kg); [fillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filly_\(horseracing\)) 121 pounds (55 kg).[^cite_note-2] It is the second jewel (or leg)[^cite_note-3] of the [Triple Crown](19116721.md), held three weeks and one day after the [Kentucky Derby](17135.md) and two weeks before the [Belmont Stakes](62398.md).

First run in 1873, the Preakness Stakes was named by Maryland governor [Oden Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oden_Bowie) after [the colt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preakness_\(horse\)) who won the first [Dinner Party Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_Party_Stakes) at Pimlico. Annual "Preakness Weekend" races include both the Saturday Preakness Stakes[^cite_note-4] and a [Grade II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_stakes_race) race on Friday for fillies only named the [Black-Eyed Susan Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-Eyed_Susan_Stakes).[^cite_note-5] Attendance at the Preakness Stakes ranks second in North America among equestrian events, surpassed only by the Kentucky Derby.

The [151st running of the Preakness Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Preakness_Stakes) was held on Saturday, May 16, 2026, with [Napoleon Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Solo_\(horse\)) winning the race. It was moved to [Laurel Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_Park_\(race_track\)) amid Pimlico's reconstruction. The 152nd running will be held on a Sunday — May 23, 2027 — in part to accommodate that year's Black-Eyed Susan Stakes being run on the Saturday instead of the Friday.

## History

Two years before the Kentucky Derby was run for the first time, Pimlico introduced its new [stakes race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stakes_race) for three-year-olds, the Preakness, during its first spring race meet in 1873. Then [Maryland governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maryland) [Oden Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oden_Bowie) named the then mile and one-half (2.41 km) race in honor of the colt [Preakness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preakness_\(horse\)) from [Milton Holbrook Sanford's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_H._Sanford) [Preakness Stud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preakness_Stud) in [Preakness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preakness,_New_Jersey), [Wayne Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](21648.md), who won the Dinner Party Stakes on the day Pimlico opened (October 25, 1870). The New Jersey name was said to have come from the Native American name *Pra-qua-les* ("[Quail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quail) [Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland)") for the area.[^cite_note-6] After Preakness won the Dinner Party Stakes, his jockey, Billy Hayward, untied a silk bag of gold coins that hung from a wire stretched across the track from the judges' stand. This was the supposed way that the "wire" at the finish line was introduced and how the awarding of "purse" money came to be.[^cite_note-7] In reality, the term "purse", meaning prize money, had been in use for well over a century.[^cite_note-turf1-8]



[Sir Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Barton), winner in 1919

The first Preakness, held on May 27, 1873, drew seven starters. John Chamberlain's three-year-old, [Survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_\(horse\)), collected the \$2,050 winning purse by galloping home easily by 10 [lengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_\(horse_racing\)). This was the largest margin of victory until 2004, when [Smarty Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smarty_Jones) won by 11 1/2 lengths.[^cite_note-9]

In 1890, [Morris Park Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park_Racecourse) in [The Bronx, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx,_New_York), hosted the Preakness Stakes. This race was run under [handicap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicap_\(horse_racing\)) conditions, and the age restriction was lifted. The race was won by a five-year-old horse named Montague. After 1890, there was no race run for three years.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-10] For the 15 years from 1894 through 1908, the race was held at [Gravesend Race Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravesend_Race_Track) on [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island), [New York](8210131.md). In 1909 it returned to Pimlico.[^cite_note-11]

Seven editions of the Preakness Stakes have been run under handicap conditions, in which more accomplished or favored horses are assigned to carry heavier weight. It was first run under these conditions in 1890 and again in the years 1910–1915. During these years, the race was known as the Preakness Handicap.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-10]

In March 2009 [Magna Entertainment Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Entertainment_Corp.), which owns Pimlico, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy thus throwing open the possibility the Stakes could move again. On April 13, 2009, the Maryland Legislature approved a plan to buy the Stakes and the Pimlico course if Magna Entertainment could not find a buyer.[^cite_note-12]

Attendance at the Preakness Stakes ranks second in North America and usually surpasses the attendance of all other stakes races including the Belmont Stakes, the [Breeders' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeders'_Cup) and the [Kentucky Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Oaks). The attendance of the Preakness Stakes typically only trails the Kentucky Derby. For more information see [American thoroughbred racing top attended events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_thoroughbred_racing_top_attended_events).

In February 2017, the Maryland Stadium Authority released the first phase of a study saying that Pimlico needed \$250 million in renovations. As of May of that year, no one showed interest in financing the work. [The Stronach Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stronach_Group), owner of Pimlico Race Course and Laurel Park, was only interested in moving the Preakness Stakes to Laurel Park unless someone else financed work on Pimlico.[^cite_note-13]

In October 2019, The Stronach Group reached an [agreement in principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_in_principle) with the city of Baltimore and groups representing Maryland horsemen that would permanently keep the Preakness at Pimlico. As part of the agreement, Pimlico's grandstand would be demolished and replaced with a smaller structure, and temporary seating would be added to handle the attendance during Preakness week.[^cite_note-SUN051019-14][^cite_note-DRF051019-15] The Racing and Community Development Act, approved by the Maryland [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_\(United_States\)) in May 2020, allows the Maryland Stadium Authority to issue \$375 million in bonds for the renovation of both Stronach Group tracks.[^cite_note-16]

The 145th running of the Preakness Stakes was held on Saturday, October 3, 2020, a delay resulting from the [COVID-19](63030231.md) outbreak earlier in the year,[^cite_note-17] and setting the year's contest four weeks after the also-delayed Kentucky Derby. It was held without spectators for health reasons because of the outbreak.[^cite_note-18]

In April 2024, the [Maryland General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_General_Assembly) approved a bill that would consolidate Maryland thoroughbred racing at Pimlico and provide for the reconstruction of the Pimlico facility.[^cite_note-19] As part of the initiative, a state board approved a \$14.3 million contract to begin demolishing and redeveloping the outdated Pimlico Race Course.[^cite_note-20] As a result of the planned reconstruction of the Pimlico grandstand, the Preakness Stakes will be temporarily relocated to Laurel Park in 2026, with a return to Pimlico scheduled for 2027.[^cite_note-bsun1224-21]

In January 2025 management of thoroughbred racing in Maryland changed from the Stronach Group to the newly-formed, state-owned Maryland Thoroughbred Racetrack Operating Authority (MTROA). The Stronach Group retained [intellectual ownership](14724.md) of the Preakness, for which the MTROA pays an annual licensing fee.[^cite_note-bsun1224-21]

In April 2026, [Churchill Downs Incorporated](3648388.md) (whose [Churchill Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Downs) property is home to the [Kentucky Derby](17135.md)) announced that they had acquired the intellectual rights to the Preakness Stakes and [Black-Eyed Susan Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-Eyed_Susan_Stakes) from The Stronach Group for \$85 million.[^cite_note-22] Following the announcement of the deal, the Maryland Jockey Club (now a non-profit entity of the MTROA) announced that they would retain operational control, licensing and [media rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting_rights) to the race.[^cite_note-23] In June 2026 the state of Maryland exercised its [right of first refusal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_first_refusal) under state law, matching Churchill Downs' \$85 million offer and giving the state the right to use the Preakness and Black-Eyed Susan name and branding.[^cite_note-24]

## Evolution of the Triple Crown series

The Preakness is the second leg in American thoroughbred racing's [Triple Crown](19116721.md) series and almost always attracts the Kentucky Derby winner, some of the other horses that ran in the Derby, and often a few horses that did not start in the Derby. The Preakness is 1+3⁄16 miles, or 9+1⁄2 [furlongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furlongs) (1.88 km), compared to the Kentucky Derby, which is 1+1⁄4 miles / 10 furlongs (2 km). It is followed by the third leg, the Belmont Stakes, which is 1+1⁄2 miles / 12 furlongs (2.4 km).

Since 1932, the order of Triple Crown races has the Kentucky Derby first, followed by the Preakness Stakes and then the Belmont Stakes. Prior to 1932, the Preakness was run before the Derby eleven times. On May 12, 1917, and again on May 13, 1922, the Preakness and the Derby were run on the same day.[^cite_note-25]

Until 2027, the Preakness was run on the third Saturday in May, two weeks after the Kentucky Derby, and three weeks before the Belmont Stakes. Consequently, the race was run no earlier than May 15, and no later than May 21. One exception was 2020, as that race was run in early October due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).

On August 5, 2026, officials announced that the date of the Preakness would be moved to the fourth Sunday in May, three weeks after the Kentucky Derby and two weeks before the Belmont Stakes. The date was moved to incentivize trainers who run horses in the Derby to also run their horses in the Preakness, especially after the formation of the [Thoroughbred Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_Championship_Series?action=edit\&redlink=1) which does not include the Preakness on its schedule.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Traditions



The weather vane at Pimlico is painted with the winner's colors (pictured: colors of 2021 winner [Rombauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rombauer_\(horse\))).

Traditionally, just after the horses for the Preakness were called to the post, the audience was invited to sing the third verse of "[Maryland, My Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland,_My_Maryland)", the then-official state song of Maryland. For many years, the [Baltimore Colts' Marching Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore's_Marching_Ravens) would lead the song from the infield;[^cite_note-28] in later years, it was sung by the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) [Glee Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glee_Club).[^cite_note-29] Use of the song was discontinued as of the 2020 edition of the race—the song "which celebrates the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America), is considered by some to be racist."[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] (The song lost its status as state song the following year.[^cite_note-CBS-32])

As soon as the Preakness winner has been declared official, a painter climbs a ladder to the top of a replica of the Old Clubhouse [cupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupola). The colors of the victorious owner's silks are applied on the jockey and horse that are part of the [weather vane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_vane) atop the infield structure. The practice began in 1909 when a horse and rider weather vane sat atop the old Members' Clubhouse, which was constructed when Pimlico opened in 1870. The Victorian building was destroyed by fire in June 1966. A replica of the old building's cupola was built to stand in the Preakness winner's circle in the infield.[^cite_note-33]

A blanket of yellow flowers daubed with black lacquer to recreate the appearance of a black-eyed Susan[^cite_note-34] is placed around the winning horse's neck at this time,[^cite_note-35] and a replica of the [Woodlawn Vase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Vase) is given to the winning horse's owner. Should that horse have also won the [Kentucky Derby](17135.md), speculation and excitement immediately begin to mount as to whether that horse will go on to win the [Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing](19116721.md) at the [Belmont Stakes](62398.md) in June.

## Winning the race

In 1917, the first Woodlawn Vase was awarded to the Preakness winner, who was not allowed to keep it. Eventually, a half-size reproduction of the trophy was given to winners to keep permanently. The original trophy is kept at the [Baltimore Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Museum_of_Art) and brought to the race each year under guard, for the winner's presentation ceremony.[^cite_note-36]



The Winner's Circle and Old Clubhouse lined with Black-Eyed Susans on Preakness Day, May 20, 2023.

In 1940, it was proposed to drape the winning horse in a garland of the Maryland State flower, *[Rudbeckia hirta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudbeckia_hirta)*, commonly called black-eyed Susans. This posed a problem, as the race is run nearly two months before the flowers come into bloom in late June or July. At first, yellow Viking daisies were painted to resemble [black-eyed Susans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudbeckia_hirta). Painted flowers have been discontinued since the first decade of the current millennium and Viking Poms, a member of the [chrysanthemum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysanthemum) family, are now used.[^cite_note-37] Although the Preakness is sometimes referred to as "the race for the black-eyed Susans", no black-eyed Susan is ever used.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

In 1918, 26 horses entered the race, and it was run in two divisions, providing for two winners that year. Currently, the race is limited to 14 horses.

In 1948, the Preakness was televised for the first time by [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS).

The Preakness has been run at seven different distances:

- 1+1⁄2 miles (2.41 km) : 1873–1888, 1890

- 1+1⁄4 miles (2.01 km) : 1889

- 1+1⁄16 miles (1.71 km) : 1894–1900, 1908

- 1 mile 70 yards (1.67 km) : 1901–1907

- 1 mile (1.61 km) : 1909, 1910

- 1+1⁄8 miles (1.81 km) : 1911–1924

- 1+3⁄16 miles (1.91 km) : 1925–present

## Purse money

At its inauguration in 1873, the Preakness carried a value of \$1,000. The first major increase occurred in 1919 when the race had a \$25,000 value. It climbed to \$100,000 in 1946 and in 1959 was raised to \$150,000. Subsequent increases occurred from 1979 to 1989, when the purse rose four times from \$200,000 to \$500,000, before going to \$1 million in 1997.[^cite_note-brisnet.com-40] On December 12, 2013, the Maryland Jockey Club announced for the 2014 running of the Preakness, the purse would be increased from \$1,000,000 to \$1,500,000.[^cite_note-brisnet.com-40]

## InfieldFest

The race has had something of a party atmosphere in the past, especially in the infield, which is general admission.[^cite_note-nyt2009-41] The course had a "[bring your own booze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYOB)" policy until 2009, formerly including kegs of beer but in the 2000s restricted to all the beer cans a person could carry in a cooler.[^cite_note-nyt2009-41] However, despite crowds in excess of 100,000, the BYOB policy was canceled in 2009 after videos of intoxicated people running along the tops of lines of portable toilets while being pelted by beer cans reached a large audience.[^cite_note-nyt2011-42][^cite_note-freak-43]

In 2009, with the alcohol ban, race attendance dropped to 77,850 after topping 100,000 for eight consecutive years. In 2010, and the Maryland Jockey Club responded with a new event called "InfieldFest" with performances by musical acts, the "Mug Club", which included an infield ticket and an unlimited-refill beer mug, and a mascot named "[Kegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kegasus)", a play on [keg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keg) and [pegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus) (though actually a [centaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centaur)).[^cite_note-nyt2011-42] The much-derided Kegasus was retired in 2013.[^cite_note-44] In 2010, ticket sales had recovered to 95,760 and have since stayed high.[^cite_note-freak-43]

Aside from InfieldFest, the race is known for its fancy hats and official [cocktail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocktail), the Black-eyed Susan, made with [vodka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodka), [St-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St-Germain_\(liqueur\)) liqueur and pineapple, lime and orange juices.[^cite_note-45]

## Records

**Speed record:&#x20;**

- 1+3⁄16 miles (1.91 km) – 1\:53 – [Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_\(horse\)) (1973)

[Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_\(horse\)), the 1973 winner (and ultimately Triple Crown winner) was originally credited with a running time of 1\:55. Two *[Daily Racing Form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Racing_Form)* clockers, however, had timed Secretariat's Preakness in 1\:53 3⁄5, which would be a new stakes record. A hearing was held over the time discrepancy, where a video replay showed Secretariat reached the wire faster than Canonero II, the then-current record holder, but instead of giving Secretariat the record, the Maryland Jockey Club decided to split the difference and make its official time that of Pimlico's clocker, who had timed the race in 1\:54 2⁄5. The matter was finally resolved in June 2012, when a meeting of the Maryland Racing Commission unanimously ruled to change Secretariat's final time to 1\:53 based on testimony and analysis of the race replays.[^cite_note-drfpreakness-46] Consequently, Secretariat holds the current official record for all three Triple Crown races.[^cite_note-47]

## Sire lines

Winners of the Preakness Stakes can be connected to each other due to the practice of [arranging horse breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_breeding_theories) based on their previous success. All of the horses can be traced back to the three foundational sires, with [Godolphin Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godolphin_Arabian) the ancestor of 7 winners, [Byerley Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byerley_Turk) the ancestor of 13 winners, and [Darley Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darley_Arabian) the ancestor of 132 winners, including all winners since 1938.

The [Mr Prospector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_Prospector) direct sire line has produced the last 6 Preakness Stakes winners, with 4 of those winners descended through his son [Fappiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fappiano).
