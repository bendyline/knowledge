**Sumy Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Сумська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Sumska oblast*), also known as **Sumshchyna** (Сумщина), is an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) in northeast [Ukraine](31750.md). The oblast was created in its modern-day form, from the merging of [raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion) from [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast), and [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) in 1939 by the [Presidium of the Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Supreme_Soviet) of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). The estimated population is 1,035,772 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-3]&#x20;

The administrative center of the oblast is the city of [Sumy](561837.md). Other important cities within the oblast include [Konotop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konotop), [Okhtyrka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhtyrka), [Romny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romny), and [Shostka](2561374.md). The modern region combines territories of the historical [Severia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severia) (northern part) and [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine) (southern part). On territory of the Sumy Oblast important centers of Ukrainian culture are located, such as the city of [Hlukhiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hlukhiv) which served as a hetman residence during the [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate) as well as the cities of [Okhtyrka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhtyrka) and [Sumy](561837.md) which were regional centers of the [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine).&#x20;

The oblast has a heavy mix of agriculture and industry, with over 600 industrial locations. Among the most notable was the Soviet [film stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_stock) manufacturer [Svema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svema) in [Shostka](2561374.md). Importantly, seven rivers pass through the oblast.&#x20;

## Geography

The Sumy Oblast is situated in the northeastern part of Ukraine. In Ukraine it borders [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast) to its west, [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) to the south, and [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md) to the southeast. Sumy Oblast also borders the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation), including [Bryansk](476473.md) in the north, [Kursk](480916.md) to the northeast, and [Belgorod](478857.md) to the east. The length of the [state border](31351346.md) [with the Russian Federation](36430484.md) is 563.8 km (350.3 mi). There are three railway border crossing (Volfine, [Pushkarne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkarne_railway_station), Zernove) and five highway border crossings (Bachivsk, Katerynivka, Ryzhivka, Yunakivka, Velyka Pysarivka). In regard to border crossings, since 2006 the city of Sumy has [an airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumy_Airport) which has an international checkpoint.&#x20;

On its territory the [Dnieper Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Lowland) transitions east to the [Central Russian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Russian_Upland). The northern part of Sumy Oblast is part of the [Polesie Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesie_Lowland) as its eastern region. The elevation of Sumy Oblast is between 110–240 m above sea level.[^cite_note-invest-6] Its area (23,800 km<sup>2</sup>) constitutes 3.95% of the country.&#x20;

Seven main rivers flow through the oblast, with [Desna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desna_\(river\)) being the largest.[^cite_note-invest-6] All of them are transit because crossing the oblast flowing from the territory of the Russian Federation west towards [Dnieper](380138.md). All rivers in Sumy Oblast are part of the Dnieper River basin.&#x20;

The Sumy Oblast contains 168 objects and territories of natural reserve. The oblast is rich in picturesque banks of numerous rivers, and sources of mineral waters. Major environmental problems are: [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion), [pesticide pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticide_pollution), air and [water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution). The city has a problem of garbage utilization.&#x20;

## History



NBU commemorative coin dedicated to Sumy region

The region was created on the ukase of [Presidium of the Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Supreme_Soviet) of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) on 10 January 1939 as part of the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). The newly created Sumy Oblast included 12 former [raions](6719452.md) of [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), 17 former raions of [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast), and 2 former raions of [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md).&#x20;

During [World War II](32927.md) in 1941–43, it was occupied by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) under administration of the German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht). After the German forces were driven out, the Soviet Union regained control of the region under jurisdiction of the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).&#x20;

In 1965 one of former Chernihiv Oblast raions ([Talalaivka Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talalaivka_Raion)) was returned to Chernihiv Oblast.&#x20;

During the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) the Sumy Oblast was one of the first regions where [Russian and Ukrainian forces clashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ukraine_offensive).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-hromadske.online-9] Parts of the oblast [came under Russian occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Sumy_Oblast) during the invasion. On 4 April 2022 [Governor of Sumy Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Sumy_Oblast) [Dmytro Zhyvytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Zhyvytskyi) stated that Russian troops no longer occupied any towns or villages in Sumy Oblast and had mostly withdrawn, while Ukrainian troops were working to push out the remaining units.[^cite_note-10] On 8 April 2022 Zhyvytskyi stated that all Russian troops had left Sumy Oblast.[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

On 9 June 2024, amid [Russian cross-border operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_northeastern_Ukraine_offensive) in neighboring [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), Russian forces claimed to have attacked and captured the border village of [Ryzhivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryzhivka), though this was denied by Ukrainian authorities.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]&#x20;

Since the collapse of Ukraine's [incursion into Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_campaign) in early 2025, Russian forces have [slowly reoccupied territories](79531455.md) in northeastern Sumy Oblast. Several border settlements were captured, including [Novenske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novenke,_Sumy_Oblast), [Basivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basivka,_Sumy_Oblast), and [Veselivka.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veselivka,_Sumy_Oblast) As of February 2026, combat continues in several settlements near the border, including [Yunakivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunakivka) and [Kindrativka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindrativka,_Sumy_Oblast).[^cite_note-14]&#x20;

## Demographics

According to the [2001 Ukrainian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Ukrainian_census), [ethnic Ukrainians](145144.md) accounted for 88.8% of the population of Sumy Oblast, and [ethnic Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine) for 9.4%.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]&#x20;

### Language

See also: [Languages of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Ukraine)



According to the [2001 Ukrainian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Ukrainian_census), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) was the native language for over 83% of Sumy Oblast's population: it was the dominant language in the absolute majority of the city, town, and village councils of the oblast. [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_Ukraine) was dominant in several differently-sized zones near the border with Russia. Approximately 40% of the predominantly Russian-speaking local councils had a significant Ukrainian-speaking minority, which accounted for over 20% of the local population.

[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) is the only official language on the whole territory of Sumy Oblast.[^cite_note-25]&#x20;

According to a poll conducted by *[Rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_\(sociological_group\))* from 16 November to 10 December 2018 as part of the project «Portraits of Regions», 60% of the residents of Sumy Oblast believed that the Ukrainian language should be the only state language on the entire territory of Ukraine. 22% believed that Ukrainian should be the only state language, while Russian should be the second official language in some regions of the country. 10% believed that Russian should become the second state language of the country. 8% found it difficult to answer.[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

On 26 April 2023, Sumy Oblast Military Administration approved the «Programme for the Development of the Ukrainian Language in All Spheres of Public Life in Sumy Oblast for 2023—2027», the main objective of which is to strengthen the positions of the Ukrainian language in various spheres of public life in the oblast.[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

According to the research of the *Content Analysis Centre*, conducted from 15 August to 15 September 2024, the topic of which was the ratio of Ukrainian and Russian languages in the Ukrainian segment of [social media](5897742.md), 75.0% of posts from Sumy Oblast were written in Ukrainian (72.5% in 2023, 55.9% in 2022, 19.4% in 2020), while 25.0% were written in Russian (27.5% in 2023, 44.1% in 2022, 80.6% in 2020).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]&#x20;

## Economy

### Industry

The main industrial activities of the oblast are: chemical mechanical engineering, pumping and energy mechanical engineering, agricultural machine-construction, instrument-making industry and radio electronics, technical equipment production for processing fields of agro-industrial complexes, mining and iron ore production industry, polygraph industry and medicine production, oil and gas processing, chemical production, film and photo material production *(See: [Svema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svema))*, and chemical fertilizer production. In general, there are 273 large industry enterprises and 327 small industry enterprises.&#x20;

### Agriculture

In 1999, the gross grain yield was about 446,000 tons, sugar beets – 664,000 tons, sunflower seeds – 27,700 tons, potatoes – 343,600 tons. The region also produced 108,700 tons of meat, 517,800 tons of milk and 295,300,000 eggs. At the beginning of 1999, there were 781 registered farms in the oblast.&#x20;

## Nomenclature

Most of Ukraine's oblasts are named after their capital cities, officially referred to as "oblast centers" ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): обласний центр, [translit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian) *oblasnyi tsentr*). The name of each oblast is a [relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_clause) [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective), formed by adding a feminine [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) to the name of respective center city: *Sumy* is the center of the *Sums’ka oblast* (Sumy Oblast). Most oblasts are also sometimes referred to in a feminine noun form, following the convention of traditional regional place names, ending with the suffix "-shchyna", as is the case with the Sumy Oblast, *Sumshchyna*.&#x20;

See also: [Romanization of Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian)
