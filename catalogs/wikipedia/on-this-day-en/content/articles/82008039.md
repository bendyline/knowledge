# 2025 term per curiam opinions of the Supreme Court of the United States



As of April 20, 2026, the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) has handed down seven *[per curiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_curiam)* opinions during its 2025 term. This term began on October 6, 2025, and will end on October 4, 2026.

Because *per curiam* decisions are issued from the Court as an institution, these opinions all lack the attribution of authorship or joining votes to specific justices. All justices on the Court at the time the decision was handed down are assumed to have participated and concurred unless otherwise noted.

## *Clark v. Sweeney*

| Full caption: | *Terence Clark v. Jeremiah Antoine Sweeney&#x20;* |
| --- | --- |
| Citations: | [607](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_607) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) 7 (2025) |
| Laws applied: | [Principle of party presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_party_presentation) |
|  |  |
| Full text of the opinion: | [official slip opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-52_4gd5.pdf)  **·** [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/607/25-52/)  **·** [SCOTUS Blog](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/case-files/clark-v-sweeney/) |

**[607](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_607) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) 7 (2025)&#x20;**\
&#x20;**Decided November 24, 2025.** \
The [Fourth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Circuit) erred in granting habeas relief to Sweeney based on a claim of constitutional error that he had never alleged. Sweeney was convicted of multiple crimes in state court. At trial, a juror unlawfully visited the crime scene. That juror reported the findings to the jury. The judge removed that delinquent juror, but the judge didn't call a mistrial and Sweeney's counsel did not [voir dire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voir_dire) the remaining jurors to assess the effect of the delinquent juror's actions on them. This failure to voir dire formed the basis of a habeas petition in Federal District Court, which denied relief under [Strickland v. Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strickland_v._Washington). Sweeney only raised one issue in federal court, the ineffective assistance of his counsel at trial. Sweeney appealed. The [Fourth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Circuit) granted relief on the basis of multiple errors committed by judge, jury and attorneys. None of those errors was ever raised, and Clark had no opportunity to respond. Therefore, the Supreme Court held that the Fourth Circuit violated the [party presentation principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_presentation_principle).[^cite_note-1]

## *Klein v. Martin*

| Full caption: | *Christopher Klein, Superintendent, Department of Detention Facilities for Anne Arundel County, et al., Petitioners v. Charles Brandon Martin&#x20;* |
| --- | --- |
| Citations: | [607](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_607) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) 213 (2026) |
| Laws applied: | [Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiterrorism_and_Effective_Death_Penalty_Act_of_1996) |
|  |  |
| Full text of the opinion: | [official slip opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-51_4g15.pdf)  **·** [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/607/25-51/) |

**[607](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_607) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) 213 (2026)&#x20;**\
&#x20;**Decided January 26, 2026.** \
The Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit affirmed the award of a new trial under two theories. First, it held that the state court had applied the wrong rule for [*Brady* materiality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_v._Maryland) after a forensic report was not disclosed to the defense before trial. Second, to satisfy the [Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiterrorism_and_Effective_Death_Penalty_Act) (AEDPA), it held that every fairminded jurist would have disagreed with the state court's decision that the forensic report was immaterial to the outcome of the trial.&#x20;

The Supreme Court held that the state court had applied the proper *Brady* test and that the Fourth Circuit had not sufficiently deferred to the state court under AEDPA because there was enough other evidence suggesting that the defendant would have been convicted regardless of the claimed *Brady* material. Therefore, the court granted the state's petition for a writ of *certiorari* and reversed the judgment below.

Justice Jackson would have denied the petition for a writ of certiorari.

The Court determined the parent plaintiffs seeking religious exemptions to California's school district policies requiring schools to keep students' gender transitioning information and knowledge confidential are likely to succeed on their Free Exercise and Due Process claims. "Hamm v. Smith" redirects here. For the 2024 case, see [2024 term per curiam opinions of the Supreme Court of the United States § Hamm v. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_term_per_curiam_opinions_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States#Hamm_v._Smith).

| Full caption: | *John Q. Hamm, Commissioner, [Alabama Department of Corrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Department_of_Corrections) v. Joseph Clifton Smith&#x20;* |
| --- | --- |
| Citations: | [608](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_608) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) \_\_\_ (2026) |
|  |  |
| Full text of the opinion: | [official slip opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-872_ec8f.pdf)  **·** [oral argument](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2025/24-872) |

**[608](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Supreme_Court_cases,_volume_608) [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Reports) \_\_\_ (2026)&#x20;**\
&#x20;**Argued December 10, 2025.**\
**Decided May 21, 2026.** \
Joseph Clifton Smith was convicted in [Alabama](303.md) for capital murder after a 1997 robbery and sentenced to death.[^cite_note-guardian-4] Smith filed a *[habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus_in_the_United_States)* petition challenging the death sentence, claiming to have an [intellectual disability](18567040.md). Smith scored 75, 74, 72, 78, and 74 on five full-scale IQ tests,[^cite_note-5] while a score of 70 or lower is considered as evidence of intellectual disability in Alabama, making one ineligible for execution.[^cite_note-guardian-4] Smith's claim was granted by the [Southern District of Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_District_of_Alabama) which vacated the death sentence. The [Eleventh Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Circuit) affirmed the district's ruling.[^cite_note-6]

The case first reached the Supreme Court in the 2024 term. In a *per curiam* opinion, the court summarily reversed and remanded the case without granting *[certiorari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certiorari)*. Justices Thomas and Gorsuch wrote a dissent to the denial of *certiorari*.

During the 2025 term, the case was appealed again and the court granted *certiorari* while limiting the question to what the cumulative effects of IQ tests should be when evaluating an *Atkins* claim. After argument, the court [dismissed the case as improvidently granted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dismissed_the_case_as_improvidently_granted).

A concurrence to the dismissal order written by Justice Sonia Sotomayor, and joined by Jackson, said the case was not appropriate to use to have the court evaluate the scientific basis of IQ tests. Thomas and Alito wrote separate dissents. Thomas joined Alito's dissent in full, while Roberts and Gorsuch joined Parts I, III, & IV.
