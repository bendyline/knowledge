**Occitania**[^cite_note-3] ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Occitanie* [\[ɔksitani\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0f/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Occitanie.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Occitanie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0f/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Occitanie.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Occitanie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language): *Occitània* [\[utsiˈtanjɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Occitan); [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language): *Occitània* [\[uksiˈtaniə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Catalan)) is the southernmost [administrative region](58846.md) of [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) excluding [Corsica](5714828.md), located in the south of the country, created on 1 January 2016 from the former regions of [Languedoc-Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc-Roussillon) and [Midi-Pyrénées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midi-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es). The [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_d'%C3%89tat_\(France\)) approved Occitania as the new name of the region on 28 September 2016, coming into effect on 30 September 2016.[^cite_note-Decree_confirming_new_region_name-4]

The modern administrative region is named after the larger cultural and historical region of [Occitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitania), which corresponds with the southern third of France. The region of Occitania as it is today covers a territory similar to that ruled by the [Counts of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Toulouse) in the 12th and 13th centuries. The banner of arms of the Counts of Toulouse, known colloquially as the [Occitan cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_cross), is used by the modern region and is also a popular cultural symbol. In 2022, Occitania had a population of 6,080,731.

## Toponymy



The [County of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Toulouse) in 1154 (shown in blue)

Enacted in 2014, the territorial reform of French regions had been subject to debate for many years.[^cite_note-5] The reform law used as the new region's provisional name, the [hyphenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-barrelled_name) names of its predecessors: [Languedoc-Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc-Roussillon) and [Midi-Pyrénées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midi-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es), in alphabetical order. As for most of the merged regions, a permanent name was then proposed by the new [regional council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_council_\(France\)) to replace that provisional name.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-reform_law-8] On 24 June 2016, the Regional Council of Languedoc-Roussillon-Midi-Pyrénées adopted the name Occitania after lengthy public consultation.[^cite_note-regionlrmp1-9] The provisional name of the region was withdrawn on 30 September 2016, when the new name took effect.[^cite_note-Decree_confirming_new_region_name-4]

Occitania, the new name, derives from the historical appellation of the broader region, and refers to the historical use throughout that territory of the [Occitan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) and its various dialects, which are so named for the word *òc*, the Occitan word for *oui* or "yes". The circa 450,000 French Catalans living in the region (or [Catalans of the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Catalonia), as they mostly call themselves) expressed dismay at the regional assembly resolution, regarding the new name as ignoring their presence.[^cite_note-10] On 10 September 2016, some 10,000 people (7,800 according to the police) demonstrated in [Perpignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan), demanding that the merged region name contain the words *Pays catalan* (literal translation: "Catalan country").[^cite_note-11]

## Geography

Occitania is the second-largest region in mainland France, covering an area of 72,724 km<sup>2</sup> (28,079 sq mi); it has a population of 5,845,102 (2017).[^cite_note-Population-12] It has a [Mediterranean](19006.md) coast on the southeast and is neighboured by [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) on the east, [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](45093325.md) on the northeast and [Nouvelle-Aquitaine](45092904.md) on the west and northwest, as well as foreign borders of [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra) ([Canillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canillo), [Encamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encamp), [La Massana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Massana), [Ordino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordino)) and [Spain](26667.md) ([Aragon](39443.md) and [Catalonia](6822.md)) on the south.

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the region was 171.2 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 7.3% of French economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 26,000 euros or 86% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 102% of the [EU](9317.md) average.[^cite_note-14]

## Transport

The region's biggest airports are [Toulouse–Blagnac Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse%E2%80%93Blagnac_Airport) and [Montpellier–Méditerranée Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier%E2%80%93M%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e_Airport). There are some smaller airports such as [Perpignan–Rivesaltes Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Rivesaltes_Airport), [Carcassonne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne_Airport) and [Rodez–Aveyron Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodez%E2%80%93Aveyron_Airport).[^cite_note-15]

## Politics

Following the creation of the region in 2016, [Carole Delga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_Delga), a member of the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)), was elected to the [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_presidents_of_the_regional_councils_of_France_and_the_Corsican_Assembly) of the [Regional Council of Occitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Occitania). The *Hôtel de région*, where the Regional Council of Occitania meets, is situated in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse).

The new administrative region includes provinces and territories of diverse cultural and historical origin: [Languedoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc) (High and Low Languedoc), [Països Catalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa%C3%AFsos_Catalans) ([Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roussillon), [Cerdanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerdanya), [Vallespir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallespir), [Conflent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflent), [Capcir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcir)), the [County of Foix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Foix), and the eastern parts of what was formerly [Gascony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gascony) ([Armagnac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armagnac_\(province\)), [Comminges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comminges), [Couserans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couserans), [Bigorre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Bigorre), [Condomois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condom,_Gers), [Nébouzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9bouzan), [Rivière-Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivi%C3%A8re-Verdun?action=edit\&redlink=1)), and [Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyenne) ([Carcin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercy), [Roergue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouergue)). ### Occitania

Main article: [Occitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitania)

[Occitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitania)[^cite_note-16] (*Occitània*[^cite_note-17] or *Óucitanìo*[^cite_note-18] in [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language)) is a [historical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_region)[^cite_note-TODD-19][^cite_note-ZIM-20][^cite_note-21] of southwestern [Europe](9239.md) in which [Occitan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) was the main [vernacular language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular_language). This territory was already united, in Roman times first as the Diocese of Vienne and then as the Seven Provinces (*[Septem Provinciae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septem_Provinciae)*)[^cite_note-22] and in [Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquitaine) at the beginning of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages)[^cite_note-23] (Aquitanica, Visigoth kingdom of Toulouse),[^cite_note-24] before the Frankish conquest. Occitania is characterized by "the Occitan culture", since the Middle Ages another expression of [Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) culture in [France](5843419.md) and to a lesser extent in [Italy](14532.md), [Spain](26667.md) and [Monaco](19261.md).[^cite_note-25] It is presented and recognized on institutional sites of French communities, such as those of the Lot-et-Garonne County Council[^cite_note-26] and the city of [Agen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agen).[^cite_note-27]

#### Languedoc



The blazon of the counts of Toulouse and Languedoc: the [Occitan cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_cross) gold on gules.

Most of the territory that came to be called [Languedoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc) (the region where *[langue d'oc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language)* is spoken; in [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) *Lengadòc*, pronounced [\[ˌleŋɡɔˈðɔ(k)\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Occitan)) became attached to the [Kingdom of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) in the 13th century, following the [Albigensian Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albigensian_Crusade) (1208–1229). This crusade aimed to put an end to what the Catholic Church considered the [Cathar heresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catharism), and enabled the [Capetian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_dynasty) to extend its influence south of the [Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loire). As part of this process, the former principalities of [Trencavel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trencavel) (the Viscounty of [Albi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albi), [Carcassona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne), [Besièrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9ziers), [Agde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agde) and [Nimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%AEmes)) were integrated into the [Royal French Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_lands_of_France) in 1224. The [Counts of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Toulouse) followed them in 1271. The remaining feudal enclaves were absorbed progressively up to the beginning of the 16th century; the [County of Gévaudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_G%C3%A9vaudan) in 1258, the [County of Melgueil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Melgueil) ([Mauguiò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauguio)) in 1293, the [Lordship of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordship_of_Montpellier) in 1349 and the [Viscounts of Narbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscounts_of_Narbonne) in 1507.

The territory falling within the jurisdiction of the [Estates of Languedoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_of_Languedoc), which convened for the first time in 1346, shrank progressively, becoming known during the [Ancien Régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime) as the [province of Languedoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France).

The year 1359 marked a turning point in the history of the province.[^cite_note-28] The three [bailiwicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailiwick) (*sénéchaussées*) of [Bèucaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaucaire,_Gard), [Carcassona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne) and [Tolosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) had the status of *bonnes villes* (towns granted privileges and protection by the king of France in return for providing a contingent of men at arms). In that year, the three entered into a perpetual union, after which their contribution of royal officers was summoned jointly rather than separately for each of the three *sénéchaussées*.[^cite_note-Cazelles_(1982)-29][^cite_note-Ourliac_(1967)-30][^cite_note-Gilles_(1965)-31][^cite_note-Guérout_(1965)-32]

Towards the end of the 14th century, the term "country of the three seneschalties" (*pays des trois sénéchaussées*), later to become known as Languedoc, designated the two bailiwicks of Bèucaire-Nimes and [Carcassona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne), and the eastern part of [Tolosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) (Toulouse), retained under the [Treaty of Brétigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Br%C3%A9tigny). At that time, the [County of Foix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Foix), which belonged to the seneschal of Carcassona until 1333 before passing to Toulouse, ceased to belong to Languedoc. In 1542, the province was divided into two *[généralités](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9n%C3%A9ralit%C3%A9)*: Toulouse for Haut-Languedoc, and Montpellier for Bas-Languedoc. This lasted until the French Revolution in 1789. From the 17th century onward, there was only one intendance for the whole of Languedoc, with its seat in Montpellier.

#### Haute-Guyenne



Coat of arms of the duchies of Aquitaine and Guyenne, taken again by the province of Guyenne, of gules with a leopard of gold

The [former provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of [Gascony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gascony) (in [Gascon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gascon_language) *Gasconha*, Occitan pronunciation: [\[ɡasˈkuɲɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Occitan)), and [Guyenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyenne); in [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) *Guiana* [\[ˈɡjanɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Occitan)) were historically part of the Great South-West of France (*Grand Sud-Ouest français*), and derived from the medieval [duchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy) of [Vasconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Gascony), [Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Aquitaine) and then [Guyenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyenne). Today, only the eastern regions of the two provinces are part of Occitania. These areas correspond essentially to the territories acquired by the kings of [England](9316.md), dukes of Guyenne, under the [treaty of Brétigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Br%C3%A9tigny) of 1360, and which then remained under the jurisdiction of the provincial appellate court of Toulouse ([Parlement of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parlement_of_Toulouse)) after the creation of the Parlement of Bordeaux in 1462. From that time, they were generally grouped under the name of [Haute-Guyenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyenne), by opposition with [Basse-Guyenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyenne), which was dependent on the Parlement of Bordeaux.



Coat of arms of the former province of [Gascogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gascony), quartered azure a lion of silver and gules a sheaf of wheat azure bound gold, was created for [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV): it refers to the lion of [counts of Armagnac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Armagnac)

The territory of the [former province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of [Guyenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyenne) (Guiana) that lies within the region corresponds with [Quercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercy) (the current department of [Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lot_\(department\)) and the north of [Tarn e Garona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarn-et-Garonne)), and with [Roergue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouergue) ([Avairon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aveyron)). These two counties are thus part of the [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) linguistic area, in its [Languedocien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedocien_dialect) dialectal variant. They were possessions of the [Counts of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Toulouse) from the 9th century, at various stages a minor branch or the main branch. Like the other possessions of the Counts of Toulouse, they were integrated for the first time with the [French royal domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_lands_of_France) in 1271, and then yielded to the kings of England under the 1360 [Treaty of Brétigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Br%C3%A9tigny). The province of [Quercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercy) was definitely reunited with the Crown in 1472; [Roergue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouergue), possession of the counts of Armagnac, did not follow until 1607. Both came within the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of Guyenne, under military rule from 1561. Together, they became the *[généralité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9n%C3%A9ralit%C3%A9)* of [Montauban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauban) from 1635, which then became the province of Haute-Guyenne in 1779. Today, the name Guyenne no longer has administrative or political currency, although the [Haut-Agenais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agen) used the term *Pais de Guiana* in a campaign which evoked its historical identity to promote the [Lot-et-Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lot-et-Garonne) and part of the valley of [Dròt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dropt). This reference is now superseded in the promotion of tourism by the designation *Pais del Dròt*. The appellations Quercy and Roergue, in contrast, retain a strong identity.

Gascony is the [former province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) located on the territory of the current [departments](38523.md) of [Gers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gers) ([Armanhac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armagnac_\(province\)) and [Condomois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condom,_Gers)), [Hautes-Pyrénées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hautes-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es) ([Bigorre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Bigorre)), [Lanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landes_\(department\)) in the neighboring region, and parts of other departments of the two [regions](58846.md) of [Aquitània](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquitaine) and [Midi-Pyrénées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midi-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es) ([Comenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comminges), [Nebosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9bouzan) and [Rivière-Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivi%C3%A8re-Verdun?action=edit\&redlink=1) mainly in the south and west of [Haute-Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Garonne), and [Couserans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couserans) in the western part of [Ariège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari%C3%A8ge_\(department\))). Successively called [Aquitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallia_Aquitania), [Novempopulania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novempopulania), [Vasconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasconia) (territory of the [Vascones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascones)) and Gascony in the 13th century, the territory was geographically diverse, situated between the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), [Garona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garonne) and the [Pyrenees](24707.md). Claiming a [cultural identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_\(social_science\)) based on evolving occupation, from the [Aquitani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquitani) peoples of [Proto-Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Basque) language to a [Gascon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gascon_language) population sharing a [latinized Occitan dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitano-Romance_languages), it constitutes the current Gascon [linguistic area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprachbund). Between the 15th and the beginning of the 16th centuries, it was progressively integrated into the [Royal French domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_lands_of_France). [Comenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comminges) yielded to the kings of France in 1443, extinguishing the local dynasty. [Armagnac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armagnac_\(province\)), [Bigorre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Bigorre) and [Nebosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9bouzan) followed, with the [county of Foix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Foix), in 1607.

#### County of Foix (Fois)

The [County of Foix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Foix)[^cite_note-Larousse-33] is an old [French](5843419.md) county created out of the [County of Carcassonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Carcassonne) in around 1050[^cite_note-Larousse-33] for [Bernard Roger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard-Roger,_Count_of_Bigorre),[^cite_note-Larousse-33] son of [Roger I of Carcassonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_I_of_Carcassonne).[^cite_note-Larousse-33] In 1398, the county passed to House of [Grailly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grailly)[^cite_note-Larousse-33] and, in 1458, King [Charles VII of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VII_of_France) raised it to the [Peerage of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage_of_France) in favor of [Gaston IV, Count of Foix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_IV,_Count_of_Foix).[^cite_note-34]

The county-peerage passed to the House of Albret in 1484,[^cite_note-Larousse-33] and then to House of [Bourbon-Vendôme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon-Vend%C3%B4me) in 1548.[^cite_note-Larousse-33] In 1607, King [Henry IV of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France) attached the county to the [Royal French domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_lands_of_France).[^cite_note-Larousse-33]

From the time of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), the county was fully incorporated into the department of [Ariège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari%C3%A8ge_\(department\)). The Occitan dialect traditionally spoken there is [Languedocien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedocien_dialect).

#### Roussillon



Roussillon's coat of arms refers to the [coat of arms of the Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_the_Crown_of_Aragon)

The [Ancien Régime province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of [Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roussillon), which had formerly been integrated with the [Catalan counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_counties), the [Kingdom of Majorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Majorca), and the [Principality of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Catalonia) within the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon), was attached to the Crown of France under the [Treaty of the Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Pyrenees), signed on 7 November 1659. Prior to this treaty, the border between the kingdom of France and the Principality of Catalonia lay further north, along a line of [citadels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel) ([Treaty of Corbeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Corbeil_\(1258\))).

These territories corresponded to the [Catalan counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_counties) of [Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Roussillon) and [Conflent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Conflent), founded in the 9th century, as well as to the northern part of the [County of Cerdanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Cerdanya), to which was added the former Vicounty of Castelnou, or [Vallespir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallespir) (the *pagus* of [County of Besalú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Besal%C3%BA), united with the [County of Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Roussillon) in 1209). The new Province of Roussillon[^cite_note-35] also known simply as Roussillon,[^cite_note-36] brought together the medieval administrative courts, or [vigueries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viguerie), of Roussillon,[^cite_note-37] [Conflent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Conflent),[^cite_note-38] and the north of the [County of Cerdanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Cerdanya)[^cite_note-39] which were part of the government structure of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon)'s (and attached to the [Principality of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Catalonia)) counties of Roussillon and Cerdanya (*governació dels comtats de Rosselló i Cerdanya* in [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language))[^cite_note-40] at the time of their attachment to France. Roussillon was subject to direct taxation as a *pays d'imposition* (taxing country) and did not have representation through [the Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Estates)[^cite_note-41] (a provincial assembly, the provincial assembly of Roussillon,[^cite_note-42] was created on 15 August 1787).[^cite_note-43] It formed both a government[^cite_note-44] and an intendance[^cite_note-Bély_(2015)-45] and reported to the [Secretary of State for War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_\(France\)) as a border province. It had sovereign jurisdiction: the Sovereign Council of Roussillon, independent of the [Parlement of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parlement_of_Toulouse).

Currently, the name [Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roussillon) is still the most widely used to designate this territory, being found in the denomination of the former region of [Languedoc-Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc-Roussillon).

Today, the territory is often subdivided into five unofficial traditional and natural [comarques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarca): [Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossell%C3%B3_\(comarca\)) proper, [Vallespir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallespir), [Conflent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflent), Upper [Cerdanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerdanya) and [Capcir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcir). A recent addition is [Fenouillèdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenouill%C3%A8des), the Occitan part of the department of [Pyrénées-Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales).
