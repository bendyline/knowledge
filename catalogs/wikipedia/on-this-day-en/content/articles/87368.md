The **history of Armenia** covers the topics related to the history of the [Republic of Armenia](10918072.md), as well as the [Armenian people](387816.md), the [Armenian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language), and the regions of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) historically and [geographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highlands) considered *Armenian*.[^cite_note-loc.gov-1]

[Armenia](10918072.md) is located between [Eastern Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Anatolia) and the [Armenian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands),[^cite_note-loc.gov-1] surrounding the Biblical mountains of [Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat). The [endonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym) of the Armenians is *hay*, and the old [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) name for the country is *[Hayk'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayk)* ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Հայք, which also means "Armenians" in [Classical Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Armenian)), later *Hayastan* ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Հայաստան).[^cite_note-loc.gov-1] Armenians traditionally associate this name with the legendary progenitor of the Armenian people, [Hayk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayk). The names Armenia and Armenian are [exonyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym), first attested in the [Behistun Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription) of [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great). The early Armenian historian [Movses Khorenatsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi) derived the name Armenia from Aramaneak, the eldest son of the legendary Hayk.[^cite_note-2] Various theories exist about the origin of the endonym and exonyms of Armenia and Armenians (see [Name of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Armenia)).

In the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), several states flourished in the [Armenian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands), including the [Hittite Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) (at the height of its power), [Mitanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitanni) (southwestern historical Armenia), and [Hayasa-Azzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayasa-Azzi) (1600–1200 BC). Soon after the Hayasa-Azzi were the [Nairi tribal confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairi) (1400–1000 BC) and the [Kingdom of Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) (1000–600 BC). Each of the aforementioned nations and tribes participated in the [ethnogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnogenesis) of the [Armenian people](387816.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] [Yerevan](34352.md), the modern capital of Armenia, dates back to the 8th century BC, with the founding of the fortress of [Erebuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erebuni_Fortress) in 782 BC by King [Argishti I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argishti_I_of_Urartu) at the western extreme of the [Ararat plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ararat_Plain).[^cite_note-5] Erebuni has been described as "designed as a great administrative and religious centre, a fully royal capital."[^cite_note-Barnett-6]

The Iron Age kingdom of [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) was replaced by the [Orontid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid_dynasty), which ruled Armenia first as [satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia) under [Achaemenid Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) rule and later as independent kings.[^cite_note-Toumanoff1963-7][^cite_note-8] Following Persian and subsequent Macedonian rule, the [Kingdom of Greater Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)) was established in 190 BC by [Artaxias I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxias_I), founder of the [Artaxiad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxiad_dynasty). The Kingdom of Armenia rose to the peak of its influence in the 1st century BC under [Tigranes the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great) before falling under Roman suzerainty.<sup>[\[9\]](#cite_note-Richard%2520Hovannisian-9)</sup> In the 1st century AD, a branch of the ruling [Arsacid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia) of the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) established itself on the throne of Armenia.

In the early 4th century, [Arsacid Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia) became the first state to institute [Christianity](5211.md) as its [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion). The Armenians later fell under [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Sassanid Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), and [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminiya) hegemony, but regained their independence with the [Bagratid kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Armenia) in the 9th century. After the fall of the kingdom in 1045, and the subsequent Seljuk conquest of Armenia in 1064, the Armenians established a kingdom in [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia), which existed until its destruction in 1375.[^cite_note-10]

In the early 16th century, much of Armenia came under [Safavid Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) rule. However, over the centuries [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia) fell under [Ottoman](22278.md) rule, with only [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) remaining under Persian control.[^cite_note-eastern-11] By the 19th century, Eastern Armenia was conquered by Russia and Greater Armenia was divided between the Ottoman and Russian empires.[^cite_note-12]

In the early 20th century, the Ottoman government subjected the Armenian people to a [genocide](140376.md) in which up to 1.5 million Armenians were killed and many more [were dispersed](214413.md) throughout the world via [Syria](7515849.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md). In 1918, an independent [Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) was established in [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) in the wake of the collapse of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). This republic was incorporated into the Soviet Union in 1920 as a [Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). In 1991, with the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), the modern-day independent [Republic of Armenia](10918072.md) was established.[^cite_note-sworn-13][^cite_note-constitutional-14][^cite_note-Croissant-15]

## Prehistory

Main article: [Prehistoric Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Armenia)

See also: [Archaeology of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Armenia) and [Archaeological heritage of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_heritage_of_Armenia)

Stone tools from 325,000 years ago have been found in Armenia which indicate the presence of early humans at this time.[^cite_note-16]

Excavations at Yerevan 1 Cave in the 1960s revealed evidence of Upper Paleolithic human habitation, including a cranial fragment and a molar tooth believed to belong to a child, dated between 47,800 and 49,000 years before present.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] These finds were discovered alongside faunal remains, shedding light on the early presence of Homo sapiens in the region.[^cite_note-19]

The [Armenian Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highland) shows traces of settlement from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) era. Archaeological surveys in 2010 and 2011 have resulted in the discovery of the world's earliest known leather shoe (3,500 BC), straw skirt (3,900 BC), and wine-making facility (4,000 BC) at the [Areni-1 cave complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areni-1_cave_complex).[^cite_note-CNNc-20][^cite_note-strawskirt-21][^cite_note-National_Geographic-22]



A 5500-year-old leather shoe—the oldest shoe in the world—was discovered in the Areni cave in Armenia. See [Areni-1 shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areni-1_shoe).

The [Shulaveri-Shomu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulaveri-Shomu_culture) of the central [Transcaucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasus) region is one of the earliest known prehistoric cultures in the area, carbon-dated to roughly 6000–4000 BC.

### Bronze Age



Bronze Age astronomical observatory [Zorats Karer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorats_Karer) (also known as [Karahunj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carahunge)).

An early Bronze-Age culture in the area is the [Kura-Araxes culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura%E2%80%93Araxes_culture), assigned to the period between c. 4000 and 2200 BC. The earliest evidence for this culture is found on the [Ararat plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ararat_Plain); thence it spread to [Georgia](48768.md) by 3000 BC (but never reaching [Colchis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchis)), proceeding westward and to the south-east into an area below the Urmia basin and [Lake Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Van).

From 2200 BC to 1600 BC, the [Trialeti-Vanadzor culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trialeti_culture) flourished in Armenia, southern Georgia, and northeastern Turkey.[^cite_note-books.google.ca-23][^cite_note-24] It has been speculated that this was an Indo-European culture.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Other, possibly related, cultures were spread throughout the Armenia Highlands during this time, namely in the [Aragats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Aragats) and [Lake Sevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sevan) regions.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

Early 20th-century scholars suggested that the name "Armenia" may have possibly been recorded for the first time on an inscription which mentions [Armanî](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armani_\(kingdom\)) (or Armânum) together with [Ibla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebla), from territories conquered by [Naram-Sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naram-Sin_of_Akkad) (2300 BC) identified with an [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire) colony in the current region of [Diyarbekir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbak%C4%B1r); however, the precise locations of both Armani and Ibla are unclear. Some modern researchers have placed Armani (Armi) in the general area of modern [Samsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsat),[^cite_note-31] and have suggested it was populated, at least partially, by an early Indo-European-speaking people.[^cite_note-32] Today, the [modern Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) (who traditionally speak [Neo-Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Aramaic_languages), not [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language)) refer to the Armenians by the name Armani.[^cite_note-33] It is possible that the name *Armenia* originates in *Armini*, Urartian for "inhabitant of Arme" or "Armean country."[^cite_note-34] The Arme tribe of Urartian texts may have been the Urumu, who in the 12th century BC attempted to invade Assyria from the north with their allies the [Mushki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushki) and the [Kaskians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskians). The Urumu apparently settled in the vicinity of [Sason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sason), lending their name to the regions of Arme and the nearby land of Urme.[^cite_note-35] [Thutmose III](155730.md) of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), in the 33rd year of his reign (1446 BC), mentioned as the people of "Ermenen", claiming that in their land "heaven rests upon its four pillars".[^cite_note-36] Armenia is possibly connected to [Mannaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannaeans), which may be identical to the region of Minni mentioned in the Bible. However, what all these attestations refer to cannot be determined with certainty, and the earliest certain attestation of the name "Armenia" comes from the [Behistun Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription) (c. 500 BC).

The earliest form of the word "Hayastan", an [endonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym) for Armenia, might possibly be [Hayasa-Azzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayasa-Azzi), a kingdom in the Armenian Highlands that was recorded in [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) records dating from 1500 to 1200 BC.

Between 1200 and 800 BC, much of Armenia was united under a confederation of tribes, which Assyrian sources called [Nairi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairi) ("Land of Rivers" in Assyrian").[^cite_note-37]

### Iron Age

Main article: [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu)



Kingdom of Ararat (Urartu) in the time of [Sarduris II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarduri_II), 743 BC



The natural borders of the Armenian plateau and its peripheral regions according to [H. F. B. Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._F._B._Lynch) (1901).

The Kingdom of Urartu, also known as the Kingdom of Van, flourished between the 9th century BC[^cite_note-38] and 585 BC[^cite_note-Urartu/Armenia-39] in the [Armenian Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highland). The founder of the Urartian Kingdom, [Aramé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramu), united all the principalities of the Armenian Highland and gave himself the title "King of Kings", the traditional title of Urartian Kings.[^cite_note-40] The Urartians established their sovereignty over all of [Taron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taron_\(historic_Armenia\)) and [Vaspurakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaspurakan). The main rival of Urartu was the [Neo-Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire).[^cite_note-41]

During the reign of [Sarduri I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarduri_I) (834–828 BC), Urartu had become a strong and organized state, and imposed taxes on neighbouring tribes. Sarduri made [Tushpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tushpa) (modern [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van,_Turkey)) the capital of Urartu. His son, [Ishpuinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishpuinis_of_Urartu), extended the borders of the state by conquering what would later be known as the [Tigranocerta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranocerta) area and by reaching [Urmia](615663.md). [Menuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menuas_of_Urartu) (810–785 BC) extended the Urartian territory up north, by spreading towards the Araratian fields. He left more than 90 inscriptions by using the Mesopotamian [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform) writing system in the [Urartian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartian_language). [Argishti I of Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argishtis_I_of_Urartu) conquered [Latakia](195594.md) from the Hittites, and reached [Byblos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byblos), and [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia).[^cite_note-42] He built the [Erebuni Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erebuni_Fortress), located in modern-day [Yerevan](34352.md), in 782 BC by using 6600 prisoners of war.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

In 714 BC, the [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire) under [Sargon II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargon_II) defeated the Urartian king [Rusa I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusa_I) at [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia) and destroyed the holy Urartian temple at [Musasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musasir). At the same time, an [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) tribe called the [Cimmerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimmerians) attacked Urartu from the north-west region and destroyed the rest of his armies. Under [Ashurbanipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashurbanipal) (669–627 BC) the boundaries of the [Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Empire) reached as far as Armenia and the Caucasus Mountains. The [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes) under [Cyaxares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyaxares) invaded Assyria later on in 612 BC, and then took over the Urartian capital of [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_\(Turkey\)) towards 585 BC, effectively ending the sovereignty of Urartu.[^cite_note-Badmoutioun_Hayots,_Volume_I-45] According to the Armenian tradition, the Medes helped the Armenians establish the [Orontid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid) dynasty.[^cite_note-46]

## Antiquity

### Orontid dynasty

Main article: [Orontid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid_dynasty)

After the fall of Urartu around 585 BC, the [Satrapy of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia) arose, ruled by the Armenian [Orontid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid_dynasty), which governed the state in 585–190 BC. Under the Orontids, Armenia during this era was a [satrapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap) of the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), and after its disintegration (in 330 BC), it became an independent kingdom. During the rule of the Orontid dynasty, most Armenians adopted the [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism) religion.[^cite_note-47]

### Artaxiad dynasty

Main article: [Artaxiad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxiad_dynasty)



The [Kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)) at its greatest extent under [Tigranes the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great)

The Hellenistic [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire), controlled Syria, Armenia, and vast other eastern regions. However, after their defeat by Rome in 190 BC, the Seleucids relinquished control of any regional claim past the Taurus Mountains, limiting Seleucids to a quickly diminishing area of Syria. A Hellenistic Armenian state was founded in 190 BC. It was a [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) successor state of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great)'s short-lived empire, with Artaxias becoming its first king and the founder of the Artaxiad dynasty (190 BC–AD 1). At the same time, a western portion of the kingdom split as a separate state under Zariadris, which became known as [Lesser Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Armenia) while the main kingdom acquired the name of [Greater Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)).[^cite_note-Urartu/Armenia-39]

The new kings began a program of expansion which was to reach its zenith a century later. Their acquisitions are summarized by Strabo. Zariadris acquired [Acilisene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acilisene) and the "country around the Antitaurus", possibly the district of Muzur or west of the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates). Artaxias took lands from the Medes, Iberians, and Syrians. He then had confrontations with [Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pontus), Seleucid Syria and Cappadocia, and was included in the treaty which followed the victory of a group of Anatolian kings over Pharnaces of Pontus in 181 BC. Pharnaces thus abandoned all of his gains in the west.[^cite_note-48]

At its zenith, from 95 to 66 BC, Greater Armenia extended its rule over parts of the Caucasus and the area that is now eastern and central [Turkey](11125639.md), north-western [Iran](14653.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Syria](7515849.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md), forming the second Armenian empire. For a time, Armenia was one of the most powerful states east of Rome. It eventually confronted the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) in wars, which it lost in 66 BC, but nonetheless preserved its sovereignty. Tigranes continued to rule Armenia as an ally of Rome until his death in 55 BC.[^cite_note-49]

The [Third Mithridatic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Mithridatic_War) and defeat of the King of Pontus by Roman [Pompeius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey) resulted in the Kingdom of Armenia becoming an allied client state of Rome. Later on, in 1 AD, Armenia came under full [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) control until the establishment of the Armenian Arsacid dynasty. The Armenian people then adopted a Western political, philosophical, and religious orientation. According to Strabo, around this time everyone in Armenia spoke "the same language."[^cite_note-50]

Main articles: [Roman Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Armenia) and [Persian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Armenia)

For more details on this topic, see [Roman relations with the Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_relations_with_the_Armenians).



The Roman Empire at its greatest extent, with the "Roman Province of Armenia".

From [Pompeius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey)' campaign Armenia was, for the next few centuries, contested between Rome and Parthia/Sassanid Persia on the other hand. Roman emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) even created a short-lived Province of Armenia between 114 and 118 AD.[^cite_note-51]

Indeed, Roman supremacy was fully established by the [campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Parthian_War_of_58%E2%80%9363) of [Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnaeus_Domitius_Corbulo),[^cite_note-52] that ended with a formal compromise: a Parthian prince of the [Arsacid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia) line would henceforth sit on the Armenian throne, but his nomination had to be approved by the Roman emperor.

Because this agreement was not respected by the Parthian Empire, in 114 [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) from [Antiochia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch) in Syria marched on Armenia and conquered the capital [Artaxata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxata).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Trajan then deposed the Armenian king [Parthamasiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthamasiris_of_Armenia) (imposed by the Parthians) and ordered the annexation of Armenia to the Roman Empire as a new province. The new province reached the shores of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) and bordered to the north with [Caucasian Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iberia) and [Caucasian Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Albania), two vassal states of Rome. As a Roman province Armenia was administered by Catilius Severus of the [Gens Claudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_gens). After Trajan's death, however, his successor [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian) decided not to maintain the province of Armenia. In 118 AD, Hadrian gave Armenia up, and installed [Parthamaspates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthamaspates_of_Parthia) as its "vassal" king.

### Arsacid dynasty

Main articles: [Arsacid dynasty of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia) and [Roman–Parthian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Parthian_Wars)



Armenia in the 4th century, 299–387 AD.

Armenia, under its [Arshakuni dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia), which was a branch of the eponymous Arsacid dynasty of Parthia, was often a focus of contention between Rome and Parthia.[^cite_note-55] The Parthians forced Armenia into submission from 37 to 47, when the Romans retook control of the kingdom.

Under [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero), the Romans fought a [campaign (55–63)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Parthian_War_of_58%E2%80%9363) against the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire), which had invaded the kingdom of Armenia, allied to the Romans. After gaining (60) and losing (62) Armenia, the Romans under [Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnaeus_Domitius_Corbulo), [legate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legatus) of [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Syria) entered (63) into an agreement of [Vologases I of Parthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologases_I_of_Parthia), which confirmed [Tiridates I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiridates_I_of_Armenia) as king of Armenia, thus founding the Arshakuni dynasty.

The Arsacid dynasty lost control of Armenia for a few years when emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) created the "Roman Province of Armenia", fully included into the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) from 114 to 117 AD. His successor, [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian), reinstalled the Arsacid dynasty when he nominated [Parthamaspates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthamaspates_of_Parthia) as "vassal" king of Armenia in 118 AD.

Another campaign was led by Emperor [Lucius Verus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Verus) in 162–165, after [Vologases IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologases_IV) of Parthia had invaded Armenia and installed his chief general on its throne. To counter the Parthian threat, Verus set out for the east. His army won significant victories and retook the capital. [Sohaemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohaemus_of_Armenia), a Roman citizen of Armenian heritage, was installed as the new [client king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state).[^cite_note-56]

The [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) Persians occupied Armenia in 252 and held it until the Romans returned in 287. In 384 the kingdom was split between the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and the Persians.[^cite_note-57] Western Armenia quickly became a province of the Roman Empire under the name of [Armenia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Armenia); Eastern Armenia remained a kingdom within Persia until 428, when the local nobility overthrew the king, and the Sassanids installed a governor in his place.

According to tradition, the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) was established by two of Jesus' [twelve apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_apostles)—[Thaddaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_the_Apostle) and [Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle)—who preached Christianity in Armenia in the 40s–60s AD.[^cite_note-58] Between 1st and 4th centuries AD, the Armenian Church was headed by patriarchs.

In 301, Armenia became the first nation to adopt [Christianity](5211.md) as a state religion,[^cite_note-62] amidst the long-lasting geo-political rivalry over the region. It established a church that today exists independently of both the [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism#Catholic_Church) and the [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) churches, having become so in 451 after having rejected the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon).[^cite_note-63] The [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) is a part of the [Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy) communion, not to be confused with the Eastern Orthodox communion. The first [Catholicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicos_of_All_Armenians) of the Armenian church was Saint [Gregory the Illuminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator).[^cite_note-64] Because of his beliefs, he was persecuted by the pagan king of Armenia, and was "punished" by being thrown in [Khor Virap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Virap), in modern-day Armenia.[^cite_note-65]

He acquired the title of Illuminator, because he illuminated the spirits of Armenians by introducing Christianity to them. Before this, the dominant religion amongst the Armenians was [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism).[^cite_note-66] Scholars have suggested that Armenia adopted Christianity "partly . . . in defiance" of the Sassanids.[^cite_note-67]

In 405–06, Armenia's political future seemed uncertain. With the help of the king of Armenia, [Mesrop Mashtots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesrop_Mashtots) created a unique alphabet to suit the people's needs.[^cite_note-68] By doing so, he ushered in a new [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)) and strengthened Armenian national identity.[^cite_note-69]

After years of rule, the Arsacid dynasty fell in 428, with Eastern Armenia being subjugated to Persia and Western Armenia, to Rome. In the 5th century, the Sassanid Shah [Yazdegerd II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazdegerd_II) tried to tie his Christian Armenian subjects more closely to the Sassanid Empire by reimposing the Zoroastrian religion.[^cite_note-70] The Armenians greatly resented this, and as a result, a rebellion broke out with Vartan Mamikonian as the leader of the rebels. Yazdegerd thus massed his army and sent it to Armenia, where the [Battle of Avarayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Avarayr) took place in 451. The 66,000 Armenian rebels,[^cite_note-71] mostly peasants, lost their morale when Mamikonian died in the battlefield. They were substantially outnumbered by the 180,000- to 220,000-strong[^cite_note-72] Persian army of [Immortals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortals_\(Achaemenid_Empire\)) and [war elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_elephant). Despite being a military defeat, the Battle of Avarayr and the subsequent guerilla war in Armenia eventually resulted in the Treaty of Nvarsak (484), which guaranteed religious freedom to the Armenians.[^cite_note-73]

### Persian Armenia

See also: [Persian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Armenia) and [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia)



The extent of Persian Armenia.

With the partition of Armenia in 387 by the Byzantines and [Sassanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanids), the western half became part of the Byzantines known as [Byzantine Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Armenia), while the eastern (and much larger half) became a vassal state within the Sassanid realm.[^cite_note-Hacikyan_2000_168-74]

In 428, the [Arsacid dynasty of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia) was completely abolished by the Sassanid Persians, and the territory was made a full province within Persia, known as [Persian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Armenia).[^cite_note-Hacikyan_2000_168-74] Persian Armenia remained in Sassanid hands up to the [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia), when the invading Muslim forces annexed the Sassanid realm.[^cite_note-75]

### Arab caliphates, Byzantium and Bagratid Armenia

Main articles: [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) and [Arminiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminiya)



[Akdamar church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akdamar_church), relief of ruler assumed to be [Gagik I Artsruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gagik_I_Artsruni), with attendants (915–921)

In 591, the Byzantine emperor [Maurice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_\(emperor\)) defeated the Persians and recovered much of the remaining territory of Armenia into the empire.[^cite_note-76] The conquest was completed by the emperor [Heraclius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclius), himself ethnically Armenian,[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] in 629. In 645, the Muslim Arab armies of the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) had [attacked and conquered the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Armenia). Armenia, which once had its own rulers and was at other times under Persian and Byzantine control, passed largely into the power of the caliphs, and established the province of [Arminiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminiya).

Nonetheless, there were still parts of Armenia held within the empire, containing many Armenians. This population held tremendous power within the empire. Emperor [Heraclius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclius) (610–641) was of Armenian descent, as was Emperor [Philippikos Bardanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippikos_Bardanes) (711–713). The emperor [Basil I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_I), who took the Byzantine throne in 867, was the first of what is sometimes called the Armenian dynasty (see [Macedonian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_dynasty)), reflecting the strong effect the Armenians had on the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).[^cite_note-80]

Evolving as a feudal kingdom in the ninth century, Armenia experienced a brief cultural, political and economic renewal under the [Bagratuni dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratuni_dynasty). [Bagratid Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Armenia) was eventually recognized as a sovereign kingdom by the two major powers in the region: Baghdad in 885, and Constantinople in 886. [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani), the new Armenian capital, was constructed at the kingdom's apogee in 964.[^cite_note-Louvre-81]



Armenian feudal kingdoms, 1000 AD

The [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples)[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] [Sallarid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sallarid_dynasty) conquered parts of Eastern Armenia in the second half of the 10th century.[^cite_note-84]

### Seljuq Armenia

Main article: [Seljuq Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_Empire)

See also: [Battle of Manzikert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manzikert)



Canon tables from the Haghbat Gospels, created in the [Haghpat Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haghpat_Monastery); 1211 (Matendaran, MS 6288, fols. 8v–9r).

[^cite_note-85]

Although the native Bagratuni dynasty was founded under favourable circumstances, the feudal system gradually weakened the country by eroding loyalty to the central government. Thus internally enfeebled, Armenia proved an easy victim for the Byzantines, who captured Ani in 1045. The [Seljuk dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_dynasty) under [Alp Arslan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alp_Arslan) in turn took the city in 1064.[^cite_note-86]

In 1071, after the defeat of the Byzantine forces by the Seljuk Turks at the [Battle of Manzikert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manzikert), the Turks captured the rest of Greater Armenia and much of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia).[^cite_note-87] So ended Christian leadership of Armenia for the next millennium with the exception of a period of the late 12th-early 13th centuries, when the Muslim power in Greater Armenia was seriously troubled by the resurgent [Kingdom of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Georgia). Many local nobles (*nakharars*) joined their efforts with the [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians), leading to liberation of several areas in northern Armenia, which was ruled, under the authority of the Georgian crown, by the [Zakarids-Mkhargrzeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarids-Mkhargrzeli), a prominent Armeno-Georgian noble family.

### Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia

Main article: [Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia)



The Kingdom of Cilician Armenia, 1199–1375.

To escape death or servitude at the hands of those who had assassinated his relative [Gagik II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gagik_II), King of [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani), an Armenian named [Roupen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_I_of_Armenia) with some of his countrymen went into the gorges of the Taurus Mountains and then into Tarsus of Cilicia. Here the Byzantine governor gave them shelter in the late 11th century. Two great dynastic families, the [Rubenids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubenids) and the [Hethumids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hethumids), ruled what became in 1199, with the coronation of [Levon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_I,_King_of_Armenia), the [Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia) and through skillful diplomacy and military alliances (explained below) maintained their political autonomy until 1375.[^cite_note-mutafian-88] The kingdom's political independence relied on a vast network of castles which controlled the mountain passes and the strategic harbours.[^cite_note-edwards-89] Almost all of the civilian settlements were located directly below or near these fortifications.[^cite_note-90]

After the members of the first Crusade appeared in Asia Minor, the Armenians developed close ties to European [Crusader States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_States). They flourished in south-eastern [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor) until it was conquered by [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) states. Count [Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_I_of_Jerusalem), who with the rest of the Crusaders was passing through Asia Minor bound for Jerusalem, left the Crusader army and was adopted by [Thoros of Edessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoros_of_Edessa), an Armenian ruler of Greek Orthodox faith.[^cite_note-Cilicia:_A_Historical_Overview-91] As they were hostile towards the [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) and unfriendly to the Byzantines, the Armenians took kindly to the crusader count. So when Thoros was assassinated, Baldwin was made ruler of the new crusader [County of Edessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Edessa). It seems that the Armenians were pleased with Baldwin's rule and with the crusaders in general, and some number of them fought alongside the crusaders. When [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch) had been taken (1097), Constantine, the son of Roupen, received from the crusaders the title of baron.[^cite_note-92]

The [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade) and other events elsewhere left Cilicia as the sole substantial Christian presence in the Middle East.[^cite_note-Cilicia:_A_Historical_Overview-91] World powers, such as Byzantium, the Holy Roman Empire, the papacy and even the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbassid) caliph competed and vied for influence over the state and each raced to be the first to recognise [Leo II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_II,_Prince_of_Armenia), Prince of Lesser Armenia, as the rightful king. As a result, he had been given a crown by both German and Byzantine emperors. Representatives from across Christendom and a number of Muslim states attended the coronation, thus highlighting the important stature that Cilicia had gained over time.[^cite_note-Cilicia:_A_Historical_Overview-91] The Armenian authorities was often in touch with the crusaders. No doubt the Armenians aided in some of the other crusades. Cilicia flourished greatly under Armenian rule, as it became the last remnant of medieval Armenian statehood.[^cite_note-93] Cilicia acquired an Armenian identity, as the kings of Cilicia were called kings of the Armenians, not of the Cilicians.

In Lesser Armenia, Armenian culture was intertwined with both the European culture of the Crusaders and with the Hellenic culture of Cilicia. As the Catholic families extended their influence over Cilicia, the Pope wanted the Armenians to follow Catholicism. This situation divided the kingdom's inhabitants between pro-Catholic and pro-Apostolic camps. Armenian sovereignty lasted until 1375, when the Mamelukes of Egypt profited from the unstable situation in Lesser Armenia and destroyed it.[^cite_note-94]

## Early modern period

### Persian Armenia

Main article: [Armenians in the Persianate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Persianate)

See also: [Khanates of the Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanates_of_the_Caucasus), [Melikdoms of Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melikdoms_of_Karabakh), and [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay)



Eastern Armenia, 1740.



Robert de Vaugondy Map of Persia, Arabia and Turkey, 1753. Armenia is divided between Persia and Turkey.



East Armenia on the Persian Empire map. John Pinkerton, 1818.



The [Erivan Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Khanate) within the Iranian [Safavid Empire.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty)

Due to its strategic significance, the historical Armenian homelands of [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia) and [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) were constantly fought over and passed back and forth between [Safavid Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) and the [Ottomans](22278.md). For example, at the height of the [Ottoman–Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Persian_Wars), Yerevan changed hands fourteen times between 1513 and 1737. Greater Armenia was annexed in the early 16th century by Shah [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I).[^cite_note-95] Following the [Peace of Amasya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Amasya) of 1555, Western Armenia fell into the neighbouring [Ottoman hands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkey), while Eastern Armenia stayed part of Safavid Iran, until the 19th century.[^cite_note-96]

In 1604, [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abbas_I) pursued a scorched-earth campaign against the Ottomans in the Ararat valley during the [Ottoman–Safavid War (1603–1618)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Safavid_War_\(1603%E2%80%931618\)). The old Armenian town of [Julfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCl%C3%BCstan,_Nakhchivan) in the province of [Nakhichevan](385358.md) was taken early in the invasion. From there Abbas' army fanned out across the Araratian plain. The Shah pursued a careful strategy, advancing and retreating as the occasion demanded, determined not to risk his enterprise in a direct confrontation with stronger enemy forces.

While laying siege to [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars), he learned of the approach of a large Ottoman army, commanded by Djghazadé [Sinan Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinan_Pasha). The order to withdraw was given; but to deny the enemy the potential to resupply themselves from the land, he ordered the wholesale destruction of the Armenian towns and farms on the plain. As part of this the whole population was ordered to accompany the Persian army in its withdrawal. Some 300,000 people were duly herded to the banks of the [Araxes River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araxes_River). Those who attempted to resist the [mass deportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Surgun) were killed outright. The Shah had previously ordered the destruction of the only bridge, so people were forced into the waters, where a great many drowned, carried away by the currents, before reaching the opposite bank. This was only the beginning of their ordeal. One eye-witness, Father de Guyan, describes the predicament of the refugees thus:

*It was not only the winter cold that was causing torture and death to the deportees. The greatest suffering came from hunger. The provisions which the deportees had brought with them were soon consumed ... The children were crying for food or milk, none of which existed, because the women's breasts had dried up from hunger ... Many women, hungry and exhausted, would leave their famished children on the roadside, and continue their tortuous journey. Some would go to nearby forests in search of something to eat. Usually they would not come back. Often those who died, served as food for the living.*

Unable to maintain his army on the desolate plain, Sinan Pasha was forced to winter in [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van,_Turkey). Armies sent in pursuit of the Shah in 1605 were defeated, and by 1606 Abbas had regained all of the territory lost to the Turks earlier in his reign. The scorched-earth tactic had worked, though at a terrible cost to the Armenian people. Of the 300,000 deported it is calculated that less than half survived the march to [Isfahan](223149.md). In the conquered territories Abbas established the [Erivan Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Khanate), a Muslim principality under the dominion of the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Empire). Armenians formed less than 20% of its population[^cite_note-hewsen-97] as a result of [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_I_of_Persia)'s deportation of many of the Armenian population from the Ararat valley and the surrounding region in 1605.[^cite_note-haxthausen-98]

An often-used policy by the Persians was the appointment of Turks as local rulers as so-called *khans* of their various *[khanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanates_of_the_Caucasus)*. These were counted as subordinate to the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire). Examples include: the [Khanate of Erevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Erevan), [Khanate of Nakhichevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Nakhichevan) and the [Karabakh Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Khanate).

Even though Western Armenia had already once been conquered by the Ottomans following the Peace of Amasya, Greater Armenia was eventually decisively divided between the vying rivals, the Ottomans and the Safavids, in the first half of the 17th century following the [Ottoman–Safavid War (1623–1639)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Safavid_War_\(1623%E2%80%931639\)) and the resulting [Treaty of Zuhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Zuhab) under which Eastern Armenia remained under Persian rule, and Western Armenia remained under Ottoman rule.[^cite_note-eastern-11]

Persia continued to rule Eastern Armenia, which included all of the modern-day Armenian Republic, until the first half of the 19th century. By the late 18th century, Imperial Russia had started to encroach to the south into the land of its neighbours; [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and Ottoman Turkey. In 1804, [Pavel Tsitsianov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Tsitsianov) [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ganja_\(1804\)) the Iranian town of [Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganja,_Azerbaijan) and massacred many of its inhabitants while making the rest flee deeper within the borders of Qajar Iran. This was a declaration of war and regarded as an invasion of Iranian territory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFisherAveryHamblyMelville1991332-99] It was the beginning of the [Russo-Persian War (1804–1813)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%931813\)). The following years were devastating for the Iranian towns in the Caucasus as well as the inhabitants of the region, as well as for the Persian army. The war eventually ended in 1813 with a Russian victory after their successful [storming of Lankaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_Lankaran) in early 1813. The [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan) that was signed in the same year forced Qajar Iran to irrevocably cede significant amounts of its [Caucasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) territories to Russia, comprising modern-day [Dagestan](751099.md), [Georgia](48768.md), and most of what is today the [Republic of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Azerbaijan).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-Mikaberidze_p_351-101] [Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Khanate) was also ceded to Russia by Persia.[^cite_note-Mikaberidze_p_351-101]

The Persians were severely dissatisfied with the outcome of the war which led to the ceding of so much Persian territory to the Russians. As a result,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFisherAveryHamblyMelville1991329–330-102] the next war between Russia and Persia was inevitable, namely the [Russo-Persian War (1826–1828)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1826%E2%80%931828\)). However, this war ended even more disastrously, as the Russians not only occupied as far as [Tabriz](439791.md), the ensuing treaty that followed, namely the [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay) of 1828, forced it to irrevocably cede its last remaining territories in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus), comprising all of modern-day Armenia, [Nakhchivan](385358.md) and [Iğdır Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C4%9Fd%C4%B1r_Province).[^cite_note-103]

By 1828, Persia had lost Eastern Armenia, which included the territory of the modern-day Armenian Republic after centuries of rule. From 1828 until 1991, Eastern Armenia would enter [a Russian dominated chapter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Armenia). Following Russia's conquest of all of [Qajar Iran's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) Caucasian territories, many Armenian families were encouraged to settle in the newly conquered Russian territories.[^cite_note-104]

Patriarch [Harutyun I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harutyun_I?action=edit\&redlink=1) [of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Armenian_Patriarchs_of_Constantinople)

[Mehmed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_II) conquered [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) from the Byzantines in 1453 and made it the Ottoman Empire's capital. Mehmed and his successors used the religious systems of their subject nationalities as a method of population control, and so Ottoman Sultans invited an Armenian archbishop to establish the [Armenian Patriarchate of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople). The Armenians of Constantinople grew in numbers, and became respected, if not full, members of Ottoman society.

The Ottoman Empire ruled in accordance to [Islamic law](28840.md). As such, the [People of the Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_the_Book) (the [Christians](5211.md) and the [Jews](15624.md)) had to pay an extra tax to fulfil their status as [dhimmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhimmi) and in return were guaranteed religious autonomy. While the Armenians of [Constantinople](3391396.md) benefited from the [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_of_the_Ottoman_Empire)'s support and grew to be a prospering community, the same could not be said about the ones inhabiting [historic Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highland).

During times of crisis the ones in the remote regions of mountainous [eastern Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Anatolia) were mistreated by local [Kurdish](17068.md) chiefs and feudal lords. They often also had to suffer (alongside the settled Muslim population) raids by nomadic Kurdish tribes.[^cite_note-105] Armenians, like the other Ottoman Christians (though not to the same extent), had to transfer some of their healthy male children to the Sultan's government due to the [devşirme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev%C5%9Firme) policies in place. The boys were then forced to convert to Islam (by threat of death otherwise) and educated to be fierce warriors in times of war, as well as [Beys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey), [Pashas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha) and even [Grand Viziers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Vizier) in times of peace.

The Armenian national liberation movement was the Armenian effort to free the [historic Armenian homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highland) of eastern [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) and [Transcaucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasus) from [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and Ottoman domination and re-establish the independent Armenian state. The national liberation movement of the [Balkan peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Balkans#National_Awakening_in_the_Balkans) and the immediate involvement of the European powers in the Eastern question had a powerful effect on the development of the national liberation ideology movement among the [Armenians of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire).[^cite_note-106]

The Armenian national movement, besides its individual heroes, was an organized activity represented around three parties of Armenian people, [Social Democrat Hunchakian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrat_Hunchakian_Party), [Armenakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenakan_Party) and [Armenian Revolutionary Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), which ARF was the largest and most influential among the three. Those Armenians who did not support national liberation aspirations or who were neutral were called *chezoks*. In 1839, the situation of the Ottoman Armenians slightly improved after [Abdul Mejid I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Mejid_I) carried out [Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat) reforms in its territories. However, later Sultans, such as [Abdul Hamid II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_II) stopped the reforms and carried out massacres, now known as the [Hamidian massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamidian_massacres) of 1895–96 leading to a failed Armenian attempt to [assassinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C4%B1ld%C4%B1z_assassination_attempt) him.[^cite_note-\:2-107]

### Russian Armenia

Main article: [Russian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Armenia)



Map of the [Armenian Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Oblast) within the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire)

In the aftermath of the [Russo-Persian War, 1826–1828](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War,_1826%E2%80%931828), the parts of historic Armenia (also known as [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia)) under Persian control, centering on [Yerevan](34352.md) and [Lake Sevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sevan), were incorporated into [Russia](25391.md) after Qajar Persia's forced ceding in 1828 per the [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay).[^cite_note-https-108] Under Russian rule, the area corresponding approximately to modern-day Armenian territory was called "Province of Yerevan". The Armenian subjects of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) lived in relative safety, compared to their Ottoman kin, albeit clashes with [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeris) and [Kurds](17068.md) were frequent in the early 20th century. Even though Russian Armenians benefited from the advanced Russian culture, and greater access to European thought, and broader economic initiative, they were denied equal educational and administrative opportunities like many other racial and religious minorities.[^cite_note-109]

The Treaty of Turkmenchay of 1828 had further stipulated the rights of the Russian tsar to resettle [Persian Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Armenians) within the newly conquered Caucasus region, which had been taken over from [Iran](14653.md). Following the resettlement of Persian Armenians alone in the newly conquered Russian territories, significant demographic shifts were bound to take place. The Armenian-American historian [George Bournoutian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bournoutian) gives a summary of the ethnic make-up after those events:[^cite_note-110]

> In the first quarter of the 19th century the Khanate of Erevan included most of Eastern Armenia and covered an area of approximately 7,000 square miles \[18,000 km<sup>2</sup>]. The land was mountainous and dry, the population of about 100,000 was roughly 80 percent Muslim (Persian, Azeri, Kurdish) and 20 percent Christian (Armenian).

After the incorporation of the [Erivan Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Khanate) into the Russian Empire, Muslim majority of the area gradually changed, at first the Armenians who were left captive were encouraged to return.[^cite_note-William_Bayne_Fisher_p._339-111] As a result of which an estimated 57,000 Armenian refugees from Persia returned to the territory of the Erivan Khanate after 1828, while about 35,000 Muslims (Persians, Turkic groups, Kurds, Lezgis, etc.) out of a total population of over 100,000 left the region.[^cite_note-potier-112]

## 20th century

### Armenian genocide (1915–1921) and First World War

Main articles: [Armenian genocide](140376.md) and [Caucasus campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_campaign)



Armenian civilians, being deported during the [Armenian genocide](140376.md)



6 Armenian provinces of Western Armenia and boundaries between countries before [World War I](4764461.md)



Map of massacre locations and deportation and extermination centers during the Armenian genocide 1915–1916

In 1915, the Ottoman Empire systematically carried out the [Armenian genocide](140376.md). This genocide was preceded by a [wave of massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamidian_massacres) in the years 1894 to 1896,[^cite_note-\:2-107] as well as another massacre [in 1909 in Adana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adana_massacre). On 24 April 1915, Ottoman authorities rounded up, arrested, and deported [235 to 270 Armenian intellectuals and community leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_Armenian_intellectuals_on_24_April_1915) from [Constantinople](3391396.md) to the region of [Ankara](802.md), where the majority were murdered. The genocide was carried out during and after [World War I](4764461.md) and implemented in two phases—the wholesale killing of the able-bodied male population through massacre and subjection of army conscripts to [forced labour](541048.md), followed by the deportation of women, children, the elderly, and the infirm on [death marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_march) leading to the [Syrian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Desert). Driven forward by military escorts, the deportees were deprived of food and water and subjected to periodic robbery, rape, and massacre.[^cite_note-113]

Most frequently, the exact number of deaths is estimated to have been 1.5 million,[^cite_note-114] with other estimates ranging from 800,000 to 1,800,000.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-Auron2000-116][^cite_note-Forsythe2009-117]<sup>: 98 </sup>[^cite_note-ChalkJonassohn1990-118] These events are traditionally commemorated yearly on 24 April, the Armenian Christian martyr day.[^cite_note-119]

### First Republic of Armenia (1918–1920)

Main article: [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia)

Between the 4th and 19th centuries, the traditional area of Armenia was conquered and ruled by Persians, Byzantines, [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab), [Mongols](19271.md), and [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks), among others. Parts of historical Armenia gained independence from the Ottoman Empire and the Russian Empire after the collapse of these two empires in the wake of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

#### Transcaucasian Federation (1917–1918)

See also: [Caucasus Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Campaign)

During the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917), the provinces of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) seceded and formed their own federal state called the [Transcaucasian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Democratic_Federative_Republic). Competing national interests and war with [Turkey](11125639.md) led to the dissolution of the republic half a year later, in April 1918.

After the [Russian Revolution of 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917) and the takeover of the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik), [Stepan Shaumyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Shaumyan) was placed in charge of Russian Armenia. In September 1917, the convention in Tiflis elected the [Armenian National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_National_Council_of_Tiflis),[^cite_note-\:1-122] the first sovereign political body of Armenians since the collapse of Lesser Armenia in 1375. Meanwhile, both the Ittihad (Unionist) and the Nationalists moved to win the friendship of the Bolsheviks.

[Mustafa Kemal (Atatürk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) sent several delegations to Moscow in an attempt to win some support for his own post-Ottoman movement in what he saw as a modernised ethno-nationalist Turkey. This alliance proved disastrous for the Armenians. The signing of the Ottoman-Russian friendship treaty (1 January 1918) helped [Vehib Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehib_Pasha) to attack the new republic. Under heavy pressure from the combined forces of the Ottoman army and the Kurdish irregulars, the Republic was forced to withdraw from Erzincan to Erzurum. In the end, the Republic had to evacuate Erzurum as well.

Further southeast, in Van, the Armenians resisted the Turkish army until April 1918, but eventually were forced to evacuate it and withdraw to Persia. Conditions deteriorated when Azerbaijani Tatars sided with the Turks and seized the Armenian's lines of communication, thus cutting off the Armenian National Councils in Baku and Yerevan from the National Council in Tiflis. The [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) was established on 28 May 1918.[^cite_note-\:1-122]

During the final stages of [World War I](4764461.md), the Armenians and Georgians had been defending against the advance of the Ottoman Empire. In June 1918, to forestall an Ottoman advance on [Tiflis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiflis), the Georgian troops had occupied the [Lori Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Province) which at the time had a 75% Armenian majority.[^cite_note-Walker-123]

After the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros) and the withdrawal of the Ottomans, the Georgian forces remained. The Georgian [Menshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menshevik) parliamentarian [Irakli Tsereteli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irakli_Tsereteli) suggested that the Armenians would be safer from the Turks as Georgian citizens. The Georgians offered a quadripartite conference comprising Georgia, Armenia, [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Democratic_Republic), and the [Mountainous Republic of the Northern Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountainous_Republic_of_the_Northern_Caucasus) to resolve the issue. The Armenians rejected this proposal. In December 1918, the Georgians were confronting a rebellion chiefly in the village of [Uzunlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odzun) in the Lori region. Within days, hostilities commenced between the two republics.[^cite_note-Walker-123]

The [Georgian–Armenian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian%E2%80%93Armenian_War) was a border war fought in 1918 between the [Democratic Republic of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Georgia) and the [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) over the then disputed provinces of [Lori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Province) and [Javakheti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javakheti) which had been historically bi-cultural Armenian-Georgian territories, but were largely populated by Armenians in the 19th century.[^cite_note-124]

#### Armenian-Azerbaijan War

Main article: [Armenian–Azerbaijani war (1918–1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian%E2%80%93Azerbaijani_war_\(1918%E2%80%931920\))

A considerable degree of hostility existed between Armenia and its new neighbor to the east, the [Democratic Republic of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Azerbaijan), stemming largely from racial, religious, cultural and societal differences. The Azeris had close ethnic and religious ties to the Turks and had provided material support for them in their drive to [Baku](4566.md) in 1918. Although the borders of the two countries were still undefined, Azerbaijan claimed most of the territory Armenia was sitting on, demanding all or most parts of the former Russian provinces of [Elizavetpol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizavetpol), [Tiflis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiflis), [Yerevan](34352.md), [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars_Oblast) and [Batum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batum).[^cite_note-125] As diplomacy failed to accomplish compromise, even with the mediation of the commanders of a British expeditionary force that had installed itself in the Caucasus, territorial clashes between Armenia and Azerbaijan took place throughout 1919 and 1920, most notably in the regions of [Nakhichevan](385358.md), [Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh), and [Syunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province) (Zangezur). Repeated attempts to bring these provinces under Azerbaijani jurisdiction were met with fierce resistance by their Armenian inhabitants. In May 1919, [Dro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drastamat_Kanayan) led an expeditionary unit that was successful in establishing Armenian administrative control in [Nakhichevan](385358.md).[^cite_note-126]

### Paris Peace Conference

| image icon [Map of Armenia, as proposed at Paris Peace Conference](https://i.imgur.com/15Zq8pv.jpg) |
| --- |

At [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919) in 1919 it was proposed to create large (330,200 km<sup>2</sup> or 127,491 sq mi) Armenian state, including the territory of former [Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia) with total population of 4.3 million, 2.5 million of which would be Armenians.[^cite_note-127]

#### Treaty of Sèvres



The planned partition of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) according to the superseded [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) of 1920



The proposed Armenian state created by the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres)

The [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) was signed between the Allied and Associated Powers and Ottoman Empire at [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A8vres), [France](5843419.md), on 10 August 1920. The treaty included a clause on Armenia: it made all parties signing the treaty recognize Armenia as a free and independent state. The drawing of definite borders was, however, left to U.S. president [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) and the United States State Department, and was only presented to Armenia on 22 November 1920. The new borders gave Armenia access to the Black Sea and awarded large portions of the eastern provinces of the Ottoman Empire to the republic.[^cite_note-128]<sup>: 40–44 </sup>

The Treaty of Sèvres was signed by the Ottoman government, but Sultan [Mehmed VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI) never signed it and thus never came into effect. The [Turkish Revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Revolutionaries), led by [Mustafa Kemal Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Pasha), began the [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) which, in opposing any territorial concessions to either the Greeks or the Armenians.

On 20 September 1920, Turkish nationalist militants invaded the region of [Sarikamish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarikamish).[^cite_note-129] In response, Armenia declared war on Turkey on 24 September and the [Turkish invasion of Armenia (1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Armenia_\(1920\)) began. In the regions of [Oltu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltu), Sarikamish, [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars), and [Alexandropol](1529357.md) (Gyumri), Armenian forces clashed with those of the Turkish armies. [Mustafa Kemal Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Pasha) had sent several delegations to Moscow in search of an alliance, where he had found a receptive response by the Soviet government, which started sending gold and weapons to the [Turkish revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_revolutionaries), which would prove disastrous for the Armenians.[^cite_note-o108-130]

Armenia gave way to [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) power in late 1920. In November 1920, the [Turkish revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_revolutionaries) captured Alexandropol and were poised to move in on the capital. A cease fire was concluded on 18 November. Negotiations were then carried out between [Kâzım Karabekir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_Karabekir) and a peace delegation led by [Alexander Khatisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Khatisian) in Alexandropol; although Karabekir's terms were extremely harsh the Armenian delegation had little recourse but to agree to them. The [Treaty of Alexandropol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alexandropol) was signed on 3 December 1920, although the Armenian government had already fallen to the Soviets the day before.[^cite_note-131]



Members of the Soviet [11th Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Red_Army) marching down Yerevan's Abovyan Boulevard, effectively ending Armenian self-rule

As the terms of defeat were being negotiated, Bolshevik [Grigoriy Ordzhonikidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigoriy_Ordzhonikidze) invaded from Azerbaijan the First Republic of Armenia to establish a new pro-Bolshevik government in the country. The [11th Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Red_Army) began its virtually unopposed advance into Armenia on 29 November 1920 at [Ijevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijevan). The actual transfer of power took place on 2 December 1920 in Yerevan.[^cite_note-\:3-132]

The Armenian leadership approved an ultimatum presented to it by the Soviet plenipotentiary [Boris Legran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Legran). Armenia decided to join the Soviet sphere, while Soviet Russia agreed to protect its remaining territory from the advancing Turkish army. The Soviets also pledged to take steps to rebuild the army, protect the Armenians and to not pursue non-communist Armenians, although the final condition of this pledge was reneged when the Dashnaks were forced out of the country.[^cite_note-133]

On 5 December, the Armenian Revolutionary Committee (*[Revkom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revkom)*, made up of mostly Armenians from Azerbaijan) also entered the city.[^cite_note-134] Finally, on the following day, 6 December, [Felix Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Dzerzhinsky)'s [Cheka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheka) entered Yerevan, thus effectively ending the existence of the Democratic Republic of Armenia. At that point what was left of Armenia was under the influence of the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks).

Although the Bolsheviks succeeded in ousting the Turks from their positions in Armenia, they decided to establish peace with Turkey. In 1921, the Bolsheviks and the Turks signed the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars), in which Turkey ceded [Adjara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjara) to the USSR in exchange for the Kars territory (today the Turkish provinces of [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars_Province), [Surmalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C4%9Fd%C4%B1r_Province), and [Ardahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardahan_Province)). The land given to Turkey included the ancient city of [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani) and [Mount Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat), the spiritual Armenian homeland. In 1922, the newly proclaimed [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), under the leadership of [Alexander Miasnikyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Myasnikyan), became part of the Soviet Union as one of three republics comprising the [Transcaucasian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_SFSR).[^cite_note-\:3-132]

### Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic (1922–1991)

Main article: [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)



The coat of arms of [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) depicting Mount Ararat in the center

The Transcaucasian SFSR was dissolved in 1936, and as a result Armenia became a constituent republic of the Soviet Union as the [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_SSR).[^cite_note-135] The transition to [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) was difficult for Armenia, and for most of the other republics in the Soviet Union. The Soviet authorities placed Armenians under supervision. The rate of [freedom of speech](21401843.md) was considered low, even less so during secretaryship of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin). Any individual who was suspected of using or introducing [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist), [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) and [conservative](6675.md) [rhetoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetoric) or elements in their works were labelled traitors or propagandists, and were sent to [prisons](19008450.md) in [Siberia](29639.md). Even [Zabel Yesayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabel_Yesayan), a writer who was fortunate enough to escape from ethnic cleansing during the Armenian genocide, was quickly exiled to Siberia after returning to Armenia from France.

Armenian [SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Socialist_Republic) participated in [World War II](32927.md) by sending hundreds of thousands of soldiers to the front line to defend the USSR. [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) system had several positive aspects. Armenia benefited from the Soviet economy, especially when it was at its apex. Provincial villages gradually became towns and towns gradually became cities. Peace between Armenia and [Azerbaijan](746.md) was reached, albeit temporarily. During this time, Armenia had a sizeable Azeri minority, mostly centred in Yerevan. Likewise, Azerbaijan had an Armenian minority, concentrated in [Baku](4566.md) and [Kirovabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirovabad).

Many Armenians still had nationalist and conservative sentiments, even though they were discouraged from expressing them publicly. On 24 April 1965, tens of thousands of Armenians flooded the streets of Yerevan to remind the world of the horrors that their parents and grandparents endured during the Armenian genocide of 1915. This was the first public demonstration of such high numbers in the USSR, which defended national interests rather than collective ones. In the late 1980s, Armenia was suffering from pollution.

### Perestroika and Glasnost

Main articles: [History of the Soviet Union (1982–1991)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_\(1982%E2%80%931991\)), [Perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika), and [Glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost)

With [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev)'s introduction of [glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost) and [perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika), public demonstrations became more common. Thousands of Armenians demonstrated in Yerevan because of the USSR's inability to address simple ecological concerns. Later on, with the conflict in [Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh), the demonstrations obtained a more nationalistic flavour. Many Armenians began to demand [statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statehood).

In 1988, the [Spitak earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitak_earthquake) killed tens of thousands of people and destroyed multiple towns in northern Armenia, such as Leninakan (modern-day [Gyumri](1529357.md)) and [Spitak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitak). Many families were left without electricity and running water. The harsh situation caused by the earthquake and subsequent events made many residents of Armenia leave and settle in [North America](21139.md), [Western Europe](33800.md) and [Australia](4689264.md).

On 20 February 1988, interethnic fighting between the ethnic Armenians of Nagorno-Karabakh and Azerbaijanis broke out shortly after the parliament of [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md), an autonomous oblast in Azerbaijan, voted to unify the region with Armenia. The [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) pitted Armenians of Nagorno-Karabakh, backed by Armenia, against the [Army of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Azerbaijan).

### Priority over Soviet Union laws and negotiations on a new Treaty

Main articles: [Parade of sovereignties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade_of_sovereignties), [War of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Laws), and [New Union Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Union_Treaty)

Armenia [declared its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_State_Sovereignty_of_Armenia) on 23 August 1990 in the following terms: "The Supreme Council of the Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic declares the beginning of the process of establishing of independent statehood" and "As the subject of international law, the Republic of Armenia conducts an independent foreign policy; it establishes direct relations with other states, national-state units of the USSR, and participates in the activity of international organizations".[^cite_note-137]

However, the status quo was maintained for some time, for example, Ter-Petrosyan took part in the work of the [State Council of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_USSR), representing Armenia as a [union republic of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_republic_of_the_USSR) and recognizing the independence of the three [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) on 6 September 1991.[^cite_note-138]

### Soviet coup attempt, the Transition Period and the end of the Soviet Union

Main articles: [1991 Soviet coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_attempt); [Dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union); [Transition period and cessation of the existence of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_period_and_cessation_of_the_existence_of_the_Soviet_Union); and [Succession, continuity and legacy of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession,_continuity_and_legacy_of_the_Soviet_Union)

Independence was confirmed by [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Armenian_independence_referendum) on 21 September 1991. On 23 September 1991, the Supreme Council of Armenia confirmed the republic's secession from the USSR based on the results of the referendum.[^cite_note-139] However, widespread recognition did not occur until the formal self-dissolution of the Soviet Union on 26 December 1991.

The [Treaty on Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_Economic_Community) was signed in Moscow on 18 October 1991 in a single copy in the Russian language by the competent representatives, including Levon Ter-Petrosyan representing Armenia.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

### Independent country and the Commonwealth

Main articles: [Belovezha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belovezha_Accords), [Alma-Ata Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma-Ata_Protocol), [Common Economic Space of the Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Economic_Space_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Independent_States), [Mobility rights arrangements of the Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_rights_arrangements_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Independent_States), and [Military of the Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Independent_States)

In Alma-Ata, on 21 December, 11 Republics including Armenia became equal co-founders of the Commonwealth of Independent States by signing [Alma-Ata Protocol to the Agreement on the Creation of the Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma-Ata_Protocol), which entered into force for Armenia on 18 February 1992.[^cite_note-142]

[Post-Soviet countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_countries) have signed a [series of treaties and agreements to settle the legacy of the former Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession,_continuity_and_legacy_of_the_Soviet_Union) multilaterally and bilaterally in particular status in international organizations including the United Nations[^cite_note-143] and debts and assets.[^cite_note-144] and general agreements[^cite_note-145]

Armenia was admitted into the United Nations on 2 March 1992 ([Armenia and the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_and_the_United_Nations)).

The Commonwealth strives to provide, in particular, a [common economic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Economic_Space_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Independent_States) and [freedom of movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_rights_arrangements_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Independent_States). Armenia has signed an agreement on visa recognition[^cite_note-146] and visa-free travel,[^cite_note-147] [Treaty on the Creation of the Economic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Creation_of_the_Economic_Union)[^cite_note-148] and [Agreement on the Establishment of a Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Independent_States_Agreement_on_the_Establishment_of_a_Free_Trade_Area)[^cite_note-149] and many other agreements and treaties.

Armenia faced many challenges during its first years as a [sovereign state](23604120.md). Several Armenian organizations from around the world quickly arrived to offer aid and to participate in the country's early years. From Canada, a group of young students and volunteers under the [CYMA - Canadian Youth Mission to Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYMA_-_Canadian_Youth_Mission_to_Armenia?action=edit\&redlink=1) banner arrived in Ararat Region and became the first youth organization to contribute to the newly independent Republic.

Following the Armenian victory in the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War), both Azerbaijan and [Turkey](11125639.md) closed their borders and imposed a blockade which they retain to this day, severely affecting the economy of the fledgling republic. In October 2009 Turkey and Armenia signed a treaty to normalize relations.

### Ter-Petrosyan presidency (1991–1998)



Inauguration of [Levon Ter-Petrosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Ter-Petrosyan) as president in 1991

[Levon Ter-Petrosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Ter-Petrosyan) was popularly elected the first president of the newly independent Republic of Armenia on 16 October 1991 and re-elected on 22 September 1996.[^cite_note-150] His re-election was marred by allegations of electoral fraud reported by the opposition and supported by many international observers. His popularity waned further as the opposition started blaming him for the economic quagmire that Armenia's post-Soviet economy was in. He was also unpopular with one party in particular, the [Armenian Revolutionary Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), which he banned and jailed on the grounds that the party had a foreign-based leadership—something which was forbidden according to the Armenian Constitution.

Ter-Petrosyan was forced to step down in February 1998 after advocating compromised settlement of the conflict over [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md) which many Armenians regarded as undermining their security. Ter-Petrosyan's key ministers, led by then-Prime Minister [Robert Kocharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kocharyan), refused to accept a peace plan for Karabakh put forward by international mediators in September 1997. The plan, accepted by Ter-Petrosyan and Azerbaijan, called for a "phased" or "step-by-step" settlement of the conflict which would postpone an agreement on Nagorno-Karabakh's status, the main stumbling block. That agreement was to accompany the return of most [Armenian-occupied Azerbaijani territories around Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-occupied_territories_surrounding_Nagorno-Karabakh) and the lifting of the Azerbaijani and Turkish blockades of Armenia. In January 1998, Ter-Petrosyan's ministers forced Ter-Petrosyan to resign.[^cite_note-\:0-151]

### Kocharyan presidency (1998–2008)



[Robert Kocharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kocharyan)

After the resignation of his predecessor [Levon Ter-Petrosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Ter-Petrosyan), [Robert Kocharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kocharyan) was elected Armenia's second president on 30 March 1998, defeating his main rival, [Karen Demirchyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Demirchyan), in an [early presidential election marred by irregularities and violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Armenian_presidential_election) by both sides as reported by international electoral observers. Complaints included that Kocharyan had not been an Armenian citizen for ten years as required by the constitution.[^cite_note-resign-152] In early 1998, Kocharyan rejected the 1997 OSCE Minsk Group peace plan and initiated a new phase of Nagorno-Karabakh negotiations, where Heydar Aliyev and Kocharyan negotiated secret from their publics and senior officials. In 1999, they orally agreed to a land swap that would annex Nagorno-Karabakh to Armenia in exchange for a strip of land connecting Azerbaijan and its exclave of Nakhichvan along the Iranian-Armenian border. In the fall of that year, Aliyev and Kocharyan informed the Minsk Group Co-Chairs of their plan and asked them to put it in writing.[^cite_note-\:0-151]

Weeks later, several opposition leaders in the Armenian Parliament and the Prime Minister of Armenia were killed by gunmen in an episode known as the [1999 Armenian parliament shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_parliament_shooting). Kocharyan himself negotiated with terrorists to lease the MP hostages. It is widely believed by Armenians at large that Kocharyan is responsible for the parliament shooting.[^cite_note-Kaeter-153][^cite_note-154] Thereafter, Kocharyan informed the Minsk Group that he was not able to support the peace deal anymore.[^cite_note-\:0-151]

The [2003 Armenian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Armenian_presidential_election) were held on 19 February and on 5 March 2003. No candidate received a majority in the first round of the election with the [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent) president Kocharyan winning slightly under 50% of the vote. Therefore, a second round was held and Kocharyan defeated [Stepan Demirchyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Demirchyan) with official results showed him winning just over 67% of the vote. In both rounds, electoral observers from the [OSCE](22796.md) reported significant amounts of [electoral fraud](382165.md) by Demirchyan's supporters and numerous supporters of Demirchyan were arrested before the second round took place.[^cite_note-flawed-155]

Demirchyan described the election as having been rigged and called on his supporters to rally against the results.[^cite_note-incumbent-156] Tens of thousands of Armenians protested in the days after the election against the results and called on President Kocharyan to step down.[^cite_note-flawed-155] Kocharyan was sworn in for a second term in early April and the [constitutional court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_court) upheld the election, while recommending that a [referendum](51582.md) be held within a year to confirm the election result.[^cite_note-sworn-13][^cite_note-constitutional-14]



Military situation of Nagorno-Karabakh in May 2016

As president, Kocharyan continued to negotiate a peaceful resolution with Azerbaijani president [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) on the status of Nagorno-Karabakh. Talks between Aliyev and Kocharyan were held in September 2004 in [Astana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur-Sultan), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), on the sidelines of the [CIS](36870.md) summit. Reportedly, one of the suggestions put forward was the withdrawal of Armenian forces from the Azeri territories adjacent to Nagorno-Karabakh, and holding [referendums](51582.md) (plebiscites) in Nagorno-Karabakh and Azerbaijan proper regarding the future status of the region. On 10–11 February 2006, Kocharyan and Aliyev met in [Rambouillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambouillet), [France](5843419.md) to discuss the fundamental principles of a settlement to the conflict, including the withdrawal of troops, formation of international peace keeping troops, and the status of Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-Ghazinyan-157]

Contrary to the initial optimism, the Rambouillet talks did not produce any agreement, with key issues such as the status of Nagorno-Karabakh and whether Armenian troops would withdraw from [Kalbajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalbajar) still being contentious. The next session of the talks was held in March 2006 in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-Ghazinyan-157] Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) applied pressure to both parties to settle the disputes.[^cite_note-158] No progress arose from further meetings in Minsk and Moscow in November 2006.[^cite_note-159]

### Sargsyan presidency (2008–2018)



[Serzh Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan)

[Serzh Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan), then [Prime Minister of Armenia](483914.md) and having President Kocharyan's backing, was viewed as the strongest contender for the post of President of Armenia in the [February 2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Armenian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

Ter-Petrosyan officially announced his candidacy in the [2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Armenian_presidential_election) in a speech in Yerevan on 26 October 2007. He accused Kocharyan's government of massive corruption, involving the theft of "at least three to four billion dollars" over the previous five years. He was critical of the government's claims of strong economic growth and argued that Kocharyan and his Prime Minister, [Serzh Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan), had come to accept a solution to the problem of [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md) that was effectively the same solution that he had proposed ten years earlier. A number of opposition parties have rallied behind him since his return to the political arena, including the [People's Party of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_of_Armenia), led by [Stepan Demirchyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Demirchyan); the Armenian Republic Party, led by [Aram Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aram_Sargsyan);[^cite_note-162] the [Social Democrat Hunchakian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrat_Hunchakian_Party); [Azadakrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadakrum?action=edit\&redlink=1), led by [Jirair Sefilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirair_Sefilian); the [New Times Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Times_Party?action=edit\&redlink=1); and the [Heritage Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_\(Armenia\)), led by [Raffi Hovannisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffi_Hovannisian).[^cite_note-163]



1 March mass protests

Main article: [2008 Armenian presidential election protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Armenian_presidential_election_protests)

Final results from the election, which was held on 19 February 2008, officially showed Sargsyan winning about 53% of the vote, and Ter-Petrosyan in second place with 21.5% of the vote.[^cite_note-164]

Ter-Petrosyan and his supporters accused the government of rigging the election and claimed victory;[^cite_note-165] beginning 20 February, he led [continuous protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Armenian_presidential_election_protests) involving tens of thousands of his supporters in Yerevan.[^cite_note-166]

On the early morning of 1 March, reportedly acting on evidence of firearms in the camp, the authorities moved in to inspect the tents set up by demonstrators. Law enforcement agents then violently dispersed the hundreds of protestors camped in. Ter-Petrosyan was placed under de facto house arrest, not being allowed to leave his home, though the authorities later denied the allegations.[^cite_note-167]

A few hours later, tens of thousands of protestors or more gathered at Miyasnikyan Square to protest the government's act. Police, overwhelmed by the sheer size of the crowd, pulled out. A state of emergency was implemented by President Kocharyan at 5 pm, allowing the army to be moved into the capital. By nightfall, a few thousand protesters had barricaded themselves using commandeered municipal buses. As a result of skirmishes with the police, ten people died, including policemen.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169]

This was followed by mass arrests and purges of prominent members of the opposition, as well as a *de facto ban* on any further anti-government protests. Sargsyan was recognized as legitimate president[^cite_note-HRW1-170][^cite_note-ArmLibMar1a-171]

On 10 October 2009, the Turkish-Armenian protocols on the establishment of diplomatic relations constituted a novelty in Turkish-Armenian relations. Sargsyan accepted the proposal of studying the issue of the Armenian genocide through a commission, and recognized the current Turkish-Armenian border. In 2009–10, the Azerbaijan's military build-up along with increasing war rhetoric and threats risked causing renewed problems in the [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasia).[^cite_note-172]

In 2011, [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Armenian_protests) erupted in Armenia as part of the [revolutionary wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_wave) [sweeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) through [North Africa](21714.md) and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia). Protesters continue to demand an investigation into the 2008 violence, the release of political prisoners, an improvement in socioeconomic conditions, and the institution of democratic reforms. The [Armenian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_National_Congress) and [Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_\(Armenia\)) have been influential in organizing and leading protests.[^cite_note-173]

Between 1 and 5 April 2016, there were renewed clashes between Armenian and Azerbaijani armed forces. (see [2016 Armenian–Azerbaijani clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nagorno-Karabakh_conflict)).

In March 2018, Sargsyan was re-elected Prime Minister, despite opposition protests.[^cite_note-174] After military forces joined the protests on 23 April, Sargsyan resigned his position.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] Former Prime Minister [Karen Karapetyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Karapetyan) succeeded Sargsyan as acting Prime Minister.

### Nikol Pashinyan premiership (2018–present)

See also: [Armenian political crisis (2020–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_political_crisis_\(2020%E2%80%93present\))



[Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md)

In March 2018, Armenian parliament elected [Armen Sarkissian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Sarkissian) as the new president of Armenia. The controversial constitutional reform to reduce presidential power was implemented, while the authority of the prime minister was strengthened.[^cite_note-177] In May 2018, parliament elected opposition leader [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) as the new prime minister. His predecessor Serzh Sargsyan resigned two weeks earlier following widespread anti-government demonstrations.[^cite_note-178]



Map of the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualties_of_the_Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) (2020)

On 27 September 2020, a [full-scale war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) erupted due to the unresolved Nagorno-Karabakh conflict. Both the armed forces of Armenia and Azerbaijan reported military and civilian casualties.[^cite_note-179] A [ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) was signed on 10 November, in which the [occupied territories surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-occupied_territories_surrounding_Nagorno-Karabakh) were handed over to Azerbaijan. [Protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_Armenian_protests) were held in Armenia over this and hundreds stormed the Parliament building in Yerevan. Protests continued throughout November, with demonstrations in Yerevan and other cities demanding the resignation of Pashinyan.[^cite_note-180]

On 25 February 2021, The Armenian military called for Pashinyan to resign. The declaration, which Pashinyan described as a [coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Armenian_political_crisis), caused a political crisis that ended with the Chief of the General Staff of the Armenian Armed Forces [Onik Gasparyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onik_Gasparyan)'s dismissal.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] On 25 April 2021, Pashinyan announced his formal resignation from his post of prime minister to allow snap parliamentary elections in June. He continued to act as interim prime minister in the leadup to the election.[^cite_note-183] His party won the [2021 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Armenian_parliamentary_election), receiving more than half of all votes. Nikol Pashinyan was officially appointed Armenia's prime minister.[^cite_note-184] On 23 January 2022, Armen Sarkissian left the office, saying the constitution does not any more give the president sufficient powers to influence.[^cite_note-185] On 3 March 2022, [Vahagn Khachaturyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahagn_Khachaturyan) was elected as the fifth president of Armenia in the second round of parliamentary vote.[^cite_note-186]
