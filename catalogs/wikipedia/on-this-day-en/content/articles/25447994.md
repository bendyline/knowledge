# Climate change policy of the United States

This article is about the policy regarding climate change. For other aspects of climate change in this country, see [Climate change in the United States](18246585.md).

The [climate change policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_policy) of the [United States](3434750.md) has major impacts on global [climate change](5042951.md) and global [climate change mitigation](2119179.md). This is because the United States is the second largest emitter of [greenhouse gasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gasses) in the world after [China](61886740.md), and is among the [countries with the highest greenhouse gas emissions per person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions_per_capita) in the world. Cumulatively, the United States has emitted over a trillion metric tons of greenhouse gases, more than any country in the world.[^cite_note-1]

Climate change policy is developed at the [state](18618239.md) and [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Federal_government) levels of government.[^cite_note-2] The [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) (EPA) defines climate change as "any significant change in the measures of climate lasting for an extended period of time." Essentially, climate change includes major changes in temperature, precipitation, or wind patterns, as well as other effects, that occur over several decades or longer.[^cite_note-3] The policy with the biggest US investment in [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) is the [Inflation Reduction Act of 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_Reduction_Act_of_2022).

The [politics of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_climate_change) have [polarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarization_in_the_United_States) certain political parties and other organizations.[^cite_note-4] The [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Democratic_Party) advocates for an expansion of climate change mitigation policies whereas the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Republican_Party) tends to advocate for slower change, inaction, or reversal of existing climate change mitigation policies. In 2025, the second Trump administration promoted [climate change denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial) and misinformation and moved to undo the [regulation of greenhouse gases under the Clean Air Act](31102860.md).

Most lobbying on climate policy in the United States is done by corporations that are publicly opposed to reducing carbon emissions.[^cite_note-5]

### International law

The United States, although a [signatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature) to the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol), has neither [ratified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratified) nor withdrawn from the protocol. In 1997, the US Senate voted unanimously under the [Byrd–Hagel Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byrd%E2%80%93Hagel_Resolution) that it was not the sense of the Senate that the United States should be a signatory to the Kyoto Protocol. In 2001, former National Security Adviser [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice), stated that the Protocol "is not acceptable to the Administration or Congress".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In October 2003, [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) published a report titled *An Abrupt Climate Change Scenario and Its Implications for United States National Security* by [Peter Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Schwartz_\(futurist\)) and Doug Randall. The authors conclude by stating, "this report suggests that, because of the potentially dire consequences, the risk of abrupt climate change, although uncertain and quite possibly small, should be elevated beyond a scientific debate to a U.S. [national security](240468.md) concern."[^cite_note-11]

### Congress

In October 2003 and again in June 2005, the [McCain-Lieberman Climate Stewardship Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCain-Lieberman_Climate_Stewardship_Act) failed a vote in the US Senate.[^cite_note-12] In the 2005 vote, Republicans opposed the Bill 49–6, while Democrats supported it 37–10.[^cite_note-13]

In January 2007, Democratic [House Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi) announced she would form a [United States Congress](31756.md) subcommittee to examine global warming.[^cite_note-14] Sen. [Joe Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Lieberman) said, "I'm hot to get something done. It's hard not to conclude that the politics of global warming has changed and a new consensus for action is emerging and it is a bipartisan consensus."[^cite_note-15] [Senators](24909346.md) [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) (I-VT) and [Barbara Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Boxer) (D-CA) introduced the [Global Warming Pollution Reduction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Warming_Pollution_Reduction_Act_of_2007) on January 15, 2007. The measure would provide funding for R\&D on geologic [sequestration of carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CO2_sequestration) (CO<sub>2</sub>), set emissions standards for new vehicles and a renewable fuels requirement for gasoline beginning in 2016, establish energy efficiency and renewable portfolio standards beginning in 2008 and low-carbon electric generation standards beginning in 2016 for electric utilities, and require periodic evaluations by the [National Academy of Sciences](37104.md) to determine whether emissions targets are adequate.[^cite_note-16] However, the bill died in committee. Two more bills, the Climate Protection Act and the Sustainable Energy Act, proposed February 14, 2013, also failed to pass committee.[^cite_note-17]

The [House of Representatives](19468510.md) approved the [American Clean Energy and Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Clean_Energy_and_Security_Act_of_2009) (ACES) on June 26, 2009, by a vote of 219–212, but the bill failed to pass the [Senate](24909346.md).[^cite_note-nyt-18][^cite_note-19]

In March 2011, the [Republicans](32070.md) submitted a [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Tax_Prevention_Act) to the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) that would prohibit the [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) (EPA) from regulating greenhouse gasses as pollutants.[^cite_note-20] As of 2012, the EPA was still overseeing regulation under the Clean Air Act.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In 2019, there were 130 elected congresspeople who had expressed doubt about the science of climate change.[^cite_note-23]

### Clinton administration

Upon the start of his presidency in 1993, [Bill Clinton](3356.md) committed the United States to lowering their [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md) to 1990 levels by 2000 through his biodiversity treaty,[^cite_note-24] reflecting his attempt to return the United States to the global platform of climate policy. Clinton's British Thermal Unit (BTU) Tax and Climate Change Action Plan were also announced within the first year of his presidency, calling for a tax on energy heat content and plans for [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) and [joint implementations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Implementation), respectively.

The Climate Change Action Plan was announced on October 19, 1993. This plan aimed to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to 1990 levels by 2000.[^cite_note-\:02-25] Clinton described this goal as "ambitious but achievable," and called for 44 action steps to achieve this goal. Among these steps were voluntary participation by industry, especially those in the commercial and energy supply fields. Clinton allotted \$1.9 billion to fund this plan from the federal budget and called for an additional \$60 billion funding to come voluntarily businesses and industries.[^cite_note-\:02-25]

The British Thermal Tax proposed by Clinton in early 1993 called for a tax on producers of gasoline, oil, and other fuels based on fuel content in accordance to the British Thermal Unit (BTU). The [British Thermal Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_thermal_unit) is a measure of heat corresponding to the quantity of heat needed to raise the temperature of water by one degree Fahrenheit.[^cite_note-26] The tax also applied to electricity produced by hydro and nuclear power, but exempted renewable energy sources such as geothermal, solar, and wind. The Clinton Administration planned to collect up to \$22.3 billion in revenue from the tax by 1997.[^cite_note-27] The tax was opposed by the energy-intensive industry, who feared that the price increase caused by the tax would make U.S. products undesirable on an international level, and thus was never fully implemented.[^cite_note-28]

In 1994, the U.S. called for a new limit on greenhouse gas emissions post-2000 in at the August 1994 INC-10. They also called for a focus on joint implementation, and for new developing countries to limit their emissions. Environmental groups, including the [Climate Action Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Network) (CAN), critiqued these efforts, questioning U.S. focus on limiting the emissions of other countries when it had not established its own.[^cite_note-29]

The U.S. government under Clinton succeeded in pushing its agenda for joint implementation in the 1995 [Conference of the Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Climate_Change_conference) (COP-1). This victory is noted in the Berlin Mandate of April 1995, which called for developed countries to lead the implementation of national mitigation policies.[^cite_note-30]

Clinton signed the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) on behalf of the United States in 1997, pledging the country to a non-binding 7% reduction of greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-31] He claimed that the agreement was "environmentally strong and economically sound," and expressed a desire for greater involvement in the treaty by developing nations.[^cite_note-32]

In his second term, Clinton announced his FY00 proposal, which allotted funding for a new set of environmental policies. Under this proposal, the President announced a new Clean Air Partnership Fund, new tax incentives and investments, and funding for environmental research of both natural and man-made changes to the climate.[^cite_note-\:12-33]

The Clean Air Partnership Fund was proposed to finance state and local government efforts for greenhouse gas emission reductions in cooperation with EPA. Under this fund, \$200 million was allotted to promote and finance innovation projects meant to reduce air pollution. It also supported the creation of partnerships between the local and federal governments, and private sector.[^cite_note-34]

The Climate Change Technology Initiative provided \$4 billion in tax incentives over a five-year period. The tax credits applied to energy efficient homes and building equipment, implementation of solar energy systems, electric and hybrid vehicles, clean energy, and the power industry.[^cite_note-35] The Climate Change Technology Initiative also provided funding for additional research and development on clean technology, especially in the building, electricity, industry, and transportation sectors.[^cite_note-\:12-33]

### G.W. Bush administration

Main article: [Climate change policy of the George W. Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_policy_of_the_George_W._Bush_administration)

In March 2001, the [George W. Bush Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) announced that it would not implement the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol), an international treaty signed in 1997 in [Kyoto, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto,_Japan) that would require nations to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions, claiming that ratifying the treaty would create economic setbacks in the U.S. and does not put enough pressure to limit emissions from developing nations.[^cite_note-36] In February 2002, President Bush announced his alternative to the Kyoto Protocol, by bringing forth a plan to reduce the intensity of greenhouse gasses by 18 percent over 10 years. The intensity of greenhouse gasses specifically is the ratio of greenhouse gas emissions and economic output, meaning that under this plan, emissions would still continue to grow, but at a slower pace. Bush stated that this plan would prevent the release of 500 million metric tons of greenhouse gases, which is about the equivalent of 70 million cars from the road. This target would achieve this goal by providing [tax credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_credits) to businesses that use renewable energy sources.[^cite_note-37]

The Bush administration has been accused of implementing an industry-formulated disinformation campaign designed to mislead the American public on global warming and to forestall limits on "climate polluters", according to a report in *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* magazine that reviewed hundreds of internal government documents and former government officials.[^cite_note-38] The book *[Hell and High Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_and_High_Water_\(book\))* asserts that there has been a disingenuous, concerted and effective campaign to convince Americans that the science is not proven, or that global warming is the result of natural cycles, and that there needs to be more research. The book claims that, to delay action, industry and government spokesmen suggest falsely that "technology breakthroughs" will eventually save us with [hydrogen cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_cars) and other fixes. It calls on voters to demand immediate government action to curb emissions.[^cite_note-39] Papers presented at an International Scientific Congress on Climate Change, held in 2009 under the sponsorship of the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) in cooperation with nine other universities in the [International Alliance of Research Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alliance_of_Research_Universities) (IARU), maintained that the [climate change skepticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_skepticism) that is so prevalent in the USA[^cite_note-40] "was largely generated and kept alive by a small number of conservative think tanks, often with direct funding from industries having special interests in delaying or avoiding the regulation of greenhouse gas emissions".[^cite_note-41]

According to testimony taken by the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md), the Bush White House pressured American scientists to suppress discussion of global warming[^cite_note-autogenerated1-42][^cite_note-43] "High-quality science" was "struggling to get out", as the Bush administration pressured scientists to tailor their writings on global warming to fit the Bush administration's skepticism, in some cases at the behest of an ex-oil industry lobbyist. "Nearly half of all respondents perceived or personally experienced pressure to eliminate the words 'climate change,' 'global warming' or other similar terms from a variety of communications." Similarly, according to the testimony of senior officers of the [Government Accountability Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Project), the White House attempted to bury the report "National Assessment of the Potential Consequences of Climate Variability and Change", produced by U.S. scientists pursuant to U.S. law,[^cite_note-44] Some U.S. scientists resigned their jobs rather than give in to White House pressure to underreport global warming.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-42] and removed key portions of a [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) report given to the U.S. Senate Environment and Public Works Committee about the dangers to human health of global warming.[^cite_note-45]

The Bush Administration worked to undermine state efforts to mitigate global warming. [Mary Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Peters_\(politician\)), the Transportation Secretary at that time, personally directed US efforts to urge governors and dozens of members of the House of Representatives to block California's first-in-the-nation limits on greenhouse gases from cars and trucks, according to e-mails obtained by Congress.[^cite_note-46]

### Obama administration

Further information: [Environmental policy of the Barack Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy_of_the_Barack_Obama_administration)

[New Energy for America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Energy_for_America) was a plan to invest in renewable energy, reduce reliance on foreign oil, address the global climate crisis, and make coal a less competitive energy source. It was announced during [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s presidential campaign. A form of it was signed into law in February 2009 as the [American Reinvestment and Recovery Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Reinvestment_and_Recovery_Act), which invests \$26.6 billion in renewable energy, \$19.9 billion in energy efficiency and conservation, \$18.1 billion in transit and [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail), \$10.5 billion in [electric power transmission](38824.md) upgrades, \$6.1 billion in [alternative fuel vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_fuel_vehicle), \$3.4 billion in [carbon capture and storage](3679268.md), and at least \$600 million in [Superfund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfund), underground fuel tank and [brownfield land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownfield_land) cleanups.[^cite_note-Romer_2010-47][^cite_note-EPA_2015-48] An estimate in 2016 by Obama's [Council of Economic Advisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Economic_Advisers) found the ARRA boosted GDP by 2-3%, supported 900,000 clean energy job-years and leveraged \$150 billion in private sector clean energy investments.[^cite_note-CEA_2016-49]

On November 17, 2008, President-elect [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) proposed, in a talk recorded for YouTube, that the US should enter a [cap and trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_and_trade) system to limit [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming).[^cite_note-50] The [American Clean Energy and Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Clean_Energy_and_Security_Act), a cap and trade bill, was passed on June 26, 2009, in the House of Representatives, but was not passed by the Senate.

President Obama established a new office in the White House, the [White House Office of Energy and Climate Change Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Office_of_Energy_and_Climate_Change_Policy), and selected [Carol Browner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Browner) as Assistant to the President for Energy and Climate Change. On January 27, 2009, Secretary of State Hillary Clinton appointed [Todd Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Stern) as the department's Special Envoy for Climate Change.[^cite_note-51] Clinton said, "we are sending an unequivocal message that the United States will be energetic, focused, strategic and serious about addressing global climate change and the corollary issue of clean energy."[^cite_note-reut012609-52] Stern said that, "The time for denial, delay and dispute is over.... We can only meet the climate challenge with a response that is genuinely global. We will need to engage in vigorous, dramatic diplomacy."[^cite_note-reut012609-52]

In February 2009, Stern said that the US would take a lead role in the formulation of a new climate change treaty in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) in December 2009.&#x20;

President Obama said in September 2009 that if the international community would not act swiftly to deal with climate change that "we risk consigning future generations to an irreversible catastrophe... our prosperity, our health, and our safety are in jeopardy, and the time we have to reverse this tide is running out." [^cite_note-53] In 2010, the president said, similarly, that it was time for the United States "to aggressively accelerate" its transition from oil to alternative sources of energy and vowed to push for quick action on climate change legislation, arguably seeking to harness the deepening anger over [the oil spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_oil_spill) in the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md).[^cite_note-54]

The [2010 United States federal budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_federal_budget) proposed to support clean energy development with a 10-year investment of US\$15 billion per year, generated from the sale of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions credits. Under the proposed cap-and-trade program, all GHG emissions credits would be auctioned off, generating an estimated \$83 billion in new revenue by FY 2019.[^cite_note-55]

New rules for power plants were proposed March 2012.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

In the US and China's Sunnylands Summit on June 8, 2013, President Obama and [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) worked in accordance for the first time, formulating a landmark agreement to reduce both production and consumption of [hydrofluorocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofluorocarbons) (HFCs). This agreement had the unofficial goal of decreasing roughly 90 gigatons of CO<sub>2</sub> by 2050 and implementation was to be led by the institutions created under the [Montreal Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Protocol), while progress was tracked using the reported emissions that were mandated under the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol). The Obama administration viewed HFCs as a "serious climate mitigation concern."[^cite_note-58]

On March 31, 2015, the Obama administration formally submitted the US [Intended Nationally Determined Contribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intended_Nationally_Determined_Contributions) (INDC) for greenhouse gas emissions (GHGs) to the [United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change](31898.md) (UNFCCC). The United States committed to reducing emissions 26-28% below 2005 levels by 2025, a reflection of the Obama administration's goal to convert the U.S. economy into one of low-carbon reliance.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

In 2015, Obama also announced the [Clean Power Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Power_Plan), which was the final version of regulations originally proposed by the EPA the previous year, and which pertained to carbon dioxide emissions from power plants.[^cite_note-61] Even though it had never been fully implemented, it was replaced by the Trump administration's Affordable Clean Energy rule in 2019,[^cite_note-thehill.com-62] itself voided by the [DC Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Circuit_Court_of_Appeals) in 2021.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

In the same year, President Obama announced his aim for a 40-45% reduction below 2012 levels in [methane](18582230.md) emissions by 2025. In March 2016, the President would later solidify this goal in an agreement with Prime Minister of Canada, [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau), stating that the two federal governments will jointly work together to reduce methane emissions in North America, coordinating particularly on research and development and standards creation.[^cite_note-65]

On May 12, 2016, the administration released an Information Collection Request (ICR), requiring all methane-emitting operations to provide emission levels reports to EPA analysts to deal with high-emitting sources. New standards set emission limits for methane; reductions were to be made through transition to newer and cleaner production equipment, fixed monitoring of leaks at operation sites using innovative techniques, and the capturing of emissions from [hydraulic fracturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing).[^cite_note-66]

A September 2016 study from [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory) analyzed a set of definite and proposed climate change policies for the United States and found that these were insufficient to meet the US [intended nationally determined contribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intended_Nationally_Determined_Contributions) (INDC) under the 2015/2016 [Paris Agreement](30242372.md). Additional [greenhouse gas](21350772.md) reduction measures were required to meet this international commitment.[^cite_note-greenblatt-and-wei-2016-67][^cite_note-68]

An October 2016 report compared US government spending on climate security and military security and found the latter to be 28 times greater. The report estimated that public sector spending of \$55 billion was needed to tackle climate change. The 2017 national budget contained \$21 billion for such expenditures, leaving a shortfall of \$34 billion that could be recouped by scrapping underperforming weapons programs. The report recommended the [F-35 fighter](11812.md) and [close-to-shore combat ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship) projects as possible targets.[^cite_note-pennington-2016-69][^cite_note-ips-2016-70][^cite_note-pemberton-etal-2016-71]

#### Transportation

##### President's 21st century clean transportation plan

In June 2015, the Obama administration released the President's 21st Century Clean Transportation Plan with the goal of reducing carbon pollution by converting the nation's century old infrastructure into one based on clean energy. The President's multibillion-dollar proposal provided incentives to reduce reliance on international oil and [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels). It involved adding \$20 billion per year transit and [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) investments, \$10 billion per year to improved regional transportation planning reform, and \$2 billion per year to alternative fuel and autonomous vehicle research.[^cite_note-72]

Previously, similar investments in transportation were supported by the [Fixing America's Surface Transportation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixing_America's_Surface_Transportation_Act) (FAST), an act passed in December 2015 by the Obama administration. FAST was formulated to reduce traffic and increase the quality of air by reducing emissions, yet it proved to be slow in gathering infrastructure investments.[^cite_note-73] Thus, the President proposed a tax on oil of \$10 per barrel to pay for it. The plan failed in the House due to the Republican majority.[^cite_note-74]

##### Climate Action Plan progress report

In June 2015, under Obama's Climate Action Plan Progress Report, the EPA announced that they were going to propose new standards for both medium and heavy-duty engines and vehicles, building off standards that were already enacted. These proposals were projected to decrease emissions by 270 million metric tons and save vehicle owners around \$50 billion in fuel costs.[^cite_note-\:04-75]

The Climate Action Plan progress report also addressed aircraft, transit, and maritime emissions. The EPA proposed a rule tightening carbon pollution standards in civil aviation. Additionally, under the [Next Generation Air Transportation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Air_Transportation_System), the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) worked with the aviation industry on lower-emissions technologies, the [Maritime Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Maritime_Administration) oversaw an increase of investments into more fuel-efficient ships, and incentives made it possible for buses and other forms of transit to switch to other forms of energy such as natural gas and electric.[^cite_note-\:04-75]

##### EPA tailpipe emissions standards

In April 2010, the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the Department of Transportation's National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) formulated a national program that would finalize new standards for model year 2012 through 2016 most consumer road vehicles. With these new standards, vehicles were required to meet an average emissions level of 250 grams of carbon dioxide per mile by model year 2016. This was the first time the EPA had taken measures to regulate vehicular GHG emissions under the Clean Air Act.

Additionally, the administration established [Corporate Average Fuel Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_average_fuel_economy) (CAFE) standards under the Energy Policy and Conservation Act.[^cite_note-76]

In August 2012, the administration expanded on these standards for model years 2017 through 2025 vehicles, issuing final rules and standards that were to result in a 163 gram emission per mile by model year 2025.[^cite_note-77]

#### Appointment of energy industry-affiliated officials

As president, Trump appointed [Scott Pruitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Pruitt), a [climate change denialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial) with a history of close ties to energy industry interests,[^cite_note-NYT8Dec-82][^cite_note-83] to head the EPA. While serving as Attorney General of Oklahoma, Pruitt removed Oklahoma's environmental protection unit and sued the EPA a total of fourteen times, thirteen of which involved "industry players" as co-parties.[^cite_note-84] He was confirmed to head the EPA on February 17, 2017, with a 52–46 vote[^cite_note-85] and resigned on July 5, 2018, amid ethics violation controversies. Trump then nominated [Andrew Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_R._Wheeler), an attorney who worked as a [coal lobbyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_lobby).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-lobbyist-87][^cite_note-88] Wheeler was confirmed as head of the EPA on February 28, 2019, by a 52–47 vote.[^cite_note-89]

Trump nominated [Rex W. Tillerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rex_Tillerson), the former CEO and chairman of [Exxon Mobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil), the multinational [oil and gas giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_oil), as [Secretary of State](32293.md). His nomination was confirmed on February 1, 2017, by a 56–43 vote.[^cite_note-90] He was fired on March 31, 2018, and replaced by [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo).

#### Pipeline expansion and attempts at major cuts to EPA

After less than a week as president, on January 24, 2017, Trump issued an executive order that removed barriers from the [Keystone XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Pipeline) and [Dakota Access Pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Access_Pipeline), making it easier for the companies sponsoring them to continue with production.[^cite_note-91] On March 28, 2017, President Trump signed an [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(United_States\)) aimed towards boosting the [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) industry. The executive order rolls back on Obama-era climate regulations on the coal industry in order to grow the coal sector and create new American jobs. The [White House](33057.md) indicated that any climate change policies that they deem hinder the growth of American jobs will not be pursued. In addition, the executive order rolled back on six Obama-made orders aimed at reducing climate change and carbon dioxide emissions and called for a review of the [Clean Power Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Power_Plan).[^cite_note-92]

#### Suppression and politicization of climate science

In his first year in office, President Trump ordered the Environmental Protection Agency to remove references to climate change from its website, suppressed government publication of scientific reports showing the threat of climate change and the effectiveness of [renewable energy](25784.md), and [politicized decisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politicization_of_science) made at the EPA.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] In a similar vein, the Trump Administration prevented scientists from reporting to Congress regarding the threat of climate change and the urgent need to address it.[^cite_note-98] However, buried inside a 500-page [Environmental Impact Statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Impact_Statement) (EIP) published by the [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration](989530.md), the Trump administration acknowledged that, without a course correction, the planet is on track for global average temperature warming by approximately four degrees Celsius by the end of the century, compared with preindustrial levels. Such warming would be catastrophic for organized human life, according to scientists. The EIP supports the U.S. government's decision to maintain without increase [fuel-efficiency standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_standards) for cars and other vehicles.[^cite_note-99]

In his [budget proposal for 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_federal_budget), President Trump proposed cutting the EPA's budget by 31% (reducing its current \$8.2 billion to \$5.7 billion). Had it passed, it would have been the lowest EPA budget in 40 years adjusted for inflation,[^cite_note-100] but Congress did not approve it. Trump tried again unsuccessfully in his [budget proposal for 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_United_States_federal_budget) to cut EPA funding by 26%.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] The EPA provides technical assistance to cities as they update their infrastructure to adapt to climate change, according to Joel Scheraga, the EPA senior advisor for climate change adaptation who has worked for the EPA for three decades. Scheraga said he was working with a reduced staff under the Trump administration.[^cite_note-103]

#### Environmental justice

The shift in direction of environmental policy in the United States under the Trump administration has led to a change in the [environmental justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_justice) sector. On March 9, 2017, Mustafa Ali, a leader of the environmental justice office at EPA, resigned over proposed cuts to the agency's environmental justice program. The preliminary budget proposals would cut the environmental justice office's budget by 1/4, causing a 20% reduction in its workforce. The program is one of a dozen vulnerable to losing all governmental funding.[^cite_note-104]

#### Inflation Reduction Act

The [Inflation Reduction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_Reduction_Act) was a [reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconciliation_\(United_States_Congress\)) bill that was the largest investment in climate change mitigation in US history to date, setting out provisions to invest in increasing [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_the_United_States) and [electrifying areas of the US economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition). The legislation, signed into law by Biden on August 16, 2022, invests approximately \$400 billion to climate-related projects, primarily in the form of tax credits for consumers and private businesses. The majority of these investments is intended to increase the amount of wind and solar energy in the United States grid by providing tax incentives to [renewable energy](25784.md) producers, as well as companies that manufacture batteries and wind and solar power components.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-Morrison_Foerster_2022-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] The Act may also invest \$28–48 billion in building retrofits and energy efficiency, \$23–436 billion in clean transportation, \$22–26 billion in [environmental justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_justice), land use, air pollution reduction and resilience, and \$3–21 billion in [sustainable agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_agriculture).[^cite_note-Beachy_2022-146][^cite_note-BCG_2022-08-16-147][^cite_note-Credit_Suisse_2022-148]

However, the law also requires that for federal lands, oil and gas projects be considered before wind and solar rights of way, even as it brought about the aforementioned royalty rate increases.[^cite_note-Hart_2023-149]

The law explicitly defines carbon dioxide as an [air pollutant](10934212.md) under the [Clean Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_\(United_States\)) to make the Act's [EPA](58666.md) enforcement provisions harder to challenge in court,[^cite_note-NYTimes_20220822-150] and created a first-of-its-kind [green bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_bank), among a wide variety of other provisions to cut pollution.[^cite_note-The_White_House_GGRF_announcement_2024-151] According to several independent analyses, the law is projected to reduce 2030 U.S. [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States) to 40% below 2005 levels.[^cite_note-152]

By the Act's first anniversary, emissions projections had begun to shift. According to Rhodium Group and the [World Economic Forum](172185.md), in the first year of implementation, the Act had a significant impact on the environment: their expectations for [GHG emissions](9528025.md) reductions by 2030, relative to 2005 levels, moved from 17%-30% to 29%-42%, and to 32%-51% by 2035. The [EPA](58666.md) used dozens of millions of dollars to improve [air quality](10934212.md) and hundreds of millions for environmental justice and local climate plans, [NOAA](37876.md) spent hundreds of millions to adapt coastlines to climate change, and more than \$1 billion was allocated to equitable access to [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) reductions.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

### California

As the most populous state in the [United States](3434750.md),[^cite_note-168] [California](5407.md)'s climate policies influence both [global climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_climate_change) and federal climate policy. In line with the views of [climate scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_scientist), the state of California has progressively passed emission-reduction legislation.

California has taken legislative steps in the hope of mitigating the risks of potential effects of [climate change in California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_California) by incentives and plans for [clean cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_car), [renewable energy](25784.md), and [pollution controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution#Pollution_control) on industry.[^cite_note-169] In California, climate change policy has been developed through both the executive and legislative branches of the state government.[^cite_note-170] Many of the policies have specifically targeted greenhouse gas emissions, which have been shown to raise global temperatures and skew natural rhythms.[^cite_note-171]

One of the most notable pieces of climate legislation in California was Assembly Bill 32, the [Global Warming Solutions Act of 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Warming_Solutions_Act_of_2006). This landmark piece of legislation required many actors in California's economy to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions to 1990 levels by 2020.[^cite_note-Climate_change_policy_of_California_auto3-172] The bill also appointed the California Air Resources Board (CARB) to devise policies and mechanisms for reaching the goal.[^cite_note-Climate_change_policy_of_California_auto3-172] CARB ultimately implemented the state's cap-and-trade program, a type of [emissions trading](37104.md), the first such program in the United States.[^cite_note-173] California was able to reach the emissions target four years ahead of schedule, in 2016.[^cite_note-174]



Though Texas and California generate the most wind + solar power of all states, various other states generate more wind + solar power *per capita*.

[^cite_note-Climate_change_policy_of_California_InsideClimateNews_20230309-175]

California (the world's fifth largest economy) has long been seen as the state-level pioneer in environmental issues related to global warming and has shown some leadership in the last four years. On July 22, 2002, Governor [Gray Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_Davis) approved AB 1493, a bill directing the [California Air Resources Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Air_Resources_Board) to develop standards to achieve the maximum feasible and cost-effective reduction of greenhouse gases from motor vehicles. Now the California Vehicle Global Warming law, it requires automakers to reduce emissions by 30% by 2016. Although it has been challenged in the courts by the automakers, support for the law is growing as other states have adopted similar legislation. On September 7, 2002, Governor Davis approved a bill requiring the [California Climate Action Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Climate_Action_Registry) to adopt procedures and protocols for project reporting and carbon sequestration in forests. (SB 812. Approved by Governor Davis on September 7, 2002) California has convened an interagency task force, housed at the California Energy Commission, to develop these procedures and protocols. Staff are currently seeking input on a host of technical questions.

In June 2005, Governor [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger) signed an executive order calling for the following reductions in state greenhouse gas emissions: to reduce GHG emissions to 2000 levels by 2010, to reduce GHG emissions to 1990 levels by 2020, to reduce GHG emissions to 80 percent below 1990 levels by 2050.[^cite_note-Climate_change_policy_of_California_S-3-05-176] Measures to meet these targets include tighter automotive emissions standards, and requirements for renewable energy as a proportion of electricity production. The [Union of Concerned Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Concerned_Scientists) has calculated that by 2020, drivers would save \$26 billion per year if California's automotive standards were implemented nationally.[^cite_note-177]

On August 30, 2006, Schwarzenegger and the [California Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Legislature) reached an agreement on AB32, the [Global Warming Solutions Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Warming_Solutions_Act). He signed the bill into law on September 27, 2006, saying, "We simply must do everything we can in our power to slow down global warming before it is too late... The science is clear. The global warming debate is over." The Act caps California's greenhouse gas emissions at 1990 levels by 2020, and institutes a mandatory emissions reporting system to monitor compliance, representing the first enforceable statewide program in the U.S. to cap all GHG emissions from major industries that includes penalties for non-compliance. It required the State Air Resources Board to establish a program for statewide greenhouse gas emissions reporting and to monitor and enforce compliance with this program, authorizes the state board to adopt market-based compliance mechanisms[^cite_note-178] including cap-and-trade, and allows a one-year extension of the targets under extraordinary circumstances.[^cite_note-Climate_change_policy_of_California_gov.ca.gov-179] Thus far, flexible mechanisms in the form of project based offsets have been suggested for five main project types. A [carbon project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_project) would create offsets by showing that it has reduced carbon dioxide and equivalent gases. The project types include: manure management, forestry, building energy, SF<sub>6</sub>, and [landfill gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landfill_gas) capture.

Additionally, on September 26, Governor Schwarzenegger signed SB 107, which requires California's three major biggest utilities – [Pacific Gas & Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Gas_&_Electric), [Southern California Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California_Edison), and [San Diego Gas & Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Gas_&_Electric) – to produce at least 20% of their electricity using renewable sources by 2010. This shortens the time span originally enacted by Gov. Davis in September 2002 to increase utility renewable energy sales 1% annually to 20% by 2017.

Gov. Schwarzenegger also announced he would seek to work with Prime Minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) of Great Britain, and various other international efforts to address global warming, independently of the federal government.[^cite_note-180]

### Connecticut

The state of Connecticut passed a number of bills on global warming in the early to mid 1990s, including—in 1990—the first state global warming law to require specific actions for reducing CO<sub>2</sub>. Connecticut is one of the states that agreed, under the auspices of the New England Governors and Eastern Canadian Premiers (NEG/ECP), to a voluntary short-term goal of reducing regional greenhouse gas emissions to 1990 levels by 2010 and by 10 percent below 1990 levels by 2020. The NEG/ECP long-term goal is to reduce emissions to a level that [eliminates any dangerous threats to the climate](2119179.md)—a goal scientists suggest will require reductions 75 to 85 percent below current levels.[^cite_note-181] These goals were announced in August 2001. The state has also acted to require additions in renewable electric generation by 2009.[^cite_note-182]

### Minnesota

In 2007, the state of Minnesota implemented a goal of 80% greenhouse gas emissions reductions from 2005 levels, by 2030. However, under the state's 2022 Climate Action Framework, the goal was revised to 50% by 2030, and 100% by 2050.[^cite_note-184]

Under a Democratic trifecta headed by [Tim Walz](2216593.md), from 2023 to 2024, the state enacted 40 Framework initiatives to deal with climate change. These include a clean energy standard of 100% by 2040, a [green-collar worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-collar_worker) apprenticeship program, a new [green bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_bank), improvements to electric vehicle charging networks, [weatherization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weatherization) and building-related [embedded emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_emissions) programs, and increased financial aid to [peatland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peatland), stream and forest conservation and pollinator breeders.[^cite_note-185] The state government also passed a \$9 billion transportation package focused on raising the gas tax and improving transit, biking and walking,[^cite_note-Brasuell_2023-186] and implemented a bipartisan energy permitting reform bill.[^cite_note-Harrison_2024-187][^cite_note-Utility_Dive_2024-188] It implemented California's stricter tailpipe emissions standards for cars,[^cite_note-Karnowwski_2021-189] and set a goal of 20% electric vehicles as a share of all cars by 2030.[^cite_note-Karnowski_2022-190]

However, the state government came under criticism for its lax approach to [regulatory capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_capture) regarding climate action in the agricultural[^cite_note-Wagenius_2023-191] and iron processing[^cite_note-Hazzard_2024-192] sectors, and for its fast-tracking of the [Line 3 pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_pipeline) expansion and [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality)-filled response to the indigenous-led [Stop Line 3 protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_Line_3_protests).[^cite_note-Baram_2024-193][^cite_note-Atkin_Samuelson_2024-194]

In August 2009, Governor [David Paterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Paterson) created the New York State Climate Action Council (NYSCAC) and tasked them with creating a direct action plan. According to the 2015 New York State Energy Plan, renewable sources, which include wind, hydropower, solar, geothermal, and sustainable biomass, have the potential to meet 40 percent of the state's energy needs by 2030. As of 2018, sustainable energy use comprises 11 percent of all energy usage.[^cite_note-\:3-196] The [New York State Energy Research and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Energy_Research_and_Development_Authority) offers incentives in the form of grants and loans to its residents to adopt renewable energy technologies and create renewable energy businesses.

Other state climate change mitigation laws have gone into effect. The [net metering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_metering) laws make it easier for both residents and businesses to use solar power by feeding unused energy back into electrical fields and receive credit from their power suppliers. Although one version was released in 1997, it was exclusively limited to residential systems using up to 10 kilowatts of power. However, on June 1, 2011, the laws were expanded to include farm and non-residential buildings.[^cite_note-197] The [Renewable Energy Portfolio Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_portfolio_standard) set a statewide target for renewable energy and offered incentives to residents to use the new technologies.[^cite_note-\:3-196]

In June 2018, the state announced its first major update in over two decades to its Environmental Quality Review (EQR) regulations. The update involves streamlining the environmental review process and encouraging renewable energy. It also expanded the Type II actions, or "list of actions not subject to further review", including [green infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_infrastructure) upgrades and retrofits. Furthermore, solar arrays are set to be installed in sites like brownfields, wastewater treatment facilities, and land zoned for industry. The regulations will take effect on January 1, 2019.[^cite_note-\:1-195]

#### New York State Energy Plan

In 2014, Governor [Andrew Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo) enforced the state's hallmark energy policy, Reforming the Energy Vision. This involves building a new network that will connect the central grid with clean, locally generated power. The method for this undertaking falls to the Energy Plan, a comprehensive plan to build a clean, resilient, affordable energy system for all New Yorkers. It will foster "economic prosperity and environmental stewardship" and cooperation between government and industry. Concrete goals thus far include a 40% reduction in greenhouse gas from 1990 levels, electricity sourced from 50% of renewable energy sources, and a 600 billion BTU increase in statewide energy efficiency.[^cite_note-198]

### Regional initiatives

Main article: [Regional climate change initiatives in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_climate_change_initiatives_in_the_United_States)

#### Clean Energy Standards

Clean Energy Standard (CES) policies are policies which favor lowering [non-renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-renewable_resource) emissions and increasing [renewable energy](25784.md) use. They are helping to drive the transition to cleaner energy, by building upon existing energy portfolio standards, and could be applied broadly at the federal level and developed more acutely at the regional and state levels. CES policies have had success at the federal level, gaining bipartisan support during the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_Administration). Iowa was the first state to adopt CES policies, and now a majority of states have adopted CES policies.[^cite_note-200] Similar to CES policies, [Renewable Portfolio Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_portfolio_standard_\(United_States\)) (RPS) are standards set in place to ensure a greater integration of renewable energies in state and regional energy portfolios. Both CES and RPS are helping increase the use of clean and renewable energies in the United States.

##### Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative

In 2003, [New York State](8210131.md) proposed and attained commitments from nine [Northeast](431669.md) states to form a cap and trade [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) emissions program for power generators, called the [Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Greenhouse_Gas_Initiative) (RGGI). This program launched on January 1, 2009, with the aim to reduce the carbon "budget" of each state's electricity generation sector to 10 percent below their 2009 allowances by 2018.[^cite_note-201] 11 Northeastern US states are involved in the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative,[^cite_note-202] It is believed that the state-level program will apply pressure on the federal government to support [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol).[^cite_note-203] The Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative (RGGI) is a [cap and trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_and_trade) system for CO<sub>2</sub> emissions from power plants in the member states. Emission permit auctioning began in September 2008, and the first three-year compliance period began on January 1, 2009.[^cite_note-rggi1-204] Proceeds will be used to promote energy conservation and renewable energy.[^cite_note-205] The system affects fossil fuel power plants with 25 MW or greater generating capacity ("compliance entities").[^cite_note-rggi1-204] Since 2005, the participating states have collectively seen an over 45% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by RGGI-affected power plants. This has resulted in cleaner air, better health, and economic growth.[^cite_note-206]

- **Participating states**: [Maine](19977.md), [New Hampshire](21134.md), [Vermont](32578.md), [Connecticut](6466.md), [New York](8210131.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [Maryland](18858.md), [Rhode Island](25410.md)

- **Observer states and regions**: [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia), [Quebec](7954867.md), [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), [Ontario](22218.md).

### Litigation by states

Several lawsuits have been filed over global warming. In 2007 the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) ruled in *[Massachusetts v. Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_v._Environmental_Protection_Agency)* that the [Clean Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_\(United_States\)) gives the [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) (EPA) the authority to regulate greenhouse gases, such as [tailpipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailpipe#Tailpipe_and_its_tip) emissions. A similar approach was taken by California Attorney General [Bill Lockyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Lockyer) who filed a lawsuit *California v. General Motors Corp.* to force car manufacturers to reduce vehicles' emissions of carbon dioxide. A third case, *Comer v. Murphy Oil*, was filed by Gerald Maples, a trial attorney in Mississippi, in an effort to force fossil fuel and chemical companies to pay for damages caused by global warming.[^cite_note-214]

In June 2011, the [United States Supreme Court](31737.md) overturned 8–0 a [U.S. appeals court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._appeals_court) ruling against five big power utility companies, brought by U.S. states, New York City, and [land trusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_trust), attempting to force cuts in [United States greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States) regarding [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming). The decision gives deference to reasonable interpretations of the [United States Clean Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_\(United_States\)) by the [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md).[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217]

*[Held v. Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Held_v._Montana)* was the first [constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law) climate lawsuit to go to [trial](3276812.md) in the United States, on June 12, 2023.[^cite_note-Guardian_20230612-218] The case was filed in March 2020 by sixteen youth residents of [Montana](19978.md), then aged 2 through 18,[^cite_note-Guardian_20220413-219] who argued that the state's support of the [fossil fuel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) had worsened the [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change) on their lives, thus denying their [right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_right) to a "clean and healthful environment in Montana for present and future generations"[^cite_note-MontanaConstitution-220]<sup>\:Art. IX, § 1</sup> as required by the [Constitution of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Montana).[^cite_note-NYTimes_20230324-221] On August 14, 2023, the trial court judge ruled in the youth plaintiffs' favor, though the state indicated it would appeal the decision.[^cite_note-APNews_20230814-222] Montana's Supreme Court heard oral arguments on July 10, 2024, its seven justices taking the case under [advisement](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/advisement).[^cite_note-APnews_02240610-223] On December 18, 2024, the Montana Supreme Court upheld the county court ruling.[^cite_note-NYTimes_20241218-224]

In June 2023, [Multnomah County, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County,_Oregon) filed a lawsuit against seven defendants, including Exxon Mobil, Shell, Chevron and the Western States Petroleum Association, for materially contributing to the [2021 heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_North_America_heat_wave) in the Pacific Northwest, which is thought to have killed hundreds of people.[^cite_note-NBCnews_20230622-225] According to the Center for Climate Integrity, the Multnomah County lawsuit is the 36th action filed against fossil fuel interests for worsening the effects of climate change.[^cite_note-NBCnews_20230622-225]

### Democratic Party

In its [2016 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Democratic_National_Convention), the Democratic Party views climate change as "an urgent threat and a defining challenge of our time." Democrats are dedicated to "curbing the effects of climate change, protecting America's natural resources, and ensuring the quality of our air, water, and land for current and future generations."[^cite_note-Planet_Experts-231]

With respect to climate change, the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) believes that "carbon dioxide, methane, and other greenhouse gasses should be priced to reflect their negative externalities, and to accelerate the transition to a clean energy economy and help meet our climate goals."[^cite_note-auto-235] Democrats are also committed to "implementing, and extending smart pollution and efficiency standards, including the [Clean Power Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Power_Plan), fuel economy standards for automobiles and heavy-duty vehicles, building codes and appliance standards."[^cite_note-auto-235]

Democrats emphasize the importance of [environmental justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_justice). The party calls attention to the [environmental racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_racism) as the climate change has disproportionately impacted low-income and minority communities, tribal nations and [Alaska Native villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_native_villages). The party believes "clean air and clean water are basic rights of all Americans."[^cite_note-auto-235]

Despite these claims, there is evidence that some individual Democrats in Congress did not support key pieces of environmental legislation, such as the [American Clean Energy and Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Clean_Energy_and_Security_Act), due to the local relevance and influence of carbon-intensive industries.[^cite_note-236]

### Republican Party

The [Republican Party](32070.md) has varied views on climate change.[^cite_note-237] The most recent [2016 Republican Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_National_Convention) denies the existence of climate change and dismisses scientists' efforts of easing global warming.

The [GOP](32070.md) does champion some energy initiatives following: opening up public lands and the ocean for further oil exploration; fast tracking permits for oil and gas wells; and hydraulic fracturing. It also supports dropping "restrictions to allow responsible development of nuclear energy."[^cite_note-Planet_Experts-231]

In 2014, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) proposed a series of [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) (EPA) regulations, known as the [Clean Power Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Power_Plan) that would reduce carbon pollution from coal-fired power plants. The [Republican Party](32070.md) has viewed these efforts as a "war on coal" and has significantly opposed them. Instead, it advocates building the [Keystone XL pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Pipeline), outlawing a carbon tax, and stopping all fracking regulations.

[Donald Trump](4848272.md), the president of the United States, has said that "climate change is a hoax invented by and for Chinese."[^cite_note-238] During his political campaign, he blamed China for doing little helping the environment on the earth, but he seemed to ignore many projects organized by China to slow [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming). While Trump's words might be counted as his campaign strategy to attract voters, it brought concerns from the left about environmental justice.

From 2008 to 2017, the Republican Party went from "debating how to combat human-caused climate change to arguing that it does not exist," according to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*.[^cite_note-239] In 2011 "more than half of the Republicans in the House and three-quarters of Republican senators" said "that the threat of global warming, as a man-made and highly threatening phenomenon, is at best an exaggeration and at worst an utter 'hoax'", according to [Judith Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Warner) writing *[The New York Times Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Magazine)*.[^cite_note-nyt20110227-240] In 2014, more than 55% of congressional Republicans were [climate change deniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial), according to [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News).[^cite_note-msnbc20140512-241][^cite_note-charlestongazette20141222-242] According to [PolitiFact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolitiFact) in May 2014, "...relatively few Republican members of Congress...accept the prevailing scientific conclusion that global warming is both real and man-made...eight out of 278, or about 3 percent."[^cite_note-\:4-232][^cite_note-243] A 2017 study by the [Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_American_Progress#Center_for_American_Progress_Action_Fund) of [climate change denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial) in the [United States Congress](31756.md) found 180 members who deny the [science behind climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_opinion_on_climate_change); all were Republicans.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]

However, many Republicans see ways to address the issue of climate change using conservative principles. In 2019, Luntz Global released polling indicating that a majority of Republican voters would support government action on emissions reduction, and worry the [GOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOP)'s position on climate hurts its standing within young voting blocs.[^cite_note-246] Also in 2019, several Republican legislators broke with the party to advocate taking action on climate change, with market-based solutions rather than traditional regulations.[^cite_note-247] Additionally, groups of younger Republicans began advocacy efforts in favor of a climate policy response, such as [Citizens for Responsible Energy Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_for_Responsible_Energy_Solutions) and [Young Conservatives For Carbon Dividends](https://yccdaction.org/) (YCCD).[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250] [republicEn.org](https://www.republicen.org/) is a conservative non-profit in support of a national, revenue-neutral [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax).

By 2022, a group of Republican [state treasurers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_treasurer) had formed which actively opposed private sector climate initiatives. The group also raised objections to government appointments and regulations due to climate-related issues.[^cite_note-251]

The [Green Party of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_the_United_States) advocates for reductions of greenhouse gas emissions and increased government regulation.

In 2010 Platform on Climate Change, the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greens/Green_Party_USA) leaders released their proposal to solve and integrate the problem and policy of climate change with six parts. Second, [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_the_United_States) advocate economic policies to create a safer atmosphere. Third, countries with few contributions should pay for adaption to climate change. Fourth, [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_the_United_States) champion more efficient but low-cost public transportation system and less energy demand economy. Fifth, the government should train more workers to operate and develop the new, green energy economy. Last, [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_the_United_States) think necessary to transform commercial plants where have uncontrolled animal feeding operations and overuse of fossil fuel to health farms with organic practices.[^cite_note-252]

### Libertarian Party

In its 2016 platform, the [Libertarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_\(United_States\)) states that "competitive free markets and property rights stimulate the technological innovations and behavioral changes required to protect our environment and ecosystems."[^cite_note-253] The Libertarians believe the government has no rights or responsibilities to regulate and control the environmental issues. The environment and natural resources belong to the individuals and private corporations.

### Constitution Party

The Constitution Party, in the 2014 Platform, states that "it is our responsibility to be prudent, productive, and efficient stewards of God's natural resources."[^cite_note-auto1-254] On the issue of [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming), it says that "[globalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalism) are using the global warming threat to gain more control via worldwide sustainable development."[^cite_note-255] According to the party, [eminent domain](144089.md) is unlawful because "under no circumstances may the federal government take private property, by means of rules and regulations which preclude or substantially reduce the productive use of the property, even with just compensation."[^cite_note-auto1-254]

In regards to energy, the party calls attention to "the continuing need of the United States for a sufficient supply of energy for national security and for the immediate adoption of a policy of free market solutions to achieve energy independence for the United States," and calls for the "repeal of federal environmental protections."[^cite_note-256] The party also advocates the abolition of the [Department of Energy](62866.md).

## Climate justice



Per person, the United States generates carbon dioxide at a far faster rate than other primary regions.

[^cite_note-Friedlingstein_2019-258]

[^cite_note-GlobalCarbonBudgetPerCapita_2025-259]

[Climate justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_justice) is part of [environmental justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_justice), which EPA defines as: "The fair treatment and meaningful involvement of all people regardless of race, color, national origin, or income, with respect to the development, implementation, and enforcement of environmental laws, regulations, and policies."[^cite_note-260]

Poor and disempowered groups often do not have the resources to prepare for, cope with or recover from climate disasters such as droughts, floods, heat waves, hurricanes, etc.[^cite_note-\:2-261] This occurs not only within the United States but also between rich nations, who predominantly create the problem of climate change by dumping [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) into the atmosphere, and poor nations who have to deal more heavily with the consequences.[^cite_note-\:2-261]

### State and regional policies

States and local governments are often tasked with defense against climate change affecting areas and peoples under state and local jurisdiction.

#### Mayors National Climate Action Agenda

Main article: [Mayors National Climate Action Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_National_Climate_Action_Agenda)

The [Mayors National Climate Action Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_National_Climate_Action_Agenda) was founded by Los Angeles mayor [Eric Garcetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Garcetti), former Houston mayor [Annise Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annise_Parker), and former Philadelphia mayor [Michael Nutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Nutter) in 2014.[^cite_note-262] The MNCAA aims to bring climate change policy into the hands of local government and to make federal climate change policies more accountable.[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264]

As a part of MNCAA, 75 mayors from across the United States, known as the "Climate Mayors", wrote to President Trump on March 28, 2017, in opposition to proposed rollbacks of several major climate change departments and initiatives. They maintain that the federal government should continue to build up climate change policies, stating "we are also standing up for our constituents and all Americans harmed by climate change, including those most vulnerable among us: coastal residents confronting erosion and sea level rise; young and old alike suffering from worsening air pollution and at risk during heatwaves; mountain residents engulfed by wildfires; farmers struggling at harvest time due to drought; and communities across our nation challenged by extreme weather."[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266] Climate Mayors currently has over 400 cities involved in the network.[^cite_note-267] Their current key initiative is the Electric Vehicle Request for Information (EV RFI).[^cite_note-268] They have also produced responses to the announcement of the plan for the United States to withdraw from the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md)[^cite_note-269] and opposition to the proposed repeal of the [Clean Power Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Power_Plan).[^cite_note-270]

#### United States Climate Alliance

Main article: [United States Climate Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Climate_Alliance)

The [United States Climate Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Climate_Alliance) is a group of states committed to meeting the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md) emissions targets despite President Trump's announced [withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_withdrawal_from_the_Paris_Agreement) from the agreement. Currently, there are 22 states that are members of this network.[^cite_note-271] This network is a bipartisan network of governors across the United States and is governed by three core principles: "States are continuing to lead on climate change", "State-level climate action is benefiting our economies and strengthening our communities", "States are showing the nation and the world that ambitious climate action is achievable."[^cite_note-272] Their current initiatives include green banks, grid modernizations, solar soft costs, [short-lived climate pollutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_and_Clean_Air_Coalition_to_Reduce_Short-Lived_Climate_Pollutants), natural and working lands, [climate resilience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_resilience), international cooperation, clean transportation, and improving data and tools.[^cite_note-273]

#### California

The California [Global Warming Solutions Act of 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Warming_Solutions_Act_of_2006) (commonly known as AB 32) mandates a reduction in greenhouse gas emissions to 1990 levels by the year 2020.[^cite_note-274] The [Environmental Defense Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Defense_Fund) and the [Air Resources Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Air_Resources_Board) recruited staffers with environmental justice expertise as well as community leaders in order to appease environmental justice groups and ensure the safe passage of the bill.[^cite_note-Sze2009-275]

The environmental justice groups who worked on AB 32 strongly opposed cap and trade programs being made mandatory.[^cite_note-Sze2009-275] A cap and trade plan was put in place, and a 2016 study by a group of California academics found that [carbon offsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_offset) under the plan were not used to benefit people in California who lived near power plants, who are mostly less well off than people who live far from them.[^cite_note-276]

### Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative

Main article: [Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Greenhouse_Gas_Initiative)

In December 2005, the governors of seven Northeastern and Mid-Atlantic states agreed to the [Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Greenhouse_Gas_Initiative) (RGGI), a [cap and trade](37104.md) system covering carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) emissions from regional power plants. Currently (at the time of this edit), Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, and Vermont have signed, and Maryland Governor [Robert Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ehrlich) signed legislation in March 2006 that commits Maryland to join RGGI by 2007. To facilitate compliance with reduction targets, RGGI will provide flexibility mechanisms that include credits for emissions reductions achieved outside of the electricity sector. The successful implementation of the RGGI model will set the stage for other states to join or form their own regional cap and trade systems and may encourage the program to expand to other greenhouse gases and other sectors.[^cite_note-277] RGGI states, along with Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, and Rhode Island, are also developing a GHG registry called the [Eastern Climate Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Climate_Registry?action=edit\&redlink=1).

On November 29, 2011, New Jersey withdrew from the initiative, effective January 1, 2012.[^cite_note-278] Groups such as Acadia Center have since reported on lost revenue resulting from New Jersey's departure, and argued for renewed participation.[^cite_note-279]

After the election of [Ralph Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Northam) in the 2017 Virginia gubernatorial election and Phil Murphy in the 2017 New Jersey gubernatorial election, New Jersey and Virginia began to make preliminary moves to join RGGI.[^cite_note-280][^cite_note-281]

#### The Western Governors' Association

The [Western Governors' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Governors'_Association) (WGA) Clean and Diversified Energy Initiative, including 18 western states, has begun investigating strategies to increase efficiency and renewable energy sources in their electricity systems. Governors Richardson (NM), Schwarzenegger (CA), Freudenthal (WY) & Hoeven (ND) serve as lead Governors on this initiative. To meet its goals, the Initiative's advisory committee (CDEAC) appointed eight technical task forces to develop recommendations based on reviews of specific clean energy and efficiency options. The CDEAC made final recommendations to the Western Governors' Association on June 11, 2006.[^cite_note-282] Additionally, the WGA and the California Energy Commission are creating the Western Renewable Energy Generation Information State (WREGIS). WREGIS is a voluntary system for renewable energy credits and tracks renewable energy credits (RECs) across 11 western states in order to facilitate trading to meet renewable energy portfolio standards.

#### Other initiatives

As of 2020, several states in the northeastern United States were discussing a regional cap and trade system for carbon emissions from motor vehicle fuel sources, called the [Transportation Climate Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Climate_Initiative).[^cite_note-283] In 2021, Massachusetts withdrew citing as one of the reasons that it was no longer necessary.[^cite_note-284]

The governors of Arizona and New Mexico signed an agreement to create the Southwest Climate Change Initiative in February 2006. The two states collaborated to assess [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md) and address the impacts of climate change in the Southwest[^cite_note-285] and on September 8, 2006, Arizona Governor [Janet Napolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Napolitano) issued an executive order to implement recommendations included in the Climate Change Advisory Group's Climate Action Plan.[^cite_note-gov.ca.gov-286] The West Coast states—Washington, Oregon, and California—are cooperating on a strategy to reduce GHG emissions, known as the Western Coast Governors' Global Warming Initiative.[^cite_note-287] Finally, on February 26, 2007, these five Western states (Washington, Oregon, California, Arizona, and New Mexico) agreed to combine their efforts to develop regional targets for reducing greenhouse emissions, creating the [Western Regional Climate Action Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Regional_Climate_Action_Initiative).[^cite_note-288]

In 2001 six New England states committed to the [New England Governors and Eastern Canadian Premiers (NEG-ECP) Climate Change Action Plan 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Governors_and_Eastern_Canadian_Premiers_\(NEG-ECP\)_Climate_Change_Action_Plan_2001), including short and long-term GHG emission reduction goals. Powering the Plains, launched in 2002, is a regional effort involving participants from the Dakotas, Minnesota, Iowa, Wisconsin and the Canadian Province of Manitoba. This initiative aims to develop strategies, policies, and demonstration projects for alternative energy sources and technology and climate-friendly agricultural development.[^cite_note-289]

### Municipal initiatives

#### ICLEI

In 1993, at the invitation of [ICLEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICLEI), municipal leaders met at the United Nations in New York and adopted a declaration that called for the establishment of a worldwide movement of local governments to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, improve air quality, and enhance urban sustainability. The result was the Cities for Climate Protection (CCP) Campaign. Since its inception, the CCP Campaign has grown to involve more than 650 local governments worldwide that are integrating climate change mitigation into their decision-making processes.[^cite_note-290]

### U.S. Mayors' Climate Protection Agreement

On February 16, 2005, [Seattle](11388236.md) Mayor [Greg Nickels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Nickels) launched an initiative to advance the goals of the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) through leadership and action by at least 141 American cities, and as of October, 2006, 319 mayors representing over 51.4 million Americans had accepted the challenge.[^cite_note-291] Under the terms of the [Mayors Climate Protection Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_Climate_Protection_Center), cities must commit to three actions in striving to meet the Kyoto Protocol in their own communities.[^cite_note-292] These actions include:

- Strive to meet or beat the Kyoto Protocol targets in their own communities, through actions ranging from anti-sprawl land-use policies to [urban forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_forest) restoration projects to public information campaigns;

- Urge their state governments, and the federal government, to enact policies and programs to meet or beat the greenhouse gas emission reduction target suggested for the United States in the Kyoto Protocol—7% reduction from 1990 levels by 2012; and

- Urge the U.S. Congress to pass the [bipartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisan) greenhouse gas reduction legislation, which would establish a national emission trading system.
