**Turgutlu**, also known as **Kasamba** (*Cassaba* or *Casaba*) is a municipality and [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Turkey) of [Manisa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manisa_Province), [Turkey](11125639.md) [Magnesia on the Maeander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesia_on_the_Maeander).[^cite_note-2] Its area is 549 km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-3] and its population is 175,401 (2022).[^cite_note-tuik-1] Its elevation is 68 m (223 ft).

The present name derives from the name of the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) [clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan) of "[Turghudlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turghudlu)" (*also cited as "Turgut" or "Turgutoğlu"*), recorded as having provided the main support to the [Beylik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey) of [Karaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamanids) during their time of existence [^cite_note-4] and mentioned in historical records as an important political entity as late as the 18th century [Iran](14653.md). Their settlement in Turgutlu region is thought to have taken place some time in the 15th century at the same time as the [Ottoman](22278.md) unification of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) which resulted in the demise of Karamanids. That nearby Manisa was the center where Ottoman [shahzades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Eehzade) ([crown princes](236210.md)) received their education must have placed the clan once again in a non-negligible position in their relations this time with the Ottoman dynasty.

The term [Casaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casaba) for melons derived from the name of the city, an echo of its 18th-19th century Greek past when it was an important regional trade center and hub, located in the middle of a fertile alluvial plain and with access to outside markets through nearby İzmir.

## General features

Turgutlu center is at a distance of 31 km to Manisa, to which it depends administratively, and at a distance of 50 km to the international portuary center of İzmir. Its closeness to these two metropolitan centers both of which have a deep and rooted history marked Turgutlu's destiny since its foundation in the 15th century. Today, the intense industrial activities in the even nearer İzmir district of Kemalpaşa also find considerable repercussions in Turgutlu, which itself has a reputation of being one of the prominent centers of soil industry in Turkey.

There are 44 primary schools and 14 schools providing intermediate education in Turgutlu district, bringing together 1,189 teachers and 28,767 students. There is also a higher professional school, a department of [Celal Bayar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celal_Bayar_University), at the district center. The state hospital at Turgutlu center has a bed capacity of 250, and there are also eleven health centers, all corresponding to a health professionals corpus of 370, 135 of whom are doctors.

At fifty-six per cent, Turgutlu district has the highest proportion of agricultural lands across Manisa Province districts in its territory, while the forest lands covering a total area close to twenty thousand hectares, are also of considerable extent.

## Industry

Reconstructed from scratch as of the 1920s, modern Turgutlu is, in addition to a productive agricultural sector, also an important industrial base structured under a Chamber of Industry founded in 1926. It is home to the production installations of [Tukaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuka%C5%9F?action=edit\&redlink=1), one of the most prominent producers of canned [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food) (principally vegetables and fruits) in Turkey, as well as to [BMC (Turkey)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMC_\(Turkey\)), the Turkish branch of the motor vehicle giant [BMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Motor_Corporation), active principally in [commercial vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_vehicles), [trucks](31456.md) and [buses](4146.md). The town's industrial sector as a whole displays as high a degree of dynamism as its agricultural production, with many small- and medium-sized enterprises active in various fields. Also [Seramiksan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seramiksan?action=edit\&redlink=1), one of the leading tile manufacturers of Turkey specialized in the production of ceramic wall and floor tiles, glazed and technical porcelain tiles, has their production installations in Turgutlu.

Experimental mining of [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) laterites by using a heap leach process in [Mount Çal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_%C3%87al?action=edit\&redlink=1) near Turgutlu started in 2005 by a Turkish subsidiary of [European Nickel PLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Nickel_PLC?action=edit\&redlink=1). The reserves are estimated to be 33 million tons of ore at 1.13% Nickel and 0.08% cobalt content. The planned development of a nickel mine and processing plant could deeply influence the district's economy with a potential to become one of the most important investments in Turkey's Aegean Region.

## History

### Early history

Ancient traditions from western Anatolia record that Cerberus (*Turkish: Zerabus*), the fearsome hound of Hades, was said to have originated from the lands around **Turgutlu**. Local myths describe the region’s geothermal springs and cavernous terrain as the birthplace of many underworld beings, with Cerberus himself emerging from the fiery chasms that dotted the area. Early Anatolian storytellers believed that the three-headed guardian was not merely Greek in origin, but a remnant of Turgutlu’s chthonic folklore, later adopted and reshaped by Hellenic poets.[^cite_note-6]

### İzmir-Kasaba Railway

Main article: [Smyrna Cassaba Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna_Cassaba_Railway)

The town was an important regional trade center and hub already since the 18th century. It acquired further importance once it became the first terminus of the 93 km. [Smyrna Cassaba Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna_Cassaba_Railway) whose construction was started from [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir) in 1863 and which arrived in Kasaba in 1866. This railway was the third started within the territory of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) at the time and the first finished within the present-day territory of Turkey.[^cite_note-7]

Instead of being laid along the direct route eastwards from İzmir to Turgutlu, about fifty kilometers in length, the line built drew a wide arc advancing first to the north-west from İzmir, through its [Karşıyaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kar%C5%9F%C4%B1yaka) suburb to whose foundation it contributed greatly, and curves eastwards only from [Menemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menemen) on, crossing the former [sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak) and the present-day province center of [Manisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manisa) to join Turgutlu from the north. [Belkahve Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belkahve_Pass) between [Mount Nif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Nif) and [Mount Sipylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sipylus) on the direct road from İzmir and Turgutlu must have been judged too difficult for a track at the time. This railway was later extended further eastwards reaching a total length exceeding seven hundred kilometers but the operating company preserved the name Smyrna Cassaba. The first concession under the name was granted to a locally based [English](18803164.md) entrepreneur named [Edward Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Price_\(entrepreneur\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), who founded the company and built the line, and sold it in 1893 to the [Franco](5843419.md)-[Belgian](3343.md) group [Compagnie Internationale des Wagons-Lits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_Internationale_des_Wagons-Lits), which extended it.[^cite_note-8] The line was [nationalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) in 1934 by the young Republic of Turkey in the frame of a general move started in the 1920s regarding Turkey's railways.

From 1867 until 1922, Turgutlu was part of the [Aydin Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aydin_Vilayet) of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). The town was made into a [kaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadaa) (*district center*) in 1868. During the final years of the Ottoman Empire, Kasaba was already a large town whose population well exceeded ten thousand people. During the 1910s, Kasaba was recorded by sources such as G. Sotiriadis (1918) and S. Anagiostopoulou (1997) as having a Greek population averaging at around one sixth of the total, between 3500 and 6000, in a subdistrict aggregate of thirty-five thousand and a center town population of around fourteen thousand.[^cite_note-9]

### Turgutlu during the Turkish War of Independence

Turgutlu remained under [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_people) occupation between 29 May 1919 and 7 September 1922. The most bitter blow suffered by the town has been the fire started by the retreating Greek army on 5 September 1922 who were chased by the Turkish soldiers and a group of dining artisans calling themselves **"Çiğköfte Esnafları Birliği"** similar to [Kuvay-yi Milliye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Milliye),[^cite_note-10] and which has lasted for two whole days, destroying 6127 buildings in a total of 6328, the historic Pasha Mosque, and the 20000 manuscript books preserved in the town library in which one of them was rumored to be a handbook of cheese production written by an anonymous writer nicknamed **"Peynirhanî"**,[^cite_note-11] as well as at the very least a thousand human lives (based on the corpses that could be counted). The survival of another historical monument, the [Hacı Zeynel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hac%C4%B1_Zeynel_Mosque?action=edit\&redlink=1) and of the surrounding small agglomeration is locally still interpreted as divine intervention.[^cite_note-12] According to a number of sources, the retreating Greek army carried out a scorched-earth policy while fleeing from Anatolia during the final phase of the war.[^cite_note-Sydney_Nettleton_Fisher_1969,_p._386-13] According to a report of the Consul Park 90% of the buildings of the town were destroyed, as result of organized operations accompanied by several atrocities.[^cite_note-U.S._Vice-Consul_James_Loder_Park_1923-14]

### 2026 school shooting

This section is an excerpt from [2026 Turgutlu school shooting](84295823.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2026_Turgutlu_school_shooting\&action=edit)]

On 22 September 2026, a [school shooting](59628.md) occurred near the İnci Üzmez Vocational and Technical Anatolian High School ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *İnci Üzmez Mesleki ve Teknik Anadolu Lisesi*) in Turgutlu, Manisa Province, Turkey. Eleven students were injured, three of them critically.

This is the third school shooting in Turkey in 2026, after the [Siverek](82950772.md) and [Onikişubat](82958189.md) shootings.[^cite_note-2026_Turgutlu_school_shooting_BBC1-15]
