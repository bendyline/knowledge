**Jessica Pegula** (born February 24, 1994) is an American professional [tennis](29773.md) player. She has a career-high [rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_rankings) in singles of world No. 3, first achieved in October 2022, and in doubles of [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_doubles_tennis_players), achieved in September 2023.[^cite_note-JP_Rankings-5] Pegula has won 11 singles titles and seven doubles titles on the [WTA Tour](1398173.md), including four [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000) titles in singles and two in doubles. She was the runner-up at the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and at the [2023 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles).

In doubles, she was runner-up at the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), partnering with [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff), and in the mixed doubles of the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Mixed_doubles), with [Austin Krajicek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Krajicek). She is a four-time major singles semifinalist (thrice at the [US Open](82729.md), once at the [Australian Open](97851.md)) and has reached eleven quarterfinals across all four major tournaments. Pegula also led the [US team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Billie_Jean_King_Cup_team) to victory in the inaugural [2023 United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Cup) and to the final of the [2025 Billie Jean King Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Billie_Jean_King_Cup).[^cite_note-6]

## Early life

Pegula was born February 24, 1994, in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York),[^cite_note-7] to petroleum tycoon and [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) owner [Terry Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pegula) and [Kim Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Pegula). The family has a net worth of over \$9.3 billion, according to [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes).[^cite_note-8] She is of [part Korean descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Americans) as her mother is Korean. Pegula has four siblings, two of whom are from her father's first marriage. Pegula started playing tennis at age seven.

## Career

### 2011–2012: Major third round in doubles

On August 30, 2011, Pegula was granted a wildcard into the main draw of the US Open doubles tournament, where she was paired with [Taylor Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Townsend). They eventually lost in the third round to the third-seeded team of [Vania King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vania_King) and [Yaroslava Shvedova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslava_Shvedova). In March 2012, Pegula was handed a wildcard to the qualifying draw in [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Wells_Open) and surprised higher ranked players [Bojana Jovanovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojana_Jovanovski) and [Paula Ormaechea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Ormaechea) to qualify for the main draw where she lost to [Magdaléna Rybáriková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdal%C3%A9na_Ryb%C3%A1rikov%C3%A1) in three sets.

### 2015: Major singles debut, first win



Pegula at the [2015 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_US_Open_\(tennis\))

Pegula made her major singles debut at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_US_Open_\(tennis\)) as a qualifier. She defeated [Shuko Aoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuko_Aoyama), [Margarita Gasparyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Gasparyan), and [Melanie Oudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Oudin) to reach the main draw, where she beat [Alison Van Uytvanck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Van_Uytvanck) in the first round. In the second, Pegula was defeated by [Dominika Cibulková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominika_Cibulkov%C3%A1) in three sets.

### 2018: WTA 250 final

In 2018, Pegula reached her first WTA Tour singles final at the [Tournoi de Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Coupe_Banque_Nationale_%E2%80%93_Singles) in September as a qualifier. She beat [Kristýna Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krist%C3%BDna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1), [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur), second seed [Petra Martić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Marti%C4%87) and fifth seed [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin) en route to the final,[^cite_note-9] where she lost to eighth seed [Pauline Parmentier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_Parmentier) in straight sets.[^cite_note-10] This brought her ranking back inside the top 200 and helped her finish the year inside the top 125.[^cite_note-JP_Rankings-5]

### 2019: WTA 250 title, top 100

Pegula began the year playing primarily on the ITF Circuit, before cracking inside the top 100 in February for the first time in her career. This allowed her to enter several larger [WTA Tour](1398173.md) events, including in [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). Her best result during the early clay court season came in [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Volvo_Car_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where she upset world No. 12, [Anastasija Sevastova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasija_Sevastova),[^cite_note-11] en route to the third round. This helped her break inside the top 75 for the first time. She also competed in the main draw of a Grand Slam tournament other than the US Open for the first time. She was defeated in the first round of the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) by eventual champion [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty), before falling in the same stage at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) to [Mihaela Buzărnescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaela_Buz%C4%83rnescu).

Pegula achieved the best result of her career at the start of the North American hardcourt season when she won her first career singles title at the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), defeating [Camila Giorgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Giorgi) in the final.[^cite_note-12] This took her to a new career-high ranking of world No. 55. Despite failing to win another main-draw match the rest of the season, Pegula finished the year ranked inside the top 100 for the first time, at No. 76.[^cite_note-JP_Rankings-5]

### 2020: WTA 250 final

Pegula started her 2020 tennis season at the [Auckland Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ASB_Classic) where she defeated [CiCi Bellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CiCi_Bellis) in the first round. She followed this up with two more straight-set victories over [Tamara Zidanšek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Zidan%C5%A1ek) and [Alizé Cornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliz%C3%A9_Cornet) to reach the semifinals. There she beat [Caroline Wozniacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki) in three sets to advance to her third Tour singles final. Facing off against 23-time Grand Slam singles winner [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams) for the first time, Pegula lost in straight sets.[^cite_note-13] She then competed at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) for the first time, where she was defeated by another American, Taylor Townsend, in straight sets in the first round.

Pegula's next big triumph came at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), a Premier 5 tournament. Having already beaten two Russians to qualify for the main draw, she opened her campaign with a straight-sets win over American [Jennifer Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Brady) followed by a win over another fellow American, 2019 French Open semifinalist [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova). She then caused a huge upset by defeating fifth seed and world No. 11, [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md),[^cite_note-14] in the third round, thus advancing to her first quarterfinal at any WTA Premier level event. Her run came to an end with a straight-sets loss to 14th seed [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens).[^cite_note-15]

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Pegula recovered from a set down to record her first Grand Slam main-draw win since the 2015 US Open, defeating [Marie Bouzková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1) in a third-set tiebreak. She then beat [Kirsten Flipkens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Flipkens) to advance to the third round of a Grand Slam event for the first time, where she lost to sixth seed and former world No. 2, [Petra Kvitová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1).[^cite_note-16]

### 2021: Australian Open quarterfinal, top 20



Pegula at the [2021 Bad Homburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Bad_Homburg_Open)

Pegula attained success at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open), defeating former Australian Open champion and 12th seed [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka), [Kristina Mladenovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristina_Mladenovic), former [US Open](82729.md) champion [Samantha Stosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Stosur) and fifth seed [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md) to reach her first [major](197638.md) quarterfinal[^cite_note-17] where she lost to eventual runner-up, Jennifer Brady, despite winning the first set. This strong showing allowed Pegula to enter top 50 for the first time, and took her to a new career-high ranking of No. 43 in the world.[^cite_note-Pegula-18]

Later in April, she achieved another career-high of No. 32, after reaching the semifinals at the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as a qualifier where she lost to the eventual champion, Petra Kvitová,[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] and a fourth-round run as a seeded player at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Miami_Open), losing to [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari) in a tight three-set match. Her third-round win over [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1) in Miami was the third win in a row in three tournaments over the same player.[^cite_note-21]

In May, at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_\(tennis\)), where she participated for the first time, she recorded the biggest victory of her career over world No. 2, [Naomi Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Osaka), in the second round. This was her fifth top-ten win in 2021.[^cite_note-22] She next prevailed over [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova) to set up a quarterfinal match with Petra Martić. Thanks to this great showing at her second WTA 1000 quarterfinal in 2021, after the one at [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), she entered top 30 for the first time.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open), she reached the third round for the first time in her career where she lost to fourth seed [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin).[^cite_note-23] At the [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_WTA_German_Open) in Berlin, Pegula reached the quarterfinals defeating for the fourth time Karolína Plíšková in their fourth in a row meeting in 2021.

She reached her third WTA 1000 quarterfinal of 2021 at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_National_Bank_Open) edition in Montreal, defeating compatriot [Danielle Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Collins) in a close three set match needing six match points to win in a thrilling finish.[^cite_note-24] She then made her first WTA 1000 semifinal and second semifinal for the season, defeating 13th seed Ons Jabeur in 88 minutes.[^cite_note-25] At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_\(tennis\)), she finished in round three for a second consecutive year. At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open), she reached her fourth WTA 1000 quarterfinal, defeating world No. 7 (her 7th top-ten win for the season) and fourth seed, Elina Svitolina, before losing to former two-time champion Victoria Azarenka.[^cite_note-26]

### 2022: Major quarterfinals, WTA 1000 title, singles No. 3



Pegula at the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_\(tennis\))

Pegula started her season in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Melbourne_Summer_Set_2) where she lost to [Irina-Camelia Begu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu) in the first round of the singles tournament but won her first career doubles title with [Asia Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Muhammad), beating former doubles world No. 1, [Sara Errani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Errani), and [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md) in the final. In [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sydney_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she lost to [Caroline Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Garcia) in the first round.

At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she defeated [Anhelina Kalinina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhelina_Kalinina), [Bernarda Pera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernarda_Pera), [Nuria Párrizas Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuria_P%C3%A1rrizas_D%C3%ADaz) and fifth seed Maria Sakkari to book her second consecutive quarterfinal at this major. She lost the quarterfinal match to world No. 1 and eventual champion, Ash Barty, but moved to highest rankings in singles (No. 16) and doubles (No. 41) on January 31, 2022.

In [Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open), she won her second doubles title (and first at the WTA 1000 level) with [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff), defeating third seeds Elise Mertens and [Veronika Kudermetova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova) in the final.[^cite_note-27] As a result, she set No. 29 in doubles on February 28, 2022.

At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open), she reached her second WTA 1000 semifinal of her career after two back-to-back retirements, Kalinina in round of 16, and [Paula Badosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Badosa) in the first set of the quarterfinal.[^cite_note-28] In the semifinals, she lost to the eventual champion and upcoming No. 1, [Iga Świątek](51838558.md), in straight sets.[^cite_note-29]

Her seventh WTA 1000 quarterfinal came at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open) by defeating [Bianca Andreescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu) in straight sets.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] She reached her third semifinal at the WTA 1000-level and second straight for the season defeating first time Madrid quarterfinalist [Sara Sorribes Tormo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Sorribes_Tormo).[^cite_note-32] Next, she defeated [Jil Teichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jil_Teichmann) for her first WTA 1000 final and become the third American to do so, after Venus Williams (2010) and Serena Williams (2012 and 2013) in Madrid, where she lost to Ons Jabeur in three sets.[^cite_note-33] As a result, she improved her career-high to No. 11 on May 9. Pegula reached the third round of the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as well, but lost to Aryna Sabalenka in straight sets.

Seeded 11th at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open), Pegula advanced to the quarterfinals for the first time at this major, defeating former world No. 12, [Wang Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Qiang_\(tennis\)), Anhelina Kalinina, [2021 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_Singles) semifinalist and 24th seed Tamara Zidanšek, and Irina-Camelia Begu.[^cite_note-34] She lost to top seed and eventual champion, Iga Świątek, in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-\:6-35] As a result, she broke into the top 10 in the singles rankings for the first time, at world No. 8, on June 6, 2022. She also entered her first major final in doubles, partnering with Gauff.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] As a result, she reached the top 15 in the doubles rankings. Seeded eighth at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships), she advanced to the third round for the first time, before losing to Petra Martić in straight sets.[^cite_note-38] Following Wimbledon, she set a new career-high of world No. 7 on July 18, 2022, becoming the American number-one-player, one spot ahead of Danielle Collins.[^cite_note-39]

As a top-seeded pair, partnering [Erin Routliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Routliffe), she won her third doubles title at the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Citi_Open) defeating fourth seeds [Caty McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caty_McNally) and Anna Kalinskaya.[^cite_note-40]

Seeded seventh at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open), she reached back-to-back semifinals in singles, for the fourth time at the WTA 1000-level in her career and third for the season, defeating defending champion Camila Giorgi[^cite_note-41] and [Yulia Putintseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva), before losing to Simona Halep.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Seeded third in doubles at the same tournament, she also reached the semifinals with Gauff defeating fifth seeds [Desirae Krawczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desirae_Krawczyk) and [Demi Schuurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Schuurs). They defeated [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys) / [Sania Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sania_Mirza) in the semifinals and [Nicole Melichar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Melichar) / [Ellen Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Perez) in the final to win their second WTA 1000 title together. As a result, Pegula reached top 10 in the doubles rankings, at world No. 8.

At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_&_Southern_Open), she reached back-to-back quarterfinals defeating tenth seed [Emma Raducanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Raducanu), her fourth WTA 1000 quarterfinal of the season.[^cite_note-44]

Seeded eighth at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_\(tennis\)), she reached the fourth round, defeating [Viktorija Golubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktorija_Golubic), [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich), and qualifier [Yuan Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Yue). Next she defeated 21st seed Petra Kvitová to reach her first quarterfinal at her home Grand Slam championship and the third major quarterfinal of the season, where she lost to world No. 1 Świątek for the third time this year.[^cite_note-45] Despite the result, she moved to world No. 5, on September 12, 2022. On October 13 and 14, 2022, she qualified for the [2022 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals) in singles and doubles with Gauff, respectively, becoming the first American to qualify for the WTA Finals since [Sloane Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloane_Stephens) in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_WTA_Finals) and the first American to qualify in both singles in doubles since Serena and [Venus Williams](167378.md) in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_WTA_Tour_Championships).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] At the [San Diego Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Diego_Open), Pegula lost to Świątek in a US Open rematch in the semifinals, but won the doubles title with Gauff, their third title of the season together and Pegula's fifth doubles title overall. As a result, she reached a new career-high doubles ranking of No. 4, on October 17, 2022.

At the [Guadalajara Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Guadalajara_Open_Akron), she reached her second final at the WTA 1000 level defeating four former Grand Slam champions [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md), Bianca Andreescu, and Sloane Stephens in the quarterfinals, and Victoria Azarenka in the semifinals.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] She defeated fourth seed Maria Sakkari in straight sets to win the second and biggest singles title of her career, becoming the first American woman to win a WTA 1000 title since Madison Keys in 2019.[^cite_note-50] At the same tournament, she also reached the quarterfinals in doubles with Gauff. As a result, she achieved new career-high rankings of world No. 3, in singles and in doubles, on October 24, 2022.

On her debut at the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles), Pegula lost all her matches of the group stage, winning only one set in three matches. At the same tournament, she and partner Gauff surprisingly failed to win a single match and also finished last in the round-robin stage of doubles.[^cite_note-51] Nonetheless, she finished her best season to date ranked No. 3 in singles and No. 6 in doubles, one of only three women (along with Gauff and Kudermetova) to finish inside the top 10 in both disciplines.

### 2023: Major quarterfinals, WTA 1000 title, doubles No. 1



Pegula at the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open)

Pegula began her season playing at the inaugural edition of the [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Cup). After dropping her opening match in Group C to Petra Kvitová, she bounced back to defeat [Laura Siegemund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Siegemund). She then beat [Harriet Dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Dart) in the first knockout stage before scoring the biggest win of her career with a straight sets victory over Iga Świątek, her first win over a current world No. 1 and first against the Pole since the [2019 Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). In the finals, she beat 2022 French Open semifinalist [Martina Trevisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Trevisan) to help the United States win the first ever United Cup title.

Coming off the heels of her win over Świątek and consistency throughout the 2022 season, Pegula was considered one of the favorites for the Australian Open title. She breezed through her first three matches with straight set wins over [Jaqueline Cristian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaqueline_Cristian), Aliaksandra Sasnovich, and [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk). In the fourth round, Pegula faced off against 2021 French Open champion [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) for the first time, defeating the 20th seed in straight sets to advance to her third consecutive Australian Open quarterfinal. However, she was unable to advance past that stage once more, being defeated by former champion Victoria Azarenka in straight sets. Pegula also played doubles with Coco Gauff; seeded second, the pairing dropped only one set en route to the semifinals, before falling to the tenth seeds, [Shuko Aoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuko_Aoyama) and [Ena Shibahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ena_Shibahara).

In February, Pegula reached the finals of the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Qatar_Open) in both singles and doubles. It was Pegula's first WTA 500 singles final. She defeated former French Open champion [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko) and world No. 2, Petra Kvitová en route, before losing to top-ranked Iga Świątek, in straight sets. In doubles, she and Gauff defended their doubles title, defeating second seeds Lyudmyla Kichenok and Jelena Ostapenko in three sets. Pegula earned 305 singles and 470 doubles ranking points.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), she reached her first WTA 1000 semifinal for the season defeating [Viktoriya Tomova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktoriya_Tomova), [Ana Bogdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Bogdan) and [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md) by walkover, before losing to eventual champion Krejčíková.

At the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open), she reached the fourth round defeating 27th seed [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) coming from a set down.[^cite_note-54] She repeated the feat at [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open) where again she came back from a set down and won against 26th seed Potapova, saving two match points, to reach this time the semifinals, after several match postponements due to a four hours rain delay. She became the first American woman to reach back to back semifinals in Miami since Serena Williams in 2014 and 2015.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] In doubles at the same tournament, she won her fifth overall and third WTA 1000 team title with her partner, Coco Gauff. They became the first all-American duo to win the Miami Open doubles title in 22 years, defeating [Leylah Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leylah_Fernandez) and Taylor Townsend in the final.[^cite_note-57] At the WTA 1000 [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open), Pegula and Gauff reached again the final where they lost to Azarenka and [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia).[^cite_note-58] The match was followed by controversy as none of the participating players were allowed to make a speech on the podium during the awards ceremony. The organizers subsequently apologized for the mistake.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] At the next WTA 1000 in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_\(tennis\)), the pair played their third consecutive final.[^cite_note-61] As a result, Pegula reached a new career-high of No. 2 in doubles.[^cite_note-62] Afflicted by a bout of food poisoning just days before the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open), Pegula was upset by the 28th seed Elise Mertens in the third round in Paris, but reached the semifinals in doubles with Gauff, before falling to the tenth seeds, Leylah Fernandez and Taylor Townsend.

At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships), she reached the quarterfinals and became just the fifth American to do so at all four majors in the last 25 years, after notching a straight-sets fourth-round win over Ukrainian [Lesia Tsurenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesia_Tsurenko).[^cite_note-63] In the quarterfinals, Pegula lost to the eventual champion, [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1), despite leading in the third set.[^cite_note-64]

Seeded fourth in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she reached her third consecutive Canadian Open semifinal by beating Yulia Putintseva, Jasmine Paolini and seventh seed Coco Gauff. Then she beat Iga Świątek for the second time in the season, in three sets, to enter her third WTA 1000 final in which she defeated 15th seed [Liudmila Samsonova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova) in 49 minutes, to win her second WTA 1000 and third singles title.[^cite_note-65]

Following the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_\(tennis\)) where she reached the fourth round in singles (losing to close friend Madison Keys) and the quarterfinals in doubles for the first time (falling to the reigning French Open champions [Hsieh Su-wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsieh_Su-wei) and [Wang Xinyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xinyu)), she became the world No. 1 in doubles for the first time.[^cite_note-66] At the same tournament, she also reached the mixed doubles final with ATP doubles world No. 1, [Austin Krajicek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Krajicek), which they lost to [Anna Danilina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Danilina) and [Harri Heliövaara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harri_Heli%C3%B6vaara), in straight sets.

At the beginning of the Asian swing, she reached the final of the WTA 500 [Japan Women's Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Toray_Pan_Pacific_Open) defeating Maria Sakkari,[^cite_note-67] before losing to Veronika Kudermetova.[^cite_note-68] She won her second singles title of the season and fourth overall at the [Korea Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Korea_Open).[^cite_note-69]

Jessica and Coco returned to top doubles ranking on October 23, after qualifying for the [2023 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals) as a pair and also individually, becoming the first players to qualify in both disciplines in back-to-back years since Sara Errani in 2012–13.[^cite_note-70] At the WTA Finals, where she qualified for a second year in a row, she recorded her first win ever at this tournament, and her 40th singles hardcourt win over Elena Rybakina in straight sets, the most of anyone in the season. It was a fourth straight win over a top 10 player extending her personal best.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] In her second match, she defeated the world No. 1 player Aryna Sabalenka also in straight sets, her third win against a number-one player in the season and in her career. It was her 43rd season win with 5+ break point conversions, the most of anyone, and her fifth straight top 10 win, the first American on record since Sloane Stephens in 2018. She also became the first American to claim three wins in a season against the WTA's No. 1 since Serena Williams in 2012. Since 2000, Pegula was fourth to achieve the feat after Williams, [Lindsay Davenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Davenport), and Jennifer Capriati.[^cite_note-73] As a result, she qualified for the semifinals.[^cite_note-74] Next, she defeated Sakkari, her sixth straight top-10 win, in straight sets in her third match in a row with this result, improving to 50–0 in 2023 after winning the opening set, becoming the third player to finish a season with 50+-0, excluding retirements, after Lindsay Davenport and Viktoria Azarenka.[^cite_note-75] In the semifinals, she defeated Gauff in one hour in straight sets, her seventh straight top-10 win, and reached the biggest final of her career.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] She became the first player in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) to play the No. 1, No. 2, No. 3 and No. 4 player in the same tournament. In the final, she lost to world No. 2, Iga Świątek, in 59 minutes.[^cite_note-\:7-78]

In January, following the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open) where she exited in the second round, she split with her coach David Witt,[^cite_note-79] with whom she began working in 2019. In [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she lost her debut match to Anna Blinkova and in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), she reached the quarterfinals with her usual partner Gauff. At the next WTA 1000 in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open), she reached the quarterfinals in singles defeating compatriot and 20th seed [Emma Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Navarro)[^cite_note-83] before losing to 14th seed [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova).[^cite_note-84]

She defended her title at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_National_Bank_Open) in Toronto, defeating Karolína Plíšková, qualifier [Ashlyn Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashlyn_Krueger), [Peyton Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Stearns), 14th seed [Diana Shnaider](68209798.md) and Amanda Anisimova in the final.[^cite_note-88] She reached another WTA 1000 final at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open) with wins over Karolina Muchová, Leylah Fernandez, qualifier Taylor Townsend, and Paula Badosa.[^cite_note-89] She lost to Aryna Sabalenka in the final.[^cite_note-90]

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she defeated [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers) in her final career match, Sofia Kenin, and [Jéssica Bouzas Maneiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9ssica_Bouzas_Maneiro) in 70 minutes[^cite_note-91] and Diana Shnaider without losing a set in all matches, to reach her seventh Grand Slam quarterfinal.[^cite_note-92] She defeated Świątek in straight sets[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-\:8-94] and Karolína Muchová in three sets in her first singles major semifinal.[^cite_note-95] However, she lost to world No. 2, Aryna Sabalenka, in the championship match.[^cite_note-\:1-96][^cite_note-97]



Pegula with Sabalenka at the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_\(tennis\)) singles final award ceremony

Partnering with [Asia Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Muhammad), she was runner-up in the doubles at the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles), losing to Anna Danilina and [Irina Khromacheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Khromacheva) in the final.[^cite_note-98]

Pegula at the [2025 Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mubadala_Citi_DC_Open)

### 2026: Major semifinals, WTA 1000 title

Pegula's 2026 season would start in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she lost in the semifinals to Marta Kostyuk.[^cite_note-136] Pegula moved on to the [Australian Open](81715708.md) as the sixth seed, advancing to the semifinals for the first time in her career.[^cite_note-\:0-137] Along the way, Pegula defeated American compatriots Madison Keys, who was the defending champion, and Amanda Anisimova before being knocked out by eventual champion Elena Rybakina in straight sets.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-\:0-137][^cite_note-139]

After Australia, Pegula would take some time off by skipping the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), citing the quick turnaround time as the reason for her withdrawal.[^cite_note-140] She would make her first appearance since Australia at the [Dubai Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she defeated young American [Iva Jovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iva_Jovic), Amanda Anisimova again, and Elina Svitolina on her way to claim her fourth WTA 1000 title.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] Pegula was then scheduled to play at the [ATX Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_ATX_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) but withdrew, citing a left knee injury.[^cite_note-144]

She returned at the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she was seeded fifth. After dropping the first set, Pegula fought back to win her second round match against [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87) in three sets.[^cite_note-145] Pegula took on Jelena Ostapenko in the third round, where she won after dropping the first set once again.[^cite_note-146] Pegula then faced [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic) in the fourth round, whom she had never beaten before, but she had no problem getting through in straight sets to advance to the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-147] There, Pegula would fall in straight sets to Elena Rybakina in what was a rematch of their Australian Open semifinal.[^cite_note-148]

Pegula would then move on to the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she was seeded fifth. Despite the rain delaying her second round match by a day, her opponent [Francesca Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesca_Jones_\(tennis\)) would eventually retire after 38 minutes of play due to an illness, giving Pegula the win.[^cite_note-149] She cruised through her next two matches against Leylah Fernandez and [Jaqueline Cristian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaqueline_Cristian) respectively to set up a quarterfinal appearance against Elena Rybakina once again.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] Despite taking the opening set, Pegula's Miami campaign would end with Rybakina defeating her in three sets.[^cite_note-153] As of March 30, Pegula had spent 200 consecutive weeks inside the WTA top 10, with 142 of those weeks spent inside the top 5.[^cite_note-154] It is the third-longest active streak among the WTA top 10 at the time of writing, only behind both Aryna Sabalenka and Iga Swiatek.

Pegula started her clay-court season in [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Credit_One_Charleston_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), entering as the top seed and defending champion. There she defeated Yulia Putintseva, Elisabetta Cocciaretto, Diana Shnaider, and Iva Jovic all in three sets on her way to the finals.[^cite_note-155] She then defeated the unseeded [Yuliia Starodubtseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuliia_Starodubtseva) in straight sets to defend her title, winning her second title of the season.[^cite_note-156] Pegula then entered the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as the fifth seed. After receiving a bye in the first round, she cruised past [Katie Boulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Boulter)[^cite_note-157] in straight sets before falling to Marta Kostyuk in the third round, also in straight sets.[^cite_note-158] At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Pegula reached the quarterfinals, where she lost to fourth seed Świątek in straight sets, winning just three games across the entire match.[^cite_note-159] She did not carry her good form on at the [French Open](83033049.md), losing to [Kimberly Birrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberly_Birrell) in the first round having let slip a lead of a set and break of serve.[^cite_note-160]

After a short French Open run, Pegula began her grass-court season as the third seed at the [Berlin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Open_\(WTA\)). She defeated [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1) in straight sets to reach the quarterfinals,[^cite_note-161] where she defeated Madison Keys in two tiebreak sets.[^cite_note-162] She then defeated world No. 1, Aryna Sabalenka, in three sets to advance to her second Berlin final[^cite_note-\:5-163] but lost the championship match to [Linda Nosková](67929086.md), in three sets.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-Berlin2026_TT-165] Her grass season ended when she lost to Coco Gauff in the [Wimbledon](83354904.md) quarterfinal.[^cite_note-\:10-166]

As the top seed in the [Washington Open](83780909.md), Pegula reached the finals and lost to Alexandra Eala in three sets.[^cite_note-167] Her next WTA 1000 final at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) saw Pegula lose in straight sets to compatriot, Coco Gauff.[^cite_note-\:11-168]

Pegula entered the [US Open](84044135.md) as the third seed, competing against Aryna Sabalenka, Elena Rybakina, and Coco Gauff for an opportunity to become world No. 1 by the end of the tournament.[^cite_note-169] Pegula's campaign would end in the semifinals, where she faced Sabalenka for the third year in a row, falling in straight sets.[^cite_note-\:12-170]

## Rivalries

### Aryna Sabalenka

Pegula has developed a fairly storied rivalry with [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md). Their consistency on hardcourts leads to them frequently facing each other in the latter stages of events. The two have met 14 times since 2020, with Sabalenka controlling the head-to-head 10–4.[^cite_note-171] Pegula would win their first meeting in the third round of the [2020 Western & Southern Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in three sets. They would face off at the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) final in which Sabalenka would win to earn her first US Open title.[^cite_note-\:1-96] The two would have a rematch of their US Open final in the [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) semifinals, where Sabalenka would win a close three-set match on her way to defending her US Open title.[^cite_note-\:2-120] Pegula would snap Sabalenka's 20-match winning streak as well as her 2025 tiebreak record in their following match in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), before pulling out a three-set win.[^cite_note-\:3-129] The two would meet again in the Riyadh [2025 WTA Finals](81306023.md), where Sabalenka defeated Pegula in three sets.[^cite_note-\:4-132] They would meet once more in the [Berlin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Open_\(WTA\)), where Pegula beat Sabalenka in three sets.[^cite_note-\:5-163] Sabalenka met Pegula at the [2026 US Open](84044135.md) for the third year in a row, with Sabalenka defeating Pegula in a straight set semifinal.[^cite_note-\:12-170]

### Iga Świątek

Pegula and [Iga Świątek](51838558.md) have played each other 13 times since 2019, with Świątek leading the head-to-head 7–6.[^cite_note-172] Świątek won four of their first five meetings including quarterfinal matchups at the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), eventually winning the title in both instances.[^cite_note-\:6-35][^cite_note-173] Świątek would also win the [2023 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) by beating Pegula in the final.[^cite_note-\:7-78] Pegula defeated Świątek on her way to her first Grand Slam final at the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where they met in the quarterfinals once again.[^cite_note-\:8-94] Their longest match came in the semifinals at the [2026 Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), with Pegula defeating the defending champion in three sets after nearly three hours.[^cite_note-174]

### Coco Gauff

Pegula and [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff) have developed a notable rivalry. They consistently swap positions as the top American player in the world and frequently compete against each other in big tournaments. The two have met 10 times since 2022, with the head-to-head split evenly at 5–5.[^cite_note-175] Pegula defeated Gauff in three sets at the [2023 Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) quarterfinals before going on to win her second WTA 1000 title.[^cite_note-176] The two have played each other at the [2023 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles), the [2024 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles), and the [2025 WTA Finals](81306023.md) with the wins split 2–1 in favor of Pegula, although Gauff went on to win the 2024 WTA Finals after beating Pegula in the round robin stage.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] They played their first Grand Slam match in the [2026 Wimbledon](83354904.md) quarterfinals, which Gauff won in three sets.[^cite_note-\:10-166] Gauff also defeated Pegula in the finals of both the [2025 Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wuhan_Open) and the [2026 Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) to win her third and fourth WTA 1000 titles respectfully.[^cite_note-\:9-130][^cite_note-\:11-168]

## Coaches

In July 2019, Pegula hired [David Witt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Witt) to be her coach.[^cite_note-witt-180] Witt is a former tennis player and coach who worked with [Venus Williams](167378.md) until they split at the end of 2018.[^cite_note-witt-180] Witt was named the [2022 WTA Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Awards).[^cite_note-181] In February 2024, Pegula ended coaching relationship with Witt.[^cite_note-witt2-182] Witt said the parting was a "total surprise" and he "never saw it coming".[^cite_note-witt2-182] She hired two new coaches: [Mark Knowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Knowles) and [Mark Merklein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Merklein), who are both former players.[^cite_note-mark-2]

## Sponsorships

In 2021, Pegula signed an endorsement deal with [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas).[^cite_note-sponsors-183] Her racquets have been provided by [Yonex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonex).[^cite_note-184] In 2022, Pegula partnered with Ready Nutrition.[^cite_note-185] In 2023, Pegula partnered with [Stella Artois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Artois)[^cite_note-186] and luxury watch brand [De Bethune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bethune),[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] and became a brand ambassador for jewelry brand [Gorjana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorjana_Reidel).[^cite_note-189]

## Personal life

Pegula loves dogs. She celebrated with Maddie, her mini [Australian Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Shepherd), during the trophy ceremony at the [2019 Citi Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-190] In 2023, Dexter, her [German Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Shepherd), died.[^cite_note-dexter-191] In 2025, Tucker, her [Labrador Retriever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Retriever), died.[^cite_note-192] She adopted Desiree, a German Shepherd, and Mabel, a [German Shorthaired Pointer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Shorthaired_Pointer), in 2024 and 2025.[^cite_note-193]

In 2016, Pegula and her sister Kelly opened a quick-serve restaurant called Healthy Scratch in [LECOM Harborcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LECOM_Harborcenter), an [ice hockey](14790.md)-themed [mixed-use development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-use_development) owned by her parents in Buffalo, New York.[^cite_note-194] A Healthy Scratch food truck followed, in 2017.[^cite_note-WGRZ-195] Multiple locations opened in 2018 and 2019, but then began closing after the start of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_New_York_\(state\)) in 2020.[^cite_note-196] The last two locations closed in May 2022.[^cite_note-WGRZ-195]

In 2018, Pegula founded the nonprofit organization A Lending Paw, which aims to connect rescued and trained service animals with those in need.[^cite_note-dexter-191][^cite_note-197]

In 2018, Pegula introduced her own skin-care line called Ready 24.[^cite_note-198]

In October 2021, Pegula married Taylor Gahagen.[^cite_note-199]

In June 2022, Pegula's mother Kim Pegula suffered a cardiac attack.[^cite_note-kim-200] She moved out of the ICU after about a week and started her recovery.[^cite_note-kim-200]

In August 2025, Pegula launched “The Player’s Box” podcast, which she co-hosts alongside fellow players [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys), [Jennifer Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Brady) and [Desirae Krawczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desirae_Krawczyk).[^cite_note-201]

## World TeamTennis

Pegula made her [World TeamTennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_TeamTennis) debut in 2020 joining the [Orlando Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Storm) at the start of the season, which was played at [The Greenbrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greenbrier).[^cite_note-202]

Pegula emerged as one of the top players in the WTT 2020 season. After the dismissal of [Danielle Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Collins), Pegula went on to play women's singles, women's doubles with [Darija Jurak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darija_Jurak), and mixed doubles with [Ken Skupski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Skupski) and [Tennys Sandgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennys_Sandgren). She posted a strong 9–2 record in singles to help the Storm earn a No. 3 seed in the WTT Playoffs. The Storm would ultimately fall to the [Chicago Smash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Smash) in the semifinals.
