**Africa–United States relations** are the political, economic and cultural ties between the [United States](3434750.md) and the independent [African](5334607.md) countries.

## History

Before [World War II](32927.md), the United States dealt directly only with the former American colony of [Liberia](17791.md), the independent nation of [Ethiopia](187749.md), the self-governing dominion of the British Empire of [South Africa](17416221.md) and the semi-independent nation of [Egypt](8087628.md).

### South Africa

Main article: [South Africa–United States relations](15996750.md)

The [United States](3434750.md) and [South Africa](17416221.md) have currently maintained bilateral relations since 1994 after the end of [Apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid), with [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela) as the first black president and head of state, with the new flag first flown on 27 April 1994, following the landslide victory of [South African general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election), which declares 27 April as the first South African public holiday as [Freedom Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Day_\(South_Africa\)). The [ANC](2503.md) represents the first democratic election take place to coincide with its first post-apartheid elections that was recognized by President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) on 10 May 1994. Originally intended to be an interim flag, the new design blended the black-green-yellow tricolour of the African National Congress with the red-white-blue colour scheme found on the flags of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the [Netherlands](21148.md). The six colours were arranged around a Y shape, representing a convergence on a new harmonious future which has led the opinions of the past since the [British settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1820_Settlers) who came to South Africa and the [Afrikaners](18947965.md) with Dutch settlement along with those opinions of the majority Black population between the tribes of [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_people) and the [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_people) peoples along with the former [German colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_South_West_Africa) that once settled by the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Namibians) and the mandate of [South African administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Africa) of [Namibia](21292.md) since independence in 1990, which shares the border between the [two countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia%E2%80%93South_Africa_border) since the end of Apartheid regime.

### Angola

Main article: [Angola–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola%E2%80%93United_States_relations)

The enslaved people of [Angola](701.md) were captured by the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire) and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) traders which transported to tens of thousands of [African slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Americans) especially the descent of [Mestiço](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesti%C3%A7o) that became a vast majority of [Luso-Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Portuguese) that lived in [Portuguese Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola) were taken from the port city of [São Paulo da Assunção de Loanda](18402.md) to the colony of [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) by the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company), later renaming the city of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York) which became part of the [British America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies). With the slave ships docked in [Manhattan](45470.md) were many [enslaved people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery_in_New_York_\(state\)) were bought and sold in auctions by the colonists, while the other slaves were brought to work on sugar and coffee plantations in the area surrounding [Cap-Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Ha%C3%AFtien) (now Cap-Haïtien), which was a major port and center of the French Caribbean colony of [Saint-Domingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue) (later known as [Haiti](13373.md)). The city of [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) once founded by [Estácio de Sá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1cio_de_S%C3%A1) became an important colony for the Portuguese after it was [discovered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_Brazil) by the navigator [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral) as the primary port for the forced migration of enslaved [Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolans_in_Brazil) to [Brazil](3383.md) with a direct route for the transatlantic slave trade was established between the Portuguese colony of Rio de Janeiro and Luanda. Rio de Janeiro is home to the largest Portuguese population outside of [Lisbon](18091.md) and for the settlers from Angola. The [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years'_War) as well as the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War) had led so many campaigns in the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cap-Fran%C3%A7ais) and in [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_campaign) which led the [French defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_Seven_Years'_War) more devastating. Following the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) which led the [surrender of Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown) and the [Evacuation of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_Day_\(New_York\)), some colonists of the newly independent young [United States](3434750.md) wanted to join the British which left [New York City](645042.md) for British-controlled [Canada](5042916.md) as the [United Empire Loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalist) which then became part of [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada). The [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) had begun with the [Storming of the Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_the_Bastille), which was later followed by the [September Massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_Massacres), the establishment of the [French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic), the [Execution of Louis XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_of_Louis_XVI) and [Robespierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilien_Robespierre)'s [Reign of Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign_of_Terror). In Saint Domingue, the black leaders such as [Toussaint Louverture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toussaint_Louverture), [Jean-Jacques Dessalines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Dessalines) and [Henri Christophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Christophe) had defeated the French army of [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) during the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) with Napoleon selling [Louisiana](18130.md), once part of [Spain](26667.md) and [France](5843419.md) to the United States as part of [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) by [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) during his [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Thomas_Jefferson). Following the betrayal and death of Toussaint Louverture at [Fort de Joux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_de_Joux) in France, Dessalines had finally defeated the French army led by [Rochambeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatien-Marie-Joseph_de_Vimeur,_vicomte_de_Rochambeau) at the [Battle of Vertières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Verti%C3%A8res) with a famous victory for the black soldiers once they became [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Haitians) by defeating the [white settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Haitians) of Saint Domingue and finally [declared independence from French rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Declaration_of_Independence) and rename the island of *Ayiti* (Land of High Mountains). Haiti became the world's first and oldest black-led republic, the first country to [abolish slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism), the first [Latin America](18524.md) and [Caribbean](18956035.md) state and the first in the [Greater Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Antilles) as a whole and also the second oldest independent nation in the [Americas](29833.md) after the United States. The [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) went on until 4 April 2002 when the war came to conclude with a victory for the [Movimento Popular de Libertação de Angola – Partido do Trabalho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPLA) for more than 26 years. In [East Flatbush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Flatbush), located in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), New York, has a notable Angolan community, and is known as a hub for the African American community, with parts of the neighborhood known as Little Haiti with 91.4% Black or African-American and 51% foreign-born, with the majority of foreign-born residents being from both [Africa](5334607.md) and Haiti.

### Democratic Republic of the Congo

Main article: [Democratic Republic of the Congo–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo%E2%80%93United_States_relations)

The [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo) proclaimed its independence from [Belgium](3343.md) on 30 June 1960, the actual transfer of sovereignty from Belgium to the Congolese people involved a handover ceremony where [King Baudouin of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baudouin_of_Belgium) declared it, as [President Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) recognized the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md)'s independence from Belgian rule as a new nation in [Africa](5334607.md). While the [United States](3434750.md) and Belgium were concerned about [Lumumba](49240.md)'s policies, particularly his close relationship with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and his perceived communist sympathies. [Joseph-Desiré Mobutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko) came to power and renamed the country [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire) until its overthrow in 1997 by the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War), which was a nationalist move supported by the United States, following the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis) that ended in 1965. Mobutu's rule was characterized by widespread corruption, human rights abuses, and economic hardship by the repressive totalitarian government of which Mobutu had created as the [Mouvement Populaire de la Révolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Movement_of_the_Revolution) (MPR), and was responsible for utilizing various methods to maintain power and silence opposition, including the establishment of a security apparatus to monitor and control the population, by reinforcing fear and intimidation that characterized Mobutu's regime. Mobutu's forces were unable to effectively counter the [ADFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Democratic_Forces_for_the_Liberation_of_Congo-Zaire)'s advance, leading to the collapse of his regime and the Republic of Zaire itself that led the capture of [Kinshasa](45773.md). He was forced to flee the capital in May 1997 that effectively ending Mobutu's long rule, while the rebel leader [Laurent Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila) had changed the country's name back to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, as Mobutu had fled into exile in [Morocco](19291.md), where he later died in the same year. With the DRC marked by significant violence and conflict, with the legacy of both [Belgian colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium%E2%80%93Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_relations) and Mobutu's totalitarian regime. The [Second Congo War](877492.md) manifested in various forms, including atrocities, genocide, starvation, and widespread violence which forced many Congolese individuals to evacuate and fled their country as refugees to the United States and Belgium due to the ongoing violence, instability, and human rights abuses, causing widespread displacement of millions more.

### Liberia

Further information: [Liberia–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia%E2%80%93United_States_relations)

U.S. relations with Liberia date back to 1819, when the Congress appropriated \$100,000 for the establishment of Liberia.[^cite_note-statedeptprofile-2] The settlers were free blacks or freed slaves who were selected and funded by the [American Colonization Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Colonization_Society) (ACS). The religious ethos and cultural norms of the ACS shaped Afro-American settler society and determined social behavior in 19th-century Liberia.[^cite_note-3] The [Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Episcopal_Church) sent black ministers as missionaries to Liberia. Although they could identify with the local population on a purely racial basis, the nature of their religious indoctrination caused them to view the Liberians as inferiors whose souls needed saving.[^cite_note-4]

Under Republican President Abraham Lincoln, The United States officially recognized Liberia in 1862, 15 years after its establishment as a sovereign nation, and the two nations shared very close diplomatic, economic, and military ties until the 1990s.

The United States had a long history of intervening in Liberia's internal affairs, occasionally sending naval vessels to help the Americo-Liberians, who comprised the ruling minority, put down insurrections by indigenous tribes (in 1821, 1843, 1876, 1910, and 1915). By 1909, Liberia faced serious external threats to its sovereignty from the European colonial powers over unpaid foreign loans and annexation of its borderlands.[^cite_note-5]

President [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft) devoted a portion of his First Annual Message to Congress (December 7, 1909) to the Liberian question, noting the close historical ties between the two countries that gave an opening for a wider intervention:

"It will be remembered that the interest of the United States in the Republic of Liberia springs from the historical fact of the foundation of the Republic by the colonization of American citizens of the African race. In an early treaty with Liberia there is a provision under which the United States may be called upon for advice or assistance. Pursuant to this provision and in the spirit of the moral relationship of the United States to Liberia, that Republic last year asked this Government to lend assistance in the solution of certain of their national problems, and hence the Commission was sent across the ocean on two cruisers.

[^cite_note-6]

In 1912, the U.S. arranged a 40-year international loan of \$1.7 million, against which Liberia had to agree to four Western powers (America, Britain, France, and Germany) controlling Liberian government revenues for the next 14 years, until 1926. American administration of the border police also stabilized the frontier with Sierra Leone and checked French ambitions to annex more Liberian territory. The American navy also established a coaling station in Liberia, cementing its presence. When World War I started, Liberia declared war on Germany and expelled its resident German merchants, who constituted the country's largest investors and trading partners – Liberia suffered economically as a result.[^cite_note-7]

In the largest American private investment in Africa, in 1926, the Liberian government gave a concession to the American rubber company [Firestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firestone_Natural_Rubber_Company) to start the world's largest rubber plantation at [Harbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbel), Liberia. At the same time, Firestone arranged a \$5 million private loan to Liberia.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In the 1930s, Liberia was again virtually bankrupt, and, after some American pressure, agreed to an assistance plan from the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations). As part of this plan, two key officials of the League were placed in positions to "advise" the Liberian government.[^cite_note-10]

### Ethiopia

Main article: [Ethiopia–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia%E2%80%93United_States_relations)



Emperor [Haile Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie) and [President Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Nixon) in 1969.

U.S.-Ethiopian relations were established in 1903, after meetings in Ethiopia between Emperor [Menelik II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelik_II_of_Ethiopia) and an emissary of President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt). This first step was augmented with treaties of arbitration and conciliation signed at Addis Ababa 26 January 1929. These formal relations included a grant of [Most Favored Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Favored_Nation) status, and were good up to the [Italian occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italo-Abyssinian_War) in 1935.

Italy invaded and conquered Ethiopia 1935, and evaded League of Nations sanctions. The United States was one of only five countries which refused to recognize the Italian conquest. During World War II, British forces expel the Italians and restored independence to Ethiopia. In January 1944, when President [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt) met personally with Emperor Haile Selassie in Egypt. The meeting both strengthened the Emperor's already strong predilection towards the United States, as well as discomforted the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia-United_Kingdom_relations) who had been at odds with the Ethiopian government over the disposition of [Eritrea](17238590.md) and the [Ogaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden).[^cite_note-11]

In the 1950s, Ethiopia became a minor player in the Cold War after signing a series of treaties with the United States, and receiving \$282 million in military assistance and \$366 million in [economic assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_aid_to_Ethiopia) in [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Ethiopia), [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Ethiopia), public health, and [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Ethiopia). In 1957, Vice President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) visited Ethiopia and called it "one of the United States' most stalwart and consistent allies".[^cite_note-12]

The economic aid came through Washington's "Point Four" program and served as a model for American assistance to the newly independent African nations. The original goal of "Point Four" was containing the spread of communism, which was not a major threat in Africa in the 1950s. More broadly it served as a political project to convince Africans that it was to their long-term interest to side with the West. The program sought to improve social and economic conditions without interfering with existing political or social order.[^cite_note-13]

### Senegal

Main article: [Senegal–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal%E2%80%93United_States_relations)

The island of [Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e) was one of the first places in Africa to be settled by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people_in_Senegal), after it was discovered by the Portuguese in 1444 before it was ceded to the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia_\(Dutch_West_India_Company\)) in 1558, then to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England) in 1664 and finally to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_conquest_of_Senegal) in 1677. Senegal was a catalyst for the slave trade, and from the Homann Heirs map figure shown, shows a starting point for migration and a firm port of trade. Slaves of the [transatlantic slave trade](49005.md) were shipped from their homeland of [West Africa](67393.md) to the British colonies of [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) as they landed in the harbor of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York), where they stood in auctions and were sold to various settlers. After exploration and trade along the West African coast during the 17th century, the two powers of [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) contended for supremacy in the [Senegambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia) region over the following centuries in the [Seven Years War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_capture_of_Senegal), the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune_\(1789%E2%80%931795\)), and the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo) with the defeat of [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon), who was sent to exile and died in [Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longwood_House) in 1821. One of the most important leading figures of the whole of [Francophone Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7afrique) was [Léopold Sédar Senghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor), who was the first African to become a member of the [*French Academy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_Fran%C3%A7aise) in [Paris](22989.md) after he wrote the memoirs of his native country of [Senegal](27278.md) under [French colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) before it finally gained its independence from [France](5843419.md) on 4 April 1960. Senghor served as its first [President of Senegal](450099.md) until 1980, after more than 283 years of French rule, and [Saint Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis,_Senegal) was the capital of Francophone Africa before the seat of government relocated to [Dakar](44251.md). Dakar became one of the most important cities of the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) and the whole of [Francophone Africa (*Black Africa*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc). Dakar is one of tropical Africa's leading industrial and service centers, which enjoys relations with [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Senegal_relations) and the [United States](3434750.md).

### Egypt

Main article: [Egypt–United States relations](15982815.md)

Trade and cultural relations date back to the late 19th century. the Presbyterians and other Protestant organizations sponsored large-scale missionary activity. Small numbers of Egyptians converted to Christianity. Influential circles in the United States gained an increased awareness of the social and economic conditions in Egypt. One major impact was bringing modern educational methods to Egypt, which the local officials and British had largely ignored. The flagship institution was the [American University in Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_in_Cairo), which offered all classes in Arabic, and practiced flexible methods that were adopted in Egyptian-sponsored schools when they began to appear in the 20th century.[^cite_note-14]

Official Modern relations were established in 1922 when the United States recognized Egypt's independence from a protectorate status of the United Kingdom. Britain nevertheless controlled Egyptian foreign affairs, and the United States rarely had direct connections with the Egyptian government. In 1956, the U.S. was alarmed at the closer ties between Egypt and the Soviet Union, and prepared the [OMEGA Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMEGA_Memorandum) as a stick to reduce the regional power of President [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser).[^cite_note-15]

When Egypt recognized [Communist China](5405.md), the U.S. ended talks about funding the [Aswan Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan_Dam), a high-prestige project much desired by Egypt. The dam was later built by the Soviet Union. When Nasser nationalized the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1956, the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis) erupted with Britain and France invading to retake control of the canal. Using heavy diplomatic and economic pressure, the [Eisenhower administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Dwight_D._Eisenhower) forced Britain and France to withdraw soon. A major result was that the United States largely replaced Great Britain in terms of regional influence in the Middle East.[^cite_note-16]

### Others

#### Kingdom of Dahomey

Main article: [Dahomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahomey)

In 1860, the *[Clotilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clotilda_\(slave_ship\))* arrived in the [Kingdom of Dahomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dahomey) to illegally transport 110 slaves to the United States after the U.S. abolished the Atlantic slave trade in 1808 with the [Act Prohibiting Importation of Slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_Prohibiting_Importation_of_Slaves), the last known slave ship to have carried slaves from Africa to the United States.

&#x20;Writing in his journal in 1860, Captain William Foster of the *Clotilda* described how he came in possession of the enslaved Africans on his ship,&#x20;

> from thence I went to see the [King of Dahomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Dahomey). Having agreeably transacted affairs with the Prince we went to the warehouse where they had in confinement four thousand captives in a state of nudity from which they gave me liberty to select one hundred and twenty-five as mine offering to brand them for me, from which I preemptorily  \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*] forbid; commenced taking on cargo of negroes, successfully securing on board one hundred and ten.[^cite_note-foster-17]

#### Togo

In 1901, the [Tuskegee Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuskegee_Institute), a state college in Alabama directed by the national Black leader [Booker T. Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Washington), sent experts to the German colony of Togo in West Africa. The goal was to introduce modern agricultural technology in order to modernize the colony, basing its economy on cotton exports.[^cite_note-18]

### World War II

Vichy France controlled much of North Africa. With the British based in Egypt pushing back German and Italian forces, the United States and Britain launched [Operation Torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch) with amphibious landings in Morocco and Algeria in November 1942. After a brief resistance, the Vichy French forces switched sides and began to collaborate with the Allies. After some delays, the eastern and western Allied forces met up in Tunisia and forced the surrender of the main German and Italian armies. The Americans then moved on to an invasion of Sicily and in southern Italy.[^cite_note-19]

Numerous locations in Africa were used in moving supplies that were either flown in via Brazil or brought in by ship. Supplies were shipped across Africa and moved through Egypt to supply the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-20]

## Contemporary era

### Decolonization, 1951–1961

All the colonial powers engaged in [decolonization](302084.md) in the 1950s, starting with Libya 1951, Sudan, Morocco and Tunisia in 1956, and Ghana in 1957. In 1958 President Eisenhower's State Department created the Bureau of African Affairs under the [Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_State_for_African_Affairs) to deal with sub- Sahara Africa. Countries in North Africa were under the Bureau of Near Eastern Affairs. [G. Mennen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Mennen_Williams), a former Democratic governor of Michigan, was the assistant secretary of state under President John F. Kennedy. Williams actively promoted and encouraged decolonization. The Kennedy administration launched the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps), which sent thousands of young American volunteers to serve in local villages. The [United States Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) (USAID) started providing cash economic assistance, and the Pentagon provided funds and munitions for local armies. Euphoria ended when the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis) of the 1960s indicated very large scale instability.

Historian James Meriweather argues that American policy towards Africa was characterized by a middle road approach, which supported African independence but also reassured European colonial powers that their holdings could remain intact. Washington wanted the right type of African groups to lead newly independent states, which tended to be noncommunist and not especially democratic. Meriweather argues that nongovernmental organizations influenced American policy towards Africa. They pressured state governments and private institutions to disinvest from African nations not ruled by the majority population. These efforts also helped change American policy towards South Africa, as seen with the passage of the [Comprehensive Anti-Apartheid Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Anti-Apartheid_Act) of 1986.[^cite_note-21]

### Kennedy–Johnson administrations, 1961–1969

Further information: [Foreign policy of the John F. Kennedy administration § Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_John_F._Kennedy_administration#Africa), and [Foreign policy of the Lyndon B. Johnson administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Lyndon_B._Johnson_administration)

Whereas Eisenhower had largely neglected Africa, President John F. Kennedy took an aggressive activist approach. Kennedy was alarmed by the implications of Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev's 1961 speech that proclaimed the USSR's intention to intervene in anticolonial struggles around the world. Since most nations in Europe, Latin America, and Asia had already chosen sides, Kennedy and Krushchev both looked to Africa as the next Cold War battleground. Under the leadership of [Sékou Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9kou_Tour%C3%A9), the former French colony of [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Guinea) in West Africa proclaimed its independence in 1958 and immediately sought foreign aid.[^cite_note-22]

Eisenhower was hostile to Touré, so the African nation quickly turned to the Soviet Union--making it the Kremlin's first success story in Africa. Kennedy and his Peace Corps director [Sargent Shriver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargent_Shriver) tried even harder than Khrushchev. He opened up the White House to receive eleven African heads of state in 1961, ten in 1962, and another seven in 1963. [^cite_note-23]

### Carter administration, 1977–1981

Further information: [Foreign policy of the Jimmy Carter administration § Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Jimmy_Carter_administration#Africa)

Historians are generally agreed that President Jimmy Carter, 1977–81, was not very successful when it came to Africa. However, there are multiple explanations available.[^cite_note-24] The orthodox interpretation posits Carter as a dreamy star-eyed idealist. Revisionists said that did not matter nearly as much as the intense rivalry between dovish Secretary of State [Cyrus Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Vance) and hawkish National Security Adviser [Zbigniew Brzezinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zbigniew_Brzezinski). Vance lost nearly all the battles, and finally resigned in disgust.[^cite_note-25]

Meanwhile, there are now post-revisionist historians who blame his failures on his confused management style and his refusal to make tough decisions.[^cite_note-26] Along post-revisionist lines, Nancy Mitchell in a monumental book depicts Carter as a decisive but ineffective Cold Warrior, who, nevertheless had some successes because Soviet incompetence was even worse.[^cite_note-27]

### Reagan–Bush administrations, 1981–1993

Further information: [Foreign policy of the Ronald Reagan administration § Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Ronald_Reagan_administration#Sub-Saharan_Africa), and [Foreign policy of the George H. W. Bush administration § Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_George_H._W._Bush_administration#Africa)

The [Presidency of Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ronald_Reagan), starting in January 1981, decided that the Carter administration had largely failed in Africa, and sharply changed directions. It abandoned the Carter emphasis on human rights, and instead emphasized anti-communism. This meant reversals of policies in South Africa and Angola, and changes in the USA's relationships with Ethiopia and Libya, which were pro-Soviet.

Carter's policy was to denounce and try to end the apartheid policies in South Africa and Angola, whereby small white minorities had full control of government. Under the new conservative administration a more conciliatory approach was taken by the Assistant Secretary of State [Chester Crocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_Crocker). He rejected confrontational approaches, and called for "Constructive engagement". Crocker was highly critical of the outgoing Carter administration for its apparent hostility to the white minority government in South Africa, by acquiescing in the United Nations Security Council's imposition of a mandatory arms embargo ([UNSCR 418/77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_418)) and the UN's demand for the end of South Africa's illegal occupation of [Namibia](21292.md) ([UNSCR 435/78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_435)).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

Crocker came up with a complex multinational peace plan, that he struggled to achieve for eight years. The most essential provision required the removal of [Cuba's large, well-armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola%E2%80%93Cuba_relations) from [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_in_Angola). The United States would then quit funding the anti-Marxist forces led by [Jonas Savimbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Savimbi), South Africa would pull out of Southwest Africa, allowing Namibia to become independent. The white rulers in South Africa would remain in power but relax their restrictions on the [African National Congress](2503.md). Crocker was successful in 1988, and during the George HW Bush administration, 1989 to 1993, the goals were realized.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

The Reagan administration mobilized private philanthropic and business sources to fund food supplies to areas in Africa devastated by famine. For example, music promoter [Bob Geldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Geldorf) in 1985 produced [Live Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Aid), a benefit concert that raised over \$65 million and dramatically raised awareness. US government focused on transportation and administrative issues.[^cite_note-32]

## Agreement

The Trump administration is using economic ties and access to minerals to strengthen America's geopolitical position vis-à-vis China and Russia. The Trump administration has already taken steps toward these changes. Ambassadors in Africa have been repurposed more as negotiators than diplomats and advisors to the Trump administration.
