The **United States secretary of defense** (**SecDef**), secondarily titled the **secretary of war** (**SecWar**),[^cite_note-9] is the head of the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) (DoD), the [executive department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments) of the [U.S. Armed Forces](32212.md), and is a high-ranking member of the [Cabinet of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-5100.1-11][^cite_note-\:0-12] The secretary of defense's position of command and authority over the military is second only to that of the [president of the United States](24113.md), who is the [commander-in-chief](2015257.md#Commander-in-chief). This position corresponds to what is generally known as a [defense minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Minister) in many other countries.[^cite_note-13] The president appoints the secretary of defense with the [advice and consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) of the [Senate](24909346.md), and is by custom a member of the Cabinet and by law a member of the [National Security Council](44026.md).[^cite_note-14]

Subject only to the orders of the president, the secretary of defense is in the [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_of_command) and exercises [command and control](7092305.md), for both operational and administrative purposes, over all DoD-administered service branches – the [Army](32087.md), [Marine Corps](17349325.md), [Navy](20518076.md), [Air Force](32090.md), and [Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) – as well as the [Coast Guard](32223.md) when its command and control is transferred to the Department of Defense.[^cite_note-UnitedStatesCode|10|162-15][^cite_note-JP1\:II9-11-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Only the secretary of defense (or the president or Congress) can authorize the transfer of operational control of forces between the three military departments ([Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army), [the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), and [the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force)) and the eleven [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command).[^cite_note-UnitedStatesCode|10|162-15]

To ensure [civilian control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military), U.S. law provides that the secretary of defense cannot have served as an active-duty [commissioned officer](36301328.md#Commission_sources_and_training) in the military in the preceding seven years except for [generals](2965165.md) and [admirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)), who cannot have served on active duty within the previous ten years. Congress can legislatively waive this restriction[^cite_note-20] and has done so three times, for [George C. Marshall Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_C._Marshall_Jr.), [James N. Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_N._Mattis), and [Lloyd J. Austin III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_J._Austin_III). The [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md) is the principal military advisor to the secretary of defense and the president; while the chairman may assist the secretary and president in their command functions, the chairman is not in the chain of command.[^cite_note-21]

Because the secretary of defense is vested with legal powers that exceed those of any commissioned officer, and is second only to the president in the military hierarchy, its incumbent has sometimes unofficially been referred to as "deputy commander-in-chief".[^cite_note-DoD50\:11-52-22][^cite_note-SupCom\:231-23][^cite_note-24] The [secretary of state](32293.md), the [secretary of the treasury](43338.md), the secretary of defense, and the [attorney general](44002.md) are generally regarded as the four most important (and are officially the four most senior and oldest) cabinet officials because of the size and importance of their respective departments.[^cite_note-25]

The current secretary of defense is [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md), who was nominated by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and was confirmed by the Senate on 25 January 2025.

## History



Seal of the [National Military Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Military_Establishment) (1947–1949)

An Army, Navy, and Marine Corps were established in 1775, in concurrence with the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). They were managed under a committee of the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress). [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams) was the early chair of this committee. Under, the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation), the [Confederation Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Congress) created a secretary to manage the military, sometimes called the Secretary at War, in 1781. [Benjamin Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Lincoln) and then [Henry Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Knox) served as secretary during the Confederation. The [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War), headed by the [secretary of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_War), was created by [Act of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Congress) in 1789 and was responsible for both the Army and Navy (including, the Marines) until the founding of a separate [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) in 1798.



[Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld) is sworn in as Secretary of Defense by [Associate Supreme Court Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States) [Potter Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter_Stewart) as [U.S. President](24113.md) [Gerald R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) and [Chairman of The Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md) General [George S. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Scratchley_Brown) watch at [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) on 20 November 1975.

Based on the experiences of [World War II](32927.md), proposals were soon made on how to more effectively manage the large combined military establishment. The Army generally favored centralization while the Navy had institutional preferences for decentralization and the status quo. The resulting [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) was largely a compromise between these divergent viewpoints. It renamed the Department of War the [Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Army), and added both it and the Department of the Navy to a newly established [National Military Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Military_Establishment) (NME). The act also separated the [Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) from the Army to become its own branch of service in the NME, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) under its own [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Air_Force).

A new title was coined by the act for the head of the NME: Secretary of Defense. At first, each of the service secretaries maintained cabinet status. The first secretary of defense, [James Forrestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Forrestal), who in his previous capacity as the [secretary of the Navy](44001.md) had opposed the creation of the new position, found it difficult to exercise authority over the other branches with the limited powers his office had at the time. To address this and other problems, the National Security Act was amended in 1949 to further consolidate the national defense structure in order to reduce [interservice rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interservice_rivalry), directly subordinate the [secretaries of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Army), the Navy and the [Air Force](305210.md) to the secretary of defense in the chain of command, and rename the National Military Establishment as the Department of Defense, making it one [Executive Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Department). The position of the [deputy secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense), the number two position in the department, was also created at this time.

The general trend since 1949 has been to further centralize management in the Department of Defense, elevating the status and authorities of civilian [OSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense) appointees and defense-wide organizations at the expense of the military departments and the services within them. The last major revision of the statutory framework concerning the position was done in the [Goldwater–Nichols Department of Defense Reorganization Act of 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldwater%E2%80%93Nichols_Department_of_Defense_Reorganization_Act_of_1986). In particular, it elevated the status of joint service for commissioned officers, making it in practice a requirement before appointments to general officer and flag officer grades could be made.

As the secretary of defense is a civilian position intended to be independent of the active-duty leadership, a secretary is required to have been retired from service for at least seven (originally ten) years unless a waiver is approved by Congress.[^cite_note-26] Since the creation of the position in 1947, such a waiver has been approved only three times, for Army general [George Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marshall) in 1950, Marine Corps General [Jim Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Mattis) in 2017, and retired Army general [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin) in 2021.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## Powers and functions

Main article: [Organizational structure of the United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_structure_of_the_United_States_Department_of_Defense)



Department of Defense organizational chart (December 2013)

The secretary of defense, appointed by the [president](24113.md) with the advice and consent of the [Senate](24909346.md), is by federal law ([10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 113](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/113)) the head of the Department of Defense, "the principal assistant to the President in all matters relating to Department of Defense", and has "authority, direction and control over the Department of Defense". Because the [Constitution](31644.md) vests all military authority in [Congress](31756.md) and the president, the statutory authority of the secretary of defense is derived from their constitutional authorities. Since it is impractical for either Congress or the president to participate in every piece of Department of Defense affairs, the secretary of defense and the secretary's subordinate officials generally exercise military authority.

As the head of DoD, all officials, employees and service members are "under" the secretary of defense. Some of those high-ranking officials, civil and military (outside of OSD and the Joint Staff) are: the [secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Army), [secretary of the Navy](44001.md), and [secretary of the Air Force](305210.md), [Army chief of staff](485507.md), [commandant of the Marine Corps](7708.md), [chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations), [Air Force chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), [chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_space_operations), and [chief of the National Guard Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_National_Guard_Bureau) and the combatant commanders of the [combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatant_commands). All these high-ranking positions, civil and military, require Senate confirmation.

The Department of Defense is composed of the [Office of the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense) (OSD), the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) (JCS) and the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff) (JS), [Office of the Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Inspector_General,_U.S._Department_of_Defense) (DODIG), the combatant commands, the Military Departments ([Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army) (DA), [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) (DON) & [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force) (DAF)), the [Defense Agencies and DoD Field Activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Department_of_Defense_agencies), the [National Guard Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_Bureau) (NGB), and such other offices, agencies, activities, organizations, and commands established or designated by law, or by the president or by the secretary of defense.

Department of Defense Directive 5100.01 describes the organizational relationships within the department and is the foundational issuance for delineating the major functions of the department. The latest version, signed by former secretary of defense [Robert Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gates) in December 2010, is the first major re-write since 1987.[^cite_note-DOMP-29][^cite_note-5100.1\:1-30]

### Office of the Secretary of Defense

Main article: [Office of the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense)

The secretary's principally civilian staff element is called the [Office of the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense) (OSD) and is composed of the [deputy secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense) (DEPSECDEF) and six under secretaries of defense in the fields of [acquisition & sustainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Acquisition,_Technology_and_Logistics), [research & engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Research_and_Engineering), [comptroller/chief financial officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_\(Comptroller\)), [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Intelligence), [personnel & readiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Personnel_and_Readiness), and [policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Policy); several [assistant secretaries of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_Defense); other directors and the staffs under them. The Secretary of Defense is issuing through the Office of the Secretary of Defense the [National Defense Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Strategy_\(United_States\)), a major policy document.

The name of the principally military staff organization, organized under the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, is the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff) (JS).

### Awards and decorations

The [Defense Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Distinguished_Service_Medal) (DDSM), the [Defense Superior Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Superior_Service_Medal) (DSSM), the [Defense Meritorious Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Meritorious_Service_Medal) (DMSM), the [Joint Service Commendation Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Service_Commendation_Medal) (JSCM) and the [Joint Service Achievement Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Service_Achievement_Medal) (JSAM) are awarded, to military personnel for service in joint duty assignments, in the name of the secretary of defense. In addition, there is the [Joint Meritorious Unit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Meritorious_Unit_Award) (JMUA), which is the only ribbon (as in non-medal) and unit award issued to joint DoD activities, also issued in the name of the secretary of defense.

The DDSM is analogous to the distinguished services medals issued by the military departments (i.e. [Army Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Distinguished_Service_Medal), [Navy Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Distinguished_Service_Medal) & [Air Force Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Distinguished_Service_Medal)), the DSSM corresponds to the [Legion of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Merit), the DMSM to the [Meritorious Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritorious_Service_Medal_\(United_States\)), the JSCM to the service commendation medals, and the JSAM to the achievement medals issued by the services. While the approval authority for DSSM, DMSM, JSCM, JSAM and JMUA is delegated to inferior DoD officials: the DDSM can be awarded only by the secretary of defense.

Recommendations for the [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor) (MOH), formally endorsed in writing by the secretary of the military department concerned and the [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md), are processed through the [under secretary of defense for personnel and readiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Personnel_and_Readiness), and such recommendations be must approved by the secretary of defense before it can be handed over to the president, who is the final approval authority for the MOH, although it is awarded in the name of [Congress](31756.md).

The secretary of defense, with the concurrence of the [secretary of state](32293.md), is the approval authority for the acceptance and wear of [NATO](21133.md) medals issued by the [secretary general of NATO](22194433.md) and offered to the [U.S. permanent representative to NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Permanent_Representative_to_NATO) in recognition of U.S. servicemembers who meet the eligibility criteria specified by NATO.[^cite_note-1348.33,_V3\:39-31]

### Congressional committees

As the head of the department, the secretary of defense is the chief witness for the congressional committees with oversight responsibilities over the Department of Defense. The most important committees, with respect to the entire department, are the two authorizing committees, the [Senate Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services) (SASC) and the [House Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Armed_Services) (HASC), and the two appropriations committees, the [Senate Appropriations Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Appropriations) and the [House Appropriations Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Appropriations).

For the DoD intelligence programs the [Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence) and the [House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Permanent_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence) have the principal oversight role.

### National Security Council

The secretary of defense is a statutory member of the [National Security Council](44026.md).[^cite_note-32] As one of the principals, the secretary along with the [vice president](32759.md), secretary of state and the [assistant to the president for national security affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_to_the_President_for_National_Security_Affairs) participates in biweekly Principals Committee (PC) meetings, preparing and coordinating issues before they are brought before full NSC sessions chaired by the president.

### Role in the military justice system

The secretary is one of only five or six civilians authorized to act as [convening authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convening_authority) in the [military justice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Code_of_Military_Justice) for [General Courts-Martial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts-martial_in_the_United_States) ([10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 822](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/822): article 22, [UCMJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Code_of_Military_Justice)), Special Courts-Martial ([10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 823](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/823): article 23, UCMJ), and Summary Courts-Martial ([10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 824](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/824): article 24 UCMJ). The others are the president, [secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Army), [secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Navy), [secretary of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Air_Force), and [secretary of homeland security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Homeland_Security) (when the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) is under the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) and has not been transferred to the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) under the Department of Defense).

## Salary

The secretary of defense is a [Level I position in the Executive Schedule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Schedule#Level_I),[^cite_note-UnitedStatesCode|5|5312-5] thus earning a salary of [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Dollar)246,400, as of October 2024.[^cite_note-Salary-33]

## List of secretaries of defense

The longest-serving secretary of defense is [Robert McNamara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McNamara_as_Secretary_of_Defense), who served for a total of 7 years, 39 days. Combining his two non-sequential services as the secretary of defense, the second-longest serving is [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld), who served just ten days fewer than McNamara. The second-longest unbroken tenure was [Caspar Weinberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_Weinberger)'s, at 6 years, 306 days.

The shortest-serving secretary of defense is [Elliot Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Richardson), who served 114 days and then was appointed [U.S. attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Attorney_General) amid the [resignations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kleindienst) of the [Watergate Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watergate_Scandal) (this is not counting [deputy secretaries of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense) [William P. Clements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Clements) and [William Howard Taft IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft_IV), who each served a few weeks as temporary/acting secretary of defense).

For precursors to this position before the establishment of the Department of Defense, see the lists of [secretaries of the Navy](44001.md#Executive_Department,_1798%E2%80%931949) and [secretaries of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_War#Secretary_of_War_\(1789%E2%80%931947\)) before 1947.

## Succession

### Presidential succession

The secretary of defense is sixth in the [presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_line_of_succession), after the [secretary of the treasury](43338.md) and before the [attorney general](44002.md).[^cite_note-70]
