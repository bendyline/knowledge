The **hectare** ([/ˈhɛktɛər, -tɑːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) symbol: **ha**) is a [non-SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-SI_units_mentioned_in_the_SI) [metric unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_unit) of [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area) equal to a square with 100-[metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) sides (1 hm<sup>2</sup>), that is, 10,000 square metres (10,000 m<sup>2</sup>), and is primarily used in the measurement of land. There are 100 hectares in one square kilometre. An [acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre) is about 0.405 hectares and thus one hectare is about 2.47 acres.



St. Enda's GAA ground, in [Omagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omagh). The playing field used in [Gaelic football](37688.md) and [hurling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurling) is a little over a hectare in size.

In 1795, when the metric system was introduced, the ***are*** was defined as 100 [square metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_metre), or one square [decametre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decametre), and the hectare ("[hecto-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecto-)" + "are") was thus 100 *ares* or ⁠1/100⁠ km<sup>2</sup> (10000 square metres). When the metric system was further rationalised in 1960, resulting in the [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) (SI), the *are* was not included as a recognised unit, and from 2026 the hectare is no longer described by the [International Bureau of Weights and Measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Weights_and_Measures) (BIPM) as accepted for use with the SI.[^cite_note-SIBrochure9-2026-1]

The name was coined in French, from the Latin *ārea*.[^cite_note-3] The hectare is equivalent to 10,000 square metres (a square hectometre). It is widely used throughout the world for the measurement of large areas of land,[^cite_note-sibrochuretable6-4] and it is the legal unit of measure in some domains concerned with land ownership, planning, and [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_management), including law ([land deeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deed)), agriculture, [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), and [town planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_planning) throughout the [European Union](9317.md),[^cite_note-5] [South Africa](17416221.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Australia](4689264.md), Canada and the United Kingdom. However, the United States and Myanmar (Burma)[^cite_note-World_Factbook-6][^cite_note-7] use the [acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre) instead of the hectare for measuring surface or land area.

In many countries, metrification redefined or clarified existing measures in terms of metric units. The following legacy units of area have been redefined as being equal to one hectare:[^cite_note-8]

In Mexico, land area measurements are commonly given as combinations of hectares, ares, and centiares.[^cite_note-12] These are commonly written separated by a [hyphen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen); for example, *1-21-00.26 ha* would mean 1 hectare, 21 are, and 0.26 centiare (12100.26 m<sup>2</sup>).

## History

The metric system of measurement was first given a legal basis in 1795 by the [French Revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) government. The law of [18 Germinal, Year III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Republican_Calendar) (7 April 1795) defined five units of measure:[^cite_note-13]

- The [metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) for length

- The [are](#Are) (100 m

  2

  ) for area \[of land]

- The [stère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A8re) (1 m

  3

  ) for volume of stacked firewood

  [^cite_note-14]

- The [litre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litre) (1 dm

  3

  ) for volumes of liquid

- The [gram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) for mass

In 1960, when the metric system was updated as the [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) (SI), the *are* did not receive international recognition. The [International Committee for Weights and Measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_for_Weights_and_Measures) (CIPM) used to classify the hectare as a "Non-SI unit accepted for use with the International System of Units"[^cite_note-15] but As of 2026, the [International Bureau of Weights and Measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Weights_and_Measures) (BIPM) no longer describes it and other widely used non-SI units as accepted.[^cite_note-SIBrochure9-2026-1]

In 1972, the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) (EEC) passed directive 71/354/EEC,[^cite_note-16] which catalogued the units of measure that might be used within the Community. The units that were catalogued replicated the recommendations of the CGPM, supplemented by a few other units including the *are* (and implicitly the *hectare*) whose use was limited to the measurement of land.

## Unit family



&#x20;Definition of a *hectare* and of an *are*

The names *centiare*, *deciare*, *decare* and *hectare* are derived by adding the standard [metric prefixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix) to the original base unit of area, the *are*.

### Decimilliare

The **decimilliare** (dma, sometimes seen in [cadastre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadastre) area evaluation of real estate plots) is ⁠1/10,000⁠ are or one square decimetre.[^cite_note-17] Such usage of a [double prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix#Double_prefixes) is non-standard. The decimilliare is (100 mm)<sup>2</sup> or roughly a four-inch-by-four-inch square. (The decimilliare is smaller by approximately 3.1%.)

### Centiare

The **centiare** is one square metre.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Deciare

The **deciare** (rarely used) is ten square metres.[^cite_note-20]

### Are

The **are** ([/ɛər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English),[/ɛr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]) is a unit of area, equal to 100 square metres (10 m × 10 m), used for measuring land area. It was defined by older forms of the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system), but is now outside the modern [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) (SI).[^cite_note-23] It is still commonly used in speech to measure real estate, in particular in Indonesia, India, and in various European countries.

In [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and some other languages of the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the *are* is called *sotka* ([сотка](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D1%80): 'a hundred', i.e. 100 m<sup>2</sup> or ⁠1/100⁠ hectare). It is used to describe the size of suburban [dacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacha) or [allotment garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotment_garden) plots or small city parks where the hectare would be too large. Many Russian dachas are 6 ares in size (in Russian, шесть соток).

### Decare

The **decare** or **dekare** ([/ˈdɛkɑːr, -ɛər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is derived from *[deca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deca-)* and *are*, and is equal to 10 ares or 1000 square metres. It is used in [Norway](21241.md)[^cite_note-24] and in the former [Ottoman](22278.md) areas of the [Middle East](19323.md) and [Bulgaria](3415.md)[^cite_note-25] as a measure of land area. The names of the older land measures of similar size are usually used, redefined as exactly one decare:

- *[Stremma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stremma)* in [Greece](12108.md)

  [^cite_note-26]

- *[Dunam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam), dunum, dulum, donum, or dönüm* in the [Balkans](4829.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Palestine](241405.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Syria](7515849.md), and [Turkey](11125639.md)

  [^cite_note-27]

- *[Mål](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_units_of_measurement#Area)* in Norway.

## Unicode

The [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) character U+33CA ㏊ SQUARE HA, in the [CJK Compatibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJK_Compatibility) block, is intended for compatibility with pre-existing East Asian character codes.[^cite_note-31] It is not intended for use in alphabetic contexts. U+3336 ㌶ SQUARE HEKUTAARU is a combination of ヘクタール (*hekutāru*), the Japanese translation of "hectare".
