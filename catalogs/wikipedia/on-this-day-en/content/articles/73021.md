**Cumbria** ([/ˈkʌmbriə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*KUM-bree-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_county) in [North West England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_England). It borders the Scottish council areas of [Dumfries and Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumfries_and_Galloway) and [Scottish Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Borders) to the north; the English ceremonial counties of [Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland) and [County Durham](400297.md) to the east, [North Yorkshire](143759.md) to the south-east, and [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) to the south; and the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea) to the west. Its largest settlement is the city of [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle).

Cumbria is predominantly rural, with an area of 6,769 km<sup>2</sup> (2,614 sq mi) and an estimated population of 510,680 in 2024. Carlisle is in the north of the county; the towns of [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington) and [Whitehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehaven) lie on the west coast, [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness) on the south coast, and [Penrith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith,_Cumbria) and [Kendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal) in the east. For local government purposes the county comprises two [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) areas, [Westmorland and Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland_and_Furness) and [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_\(unitary_authority_area\)), the councils of which collaborate through the [Cumbria Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_Combined_Authority). Cumbria was created in 1974 from the historic counties of [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland) and [Westmorland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland), the [Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furness) area of Lancashire, and a small part of [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire).

The interior of Cumbria contains several upland areas. Together they fringe the [Vale of Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_Eden), the wide valley of the [River Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Eden,_Cumbria), which runs south-east to north-west across the county and broadens into the [Solway Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solway_Plain) near Carlisle.[^cite_note-Wastwater_and_the_Lake_District_West_Coast_-_explore_and_visit-5][^cite_note-Lake_District_National_Park_-_Explore_Windermere-6] To the north-east are part of the [Border Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Moors_and_Forests), and to the east part of the [North Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pennines); the latter have been designated a [national landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Landscape). South of the vale are the [Orton Fells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orton_Fells), [Howgill Fells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howgill_Fells), and part of the [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales), which are all within the [Yorkshire Dales national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales_National_Park).[^cite_note-An_introduction_to_the_Yorkshire_Dales_in_the_County_of_Cumbria-7] The south-west contains the [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District), a large upland area which has been designated a [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md). It includes England's highest mountain, [Scafell Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scafell_Pike);[^cite_note-Cumbrian_Mountains:_''Philips'_Elementary_Atlas_and_Geography''-8] its longest and largest lake, [Windermere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windermere); and its deepest lake, [Wast Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wast_Water). The county has a long coast to the west which is bordered by a plain for most of its length. The north-west coast is part of the [Solway Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solway_Firth), a national landscape, and the south coast includes the [Cartmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartmel_Peninsula) and [Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furness) peninsulas. East of the peninsulas, the county contains part of [Arnside and Silverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnside_and_Silverdale), another national landscape.

The county contains several [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) monuments, such as [Mayburgh Henge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayburgh_Henge). The region was on the border of [Roman Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain), and [Hadrian's Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian's_Wall) runs through the north of the county. In the Early Middle Ages parts of the region successively belonged to [Rheged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheged), [Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria), and [Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Strathclyde), and there was also a [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) presence. It became the border between England and Scotland, and was unsettled until the Union of the Crowns in 1603. During the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) mining took place on the [Cumberland coalfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_Coalfield) and Barrow-in-Furness became a shipbuilding centre, but the county was not heavily industrialised and the Lake District became valued for its [sublime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_\(philosophy\)) and [picturesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picturesque) qualities, notably by the [Lake Poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Poets).

## Toponymy

Main article: [Cumbrian toponymy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbrian_toponymy)

The place names *Cumbria* and *Cumberland* both mean *"land of the Cumbrians"* and are names derived from the term that had been used by the inhabitants of the area to describe themselves. In the period c. 400 – c. 1100, it is likely that any group of people living in Britain who identified as 'Britons' called themselves by a name similar to 'Cum-ri' which means "fellow countrymen" (and has also survived in the Welsh name for Wales which is *Cymru*).[^cite_note-9] The first datable record of the place name as *Cumberland* is from an entry in the [Anglo-Saxon Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle) for the year AD 945.[^cite_note-10] This record refers to a kingdom known to the Anglo-Saxons as *Cumbraland* and in Latin as *Cumbria*, often also known as [Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Strathclyde),[^cite_note-Koch-11] which in the 10th century may have stretched from Loch Lomond to Leeds.[^cite_note-12] The first king to be unequivocally described as king of the Cumbrians is [Owain ap Dyfnwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owain_ap_Dyfnwal_\(fl._934\)), who ruled from c. 915 – c. 937.[^cite_note-13]

## History

Main article: [History of Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cumbria)



The [Castlerigg stone circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlerigg_stone_circle) dates from the late [Neolithic age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_age) and was constructed by some of the earliest inhabitants of Cumbria

During the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period what is now Cumbria area contained an important centre of stone axe production (the so-called [Langdale axe factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langdale_axe_industry)), products of which have been found across Great Britain.[^cite_note-14] During this period, [stone circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_circle) and [henges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henge) were built across the county, and today, Cumbria has one of the largest number of preserved field monuments in England'.[^cite_note-15]

While not part of the region conquered in the [Romans' initial conquest of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_conquest_of_Britain) in AD 43, most of modern-day Cumbria was later conquered in response to a revolt deposing the Roman-aligned ruler of the [Brigantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigantes) in AD 69.[^cite_note-16] The Romans built a number of fortifications in the area during their occupation, the most famous being [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) [Hadrian's Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian's_Wall) which passes through northern Cumbria.[^cite_note-17]

At the end of the period of British history known as [Roman Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) (c. AD 410) the inhabitants of Cumbria were [Cumbric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbric)-speaking native [Celtic Britons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Britons) who were probably descendants of the [Brigantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigantes) and [Carvetii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carvetii) (sometimes considered to be a sub-tribe of the Brigantes) that the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) had conquered in about AD 85. Based on inscriptional evidence from the area, the Roman *_[civitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitas)_* of the Carvetii seems to have covered portions of Cumbria. The names *Cumbria*, *_Cymru_* (the native [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) name for [Wales](69894.md)), *Cambria*, and *Cumberland* are derived from the name these people gave themselves, \**kombroges* in [Common Brittonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Brittonic), which originally meant "compatriots".[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Davies-19]

Although Cumbria was previously believed to have formed the core of the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages) [Brittonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Britons) kingdom of [Rheged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheged), more recent discoveries near [Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galloway) appear to contradict this.[^cite_note-20] For the rest of the first millennium, Cumbria was contested by several entities who warred over the area, including the Brythonic Celtic [Kingdom of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Strathclyde) and the [Anglian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_\(tribe\)) [Kingdom of Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria). Most of modern-day Cumbria was a principality in the Kingdom of [Scotland](26994.md) at the time of the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) of England in 1066 and thus was excluded from the [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) survey of 1086.[^cite_note-21]

In 1092 the region was invaded by [William II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_II_of_England) and incorporated into England.[^cite_note-22] In 1133 the diocese of Carlisle was founded, cementing Cumbria's position within the English church.[^cite_note-23] Cumbria left Scottish hands for the last time in 1157. Nevertheless, the region was dominated by the many [Anglo-Scottish Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Scottish_Wars) of the latter [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period) and the associated [Border Reivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Reivers) who exploited the dynamic political situation of the region.[^cite_note-24] There were at least three [sieges of Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Carlisle_\(disambiguation\)) fought between England and Scotland, and two further sieges during the [Jacobite risings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_risings).

After the Jacobite Risings of the 18th century, Cumbria became a more stable place and, as in the rest of [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England), the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) caused a large growth in urban populations. In particular, the west coast towns of [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington), [Millom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millom) and [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness) saw large iron and [steel mills](1143008.md) develop, with Barrow also developing a significant [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) industry.[^cite_note-25] [Kendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal), [Keswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keswick,_Cumbria) and Carlisle all became [mill towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_town), with textiles, pencils and biscuits among the products manufactured in the region.[^cite_note-26]

The early 19th century saw the county gain fame when the [Lake Poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Poets) and other artists of the [Romantic movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_movement), such as [William Wordsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wordsworth) and [Samuel Taylor Coleridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Taylor_Coleridge), lived among, and were inspired by, the lakes and mountains of the region. Later, the children's writer [Beatrix Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrix_Potter) wrote in the region and became a major landowner, granting much of her property to the [National Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust) on her death.[^cite_note-27] The large amount of land owned by the National Trust assisted in the formation in 1951 of the [Lake District National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District_National_Park), which remains the largest National Park in England and has come to dominate the identity and economy of the county.

The [Windscale fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windscale_fire) of 10 October 1957 was the worst [nuclear accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_accident) in Great Britain's history.[^cite_note-28]

Cumbria was created in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972) from the [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties) of [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland) and [Westmorland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland); the county boroughs of [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Borough_of_Carlisle) and Barrow-in-Furness; the districts of [North Lonsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Lonsdale), Grange, Ulverston, and Dalton-in-Furness from [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire); and the [Sedbergh Rural District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedbergh_Rural_District) from the [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire).[^cite_note-lga1972-29] [Cumbria County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_County_Council) and the seven district councils were abolished in 2023 and replaced by two new unitary authorities, [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_\(unitary_authority_area\)) Council and [Westmorland and Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland_and_Furness) Council.[^cite_note-\:0-30]

On 2 June 2010, taxi driver Derrick Bird killed 12 people and injured 11 others in [a shooting spree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_shootings) that spanned over 24 kilometres (15 mi) along the Cumbrian coastline.[^cite_note-times-31]

## Geography



A topographic map of Cumbria

Cumbria is the most northwesterly ceremonial county of England and is mostly mountainous, with large upland areas to the south-west and east. The south-west contains the [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District), a [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [UNESCO world heritage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_world_heritage_site) which includes [Scafell Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scafell_Pike), England's highest mountain at 978 metres (3,209 ft),[^cite_note-Cumbrian_Mountains:_''Philips'_Elementary_Atlas_and_Geography''-8] and [Windermere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windermere), its longest and largest lake. The [Border Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Moors_and_Forests) and [North Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pennines) lie along the county's eastern border. The south-east contains the [Orton Fells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orton_Fells), [Howgill Fells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howgill_Fells) and part of the [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales), which are all within the [Yorkshire Dales national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales_National_Park).[^cite_note-An_introduction_to_the_Yorkshire_Dales_in_the_County_of_Cumbria-7]

The [Vale of Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_Eden), the valley of the [River Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Eden,_Cumbria), runs south-east to north-west between these upland areas, and broadens into the [Solway Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solway_Plain) near Carlisle.[^cite_note-Wastwater_and_the_Lake_District_West_Coast_-_explore_and_visit-5][^cite_note-Lake_District_National_Park_-_Explore_Windermere-6] The county has long coast to the west, which is bordered by a plain for most of its length. In the north-west it borders the [Solway Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solway_Firth), a [national landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Landscape), and to the south are the [Cartmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartmel_Peninsula) and [Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furness) peninsulas.&#x20;

East of the peninsulas, the county contains part of [Arnside and Silverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnside_and_Silverdale), also a national landscape. The [Lancaster Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_Canal) runs from Preston into southern Cumbria and is partly in use. The [Ulverston Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulverston_Canal) which once reached to [Morecambe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morecambe_Bay) is maintained although it was closed in 1945.

The county contains a number of [Sites of Special Scientific Interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sites_of_Special_Scientific_Interest_in_Cumbria), several of which are grouped into the [Lake District High Fells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District_High_Fells), which is a designated [Special Area of Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Area_of_Conservation).

### Boundaries and divisions

The northernmost and southernmost points in Cumbria are just west of [Deadwater, Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadwater,_Northumberland) and [South Walney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walney_Island) respectively. [Kirkby Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkby_Stephen) (close to [Tan Hill, North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Hill,_North_Yorkshire)) and [St Bees Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bees_Head) are the most easterly and westerly points of the county. The boundaries are along the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea) to [Morecambe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morecambe_Bay) in the west, and along the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) to the east. Cumbria's northern boundary stretches from the [Solway Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solway_Firth) from the [Solway Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solway_Plain) eastward along the border with [Scotland](26994.md).

Cumbria is bordered by [Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland), [County Durham](400297.md), [North Yorkshire](143759.md), [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) in England, and [Dumfries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumfriesshire) and [Roxburgh, Ettrick and Lauderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxburgh,_Ettrick_and_Lauderdale) in Scotland.

## Economy



[BAE Systems Submarine Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Submarine_Solutions) in Barrow-in-Furness has a workforce of around 14,500 people

Many large companies and organisations are based in Cumbria. The county council itself employs around 17,000 individuals, while the largest private employer in Cumbria, [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) in Barrow employs around 14,500[^cite_note-32] with further job growth associated with new contracts expected, the [Sellafield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sellafield) nuclear processing site, has a workforce of 10,000.[^cite_note-Largestemployers1-33]

The largest and most widespread industry is tourism, with the county attracting over 47 million visitors annually.[^cite_note-34] The [Lake District National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District) alone receives some 15.8 million visitors every year.[^cite_note-LakeDistrictstats-35] Despite this, fewer than 50,000 people reside permanently within the Lake District: mostly in [Ambleside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambleside), [Bowness-on-Windermere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowness-on-Windermere), [Coniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coniston,_Cumbria), [Gosforth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosforth,_Cumbria), [Grasmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasmere_\(village\)), [Keswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keswick,_Cumbria), and [Windermere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windermere,_Cumbria_\(town\)).[^cite_note-LakeDistrictstats-35] Over 36,000 Cumbrians are employed in the tourism industry, which adds £1.1 billion a year to the county's economy. ## Governance

### Local

Main articles: [Cumberland Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_Council) and [Westmorland and Furness Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland_and_Furness_Council)

Cumbria comprises two unitary authority areas: [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_\(unitary_authority_area\)), which covers the north and west of the county, and [Westmorland and Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland_and_Furness), which covers the south and east. They are named after the [historic counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) of [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland) and [Westmorland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland), although they have different boundaries, and the [Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furness) region, which was historically part of Lancashire.[^cite_note-\:0-30] The [Cumbria Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_Combined_Authority) is the [strategic authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_authority) for the area.

The two areas are governed by [Cumberland Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_Council) and [Westmorland and Furness Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmorland_and_Furness_Council), respectively. They are [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England), with the powers and responsibilities of both a [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) council and [non-metropolitan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district) council. Cumberland has had a [Labour](19279158.md) majority administration since the [2022 Cumberland Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Cumberland_Council_election), and Westmorland and Furness has had a [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) majority administration since the [2022 Westmorland and Furness Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Westmorland_and_Furness_Council_election).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

The [Duchy of Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Lancaster), the private estate of the sovereign, exercises some rights of [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown) in the County Palatine of Lancaster, which includes the Furness area of Cumbria.[^cite_note-41]

#### History



The historic counties within what is now Cumbria&#x20;

The [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888) reformed English local government. Cumberland, Westmorland, Lancashire, and the [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire) were reconstituted as [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties_of_England), with elected county councils. [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness) was considered to be within Lancashire, but was made a [county borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough) in the same act and therefore had independent local government.[^cite_note-42] [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle) was made a county borough within Cumberland in 1914.[^cite_note-43]

The [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972) reformed English local government again, and in 1974 the [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) of Cumbria was created from all of Cumberland and Westmorland, the Furness area of Lancashire, and the [Sedbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedbergh) district of the West Riding.[^cite_note-\:04-44] The new county was divided into six [non-metropolitan districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district): [Allerdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allerdale), [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Barrow-in-Furness), [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_\(district,_1974%E2%80%932023\)), [Copeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Copeland), [Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_District), and [South Lakeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lakeland).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The provision of local government services was divided between [Cumbria County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_County_Council) and the district councils of the six non-metropolitan districts.[^cite_note-\:04-44]

In 2023, the non-metropolitan county of Cumbria, its six districts, and their respective councils were abolished. They were replaced by two unitary authority areas: Cumberland, which replaced the districts of Allerdale, Copeland, and Carlisle; and Westmorland and Furness, which replaced Barrow-in-Furness, Eden, and South Lakeland.[^cite_note-47]

### Regional

Cumberland Council and Westmorland and Furness Council collaborate through the [Cumbria Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_Combined_Authority), which was established in February 2026. It will elect its first mayor on 10 May 2027.[^cite_note-48]

Several bus companies run services in Cumbria serving the main towns and villages in the county, with some services running to neighbouring areas such as [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Lancashire). [Stagecoach Cumbria & North Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Cumbria_&_North_Lancashire) is the largest; it has depots in Barrow-in-Furness, Carlisle, Kendal and Workington. Stagecoach's flagship X6 route connects Barrow-in-Furness and Kendal in south Cumbria.

### Air

There are two airports in the county: [Carlisle Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Lake_District_Airport) and [Barrow/Walney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow/Walney_Island_Airport). Both airports formerly served scheduled passenger flights and both are proposing expansions and renovations to handle domestic and European flights in the near future. The nearest international airports to south Cumbria are [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool_International_Airport), [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport), [Liverpool John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_John_Lennon) and [Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside_Airport). North Cumbria is closer to [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_International_Airport), [Glasgow Prestwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Prestwick) and [Glasgow International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_International_Airport).

### Ports

Barrow-in-Furness is one of the country's largest shipbuilding centres. The [Port of Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barrow) is minor, operated by [Associated British Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_British_Ports) alongside the Port of [Silloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silloth) in Allerdale. There are no ferry links from any port or harbour along the Cumbria coast.

### Rail

The busiest railway stations in Cumbria are [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_railway_station), [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness_railway_station), [Penrith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith_railway_station) and [Oxenholme Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxenholme_Lake_District). The 399 miles (642 km) [West Coast Main Line](182538.md) runs through the Cumbria countryside, adjacent to the M6 motorway. The [Cumbrian Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbrian_Coast_Line) connects Barrow-in-Furness to Carlisle and is a vital link in the west of the county. Other railways in Cumbria are the [Windermere Branch Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windermere_Branch_Line), most of the [Furness Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furness_Line) and much of the [Settle-Carlisle Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settle-Carlisle_Railway).

### Population

Cumbria's largest settlement and only city is [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle), in the north of the county. The largest town, [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness), in the south, is slightly smaller. The county's population is largely rural: it has the second-lowest population density among English counties, and only five towns with over 20,000 people. Cumbria is one of the country's most ethnically homogeneous counties, with 95% categorised as [White British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_British) (around 471,000 of the 500,000).[^cite_note-2009ethnicity-50] The larger towns have ethnic makeups closer to the national average. The [2001 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_2001_census) indicated Christianity was the religion with the most adherents in the county.

## Sport

### Running

[Fell running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fell_running) is a popular sport in Cumbria, with an active calendar of competitions taking place throughout the year. Cumbria is also home to several of the most active [orienteering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orienteering) clubs in the UK as well as the Lakes 5 Days competition that takes place every four years.

### Football codes

Main article: [Uppies and Downies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppies_and_Downies)

[Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington) is home to the [ball game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_game) known as Uppies and Downies,[^cite_note-56] a traditional version of football, with its origins in [medieval football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_football) or an even earlier form.[^cite_note-57] Players from outside Workington also take part, especially fellow West Cumbrians from [Whitehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehaven) and [Maryport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryport).[^cite_note-58]

Cumbria formerly had minor American football clubs, the Furness Phantoms (the club is now defunct, its last name was [Morecambe Bay Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morecambe_Bay_Storm)) and the Carlisle Kestrels.[^cite_note-59]

#### Association



[Brunton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunton_Park), the home of Carlisle United

[Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow_A.F.C.) and [Carlisle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_United) are the only professional football teams in Cumbria. Carlisle United attract support from across Cumbria and beyond, with many Cumbrian "ex-pats" travelling to see their games, both home and away.

[Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington_A.F.C.)—who are always known locally as "the reds"—are a well-supported non-league team, having been relegated from the Football League in the 1970s. Workington made a rapid rise up the non league ladder and in 2007/08 competed with Barrow in the [Conference North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_North). Barrow were then promoted to the [Conference Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_Premier) in 2007/08. In 2020, Barrow were promoted to the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League) as a result of winning the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_National_League).

#### Rugby codes

[Rugby union](25405.md) is popular in the county's north and east with teams such as Furness RUFC & Hawcoat Park RUFC (South Cumbria), Workington RUFC (Workington Zebras), Whitehaven RUFC, Carlisle RUFC, Creighton RUFC, [Aspatria RUFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspatria_RUFC), Wigton RUFC, [Kendal RUFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal_RUFC), Kirkby Lonsdale RUFC, Keswick RUFC, Cockermouth RUFC, Upper Eden RUFC and [Penrith RUFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith_RUFC).



[Craven Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craven_Park,_Barrow-in-Furness), home of Barrow Raiders

[Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) is a very popular sport in south and West Cumbria. [Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow_Raiders), [Whitehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehaven_R.L.F.C.) and [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington_Town) play in the [Rugby League Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFL_League_1). [Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Amateur_Rugby_League_Association) teams; Wath Brow Hornets, [Askam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askam_and_Ireleth#Sport), [Egremont Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egremont_Rangers), [Kells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kells_A.R.L.F.C.), Barrow Island, Hensingham and [Millom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millom_RLFC) play in the [National Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Conference_League).

### Bat-and-ball

[Cumbria County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_County_Cricket_Club) is one of the [cricket](25675557.md) clubs that constitute the [National Counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Counties_of_English_and_Welsh_cricket) in the English domestic cricket structure. The club, based in [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle), competes in the [National Counties Cricket Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Counties_Cricket_Championship) and the [NCCA Knockout Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCCA_Knockout_Trophy). The club also play some home matches in [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington), as well as other locations. Cumbrian [club cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_cricket) teams play in the [North Lancashire and Cumbria League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Lancashire_and_Cumbria_League).

Cumbria is home to the [Cartmel Valley Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartmel), an amateur baseball team based in Cartmel.

### Wrestling

Main article: [Cumberland and Westmorland wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_and_Westmorland_wrestling)

Cumberland and Westmorland wrestling is an ancient and well-practised tradition in the county with a strong resemblance to [Scottish Backhold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Backhold).

In the 21st century Cumberland and Westmorland wrestling along with other aspects of Lakeland culture are practised at the Grasmere Sports and Show, an annual meeting held every year since 1852 on the August [Bank Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Holiday).

The origin of this form of wrestling is a matter of debate, with some describing it as having evolved from Norse wrestling brought over by Viking invaders,[^cite_note-60] while other historians associate it with the [Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_Wrestling) and [Gouren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouren) styles[^cite_note-61] indicating that it may have developed out of a longer-standing Celtic tradition.[^cite_note-62]

### Racing

Cumbria Kart Racing Club is based at the Lakeland Circuit, [Rowrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowrah), between [Cockermouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockermouth) and [Egremont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egremont,_Cumbria) [Lakeland Circuit](https://web.archive.org/web/20120421201446/http://www.cumbriakrc.co.uk/index.php?option=com_content\&view=article\&id=43\&Itemid=48). The track is a venue for rounds of both major UK national karting championships [About Cumbria Kart Racing Club](https://web.archive.org/web/20120421212608/http://www.cumbriakrc.co.uk/index.php?option=com_content\&view=article\&id=1\&Itemid=19). [Formula One](10854.md) world champions [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton) and [Jenson Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenson_Button) both raced karts at Rowrah many times in the formative stages of their motor sport careers.[^cite_note-63] Other F1 drivers, past and present, to have competed there include [Johnny Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Herbert), [Anthony Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Davidson), [Allan McNish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_McNish), [Ralph Firman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Firman), and [Paul di Resta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_di_Resta). [David Coulthard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Coulthard) hailed from just over the nearby Anglo-Scottish border and regarded Rowrah as his home circuit, becoming Cumbria Kart Racing Club Champion in 1985 in succession to McNish, di Resta taking the CKRC title subsequently.[^cite_note-64]

[Workington Comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington_Comets) were a Workington-based professional [speedway](230182.md) team,[^cite_note-65] which competed in the British Speedway Championship.[^cite_note-66]

The [World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championship) team [Ford World Rally Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_World_Rally_Team) were based in Cumbria until their withdrawal after the 2012 season. A successful team, they won the [Championship for manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Rally_Championship_Manufacturers'_champions) three times. The [M-Sport Ford World Rally Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Sport_Ford_World_Rally_Team) succeeded this team and have since won the Championship for manufacturers in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIA_World_Rally_Championship).[^cite_note-67]

## Food

Cumbria is the UK county with the highest number of [Michelin-starred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Guide) restaurants, with seven in this classification in the Great Britain and Ireland Michelin Guide of 2021. Traditional Cumbrian cuisine has been influenced by the spices and molasses that were imported into [Whitehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehaven) in the 18th century. The [Cumberland sausage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_sausage), which has a [protected geographical status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_geographical_status), is a well-recognised result of this. Other regional specialities include Herdwick mutton and the salt-marsh raised lamb of the [Cartmel Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartmel_Peninsula).[^cite_note-68]

### Celtic

- Cumbria was mostly Celtic speaking until the Viking invasion, if not later (Cymry)

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- English spoken in parts of Cumbria; relatively sparsely populated until 12th/13th centuries

  [^cite_note-70]

- The invading Angles and Saxons forced the indigenous Celtic peoples back to the western highlands of Cumbria, Wales and Cornwall, with little linguistic consequence, apart from a residual scattering of place-names.

- Northwest – possibility of direct influence from Irish Gaelic across Irish Sea via Whitehaven until 10th century

  [^cite_note-71]

- Celtic influence/kingdoms may have confirmed perception of difference between the north–south

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Linguistic interaction between Celts and English underrated: effectively Celtic influence marked the beginnings of a linguistic divide between English and other West Germanic dialects.

  [^cite_note-72]

- [Lexis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexis_\(linguistics\)) – Celtic influence left specifically on the sound pattern of sheep-scoring numerals of Cumbrian and West Yorkshire

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Loss of inflections may be explained by contact with Celtic tribes and inter-marriage.

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

### Anglo-Saxon/Viking

- Earliest Anglo-Saxon settlements in the east of England. Took over 200 years to establish a frontier in the west where the displaced British had settled

  [^cite_note-73]

- Morphology – Old Northumbrian (little evidence) signs of loss of inflexions long before southern dialects below the Humber, precede Viking settlements and dialect contact situation

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

### Scandinavian/Norse/Dane

- Lack of extent of Old English written evidence

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Main attacks/raids on the North-East coast at Lindisfarne and Jarrow in 793/ 794

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Settlement patterns (Danes) contributed to emerging differences over time between Northumberland. Durham and Yorkshire dialects

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Norwegian settlers via Ireland to Isle of Man, Mersey estuary (901) and the Cumbrian/ Lancashire coasts (900–50) – dialectal differences (Danes/ Norwegians) often lumped together in standard histories – MUST have confirmed emerging dialectal differences east and west of the Pennines

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Danelaw – land of north and east of land ruled under Danish law and Danish customs (978–1016)

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Scandinavian influences vocabulary – common words gradually diffused/ entered word stock (borrowings) which survive in regional use – *fell* ('hillside'), *lug* ('ear'), *loup* ('jump'), *aye* ('yes')

- Influence on grammatical structure – Middle English texts reveal that present participle form *-and*, and possible that use of *at* and *as* as relative pronouns from Cumbria to East Yorkshire

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Phonetically /g/, /k/ and cluster /sk/ have a northern/ Norse pronunciation /j/, /ʧ/ and /ʃ/ which are West Saxon – hard vs. soft consonants of north–south dialects – e.g. *give/ rigg* ('ridge'), *skrike* ('shriek'), *kist* ('chest') and *ik* ('I')

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- 'Interdialect forms' in Danelaw area (diffuse > focussed situation) – no clear idea about what language they were speaking – mixture of Old English and Norse e.g. *she* (3rd person pronoun) is claimed by both languages

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

  [^cite_note-74]

- Bilingualism was norm in areas under Danelaw (plausible)

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Norse runic inscriptions survive from 11th century in Cumbria – therefore may only been after Norman Conquest that 'Norse as a living language died out'

  [^cite_note-75]

- Norse surviving longest in closed communities

  [^cite_note-76]

### Normans

- Jewell (1994: 20) – Northumbria retained relative independence until 13th century – effective government of North by Normans 'petered-out' at Lake District and North of Tees (not recorded in Domesday Book)

  [^cite_note-77]

- Carlisle retaken by Scots in 1136

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

### Cumbric

Main article: [Cumbric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbric)

- Early 10th century – The grip from Northumbrian on the former territory of [Rheged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheged) was that of Britons of Strathcylde reoccupied southwest Scotland and northwest England as far south as Derwent and Penrith.

  [^cite_note-price-78]

  &#x20;which was held until Carlisle retaken by Scots in 1136

  [^cite_note-wales-69]

- Cumbric perhaps survived until it faded in the early 12th century throughout Cumbria.

  [^cite_note-79]

- Cumbric score – counting sheep – Welsh correspondence Welsh ("un, dau, tri") – Cumberland ("yan, tyan, tethera") – Westmorland ("yan, than, teddera") – Lancashire ("yan, taen, tedderte") – West Yorkshire ("yain, tain, eddero")

  [^cite_note-price-78]

  &#x20;– survived 7–8 centuries after the language itself had died – Brittonic origin

- Not one single complete phrase in Cumbric survives, evidence to suggest strong literary tradition, probably oral, some of this early material is known in a Welsh version

  [^cite_note-price-78]

## Media

Two evening newspapers are published daily in Cumbria. The *[News and Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_and_Star)* focuses largely on Carlisle and the surrounding areas of north and west Cumbria, and the *[North-West Evening Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Evening_Mail)* is based in Barrow-in-Furness and covers news from across Furness and the South Lakes. The *[Cumberland and Westmorland Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_and_Westmorland_Herald)* and *[The Westmorland Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Westmorland_Gazette)* are weekly newspapers based in [Penrith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith,_Cumbria) and [Kendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal) respectively.&#x20;

The Egremont 2Day newspaper, formerly Egremont Today when affiliated with the [Labour Party](19279158.md), was a prominent monthly publication – founded by Peter Watson (and edited by him until his death in 2014) in 1990 until July 2018. In February 2020 The Herdwick News, run by the last editor of The Egremont 2Day, was launched and is an independent online news publication covering the county of Cumbria and the North West.

Due to the size of Cumbria the county spans two television zones: [BBC North East and Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_East_and_Cumbria) and [ITV Border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Border) in the north and centre, and [BBC North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_West) and [ITV Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Granada) in the south. [Heart North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_North_West), [Greatest Hits Radio Cumbria & South West Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_Radio_Cumbria_&_South_West_Scotland) and [Smooth Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_Lake_District) are the most popular local radio stations throughout the county, with [BBC Radio Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Cumbria) being the only station that is aimed at Cumbria as a whole. [BBC Radio Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Lancashire) can also be received in southern parts of the county.

Cumbria is host to a number of festivals, including [Kendal Calling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal_Calling) (actually held in Penrith since 2009)[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] and [Kendal Mountain Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal_Mountain_Festival).

## Symbols



The [Flag of Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Cumbria), adopted in 2025

The county is represented by the [Flag of Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Cumbria), also known as the Cumbria Community Flag. The design features green and gold chevrons symbolising the county's hills, fells and farmland, a blue field for its lakes, rivers and coastline, and a golden crown representing unity, heritage and civic pride.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]
