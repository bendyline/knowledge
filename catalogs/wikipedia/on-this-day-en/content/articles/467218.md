**Voronezh**[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] is a city and the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of [Voronezh Oblast](473401.md) in southwestern Russia, straddling the [Voronezh River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_River) 12 kilometers (7.5 mi) from where it flows into the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)). The city sits on the [Southeastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Railway_\(Russia\)), which connects western Russia with the [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(region\)) and [Siberia](29639.md), the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) and [Ukraine](31750.md), and the [M4 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_highway_\(Russia\)) ([Moscow](19004.md)–Voronezh–[Rostov-on-Don](317562.md)–[Novorossiysk](1190336.md)). In recent years the city has experienced rapid population growth, rising to 1,057,681 in the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census),[^cite_note-2021Census-14] up from 889,680 recorded in the [2010 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Russian_census),[^cite_note-2010Census-4] making it the [14th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Russia_by_population) in the country.

### Foundation and name



[Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian) vessel from Voronezh, 4th century BC. [Hermitage Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Museum).

The first chronicle references to the word *Voronezh* are dated 1177, when Yaropolk, the prince of [Ryazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan), having lost the battle, fled "to Voronozh" and there was moving "from town to town". Modern data of archaeology and history interpret Voronezh as a geographical region, which included the [Voronezh River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_\(river\)) (a tributary of the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River)) and a number of settlements. In the lower reaches of the river, a unique Slavic town-planning complex of the 8th to early 11th century was discovered, which covered the territory of the present city of Voronezh and its environs (about 42 km long, about 13 forts and many unfortified villages). By the 12th to 13th centuries, most of the old towns were desolate, but new settlements appeared upstream, closer to Ryazan.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:0-18]

For many years, the hypothesis of the Soviet historian [Vladimir Zagorovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Zagorovsky) dominated: he produced the toponym *Voronezh* from the hypothetical Slavic personal name *Voroneg*. This man allegedly gave the name of a small town in the [Principality of Chernigov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Chernigov) (now the village of [Voronizh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronizh) in [Ukraine](31750.md)).[^cite_note-19] Later, in the 11th or 12th century, the settlers were able to "transfer" this name to the Don region, where they named the second city Voronezh, and the river received its name from the city.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] However, now many researchers criticize the hypothesis, since in reality neither the name of *Voroneg* nor the second city was revealed, and usually the names of Russian cities repeated the names of the rivers, but not vice versa.

The linguistic [comparative analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_linguistics) of the name *Voronezh* was carried out by the Khovansky Foundation in 2009. There is an indication of the place names of many countries in Eurasia, which may partly be not only similar in sound, but also united by common Indo-European languages: [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), [Varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria), [Verona](263991.md), [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno), etc.[^cite_note-Lazarev-22]

A comprehensive scientific analysis was conducted in 2015–2016 by the historian Pavel Popov. His conclusion: *Voronezh* is a probable Slavic macrotoponym associated with outstanding signs of nature, has a root *voron-* (from [Proto-Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Slavic) *vorn*) in the meaning of "black, dark" and the suffix *-ezh* *(-azh, -ozh*). It was not "transferred" and in the 8th to 9th centuries it marked a vast territory covered with black forests (oak forests) – from the mouth of the Voronezh River to the Voronozhsky annalistic forests in the middle and upper reaches of the river, and in the west to the Don (many forests were cut down). This historian believes that the main "city" of the early town-planning complex could repeat the name of the region – Voronezh. Now the hillfort is located in the administrative part of the modern city, in the Voronezh upland oak forest. This is one of Europe's largest ancient Slavic hillforts, the area of which – more than 9 hectares – 13 times the area of the main settlement in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) before the [baptism of Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism_of_Rus') in the year 988.[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-23]

[Folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology) claims the name comes from combining the Russian words for raven ([ворон](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BD#Russian), *voron*) and hedgehog ([ёж](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D1%91%D0%B6#Russian), *yozh*) into *Voronezh* (Воронеж). According to this explanation, two Slavic tribes named after the animals used this combination to name the river which later in turn provided the name for a settlement. There is not believed to be any scientific support for this explanation.

In the 16th century, the Middle Don basin, including the Voronezh River, was gradually conquered by the [Grand Principality of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Principality_of_Moscow) from the [Nogai Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogai_Horde) (a successor state of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde)), and the current city of Voronezh was established in 1586 by [Feodor I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodor_I_of_Russia) as a fort protecting the [Muravsky Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muravsky_Trail) trade route against the [slave raids of the Nogai and Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe). The city was named after the river.[^cite_note-Foundation-2][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

Voronezh. Ship Museum [Goto Predestinatsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goto_Predestinatsia)

In the 17th century, Voronezh gradually evolved into a sizable town. *Weronecz* is shown on the *Worona* river in [Resania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Ryazan) in [Joan Blaeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Blaeu)'s map of 1645.[^cite_note-26] [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) built a [dockyard in Voronezh](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B6%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B5%20%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%B9%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%BE) where the Azov Flotilla was constructed for the [Azov campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_campaigns) in 1695 and 1696. This fleet, the first ever built in Russia, included the first Russian [ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line), *[Goto Predestinatsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goto_Predestinatsia)*. The Orthodox diocese of Voronezh was instituted in 1682 and its first bishop, [Mitrofan of Voronezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitrofan_of_Voronezh), was later proclaimed the town's patron saint.



View of Voronezh in the 18th century

Owing to the Voronezh Admiralty Wharf, for a short time, Voronezh became the largest city of South Russia and the economic center of a large and fertile region. In 1711, it was made the seat of the [Azov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Governorate), which eventually morphed into the [Voronezh Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_Governorate).

In the 19th century, Voronezh was the center of Voronezh Governorate. Manufacturing industry (mills, tallow-melting, butter-making, soap, leather, and other works) as well as bread, cattle, [suet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suet), and the hair trade developed in the town. A railway connected Voronezh with [Moscow](19004.md) in 1868 and [Rostov-on-Don](317562.md) in 1871.

### 20th century

#### World War II

During [World War II](32927.md), Voronezh was the scene of fierce fighting between Soviet and combined Axis troops. The Germans used it as a staging area for their attack on [Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad), and made it a key crossing point on the Don River. In June 1941, two BM-13 (Fighting machine #13 *[Katyusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rocket_launcher)*) artillery installations were built at the Voronezh excavator factory. In July, the construction of *Katyushas* was rationalized so that their manufacture became easier and the time of volley repetition was shortened from five minutes to fifteen seconds. More than 300 BM-13 units manufactured in Voronezh were used in a counterattack near Moscow in December 1941. In October 22, 1941, the advance of the German troops prompted the establishment of a defense committee in the city. On November 7, 1941, there was a troop parade, devoted to the anniversary of the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution). Only three such parades were organized that year: in Moscow, [Kuybyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara,_Russia), and Voronezh. In late June 1942, the city was [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Voronezh_\(1942\)) by German and Hungarian forces. In response, Soviet forces formed the [Voronezh Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_Front). By July 6, the German army occupied the western river-bank suburbs before being subjected to a fierce Soviet counter-attack. By July 24 the frontline had stabilised along the Voronezh River as the German forces continued southeast into the Great Bend of the Don. The attack on Voronezh represented the first phase of the German Army's 1942 campaign in the Soviet Union, codenamed [Case Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Blue).



German mechanized forces on their way to Voronezh, July 1942

Until January 25, 1943, parts of the [Second German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)) and the [Second Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Army_\(Hungary\)) occupied the western part of Voronezh. During [Operation Little Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Little_Saturn), the [Ostrogozhsk–Rossosh Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogozhsk%E2%80%93Rossosh_Offensive), and the Voronezhsko-Kastornenskoy Offensive, the Voronezh Front exacted heavy casualties on Axis forces. On January 25, 1943, Voronezh was [liberated after ten days of combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Voronezh_\(1943\)). During the war the city was almost completely ruined, with 92% of all buildings destroyed.

#### Post-war

By 1950, Voronezh had been rebuilt. Most buildings and historical monuments were repaired. It was also the location of a prestigious [Suvorov Military School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvorov_Military_School), a [boarding school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_school) for young boys who were considered to be prospective military officers, many of whom had been orphaned by war.[^cite_note-27]

In 1950–1960, new factories were established: a tire factory, a machine-tool factory, a factory of heavy mechanical pressing, and others. In 1968, Serial production of the [Tupolev Tu-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-144) supersonic plane was established at the Voronezh Aviation factory. In October 1977, the first Soviet domestic wide-body plane, [Ilyushin Il-86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-86), was built there.

In 1989, [TASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph_Agency_of_the_Soviet_Union) published details of an alleged [UFO](31814.md) [landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_UFO_incident) in the city's park and purported encounters with [extraterrestrial beings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterrestrials) reported by a number of children. A Russian scientist that was cited in initial TASS reports later told the [Associated Press](18935732.md) that he was misquoted, cautioning, "Don't believe all you hear from TASS," and "We never gave them part of what they published",[^cite_note-AP-28] and a TASS correspondent admitted the possibility that some "make-believe" had been added to the TASS story, saying, "I think there is a certain portion of truth, but it is not excluded that there is also fantasizing".[^cite_note-UPI-29][^cite_note-FeinTimes-30]

From 1989 onwards explorers found burials of those shot during the [Great Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Terror) and in 1993 the wooded land of Dubovka was turned into a memorial area where they were reburied.[^cite_note-31]

From 10 to 17 September 2011, Voronezh celebrated its 425th anniversary. The anniversary of the city was given the status of a federal scale celebration that helped attract large investments from the [federal and regional budgets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_budget_of_Russia) for development.[^cite_note-32] On December 17, 2012, Voronezh became the fifteenth city in Russia with a population of over one million people.[^cite_note-33] Today Voronezh is the economic, industrial, cultural, and scientific center of the [Central Black Earth Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Black_Earth_Region). As part of the annual tradition in the Russian city of Voronezh, every winter the main city square is thematically drawn around a classic literature. In 2020, the city was decorated using the motifs from [Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky)'s [The Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nutcracker). In the year of 2021, the architects drew inspiration from [Hans Christian Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen)'s fairy tale *[The Snow Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Snow_Queen)* as well as the animation classic *[The Snow Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Snow_Queen_\(1957_film\))* from the Soviet Union. The fairy tale replica city will feature the houses of Kai and Gerda, the palace of the snow queen, an ice rink, and illumination.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In June 2023, during the [Wagner Group rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group_rebellion), forces of the Wagner Group took control of the military facilities in the city. Later they were confirmed to have taken the city itself.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

On the night of 27 October 2024, during the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), [Ukraine](31750.md) launched a drone attack on two distilleries in Voronezh, distilleries that make rocket and aviation fuel and explosives.[^cite_note-newsweek20241028-40][^cite_note-nv20241027-41]

## Geography

### Urban layout

Information about the original urban layout of Voronezh is contained in the "Patrol Book" of 1615. At that time, the city fortress was logged and located on the banks of the Voronezh River. In plan, it was an irregular quadrangle with a perimeter of about 238 meter. inside it, due to lack of space, there was no housing or siege yards, and even the cathedral church was supposed to be taken out. However, at this small fortress there was a large garrison—666 households of service people. These courtyards were reliably protected by the second line of fortifications by a standing prison on taras with 25 towers covered with earth; behind the prison was a moat, and beyond the moat there were stakes. Voronezh was a typical military settlement ([ostrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrog_\(fortress\))).[^cite_note-45]

In the city prison there were only settlements of military men: Streletskaya, Kazachya, Belomestnaya atamanskaya, Zatinnaya and Pushkarskaya.[^cite_note-46] The posad population received the territory between the [ostrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrog_\(fortress\)) and the river, where the Monastyrskaya settlements (at the Assumption Monastery) was formed. Subsequently, the Yamnaya Sloboda was added to them, and on the other side of the fort, on the Chizhovka Mountain, the Chizhovskaya Sloboda of archers and Cossacks appeared. As a result, the Voronezh settlements surrounded the fortress in a ring. The location of the parish churches emphasized this ring-like and even distribution of settlements: the Ilyinsky Church of the Streletskaya Sloboda, the Pyatnitskaya Cossack and Pokrovskaya Belomestnaya were brought out to the passage towers of the prison. The Nikolskaya Church of the Streletskaya Sloboda was located near the marketplace (and, accordingly, the front facade of the fortress), and the paired ensemble of the Rozhdestvenskaya and Georgievskaya churches of the Cossack Sloboda marked the main street of the city, going from the Cossack Gate to the fortress tower.[^cite_note-47]

### Climate

Summer temperatures can often go over 35 °C (95 °F), and sometimes over 40 °C (104 °F).&#x20;

### Air

The city is served by the [Voronezh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_International_Airport), which is located north of the city and is home to Polet Airlines. Voronezh is also home to the [Pridacha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pridacha_Airport), a part of a major aircraft manufacturing facility [VASO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_Aircraft_Production_Association) (*Voronezhskoye Aktsionernoye Samoletostroitelnoye Obshchestvo*, Voronezh aircraft production association) where the [Tupolev Tu-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-144) (known in the West as the "Concordski"), was built and the only operational unit is still stored. Voronezh also hosts the [Voronezh Malshevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_Malshevo) [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) base in the southwest of the city, which, according to a [Natural Resources Defense Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Resources_Defense_Council) report, houses [nuclear bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_bomber).

### Rail

Since 1868, there is a railway connection between Voronezh and Moscow.[^cite_note-51] Rail services form a part of the [South Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Eastern_Railway_\(Russia\)) of the [Russian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Railways). Destinations served direct from Voronezh include Moscow, Kyiv, Kursk, Novorossiysk, Sochi, and Tambov. The main train station is called [Voronezh-1 railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh-1_railway_station) and is located in the center of the city.

### Bus

There are three bus stations in Voronezh that connect the city with destinations including [Moscow](19004.md), [Belgorod](478861.md), [Lipetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipetsk), [Volgograd](32585.md), [Rostov-on-Don](317562.md), and [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan).

## Religion



Annunciation Orthodox Cathedral in Voronezh

[Orthodox Christianity](10186.md) is the predominant religion in Voronezh. There is an [Orthodox Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) community in Voronezh, with a synagogue located on Stankevicha Street.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] A notable [Christian Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) community, up to 4,000 individuals, is also present.

In 1682, the Voronezh diocese was formed to fight the schismatics. Its first head was Bishop [Mitrofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitrophan_of_Voronezh) (1623–1703) at the age of 58. Under him, the construction began on the new Annunciation Cathedral to replace the old one. In 1832, Mitrofan was [glorified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonized) as a saint by the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church).

In the 1990s, many Orthodox churches were returned to the diocese. Their restoration was continued. In 2009, instead of the lost one, a new Annunciation Cathedral was built with a monument to St. Mitrofan erected next to it.
