**Indiana** ([/ˌɪndiˈænə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/28/En-us-Indiana.ogg/En-us-Indiana.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/28/En-us-Indiana.ogg/En-us-Indiana.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*IN-dee-AN-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-16] is a [state](18618239.md) in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) region of the [United States](3434750.md). It borders [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) to the northwest, [Michigan](18859.md) to the north and northeast, [Ohio](22199.md) to the east, the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) and [Kentucky](16846.md) to the south and southeast, and the [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River) and [Illinois](14849.md) to the west. Nicknamed "the [Hoosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosier) State",[^cite_note-17] Indiana is the [38th-largest by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) and the [17th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) of the [50 states](12610470.md). The state's capital and largest city is [Indianapolis](57707.md). Indiana was admitted to the Union as the 19th state on December 11, 1816.

[Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) resistance to American settlement was broken with their defeat in [Battle of Tippecanoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tippecanoe) in 1811, and the collapse of [Tecumseh's confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh's_confederacy) in 1813. The new settlers were primarily Americans of [British](19097669.md) ancestry from the [eastern seaboard](89126.md) and the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South), and [Germans](152735.md). After the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), in which the state fought for the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)), natural gas attracted heavy industry and new [European immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_immigration_to_the_United_States) to its northern counties. In the first half of the 20th century, northern and central sections experienced a boom in goods manufacture and automobile production. Southern Indiana remained largely rural. After the rise and fall of the [Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Klan) in the 1920s, the state swung politically from the [Republican](32070.md) to [Democratic Party](5043544.md) in the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) 1930s. Today, with a decades-long record of returning Republican majorities, Indiana is counted a "[red state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states)".

Indiana has [a diverse economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Indiana) with a gross state product in 2023 of 404.3 billion.[^cite_note-18] Indianapolis is at the center of the state's largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_metropolitan_area), with a population of over two million.[^cite_note-19] The [Fort Wayne metro area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne_metropolitan_area,_Indiana) follows with a population of 645,000.[^cite_note-2010census-20]

The state's largest city, [Indianapolis](57707.md), is home to professional sports teams, including the [NFL](21211.md)'s [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts), the [NBA's](22093.md) [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers), and the [WNBA](57721.md)'s [Indiana Fever](240214.md). The city also hosts several notable competitive events, such as the [Indianapolis 500](159018.md), held at [Indianapolis Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway).

## Etymology

Indiana's name means "Land of the [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States)", or simply "Indian Land".[^cite_note-21] It also stems from Indiana's territorial history. On May 7, 1800, the [United States Congress](31756.md) passed legislation to divide the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) into two areas and named the western section the [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory). In 1816, when Congress passed an Enabling Act to begin the process of establishing statehood for Indiana, a part of this territorial land became the geographic area for the new state.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-IHBNI-24]

Formal use of the word *Indiana* dates from 1768, when a [Philadelphia](50585.md)-based trading company gave its land claim in present-day [West Virginia](32905.md) the name "Indiana" in honor of its previous owners, the [Iroquois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois). Later, ownership of the claim was transferred to the [Indiana Land Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Land_Company), the first recorded use of the word *Indiana*. But the [Virginia](32432.md) colony argued that it was the rightful owner of the land because it fell within its geographic boundaries. The [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) denied the land company's right to the claim in 1798.[^cite_note-IHBNI-24]

### Hoosier

A native or resident of Indiana is known as a [Hoosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosier).[^cite_note-25] The etymology of this word is disputed, but the leading theory, advanced by the Indiana Historical Bureau and the Indiana Historical Society, has its origin in Virginia, Kentucky, the Carolinas, and Tennessee (the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South)) as a term for a backwoodsman, a rough countryman, or a country bumpkin.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### Indigenous inhabitants



&#x20;[Angel Mounds State Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Mounds_State_Historic_Site) was one of the northernmost [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) settlements, occupied from 1100 to 1450.

The first inhabitants in what is now Indiana were the [Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians), who arrived about 8000 BC after the melting of the glaciers at the end of the [Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). Divided into small groups, the Paleo-Indians were [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) who hunted large [game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_\(food\)) such as [mastodons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastodons). They created stone tools made out of [chert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chert) by chipping, [knapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knapping) and [flaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_reduction).[^cite_note-HIS-28]

The [Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_the_Americas) between 5000 and 4000 BC saw the development of new ground-stone tools the building of earthwork [mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mound) and [middens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden), suggesting that settlements were becoming more permanent.[^cite_note-HIS-28] The [Woodland period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_period) began around 1500 BC marked by ceramics and pottery and the extended cultivation of plants eventually including crops such as [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(plant\)), and the development of long-term trade, notably by the [Hopewell people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_tradition).[^cite_note-HIS-28]

The [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) emerged, lasting from 1000 AD until the 15th century, shortly before the arrival of Europeans. During this stage, people created large urban settlements designed according to their [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology), with large mounds and plazas defining ceremonial and public spaces. The concentrated settlements depended on the agricultural surpluses. One such complex was the [Angel Mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Mounds). They had large public areas such as plazas and platform mounds, where leaders lived or conducted rituals. Mississippian civilization collapsed in Indiana during the mid-15th century for reasons that remain unclear.[^cite_note-HIS-28]

The historic Native American tribes in the area at the time of European encounter spoke different languages of the [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) family. They included the [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee), [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_people), and [Illini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiniwek). Refugee tribes from eastern regions, including the [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Nation) who settled in the White and Whitewater River Valleys, later joined them.

### European exploration and sovereignty

See also: [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France), [Louisiana (New France)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)), [Illinois Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Country), and [Province of Quebec (1763–1791)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec_\(1763%E2%80%931791\))



Native Indians guide French explorers through Indiana, as depicted by [Maurice Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Thompson) in *Stories of Indiana*

In 1679, French explorer [René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9-Robert_Cavelier,_Sieur_de_La_Salle) was the first European to cross into Indiana after reaching present-day [South Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana) at the [St. Joseph River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph_River_\(Lake_Michigan\)).[^cite_note-29] He was followed by French-Canadian [fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) exchanging blankets, jewelry, tools, whiskey and weapons with the Native Americans for skins.

By 1702, [Sieur Juchereau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Juchereau_de_St._Denis) established the first trading post near [Vincennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincennes,_Indiana). In 1715, [Sieur de Vincennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Bissot,_Sieur_de_Vincennes) built [Fort Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Miami_\(Indiana\)) at [Kekionga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kekionga), now [Fort Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne). In 1717, another Canadian, [Picote de Beletre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picote_de_Beletre), built [Fort Ouiatenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ouiatenon) on the [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River), to try to control Native American trade routes from [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie) to the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River).

In 1732, Sieur de Vincennes built a second fur trading post at Vincennes. In a period of a few years, British colonists arrived from the East and contended with the Canadians for control of the lucrative fur trade. Fighting between the French and British colonists occurred throughout the 1750s.

The Native American tribes of Indiana sided with the French Canadians during the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War) (an episode of the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War) between the European [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power)). With British victory in 1763, the French were forced to cede to the British crown all their lands in North America east of the Mississippi River and north and west of the [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies).

Tribal resistance continued with [Pontiac's Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_Rebellion) and the capture of forts [Ouiatenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ouiatenon) and [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forts_of_Fort_Wayne,_Indiana#Fort_Miami). The [Royal Proclamation of 1763](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Proclamation_of_1763) designated the land west of the [Appalachians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachians) as an [Indian Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Reserve_\(1763\)), and excluded British colonists from the area, which the Crown called "Indian Territory".[^cite_note-30] The measure was one of the first significant areas of dispute between Britain and the [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) and would become a contributing factor leading to the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution).[^cite_note-31]

In 1775, the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) began as the colonists sought self-government and independence from the British. The majority of the fighting took place near the East Coast, but the Patriot military officer [George Rogers Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rogers_Clark) called for an army to help fight the British in the west.[^cite_note-32] Clark's army won significant battles and took over [Vincennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincennes,_Indiana) and [Fort Sackville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sackville_\(Vincennes\)) on February 25, 1779.[^cite_note-NO-33]

During the war, Clark managed to cut off British troops, who were attacking the eastern colonists from the west. His success is often credited for changing the course of the American Revolutionary War.[^cite_note-34] At the end of the war, through the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)), the British crown ceded their claims to the land south of the Great Lakes to the newly formed United States, including Native American lands.

In 1787, the U.S. defined the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) which included the area of present-day Indiana. In 1800, Congress separated [Ohio](22199.md) from the Northwest Territory, designating the rest of the land as the [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory).[^cite_note-GAC-35] President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) chose [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison) as the governor of the territory, and [Vincennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincennes,_Indiana) was established as the capital.[^cite_note-36] After the [Michigan Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Territory) was separated and the [Illinois Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Territory) was formed, Indiana was reduced to its current size and geography.[^cite_note-GAC-35]

Starting with the [Battle of Fallen Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fallen_Timbers) in 1794 and the [Treaty of Greenville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Greenville) in 1795, [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) titles to Indiana lands were extinguished by usurpation, purchase, or war and treaty. About half the state was acquired in the [Treaty of St. Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_St._Mary's) from the [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_people) in 1818. Purchases were not complete until the [Treaty of Mississinewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Mississinewas)[^cite_note-37] again with the Miami in 1826 acquired the last of the reserved Native American lands in the northeast.

By 1810, only two counties in the extreme southeast, [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Indiana) and [Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dearborn_County,_Indiana), had been organized by European settlers. Land titles issued out of Cincinnati were sparse. Settler migration was chiefly via flatboat on the Ohio River westerly, and by wagon trails up the Wabash/White River Valleys (west) and [Whitewater River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewater_River_\(Indiana\)) Valleys (east).

After working to maintain peaceful coexistence with the United States, the [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee) tribal chief [Tecumseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh) and his brother [Tenskwatawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenskwatawa), "the Shawnee Prophet", encouraged other indigenous tribes in the territory to reject European influences, stop drinking alcohol, and resist further encroachment. Tensions rose and the U.S. authorized Harrison to launch a preemptive expedition against [Tecumseh's Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh's_Confederacy); the U.S. gained victory at the [Battle of Tippecanoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tippecanoe) on November 7, 1811.[^cite_note-38]

In the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), the British, who had been assisted by Tecumseh in the [capture of Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Detroit), proposed creating of an [Indian barrier state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_barrier_state) to ensure the security of [Upper Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada).[^cite_note-39] After Tecumseh was killed in 1813 at the [Battle of the Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Thames), resistance to United States control ended in the region. Most Native American tribes in the state were later [removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_removals_in_Indiana) to west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) in the 1820s and 1830s after being forced to accept the "purchase" of their lands.[^cite_note-40]

### Statehood and settlement

[Corydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corydon,_Indiana), a town in the far southern part of Indiana, was named the second capital of the Indiana Territory in May 1813 in order to decrease the threat of Native American raids following the Battle of Tippecanoe.[^cite_note-GAC-35] Two years later, a petition for statehood was approved by the territorial general assembly and sent to Congress. An [Enabling Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act) was passed to provide an election of delegates to write a [constitution for Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Indiana). On June 10, 1816, delegates assembled at Corydon to write a state constitution, which was completed in 19 days. [Jonathan Jennings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Jennings) was elected the fledgling state's first governor in August 1816. President [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison) approved Indiana's [admission into the union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) as the [nineteenth state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union) on December 11, 1816.[^cite_note-NO-33] In 1825, the state capital was moved from Corydon to [Indianapolis](57707.md).[^cite_note-GAC-35]

Many European [immigrants](4599312.md) went west to settle in Indiana in the early 19th century. The largest immigrant group to settle in Indiana were [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-American) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-American) remains the largest ancestry reported in Indiana),[^cite_note-Germanic-42] as well as many immigrants from [Ireland](147575.md) and [England](9316.md). Americans who were primarily [ethnically English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) migrated from the Northern Tier of [New York](8210131.md) and [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), as well as from the mid-Atlantic state of [Pennsylvania](23332.md).[^cite_note-The_H-43][^cite_note-44] The arrival of steamboats on the Ohio River in 1811, and the [National Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Road) at Richmond in 1829, greatly facilitated settlement of northern and western Indiana.

Following statehood, the new government worked to transform Indiana from a [frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_frontier) into a developed, well-populated, and thriving state, beginning significant demographic and economic changes. In 1836, the state's founders initiated a program, the [Indiana Mammoth Internal Improvement Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Mammoth_Internal_Improvement_Act), that led to the construction of roads, [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal), railroads and state-funded public schools. The plans bankrupted the state and were a financial disaster, but increased land and produce value more than fourfold.[^cite_note-45] In response to the crisis and in order to avert another, in 1851, a second [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Indiana) was adopted, which prohibited public debt. At the same time, however, it included a mandate for a "uniform system of common schools, equally open to all and free of tuition."[^cite_note-46]

In a reaction against an influx of [free people of color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_people_of_color) and emancipated slaves who had been expelled from slave states, Article 13 of the new constitution sought to bar their further immigration into Indiana and proposed their resettlement in [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Liberia).[^cite_note-Madison49-47] Citing the newly passed [Thirteenth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment_to_the_U.S._Constitution), the Indiana Supreme Court struck down the article in 1866, and it was removed by amendment in 1881.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-Shephard40-41-49] Nevertheless, numerous communities and counties implemented practices to exclude [African Americans](2154.md). These jurisdictions, known as "[sundown towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundown_town)", were prevalent during the 1890s.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Civil War and late 19th-century industry

Main article: [Indiana in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_in_the_American_Civil_War)

Indiana was the first western state to mobilize for the United States in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), and soldiers from Indiana participated in all the war's major engagements. In 1861, Indiana was assigned a [quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War#Mobilization) of 7,500 soldiers to join the [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army).[^cite_note-52] So many volunteered in the first call that thousands had to be turned away. Before the war ended, Indiana had contributed 208,367 organized in 126 infantry regiments, 26 batteries of artillery and 13 regiments of cavalry to the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)).[^cite_note-53] Casualties were over 35% of the enlisted: 24,416 men lost their lives and over 50,000 more were wounded.[^cite_note-54] The only Civil War conflicts fought in Indiana were the [Newburgh Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newburgh_Raid), a bloodless capture of the city; and the [Battle of Corydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Corydon), which occurred during [Morgan's Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan's_Raid) leaving 15 dead, 40 wounded, and 355 captured.[^cite_note-55]

After the war, Indiana remained a largely agricultural state. Post-war industries included mining, including limestone extraction; meatpacking; food processing, such as milling grain, distilling it into alcohol; and the building of [wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon), [buggies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buggy_\(carriage\)), farm machinery, and hardware.[^cite_note-INdustry-56] However, the [discovery of natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_gas_boom) in the 1880s in northern Indiana led to an economic boom: the abundant and cheap fuel attracted [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry); the availability of jobs, in turn, attracted new settlers from other parts of the country as well as from Europe.[^cite_note-Gray_1995,_p._202-57] This led to the rapid expansion of cities such as [South Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend), Indianapolis, and [Fort Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne).[^cite_note-INdustry-56]

### Early 20th century



Child labor in Indiana glassworks, 1908, by [Lewis Hine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hine)

The early decades of the 20th century saw Indiana develop into a leading [manufacturing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt) with heavy industry concentrating in the north.[^cite_note-The_H-43] In 1906 the [United States Steel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Steel_Corporation) created a new industrial city on Lake Michigan named [Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_Indiana), after [Elbert Henry Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbert_Henry_Gary), its founding chairman. With industrialization, workers developed labor unions (their strike activities induced governor [James P. Goodrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Goodrich) to declare martial law in Gary in 1919)[^cite_note-58] and a [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_America).[^cite_note-59] Railroader [Eugene Debs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_V._Debs) of [Terre Haute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terre_Haute,_Indiana), the Socialist candidate received 901,551 votes (6.0% of the national vote) in the 1912 presidential election.[^cite_note-Election1912-60] Suffrage movements also arose to enfranchise women.[^cite_note-Gray_1995,_p._202-57]

In its earlier years, Indiana was a leader in the automobile boom. Beginning its production in [Kokomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokomo,_Indiana) in 1896, [Haynes-Apperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynes-Apperson) was the nation's first commercially successful auto company.[^cite_note-61] The importance of vehicle and parts manufacture to the state was symbolized by the construction in 1909 of the [Indianapolis Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway).[^cite_note-history-62]

In the 1920s, state politics was heavily influenced by the rise of the [Indiana Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Klan). First organized in 1915 as a branch of the [Ku Klux Klan](16779.md), it appealed to white Protestants alarmed by social and economic trends, including changes induced by immigration from southern and central Europe.[^cite_note-63] In the name of defending "hundred-per-cent Americanism", the Klan sought to exclude from public life "[Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), [Catholics](606848.md), [Jews](25955086.md), [Negroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negro), [bootleggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum-running), [pacifists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism), [evolutionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionism), foreigners, and all persons it considered immoral".[^cite_note-64]

By 1925 the Klan had 250,000 members, an estimated 30% of native-born white men.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] By 1925 over half the elected members of the [Indiana General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly), the [governor of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Indiana), and many other high-ranking officials in local and state government were members of the Klan. Politicians had also learned they needed Klan endorsement to win office.[^cite_note-Moore-1991-67] That year, "Grand Dragon" [D.C. Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.C._Stephenson), who had begun to brag "I am the law in Indiana",[^cite_note-Lutholtz-1991-68] was charged and convicted for the rape and murder of [Madge Oberholtzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madge_Oberholtzer), a young school teacher. Denied pardon, in 1927 Stephenson gave the *[Indianapolis Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Times)* lists of people the Klan had paid. Partly as a result of compounded scandal, membership collapsed.[^cite_note-Lutholtz2-69]

Throughout the 1930s, [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) [Democrats](5043544.md) topped the polls[^cite_note-Madison-2014-70] and "the Klan was political poison".[^cite_note-71] During those years, Indiana, like the rest of the nation, was affected by the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression): businesses were shuttered and farm income collapsed. The numbers seeking employment, shelter and relief were augmented by out-of-state [Dust Bowl](59749.md) migrants. Swept into office in the nationwide landslide for [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) in 1932, Democratic governor [Paul V. McNutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_V._McNutt), called on Hoosiers to "prove that government may be a great instrument of human progress",[^cite_note-Madison-2014-70] and struggled to build a state-funded welfare system to help overwhelmed private charities. He reorganized state government, greatly enlarging his executive powers, introduced state income tax and ended [Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States). On several occasions, he declared [martial law](50477.md) to put an end to worker strikes.[^cite_note-72]

[World War II](32927.md) helped lift Indiana's economy, as the war required steel, food and other goods the state produced.[^cite_note-TO-73] Roughly 10% of Indiana's population joined the armed forces, while hundreds of industries earned war production contracts and began making war material.[^cite_note-74] Indiana manufactured 4.5% of total U.S. military armaments during World War II, ranking eighth among the 48 states.[^cite_note-75] The expansion of industry to meet war demands helped end the Great Depression.[^cite_note-TO-73][^cite_note-76]

### Post World War II

With economic recovery, the Republican supremacy in the state was restored.[^cite_note-77] Manufacturers became the primary employers, a trend that continued into the 1960s. [Urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) during the 1950s and 1960s led to substantial growth in the state's cities. The auto, steel and pharmaceutical industries topped Indiana's major businesses. Indiana's population continued to grow after the war, exceeding five million by the 1970 census.[^cite_note-78]

In the early 1960s, Democrats briefly returned to state office, and under the administration of [Matthew E. Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_E._Welsh) the state adopted its first sales tax of 2%.[^cite_note-79] Indiana schools were [desegregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desegregated) in 1949. In 1950, the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) reported Indiana's population as 95.5% white and 4.4% black.[^cite_note-USCB-80] Governor Welsh also worked with the General Assembly to pass the Indiana [Civil Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights) Bill, granting equal protection to minorities in seeking employment.[^cite_note-81]

The [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) created a recession that hurt the automotive industry in Indiana. Companies began a long series of downsizing that contributed to high unemployment rates in long-established manufacturing centers such as [Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson,_Indiana), [Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_Indiana), [Muncie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muncie,_Indiana), and [Kokomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokomo,_Indiana). The trend continued until the 1980s when the national and state economy began to diversify from heavy industry toward services and high-tech manufacturing, and to recover.[^cite_note-82] Meanwhile, the [farm crisis of the 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_farm_crisis) accelerated [rural flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_flight) and farm consolidation,[^cite_note-83] a trend that has resumed in the 21st century.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

### 21st century

On March 6, 2020, following the first confirmed case of [COVID-19](63030231.md) in the state, Governor [Eric Holcomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Holcomb) and state health officials declared a public health emergency. After a period in which there were a number of [pandemic](62750956.md)-related restrictions and advisories, and sharp job cuts,[^cite_note-86] Holcomb signed a bill to end the emergency on March 3, 2022.[^cite_note-87]

At that point, employment had already recovered,[^cite_note-88] but in comparative terms state appeared, post-COVID, to face greater challenges. In 2025 Indiana's economy (the state's business environment, labor market and overall economic growth) ranked 40th in the first [U.S. News & World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report) Report's annual report on how well all 50 states in the United States serve citizens,[^cite_note-USWR_Best_State_Rankings_2025-89] down from 25th in 2018.[^cite_note-90]

Indiana's highest ranking on the 2025 "scorecard" was 16th in the opportunity category. It was 30th in economic opportunity, and 39th in equality, but benefitted from being 18th in affordability (by comparison New Hampshire, which was 1st in economic opportunity and 3rd in equality, was 42nd in affordability).[^cite_note-USWR_Best_State_Rankings_2025-89] Its lowest score was for pollution and natural environment, with Indiana ranking worst among the 50 states.[^cite_note-91]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Indiana)



With a total area (land and water) of 36,418 square miles (94,320 km<sup>2</sup>), Indiana ranks as the 38th largest state in size.[^cite_note-NatAtlasProfile-92] The state has a maximum dimension north to south of 250 miles (400 km) and a maximum east to west dimension of 145 miles (233 km).[^cite_note-93] The state's geographic center (39° 53.7'N, 86° 16.0W) is in [Marion County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_County,_Indiana).[^cite_note-Netstate-94]

Located in the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States), Indiana is one of eight states that make up the [Great Lakes Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region_\(North_America\)).[^cite_note-95] Indiana is bordered on the north by [Michigan](18859.md), on the east by [Ohio](22199.md), and on the west by [Illinois](14849.md), partially separated by the [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River).[^cite_note-FW-96] [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) borders Indiana on the northwest and the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) separates Indiana from [Kentucky](16846.md) on the south.[^cite_note-Netstate-94][^cite_note-97]

### Geology and terrain

See also: [Paleontology in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontology_in_Indiana), [List of ecoregions in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Indiana), and [List of wildflowers in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wildflowers_in_Indiana)



Rolling hills in the [Charles C. Deam Wilderness Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_C._Deam_Wilderness_Area) of [Hoosier National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosier_National_Forest), in the [Indiana Uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Uplands)

The average altitude of Indiana is about 760 feet (230 m) above sea level.[^cite_note-98] The highest point in the state is [Hoosier Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosier_Hill) in [Wayne County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_County,_Indiana) at 1,257 feet (383 m) above sea level.[^cite_note-NatAtlasProfile-92][^cite_note-99] The lowest point at 320 feet (98 m) above sea level is in [Posey County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posey_County,_Indiana), where the [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River) meets the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River).[^cite_note-NatAtlasProfile-92][^cite_note-Netstate-94] The resulting [elevation span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_elevation), 937 feet (286 m), is the narrowest of any non-coastal U.S. state. Only 2,850 square miles (7,400 km<sup>2</sup>) have an altitude greater than 1,000 feet (300 m) and this area is enclosed within 14 counties. About 4,700 square miles (12,000 km<sup>2</sup>) have an elevation of less than 500 feet (150 m), mostly concentrated along the Ohio and lower Wabash Valleys, from [Tell City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_City,_Indiana) and [Terre Haute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terre_Haute,_Indiana) to [Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana) and [Mount Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon,_Indiana).[^cite_note-100]

The state includes two natural regions of the United States: the Central Lowlands and the [Interior Low Plateaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Low_Plateaus).[^cite_note-101] The [till plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Till_plain) make up the northern and central regions of Indiana. Much of its appearance is a result of elements left behind by [glaciers](12463.md). Central Indiana is mainly flat with some low rolling hills (except where rivers cut deep valleys through the plain, like at the Wabash River and Sugar Creek) and soil composed of glacial sands, gravel and clay, which results in exceptional farmland.[^cite_note-FW-96] Northern Indiana is similar, except for the presence of higher and hillier [terminal moraines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_moraine) and hundreds of [kettle lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_lake). In [northwest Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Indiana) there are various sand ridges and dunes, some reaching nearly 200 feet in height; most of them are at [Indiana Dunes National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Dunes_National_Park). These are along the Lake Michigan shoreline and also inland to the [Kankakee Outwash Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankakee_Outwash_Plain).

Southern Indiana is characterized by valleys and rugged, hilly terrain, contrasting with much of the state. Here, bedrock is exposed at the surface. Because of the prevalent [Indiana limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_limestone), the area has many caves, caverns, and quarries. Harrison Spring, the largest spring in the state, is located in Harrison County.

### Hydrology

See also: [List of Indiana rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indiana_rivers), [List of dams and reservoirs in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dams_and_reservoirs_in_Indiana), [List of lakes in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_in_Indiana), and [Watersheds of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watersheds_of_Indiana)



The [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River) converges with the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) at [Posey County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posey_County,_Indiana). The southernmost point in Indiana is located at Uniontown Bend 4.6 miles upstream of the confluence, south of [Hovey Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovey_Lake?action=edit\&redlink=1) (near the center of the photograph)

Major river systems in Indiana include the Whitewater, White, Blue, Wabash, St. Joseph, and Maumee rivers.[^cite_note-102] According to the Indiana Department of Natural Resources, as of 2007, there were 65 rivers, streams, and creeks of environmental interest or scenic beauty, which included only a portion of an estimated 24,000 total river miles within the state.[^cite_note-103]

The [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River), which is the longest free-flowing river east of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River), is the official river of Indiana.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] At 475 miles (764 kilometers) in length, the river bisects the state from northeast to southwest, forming part of the state's border with Illinois, before converging with the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River). The river has been the subject of several songs, such as "[On the Banks of the Wabash, Far Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Banks_of_the_Wabash,_Far_Away)", "[Wabash Cannonball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_Cannonball)", and "[Back Home Again in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Home_Again_in_Indiana)".[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

There are about 900 lakes listed by the Indiana Department of Natural Resources.[^cite_note-108] To the northwest, Indiana borders [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan), one of five lakes comprising the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes), the largest group of freshwater lakes in the world. [Tippecanoe Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tippecanoe_Lake), the deepest lake in the state, reaches depths at nearly 120 feet (37 m), while [Lake Wawasee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Wawasee) is the largest natural lake in Indiana.[^cite_note-109] At 10,750 acres (summer pool level), [Monroe Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Lake) is the largest lake in Indiana.[^cite_note-110]

### Climate

Further information: [Climate change in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Indiana)



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Indiana, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](42778673.md)

In the past, almost all of Indiana had a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Dfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), with cold winters and hot, wet summers;[^cite_note-111] only the extreme southern portion of the state lay within the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Cfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), which receives more precipitation than other parts of Indiana.[^cite_note-FW-96] But as of the 2016 update, about half the state is now classified as humid subtropical. Temperatures generally diverge from the north and south sections of the state. In midwinter, average high/low temperatures range from around 30 °F/15 °F (−1 °C/−10 °C) in the far north to 41 °F/24 °F (5 °C/−4 °C) in the far south.[^cite_note-weather.gov-112]

In midsummer there is generally a little less variation across the state, as average high/low temperatures range from around 84 °F/64 °F (29 °C/18 °C) in the far north to 90 °F/69 °F (32 °C/21 °C) in the far south.[^cite_note-weather.gov-112] Indiana's record high temperature was 116 °F (47 °C) set on July 14, 1936, at [Collegeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegeville,_Indiana). The record low was −36 °F (−38 °C) on [January 19, 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_North_American_cold_wave) at [New Whiteland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Whiteland,_Indiana).[^cite_note-113] The [growing season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_season) typically spans from 155 days in the north to 185 days in the south.

While droughts occasionally occur in the state, rainfall totals are distributed relatively equally throughout the year. Precipitation totals range from 35 inches (89 cm) near Lake Michigan in northwest Indiana to 45 inches (110 cm) along the Ohio River in the south, while the state's average is 40 inches (100 cm). Annual snowfall in Indiana varies widely across the state, ranging from 80 inches (200 cm) in the northwest along Lake Michigan to 14 inches (36 cm) in the far south. [Lake effect snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_effect_snow) accounts for roughly half the snowfall in northwest and north central Indiana due to the effects of the moisture and relative warmth of Lake Michigan upwind. The average wind speed ranges from 6.5 miles per hour (10.5 km/h) to 12.7 miles per hour (20.4 km/h).[^cite_note-114]

In a 2012 report, Indiana was ranked eighth in a list of the top 20 tornado-prone states based on [National Weather Service](286989.md) data from 1950 through 2011.[^cite_note-115] A 2011 report ranked [South Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana) 15th among the top 20 tornado-prone U.S. cities,[^cite_note-116] while another report from 2011 ranked [Indianapolis](57707.md) eighth.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]Despite its vulnerability, Indiana is not part of [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley).[^cite_note-120]

### Time zones

Main article: [Time in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Indiana)

Indiana is one of 13 U.S. states that are divided into more than one time zone. Indiana's [time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone) have fluctuated over the past century. At present, most of the state observes [Eastern Time](496028.md); six counties near Chicago and six near Evansville observe [Central Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Time_Zone_\(North_America\)).[^cite_note-123] Debate continues on the matter.[^cite_note-124]

Before 2006, most of Indiana did not observe [daylight saving time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_saving_time) (DST). Some counties within this area, particularly [Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_County,_Indiana), [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Indiana), and [Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_County,_Indiana) counties near [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), and [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_County,_Indiana) and [Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dearborn_County,_Indiana) counties near [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati), Ohio, unofficially observed DST by local custom. Since April 2006 the entire state observes DST.[^cite_note-125]

[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-American) is the largest ancestry reported in Indiana, with 18.8% of the population reporting that ancestry in the census. Persons listing themselves as [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ancestry) (7.2%) and those of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-American) ancestry (11.1%) are also numerous, as are [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) (9.8%) and [Polish](22936.md) (2.6%).[^cite_note-Germanic-42] Most of those citing American ancestry are actually of European descent, including many of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-American) descent, but have family that has been in North America for so long, in many cases since the early [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America), that they identify simply as American.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] In the 1980 census 1,776,144 people claimed German ancestry, 1,356,135 claimed English ancestry and 1,017,944 claimed Irish ancestry out of a total population of 4,241,975 making the state 42% German, 32% English and 24% Irish.[^cite_note-153]

The state is home to a growing Hispanic population, making up 7.8% of the total population. The largest Hispanic ancestry in the state is Mexican (5.3%), making up a large majority of the Hispanic population.[^cite_note-154]

In the [2020 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Census), 648,513 Indiana residents were identified as African American (of the total 6,785,528).[^cite_note-155] In five of the state's 92 counties, African Americans make up more than 10% of the population: [Marion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_County,_Indiana) (27.5%), [Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_County,_Indiana) (24.9%), [St. Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph_County,_Indiana) (13.2%), [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_County,_Indiana) (11.5%), and [LaPorte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaPorte_County,_Indiana) (10.8%). African Americans in the seven counties of Marion (268,694), Lake (124,073), Allen (44,481), St. Joseph (36,123), [Vanderburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderburgh_County,_Indiana) (17,668), [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_County,_Indiana) (14,993), and [Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendricks_County,_Indiana) (13,521) make up more than 80% of all African Americans in the state.[^cite_note-156]

Population growth since 1990 has been concentrated in the counties surrounding Indianapolis, with four of the five fastest-growing counties in that area: [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_County,_Indiana), [Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendricks_County,_Indiana), [Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County,_Indiana), and [Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hancock_County,_Indiana). Hamilton County has also grown faster than any county in the states bordering Indiana ([Illinois](14849.md), [Michigan](18859.md), [Ohio](22199.md) and [Kentucky](16846.md)), and is the 20th-fastest growing county in the country.[^cite_note-157]

With a population of 887,642, [Indianapolis](57707.md) is the largest city in Indiana and the 15th-largest in the United States, according to the 2020 census. Three other cities in Indiana have a population greater than 100,000: [Fort Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana) (253,617), [Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana) (117,429) and [South Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana) (101,168).[^cite_note-158] Since 2000, [Fishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishers,_Indiana) has seen the largest population rise amongst the state's twenty largest cities with an increase of 100%.[^cite_note-Nevers-159] Other cities that have seen extensive growth since 2000 are [Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood,_Indiana) (81%), [Noblesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noblesville,_Indiana) (39.4%), [Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel,_Indiana) (21.4%), [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Indiana)[^cite_note-wwwcensusgov-160] (12.8%) and [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Indiana) (9.3%).

[Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_Indiana) and [Hammond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond,_Indiana) have had the largest population declines regarding the 20 largest cities since 2000, with a decrease of 21.0% and 6.8% respectively.[^cite_note-Nevers-159] [Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana) (−4.2%), [Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson,_Indiana) (−4.0%) and [Muncie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muncie,_Indiana) (−3.9%) have also had declines.[^cite_note-161]

### Median income

See also: [Indiana locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_locations_by_per_capita_income)



As of the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census), Indiana's median household income was \$44,616, ranking it 36th among the United States and the District of Columbia.[^cite_note-175] In 2005, the median household income for Indiana residents was \$43,993. Nearly 498,700 Indiana households had incomes between \$50,000 and \$75,000, accounting for 20% of all households.[^cite_note-Justis_2006-176]

Hamilton County's median household income is nearly \$35,000 higher than the Indiana average. At \$78,932, it ranks seventh in the country among counties with fewer than 250,000 people. The next highest median incomes in Indiana are also found in the Indianapolis suburbs; Hendricks County has a median of \$57,538, followed by Johnson County at \$56,251.[^cite_note-Justis_2006-176]

Indiana is home to the [third largest population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish_in_Indiana) of [Amish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish) in the U.S.

## Law and government

Main article: [Government of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Indiana)

See also: [Indiana's congressional delegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana's_congressional_delegations) and [Indiana's congressional districts](4006882.md)



The [Indiana Statehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Statehouse) in [Indianapolis](57707.md), seat of [Indiana's state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Indiana), hosts the [Indiana General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly), the [Indiana Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Supreme_Court), and the [Governor of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Indiana).



[Constitution of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Indiana) (1816)

Indiana has a constitutional democratic republican form of government with three branches: the executive, including an elected governor and lieutenant governor; the legislative, consisting of an elected [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) General Assembly; and the judicial, the Supreme Court of Indiana, the Indiana Court of Appeals and circuit courts.

The [Governor of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Indiana) serves as the state's chief executive and has the authority to manage the government as established in the Constitution of Indiana. The governor and the [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Indiana) are jointly elected to four-year terms, with gubernatorial elections running concurrently with United States presidential elections (1996, 2000, 2004, 2008, etc.).[^cite_note-ST-191] The governor may not serve more than two consecutive terms.[^cite_note-ST-191] The governor works with the [Indiana General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly) and the [Indiana Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Supreme_Court) to govern the state and has the authority to adjust the other branches. The governor can call special sessions of the General Assembly and select and remove leaders of nearly all state departments, boards and commissions. Other notable powers include calling out the [Indiana Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Guard_Reserve) or the [Indiana National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_National_Guard) in times of emergency or disaster, issuing pardons or commuting the sentence of any criminal offenders except in cases of treason or impeachment and possessing an abundant amount of statutory authority.[^cite_note-ST-191][^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193]

The lieutenant governor serves as the President of the Senate and ensures the senate rules are acted in accordance with by its constituents. The lieutenant governor votes only when needed to break ties. If the governor dies in office, becomes permanently incapacitated, resigns or is impeached, the lieutenant governor becomes governor. If both the governor and lieutenant governor positions are unoccupied, the Senate President pro tempore becomes governor.[^cite_note-194]

The Indiana General Assembly is composed of a 50-member [Senate](4964463.md) and 100-member [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_House_of_Representatives). The Senate is the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) of the General Assembly and the House of Representatives is the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house).[^cite_note-ST-191] The General Assembly has exclusive legislative authority within the state government. Both the Senate and the House can introduce legislation, with the exception that the Senate is not authorized to initiate legislation that will affect revenue. Bills are debated and passed separately in each house, but both houses must pass them before they can be submitted to the Governor.[^cite_note-For-195] The legislature can nullify a veto from the governor with a majority vote of full membership in the Senate and House of Representatives.[^cite_note-ST-191] Each law passed by the General Assembly must apply without exception to the entire state. The General Assembly has no authority to create legislation that targets a particular community.[^cite_note-For-195][^cite_note-196] The General Assembly can manage the state's judiciary system by arranging the size of the courts and the bounds of their districts. It also can oversee the activities of the executive branch of the state government, has restricted power to regulate the county governments within the state, and has exclusive power to initiate the method to alter the Indiana Constitution.[^cite_note-For-195][^cite_note-197]

The [Indiana Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Supreme_Court) is made up of five judges with a [Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Court_of_Appeals) composed of 15 judges. The governor selects judges for the supreme and appeals courts from a group of applicants chosen by a special commission. After serving for two years, the judges must acquire the support of the electorate to serve for a 10-year term.[^cite_note-ST-191] In nearly all cases, the Supreme Court does not have [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) and can hear only cases petitioned to it after being heard in lower courts. Local circuit courts are where most cases begin with a trial and the consequence is decided by the jury. The Supreme Court has original and sole jurisdiction in certain areas including the practice of law, discipline or disbarment of Judges appointed to the lower state courts, and supervision over the exercise of jurisdiction by the other lower courts of the State.[^cite_note-Fur-198][^cite_note-199]

The state is divided into 92 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)), which are led by a board of county commissioners. 90 counties in Indiana have their own [circuit court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_court) with a judge elected for a six-year term. The remaining two counties, Dearborn and Ohio, are combined into one circuit. Many counties operate [superior courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_court) in addition to the circuit court. In densely populated counties where the caseload is traditionally greater, separate courts have been established to solely hear either juvenile, criminal, probate or small claims cases. The establishment, frequency and jurisdiction of these additional courts vary greatly from county to county. There are 85 city and town courts in Indiana municipalities, created by local ordinance, typically handling minor offenses and not considered [courts of record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_record). County officials elected to four-year terms include an auditor, recorder, treasurer, sheriff, coroner and clerk of the circuit court. All incorporated cities in Indiana have a mayor and council form of municipal government. Towns are governed by a town council and townships are governed by a township trustee and advisory board.[^cite_note-ST-191][^cite_note-200]

*[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)* ranked Indiana first in the publication's inaugural 2017 Best States for Government listing. Among individual categories, Indiana ranked above average in budget transparency (#1), government digitization (#6), and fiscal stability (#8), and ranked average in state integrity (#25).[^cite_note-201]

In a 2020 study, Indiana was ranked as the 10th hardest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-202] [Abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Indiana) is illegal in Indiana with limited exceptions.[^cite_note-203]

In 2015, Indiana expanded Medicaid eligibility from ACA provisions. A study from Kaiser Family Foundation found that the uninsured rate in Indiana dropped from 14% in 2014 to 8.5% in 2015 after the expansion. Under Obamacare, when a state adopts Medicaid expansion, the eligibility income requirement goes from 100% of the federal poverty line to 138% of the federal poverty rate. However, Indiana requires enrollees to contribute to a "POWER account" which functions as a health savings account for all beneficiaries.[^cite_note-204] This has been controversial because beneficiaries would have to pay into an account with money they would not have.

### Military installations



Members of the [Indiana National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_National_Guard) at the [Muscatatuck Urban Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscatatuck_Urban_Training_Center) near [Butlerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butlerville,_Indiana)

Indiana is home to several current and former [military installations](843566.md). The largest of these is the [Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Surface_Warfare_Center_Crane_Division), approximately 25 miles southwest of [Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana), which is the third-largest naval installation in the world, comprising approximately 108 square miles of territory.[^cite_note-205] Located inside NSWC Crane is Constitution Grove, a 64,000-acre forest containing approximately 120 old-growth [white oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_oak) trees selected for the maintenance of the [USS Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constitution), the world's oldest commissioned warship still afloat.[^cite_note-206]

Other active installations include [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard) fighter units at [Fort Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana), and [Terre Haute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terre_Haute) airports (to be consolidated at Fort Wayne under the [2005 BRAC proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure,_2005), with the Terre Haute facility remaining open as a non-flying installation). The [Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_National_Guard) conducts operations at [Camp Atterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Atterbury) in [Edinburgh, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh,_Indiana), helicopter operations out of [Shelbyville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelbyville,_Indiana) Airport and urban training at [Muscatatuck Urban Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscatatuck_Urban_Training_Center). The Army's [Newport Chemical Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Chemical_Depot), which is now closed and turning into a coal purifier plant.

Indiana was formerly home to two major military installations; [Grissom Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grissom_Air_Force_Base) near Peru (realigned to an [Air Force Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Reserve) installation in 1994) and [Fort Benjamin Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benjamin_Harrison) near Indianapolis, now closed, though the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) continues to operate a large finance center there ([Defense Finance and Accounting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Finance_and_Accounting_Service)).

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Indiana)

See also: [Political party strength in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_Indiana) and [United States presidential elections in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_elections_in_Indiana)

An older man in a tan suit reaches across a table to shake a woman's hand.

[Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence) at the Indiana State Fair, 2014

From 1880 to 1924, a resident of Indiana was included in all but one presidential election. Indiana Representative [William Hayden English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hayden_English) was nominated for vice president and ran with Winfield Scott Hancock in the [1880 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1880_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-207] Former Indiana Governor [Thomas A. Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Hendricks) was elected vice president in 1884. He served until his death on November 25, 1885, under President [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland).[^cite_note-208] In 1888, former Senator from Indiana [Benjamin Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Harrison) was elected president and served one term. He remains the only President from Indiana. Indiana Senator [Charles W. Fairbanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Fairbanks) was elected vice president in 1904, serving under President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) until 1909.[^cite_note-209] Fairbanks made another run for vice president with [Charles Evans Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Evans_Hughes) in 1916, but they both lost to [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) and former Indiana Governor [Thomas R. Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_R._Marshall), who served as vice president from 1913 until 1921.[^cite_note-210] Not until 1988 did another presidential election involve a native of Indiana when Senator [Dan Quayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Quayle) was elected vice president and served one term with [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush).[^cite_note-FW-96] Governor [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence) was elected vice president in 2016 and served one term with [Donald Trump](4848272.md).

Indiana has long been considered a [Republican](32070.md) stronghold.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212] It was through the Republican Party that in the mid-1920s the 250,000 member [Indiana Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Klan) exerted its influence in the [Indiana General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly), where more than half the elected representatives were Klansmen, and in the governor's office.[^cite_note-Moore-1991-67] The party suffered accordingly when the [Madge Oberholtzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madge_Oberholtzer) scandal discredited the Klan leadership.[^cite_note-Lutholtz-1991-68] A further opening was created for the Democrats when the state shared in the country's disillusion with the [Depression-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) administration of [President Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover). In 1932, [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) won the state with 55% of the vote. The ascendancy of the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) Democrats, however, was temporary. In 1940, Indiana was one of only 10 states to support Republican [Wendell Willkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Willkie).[^cite_note-FW-96]

On 14 occasions the Republican candidate has defeated the Democrat by a double-digit margin in the state, including six times where a Republican won the state by more than 20 percentage points.[^cite_note-Atlas-213] Roosevelt's victory in 1932 was one of only five occasion since 1900 in which the state has favored a [Democrat](5043544.md) for president. The first was [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) with 43% of the vote in 1912; the last, by a narrow margin of 50% to 49%, was [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) in 2008.[^cite_note-214]

Most recently, Republican [Donald Trump](4848272.md) carried the state in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election_in_Indiana), [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_Indiana), and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Indiana).

While only five Democratic presidential nominees have carried Indiana since 1900, 11 Democrats [were elected governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Indiana) during that time. Before [Mitch Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Daniels) became governor in 2005, Democrats had held the office for 16 consecutive years. Indiana elects two senators and nine representatives to Congress. The state has 11 electoral votes in presidential elections.[^cite_note-Atlas-213] Seven of the [districts](4006882.md) favor the Republican Party according to the CPVI rankings; there are seven Republicans serving as representatives and two Democrats.

Historically, Republicans have been strongest in the eastern and central portions of the state, while Democrats have been strongest in the northwestern part of the state. Occasionally, certain counties in the southern part of the state will vote Democratic. [Marion County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_County,_Indiana), Indiana's most populous county, supported the Republican candidates from 1968 to 2000, before backing the Democrats in the 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016, 2020, and 2024 elections. Indiana's second-most populous county, [Lake County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_County,_Indiana), strongly supports the Democratic party and has not voted for a Republican since 1972.[^cite_note-Atlas-213]

In 2005, the Bay Area Center for Voting Research rated the most liberal and conservative cities in the United States on voting statistics in the 2004 presidential election, based on 237 cities with populations of more than 100,000. Five Indiana cities were mentioned in the study. On the liberal side, [Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_Indiana) was ranked second and [South Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana) came in at 83. Among conservative cities, [Fort Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana) was 44th, [Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana) was 60th and [Indianapolis](57707.md) was 82nd on the list.[^cite_note-215] Republicans also currently hold [supermajorities](638313.md) in both chambers of the [Indiana General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly) and have dominated the [governorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Indiana) since 2005.

## Culture

### Arts

See also: [Golden Age of Indiana Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Indiana_Literature)

The last decades of the 19th century began what is known as the "golden age of Indiana literature", a period that lasted until the 1920s.[^cite_note-artsummary-216] [Edward Eggleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Eggleston) wrote *The Hoosier Schoolmaster* (1871), the first best-seller to originate in the state. Many more followed, including [Maurice Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Thompson)'s *Hoosier Mosaics* (1875) and [Lew Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Wallace)'s *[Ben-Hur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Hur:_A_Tale_of_the_Christ)* (1880). Indiana developed a reputation as the "American heartland" after the publication of several widely read novels, beginning with [Booth Tarkington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booth_Tarkington)'s *The Gentleman from Indiana* (1899), [Meredith Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_Nicholson)'s The Hoosiers (1900), and Thompson's *Alice of Old Vincennes* (1900).[^cite_note-artsummary-216] [James Whitcomb Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Whitcomb_Riley), known as the "Hoosier Poet" and the most popular poet of his age, wrote hundreds of poems with Hoosier themes, including *[Little Orphant Annie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Orphant_Annie)*. A unique art culture also began to develop in the late 19th century, beginning the [Hoosier School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosier_Group) of landscape painting and the [Richmond Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Group) of impressionist painters. The painters, including [T. C. Steele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._C._Steele), whose work was influenced by southern Indiana's colorful hills, were known for their use of vivid colors.[^cite_note-artsummary-216] Prominent musicians and composers from Indiana also reached national acclaim, including [Paul Dresser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dresser), whose most popular song, "[On the Banks of the Wabash, Far Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Banks_of_the_Wabash,_Far_Away)", was later adopted as the official state song.[^cite_note-ihspauldresser-217]

### Sports

Main article: [Sports in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Indiana)

#### Motorsports



Indianapolis is home to the annual [Indianapolis 500](159018.md) race.

Indiana has an extensive history with [auto racing](1022.md). Indianapolis hosts the [Indianapolis 500](159018.md) mile race over [Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day) weekend at the [Indianapolis Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway) every May. The name of the race is usually shortened to "Indy 500" and also goes by the nickname "The Greatest Spectacle in Racing".[^cite_note-indianapolismotorspeedway.com-218] The race attracts more than 350,000 people every year, making it the largest single-day sporting event in the world.[^cite_note-219] The track also hosts the [Brickyard 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brickyard_400) ([NASCAR](21114.md)) and the [Red Bull Indianapolis Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Indianapolis_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-indianapolismotorspeedway.com-218] From 2000 to 2007, it hosted the [United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Grand_Prix) ([Formula One](10854.md)). Indiana features the world's largest and most prestigious [drag race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_race), the [NHRA Mac Tools U.S. Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHRA_U.S._Nationals), held each [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day) weekend at [Lucas Oil Raceway at Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Oil_Raceway_at_Indianapolis) in [Clermont, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont,_Indiana). Indiana is also host to a major [unlimited hydroplane racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroplane_racing) power boat race circuits in the major [H1 Unlimited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H1_Unlimited) league, the [Madison Regatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Regatta) ([Madison, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Indiana)).[^cite_note-220]

#### Professional sports



The [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts) of the [National Football League](21211.md) have been based in the state since 1984.

As of 2013 Indiana has produced more [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA) players per capita than any other state. Muncie has produced the most per capita of any American city, with two other Indiana cities in the top ten.[^cite_note-deadspin20130617-221] It has a rich basketball heritage that reaches back to the sport's formative years. The NBA's [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) play their home games at [Gainbridge Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainbridge_Fieldhouse); they began play in 1967 in the [American Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association) (ABA) and joined the NBA when the leagues [merged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABA-NBA_merger) in 1976. Although [James Naismith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Naismith) developed basketball in [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](1645518.md) in 1891, high school basketball was born in Indiana. In 1925, Naismith visited an Indiana basketball state finals game along with 15,000 screaming fans and later wrote "Basketball really had its origin in Indiana, which remains the center of the sport." The 1986 film *[Hoosiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosiers_\(film\))* is inspired by the story of the 1954 Indiana state champions [Milan High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Milan_High_School_basketball_team). Professional basketball player [Larry Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Bird) was born in [West Baden Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Baden_Springs,_Indiana) and was raised in [French Lick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Lick,_Indiana). He went on to lead the [Boston Celtics](43376.md) to the NBA championship in 1981, 1984, and 1986.[^cite_note-222]

Indianapolis is home to the [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts). The Colts are members of the [South Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_South) of the [American Football Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_Conference). The Colts have roots back to 1913 as the [Dayton Triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Triangles). They became an official team after moving to [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), [MD](18858.md), in 1953. In 1984, the [Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Baltimore_Colts) [relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Colts_relocation_controversy) to Indianapolis, leading to an eventual rivalry with the [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens). After calling the [RCA Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Dome) home for 25 years, the Colts play their home games at [Lucas Oil Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Oil_Stadium) in Indianapolis. While in Baltimore, the Colts won [Super Bowl V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_V). In Indianapolis, the Colts won [Super Bowl XLI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLI), bringing the franchise total to two. In recent years the Colts have regularly competed in the NFL playoffs.

Indiana was home to two charter members of the [National Football League](21211.md) teams, the [Hammond Pros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_Pros) and the [Muncie Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muncie_Flyers). Another early NFL franchise, the [Evansville Crimson Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville_Crimson_Giants) spent two seasons in the league before folding.

## Economy and infrastructure

Main article: [Economy of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Indiana)



[Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan)'s beaches, popular with tourists, are juxtaposed with heavy industry.



Indiana is the fifth largest corn-producing state in the U.S., with over a billion bushels harvested in 2013.

[^cite_note-223]

In 2017, Indiana had a civilian labor force of nearly 3.4 million, the 15th largest in the United States. Indiana has an unemployment rate of 3.7%, as of May 2025.[^cite_note-224] The total gross state product in 2016 was \$347.2 billion.[^cite_note-225] A high percentage of Indiana's income is from manufacturing.[^cite_note-226] According to the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Labor_Statistics), nearly 17% of the state's non-farm workforce is employed in manufacturing, the highest of any state in the U.S.[^cite_note-227] The state's five leading exports were motor vehicles and auto parts, pharmaceutical products, industrial machinery, optical and medical equipment, and electric machinery.[^cite_note-228]

Despite its reliance on manufacturing, Indiana has been less affected by declines in traditional [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt) manufacturers than many of its neighbors. The explanation appears to be certain factors in the labor market. First, much of the heavy manufacturing, such as industrial machinery and steel, requires highly skilled labor, and firms are often willing to locate where hard-to-train skills already exist. Second, Indiana's labor force is primarily in medium-sized and smaller cities rather than in very large and expensive metropolises. This makes it possible for firms to offer somewhat lower wages for these skills than would normally be paid. Firms often see in Indiana a chance to obtain higher than average skills at lower than average wages.[^cite_note-229]

Indiana is in the U.S. [Corn Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Belt) and [Grain Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_Belt). It has a feedlot-style system raising corn to fatten hogs and cattle. Along with corn, [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans) are also a major cash crop. Its proximity to large urban centers, such as [Indianapolis](57707.md) and Chicago, assure dairying, egg production, and specialty horticulture occur. Other crops include melons, tomatoes, grapes, mint, popping corn, and tobacco in the southern counties.[^cite_note-230] Most of the original land was not prairie and had to be cleared of deciduous trees. Many parcels of woodland remain and support a furniture-making sector in southern Indiana.

### Business

In 2025, Indiana was home to seven [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies. These included [Eli Lilly and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Lilly_and_Company), the pharmaceutical giant and the state's largest corporation, headquartered in Indianapolis.[^cite_note-Beard-2025-231] Indiana ranks fifth among all U.S. states in total sales and shipments of pharmaceutical products and second in the number of biopharmaceutical related jobs.[^cite_note-232]

[Northwest Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Indiana) has been the largest steel producing center in the U.S. since 1975,[^cite_note-233] with Fort Wayne hosting Fortune 500 [Steel Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Dynamics), the third largest producer of carbon steel products in the United States.

Newer manufacturing sectors include orthopedic and surgical products, represented by [Zimmer Biomet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimmer_Biomet), based in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw,_Indiana). Among the major service providers are [Elevance Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevance_Health) (formerly Anthem, Inc.) in Indianapolis which is ranked among the nation's top healthcare insurers.[^cite_note-Beard-2025-231]

In 2025, small businesses (generally defined as those with under 500 employees) accounted for 43 percent of Indiana's employees. While the number of small businesses has increased since the end of the COVID pandemic in 2022, it has done so without a commensurate increase in employment. Many are sole traders and self-employed contractors.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

Evaluating factors such as the private sector business creation rate, patent grants, tax burden, venture capital investment and how many top businesses are headquartered in a state, in 2025, *US News & World Report* ranked Indiana 39th among the 50 states for its "business environment".[^cite_note-USWR_Best_State_Rankings_2025-89]

### Taxation

See also: [Taxation in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Indiana)

Tax is collected by the [Indiana Department of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Department_of_Revenue).[^cite_note-236]

Indiana has a flat state [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) rate of 3.23%. Many of the state's counties also collect income tax. The state [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) rate is 7% with exemptions for food, prescription medications and over-the-counter medications.[^cite_note-237] In some jurisdictions, an additional Food and Beverage Tax is charged, at a rate of 1% (Marion County's rate is 2%), on sales of prepared meals and beverages.[^cite_note-238]

[Property taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) are imposed on both real and personal property in Indiana and are administered by the Department of Local Government Finance.[^cite_note-239] Property is subject to taxation by a variety of taxing units (schools, counties, townships, municipalities, and libraries), making the total tax rate the sum of the tax rates imposed by all taxing units in which a property is located. However, a "circuit breaker" law enacted on March 19, 2008, limits property taxes to 1% of assessed value for homeowners, 2% for rental properties and farmland, and 3% for businesses.

### State budget

Indiana does not have a legal requirement to balance the state budget either in law or its constitution. Instead, it has a constitutional ban on assuming debt. The state has a [Rainy Day Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_Day_Fund) and for healthy reserves proportional to spending. Indiana is one of six U.S. states to not allow a [line-item veto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-item_veto_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-240]

Since 2010, Indiana has been one of a few states to hold [AAA bond credit ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_credit_rating#Credit_rating_tiers) with the [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(credit_rating_agencies\)) credit rating agencies, the highest possible rating.[^cite_note-241]

### Energy

See also: [List of power stations in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Indiana)



[Coal-fired electric plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station), like [Clifty Creek Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifty_Creek_Power_Plant) in [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Indiana), produced about 85 percent of Indiana's energy supply in 2014.

[^cite_note-242]

Indiana's power production chiefly consists of the consumption of fossil fuels, mainly coal. It has 24 coal power plants, including the country's largest coal power plant, [Gibson Generating Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibson_Generating_Station), across the Wabash River from [Mount Carmel, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel,_Illinois). Indiana was also home to the coal-fired plant with the highest sulfur dioxide emissions in the United States, the [Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Gallagher_Generating_Station) power plant, just west of New Albany.[^cite_note-243]

In 2010, Indiana had estimated coal reserves of 57 billion tons, and state mining operations produced 35 million tons of coal annually.[^cite_note-244] Indiana also has at least 900 million barrels of petroleum reserves in the [Trenton Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenton_Gas_Field), though they are not easily recoverable. While Indiana has made commitments to increasing the use of renewable resources such as wind, hydroelectric, biomass, or solar power, progress has been very slow, mainly because of the continued abundance of coal in southern Indiana. Most of the new plants in the state have been [coal gasification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_gasification) plants. Another source is hydroelectric power.

[Wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Indiana) has been growing rapidly. Estimates in 2006 raised Indiana's wind capacity from 30 MW at 50 m turbine height to 40,000 MW at 70 m, and to 130,000 MW at 100 m, in 2010, the height of newer turbines.[^cite_note-245] By the end of 2011, Indiana had installed 1,340 MW of wind turbines.[^cite_note-246] In 2020, this total had more than doubled to 2,968 MW.[^cite_note-247]

### Transportation

#### Airports

See also: [List of airports in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Indiana)

[Indianapolis International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_International_Airport) serves the greater Indianapolis area. It was rebuilt in November 2008 and offers a midfield passenger terminal, concourses, air traffic control tower, parking garage, and airfield and apron improvements.[^cite_note-248]

Other major airports include [Evansville Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville_Regional_Airport), [Fort Wayne International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne_International_Airport) (which houses the [122d Fighter Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/122d_Fighter_Wing) of the [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard)), and [South Bend International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend_International_Airport). A long-standing proposal to turn [Gary Chicago International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Chicago_International_Airport) into Chicago's third major airport received a boost in early 2006 with the approval of \$48 million in federal funding over the next ten years.[^cite_note-249]

No airlines operate out of [Terre Haute Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terre_Haute_Regional_Airport) but it is used primarily for general aviation. Since 1954, the [181st Fighter Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/181st_Fighter_Wing) of the Indiana [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard) was stationed there, but the [Base Realignment and Closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure) (BRAC) Proposal of 2005 stated the 181st would lose its fighter mission and [F-16](11642.md) aircraft, leaving the Terre Haute facility a general-aviation-only facility.

[Louisville International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville_International_Airport), across the Ohio River in [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), serves southern Indiana, as does [Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati/Northern_Kentucky_International_Airport) in [Hebron, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron,_Kentucky). Many residents of [Northwest Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Indiana), which is primarily in the [Chicago Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Metropolitan_Area), use Chicago's airports, [O'Hare International Airport](248449.md) and [Chicago Midway International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Midway_International_Airport).

#### Highways



The [Interstate 69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_69_in_Indiana) extension project in [Monroe County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_County,_Indiana)

The U.S. [Interstate highways in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Interstate_Highways_in_Indiana) are [I-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_64_in_Indiana), [I-65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_65_in_Indiana), [I-265](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_265_in_Indiana), [I-465](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_465), [I-865](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_865), [I-69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_69_in_Indiana), [I-469](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_469), [I-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_70_in_Indiana), [I-74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_74_in_Indiana), [I-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Indiana), [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Indiana), [I-94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94_in_Indiana), and [I-275](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_275_\(Ohio%E2%80%93Indiana%E2%80%93Kentucky\)). The various highways intersecting in and around [Indianapolis](57707.md), along with its historical status as a major railroad hub, and the canals that once crossed Indiana, are the source of the state's motto, the Crossroads of America. There are also many [U.S. routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._Routes_in_Indiana) and [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_State_Roads_in_Indiana) maintained by the [Indiana Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Department_of_Transportation). These are numbered according to the same convention as [U.S. Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Highways). Indiana allows highways of different classifications to have the same number. For example, I-64 and [Indiana State Road 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_State_Road_64) both exist (rather close to each other) in Indiana, but are two distinct roads with no relation to one another.

A \$3 billion project extending I-69 is underway. The project was divided into six sections, with the first five sections (linking [Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana) to [Martinsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsville,_Indiana)) now complete. The sixth and final phase from Martinsville to Indianapolis is under construction. When complete, I-69 will traverse an additional 142 miles (229 km) through the state.[^cite_note-250]

#### County roads

Most Indiana counties use a grid-based system to identify county roads; this system replaced the older arbitrary system of road numbers and names, and (among other things) makes it much easier to identify the sources of calls placed to the [9-1-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-1-1) system. Such systems are easier to implement in the glacially flattened northern and central portions of the state. Rural counties in the southern third of the state are less likely to have grids and more likely to rely on unsystematic road names (for example, Crawford, Harrison, Perry, Scott, and Washington Counties).

There are also counties in the northern portions of the state that have never implemented a grid or have only partially implemented one. Some counties are also laid out in an almost diamond-like grid system (e.g., Clark, Floyd, Gibson, and Knox Counties). Such a system is also almost useless in those situations as well. Knox County once operated two different grid systems for county roads because the county was laid out using two different survey grids, but has since decided to use road names and combine roads instead.

Notably, the county road grid system of St. Joseph County, whose major city is South Bend, uses perennial (tree) names (i.e. Ash, Hickory, Ironwood, etc.) in alphabetical order for north–south roads and presidential and other noteworthy names (i.e., Adams, Edison, Lincoln Way, etc.) in alphabetical order for east–west roads. There are exceptions to this rule in downtown South Bend and Mishawaka. Hamilton County's east–west roads continue Indianapolis's numbered street system from 96th Street at the Marion County line to 296th street at the Tipton County line.

#### Rail

See also: [List of Indiana railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indiana_railroads)



A [South Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Shore_Line) commuter train in [Michigan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_City,_Indiana)

Indiana has more than 4,255 railroad route miles (6,848 km), of which 91% are operated by [Class I railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_I_railroad), principally [CSX Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSX_Transportation) and the [Norfolk Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Railway). Other Class I railroads in Indiana include the [Canadian National Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Railway) and [Soo Line Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soo_Line_Railroad), a [CPKC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Kansas_City) subsidiary, as well as [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak). The remaining miles are operated by 37 regional, local, and switching and terminal railroads. The [South Shore Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Shore_Line_\(NICTD\)) is one of the country's most notable commuter rail systems, extending from Chicago to [South Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend). Indiana is implementing an extensive rail plan prepared in 2002 by the [Parsons Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsons_Corporation).[^cite_note-251] Many recreational trails, such as the [Monon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monon_Trail) and [Cardinal Greenway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Greenway), have been created from [abandoned rails routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rails-to-trails).

#### Ports



Barges are a common sight along the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River). Ports of Indiana manages three maritime ports in the state, two located on the Ohio.

Indiana annually ships more than 70 million tons of cargo by water each year, which ranks 14th among all U.S. states.[^cite_note-252] More than half of Indiana's border is water, which includes 400 miles (640 km) of direct access to two major freight transportation arteries: the Great Lakes/St. Lawrence Seaway (via Lake Michigan) and the Inland Waterway System (via the Ohio River). The [Ports of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Indiana) manages three major ports which include [Burns Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burns_Harbor,_Indiana), [Jeffersonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffersonville,_Indiana), and [Mount Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon,_Indiana).[^cite_note-253]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Indiana)

### Public schools



Established in 1864, [Shortridge High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortridge_High_School) in Indianapolis is Indiana's oldest free public high school.

[^cite_note-254]

Indiana's 1816 constitution was the first in the country to implement a state-funded [public school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(government_funded\)) system. It also allotted one township for a public university.[^cite_note-255] However, the plan turned out to be far too idealistic for a pioneer society, as tax money was not accessible for its organization. In the 1840s, [Caleb Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Mills) pressed the need for tax-supported schools, and in 1851 his advice was included in the new state constitution. In 1843 the Legislature ruled that African Americans could not attend the public schools, leading to the foundation of [Union Literary Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Literary_Institute) and other schools for them, funded by donations or the students themselves.[^cite_note-cepr.indiana.edu-256] The Indiana General Assembly authorized separate but equal schools for Black students in 1869, and in 1877 language in the law changed to allow for integrated schools.[^cite_note-cepr.indiana.edu-256]

Although the growth of the public school system was held up by legal entanglements, many public elementary schools were in use by 1870. Most children in Indiana attend public schools, but nearly ten percent attend private schools and [parochial schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parochial_school).[^cite_note-257] About half of all college students in Indiana are enrolled in state-supported four-year schools.

Indiana public schools have gone through several changes throughout Indiana's history. Modern, public school standards have been implemented all throughout the state. These new standards were adopted in April 2014. The overall goal of these new state standards is to ensure Indiana students have the necessary skills and requirements needed to enter college or the workforce upon high school graduation.[^cite_note-258] State standards can be found for nearly every major subject taught in Indiana public schools. Mathematics, English/Language Arts, Science, and Social Studies are among the top, prioritized standards. In 2022, the [Indiana Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Department_of_Education) reported that the state's overall graduation rate was 86.7%, down one percent from 2021.[^cite_note-259]

The rate of Indiana high school students attending college fell to 53% in 2022, a significant decline from 65% in 2017.[^cite_note-Chalkbeat_Indiana-2022-260][^cite_note-261] Indiana's college-going rates have fallen further than most states'.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-Chalkbeat_Indiana-2022-260][^cite_note-263] Trends reveal widening gaps for ethnic minorities and low-income families.[^cite_note-Chalkbeat_Indiana-2022-260]

### Vocational schools

Indiana has a strong vocational school system. [Charles Allen Prossor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Allen_Prossor), known as the father of [vocational education](56551.md) in the United States, was from [New Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Albany,_Indiana). The [Charles Allen Prosser School of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Allen_Prosser_School_of_Technology) is named in his honor. There are vocational schools in every region of Indiana, and most Indiana students can freely attend a vocational school during their high school years and receive training and job placement assistance in trade jobs. The International Union Of Operating Engineers (IUOE) has seven local unions in Indiana, offering apprenticeship and training opportunities.[^cite_note-264] According to the Electrical Training Alliance website, there are ten electrical training centers in Indiana.[^cite_note-265]

### Colleges and universities

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Indiana)

The state's community college system, [Ivy Tech Community College of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Tech_Community_College_of_Indiana), serves nearly 200,000 students annually, making it the state's largest public post-secondary educational institution and the nation's largest singly accredited statewide community college system.[^cite_note-266] In 2008, the Indiana University system agreed to shift most of its associate (2-year) degrees to the Ivy Tech Community College System.[^cite_note-267]

The largest non-community educational institution is [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University), a multi-campus university system; its [flagship campus at Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington) was endorsed as the Indiana Seminary in 1820. [Indiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_State_University) was established in Terre Haute as the state's [Normal School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_School) in 1865. [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) was chartered in West Lafayette as the state's [land-grant university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university) in 1869 and is also now a multi-campus institution. The three other independent state universities are [Vincennes University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincennes_University) (founded in 1801 by the Indiana Territory), [Ball State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_State_University) (founded 1918 as the East Division of Indiana State), and the [University of Southern Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_Indiana) (founded 1965 as the Evansville campus of Indiana State).

Many of Indiana's private colleges and universities are affiliated with religious organizations. The [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame), [Marian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_University_\(Indiana\)), and the [University of Saint Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saint_Francis_\(Indiana\)) are [Roman Catholic schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_school). Universities affiliated with Protestant denominations include [Anderson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_University_\(Indiana\)), [Butler University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_University), [Huntington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_University_\(United_States\)), [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University_\(Indiana\)), [Indiana Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Wesleyan_University), [Taylor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_University), [Franklin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_College_\(Indiana\)), [Hanover College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover_College), [DePauw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePauw_University), [Earlham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earlham_College), [Valparaiso University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valparaiso_University), the [University of Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Indianapolis),[^cite_note-ST-191] and the [University of Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Evansville).[^cite_note-268]

The state has several universities ranked among the best by *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)*. The University of Notre Dame ranks among the top 20, Purdue University among the top 50, and Indiana University Bloomington among the top 100.[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271] The three former schools are all [R1 Research Institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1_institution), along with [Indiana University Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Indianapolis) from the former [Indiana University–Purdue University Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University%E2%80%93Purdue_University_Indianapolis).[^cite_note-272] Butler, Valparaiso, and the University of Evansville are ranked among the top ten in the Regional University Midwest Rankings. Purdue's engineering programs are ranked fourth in the country.[^cite_note-273] In addition, Taylor University is ranked first in the Regional College Midwest Rankings and [Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose-Hulman_Institute_of_Technology) has been considered the nation's top undergraduate engineering school for 25 consecutive years.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] In 2023, the University of Notre Dame had the [seventh largest endowment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_the_United_States_by_endowment) among private postsecondary institutions in the U.S. (11th overall).

The state is also home to the largest [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_school_in_the_United_States) system in the country (the [Indiana University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_School_of_Medicine)) and a smaller, [osteopathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteopathic_medicine_in_the_United_States) medical school (Marian University's [Tom and Julie Wood College of Osteopathic Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_and_Julie_Wood_College_of_Osteopathic_Medicine)). In addition, Indiana boasts one [veterinary medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary_medicine) school (the Purdue College of Veterinary Medicine), one [optometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optometry) school (Indiana University School of Optometry), three [pharmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacy) schools (the [Purdue College of Pharmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University_College_of_Pharmacy), Butler College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences, and the Manchester College of Pharmacy, Natural, and Health Sciences) and four [law schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_school) ([IU Maurer School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Maurer_School_of_Law), [IU McKinney School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Robert_H._McKinney_School_of_Law), [Notre Dame Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_Law_School), and [Purdue Global Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_Global_Law_School)).
