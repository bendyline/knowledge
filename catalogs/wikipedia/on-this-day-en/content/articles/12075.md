The **Ghana Armed Forces** (**GAF**) is the state military organisation of [Ghana](12067.md), consisting of the [Army](9555564.md) (GA), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Navy) (GN), and [Ghana Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Air_Force).[^cite_note-3]

The Commander-in-Chief of the Ghana Armed Forces is the [president of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana), who is also the supreme military commander of the [Border Guard Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Guard_Unit) (BGU). The armed forces are managed by the [Minister of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Defence_\(Ghana\))[^cite_note-4] and the [Chief of Defence Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff_\(Ghana\)).

## History

In 1879, the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)) Constabulary was established by personnel of the Hausa Constabulary of Southern Nigeria, to perform internal security and police duties in the British colony of the Gold Coast. In this guise, the regiment earned its first battle honour as part of the Ashanti campaign.[^cite_note-5]



[War Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_flag) of the [Ghana Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Regiment) during the [Burma Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign) in the [South-East Asian Theatre of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Asian_Theatre_of_World_War_II).

The Gold Coast Constabulary was renamed in 1901 as the Gold Coast Regiment, following the foundation of the [West African Frontier Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_West_African_Frontier_Force), under the direction of the [Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Office) of the [British Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Government). The regiment raised a total of five [battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) for service during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), all of which served during the East Africa campaign. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the regiment raised nine battalions, and saw action in Kenya's Northern Frontier District, Italian Somaliland, [Abyssinia](187749.md) and [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) as part of the [2nd (West Africa) Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_\(West_Africa\)_Infantry_Brigade).[^cite_note-Combat_Support_Arms-6] Gold Coast soldiers returning from the Far East carried different perspectives from when they had departed.

### Internal operations

See also: [History of Ghana (1966-1979)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ghana_\(1966-1979\))

The Ghana Armed Forces were formed in 1957. Major General [Stephen Otu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Otu) was appointed Chief of Defence Staff in September 1961. From 1966, the Armed Forces were extensively involved in politics, mounting several coups. [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah) had become Ghana's first [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister) when the country became independent in 1957. As Nkrumah's rule wore on, he began to take actions which disquieted the leadership of the armed forces, including the creation and expansion of the [President's Own Guard Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Own_Guard_Regiment) (POGR).[^cite_note-Ghana_Country_Study-7][^cite_note-8]

As a result, on February 24, 1966, a small number of Army personnel and senior police officials, led by Colonel [Emmanuel Kotoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Kwasi_Kotoka), commander of the Second Brigade at [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi), Major [Akwasi Afrifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwasi_Afrifa), (staff officer in charge of army training and operations), Lieutenant General (retired) [Joseph Ankrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Arthur_Ankrah), and [J.W.K. Harlley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.W.K._Harlley), (the police inspector general), successfully launched "Operation Cold Chop", the [1966 Ghanaian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Ghanaian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), against the Nkrumah regime.[^cite_note-Ghana_Country_Study-7] The group formed the [National Liberation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Council), which ruled Ghana from 1966 to 1969.

The Armed Forces seized power again in January 1972, after the reinstated civilian government cut military privileges and started changing the leadership of the army's combat units. Lieutenant Colonel [Ignatius Kutu Acheampong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_Kutu_Acheampong) (temporary commander of the First Brigade around Accra) led the bloodless [1972 Ghanaian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Ghanaian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) that ended the Second Republic.[^cite_note-9] Thus the [National Redemption Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Redemption_Council) was formed. Acheampong became head of state, and the NRC ruled from 1972 to 1975.

On October 9, 1975, the NRC was replaced by the [Supreme Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Military_Council,_Ghana) (SMC).[^cite_note-10] Council members were Colonel Acheampong, (chairman, who was also promoted straight from [Colonel](199023.md) to [General](148537.md)), Lt. Gen. [Fred Akuffo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Akuffo), (the [Chief of Defence Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff_\(Ghana\))), and the army, navy, air force and [Border Guard Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Guard_Unit) commanders.

In July 1978, in a sudden move, the other SMC officers forced Acheampong to resign, replacing him with Lt. Gen. Akuffo. The SMC apparently acted in response to continuing pressure to find a solution to the country's economic dilemma; inflation was estimated to be as high as 300% that year. The council was also motivated by Acheampong's failure to dampen rising political pressure for changes. Akuffo, the new SMC chairman, promised publicly to hand over political power to a new government to be elected by July 1, 1979.[^cite_note-nrc-11]

The decree lifting the ban on party politics went into effect on January 1, 1979, as planned. However, in June, just before the scheduled resumption of civilian rule, a group of young armed forces officers, led by [Flight Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_Lieutenant) [Jerry Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Rawlings), mounted the [1979 Ghanaian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Ghanaian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). They put in place the [Armed Forces Revolutionary Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Revolutionary_Council,_Ghana), which governed until September 1979. However, in 1981, Rawlings deposed the new civilian government again, in the [1981 Ghanaian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Ghanaian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-12] This time Rawlings established the [Provisional National Defence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_National_Defence_Council) (PNDC). The PNDC remained in government until January 7, 1993. In the last years of the PNDC, Jerry Rawlings assumed civilian status; he was elected as a civilian President in 1993 and continued as president until 2001.

### External operations



[Ghana Army](9555564.md) [soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier) boarding a [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md) at [Accra International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Airport) to take part in an [Economic Community of West African States Monitoring Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States_Monitoring_Group) mission in [Liberia](17791.md).

The Armed Forces' first external operation was the [United Nations Operation in the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_the_Congo) in the early 1960s.[^cite_note-ONUC−Congo-13] The GAF operated in the [Balkans](4829.md), including with [UNMIK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNMIK).[^cite_note-14] Ghanaian operations within [Africa](5334607.md) included the [UNAMIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAMIR) deployment which became entangled in the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md). In his book *[Shake Hands with the Devil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shake_Hands_with_the_Devil_\(book\))*, [Canadian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces) commander [Romeo Dallaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Dallaire) gave the Ghanaian soldiers high praise for their work during that deployment. During the [Liberian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Liberian_Civil_War), Ghanaian activities helped pave the way for the [Accra Comprehensive Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Comprehensive_Peace_Agreement), among others.[^cite_note-ECOMOG−Liberia-15] Additional operations in Asia have included Iran and Iraq in the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War),[^cite_note-Iran–Iraq_War-16] [Kuwait](7515890.md) and [Lebanon civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) among others.[^cite_note-UNIFIL−Lebanon-17][^cite_note-Lebanese_Govt_to_erect_monument-18]

A total of 3,359 Ghana Army soldiers and 283 Ghana Military Police operated as part of UNTAC in [Cambodia](334751.md).[^cite_note-Cambodia−UNTAC-19] The UNTAC operation lasted two years, 1992−1993.[^cite_note-Cambodia−UNTAC-19] After the long running Cambodia civil war ignited by external interventions, a resolution was accepted by the four warring factional parties.[^cite_note-Cambodia−UNTAC-19] Operation UNTAC was the largest Ghanaian external operation since Ghana's first external military operation, [ONUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ONUC) in the Congo in the 1960s.[^cite_note-Cambodia−UNTAC-19] Operation [UNTAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNTAC) and its contingent UNAMIC had a combined budget of more than \$1.6 billion.[^cite_note-Cambodia−UNTAC-19]

In 2012, closer military cooperation was agreed with the [Armed Forces of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Russian_Federation). In 2013, the Armed Forces agreed closer military cooperation with the China [People's Liberation Army](66890.md),[^cite_note-China-Ghana_strengthen_military_ties-20] and with the [Armed Forces of the Islamic Republic of Iran](14305018.md).

## Ghana Army

The [Ghana Army](9555564.md) is structured as follows:



The [Ghana Armed Forces Central Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Armed_Forces_Central_Band)

- The Northern Command with headquarters in [Tamale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale,_Ghana), Central Command with headquarters in [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi) and the Southern Command with headquarters in [Accra](56824.md). In March 2000 Northern and Southern Commands were formed after the two infantry brigades were upgraded in status.

  [^cite_note-21]

  &#x20;Previously there were three brigades: 1st Infantry Brigade (HQ in Teshie), 2nd Infantry Brigade (HQ in Kumasi) and Support Services Brigade (HQ in Burma Camp).

- 6 [Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) [Battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) of the [Ghana Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Regiment). 3rd Battalion of Infantry, 4th Battalion of Infantry and 6th Battalion of Infantry in the Northern Command, 1st Battalion of Infantry, 2nd Battalion of Infantry and 5th Battalion of Infantry in the Southern Command.

- two Airborne [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_\(military_unit\)) attached to Northern Command; Airborne Force

- 64 Infantry Regiment, a presidential guard force (formerly known as [President's Own Guard Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Own_Guard_Regiment))

- 1 Training Battalion

- One Staff College

- Reconnaissance Armoured Regiment (two armoured reconnaissance squadrons)

- [Defence Signal Regiment (Ghana)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Signal_Regiment_\(Ghana\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- Two Engineer Regiments (48 Engineer Regiment and 49 Engineer Regiment)

- 66 Artillery Regiment

In 1996, the [Support Services Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Support_Services_Brigade) was reorganized and transferred from the Army to be responsible to the Armed Forces GHQ. From that point its units included [49 Engineer Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49_Engineer_Regiment?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Ghana Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Military_Police?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Defence Signal Regiment (Ghana)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Signal_Regiment_\(Ghana\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), FRO, Forces Pay Office, [37 Military Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_Military_Hospital), Defence Mechanical Transport Battalion (Def MT Bn), Base Ordnance Depot, Base Ammunition Depot, Base Supply Depot, Base Workshop, Armed Forces Printing Press (AFPP), Armed Forces Fire Service (AFFS), the [Ghana Armed Forces Central Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Armed_Forces_Central_Band), Ghana Armed Forces Institution (GAFI), 1 Forces Movement Unit ([Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema) Port), 5 Forces Movement Unit, Base Engineer Technical Services (BETS), 5 Garrison Education Centre (5 GEC), the Armed Forces Museum, Army Signals Training School, and the Armed Forces Secondary Technical School (AFSTS). By 2016 the Forces Pay Office had been upgraded to the Forces Pay Regiment.

The Armed Forces uses imported weaponry and locally manufactured secondary equipment. [M16 rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M16_rifle), [AK-47s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47), [Type 56 assault rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_56_assault_rifle), [ballistic vests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_vest) and [personal armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_armor) are standard issue, while much of the secondary equipment used by the Army and Air Force are manufactured internally by the [Defence Industries Holding Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Industries_Holding_Company?action=edit\&redlink=1) (DIHOC). External suppliers include Russia, Iran, and China.[^cite_note-22]

### Peacekeeping Operations



Peacekeepers from Ghana Army (GA) in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali), [Rwanda](25645.md) in a [Volkswagen Iltis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Iltis).

The Armed Forces are heavily committed to international [peacekeeping](84617.md) operations. Ghana prefers to send its troops to operations in Africa. However the [United Nations](31769.md) has used Ghanaian forces in countries as diverse as [Afghanistan](737.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Kosovo](17391.md), [Georgia](48768.md), [Nepal](171166.md), [Cambodia](334751.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md).[^cite_note-Ghana's_Regional_Security_Policy-23] Currently, Ghanaian armed forces are posted to United Nations peacekeeping missions in:[^cite_note-Ghana_Armed_Forces_−_Peace_operations-24]

- [MONUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUC) ([Democratic Republic of Congo](76762.md)) − 464

- [UNMIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNMIL) ([Liberia](17791.md)) − 852 (disestablished 2018)

- [UNAMSIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAMSIL) ([Sierra Leone](27308.md)) − 782

- [UNIFIL](787766.md) ([Lebanon](17771.md)) − 651

Ghana armed forces provided the first Force Commander of the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) Monitoring Group ([ECOMOG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECOMOG)), Lieutenant General [Arnold Quainoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Quainoo). Quainoo led the force from July 1990 to September 1990.[^cite_note-25]

Ghana Armed Forces peacekeepers have many roles: [patrolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrolling), as [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police), [electoral observers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_observers), [de-miners](2206580.md) ([bomb disposal units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_disposal) and [clearance divers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearance_diver)), [ceasefire](592591.md) monitors, [humanitarian aid](434679.md) workers, and even [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) or [frogmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogmen) against [insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgent).[^cite_note-26]

### Niger Coup

A group of opposition political parties and civil society organizations, comprising the [National Democratic Congress (NDC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Congress_\(Ghana\))[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] and [Ghana Union Movement (GUM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Union_Movement),[^cite_note-29] has jointly urged the Akufo-Addo administration to refrain from deploying the Ghana Armed Forces to restore the democratically elected president of [Niger](21373.md), [Mohamed Bazoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Bazoum), who was ousted from power by General [Abdourahamane Tiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdourahamane_Tiani).[^cite_note-30]

However, [Hon. Kennedy Ohene Agyapong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Agyapong), the Chairperson of the Interior and Defense Committee in Parliament, has expressed his endorsement of the nation's deployment of troops to Niger.[^cite_note-31]

## Ghana Air Force

Further information: [Ghana Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Air_Force)

The Ghana Air Force is headquartered in [Burma Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Camp) in [Accra](56824.md), and operates from bases in Accra (main transport base), [Tamale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale,_Ghana) (combat and training base)and [Sekondi-Takoradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekondi-Takoradi) (training base). The GHF [military doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_doctrine) and stated mission is to perform [counterinsurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency) operations within Ghana or externally and to provide [logistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) support to the Ghana Army.[^cite_note-Ghana_Gair_force-32]

## Ghana Navy

Further information: [Ghana Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Navy)

The Ghana Navy's mission is to provide defence of Ghana and its territorial waters, fishery protection, [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone), and internal security on [Lake Volta](1477772.md). It is also tasked with resupplying GA (Ghana Army) peacekeepers in Africa, fighting maritime criminal activities such as [Piracy](50715.md), disaster and humanitarian relief operations, and evacuation of Ghanaian citizens and other nationals from troubled spots.[^cite_note-history-33] In 1994 the Navy was re-organized into an Eastern command, with headquarters at [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema,_Ghana), and a Western command, with headquarters at [Sekondi-Takoradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekondi-Takoradi).[^cite_note-history-33]

### Military hospitals

The GAF has two hospitals, the [37 Military Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_Military_Hospital) in Accra and the [Kumasi Military Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi_Military_Hospital) in the north.[^cite_note-gog-35] The 37 Military Hospital has recently undergone expansion and its facilities include a twenty-four-hour [Emergency Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_department) (ED).[^cite_note-37_Military_Hospital_−_Hospital_description-36]

The GAF main military hospital has been organized into departments and divisions, which created structure within the establishment.[^cite_note-37_Military_Hospital_−_Hospital_description-36] The Divisions and Departments (the units) are developed and joined according to medical, [paramedical](75006.md) and administrative lines and each of these units has its own departmental head.[^cite_note-37_Military_Hospital_−_Hospital_description-36] The GAF military hospital is staffed by GAF [military personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_personnel) and also houses a [medical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_education) training facility.[^cite_note-37_Military_Hospital_−_Hospital_description-36] 37 Military Hospital is also accredited for post-graduate medical education teaching.[^cite_note-37_Military_Hospital_−_Hospital_description-36] [Vyacheslav Lebedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Mikhailovich_Lebedev), Chairman of the [Supreme Court of Russia](5915064.md), expressed gratitude following his emergency treatment at the hospital.[^cite_note-37]

## Cadets and schools

The Ghana Army operates a [Cadet Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet_Corps) for GAF [Cadets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet) whom go on to [Military Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_education_and_training) and [Recruit Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training) graduation from the GAF [Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_academy) for [Army Recruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_recruit) and [Seaman Recruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaman_recruit) prior to enlistment into the Army, Navy or Air Force.[^cite_note-38]

Training institutions include the [Ghana Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Military_Academy) and the Ghana Army-sponsored [Cadet Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet_Corps). Also located in Accra is the internationally funded [Kofi Annan International Peacekeeping Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan_International_Peacekeeping_Training_Centre), which is not part of the Armed Forces but provides a wide-ranging of peace operations training, including to GAF personnel.

### Ghana Armed Forces Command and Staff College

The Ghana Armed Forces Command and Staff College (GAFCSC) dates back to 1963. It was to provide training for Ghana Armed Forces (GAF) officers and affiliated officers from Africa, focusing on command and staff duties. Throughout its history, it has hosted and educated individuals from neighboring African states. It focuses on military and defense courses, culminating in the issuance of the Pass Staff College (PSC) certificate.[^cite_note-\:0-39]

The range of programs expanded, driven by the demands of the global environment. The Ghana Armed Forces have been engaged in peacekeeping operations since 1960. This meant broadening the array of courses provided by GAFCSC. Consequently, the college aimed to establish partnerships with the University of Ghana and GIMPA to offer diverse peacekeeping and other courses. With the attainment of Institutional Accreditation, the college is now prepared to conduct its own courses, while still maintaining its collaborative association with the University of Ghana and GIMPA.[^cite_note-\:0-39]

## Military clothing and prohibition of photography

Ghanaian [statutory law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_law) officially prohibits [civilians](145804.md) and foreign nationals from wearing military apparel such as [camouflage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camouflage) clothing, or clothing which resembles military dress. Officially, fines and/or short prison sentences can be passed against civilians seen in military dress in public.[^cite_note-Ghana_armed_forces-42] In addition, Ghanaian law prohibits the [photographing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography) of Ghana Armed Forces (GAF) Ghana [Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police) (GMP) police or GAF [military personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_personnel) and vehicles while on duty, strategic sites such as [Accra International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Airport) when in use, and the seat of the Ghanaian government, [Jubilee House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee_House).[^cite_note-Ghana_armed_forces-42]
