Parliamentary elections were held in the [Czech Republic](5321.md) on 3 and 4 October 2025,[^cite_note-4] to elect all 200 members of the [Chamber of Deputies](3991120.md), the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) of the [Czech Parliament](3991200.md). The incumbent centre-right government of [Petr Fiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Fiala) was defeated after one term, with the populist [ANO](40808475.md) led by former prime minister [Andrej Babiš](40378134.md) returning to power as the largest party in parliament. The parties that formed the previous government lost their majority but performed better than polling suggested, winning 92 seats after being projected to win only 62–70 seats. ANO also performed better than expected, while the far-right [Freedom and Direct Democracy](46867556.md) (SPD) performed worse than expected based on polling. The [left-conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-conservatism) [Stačilo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sta%C4%8Dilo!) failed to enter Parliament. Babiš went on to form a coalition government with SPD and another right-wing party, [Motorists for Themselves](76614519.md) (AUTO). He was appointed prime minister for a second time on 9 December.&#x20;

The main issues in the election were the economy, inflation, foreign policy and defence spending.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The 2025 election marked the first time that [mail-in voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_voting) was permitted for citizens living or stationed abroad.[^cite_note-9]&#x20;

The [2021 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Czech_parliamentary_election) saw the conservative alliance [Spolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolu) (consisting of the [Civic Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Democratic_Party) (ODS), [KDU-ČSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDU-%C4%8CSL) and [TOP 09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOP_09)) finish first with 27.8% of the vote. [ANO 2011](40808475.md) finished second and liberal alliance [Pirates and Mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_and_Mayors) third. [Freedom and Direct Democracy](46867556.md) was the only other party to win seats. [Spolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolu) formed a government with [Pirates and Mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_and_Mayors) with ODS leader Petr Fiala as Prime Minister.[^cite_note-12]&#x20;

Soon after the 2021 elections, the leader of the Mayors and Independents, Vít Rakušan, said that his party would run in the next elections as a single party rather than continue their alliance with the Pirate Party. According to internal Pirate Party analysis, the Mayors violated their joint agreement by asking their voters to give their candidates preference votes on the joint list, which resulted in just four Pirate MPs being elected.[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

### 2024 European Parliament election

Main article: [2024 European Parliament election in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_the_Czech_Republic)

The [2024 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_the_Czech_Republic) saw losses for the ruling parties, as the [Czech Pirate Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Pirate_Party) lost two of its three seats, and both the [Civic Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Democratic_Party) and [KDU-ČSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDU-%C4%8CSL) lost one [MEP](258909.md) each. [TOP 09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOP_09) retained both its mandates and [Mayors and Independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_and_Independents) gained one, resulting in a net loss of three seats for the ruling coalition.[^cite_note-sz-20240609-14]&#x20;

Among the parliamentary opposition, [ANO 2011](40808475.md) gained one mandate compared to the previous election, and [SPD](46867556.md) lost one, just passing the parliamentary threshold. Most of the gains went to [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) extra-parliamentary parties, with two MEPs each won by [Stačilo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sta%C4%8Dilo!) and the right-wing populist [Přísaha and Motorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99%C3%ADsaha_and_Motorists), whose leader [Filip Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Turek) had faced [Neo-Nazism](54361.md) allegations just days before the election. The election also saw the worst result ever for [Social Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democracy_\(Czech_Republic\)), which won just 1.8% of the vote, far below the threshold.[^cite_note-sz-20240609-14][^cite_note-15]&#x20;

### 2024 regional and Senate elections

Main articles: [2024 Czech Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Czech_Senate_election) and [2024 Czech regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Czech_regional_elections)

The [2024 Czech regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Czech_regional_elections) saw a near-total wipeout for the Pirate Party, the smallest party in the government coalition, which surpassed the threshold in just one out of 13 contested regions and lost all but four councillors nationwide. This led to the resignation of the party leadership and the party's departure from the [Fiala cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Petr_Fiala).[^cite_note-16] The election was also successful for the Stačilo! coalition, which entered 12 out of 13 regional assemblies, winning 40 councillors.[^cite_note-17]&#x20;

### Pirate Party departure from the Fiala Government

Following the underwhelming results in the regional elections, several regional branches of the Pirate Party called for the resignation of the national leadership, led by [Ivan Bartoš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Barto%C5%A1), who said that he would respect the results of an internal referendum on that matter; however, there were few calls to leave the Fiala Cabinet.[^cite_note-18]&#x20;

A few days later, Prime Minister Petr Fiala announced that he would propose the dismissal of Bartoš as Minister of Regional Development and Deputy Prime Minister for Digitalisation, due to Bartoš's failure to roll out the digitalisation of construction permits. Fiala asked the Pirate Party to propose a new candidate for Minister of Regional Development.[^cite_note-19] This decision came as a surprise to Bartoš and the Pirate Party leadership. Bartoš stated that he had a meeting with Fiala earlier that day, where his dismissal had not been mentioned, and that he learned about his dismissal through a telephone call. The rest of the Pirate Party leadership expressed anger at not having been consulted first, as required by the coalition agreement.[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

Fiala later confirmed that he had announced his decision to Bartoš through a call. He said he hoped the Pirates would remain in the government, and that he had not broken the coalition agreement, as the whole Pirate's leadership resigned and remained as caretaker leadership until new leadership elections could be held.[^cite_note-21] Pirate members then called for an internal referendum on leaving the government, which was supported by an overwhelming majority of members.[^cite_note-22] The remaining Pirate ministers subsequently submitted their resignations.[^cite_note-23] However, Fiala did not accept the resignation of Foreign Minister [Jan Lipavský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Lipavsk%C3%BD), who was asked to finish his term as an independent, as a joint nominee of all four remaining coalition parties.[^cite_note-24]&#x20;

### Bitcoin scandal

Main article: [2025 Czech government Bitcoin scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Czech_government_Bitcoin_scandal)

The 2025 Czech government Bitcoin scandal, which arose after it became public that the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Czech_Republic\)) had accepted a large [Bitcoin](28249265.md) donation from criminal [Tomáš Jiřikovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Ji%C5%99ikovsk%C3%BD\&action=edit\&redlink=1), became a topic of the election campaign.[^cite_note-Reuters-25][^cite_note-f745-26] The controversy led to the resignation of Justice Minister [Pavel Blažek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Bla%C5%BEek), who approved the donation without verifying its origin, sparking allegations of potential [money laundering](19390.md).[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

## Electoral system

The [Constitution of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Czech_Republic) stipulates that an election to the Chamber of Deputies, the lower house of the [Parliament](3991200.md), must be held every four years. The executive government is answerable to the Chamber of Deputies and remains in power only as long as it commands the confidence of the majority of its members. Article 19(1) of the Constitution states that any [citizen of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_nationality_law) over the age of 21 years old is eligible to serve as a Member of Parliament. All 200 deputies are [proportionally](23298.md) elected on [open lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) in 14 electoral regions, which follow the borders of the 13 [Czech regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Czech_Republic) and the capital city of [Prague](23844.md). Seats are distributed to the regions based on the number of valid votes in each region. Mandates are assigned using the [largest remainder method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_remainder_method), using the [Imperiali quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperiali_quota) in the first round and the [Hagenbach-Bischoff quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagenbach-Bischoff_quota) in the second round. In the first round, mandates are divided between each region. Seats not assigned in the first round are then transferred to a national second round, where the sum of parties' remaining votes from all regions are used. To be eligible for seats, a single party must earn at least 5% of the national vote, a coalition of two parties needs 8%, and a coalition of three or more parties requires 11%, unless only one group makes it into the Chamber.[^cite_note-28]&#x20;

### Postal voting

For first time in Czech history, citizens living abroad will be able to vote via mail-in voting, following an amendment to the electoral law in 2024.[^cite_note-29] Several media sources, including *[Seznam Zprávy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seznam_Zpr%C3%A1vy)* and [*e15*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E15_\(newspaper\)), noted that the number of citizens registered for postal voting was far lower than the original estimates from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]&#x20;

### Non-coalitions

Before the election, three candidate lists (the [Stačilo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sta%C4%8Dilo!) alliance; the joint list of the [Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Pirate_Party) and [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Czech_Republic\)); and the joint list of [SPD](46867556.md), [Tricolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricolour_\(political_party\)), [PRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law,_Respect,_Expertise) and [Svobodní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svobodn%C3%AD)) were accused of creating "non-coalitions", as they registered as single party lists rather than coalitions in order to pass a lower electoral threshold. This practice was criticised by some other parties,[^cite_note-32] political analysts from *[Seznam Zprávy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seznam_Zpr%C3%A1vy)*[^cite_note-33] and [*Reflex*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflex_\(magazine\)),[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] and academics from the [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University),[^cite_note-36] who suggested that these "non-coalitions" were circumventing the electoral law.&#x20;

## Campaign

### Pre-campaign period and cooperation agreements



Overview of a studio of [ČT24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CT24) Super-debate in the [National Museum in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_\(Prague\))

On 8 February 2023, Babiš announced he would limit his role within ANO. He would remain as an MP and the leader of the party, while [Karel Havlíček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Havl%C3%AD%C4%8Dek_\(politician\)) and [Alena Schillerová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alena_Schillerov%C3%A1) would become the primary faces of the party, with Havlíček becoming leader of the [shadow cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_cabinet).[^cite_note-Novinky_8_12_23-37][^cite_note-iDNES_8_2_23-38] Babiš described Havlíček as a future prime minister of the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-HN_090623-39]&#x20;

On 21 March 2025, [SPD](46867556.md), [Svobodní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svobodn%C3%AD), [Tricolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricolour_\(political_party\)) and [PRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law,_Respect,_Expertise) signed a memorandum agreeing to cooperate in the 2025 parliamentary election on the SPD candidate list.[^cite_note-40] In July, PRO threatened to leave the joint list, citing insufficient representation of its candidates on the electoral lists, and floated the idea of joining Stačilo! instead.[^cite_note-41] They later decided to remain on the SPD list.[^cite_note-42] It was subsequently confirmed that each of the smaller parties had only two candidates across all the regional lists (albeit all in the top three candidates in their respective regions), with the rest of the places taken by SPD candidates. Svobodní voiced their disappointment at this arrangement, claiming that "there was not enough time to put together fairer lists", but that "the basic goals are met".[^cite_note-43]&#x20;

[Stačilo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sta%C4%8Dilo!) announced its regional leaders in March 2025 at a press conference in [Ostrava](464078.md). The national leader was designated as MEP and [KSČM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia) leader [Kateřina Konečná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Kone%C4%8Dn%C3%A1), who stated that she would resign her seat in the [European Parliament](9581.md) if elected to the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies). In the [Central Bohemian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bohemian_Region), the list was led by former MEP [Jana Bobošíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jana_Bobo%C5%A1%C3%ADkov%C3%A1), in the [South Moravian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Moravian_Region) by blogger [Daniel Sterzik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Sterzik), in the [Pardubice Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardubice_Region) by Roman Roun, and in the [Liberec Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberec_Region) by former [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia) MP [Stanislav Mackovík](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Stanislav_Mackov%C3%ADk\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-44]&#x20;

On 23 June, the [Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Pirate_Party) and the [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Czech_Republic\)) announced that 30 Green candidates would be included on Pirate Party candidate lists in eight regions, with the Greens' co-leader [Gabriela Svárovská](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gabriela_Sv%C3%A1rovsk%C3%A1\&action=edit\&redlink=1) running in [Prague](23844.md). On 17 July, [Stačilo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sta%C4%8Dilo!) and [SOCDEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democracy_\(Czech_Republic\)) reached an agreement which would see the Social Democrats endorsing the coalition's manifesto and their candidates running on the Stačilo! list. [Jana Maláčová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jana_Mal%C3%A1%C4%8Dov%C3%A1) led the list in [Prague](23844.md), Jiří Nedvěd in the [Karlovy Vary Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovy_Vary_Region), and [Lubomír Zaorálek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubom%C3%ADr_Zaor%C3%A1lek) took the second spot on the list in the [Moravian-Silesian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian-Silesian_Region). Under the agreement, SOCDEM would be represented by two candidates on each regional list.[^cite_note-idnes1772025-45] This decision triggered a backlash from SOCDEM figures and members, with a substantial drop in membership reportedly linked to the agreement.[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

### Lawsuits against non-coalitions

[Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Czechia) argued that SPD and Stačilo!'s non-coalitions circumvented the higher threshold in place for coalitions, and prepared to file a lawsuit.[^cite_note-47] They later confirmed that they would bring 28 lawsuits in total, challenging each regional list separately. Volt co-leader [Adam Hanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adam_Hanka\&action=edit\&redlink=1) also stated that the party was prepared to bring the case to the [Czech Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic).[^cite_note-48] By 27 August 3 regional courts had thrown out the case as a final ruling, saying that the 'non-coalitions' were legitimate.[^cite_note-49] In response, Přísaha filed a similar lawsuit against the Pirates' candidate list in the [Ústí nad Labem Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ast%C3%AD_nad_Labem_Region) on 20 August,[^cite_note-50] related to the inclusion of Green Party members.[^cite_note-51]&#x20;

The last lawsuit was resolved on 3 September, when the [Brno Regional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brno_Regional_Court\&action=edit\&redlink=1) ruled that Stačilo's "circumvention of law is clearly visible and has reached heaven-appealing levels" and that "all requirements needed for a denial of registration were met". However, it chose to register the party list, saying that "courts should very seldomly influence the electoral process" and stated that lawmakers should clarify the law.[^cite_note-52] Volt said they would appeal the case to the Constitutional Court, which will rule on the case after the election, during the result certification process.[^cite_note-53] In the end, all lawsuits were rejected, and all originally submitted lists were approved. The unprecedentedly high number of lawsuits led to a delayed start of printing of the voting lists, which was originally scheduled to start on 22 August, but only started on 3 September, the latest feasible date.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]&#x20;

### Possible coalitions

In August, all parties of the original Fiala cabinet (SPOLU, STAN and the Pirates) declared that they were not willing to work with ANO in any future government, and also rejected cooperation with Stačilo! or SPD.[^cite_note-\:0-56][^cite_note-57] Commentators said that it would be possible to re-form the SPOLU-STAN-Pirates government if the parties won enough seats to do so. They also noted that the Pirates would have a bigger influence in this scenario, as they would have won more seats by passing the electoral threshold running as a single party.[^cite_note-58] By the same token, the two parliamentary opposition parties (ANO+SPD) ruled out working with any of the parties in the existing government, while ANO's leader was seen as the most likely Prime Minister after the elections.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]&#x20;

Commentators suggested that any ANO-led government would be hard to form and maintain, as Babiš would be forced to cooperate with parties on either the far-right or the far-left. In the case of a strong result for both Stačilo! and SPD, Babiš would be forced to form a government consisting of eight parties, the most in Czech history (surpassing the record set by the outgoing government, which originally consisted of five parties).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] [Czech Radio](3100526.md) reported that Babiš was fearful of "lost votes", that would go to parties finishing below the electoral threshold. In 2021, there was over a million such votes, over a fifth of the total votes cast.[^cite_note-63] It was reported that Babiš had pushed for electoral cooperation with Stačilo! and Social Democracy, and a recreation of the [Přísaha and Motorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99%C3%ADsaha_and_Motorists) coalition. A one-party government was seen as the preferred option for ANO after the election, but it was considered unlikely that the party would win a majority on its own.[^cite_note-\:1-64] The Motorists were perceived as the most preferred coalition partner for Babiš,[^cite_note-\:1-64] although according to opinion polls it was uncertain whether they would pass the 5% threshold. It was also suggested that the most likely outcome of the election would be a single-party ANO minority government, supported by either SPD, Stačilo!, or both.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-\:0-56][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Some contributors for *[Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs)* and *[Seznam Zprávy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seznam_Zpr%C3%A1vy)* also warned of the possibility of far-right parties being included in the next government.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]&#x20;

### President Pavel's opposition to anti-NATO/EU ministers

In May, President [Petr Pavel](42272533.md) said that he may refuse to appoint ministers who support the Czech Republic's withdrawal from [NATO](21133.md) or the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] In June, he repeated this position, stating that if Stačilo! were in a position to set the country's foreign policy, he would consider this a "direct threat for this country", and that he would not appoint Stačilo! nominees to any ministry with responsibility for "security or foreign policy". He also confirmed that the same would apply to any SPD candidates.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]&#x20;

### September assault on Andrej Babiš

On 1 September, at a pre-election rally in the village of [Dobrá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobr%C3%A1_\(Fr%C3%BDdek-M%C3%ADstek_District\)),[^cite_note-75] an elderly man struck Andrej Babiš on the head and the back with a [crutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crutch). The police apprehended the perpetrator at the scene,[^cite_note-crutch_02-76] and investigated the attack as an act of hooliganism.[^cite_note-77] Babiš was taken to hospital,[^cite_note-78] but was discharged and stated on social media that he would likely be fine.[^cite_note-79] In another statement on social media the day after the attack, Babiš said he was awaiting further medical examinations.[^cite_note-80] Following the incident, Babiš cancelled the following day's election rallies.[^cite_note-crutch_02-76] Babiš's electoral opponents condemned the attack, including SPOLU leader and PM Fiala.[^cite_note-81]&#x20;

### Campaign incidents

In August 2025, media outlets published an audio recording from 2023 in which [Margita Balaštíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Margita_Bala%C5%A1t%C3%ADkov%C3%A1\&action=edit\&redlink=1), a member of parliament and candidate for ANO, expressed intent to hire someone to kill her ex-husband's new partner's dog and damage her ex-husband's business.[^cite_note-dog_1-82] Balaštíková was removed from the list of candidates.[^cite_note-dog_2-83] Pavel Jajtner, a physician from [Znojmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Znojmo), was a candidate ranked 13th on SPOLU's candidate list. Investigative journalists discovered that he had failed to provide adequate care for newborns during a night shift at [Znojmo Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Znojmo_Hospital\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in January 2022.[^cite_note-84] A confidentiality agreement was reached among regional representatives and family members. Marek Výborný, the leader of KDU-ČSL, for which Jajtner was running, stated that he would investigate the matter on the same day. The following day, Jajtner withdrew from the candidate list.[^cite_note-85]&#x20;

Nemocnice Znojmo was also the subject of another incident. ODS politician Veronika Kachlíková, with the backing of senior ODS figures (including [Pavel Blažek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Bla%C5%BEek) and Karel Podzimek), acquired a former hospital [dormitory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormitory) in Znojmo that was sold for 38 million CZK, significantly surpassing its estimated value of 15 million CZK. Soon after the sale, local politicians changed the zoning regulations, allowing Kachlíková's company to convert the building into residential apartments, which have since been sold for around 150 million CZK.[^cite_note-86] The acquisition process raised questions about political favoritism and potential [conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest), especially concerning the involvement of regional ODS leaders who lobbied for the project. Kachlíková denied any wrongdoing, distancing herself from political discussions surrounding the sale and subsequent zoning changes. Karel Podzimek resigned from the candidate list for the SPOLU coalition in the South Moravian region after the allegations emerged of his involvement in the redevelopment.[^cite_note-87]&#x20;

### Public concerns about electoral fraud

In May 2025, the media reported that the Czech public were concerned about [electoral fraud](382165.md) on the part of Petr Fiala's cabinet, according to the STEM agency.[^cite_note-88] In September 2025, another survey found that four out of ten Czechs feared election fraud. According to an analyst at NMS, the topic of stolen elections has appeared in the public sphere.[^cite_note-89]&#x20;

## Parties

28 lists were submitted to take part in the elections before the 29 July deadline,[^cite_note-108] of which one was a coalition list (SPOLU) and 27 were party lists (including unofficial joint electoral lists).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] The respective regional courts had time to register or reject these lists until 15 August.[^cite_note-111] 25 party lists and one coalition list were registered at the end of this process, as the [Democratic Party of Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Greens) and 'Yes, Better Czechia with Aliens' both withdrew their candidate lists.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]&#x20;

*Deník N* noted that the number of candidates was the lowest since 1998, due to the numerous electoral alliances. The share of women on the lists was 31.3%, a slight drop from 2021. Pirates had the largest number of female candidates, while Motorists for Themselves had the least. The average age of candidates was 49.2 years, the highest in the history of the Czech Republic. The youth-oriented Generation Movement had the youngest list, while the Right Bloc's candidates had the highest average age.[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

## Aftermath

After the results were released, incumbent Prime Minister [Petr Fiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Fiala) conceded the election and congratulated [Andrej Babiš](40378134.md) on [ANO 2011](40808475.md)'s election victory, acknowledging that it was not possible to form a government of [Spolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolu), [STAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_and_Independents), and the [Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Pirate_Party). ANO leader [Andrej Babiš](40378134.md) declared victory and repeated that he would not enter a coalition with the current government parties (SPOLU and STAN) or with the Pirates, while those three parties also ruled out coalitions with ANO. He stated his intention to try to form a minority government, with ANO ruling alone, and said he would seek to negotiate with [Freedom and Direct Democracy](46867556.md) (SPD) and the [Motorists](76614519.md) to agree to a [confidence and supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) deal for his government.[^cite_note-139] On 6 October 2025, Máláčová announced that the SOCDEM leadership would resign at an extraordinary congress.[^cite_note-140]&#x20;

On 7 October 2025, Fiala accepted responsibility for the electoral result and announced that he would not seek reelection as leader of ODS. He said he would not be leaving politics altogether, and would serve his term as a [backbench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backbencher) MP. First deputy leader of ODS [Zbyněk Stanjura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zbyn%C4%9Bk_Stanjura) also stated he would not run for reelection in the future, after losing his mandate due to preferential votes.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]&#x20;

### Government formation

On 5 October, the day after the election, Babiš said ANO would lead talks with SPD and AUTO to seek a single-party government.[^cite_note-\:2-143] All other parties (SPOLU, STAN, and Pirates) had ruled out working with ANO.[^cite_note-\:2-143][^cite_note-144] President [Petr Pavel](42272533.md) held meetings with the leaders of all parties that made it into parliament in the days following the election. On 6 October, Pavel said it was "clear" that there was interest in forming a coalition government composed of ANO, SPD, and AUTO. ANO's initial goal to form a single-party minority cabinet had become "difficult to attain", as SPD and AUTO both said they wanted to participate directly in the government.[^cite_note-145] It was reported that both smaller parties wanted at least two ministries each; SPD were interested in the [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education,_Youth_and_Sports_\(Czech_Republic\)), [defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Czech_Republic\)), [foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Czech_Republic\)), [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Industry_and_Trade_\(Czech_Republic\)), and [interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Czech_Republic\)) ministries, and proposed nominating non-party experts to lead them in order to avoid internal party frictions. AUTO said they wished to nominate leader [Petr Macinka](79962349.md) as [environment minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Environment_\(Czech_Republic\)), [Filip Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Turek) as foreign affairs minister, and [Oto Klempíř](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oto_Klemp%C3%AD%C5%99) as [culture minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Czech_Republic\)).[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] It was also reported that SPD wanted to nominate their leader [Tomio Okamura](37423659.md) for the post of [President of the Chamber of Deputies](17891992.md), which was not ruled out by either Babiš or Okamura.[^cite_note-149]&#x20;

Over 470 artists, academics and representatives of the cultural community signed an open letter to [Andrej Babiš](40378134.md) and President [Petr Pavel](42272533.md) opposing an AUTO nominee for the post of Minister of Culture.[^cite_note-150] The association of Czech non-profit environmental organizations [Zelený kruh](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Zelen%C3%BD_kruh\&action=edit\&redlink=1), expressed opposition to an AUTO nominee for the post of Environment Minister.[^cite_note-151] On 19 October 2025, thousands of people attended a demonstration against Macinka's nomination on Prague's [Hradčany Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrad%C4%8Dany_Square), organized by non-profit environmental organizations [Greenpeace](12233.md), Re-set and [Fridays for Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridays_for_Future).[^cite_note-152]&#x20;

On 27 October 2025, Pavel asked Babiš to form a government.[^cite_note-153] The leaders of ANO, SPD and AUTO signed the coalition agreement on 3 November. The new Chamber of Deputies had its constituent meeting the same day.[^cite_note-154] ANO MPs [Richard Brabec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Brabec), Petr Kubis and Roman Zarzycký resigned in order to focus on regional politics.[^cite_note-155] They were replaced by [Jana Demjanová](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jana_Demjanov%C3%A1\&action=edit\&redlink=1), [Jiří Penc](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Penc\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Vlastimil Hebr](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Vlastimil_Hebr\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-156]&#x20;

Babiš met Pavel on 26 November to present his cabinet and proposed government ministers.[^cite_note-157] Pavel stated that he would appoint Babiš as the new prime minister within a week after he publicly addressed his business interests, and expressed his ongoing disagreement with potential minister Filip Turek, the candidate for Minister of Environment.[^cite_note-candidates-158] AUTO leader Macinka stated that he saw no legal reasons why Turek could not be appointed minister.[^cite_note-159]&#x20;

### Subsequent political developments

Pavel started meeting the ministerial nominees on 28 November 2025, when he met [Robert Plaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Plaga), [Adam Vojtěch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Vojt%C4%9Bch) and [Jeroným Tejc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeron%C3%BDm_Tejc).[^cite_note-160] On 1 December 2025, he met [Karel Havlíček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Havl%C3%AD%C4%8Dek_\(politician\)), [Alena Schillerová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alena_Schillerov%C3%A1) and [Aleš Juchelka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ale%C5%A1_Juchelka).[^cite_note-161] A day later, Pavel met [Lubomír Metnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubom%C3%ADr_Metnar), [Zuzana Schwarz Bařtipánová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuzana_Schwarz_Ba%C5%99tip%C3%A1nov%C3%A1) and [Ivan Bednárik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Bedn%C3%A1rik).[^cite_note-162] On 4 December 2025, Pavel met [Jaromír Zůna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarom%C3%ADr_Z%C5%AFna), [Martin Šebestyán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_%C5%A0ebesty%C3%A1n) and [Petr Macinka](79962349.md).[^cite_note-163] The meetings concluded on 8 December when Pavel met [Oto Klempíř](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oto_Klemp%C3%AD%C5%99) and [Boris Šťastný](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_%C5%A0%C5%A5astn%C3%BD).[^cite_note-164] Environment minister candidate [Filip Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Turek) cancelled a planned meeting with Pavel that day, after he was hospitalized with a [herniated disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_herniation).[^cite_note-165]&#x20;

Babiš announced on Facebook on 5 December that he would shift his holding company [Agrofert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrofert) to an independently governed trust structure, and in response Pavel agreed to appoint him as Prime Minister,[^cite_note-166] which he did on 9 December at [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle).[^cite_note-167] Babiš submitted an updated proposed cabinet to Pavel, from which Filip Turek was omitted, citing health reasons. Macinka will become the interim head of the environment ministry.[^cite_note-168] Hundreds of students in Prague and several other Czech cities attended a demonstration against the prospect of an AUTO nominee leading the Ministry of Environment.[^cite_note-169]&#x20;

Babiš' cabinet was appointed by Pavel on 15 December 2025,[^cite_note-170] with a vote of confidence scheduled in the Chamber of Deputies for 13 January 2026.[^cite_note-171] In the morning of 15 December, several Greenpeace members displayed a large banner on the Ministry of the Environment building in protest against the AUTO representatives leading the ministry. Several hundred students attended a demonstration on Prague's Hradčany Square, Brno's Malinovsky Square and Ostrava's Prokeš Square for the same reasons, organized by Fridays for Future.[^cite_note-172]&#x20;

On 17 December 2025, Turek asked Pavel for a new meeting date,[^cite_note-173] and the two men finally met on 22 December 2025.[^cite_note-174] Turek tried to explain [his affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Turek#Controversies) to Pavel, but was unable to convince Pavel to withdraw his objections to Turek's appointment. Pavel said he expected PM Babiš not to propose Turek for the post, and said he may reject Turek's nomination if it is proposed.[^cite_note-175] Macinka stated that the Motorists' position on Turek's nomination remained the same, and they will ask Babiš to propose Turek as environment minister.[^cite_note-176]&#x20;

On 7 January 2026, Pavel hosted Babiš at a traditional New Year's lunch at Prague Castle. Babiš again submitted the nomination of Turek for environment minister, but Pavel refused to appoint him. The Motorists insisted on Turek's nomination and criticised what it described as "the president's approach to his constitutional duties."[^cite_note-177] In a letter to Babiš, Pavel said he must protect fundamental constitutional values, including the rejection of totalitarian ideologies, arguing that Turek's past statements and conduct cast doubt on his loyalty to those values, such as remarks that praised or downplayed [Nazi Germany](21212.md), and that Turek had repeatedly shown a lack of respect for the Czech legal order.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-sue-179] Turek said Pavel's reasoning had deeply offended him and he would file a lawsuit for protection of personality right.[^cite_note-sue-179] On 12 January 2026, Turek was appointed government commissioner for climate policy as a temporary measure,[^cite_note-180] though the opposition argued that this was unconstitutional.[^cite_note-181]&#x20;

Babiš' cabinet was presented to the Chamber of Deputies on 13 January 2026 for a [confidence vote](208101.md). The vote did not occur on that day as 60 speakers were waiting in the pre-vote debate.[^cite_note-182] The confidence debate session continued in the morning of 14 January 2026.[^cite_note-183] On 15 January 2026, after 26 hours of debate, the cabinet won the confidence vote by 108–91, with 199 MPs present.[^cite_note-184]&#x20;

On 16 January 2026, MPs from the governing coalition urged Pavel to appoint ministers proposed by the prime minister, in reference to Turek. Pavel acknowledged the resolution.[^cite_note-185] Babiš stated that he would not submit the nomination of Turek for environment minister again, because Pavel would not appoint him anyway.[^cite_note-186]&#x20;

On 27 January 2026, Pavel announced at a press conference that Foreign Minister Petr Macinka had sent two text messages to Pavel's adviser Petr Kolář, demanding that Pavel appoint Turek as the Minister of Environment, or else he would "burn bridges in a way that will go down in political science textbooks..." and "the consequences will come as a great surprise." Pavel denounced the messages as [blackmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackmail), shared them on social media, and submitted a report to the security services. Macinka said he did not consider his text messages to be blackmail. PM Babiš said that Macinka's words addressed to Pavel's adviser were "unfortunate" but argued that as a private communication with an adviser, they "certainly cannot be considered blackmail."[^cite_note-187] Several lawyers approached by journalists described the text messages as "disgusting" and "arrogant", but said they did not consider them to be blackmail.[^cite_note-188] Opposition leaders called for Macinka to resign or be dismissed by Babiš.[^cite_note-189] On 4 February 2026, the coalition government survived a motion of no confidence in the Chamber of Deputies, filed by opposition parties over the Pavel-Macinka dispute. The cabinet won the no-confidence vote by 99–84, with 183 MPs present.[^cite_note-190]&#x20;

The Presidential Office announced that Pavel was ready to appoint an environment minister, and was expecting Babiš to propose a new candidate. Rejected candidate Turek told reporters that the Motorists would not propose another candidate for the ministry,[^cite_note-191] but the party subsequently nominated MP [Igor Červený](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Igor_%C4%8Cerven%C3%BD\&action=edit\&redlink=1), who was appointed by Pavel on 23 February 2026.[^cite_note-192]&#x20;

On 30 April 2026, the Czech police concluded their investigation of Macinka's text messages.[^cite_note-193] The Presidential Office announced that they would not file a complaint against the concluded investigation.[^cite_note-194]&#x20;
