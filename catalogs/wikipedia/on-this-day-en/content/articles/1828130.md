The **Chin peoples** ([Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language): ချင်းလူမျိုး, [MLCTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLC_Transcription_System): *hkyang: lu. myui:*, pronounced [\[tɕɪ́ɰ̃ lù mjó\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Burmese)) are a collection of [Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman) ethnic groups native to western [Myanmar](19457.md) (Burma). The Chin primarily live in the mountains of [Chin State](493366.md) but are also found in parts of [Sagaing Region](493361.md), [Magway Region](493360.md), and [Lower Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Myanmar). Significant Chin [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) communities are also present in [northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India), the [United States](3434750.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), and [Australia](4689264.md).

The Chin are divided into [numerous tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity_in_Myanmar#Chin) and speak a variety of [Kuki-Chin languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Chin_languages) that are closely related but often [mutually unintelligible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility). The Chin identity, as a [pan-ethnic identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panethnicity), is a modern construction, shaped by [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma) and post-independence ethnic politics, built upon older tribal and regional identities.

## Ethnonyms

Chin (ချင်း, [MLCTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLC_Transcription_System): khyang:) is a pseudo-[exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym), a [Burmese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language) adaptation of the [Asho Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C3%B6_language) word *khlong* or *khlaung*, which means "man" or "person". Burmese speakers approximated the Asho Chin word and began to apply the exonym to all nearby groups residing in the [Arakan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Mountains) and [Chin Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_Hills).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenneth_Van_Bik2021350-11] The Burmese term first appeared in [stone inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) dating to the reign of King [Kyansittha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyansittha) in the 11th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChin_Human_Rights_Organization-12]

The term "Chin" is not universally accepted by all groups living in Chin State nor by all Kuki-Chin-speaking groups. Groups in the north tend to prefer the term "Zo",[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenneth_Van_Bik2021350-11] while related ethnolinguistic groups in India prefer the terms "[Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi)" or "[Mizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_people)".[^cite_note-\:7-13]

During the [era of British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma), the colonial government used the compound term "Chin-Kuki-Mizo" to group the [Kuki-Chin language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Chin_language) speaking peoples, and the [Indian government](553883.md) inherited this [nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenclature).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The Burmese government classifies the Chin as a "national race" composed of [53 sub-groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_Myanmar#Chin) that predominantly live in Chin State.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrisis_Group2025-16]

## Subgroups

The Chin peoples are divided into five main tribes, namely the [Asho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asho_Chin_people), [Daai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daai_Chin), [Khami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mro-Khimi_people) (Mro), [Laimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_people), and [Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi_people),[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPau_Sian_Lian2023118-18] which can be further distinguished by at least 60 different sub-tribal categories.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaokip2018103-19] According to anthropologist [F. K. Lehman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._K._Lehman), the Chin people can be divided into two broad categories under geography and culture: the Northern Chin[^cite_note-20] and Southern Chin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202015-21] The division between the Northern and Southern Chin is demarcated at 21°45'N.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202016-22]

The Northern Chin can be divided into three tracts: Tedim, Falam and Hakha. The Tedim tract consists of the [Sukte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukte_people), Kamhau and [Sizang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizang_people). These can be further subdivided into subtribes such as the Dim, Khuano, Hualngo, Sizang, [Tedim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim_people), Saizang, [Thadou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thadou_people), Teizang, Vangte, [Guite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guite_people), [Vaiphei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaiphei_people), and [Zou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zou_people), most of whom collectively identify as [Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi_people).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202015-21]

The Falam tract is the most thickly populated, with the tribes: Sunthla, Zahau, Hualngo, Khualsim, Tawyan, Zanniat, [Ngawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngawn_people), Laizo, and Khuangli, who are known as *Pawi* or *Sunthla*. Historically, the British recorded them as "[Tashon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashons_people)", an [anglicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_change) form of Taisun or Klashun, a village located west of [Falam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falam,_Myanmar).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202015-21]

In the Hakha tract, the main tribes include the Hakha, Thantlang, Zokhua, Thetta, and Kapi. They call themselves [*Laimi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_people) and share similarities with the [Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_people).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202016-22] Historically, the Northern Chin were [headhunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headhunting).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETurner2014-23]

The Southern Chin largely consist of the plains or valley Chin. The Southern Chin are composed of the Chinme, Welaung, Chinbok, Yindu, Chinbon, [Letu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letu_Chin), [Khyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyang_people), Sho, [Asho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asho_Chin_people), Cho, [Khami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mro-Khimi_people), [Mro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mru_people) and some Burmese tribes such as the Anu, Kun, Pallaing, and [Sak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sak_people). Their cultural practices differ, such as [cremation](175142.md) of the dead and [face tattoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_tattoo).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202016-22]

## Geographic distribution

### In Myanmar



Chin community in Chin State, Myanmar

The Chin peoples predominantly live in the western part of Myanmar in [Chin State](493366.md) and [Rakhine State](493373.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichaudSwainBarkataki-Ruscheweyh201693-24][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYinElias201254-25] Some Chin also inhabit [Sagaing](493361.md), [Magway](493360.md), [Bago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bago_Region), and [Ayeyarwady Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayeyarwady_Region).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200317-26] Chin settlements are usually above 1,000 metres (3,300 ft) in elevation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichaudSwainBarkataki-Ruscheweyh201693-24] Major cities such as [Yangon](57404.md) and [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay) are also home to sizable Chin communities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenneth_Van_Bik2021350-11]

### In India

An estimated 60,000–100,000 Chin [refugees](45547.md) live in India, primarily in [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram). Due to restrictions on [freedom of movement](1270497.md) imposed by the [State Peace and Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council) (SPDC), many fled Myanmar without documents to seek refuge across the border.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChin_Human_Rights_Organization201214-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilchZo_Tum_Hmung201115-28] Since the [2021 Myanmar coup d'état](66567997.md) and resulting [civil war](69807451.md), around forty thousand Chin people have fled to India.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaikia2026-29] Chin people in India live without legal status, making them vulnerable to arrest, detention, and exploitation. They face challenges accessing livelihoods, healthcare, and shelter, often living on the margins of society.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhai20244-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilchZo_Tum_Hmung201181–82-31] Mizoram has facilitated the integration of Chin refugees into local communities, including enrolling children in schools.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhai20243-32]

### Diaspora

Due to [political instability](13299430.md) and [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty) in Myanmar, many Chin have migrated to [Bangladesh](3454.md), India, [Thailand](30128.md), Malaysia, [Canada](5042916.md), Australia, and the United States as refugees.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERogers2012123-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFishbein2023-34] As of August 2024, 27,250 Chin people were registered with [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR) in Malaysia.[^cite_note-unhcrr-7] [Indiana](21883857.md) has the largest Chin population in the United States, with around 32,000 in the [Indianapolis](57707.md) area alone.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWRTV2025-35] [Tulsa, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa,_Oklahoma) is home to approx. 7,000–9,000 [Zomi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi_people), and is referred to as "[Zomi Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi_Town,_Tulsa)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrishna2022-36][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEaton2016-37]

Chin people who flee from Myanmar usually seek refuge in Thailand, Malaysia, or India before eventually resettling in the United States. Leaving Myanmar is often expensive, dangerous, and illegal. Refugees with limited financial means flee by boat, car, or on foot, whereas those with greater resources may travel by air.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraceffo2010-38] [Human smugglers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_smugglers), or [brokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broker), typically charge around [US\$](18717338.md)1,000 per person to cross borders. Refugees caught by either Myanmar or foreign authorities may face imprisonment and mistreatment, including [physical abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_abuse).[^cite_note-youo-39][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERogers2012123–125-40]

In [refugee camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_camps), particularly those in Thailand, families are advised that having children could improve their prospects for resettlement in the United States. Consequently, many young Chin parents arrive in the U.S. needing immediate employment in order to support their families.[^cite_note-ethnomed12-41][^cite_note-youo-39]

## History

### Origins

Main article: [Chinlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinlung)

The Chin maintain an origin known as [Chinlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinlung).[^cite_note-42] Due to the nature of oral history, it is difficult to locate Chinlung, and several theories have been proposed. Zawla, a [Mizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_people) historian, argues that the Chin departed from Chinlung during the reign of [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang) (modern-day China). Historians Sing Khaw Khai and Chawn Kio both argue that the Chin are descendants of the historical Tibeto-Burman [Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_\(historical_people\)) people. Based on this account, the Chin originated in the [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu)-[Tibet](31516.md) frontier.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong20038-43]

The [Tibeto-Burman peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages) are said to have migrated in three waves: first with the Chin-Kachin-Naga group, followed by the [Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamar_people) and [Lolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolo_people) peoples.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong20039-44] However, anthropologists like [Edmund Leach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Leach) challenge the link between Tibeto-Burman groups and [ancient China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_China). They argue that the people did not originate from cultural units known today and cannot be identified with any particular groups of today. [F. K. Lehman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._K._Lehman) contends that the ancestors of the Chin and Burmans were already distinct before migrating into Burma.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200310-45] He states that the Chin originated from western China and eastern Tibet towards the [Hukong Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukawng_Valley), separate from that of Burmans.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200311-46]

### Chindwin settlement

Burma scholar [Gordon Luce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Luce) suggested the Chin first settled in the Chindwin Valley[^cite_note-47] around the mid-eighth century. However, he considered an earlier date, possibly as early as the fourth century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200311–12-48] The Chindwin Valley was uninhabited until the Chin arrived. While the Burmans fought groups such as the [Thet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chak_people), [Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_people), and Pyu, they did not fight the Chin. Luce states that [Pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_kingdom) Burmans referred to the Chin as "friends". Luce postulates that the Chin did not join the enemies of the Burmans due to hostile relations. The migration of the Chin through the Hukong Valley split Thet tribes, leaving some in western [Manipur](422642.md). Burmans referred to the Chin as *khyan* and are inferred to have agreed to their occupation of the upper Chindwin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200312-49]

### Kale–Kabaw Valley

The Chin would eventually migrate from the eastern bank of the Chindwin into the [Kale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalay) valley, around the late 13th century or the early 14th century. Evidence from Pagan-era records places the Chin between the eastern bank of the upper [Chindwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chindwin_River) and west of the [Irrawaddy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_River). Zophei and [Laimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_people) folklore suggests [floods](50482.md) drove them from the low valley into the mountains across the river, while scholars argue that the Chin moved after losing their alliance with [Pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_kingdom).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200313-50]

After the [fall of Pagan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_kingdom#Fall), the Chin people lived peacefully in the [Kale–Kabaw Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kale%E2%80%93Kabaw_Valley) for about a century and established their capital at [Khampat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khampat). In 1395, the [Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people) founded Kale town on the border of the Chin Hills, bringing the Kale Valley under Shan control until British annexation. The rise of the [Shan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_States) weakened Chin influence in Upper Chindwin and central Burma. Zomi tribes note that a prince from the lowlands ruled Kale, during which the Chin people were forced to build [fortifications](204118.md) and suffered severe oppression under Shan rule, while historians Sing Khaw Khai and Lal Thang Lian argue that the Chin and Shan peoples coexisted for a period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200315-51]

### Movement into the Chin Hills

After 1395, Chin people began leaving [Kale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalay) and established new settlements in the [Chin Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_Hills), including the present township of [Tedim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim). The first settlement in the Chin Hills was named [Ciimnuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciimnuai).[^cite_note-52] Chin people lived in Ciimnuai until splitting into different tribal groups due to political infighting, lack of land, and economic issues.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200316-53] The [Asho people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asho_people) split away early and scattered around the [Irrawaddy Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_Delta), eventually going as far as the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) in Bangladesh, where they are known as "[Khyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyang_people)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200317-26] One group split off to make a settlement in present-day [Falam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falam,_Myanmar) and formed the [Laimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laimi) tribe. Tribes that moved north of Ciimnuai became known as [Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi). Historians posit that the Chins reached the northernmost extent of their settlement in present-day Manipur around 1554. Prior to Ciimnuai, there was no historical evidence of tribal differentiation between the [Zo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zo_people).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200316-53]

The true reason why the Chins left Khampat and the Chindwin Valley is unknown. Historian [D.G.E. Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.G.E._Hall) asserts that the Shans drove out the Chins from the valley into the Chin Hills. Lal Thang Lian, a Mizo historian, presents the oral story of the [banyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyan) sapling at the [altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar) of the *khua-hrum*. According to the legend, the Chins planted the sapling before they were forced to abandon Khampat and pledged to return when the branches of the sapling grew to reach the ground.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200316-53]

### Rise of the Sukte

See also: [Chin chieftainship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_chieftainship)

Following the establishment of political agency in the [Kingdom of Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Manipur), the treaty of 1834 transferred the [Kabaw Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaw_Valley) to Burma, including the Shan *[sabwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saopha)* (transl. lord of the heavens) of Kale and Sumjok to Burmese sovereignty.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202055-54] This coincided with the rise of the [Sukte clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukte_clan) in the northern Chin Hills. Chief [Khan Thuam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Thuam) was a powerful chief who became the target of a coalition of *mang-kua* (transl. nine-chiefs) against him. Khan Thuam fled to Falam and took shelter under the Chief of Rallang, Khuang Ceu, paying tribute. With his son Kam Hau, Khan Thuam soon overcame opposition with aid from Rallang. He returned to Mualbem and took revenge on the previous chiefs. His invasion of the Tedim tribes led to the migration of several tribes into neighbouring Manipur. Colonel [William McCulloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McCulloch_\(political_officer\)) described his observations of the scattered tribes in the Manipur Valley. The [Guite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guite_people) and [Zou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zou_people) tribes settled in the eastern [Lushai Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lushai_Hills) and the Manipur Valley.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202056-55] The Sukte absorbed the remaining tribes, while Khan Thuam levied customary taxes and dues from subordinate villages, thereby establishing the Sukte domain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202055-54]

> *Siahtaang kaihna sak ciang teimei, ka hialna lamtui hi e.\
> Sak ciang teimei sang ciang lamtui, a lai ah kamkei hi'ing e.*\
> **English**:\
> *What I rule extends to Manipur in the North, and ends at Falam in the South.\
> Manipur to the North, Falam to the South, I am the tiger in the middle.*

— Sing Khaw Khai, Zo People and Their Culture (1995)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESing_Khaw_Khai199526-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202057-57]

Khan Thuam died in 1848 and was succeeded by his youngest son, Za Pau. However, his eldest son, Kam Hau, was more capable and established the village of Tedim. While Kam Hau lost Tedim to the Zou and Thadou, he regained it years later. Tedim grew and attracted many warriors, becoming one of the largest settlements and a seat of power in the Chin Hills.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202057-57] Sukte tradition states that when Khan Thuam was taking refuge in Falam, Kam Hau, who was with him, mastered the use of the gun by shooting a wild [mithun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayal) that no one else dared to hunt. Kam Hau's rule extended from the region east of the Manipur River to the Manipur Valley, encompassing 135 villages. His brother, Za Pau, controlled territory west of the Manipur River but had been eclipsed by Kam Hau's prowess.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202058-58]

Kam Hau spread his influence into the Kale-Kabaw valley by raiding the settlements. Reports have suggested that the Shan *sabwas* took revenge in 1850. The raids under Kam Hau were directed towards [Yazagyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazagyo), which was a market for the Kamhau people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202058-58] The power of the Sukte clan became a security concern in Manipur.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Khan_Pau202059-59]

### British rule



Nupi Kulh Fort, where women, children, and the elderly hid during the [Battle of Siallum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siallum_Fort#The_Battle_of_Siallum)



Sizang chiefs, 1900

Following the [Third Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Burmese_War), the Chins were surrounded by the British Empire. A series of raids and hostilities in the Lushai Hills and the Chin Hills led to the [Chin-Lushai Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin-Lushai_Expedition). This expedition would annex both the Mizos and the Chins. From December 1888 to January 1889, 36 British people were killed, and 54 were wounded.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200396-60] The Chin-Lushai Expedition was launched with the goal of permanent occupation of the Lushai and Chin tribes. The British invaded from Burma, [Assam](186162.md) and [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong200398-61] Chin resistance lasted longer than the British expected, and the terrain was becoming difficult to occupy. The British decided to conquer each tribe at a time. By 1892, the majority of the tribes were put into submission under the British. The [Sizang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizang_people), [Sukte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukte_clan), Kamhau, and Thantlang of the [Laimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laimi) opposed the British, while others refused to give up their slaves.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003100-62] In 1895–1896, after experiencing [famine](51329.md) due to long-term warfare and British burning of local [granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granaries) and crops, the Chins surrendered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003101-63]

The Chin Hills Regulation 1896 was applied. This legalised the classification of the name "Chin" across the various clans and tribes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003102-64] The Chin Hills district was given a superintendent, the first one being B.S. Carey, as the chief executive of the administrative division.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003103-65] The British implemented an administrative policy of "[indirect rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_rule)" and recognised the system of chieftainship known as *khua-bawi*. The democratic forms of chieftainship, such as in the [Tlaisun tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlaisun_people), were abolished and the *[ram-uk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_chieftainship#Ram-uk)* system of chieftainship was ignored until 1919.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003104-66]

In 1899, Superintendents Carey and Captain Drury invited the American Baptist [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) couple Reverend Arthur Carson and Laura Carson. Carey and Drury cited this policy as part of the pacification of the Chins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003119-67][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESing_Khaw_Khai1995120-68] Additional missionaries arrived, totaling seven.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003121-69] In 1902, the first medical missionary, Dr East, arrived at [Hakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakha) with the assistance of the Carsons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003126-70] The Chin Hills Baptist Association was established at Hakha in 1907.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003140-71] The missionaries established schools throughout the region, which they regarded as "the most effective instruments of [evangelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003126-70]

#### Anglo-Chin War

Main article: [Kuki Rebellion of 1917–1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_Rebellion_of_1917%E2%80%931919)



British-allied column crossing a river in the southern Chin Hills

In September 1890, the British persuaded eighteen Chin chiefs to visit [Rangoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon),[^cite_note-72] where an outbreak of [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) killed six of the Chin chiefs. The Chins believed they died because they had travelled beyond the [sovereignty](28791.md) of *Khua-hrum*, which could no longer protect them. When the British tried to raise members for [labour corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_corps), fears arose that *Khua-hrum* would not be able to protect them in a far-away country such as France.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003156-73] With the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md), 1,000 men were recruited from Hakha, [Falam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falam), and [Tedim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim) in 1917. The [Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi) joined the forces, but the [Laimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laimi) in Hakha region refused the order,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003155-74] with 5,000 Laimi men making a sacred [oath](273418.md), known as *Sathintuh*, to wage war against the British. However, [Christian convert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity) Chia Khaw leaked the plans to the British.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003156–157-75] The Laimi were unable to overrun the British and their [sepoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy), who were well armed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003158-76] The sepoys burnt down villages in the Senthang and Zophei areas, killed [livestock](25160767.md), and destroyed crops and grain. The war lasted for two years, with a famine leading to a Chin surrender in 1919. The leaders of the rebellion were brought to trial, and 61 were sentenced. Two people who were [sentenced to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentenced_to_death) committed [suicide](16831059.md) before they could be executed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003159–160-77] The uprising led to a lack of shelter and food, leading to famine, disease, and a [refugee crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_crisis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003163-78]

The Chin men who were deployed to Europe returned with more money than before; some learnt a [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft), and most of them became professional soldiers after the war. Most of the men dropped their traditional belief that one could not travel outside of *Khua-hrum* and accepted Christianity. They began evangelizing the local Chin population, contributing to the growing acceptance of Christianity among the Chin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003161-79]

#### Crown Colony of Burma

Following the [Government of India Act 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935), the British established the [Crown Colony of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Burma). However, this government excluded the Chin, [Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people), [Kachin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_people) and [Karenni people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_people) from gaining any representation in the political structure, as they had been conquered separately as independent countries. Furthermore, the demarcated borders of Burma and India divided the land of Zoram (transl. Zo land) between [Lushai Hills district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Lushai_Hills) and the Chin Hills, the latter of which became part of the [Frontier Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Areas); the Chittagong Hill Tracts remained a part of Bangladesh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003179–180-80]

The demarcation also split Maraland of the [Mara people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_people) and caused a confrontation between the Lakher Pioneer Mission (LPM), which was established in 1937, and the American Baptist Chin Mission (ABCM).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003180-81] The new boundaries made the LPM's operations in the Burmese territory unauthorised, and the ABCM accused them of "encroachment of preachers into territory not their own". The Deputy Commissioner, Mr Naylor, held a meeting regarding the dispute between the missionary groups on 24 May 1938.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003181-82] The LPM was thus shifted back into India, and the ABCM continued their work in the Chin Hills unopposed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003182-83]

The [Govenor of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Assam), [Robert Neil Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Neil_Reid), opposed the new borders, and, in 1941, he put forward a proposal known as the [Crown Colony of Eastern Agency Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Eastern_Agency_Scheme). He proposed the formation of a separate colonial province called "Chin-Lushai" as he considered neither the Chin nor the Mizo as "[Indian](7564733.md)". The province in the scheme would have its own governorship and operate as a unit akin to Assam, Bengal and Burma. The proposal was supported by [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) and the [UK Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Conservative_Party); however, the advent of [World War II](32927.md) prevented it from being implemented.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003187-84]

#### World War II



Soliders gather for an evening meal in the Chin Hills, c. 1940s

During the [Japanese occupation of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Burma), Aung San and his "[Thirty Comrades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Comrades)", receiving military training from the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army), established the [Burma Independence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Independence_Army) in 1941. During the war, the British recruited non-Burmans, with the largest group being the Chin.[^cite_note-86] The Chin, along with the [Kachin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_people) and [Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_peoples) battalions, fought the Japanese army and resisted the Burma Independence Army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003194-85]

In 1944, after the establishment of a [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Burma) under [Ba Maw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_Maw), the Japanese attempted to invade the Chin Hills. Aware of the atrocities committed by the Burma Independence Army and the Japanese, the Chin [battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) retreated and entrenched themselves in the Chin Hills.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003196-87] The Chin Hills battalion and the Chin Levies succeeded in stopping the Burma Independence Army and the [33rd Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-Third_Army_\(Japan\)) at the battle of Tedim. This delay was accredited as making the victory decisive at the [Battle of Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kohima) and the [Battle of Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal). The Japanese, however, did manage to occupy the areas of Hahka, Falam and Tedim. The reclamation by British troops in 1944–1945 saw intense fighting and the widespread destruction of villages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003197-88]

Since the Christian missionaries had left during the war, Northern Chins who had converted traveled to the southern Chin Hills to evangelize their kindred tribes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBitrusKeijzerEverettFredrickson202361-89]

#### Post-war era

After World War II, [the Crown Colony plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Eastern_Agency_Scheme), supported by Winston Churchill, was quashed with [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee)'s [Labour Party](19279158.md) entering into power. The plan was attempted to be revived by the Director of the Frontier Area Administration, H.N.C. Stevenson, to make a United Frontier Union.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003201-90] Burmese revolutionary [Aung San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_San) cooperated with the British on the "White Paper", which outlined a long-term plan for the future of Burma.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003203-91] The British would rule for three years for economic recovery and then restore the Legislative Council of Ministerial Burma. After elections were held, a [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) would be drafted and enforced with [dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) status. The White Paper considered the non-Burman areas, such as the Chin Hills, to be maintained via the pre-war [status quo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo). Chin State, [Kachin State](493369.md), and the [Federated Shan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_Shan_States) would have, according to the White Paper, "a special regime under the Governor".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003203–204-92]

The Governor of Burma, [Reginald Dorman-Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Dorman-Smith), led civil servants who were against the [Anti-Fascist People's Freedom League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Fascist_People's_Freedom_League) (AFPFL). They wished to declare the organisation illegal and to arrest Aung San.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003204-93] Attlee replaced Dorman-Smith with Major General [Hubert Rance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Rance), and the White Paper was scrapped for direct negotiations with Aung San instead. The early post-war era highlighted the rights and interests of non-Burmans such as the Chins, but in 1946, under Rance, the British only considered the creation of a sovereign Burma without any deliberation or representation with the Frontier Areas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003205-94]

On the other hand, the Frontier Areas that fought the Japanese expected lenient treatment for political matters in the future of independence. Unlike Indians and Burmans, the Chin, Kachin and Karen were willing to remain under British rule. Dorman-Smith sought to revive the Crown Colony Scheme of 1941, but British policymakers adapted it to a new idea. Under [Reginald Coupland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Coupland), they argued for a [commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth) of hill areas in both India and Burma consisting of:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003206-95]

- Chin Hills

- Lushai Hills

- Chittagong Hill Tracts

- Arakan Hill Tracts

- Pakkoku

- Naga Hills

- Manipur

- North Cachar and Mikir

- Sadiya Tracts

- Tripura

- Parts of Chindwin

- Kale

However, these did not come to fruition as the United Kingdom was too weak to impose new obligations and negotiations. Atlee vetoed the plan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003206-95] Stevenson's plan of the United Frontier Union consisted of:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003207-96]

- Chin Hills

- Kachin State

- Federated Shan States

- Independent Karenni State

- Toungoo District

- Hill districts of Salween

- Tenasserim

This plan was opposed by the Burman nationalists, who saw [Arakan](493373.md) and [Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_State) as integral parts of Burma before British conquest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003207-96] However, Stevenson had already held a meeting with the frontier area representatives in the [Panglong Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panglong_Agreement), which had been accepted.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003208-97]

In negotiations with the British, the Burmese opposed Stevenson's United Frontier Union Scheme. The London Talks (constitutional discussions in [London](17867.md) between British and Burmese representatives) excluded any representation of the hill people. On Burmese demands for a unified Burma, the British opposed the amalgamation, arguing that they were obligated to consider the wishes of the frontier people in recognition of their efforts in helping the British during World War II. The British were aware that the Frontier Areas opposed union with Burma.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003208-97]

Aung San thus cooperated, stating that the non-Burman people, including the Chin, had the right to freedom, independence, sovereignty, and [self-determination](29269.md) as they had never been under Burmese rule. He signed the Aung San-Attlee Agreement on 27 January 1947, where the second Panglong Conference would be convened to discuss the future administration of the Frontier Areas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003209-98] [U Saw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Saw) and [Ba Sein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_Sein?action=edit\&redlink=1) refused to sign the agreement, arguing that the agreement would divide Burma. Aung San's actions led U Saw, [Ba Maw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_Maw), and [Paw Tun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paw_Tun) to form the National Opposition Front, claiming that Aung San was collaborating with [imperialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialists). Aung San, however, continued negotiations, and the [Panglong Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panglong_Conference) of February 1947 acknowledged that freedom would be achieved for the groups through cooperation with the Interim Burmese Government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003210-99]

##### Panglong Agreement

Before the Panglong Conference, Chin chiefs, advisors, and representatives held a preliminary conference at [Falam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falam,_Myanmar) on 28 October 1946. They discussed whether the Chin Hills should remain under the British Governor or join dominion Burma. The Falam Conference passed resolutions to remain with the Governor and cooperate with the Shan and Kachin; they also chose chiefs to represent them at Panglong.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003211-100]

Aung San, the Chief Minister of the interim Burmese Government, arrived in Panglong on 8 February 1947 and persuaded the representatives to join independent Burma. He promised separate status with full autonomy within Burma, protection of minority rights, and the privilege of [secession](144732.md). He agreed to put the terms of the Panglong into law to guarantee the outcomes and to alleviate fears of backtracking.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003213-101]

The Chin chiefs stated that if they were to join Burma, it would be temporary before governing themselves as an independent territory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003212-102] The representatives all demanded a right to secession from Burma at any time after independence. However, when Vum Kho Pau translated for the Lai chiefs, he mistranslated the word *ram* into "district" rather than "country" or "nation-state", leading to the belief that the Chin did not ask for separate status.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003212–213-103]

Aung San further granted the Chin Hills special administrative status in the constitution to maintain their old customary law. This led to the Chin Special Division being made instead of a state within the Union of Burma. The Panglong Agreement, signed by the Frontier Tribes, was not to join Burma but to hasten their right to secession and to gain independence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003214-104] Aung San expressed to the opposition that the right of secession was granted so that by showing sincerity, the frontier areas could be made to stay. He fulfilled his promise to [codify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codification_\(law\)) the Panglong resolutions and legalised them in the [1947 Union Constitution of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Myanmar#1947_constitution).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003216-105]

Despite the right to secession being codified, Burma did not become a true federal union. Aung San was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassinated) by Burma's last pre-World War II Prime Minister, [U Saw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Saw), on 19 July 1947 and succeeded by [U Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Nu). U Nu redrafted the constitution despite it already being approved by the AFPFL in May 1947. The new constitution drafted by [U Chan Htun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Chan_Htun) was [promulgated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promulgated) by the Interim Government's assembly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003216-105]

The new constitution had a [bicameral structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) consisting of the Chamber of Deputies and the Chamber of Nationalities. The latter would allow unelected chiefs and leaders of the Chin, Shan and Kachin to retain their traditional powers. Eight parliamentary seats were reserved for the six major tribes of the Chin: the [Asho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asho_Chin_people), Cho, [Khami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mro-Khimi_people), [Laimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laimi), [Mizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_people) and [Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi_people). The system was considered problematic. The Mizo were outside of Burma, the Asho were outside of the Chin Hills, and the remaining tribes had a democratic council and a [hereditary chieftainship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_chieftainship) structure. The Cho and Khami tribes had no hereditary chieftainship system, thus lacking eligibility to have representatives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003220-106]

### Since Burmese independence in 1948

On 20 February 1948, the Chins, who were dissatisfied with the Chin chiefs, held a popular vote to dismantle the system of chieftainship for the Chamber of Nationalities and the local administration. The new system held four seats for the Northern Chin of [Hakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakha_District), [Falam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falam_District), [Thantlang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thantlang_Township) and [Tedim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim_District) and four seats for the Southern Chin of [Matupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matupi_District), [Paletwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paletwa_District), [Mindat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindat_District) and [Kanpalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpetlet_Township).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003221-107] The first celebration of the date, known as [Chin National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_National_Day), took place on 20–22 February 1951, in [Mindat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindat,_Chin_State).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESalai_Van_Cung_Lian2020-108]

In 1988, during the nationwide [8888 Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8888_Uprising), the [military](20394.md) cracked down on pro-democracy protests in Chin State, causing some residents to flee to India, where they formed the [Chin National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_National_Front) (CNF) and its armed wing, the [Chin National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_National_Army) (CNA).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECallahan200740-109] The CNA signed a [ceasefire](592591.md) deal with the Myanmar military in 2012.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFishbeinHkawngBrang2024-110]



CNA soldiers raise flag on recaptured territory in Lialaipi, Chin State, 2023

Following the [2021 Myanmar coup d'état](66567997.md), Chin residents participated in [anti-coup protests](66612294.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrisis_Group2025-16] and the CNA resumed fighting against the Burmese armed forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaung_Zami2023-111] Rival Chin insurgent groups, such as the CNA and the [Chin Brotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_Brotherhood), the latter of which is allied with the [Arakan Army](38106209.md), have engaged in [infighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intragroup_conflict).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrisis_Group2025-16] Thousands of Chin have fled to the Indian state of Mizoram. Despite the Indian government's directives, the [Mizoram government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Mizoram) has sheltered over 10,000 Chin refugees, citing ethnic and historical ties. Mizoram Chief Minister [Zoramthanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoramthanga) stated to Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md):[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaushik2021-112]

> Myanmar areas bordering Mizoram are inhabited by Chin communities who are ethnically our Mizo brethren... Mizoram cannot remain indifferent to its suffering today. India cannot turn a blind eye to this humanitarian crisis unfolding right in front of us in our own backyard.

This sentiment was echoed across Mizoram. The [YMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Mizo_Association) built refugee camps and coordinated donations of food, clothing, and funds from across the state.[^cite_note-113]

## Culture

### Clothing

The Chin people wear [wrap-around cloths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longyi) (*niik*);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELowis1908384-114] women [weave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) bright-patterned [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) with complex patterns using [backstrap looms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backstrap_loom).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETurner2014-23][^cite_note-115] The main colors used for traditional attire vary among Chin communities. Red and dark textiles are favored by the [Lutuv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutuv) and Hakha Chin, while the [Mindat Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindat,_Chin_State) prefer green and purple.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELingIntaArmstrongLittle202211-116] Accessories include [necklaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necklace), [earrings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earring), and [bangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangle).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELowis1908384-114] Magan Chin women wear large earrings made of beads and [calabashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabash).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMilanian2016-117]

Traditionally, the Chin wear [loincloths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loincloth) and men occasionally wear [turbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban). Both garments have two types: one for everyday use and one reserved for special occasions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrait2014312-118]

### Cuisine

Chin cuisine is typified by abundant use of vegetables, and meat is typically boiled and served with vegetables.[^cite_note-\:7-13] [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), and [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet) are common staple foods.[^cite_note-bri-119] The Chin brew a homemade [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) made of [fermented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fermentation) grain called *[zu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zozu)*;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELowis1908384-114] it plays a ritual role in Chin culture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESing_Khaw_Khai1995167,_177-120] Chin elders also consume nicotine juice called *thibur*, which is carried in small [gourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourd) containers.[^cite_note-ethnomed12-41]

### Holidays

[Chin National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_National_Day) is celebrated annually on 20 February, commemorating the abolition of the traditional slavery system and [chieftainship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_chieftainship) among the Chin people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003223–224-121] During the event, [beauty pageants](95198.md), [fashion shows](8312977.md), and musical performances are held.[^cite_note-122] Traditional food, such as *sabuti* (hominy corn soup) and *chang* (rice cake) is served.[^cite_note-123]

Chin Christians celebrate [Easter](9325.md) and [Christmas](6237.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003237-124]

### Sports

[Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling) is a part of the Chins' sporting traditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESalai_Lian_Ching2019-125]

### Tattoos



Chin women with [face tattoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_tattoo)

South and central Chin tribes traditionally [tattooed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattoo) the faces of women,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBihar_and_Orissa_Research_Society1930428-126] with designs varying by tribe.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAung_San_Suu_Kyi199158-127] The M'kaan, [Daai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daai_Chin), and Yin Du women got tattooed across their entire faces, while N'men (Muun) and [Matu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matu_people) women only certain parts of the face. The Uppriu tribe covers the entire face with dots, making their face appear blackened or ashen-looking.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamboulis2022-128][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFraserFraser2005141-129] Ngagah tattoos are a mix of vertical lines and dots, and Daai women display dots that are mixed with vertical and horizontal lines on the [forehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forehead) and [cheeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheek).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThe_Irrawaddy2014-130] [Mro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mro-Khimi_people) women wear tattoos in the form of small marks or [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_polygon) on the cheek, forehead, or [breast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast), which they associate with [fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHughes188113-131]

The practice, known as *Ming*,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhitnyIftekharSomblion20201-132] was banned in the 1960s by the [Union Revolutionary Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Council_of_the_Union_of_Burma) and was discouraged by both Christian and Buddhist missionaries.[^cite_note-133] Today, it is a vanishing tradition, with the remaining tattooed women being elderly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamboulis2022-128][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESouth_China_Morning_Post2016-134] Women were typically tattooed between the ages of 15 and 20.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMyo_Myo2011-135] According to folklore, a Burmese king travelled to the region and married a beautiful Chin woman, taking her to his palace. Unhappy there, she snuck away to return home and disguised herself by making incisions in her face.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENyein_Ei_Ei_Htwe2013-136] Because of this, Chin families began tattooing their daughters to ensure they would not be taken away. Other explanations suggest it was done for beauty or to distinguish members of different tribes in case one was [kidnapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnap) by another.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamboulis2022-128]

The tattoos are made by heating the [bark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bark_\(botany\)) of green [pine trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_tree) and capturing the smoke in a mud pot. The [soot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soot) is then mixed with a kind of bean leaves, and the resulting liquid is inserted under the skin using the [thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorns,_spines,_and_prickles) of a cane plant.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENyein_Ei_Ei_Htwe2013-136] The process takes around 2–4 days to complete.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhitnyIftekharSomblion20201-132]

## Languages

The Chin peoples speak a variety of related but often mutually unintelligible [Kuki-Chin languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Chin_languages), which preserve many features of [Proto-Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Tibeto-Burman_language) phonology, including preservation of many initial and final [consonants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consonant).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenneth_Van_Bik2021350–378-137][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYamahataSeekinsTakeda2021229-138] Several Chin languages are facing [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_death) due to competing local and [national languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language), such as [Lamtuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_language), which is being displaced by [Hakha Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakha_Chin) and [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language). [Multilingualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism) is common among the Chin, who invariably speak their native language, a nearby local language, and the national language, Burmese.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenneth_Van_Bik2021351-139]

In 2019, the [Chin State government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_State_Government) tentatively proposed prioritising five Chin languages—[Zolai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim_language) (for [Tedim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim_Township) and [Tonzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonzang_Township)), [Laizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falam_language) (for [Falam](15141612.md)), [Lai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakha_Chin) (for [Hakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakha_Township) and [Thantlang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thantlang_Township)), [Khumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khumi_language) (for [Paletwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paletwa_District)), and [Kcho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%CA%BCch%C3%B2_language) (for [Mindat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindat_Township) and [Kanpalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpetlet_Township))—to be taught as [mother tongues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_tongue) at state schools.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESalai_Van_Cung_LianSalem-Gervais2020137-140]

## Religion

Traditionally, the Chin peoples believe in numerous [deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deities) and [spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_\(supernatural_entity\)) that inhabit natural phenomena.[^cite_note-bri-119] They worship a supreme god, *Pathian*,[^cite_note-154] who is believed to have come from [heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERubén_Salgado_Escudero2013-155] and possesses the capability to control the universe.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohn_Tha_Cung201863–66-156] [Gayals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayal) and [pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs) are commonly sacrificed during rituals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESing_Khaw_Khai1995174-157] Expert [hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting) are believed to enjoy a high rank in the [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife).[^cite_note-bri-119] Traditional Chin stories are similar to those of the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible), such as [Tower of Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Babel) and [world flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_myth) narratives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERubén_Salgado_Escudero2013-155] The Chin also practice forms of [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_worship).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESing_Khaw_Khai199579,_115,_121-158][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenderson196882-159]

In the late 1800s, the first Christian missionaries arrived in Chin State and began converting the Chin to Christianity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohn_Tha_Cung201830–31-160] They translated the Bible into local dialects and established schools for the Chin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakhong2003172,_226,_234-161] Missionaries and Chin evangelicals successfully helped spread Christianity throughout the region and today, the majority of Chin are Christians, with most belonging to [Protestant denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), especially [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMühlhausLee202597-10][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBitrusKeijzerEverettFredrickson202359–62-162] Christianity grew from 35% in 1966 to 90% in 2010.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPum_Za_Mang2016163-163] Chin Christians have faced [religious persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution) from the military government in [predominantly Buddhist Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Myanmar).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYoung202220-164][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEManchandaBose1997206-165][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBagnall2010-166] [Animal sacrifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_sacrifice) continues to be practiced among some Chin Christians.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERubén_Salgado_Escudero2013-155]

Several Chin tribes, including the [Asho Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asho_Chin_people), are majority Buddhist. Some Buddhist [Kuki-Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Chin_languages)-speaking ethnic groups, like the [Taungtha people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taungtha_people) in [Magway Region](493360.md), do not self-identify as Chin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKee_Shein_Mang2006-167]

Since the late 20th century, a group of Chin, Kuki, and Mizo peoples have claimed descent from [Manasseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Manasseh), one of the [Lost Tribes of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Lost_Tribes), and have adopted Judaism.[^cite_note-168] They are known as the [Bnei Menashe](1674436.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETepper2021-169]

## Human rights violations

Cross statue set on top of a mountain.

Chin Christians often set up [crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_cross) on local mountaintops; many have been destroyed by Myanmar authorities.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERogers2012109-170]

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavid_Thang_Moe202473-171]

Since the [1962 military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Burmese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) by [Ne Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne_Win), the Chin—a mostly Christian minority in a predominantly Buddhist Myanmar—have faced systemic ethnic and religious persecution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChin_Human_Rights_Organization201264,_82-172] The [Tatmadaw](20394.md) and successive military juntas, including the [State Peace and Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council) (SPDC), have committed widespread abuses, including [forced labor](541048.md), [torture](47702.md), [arbitrary detention](13435705.md), [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killings),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlexander20094-173] and [forced assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_assimilation).[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECady1965638-177] SPDC soldiers have been incentivized to marry and convert Chin Christian women.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall2008110-178][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlexander200971-179] These conditions have driven many to flee to India, Thailand, and Malaysia, often without legal protection or refugee status.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBagnall2010-166][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilchZo_Tum_Hmung201116-180]

### Extrajudicial killings

Although Myanmar is not a party to the [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights), it is still bound by other international obligations that protect the [right to life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_life).[^cite_note-181] Despite this, extrajudicial killings have been repeatedly reported in Chin State.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlexander200926-182][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrangio2026-183] [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) and the Chin Human Rights Organization have documented killings, including children and women, for suspected opposition ties.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlexander2009-184][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChin_Human_Rights_Organization20104-185]

### Forced labor

Despite Myanmar's ratification of the 1930 [Forced Labour Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_Labour_Convention), forced labor has been widely reported.[^cite_note-186] Chin civilians are often forced to construct military buildings, carry supplies, or serve as porters under threat of violence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlexander200940-187][^cite_note-hrwi-188] A 2011 study using household interviews found that 91.9% of Chin households surveyed experienced forced labor, and 78.4% were forced to build infrastructure. 14.8% of households reported beatings or torture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESollomRichardsParmarMullany20114–5-189]
