**Samara Oblast**[^cite_note-7] is a [federal subject](701438.md) of [Russia](25391.md) (an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)). Its [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) is the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) of [Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara). From 1935 to 1991, it was known as **Kuybyshev Oblast**.[^cite_note-8] As of the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\)), the population of the oblast was 3,172,925.[^cite_note-2021Census-5]

The oblast borders [Tatarstan](67135.md) in the north, [Orenburg Oblast](481957.md) in the east, [Kazakhstan](16642.md) ([West Kazakhstan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Kazakhstan_Province)) in the south, [Saratov Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_Oblast) in the southwest and [Ulyanovsk Oblast](481965.md) in the west.

It is located in 3 natural landscape zones: the [forest zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_zone) (coniferous and broad-leaved forests), the basis of which is pine-oak forests, pine forests and broad-leaved forests with the participation of oak and maple. Spruce occasionally joins them. Areas of the southern taiga are found on the coast of the region. The [forest-steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest-steppe) zone occupies the central regions of the region and is represented by a combination of areas of broad-leaved forests, most often oak and meadow steppes. [Red deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_deer), [roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer), [elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose), and sometimes bears are found in these forests. The south of the region is occupied by steppes consisting of [typchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca_valesiaca) and [sagebrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagebrush).

## History

The Samara region contains a remarkable succession of archaeological cultures from 7000 BC to 4000 BC. These sites have revealed Europe's earliest pottery ([Elshanka culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elshanka_culture)),[^cite_note-9] the world's oldest [horse burial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_burial) and signs of [horse worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_worship) (the Syezzheye cemetery of [Samara culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_culture))[^cite_note-10] and the earliest [kurgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan) associated with [Proto-Indo-Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) (e.g., Krivoluchye assigned to [Khvalynsk culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khvalynsk_culture)[^cite_note-11]).

The [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) established the [Samara Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_Governorate) as a [*guberniya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_\(Russia\)) in the area in 1851. It was administered from the city of [Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara). During the [Revolution of 1905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_1905), a rebellion took place in November that year at the village of Novaya Tsarevshchina (now Volzhsky) and spread to the village of Stary Buyan, leading to the formation of the short-lived separatist state, the [Stary Buyan Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stary_Buyan_Republic), within the governorate. There was little local opposition to the new state, and after the failure of local police to end the rebellion, it was successfully suppressed by a [punitive expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punitive_expedition) of [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) and [gendarmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Corps_of_Gendarmes) led by the vice-governor of the governorate in Samara.

After the decree of [Tsar Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_Nicholas_II) on [religious tolerance](3060242.md#History) the [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers) were forced to pay taxes in favour to the Orthodox Church that they believed to be heretical. The then Ministry of Interior forbade Samara's public officials to prosecute in this practice, which was contrary to the spirit of the decree.[^cite_note-12]

During the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), the region was heavily affected by the [Russian famine of 1921–1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_famine_of_1921%E2%80%931922).

Under [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) rule most of the governorate's territory was transformed into the Middle Volga Oblast, which was established on May 14, 1928, and a year later on October 20, 1929, it was again transformed into Middle Volga Krai.[^cite_note-Established-1] On January 27, 1935, Samara and the Middle Volga Krai were renamed Kuybyshev and Kuybyshev Krai, respectively, in honor of the [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) leader [Valerian Kuybyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerian_Kuybyshev).[^cite_note-Established-1] On December 5, 1936, Kuybyshev Krai was transformed into Kuybyshev Oblast upon the adoption of the [1936 Soviet Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Soviet_Constitution).[^cite_note-Established-1] On January 25, 1991 by the Decree of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the RSFSR, the Kuibyshev Oblast was renamed the Samara Oblast.[^cite_note-13] On April 21, 1992, the Congress of People's Deputies of Russia approved the decision of the presidium of the parliament to rename the region, amending Art. 71 of the Constitution of the RSFSR of 1978,[^cite_note-14] which entered into force on May 16, 1992.[^cite_note-15]

On 1 August 1997 Samara Oblast signed a power-sharing agreement with the federal government, granting it autonomy.[^cite_note-16] This agreement would be abolished on 22 February 2002.[^cite_note-demokratiztsiya-17]

[Tolyatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolyatti) is the largest city in Russia which does not serve as the administrative center of a federal subject.

## Economy

In 1997, Samara Oblast became one of the few federal subjects to receive the approval of the [President of Russia](239646.md) to implement external bonded loans (Presidential decree No. 1212, dated 12.10.1997 "On Creating Conditions to Conduct Loans Operations on the Internal and External Capital Markets").

Samara Oblast (or Samara Region) has several special investment sites which are built to implement new plants, industries, factories, create new jobs and increase investments in the Samara Region, in addition to legacy plants such as the [Kuibyshev refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuibyshev_refinery).[^cite_note-Neftepererabotka-27]

## Politics

See also: [Samara constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_constituency)



[LDPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDPR) meeting in front of the Oblast administration building

During the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) period, the high authority in the oblast was shared between three persons: The first secretary of the Samara CPSU Committee (who in reality had the biggest authority), the chairman of the oblast Soviet (legislative power), and the Chairman of the oblast Executive Committee (executive power). After the abolition of Article 6 of the Constitution of the USSR in March 1990, the CPSU lost its monopoly on power. The head of the Oblast administration, and eventually the governor was appointed/elected alongside elected [regional parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_parliaments_of_Russia).

The Charter of Samara Oblast is the fundamental law of the oblast. The Legislative Assembly of Samara Oblast is the province's standing legislative (representative) body. The Legislative Assembly exercises its authority by passing laws, resolutions, and other legal acts and by supervising the implementation and observance of the laws and other legal acts passed by it. The highest executive body is the Oblast Government, which includes territorial executive bodies such as district administrations, committees, and commissions that facilitate development and run the day to day matters of the province. The Oblast administration supports the activities of the Governor who is the highest official and acts as guarantor of the observance of the oblast Charter in accordance with the [Constitution of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia).

## Geography

The region is located in the southeastern part of the European territory of Russia, in the middle reaches of the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), on both sides of it. This is the fifth largest region of the [Volga region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_region) — it occupies an area of 53.6 thousand km2, which is 0.31% of the territory of Russia. The region stretches from north to south for 335 km, and from west to east for 315 km. The southernmost point of the region lies on the border with Kazakhstan (51°47' s. w. and 50°47' v. d.), the northernmost point is on the border with the Republic of Tatarstan (54°41's. w. and 51°23' v. d.). The westernmost point lies on the border with the Ulyanovsk region (53°22' s. w. and 47°55' v. d.), and the extreme eastern one is on the border with the Orenburg region (54°20's. w. and 52°35' v. d.). Due to the proximity of the West Kazakhstan region of [Kazakhstan](16642.md), part of the Bolshechnigovsky district has the status of a border territory.

Zhiguli Nature Reserve. Samara region

At the moment, a unique network of various protected natural areas has been formed in the Samara region. It is based on protected areas of federal significance: the I. I.
