Placing "dirty" money in a service company, where it is layered with legitimate income and then integrated into the flow of money, is a common form of money laundering.

**Money laundering** is the process of illegally concealing the origin of [money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money) obtained from illicit activities (often known as **dirty money**), and converting the funds into a seemingly legitimate source.[^cite_note-1] As [financial crime](11656148.md) has become more complex and [financial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_intelligence) is more important in combating [international crime](6760775.md) and terrorism, money laundering has become a prominent [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_debate), [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), and [legal debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_debate). Most countries implement some [anti-money-laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-money_laundering) measures.&#x20;

In the past, the term "money laundering" was applied only to [financial transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_transaction) related to [organized crime](22625.md). Today its definition is often expanded by government and international regulators such as the US [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Comptroller_of_the_Currency) to mean "any financial transaction which generates an [asset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_\(economics\)) or a [value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_value) as the result of an illegal act", which may involve actions such as [tax evasion](2256795.md) or [false accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_accounting). In the UK, it does not need to involve money, but any [economic good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_good). Courts have characterized as money laundering, acts of financial crimes committed by private individuals, drug dealers, businesses, corrupt officials, members of criminal organizations such as the [Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia), and even states.&#x20;

In [United States](3434750.md) law, money laundering is the practice of engaging in financial transactions to conceal the identity, source, or destination of illegally gained money. In [United Kingdom](31717.md) law, the [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) definition is wider. The act is defined as "the process by which the proceeds of crime are converted into assets which appear to have a legitimate origin, so that they can be retained permanently or recycled into further criminal enterprises".[^cite_note-2]&#x20;

## Legislative history

While existing laws were used to fight money laundering during the period of [Prohibition in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States) during the 1930s, dedicated [anti-money laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-money_laundering) legislation was only implemented in the 1980s.[^cite_note-3] [Organized crime](22625.md) received a major boost from Prohibition and a large source of new funds that were obtained from illegal sales of alcohol. The successful prosecution of [Al Capone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Capone) on [tax evasion](2256795.md) brought in a new emphasis by the state and law enforcement agencies to track and confiscate money, but existing laws against tax evasion could not be used once gangsters started paying their taxes.&#x20;

In the 1980s, the [war on drugs](1181646.md) led governments again to turn to money laundering rules in an attempt to track and seize the proceeds of [drug crimes](6886776.md) in order to catch the organizers and individuals running drug empires. It also had the benefit, from a law enforcement point of view, of turning rules of evidence "upside down". Law enforcers normally have to prove an individual is guilty to seize their property, but with [civil forfeiture laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_forfeiture_in_the_United_States), money can be confiscated and it is up to the individual to prove that the source of funds is legitimate to get the money back.[^cite_note-4] This makes it much easier for law enforcement agencies and provides for much lower [burdens of proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burden_of_proof_\(law\)).&#x20;

However, this process has been abused by some law enforcement agencies to take and keep money without strong evidence of related criminal activity, to be used to supplement their own budgets. Civil asset forfeiture has been harshly criticized by [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties) advocates for its greatly reduced standards for conviction, [reverse onus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_onus), financial [conflicts of interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) arising when the law enforcement agencies who decide whether or not to seize assets stand to keep those assets for themselves,[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] and violation of [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) and [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process).[^cite_note-q973-9]&#x20;

The [11 September attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_September_attacks) in 2001, which led to the [Patriot Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_Act) in the U.S. and similar legislation worldwide, led to a new emphasis on money laundering laws to combat [terrorism financing](4308598.md).[^cite_note-10] The [Group of Seven (G7)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Seven_\(G7\)) nations used the [Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force_on_Money_Laundering) to put pressure on governments around the world to increase surveillance and monitoring of financial transactions and share this information between countries. Starting in 2002, governments around the world upgraded money laundering laws and surveillance and monitoring systems of financial transactions. Anti-money laundering regulations have become a much larger burden for [financial institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_institution), and enforcement has stepped up significantly.&#x20;

During 2011–2015 a number of major banks faced ever-increasing fines for breaches of money laundering regulations. This included [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC), which was fined \$1.9 billion in December 2012,[^cite_note-11] and [BNP Paribas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNP_Paribas), which was fined \$8.9 billion in July 2014 by the U.S. government.[^cite_note-nytimes_bnp_admits_guilt-12] Many countries introduced or strengthened border controls on the amount of cash that can be carried and introduced central transaction reporting systems where all financial institutions have to report all financial transactions electronically. For example, in 2006, [Australia](4689264.md) set up the [AUSTRAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Transaction_Reports_and_Analysis_Centre) system and required the reporting of all financial transactions.[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

With the surge in digital assets in the late 2010s, there has been a noticeable rise in money laundering and fraud tied to cryptocurrency. In 2021 alone, cybercriminals managed to secure US\$14 billion in [cryptocurrency](36662188.md) through various illicit activities.[^cite_note-14] It has been suggested that the expansion of the cryptocurrency trading created new avenues for "secrecy-seeking capital" from the 2010s onward, when money laundering and [tax evasion](2256795.md) through [tax haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven) jurisdictions became more difficult, following various international policy initiatives for example within the [OECD](33853117.md) and the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-15]&#x20;

[Chinese organized criminal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_\(organized_crime\)) have become the principal money launderers for [drug cartels](197015.md) in [Mexico](3966054.md), [Italy](14532.md), and elsewhere.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In the U.S., Chinese money laundering networks were associated with over US\$312 billion in suspicious transactions from 2020 to 2024, according to the [Financial Crimes Enforcement Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Crimes_Enforcement_Network).[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

The [East](19605700.md) and [Southeas](28741.md)t Asia regions have become areas of major concern for money laundering. The [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime) noted in a 2019 transnational organized crime assessment that threats arising from organized crime in Southeast Asia were becoming more deeply integrated within the region itself, as well as with neighboring and connected regions.[^cite_note-SEA_TOCTA_2019_web-21] As the region's illicit economies expanded and evolved, including the growth of the [synthetic drug](70322626.md) industry, casinos and economic zones in the region's border areas became important hubs for money laundering.[^cite_note-SEA_TOCTA_2019_web-21] Sites such as the [Golden Triangle Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_Special_Economic_Zone) in Laos have been identified as hotspots for money laundering and various other types of [transnational crime](6760775.md).[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

Other high risk sectors for money laundering include commercial banks, securities companies, currency exchange shops, money transfer service providers, insurance companies, real estate agencies,[^cite_note-23] and the trade in valuable materials such as art, antiquities and wildlife products.[^cite_note-Douglas-24][^cite_note-25] As these industries have grown, the presence and sophistication of money laundering operations has too, creating a backdoor for organized crime to launder illicit funds into the global financial system.[^cite_note-Douglas-24]&#x20;

Casino junkets operating from Macao emerged as a major facilitator of money laundering, as has the more recently established online gambling industry.[^cite_note-26] The rise of the cyber-enabled fraud industry across Southeast Asia, especially in [Cambodia](334751.md), [Myanmar](19457.md), [Laos](17752.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md), has given rise to new platforms providing guarantees and facilitating the laundering of funds through app-based channels.[^cite_note-27] One such case is that of [Huione](79531940.md), a Cambodia-based operation that [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain) analysis firms have identified as one of the leading actors in this space. Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic described Huione as the "largest illicit online marketplace to have ever operated".[^cite_note-28] Global law enforcement has begun to respond to the threats posed by such platforms, and in May 2025 the U.S. Department of the Treasury's [Financial Crimes Enforcement Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Crimes_Enforcement_Network) (FinCEN) issued a finding and notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) pursuant to Section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act that identified Huione Group as a financial institution of primary money laundering concern, seeking to sever its access to the U.S. financial system.[^cite_note-29] Reuters identified Cambodian businessman [Hun To](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hun_To), cousin of Prime Minister [Hun Manet](42929019.md), as one of the directors of Huione Pay.[^cite_note-HunTo_Reuters_2024_ML-30]&#x20;

## Features

### Definition

Money laundering is the conversion or transfer of property; the concealment or disguising of the nature of the proceeds; the acquisition, possession or use of property, knowing that these are derived from criminal acts; the participating in or assisting the movement of funds to make the proceeds appear legitimate.&#x20;

Money obtained from certain crimes, such as [extortion](162449.md), [insider trading](15368.md), [drug trafficking](218449.md), [human trafficking](19333662.md), and [illegal gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_gambling) is "dirty" and needs to be "cleaned" to appear to have been derived from legal activities, so that banks and other financial institutions will deal with it without suspicion. Money can be laundered by many methods that vary in complexity and sophistication.&#x20;

Money laundering typically involves three steps: The first involves introducing cash into the financial system by some means ("placement"); the second involves carrying out complex financial transactions to camouflage the illegal source of the cash ("layering"); and finally, acquiring wealth generated from the transactions of the illicit funds ("integration"). Some of these steps may be omitted, depending on the circumstances. For example, non-cash proceeds that are already in the financial system would not need to be placed.[^cite_note-chasing_dirty_money-31]&#x20;

According to the [United States Treasury Department](53667.md):&#x20;

> Money laundering is the process of making illegally-gained proceeds (i.e., "dirty money") appear legal (i.e., "clean"). Typically, it involves three steps: placement, layering, and integration. First, the illegitimate funds are furtively introduced into the legitimate financial system. Then, the money is moved around to create confusion, sometimes by wiring or transferring through numerous accounts. Finally, it is integrated into the financial system through additional transactions until the "dirty money" appears "clean".[^cite_note-32]

### Methods

#### List of methods

Money laundering can take several forms, although most methodologies can be categorized into one of a few types. These include "bank methods, smurfing \[also known as structuring], currency exchanges, and double-invoicing".[^cite_note-encyclopedia-33]&#x20;

- [Structuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structuring): Often known as *smurfing*, is a method of placement whereby cash is broken into smaller deposits of money, used to defeat suspicion of money laundering and to avoid anti-money laundering reporting requirements. A sub-component of this is to use smaller amounts of cash to purchase bearer instruments, such as money orders, and then ultimately deposit those, again in small amounts.

  [^cite_note-NDIC-34]

- Bulk cash smuggling: This involves physically smuggling cash to another jurisdiction and depositing it in a financial institution, such as an [offshore bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_bank), that offers greater [bank secrecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_secrecy) or less rigorous money laundering enforcement.

  [^cite_note-National_Money_Laundering_Threat_Assessment-35]

- Cash-intensive businesses: In this method, a business that is typically expected to receive a large proportion of its revenue as cash uses its accounts to deposit criminally derived cash. This method of money laundering often causes organized crime and [corporate crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_crime) to overlap.

  [^cite_note-36]

  &#x20;Such enterprises often operate openly and in doing so generate cash revenue from incidental legitimate business in addition to the illicit cash. In such cases, the business will usually claim all cash received as legitimate earnings. Service businesses are best suited to this method, as such enterprises have little or no [variable costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_cost) and/or a large ratio between revenue and variable costs, which makes it difficult to detect discrepancies between revenues and costs. Examples are [parking structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_structures), [strip clubs](1380550.md), [tanning salons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_salon), [car washes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_wash), [arcades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amusement_arcade), [bars](272207.md), [restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restaurants), [casinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino), [barber shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber), [DVD stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD_store), [movie theaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_theaters), and [beach resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_resort).

- Trade-based laundering: This method is one of the newest and most complex forms of money laundering.

  [^cite_note-37]

  &#x20;This involves under- or over-valuing [invoices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invoice) to disguise the movement of money.

  [^cite_note-Achilles_Heel-38]

  &#x20;For example, the art market has been accused of being an ideal vehicle for money laundering due to several unique aspects of art such as the subjective value of artworks as well as the secrecy of auction houses about the identity of the buyer and seller.

  [^cite_note-Has_the_Art_Market_Become_an_Unwitting_Partner_in_Crime?-39]

  &#x20;According to the [National Crime Agency](28153284.md), one strategy that is favored by high-net-worth individuals is specialist storage facilities. Art kept in these spaces has been used by individuals to evade sanctions and launder the proceeds of crime.

  [^cite_note-40]

- [Shell companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_corporation) and trusts: Trusts and shell companies disguise the true owners of money. Trusts and corporate vehicles, depending on the jurisdiction, need not disclose their true owner. Sometimes referred to by the slang term *rathole*, though that term usually refers to a person acting as the fictitious owner rather than the business entity.

  [^cite_note-FATF_threat_assessment-41]

- [Round-tripping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round-tripping_\(finance\)): Here, money is deposited in a [controlled foreign corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_foreign_corporation) offshore, preferably in a [tax haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven) where minimal records are kept, and then shipped back as a [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment), exempt from taxation. A variant of this is to transfer money to a law firm or similar organization as funds on account of fees, then to cancel the retainer and, when the money is remitted, represent the sums received from the lawyers as a legacy under a will or proceeds of litigation.

- Bank capture: In this case, money launderers or criminals buy a controlling interest in a bank, preferably in a jurisdiction with weak money laundering controls, and then move money through the bank without scrutiny.

- [Casinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino): In this method, an individual walks into a casino and buys chips with illicit cash. The individual will then play for a relatively short time. When the person cashes in the chips, they will expect to take payment in a check, or at least get a receipt so they can claim the proceeds as [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling) winnings.

  [^cite_note-National_Money_Laundering_Threat_Assessment-35]

- Other gambling: Money is spent on gambling, preferably on high odds games. One way to minimize risk with this method is to bet on every possible outcome of some event that has many possible outcomes, so no outcome(s) have short odds, and the bettor will lose only the [vigorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigorish) and will have one or more winning bets that can be shown as the source of money. The losing bets will remain hidden.

- Black salaries: A company may have unregistered employees without written contracts and pay them cash salaries. Dirty money might be used to pay them.

  [^cite_note-Underground_Economy_Issues._Ontario_Construction_Secretariat-42]

- [Tax amnesties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_amnesty): For example, those that legalize unreported assets and cash in tax havens.

  [^cite_note-43]

- Transaction Laundering: When a merchant unknowingly processes illicit credit card transactions for another business.

  [^cite_note-44]

  &#x20;It is a growing problem

  [^cite_note-45]

  [^cite_note-46]

  &#x20;and recognised as distinct from traditional money laundering in using the payments ecosystem to hide that the transaction even occurred

  [^cite_note-47]

  &#x20;(e.g. the use of fake front websites

  [^cite_note-48]

  ). Also known as "undisclosed aggregation" or "factoring".

  [^cite_note-49]

  [^cite_note-50]

- Online job marketplaces such as [Freelancer.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freelancer.com) and [Fiverr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiverr), which accept funds from clients and hold them in [escrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escrow) to pay freelancers. A money launderer can post a token job on one of these sites, and send the money for the site to hold in escrow. The launderer (or his associate) can then sign on as a freelancer (using a different account and IP address), accept and complete the job, and be paid the funds.

  [^cite_note-Wired.uk-51]

- Through sports: Investigation teams have identified sport profits as a common way to launder money. In Latin America, in particular, drug traffickers are frequently found to own Soccer Clubs, and to launder money through their intermediate, by buying and selling players, selling tickets and merchandise.

  [^cite_note-52]

#### Digital electronic money

See also: [Cryptocurrency and crime](56412681.md)

In theory, [electronic money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_money) should provide as easy a method of transferring value without revealing identity as untracked banknotes, especially wire transfers involving anonymity-protecting numbered bank accounts. In practice, however, the record-keeping capabilities of Internet service providers and other network resource maintainers tend to frustrate that intention. While some [cryptocurrencies](36662188.md) under recent development have aimed to provide more possibilities of transaction anonymity for various reasons, the degree to which they succeed — and, in consequence, the degree to which they offer benefits for money laundering efforts — is controversial. Solutions such as [ZCash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zcash) and [Monero](45714713.md) ― known as *privacy coins*[^cite_note-53] ― are examples of cryptocurrencies that provide unlinkable anonymity via proofs and/or obfuscation of information ([ring signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_signature)).[^cite_note-54] While not suitable for large-scale crimes, privacy coins like Monero are suitable for laundering money made through small-scale crimes.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

Apart from traditional cryptocurrencies, [Non-Fungible Tokens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFT) (NFTs) are also commonly used in connection with money laundering activities.[^cite_note-56] NFTs are often used to perform [Wash Trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wash_Trade) by creating several different [wallets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet) for one individual, generating several fictitious sales and consequently selling the respective NFT to a third party.[^cite_note-57] According to a report by [Chainalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chainalysis), these types of wash trades are becoming increasingly popular among money launderers especially due to the largely anonymous nature of transactions on NFT marketplaces.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Auction platforms for NFT sales may face regulatory pressure to comply with anti-money laundering legislation.[^cite_note-60]&#x20;

Additionally, [cryptocurrency mixers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_tumbler) have been increasingly used by cybercriminals over the past decade to launder funds.[^cite_note-61] A mixer blends the cryptocurrencies of many users together to obfuscate the origins and owners of funds, enabling a greater degree of privacy on public blockchains like [Bitcoin](28249265.md) and [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum).[^cite_note-62] Although not explicitly illegal in many jurisdictions, the legality of mixers is controversial.[^cite_note-63] The use of the mixer [Tornado Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Cash) in the laundering of funds stolen by the [DPRK](21255.md)-associated [Lazarus Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Group) led the [Office of Foreign Assets Control](231045.md) to sanction it, prompting some users to sue the Treasury Department.[^cite_note-64] Proponents have argued mixers allow users to protect their privacy and that the government lacks the authority to restrict access to decentralized software. In the United States, [FinCEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Crimes_Enforcement_Network) requires mixers to register as money service businesses.[^cite_note-65]&#x20;

In 2013, [Jean-Loup Richet](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jean-Loup_Richet\&action=edit\&redlink=1), a research fellow at [ESSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESSEC) ISIS, surveyed new techniques that cybercriminals were using in a report written for the [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime).[^cite_note-Jean-Loup_Richet-66] A common approach was to use a [digital currency exchanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_currency_exchanger) service which converted dollars into a digital currency called [Liberty Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Reserve), and could be sent and received anonymously. The receiver could convert the Liberty Reserve currency back into cash for a small fee. In May 2013, the US authorities shut down Liberty Reserve, charging its founder and various others with money laundering.[^cite_note-Wired-67]&#x20;

Another increasingly common way of laundering money is to use online gaming. In a growing number of online games, such as *[Second Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Life)* and *[World of Warcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Warcraft),* it is possible to [convert money into virtual goods, services, or virtual cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_farming) that can later be converted back into money.[^cite_note-Wired.uk-51]&#x20;

To avoid the usage of decentralized digital money such as [Bitcoin](28249265.md) for the profit of crime and corruption, Australia is planning to strengthen the nation's anti-money laundering laws.[^cite_note-ABC-68] The characteristics of Bitcoin—it is completely deterministic, protocol-based and can be difficult to censor[^cite_note-69]—make it possible to circumvent national laws using services like [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_\(network\)) to obfuscate transaction origins. Bitcoin relies completely on cryptography, not on a central entity running under a [KYC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer) framework. There are several cases in which criminals have cashed out a significant amount of [Bitcoin](28249265.md) after ransomware attacks, drug dealings, cyber fraud and gunrunning.[^cite_note-cnbc.com-70] However, many digital currency exchanges are now operating KYC programs under threat of regulation from the jurisdictions they operate.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]&#x20;

#### Reverse money laundering

See also: [Hawala and crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawala_and_crime)

Reverse money laundering is a process that disguises a legitimate source of funds that are to be used for illegal purposes.[^cite_note-REVERSE_LAUNDERING-73] It is usually perpetrated for the purpose of financing terrorism[^cite_note-74] but can be also used by criminal organizations that have invested in legal businesses and would like to withdraw legitimate funds from official circulation. Unaccounted cash received via disguising financial transactions is not included in official financial reporting and could be used to evade taxes, hand in bribes and pay "under-the-table" salaries.[^cite_note-75] For example, in an [affidavit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affidavit) filed on 24 March 2014 in [United States District Court, Northern California](891692.md), San Francisco Division, FBI special agent Emmanuel V. Pascua alleged that several people associated with the [Chee Kung Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chee_Kung_Tong) organization, and California State Senator [Leland Yee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leland_Yee), engaged in reverse money laundering activities.&#x20;

The problem of such fraudulent encashment practices (*obnalichka* in Russian) has become acute in Russia and other countries of the former Soviet Union. The Eurasian Group on Combating Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism (EAG) reported that the Russian Federation, Ukraine, Turkey, Serbia, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Armenia and Kazakhstan have encountered a substantial shrinkage of tax base and shifting money supply balance in favor of cash. These processes have complicated the planning and management of the economy and contributed to the growth of the [shadow economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_economy).[^cite_note-76]&#x20;

### Magnitude

Many regulatory and governmental authorities issue estimates each year for the amount of money laundered, either worldwide or within their national economy. In 1996, a spokesperson for the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) estimated that 2–5% of the worldwide global economy involved laundered money.[^cite_note-77] The [Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force_on_Money_Laundering) (FATF), an intergovernmental body set up to combat money laundering, stated, "Due to the illegal nature of the transactions, precise statistics are not available and it is therefore impossible to produce a definitive estimate of the amount of money that is globally laundered every year. The FATF therefore does not publish any figures in this regard."[^cite_note-FATF_FAQ-78] Academic commentators have likewise been unable to estimate the volume of money with any degree of assurance.[^cite_note-chasing_dirty_money-31] Various estimates of the scale of global money laundering are sometimes repeated often enough to make some people regard them as factual—but no researcher has overcome the inherent difficulty of measuring an actively concealed practice.&#x20;

Regardless of the difficulty in measurement, the amount of money laundered each year is in the [billions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000000000_\(number\)) of US dollars and poses a significant policy concern for governments.[^cite_note-chasing_dirty_money-31] As a result, governments and international bodies have undertaken efforts to deter, prevent, and apprehend money launderers. Financial institutions have likewise undertaken efforts to prevent and detect transactions involving dirty money, both as a result of government requirements and to avoid the reputational risk involved. Issues relating to money laundering have existed as long as there have been [large-scale criminal enterprises](22625.md). Modern anti-money laundering laws have developed along with the modern [war on drugs](1181646.md).[^cite_note-79] In more recent times anti-money laundering legislation is seen as an adjunct to the financial crime of [terrorist financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_financing) in that both crimes usually involve the transmission of funds through the financial system (although money laundering relates to where the money has come *from*, and terrorist financing relating to where the money is going *to*). Finally, people, vessels, organisations and governments can be sanctioned due to international law-breaking, war (and of course tit-for-tat sanctions), and still want to move funds into markets where they are *[persona non grata](521241.md)*.&#x20;

Transaction laundering is a massive and growing problem.[^cite_note-80] Finextra estimated that transaction laundering accounted for over \$200 billion in the US in 2017 alone, with over \$6 billion of these sales involving illicit goods or services, sold by nearly 335,000 unregistered merchants.[^cite_note-81] Money laundering can erode [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy).[^cite_note-v890-82][^cite_note-f925-83]&#x20;

### Laws by country

#### India

The Prevention of Money Laundering Act of 2002,[^cite_note-126] and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita of 2023[^cite_note-127] make money-laundering a punishable offence.[^cite_note-128]&#x20;

#### Australia

Introduced the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (AML/CTF Act) to regulate financial transactions and enhance transparency.[^cite_note-129]&#x20;

#### Canada

In 2000, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) Act was enacted, which established the [Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Transactions_and_Reports_Analysis_Centre_of_Canada) (FINTRAC).[^cite_note-130]&#x20;

#### United Kingdom

The United Kingdom implemented the Proceeds of Crime Act of 2002 (POCA) to establish money laundering offenses and facilitate asset recovery.[^cite_note-131]&#x20;

#### United States

The Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) was enacted to require companies to disclose their beneficial owners to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), effectively reducing the number of shell corporations and enhancing transparency in corporate ownership.[^cite_note-132]&#x20;

#### Singapore

Singapore established the Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crimes (Confiscation of Benefits) Act to confiscate proceeds from serious crimes.[^cite_note-133]&#x20;

#### Switzerland

Switzerland strengthened its anti-money laundering framework through the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), which mandates due diligence and reporting requirements for financial institutions.[^cite_note-134]&#x20;
