**Abdelaziz Adam al-Hilu**[^cite_note-1] (born 7 July 1954) is a Sudanese politician who has been serving as the vice chairman of the [Government of Peace and Unity](79260861.md) since 2025. He is also the leader of the [Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North (al-Hilu)](30884032.md).

## Biography

Al-Hilu was born in Al-Faydh Umm Abdullah, [South Kordofan](767653.md). He was born, raised and educated in the [Nuba Mountains](1139195.md). He studied economics at the [University of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Khartoum), and graduated in 1979.&#x20;

He is considered one of the most successful SPLA/M commanders in the history of the SPLA and worked with Sudanese revolutionary leader [John Garang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Garang) with the stated goal of creating a [Sudan](27421.md) that is democratic, fair and free to all [Sudanese](27421.md).

In 2011 he lost the election for governor of South Kordofan to [Ahmed Haroun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Haroun) in a poll rejected by the SPLA as rigged.[^cite_note-2] He had been fighting the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) in the [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War) and the [South Kordofan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kordofan_conflict).

In mid-2017, the SPLM–N split into two factions, the SPLM–N (al-Hilu) and SPLM–N (Agar), over disagreements on secularization.[^cite_note-SudTrib_whySPLMN_split2-3] Following the [Sudanese revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_revolution), he declared a temporary unilateral ceasefire "to give the ongoing peace talks an opportunity for success",[^cite_note-4] which was later further extended,[^cite_note-5] during which he reached an agreement with the [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)) to separate religion and state and not discriminate against anyone's ethnicity on 3 September 2020.[^cite_note-6] He has also called to remove former president [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md)'s militias and to reorganize the Sudanese military, in addition to [self-determination](29269.md) in areas controlled by his faction.[^cite_note-7]

On 28 March 2021, al-Hilu signed a peace agreement with the Sudanese government in [Juba](1514879.md), South Sudan, in which it would pave the path to establish a civil, democratic federal state in Sudan, in addition to guaranteeing freedom of religion and having a single army to protect national security.[^cite_note-8]

During the [civil war in Sudan](73556699.md) which began in 2023, al-Hilu was hesitant to join the [Tagadum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagadum) coalition, insisting on [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular) governance as a foundation for Sudan's unity and stability. He also supported initiatives to end conflict but emphasized addressing Sudan's deep-rooted issues.[^cite_note-9] His forces in South Kordofan and Blue Nile prioritized defending citizens and ensuring aid reached civilians.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] In February 2025, al-Hilu supported the [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF)'s attempt to form a [parallel government](79260861.md) in [Nairobi](21482.md), [Kenya](188171.md).[^cite_note-13] In July 2025, he was proclaimed the deputy leader of a governing alliance headed by the RSF's leader [Hemedti](60669608.md).[^cite_note-14]
