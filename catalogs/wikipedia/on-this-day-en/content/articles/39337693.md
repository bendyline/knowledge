# Law of Italy



[Supreme Court of Cassation](3588041.md), [Rome](25458.md)

The **law of Italy** is the system of [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) across the [Italian Republic](14532.md). The Italian legal system has a plurality of sources of production. These are arranged in a hierarchical scale, under which the rule of a lower source cannot conflict with the rule of an upper source (hierarchy of sources).[^cite_note-1]

The [Constitution of 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy) is the main source.[^cite_note-auto-2] The [Italian civil code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_civil_code) is based on codified [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law) with elements of the [Napoleonic civil code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_civil_code) and later statutes. The civil code of 1942 replaced the original one of 1865.[^cite_note-auto-2] The penal code ("The Rocco Code") was also written under fascism (1930).

Both the civil code and the penal code have been modified in order to be in conformity with the current democratic constitution and with social changes.[^cite_note-auto-2]

## Legislative power



One of three original copies of the [Constitution of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy), now in the custody of Historical Archives of the [President of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Italian_Republic).

Article 117 of the [Constitution of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy) shares [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power), according to the concerned matters, between [Italian Parliament](1417859.md)[^cite_note-3] and [regional councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy#institutions). While a law ratified by the national Parliament is simply called *legge* and is enforced on the whole country, laws ratified by regional councils are named *leggi regionali* (regional laws) and can be applied only in the concerned region.[^cite_note-4] Parliament is competent to legislate for the matters expressly indicated in the second paragraph of Article 117, while the Regions are competent to legislate for the remaining matters (residual competence).[^cite_note-5]

There is also a second list of matters contained in the third paragraph of article 117 called matters of concurrent legislation, in which the Regions have legislative power, except for the determination of fundamental principles ([framework laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_law)), reserved to the State.[^cite_note-6]

The Government can also issue an act having the force of law (called *decreto-legge*, "law decree"), but this must be confirmed later by Parliament, under penalty of forfeiture of the law decree.[^cite_note-7] Furthermore, the Parliament can delegate the Government (through a law called *legge delega*, "delegation law", which must precede government regulation) to legislate on a certain subject, but at the same time establishes the margins within which the Government can move in legislating.[^cite_note-8] The normative act issued in this way by the Government takes the name of *decreto legislativo*, "legislative decree".[^cite_note-9] The power of legislative initiative is attributed to each parliamentarian, to the people, through the institution of the popular [law proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)), carried out by collecting at least 50,000 signatures, and to the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Italy\)), whose bills must however be countersigned by the [President of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Italian_Republic).[^cite_note-10]

Still in the sphere of legislative power, there are some cases in which it belongs to the sovereign people: through the institution of the abrogative [referendum](51582.md) and, in constitutional matters, through the institution of the confirmatory referendum of constitutional laws.[^cite_note-11] All laws must be promulgated by the President of the Italian Republic who can postpone it only once, otherwise he would have the right to veto a law in Parliament if he considers that it is in conflict with the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy).[^cite_note-12]

## Sources of law

In Italy the legal norms [originate from the following sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sources_of_law):[^cite_note-13]

- Internal sources:&#x20;

  1. [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy), which regulates the formation of laws and determines the discipline of regulatory acts;

  2. State and regional [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_law), as well as decrees (government acts issued when there is an urgent matter that requires timely intervention) and legislative decrees (acts issued by the government as delegated by the Parliament which establishes basic principles and indications that the government must follow to propose the law). More recently many laws were reorganised under different codes ([Italian law codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_law_codes)) and testi unici (unified law);

  3. Government regulation, issued by the Government, ministers and other administrative authorities;

  4. [Customary law](218798.md), a source subordinated to the law and which can operate only within the limits permitted by the law itself.

- International sources: norms adopted by a group of states on the basis of one or more international agreements, united in a supranational organization, which stand above the individual laws of each member state but which have no direct effect on citizens. They are called [treaties or agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty). In addition to written conventions, international customs must be included among the international sources.

- Acts of the [European Union](9317.md).&#x20;

  1. [European Union regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_\(European_Union\));

  2. [European Union directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_\(European_Union\)).

With regard to external sources art. 117 of the Italian Constitution: "Legislative power is exercised by the State and by the Regions in compliance with the Constitution, as well as with the constraints deriving from the community order and [international obligations](8195726.md)".[^cite_note-14]

## Private law

[Private law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) is that part of a [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_system) which is part of the *[jus commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jus_commune)* that involves relationships between individuals, such as the law of [contracts](19280537.md) and [torts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tort)[^cite_note-Mattei-15] (as it is called in the [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law)), and the [law of obligations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_obligations) (as it is called in [civil legal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\))). In Italian law, the main regulatory body for private law is the [Italian civil code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_civil_code), which governs both civil and [commercial law](77444.md).[^cite_note-16] The Italian civil code was approved with Royal decree no. 262 of 16 March 1942 and entered into force on 21 April of the same year.[^cite_note-17] It was born from the merger between the Italian Civil Code of 1865 and the Italian Commercial Code of 1882.[^cite_note-18] It is divided into six books, composed in turn into titles, chapters, sections, as well as 2,969 articles.[^cite_note-testolegge-19] The six books deal respectively of people and family, heritage, property, bonds, working and protection of rights.[^cite_note-testolegge-19]

## Public law

[Public law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_law) is the part of law that governs relations between [legal persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_person) and a [government](12229.md),[^cite_note-oxdic-20] between different institutions within a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), between [different branches of governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers),[^cite_note-PubLaw-21] as well as relationships between persons that are of direct concern to society. Public law comprises [constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law), [administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law) and [criminal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law).[^cite_note-oxdic-20]

### Constitutional law

[Constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law) is a body of law which defines the role, powers, and structure of different entities within a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), namely, the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) or [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), and the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary). The [Constitution of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Italian_Republic)[^cite_note-ItaConst-22] is composed of 139 articles (five of which were later abrogated) and arranged into three main parts: *Principi Fondamentali*, the Fundamental Principles (articles 1–12); Part I concerning the *Diritti e Doveri dei Cittadini*, or Rights and Duties of Citizens (articles 13–54); and Part II the *Ordinamento della Repubblica*, or Organisation of the Republic (articles 55–139); followed by 18 *Disposizioni transitorie e finali*, the Transitory and Final Provisions.

The Constitution primarily contains general principles; it is not possible to apply them directly. As with many written constitutions, only few articles are considered to be self-executing. The majority require enabling legislation, referred to as *accomplishment of constitution*.[^cite_note-23] This process has taken decades and some contend that, due to various political considerations, it is still not complete.

### Administrative law

[Administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law) is the division of law that governs the activities of [executive branch agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agency) of [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forms_of_government). In Italian law, the main regulatory body for private law is the Italian administrative process code, which was approved with legislative decree no. 104 of 2 July 2010.[^cite_note-edizionieuropee-24] It was issued in implementation of article 44 of the delegated law no. 69, which authorized the government to reorganize the administrative process, and entered into force on 16 September 2010.[^cite_note-edizionieuropee-24] It includes 137 articles.[^cite_note-edizionieuropee-24]

### Criminal law

[Criminal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law) is the body of law that relates to [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime). It prescribes conduct perceived as threatening, harmful, or otherwise endangering to the [property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property), [health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health), [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety), and [moral welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_spending) of people inclusive of one's self. Criminal law includes the [punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punishment) and [rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehabilitation_\(penology\)) of people who violate the penal laws. In Italian law, the main regulatory body for criminal law is the Italian penal code, which is one of the sources of Italian criminal law together with the Constitution and special laws.[^cite_note-25] The Italian penal code was approved with Royal decree no. 1,398 of 19 October 1930, entered into force on 1 July 1931[^cite_note-26] and has been amended several times. The Italian penal code is organized into three books, which are in turn divided into titles, chapters, sections, paragraphs and articles, as well as 734 articles.[^cite_note-officeadvice-27] The three books deal respectively of the crimes in general, of the crimes in particular and of the [misdemeanors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdemeanor) in particular.[^cite_note-officeadvice-27]
