**Long March 10B** (**LM-10B**, [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 长征十号乙; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Chángzhēng shí hào yǐ*, **CZ-10B**) is a [partially reusable](336014.md) [two-stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stage-to-orbit) [medium-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-lift_launch_vehicle) developed by China Rocket, the [commercial launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_use_of_space) subsidiary of [China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Academy_of_Launch_Vehicle_Technology) (CALT).[^cite_note-reuters-1][^cite_note-nyt-2] It is the third in the [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) series of rockets and is derived using technologies developed for the [Long March 10A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10#Long_March_10A_variant).[^cite_note-AJ-SN-31122025-4]

Similar to the heavy-lift [Long March 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_5), the CZ-10B stages have a five-meter diameter. Like other CZ-10 variants, the [kerolox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerolox) first stage is powered by seven [YF-100K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YF-100#Versions) engines. The second stage uses one [YF-219](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YF-219_\(rocket_engine\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) [methalox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methalox) engine. Like other 21st century Long March designs, the CZ-10B launches from China's southernmost [Wenchang Space Launch Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenchang_Space_Launch_Site).

The CZ-10B's maiden flight in July 2026 successfully achieved orbit and booster recovery, using a net-based catching system on a [floating landing platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_launch_vehicle_operations_platform) in the [South China Sea](74209.md).[^cite_note-STDaily-maidenFlight-5][^cite_note-6] This marked the first orbital booster recovery by the [Chinese space program](343674.md), the second country to achieve this after the United States, and the first recovery of any rocket using a net-based system.[^cite_note-wsj-7][^cite_note-8]

## Overview

The Long March 10B is closely related to the [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) family (from which the Long March 10B is derived), which is designed to fly the [Mengzhou spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzhou_\(spacecraft\)) to the moon.[^cite_note-sd-9]

In December 2025, a representative from China Rocket, the commercial arm of [CALT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Academy_of_Launch_Vehicle_Technology), presented plans for a commercial variant of the [Long March 10A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10#Long_March_10A_variant) during the [Wenchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenchang) International Aviation and Aerospace Forum 2025. This new variant, the *Long March 10B*, will have a payload capacity of at least 16 tonnes, likely in a reusable mode, to a 200 km low Earth orbit, and at least 11 tonnes to a 900 km orbit at 50 degrees orbital inclination.[^cite_note-AJ-SN-31122025-4]

In a July 2026 interview, Qian Hang, from the First Academy of the [China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Aerospace_Science_and_Technology_Corporation) (CASC), referred to the Long March 10B as the "younger sibling' of the [Long March 10A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10A). Long March 10A is designed to carry [crewed spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewed_spacecraft) to [low Earth orbit](47568.md) (LEO) and is a comprehensively upgraded evolution of the older [Long March 2F](340934.md) and [Long March 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_7) launchers. Thus, the Long March 10B is configured to gather flight data for the Long March 10A, thereby reducing development risks and enhancing the reliability of future crewed missions. In addition, the 10B variant will verify new technologies for the recovery. The Long March 10B will also reuse recovered first-stage boosters from Long March 10A missions and, by using a more powerful liquid methane-based second stage, generate greater lifting capacity than the latter. Unlike the Long March 10A, the 10B variant will be aimed at providing launch services for China's commercial space sector.[^cite_note-NSF-10072026-10][^cite_note-VOC-10072026-11]

## Design

The Long March 10B is a [medium-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-lift_launch_vehicle) at 10 m (33 ft) tall and 5 m (16 ft) in diameter. It features two stages, with the first stage powered by seven [YF-100K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YF-100) engines. The booster can deliver up to 16 t (35,000 lb) in recoverable configuration to [low Earth orbit](47568.md). It's primarily designed for cargo missions.[^cite_note-12]

The new Long March 10B inherits the first stage of the Long March 10A but reportedly will use a more powerful second stage that employs methane and liquid oxygen as fuel and oxidizer, respectively. There may also be an additional commercial variant tentatively named the Long March 10C.[^cite_note-NSF-25122025-13][^cite_note-WB-25122025-14] The first stage of the Long March 10B, just as the Long March-10A variant, will be recoverable via a "wire recovery apparatus" located on a recovery vessel after launch from the [Wenchang Commercial Space Launch Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HICAL) on [Hainan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan). The first stage of the rocket is equipped with hooks that will catch the wire network on the recovery ship; the tensioned wires are carried by rail-mounted dollies, which automatically position themselves to optimize the likelihood that the rocket will successfully "catch" the wires, akin to a fighter aircraft landing on an [aircraft carrier](2219.md).[^cite_note-AJ-SN-31122025-4][^cite_note-NSF-wires-15][^cite_note-ntg-16]

Net-based booster recovery differs from landing-leg systems primarily in the location of the recovery hardware. Reusable rockets like Falcon 9 carry the landing legs and reinforced support structures throughout the flight, whereas the Long March 10B places the recovery system and shock-absorbing equipment on the landing [barge](4177.md), leaving the booster with only lightweight hooks to catch [arresting wires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arresting_wire). This reduces booster mass, simplifies its structure, and increases payload capacity, while the larger capture area of a net could tolerate greater landing error in difficult maritime conditions.[^cite_note-ntg-16][^cite_note-17]

## Launches

### Debut launch



The Long March 10B first stage decelerates into the [floating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_launch_vehicle_operations_platform) net recovery system on its maiden flight, [South China Sea](74209.md), 10 July 2026.

The first launch of the Long March 10B took place at 4\:15 (UTC) on 10 July 2026. The upper stage successfully placed a satellite into its designated orbit.[^cite_note-Xihuan-News-18] The first-stage booster was successfully recovered using the wire-based recovery system fitted on the *Linghangzhe* (Navigator) recovery ship.[^cite_note-sd-9] Long March 10B is the first system in China and the fourth launcher system in the world to achieve first-stage recovery during an orbital or sub-orbital launch attempt, after [Falcon 9](2647515.md) (2015), [Starship](59118602.md) (2024), and [New Glenn](51585988.md) (2025). It is the first system to successfully recover a booster using a net-based system.[^cite_note-19] This was China's third attempt at rocket booster recovery in orbital flights, following [LandSpace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LandSpace)'s [Zhuque-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuque-3) and SAST's [Long March 12A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_12A).[^cite_note-20]
