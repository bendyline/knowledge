**Beta Pictoris b** (abbreviated as **β Pic b**) is an [exoplanet](9763.md) orbiting the young [debris disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debris_disk), [A-type main sequence star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-type_main_sequence_star), [Beta Pictoris](656713.md) located approximately 63 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year) (19.4 [parsecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsec), or 6×10<sup>14</sup> [km](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Km)) away from [Earth](9228.md) in the [constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) of [Pictor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictor). It has a mass of around 10 [Jupiter masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_mass) and a radius some 37% to 68% larger than [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter)'s. It orbits at 10 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit) from Beta Pictoris, which is about 3.7 times farther than the orbit of [Beta Pictoris c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Pictoris_c). It orbits close to the plane of the debris disk orbiting the star, with a low eccentricity and a period of 23.77 years.

## Physical characteristics

### Mass, radius and temperature

Beta Pictoris b is a [super-Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-Jupiter), an exoplanet that has a radius and mass greater than that of the planet [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter). It has a temperature of 1,629 K (1,356 °C; 2,473 °F),[^cite_note-Picos2026-7] most likely due to its dusty atmosphere and mass (normally it would be much colder). It has a mass of nine [Jupiter masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_mass) (M<sub>J</sub>),[^cite_note-Sutlieff2026-2] and a radius of either 1.37 or 1.680 [R<sub>J</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_radius), depending on the method used for modelling the planet's spectrum.[^cite_note-Stauffenberg2026-4] In 2018, a study directly measured the [astrometric perturbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_detecting_exoplanets#Astrometry) of Beta Pictoris by Beta Pictoris b, one of the first examples of an exoplanet being measured directly by its astrometric perturbation. Its mass was directly measured as 11±2 M<sub>J</sub>.[^cite_note-Snellen-8]

### Host star

Main article: [Beta Pictoris](656713.md)

The planet orbits an [A-type main sequence star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-type_main_sequence_star) named [Beta Pictoris](656713.md). The star has a mass of 1.79 [solar masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_mass) (M<sub>☉</sub>)[^cite_note-Sutlieff2026-2] and a radius of 1.53 [solar radii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_radii) (*R*<sub>☉</sub>).[^cite_note-Kammerer2025-9] It has a surface temperature of 8054 [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin)[^cite_note-Baburaj2026-10] and is only 23 million years old.[^cite_note-Mamajek2014-11] In comparison, the Sun is about 4.6 billion years old[^cite_note-12] and has a surface temperature of 5778 K.[^cite_note-13] It has a near solar [metallicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallicity), with a metal-to-hydrogen ratio equivalent to 102% of that found in the Sun.[^cite_note-Baburaj2026-10] Its luminosity ([L<sub>☉</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_luminosity)) is 7.2 times that of the Sun.[^cite_note-tycho-gaia-14]

The star's [apparent magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_magnitude), or how bright it appears from Earth's perspective, is 3.861.[^cite_note-Ducati2002-15] Therefore, it can be seen with the naked eye.

### Orbit

Beta Pictoris b orbits its host star every 23.77 years at a [semi-major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) of 10.0 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit)[^cite_note-Sutlieff2026-2] (about the same as [Saturn](44474.md)'s distance, which is about 9.55 AU). It receives 11% of the amount of [sunlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_flux) that Earth does from the Sun.[^cite_note-PHL-16]

The orbit of the planet is well aligned to the rotation of the parent star and debris disk, with misalignment measured to be 3±5 degrees in 2020.[^cite_note-17]

### Planetary rotation

In 2014, the projected rotational velocity of Beta Pictoris b was calculated from the broadening of its carbon monoxide infrared absorption line. This makes it the first extrasolar planet to have its rotation rate measured.[^cite_note-eso-18][^cite_note-Cowen2014-19] The rotational broadening corresponds to an equatorial velocity of 25±3 km/s, significantly faster than the planets in our Solar System.[^cite_note-Snellen2014-20] Assuming zero [obliquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) and a planetary radius 1.65±0.06 times that of Jupiter, this corresponds to a rotation period of 8.1±1.0 hours.[^cite_note-Snellen2014-20] A 2024 study using CRIRES+ gave a slightly lower rotation velocity of 19.9±1.0 km/s.[^cite_note-Landman2024-21] Using a radius value of 1.4±0.1 Jupiter radii, this corresponds to a rotation period of 8.7±0.8 hours, again assuming zero obliquity.[^cite_note-Landman2024-21]

If the planet has a significant obliquity, the true rotation period may be substantially shorter than these inferred values. While giant planets are expected to form with low obliquities, dynamical processes such as collisions or secular spin-orbit resonances induced by satellites may result in the planet's rotation being tilted.[^cite_note-Poon2024-22]

Observations of the planet with [GRAVITY+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRAVITY_\(Very_Large_Telescope\)) over a 7-hour timeframe indicate variability at roughly half the predicted rotation period, which may correspond to features at opposing longitudes rotating in and out of view, but further observations are needed to confirm this.[^cite_note-Stauffenberg2026-4] Another study, which monitors the planet's variability over a 16-hour timeframe with the [James Webb Space Telescope](434221.md), found a rotation period of 9.00±0.13 hours, suggesting that the planet's rotation is aligned with its orbital plane.[^cite_note-Zhou2026-6]

### Aurora

In 2026 a [pre-print](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-print) described radio emission at 0.85 to 3.5 [GHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHz) with the [MeerKAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeerKAT) array located in [South Africa](17416221.md). The researchers showed that the emission is inconsistent with the position of the star and with planet c. It is instead consistent with the position of planet b. The emission has two components. One is persistent emission. The other is rapid reocurring and highly [circularly polarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circularly_polarized) bursts, which come from [electron cyclotron maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_radio_emission#Electron-cyclotron_maser_emission), associated with an [aurora](49658.md). The researchers detected three [left-handed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality) bursts in the 2025 observation, with the first and last burst being around 8 hours apart, which may be a signature of rotational modulation. A fourth right-handed burst was detected in 2026. The researchers estimate a magnetic field of [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field#magnetic_flux_density)≥1.25 [Kilogauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogauss).[^cite_note-Ortiz_Ceballos2026-23]

## Discovery

The planet was discovered on November 18, 2008, by [Anne-Marie Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne-Marie_Lagrange) *et al.*, using the NACO instrument on the [Very Large Telescope](151648.md) at Cerro Paranal in northern [Chile](5489.md).[^cite_note-LagrangeGratadour2009-1] This planet was discovered using the [direct imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_detecting_exoplanets#Direct_imaging) technique, using reference star differential imaging. The discovery image was taken in 2003, but the planet was not detected when the data were first reduced. A re-reduction of the data in 2008 using modern image processing tools revealed the faint [point source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_source) now known to be a planet.

### Further studies

Follow-up observations performed in late 2009 and early 2010 using the same instrument recovered and confirmed the planet, but on the opposite side of the star. These findings were published in the journal *[Science](193513.md)*[^cite_note-LagrangeBonnefoy2010-24] and represented the closest orbiting planet to its star ever imaged. Observations performed in late 2010 and early 2011 allowed scientists to establish an [inclination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_inclination) angle of the planet's orbit of 88.5 degrees, nearly edge-on. The location of the planet was found to be approximately 3.5 to 4 degrees tilted from the main disk in this system, indicating that the planet is aligned with the warped inner disk in the Beta Pictoris system.[^cite_note-ChauvinLagrange2012-25]

The first study of the spectral energy distribution of the planet was published in July 2013.[^cite_note-BonnefoyBoccaletti2013-26] This study shows detections at 1.265, 1.66, 2.18, 3.80, 4.05 and 4.78 [μm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometre) demonstrating that the planet has a very dusty and/or cloudy atmosphere. The SED is consistent with that of an early [L dwarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_dwarf), but with a lower surface gravity. The effective temperature is constrained to 1700±100 [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin) and the surface gravity to log g = 4.0±0.5. A second study, published in September 2013,[^cite_note-Currie2013-27] provided a new detection at 3.1 μm obtained at the [Gemini Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_Observatory) along with a reanalysis of previous data. They found the planet to be overluminous in the [mid-infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared#Regions_within_the_infrared) 3.1 μm band compared to models of early L dwarfs. Models incorporating small dust particles and thick clouds provided the best fit to the SED. The effective temperature is constrained to 1600+50\
−25 K and the surface gravity to log g = 3.8±0.02. This fit corresponds to a planet radius of 1.65 times that of Jupiter, arguing that Beta Pictoris b may be younger than its host star (finished forming at 5 Ma).

In 2015, a short video was made from direct images of Beta Pictoris b taken by the [Gemini Planet Imager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_Planet_Imager) over the course of about two years showing a time-lapse of the planet orbiting around its parent star.[^cite_note-28] The orbit is close to edge-on and the planet had been suspected of causing a [transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_transit)-like event in 1981.[^cite_note-29] Nevertheless, a campaign to observe the [transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_transit) of the planet or of material within its [Hill sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_sphere) during the 2017–2018 transit window indicated that no transits are occurring.[^cite_note-Kenworthy2021-30] In 2018, the [PicSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PicSat) cubesat was launched to observe the potential transit, but failed several weeks after launch.[^cite_note-Kenworthy2021-30]

As of 2026, the orbital parameters and mass of Beta Pictoris b have been measured using a combination of data from [radial velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_velocity), [astrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrometry) and imaging, showing that it has about 8.7 times the mass of Jupiter with an orbital period of 23.77 years and a [semi-major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) of about 10.0 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit).[^cite_note-Sutlieff2026-2]

## Searches for exomoons

See also: [List of exomoon candidates](76049781.md)

The use of [astrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrometry) has set limits on the presence of [exomoons](2505466.md) around the planet. For short orbital periods (around 50 days), objects with masses over 0.6 M<sub>J</sub> are ruled out, while at periods of roughly 200 days, this limit is 0.3 M<sub>J</sub>. At orbital periods of 700 and 1,100 days, exomoons with masses over 0.15 M<sub>J</sub> and 0.10 M<sub>J</sub>, respectively, are ruled out.[^cite_note-Macias2026-31]

One 2026 study searched for exomoons by analysing [radial velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_spectroscopy) data from the CRIRES+ [spectrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrograph) aboard the [Very Large Telescope](151648.md), but none was revealed. Their detection limits for a single moon are a 80 M<sub>🜨</sub> object orbiting with a period of one day, and a 1 M<sub>J</sub> object orbiting with a period of 200 days. The non-detections in both radial-velocity and astrometric data rule out any exomoon over three times more massive than [Saturn](44474.md) in the [Hill sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_sphere).[^cite_note-Kenworthy2026-32]

A potential clue to the presence of exomoons would be if the planet has a substantial obliquity. Giant planets are expected to form with low obliquities, but this could be modified by collisions with other planets, or by secular spin-orbit resonances induced by exomoons. In the case of Beta Pictoris b, collisions capable of significantly altering the obliquity are considered unlikely, while dynamical interactions with satellites remain a viable possibility.[^cite_note-Poon2024-22] For example, a moon exceeding 15 Earth masses (roughly the mass of [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune)) on an orbit between 40 and 70 planetary radii would be able to excite the planetary obliquity to 60° on a timescale of roughly 1 million years.[^cite_note-Poon2024-22] Measurements indicate that the planet's equator is viewed edge-on, consistent with aligned rotation.[^cite_note-Zhou2026-6]
