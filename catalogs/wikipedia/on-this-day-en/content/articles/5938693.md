**Ukrainian Americans** are [Americans](19792942.md) who are of full or partial [Ukrainian](145144.md) ancestry. According to U.S. census estimates, in 2021 there were 1,017,586 Americans of Ukrainian descent representing 0.3% of the American population.[^cite_note-data.census.gov-1] The Ukrainian population of the United States is thus the second largest outside the former [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc); only [Canada](5042916.md) has a larger [Ukrainian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians) under this definition. According to the 2000 U.S. census, the [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_metropolitan_area) with the largest numbers of Ukrainian Americans are: [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) with 160,000; [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_metropolitan_area) with 60,000; [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area) with 46,000; [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Detroit) with 45,000; [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) with 36,000; [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland) with 26,000; [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California) with 20,000;[^cite_note-4] and [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_metropolitan_area) with 19,000.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] In 2018, the number of Ukrainian Americans surpassed 1 million.[^cite_note-7]

Despite the United States’s much larger total population, Ukrainian Americans are outnumbered in absolute numbers by their [Canadian counterparts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] They are one of the few, if not the only, ethnic groups in North America for which this is the case. In contrast, most other groups—including [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Canadians), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Canadians), [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Canadians), and [Indigenous](196075.md) communities—represent a larger share of the population in Canada but remain more numerous in the United States due to its much larger overall population.

## History



&#x20;[Distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maps_of_American_ancestries) of Ukrainian Americans, as a percentage of the population, according to the [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census).



The [New York City Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), including [Brighton Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Beach) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](8210131.md), and [Fair Lawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Lawn,_New_Jersey) in [Bergen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_County), [New Jersey](21648.md), is home to by far the largest Ukrainian population in the United States.

[^cite_note-10]

Large-scale Ukrainian immigration to America did not begin until the 1880s.[^cite_note-11] Between 1870 and 1914, the majority of Ukrainian immigrants came from [Austro-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungary) ([Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)) and other regions). They were described as [Ruthenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians), and many arrived in [New York City](645042.md) and [Pennsylvania](23332.md). In 1899 estimates of the number of Ukrainians in the US ranged from 200,000 to 500,000.[^cite_note-Ukrainians_in_the_United_States-12] The [Ukrainian National Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_National_Association) ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Український народний союз), known before 1914 as the Ruthenian National Union ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Руський Народний Союз) was founded in [Shamokin, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamokin,_Pennsylvania), on February 22, 1894.

Between 1920 and 1939, about 40,000 more Ukrainians arrived, mostly from [Western Ukraine](3258511.md).[^cite_note-Ukrainians_in_the_United_States-12] After World War II, about 85,000 Ukrainian [displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_persons) emigrated to the United States from Europe.[^cite_note-Ukrainians_in_the_United_States-12]

From 1955 to 1965, [St. Andrew Memorial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrew_Memorial_Church_\(South_Bound_Brook,_New_Jersey\)) in [South Bound Brook, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bound_Brook,_New_Jersey), was constructed as a memorial honoring victims of the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor) of 1932–1933.[^cite_note-13]

The largest wave of Ukrainians came in the early 1990s, after the 1991 [fall of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union). Some of those emigrating from Ukraine after the fall of the Soviet Union were [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ukraine) or [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Ukraine). Many Ukrainians of the newest immigration wave settled in large cities and regional centers, forming [ethnic enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_enclave). In addition, many Ukrainian Americans arrived by way of Canada, which has a larger [Ukrainian presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians).[^cite_note-14]

On September 11, 2001, 11 Ukrainian Americans perished at the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)) in [New York City](645042.md) during the [acts of mass terrorism](5058690.md) committed on that day. All of their names were listed and commemorated by [Ukrinform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrinform), the National News Agency of Ukraine, during the nineteenth anniversary of the attacks in 2020.[^cite_note-15]

Ukrainian Americans living in [Northern New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_New_Jersey) and the remainder of the [Northeastern United States](431669.md) have long been politically vocal about Ukrainian affairs, often traveling to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), to express their concerns.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In [Bloomingdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomingdale,_Illinois) ([near Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPage_County,_Illinois)) on September 21, 2015, [Filaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filaret_\(Denysenko\)), the Ukrainian Orthodox [Patriarch of Kyiv and All Rus'-Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Metropolitans_and_Patriarchs_of_Kyiv), consecrated the first North American monument to the [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity)'s "[Heavenly Hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_killed_during_the_Revolution_of_Dignity)".[^cite_note-18]

In February 2022, the Pastor Right Reverend Mitred Archpriest Philip Weiner, the leader of St. Josaphat's [Ukrainian Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Philadelphia) Church in [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), said that there were more than 40,000 Ukrainians in the Rochester metropolitan area, which would make it one of the largest Ukrainian American communities in the country.[^cite_note-19]

There are a large number of Ukrainian Protestants in the [Sacramento metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_metropolitan_area) who have organized support to those affected by the [invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Invasion_of_Ukraine) through their local congregations.[^cite_note-20]

It is estimated that during 2022–2023, around 300,000 Ukrainians have sought asylum in the United States through various means, making the U.S. the fifth-largest destination for [refugees of the Russian invasion of Ukraine](70199806.md), and also making Ukrainians one of the fastest-growing ethnic groups in the United States that don't originate from the American continent.[^cite_note-21]

## Religion

Most American Ukrainians are [Christians](18337522.md) (Orthodox, Catholics, Baptists, Evangelicals).[^cite_note-22]
