# United States special envoy

**United States special envoys**[^cite_note-1] or **special representatives** are senior officials of the [U.S. Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_State) representing the [federal government of the United States](195149.md). In contrast to [U.S. ambassadors](153068.md) who are assigned to lead a [diplomatic mission](6654039.md) overseas and are responsible for the United States' [bilateral relations](24296062.md) with a specific country, a special envoy is assigned a specific portfolio or broader issue.[^cite_note-AFSA_Special_Envoys-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] These officials will typically report directly to the [U.S. secretary of state](32293.md) and hold the rank of [ambassador](68651.md). On occasion, the [president of the United States](24113.md) will appoint a **special presidential envoy** who would be a [White House](33057.md) official, rather than a State Department official. Many special envoys do not require [United States Senate](24909346.md) confirmation.[^cite_note-5]

Many special envoy positions and offices are created and staffed by the direction of the president and the secretary, whose political or organizational management philosophies may not be shared by their successors.[^cite_note-6] As such, many of these positions may go unfilled upon assumption of office by successor presidential administrations, with their offices sometimes merged with or subsumed into other offices, or abolished altogether.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## History

According to the [Congressional Research Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Research_Service), President [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) set the precedent for making special, temporary diplomatic appointments in 1789 when he named [Gouverneur Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouverneur_Morris) as a private agent to establish normal diplomatic relations with British officials.[^cite_note-9] As the United States grew as a [global superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_superpower), the number of these temporary special appointments grew, particularly in the 20th century and 21st century.[^cite_note-CRS-10]
