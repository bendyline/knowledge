The **Fifth Hasina Ministry**, the 23rd [cabinet of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Bangladesh), was led by [Prime Minister of Bangladesh](461887.md) [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md). It was established following the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2024_Bangladeshi_general_election) held on 7 January 2024. The election results were declared on the same day, leading to the formation of the [12th assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Jatiya_Sangsad) in the [Jatiya Sangsad](577333.md). A cabinet comprising 43 members was subsequently sworn in.[^cite_note-1] On 5 August 2024, after days of [deadly unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student%E2%80%93People's_uprising) in the country, Hasina was given an ultimatum by [General](148537.md) [Waker-uz-Zaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waker-uz-Zaman), the Bangladesh [Chief of the Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Bangladesh\)), to resign.[^cite_note-2] Later the same day, she [resigned](77539154.md) and fled to India.[^cite_note-7] Her rule ended via self-imposed exile following [nationwide protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_\(2024\)).[^cite_note-17] The parliament was dissolved by the [President](404877.md) [Mohammed Shahabuddin](73010562.md) the following day.[^cite_note-18]

## Swearing-in ceremony



President Mohammed Shahabuddin administered the oath of office to newly appointed Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina at the Darbar Hall of Bangabhaban in Dhaka.

The fifth [oath of office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_office#Bangladesh) ceremony of [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md) as [Prime Minister of Bangladesh](461887.md) took place on January 11, 2024, following [her party](404861.md)'s victory in the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladeshi_general_election).[^cite_note-Hasina_wins_fifth_term-19][^cite_note-26] The oath of office was administered by [President](404877.md) [Mohammed Shahabuddin](73010562.md), marking the formation of the Fifth Hasina ministry and the [12th Jatiya Sangsad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Jatiya_Sangsad).

On 7 January 2024, the [Awami League](404861.md) won the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladeshi_general_election). They defeated the opposition under [GM Quader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GM_Quader) of the [Jatiya Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatiya_Party_\(Ershad\)). Awami League won 216 Seats while the opposition only won 11 Seats.[^cite_note-30] The election, however, was boycotted by all major political parties in [Bangladesh](3454.md).[^cite_note-31] The election only had a voter turnout of 41%, a 38 percentage point decrease from the [prior election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bangladeshi_general_election).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Hasina_wins_fifth_term_amid_low_voter_turnout-28] The Awami league has been accused for forging the opposition.[^cite_note-33]

The [12th Jatiya Sangsad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Jatiya_Sangsad) was formed.

They held their first session on January 30.[^cite_note-34]

### Later developments

Main articles: [July Uprising](78109234.md), [2024 Bangladesh quota reform movement](77355536.md), and [Non-cooperation movement (2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_\(2024\))

The mass uprising in [Bangladesh](3454.md) began as a [quota reform movement](77355536.md) to reform the [quota rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_rule) for government jobs, after the [Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh) invalidated the government's 2018 circular regarding job quotas in the public sector. It began in early June 2024 and was led by the [Students Against Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_Against_Discrimination). Coinciding with the first anniversary of the [Resignation of Sheikh Hasina](77539154.md), on 5 August 2025, the uprising received an acknowledgment with the announcement of the [July Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Declaration).[^cite_note-citation_no3-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The movement escalated into a full-fledged mass uprising after the government carried out [mass killings](135327.md) of protesters, known as the [July massacre](77480962.md), by late July.[^cite_note-scmp2Aug2024-38] [Amnesty International](18947898.md) blamed the government's "heavy-handed response" for causing the death of "students, journalists, and bystanders" and demanded that the Hasina-led "government to urgently end this repression."[^cite_note-\:23-39] [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) also called on foreign governments to urge Hasina to "end the use of excessive force against protesters and hold troops to account for human rights abuses."[^cite_note-\:24-40]

By early August, the movement evolved into a [non-cooperation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_\(2024\)), ultimately leading to the [resignation of Sheikh Hasina](77539154.md), who fled to India which triggered a [constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladesh_constitutional_crisis), leading to the formation of an [interim government](77721275.md) led by [Muhammad Yunus](548105.md) as the chief adviser.
