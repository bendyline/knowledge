The **2026 Men's FIH Hockey World Cup** was the 16th edition of the [Men's FIH Hockey World Cup](951198.md), the quadrennial world championship for men's national [field hockey](10886.md) teams organized by the [International Hockey Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hockey_Federation). It was held from 15 to 30 August 2026 in [Wavre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavre), Belgium and [Amsterdam](844.md), Netherlands.

Belgium and Netherlands were given the hosting rights in November 2022, beating a joint bid from [England](9316.md) and [Wales](69894.md) as well as a [South African](17416221.md) bid. It was the first time the men's event was co-hosted. This also marked Belgium's first and Netherlands' fourth time hosting. It was also the first time the men's and [women's world cup](71081645.md) were held as one joint tournament.

16 teams participated, for the third time after the expansion in 2018. Qualification was an amalgamation of the [FIH Pro League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_Pro_League), continental championships and the [World Cup Qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Men's_FIH_Hockey_World_Cup_Qualifiers). Qualification took place from December 2023 to March 2026.

[Germany](7032540.md) were the defending champions, having defeated [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_men's_national_field_hockey_team) 5–4 in a [shootout](42538249.md) after the match finished 3–3 in regular time in [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar). They defended their title after defeating [Spain](7091174.md) in the [final](84094071.md) by 1–0 and lifted their fourth title.[^cite_note-Final-1]

## Host selection

Main article: [2026 Men's FIH Hockey World Cup bidding process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Men's_FIH_Hockey_World_Cup_bidding_process)

The [International Hockey Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hockey_Federation) announced on 15 June 2022 that they had received the following five bids for the 2026 World Cup:[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

#### For only men's event

-  [England](9316.md) (in partnership with  [Wales](69894.md))

-  [Germany](11867.md) (withdrew)

-  [South Africa](17416221.md)

#### For combined men's and women's event

-  [Belgium](3343.md) and  [Netherlands](21148.md)

-  [South Africa](17416221.md)

Wavre, Belgium and Amsterdam, Netherlands were awarded the hosting rights on 3 November 2022.[^cite_note-4]

## Teams

### Qualification

Just as in 2018 and 2023, 16 teams competed in the tournament. Alongside hosts, [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_men's_national_field_hockey_team) and [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_men's_national_field_hockey_team), the top-ranked teams in the 2023–24 and 2024–25 seasons in the [FIH Pro League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_FIH_Pro_League) and the five continental champions received an automatic berth. Then 16 teams from all over the five continents competed for the last seven places at the World Cup Qualifiers.[^cite_note-FIHWorldCup2026-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Of the team who qualified, 14 had taken part in the previous edition.

**Returners**

- [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_men's_national_field_hockey_team) and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_men's_national_field_hockey_team) qualified after missing out in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Men's_FIH_Hockey_World_Cup).

**Absentees**

- [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_men's_national_field_hockey_team) and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_men's_national_field_hockey_team) did not advance, after making a sporadic appearance in 2023.

The highest ranked team not to qualify was [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_men's_national_field_hockey_team), ranked 17th, while the lowest ranked team to qualify was [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_men's_national_field_hockey_team), ranked 16th.

## Draw



The [Wagener Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagener_Stadium) in [Amstelveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelveen) hosted the draw.

The final draw took place at 15\:00 [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time) on 17 March 2026 at the [Wagener Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagener_Stadium) in [Amstelveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelveen), Netherlands.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Model and presenter, [Joyce Colfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Colfs?action=edit\&redlink=1), hosted the draw. Field Hockey players, [Naomi van As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_van_As), [Barbara Nelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Nelen) and [Teun de Nooijer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teun_de_Nooijer), alongside Dutch rapper [La Fuente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Fuente), were the guests who assisted the draw. The draw started with the co-hosts being placed into their respective groups held in their country, as well as the two remaining pot 1 teams, [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_men's_national_field_hockey_team) and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_men's_national_field_hockey_team), being pre-drawn into groups C and D respectively. The draw continued with, in order, pots 4, 3 and 2 being drawn, with each team selected then allocated into the first available group alphabetically. There were no restrictions.
