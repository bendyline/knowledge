The **ESPY Awards** (short for **Excellence in Sports Performance Yearly Awards**, and often referred to as the **ESPYs**) is an annual American awards show produced by [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) since 1993, recognizing individual and team athletic achievement and other sports-related performance during the calendar year preceding a given annual ceremony. From 2015 to 2019, and since 2021, the ceremony has aired live on sister [broadcast television network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_broadcaster) [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company), while ESPN continues to air simulcasts of the awards and reruns.[^cite_note-ABC2020-1] Because of the ceremony's [rescheduling](#Timing) prior to the 2002 event, awards presented in 2002 were for achievement and performances during the seventeen previous months. As the similarly styled [Grammy](44635.md) (for music), [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards) (for television), [Academy Awards](324.md) (for film), and [Tony Awards](54741.md) (for theater), the ESPYs are hosted by a contemporary celebrity; the style is lighter, more relaxed and self-referential than several other awards shows, with comedic sketches usually included.

## Charitable role

A portion of the proceeds from sales of tickets to the event goes to the [V Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Valvano#V_Foundation), a charity established by [collegiate basketball](768856.md) [coach](533149.md) and television commentator [Jim Valvano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Valvano) to promote [cancer research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_research). Valvano announced the creation of the charitable foundation during his acceptance of the [Arthur Ashe Courage Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe_Courage_Award) during the inaugural ESPY telecast on March 3, 1993, 55 days before Valvano's death from [metastatic adenocarcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinoma).

## Design

The ESPY Award statuette was designed and created by sculptor [Lawrence Nowlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Nowlan).[^cite_note-bmta-2] The statuette consists of a silver sphere, with the word "ESPY" engraved on it, mounted on a silver pedestal.

## Ceremonies

### Timing

Between 1993 and 2001, the ceremony was held each year in either February or March and was broadcast recorded on ESPN.

Between 2002 and 2019, from 2022 to 2023, and since 2025, the ceremony has been held on the second or third Wednesday of July, one day after the [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) [All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game), as it marks the only day of the year on which none of the [major North American professional leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) nor college sports programs have games scheduled. The [National Basketball Association](22093.md), [National Football League](21211.md), and [National Hockey League](21809.md) are not [in-season](690345.md), although the NBA's post-draft training camp [NBA Summer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Summer_League) occurs and NFL teams prepare for [training camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_camp_\(NFL\)), colleges are on [summer vacation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_vacation), and MLB does not contest games on the day following its all-star game. Therefore, major sports figures except for those in the [WNBA](57721.md), which is in-season; cycling, which has the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France); minor league baseball; and golf, where [The Open Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Open_Championship) usually starts that evening, are available to attend. The show historically aired on the subsequent Sunday four days later, although the results were reported publicly by [ESPN.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN.com). In 2024, the ceremony was held on the second Thursday of July.

In 2010, the ceremony was aired live by ESPN for the first time since 2003. In 2015, the ESPY Awards moved to network television, airing on ESPN's corporate sister network [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company).

### Location

The first seven editions of the ESPYs were held in [New York City](645042.md), in 1993 and 1994 at [Madison Square Garden](75129.md) and from 1995 through 1999, at [Radio City Music Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_City_Music_Hall). The awards relocated to [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip), [Nevada](21216.md), for two years beginning in 2000, and in 2002, moved to the Kodak Theater (now the [Dolby Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Theatre)) in [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), [California](5407.md). In 2006, it was announced that the awards would move in 2008 to the Nokia Theatre (now the [Peacock Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_Theater)), to be situated as the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) headquarters of ESPN at [L.A. Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A._Live), adjacent to the [Crypto.com Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto.com_Arena) in [Los Angeles](18110.md). The awards returned to Dolby Theatre in 2022.

In January 2026, it was reported that the ESPYs would return to New York City in order to coincide with [Fanatics Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanatics,_Inc.), as part of a partnership with [Michael Ratner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Ratner)'s newly launched Fanatics Studios to produce the ceremony.[^cite_note-3]

### Hosts

The ceremonies have been hosted by comedians, television and film actors, and athletes. American actor [Samuel L. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L._Jackson) has hosted the ceremony four times (in 1999, 2001, 2002, and 2009). Comedian [Dennis Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Miller), actor and singer [Jamie Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Foxx), and talk show host and comedian [Seth Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Meyers) have hosted the show twice.

## Awards

American professional golfer [Tiger Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Woods) is the most-awarded individual at the ESPYs, having received 21 awards.

### Extant

#### Cross-cutter categories

*Cross-cutter* awards are those for which the eligibility is not confined to those athletes participating in, or those events occurring in, any single or specific sport.

- [Arthur Ashe Courage Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe_Courage_Award), presented to the sports-related person or team, irrespective of gender or sport contested, adjudged to have made the most significant or compelling humanitarian contribution in transcendence of sports in a given year (presented since 1993)

  [^cite_note-college-5]

  [^cite_note-college-5]
