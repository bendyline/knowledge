**Bucks County** is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)) in the [Commonwealth of Pennsylvania](23332.md). As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 646,538,[^cite_note-2] making it the [fourth-most populous county in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Pennsylvania). Its [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) is [Doylestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doylestown,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-GR6-3] The county is named after the English county of [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire). The county is part of the [Southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Pennsylvania) region of the commonwealth.[^cite_note-4]

The county represents the northern boundary of the [Philadelphia metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_metropolitan_area).

To its southwest, Bucks County borders [Montgomery County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Pennsylvania) and [Philadelphia](50585.md), the nation's sixth-largest city. To its east, the county borders the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) and U.S. state of [New Jersey](21648.md). To its north, the county borders [Lehigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_County,_Pennsylvania) and [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_County,_Pennsylvania) counties in the state's [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley) region. The county is approximately 30 miles (48 km) southeast of [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), the state's third-largest city, and 40 miles (64 km) north of Philadelphia, the state's largest city.

## History

### Founding



[Mercer Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_Museum) in [Doylestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doylestown,_Pennsylvania)

Bucks County is one of the three original counties created by colonial proprietor [William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn) in 1682. Penn named the county after [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire), the county in which he lived in England; "Bucks" is the traditional abbreviation for the English county, which became the actual name of the Pennsylvania one. He built a country estate, [Pennsbury Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsbury_Manor), in [Falls Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania) in present-day Bucks County.

Some places in Bucks County were named after locations in Buckinghamshire, including Buckingham and [Buckingham Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania), named after the former [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of Buckinghamshire; Chalfont, named after [Chalfont St Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalfont_St_Giles), the parish home of William Penn's first wife and the location of the Jordans Quaker Meeting House, where Penn is buried; [Solebury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solebury,_Pennsylvania), named after [Soulbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soulbury), England; and [Wycombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycombe,_Pennsylvania), named after the town of [High Wycombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Wycombe).

Bucks County was originally much larger than it is today. [Northampton County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_County,_Pennsylvania) was formed in 1752 from part of Bucks County, and [Lehigh County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_County,_Pennsylvania) was formed in 1812 from part of Northampton County.

### American Revolutionary War

See also: [George Washington's crossing of the Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington's_crossing_of_the_Delaware_River)

General [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) and his troops camped in Bucks County as they prepared to cross the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) to take [Trenton, New Jersey](57843.md) by surprise on the morning of December 26, 1776. Their successful attack on Britain's [Hessian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_\(soldiers\)) forces was a turning point in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). The town of [Washington Crossing, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Crossing,_Pennsylvania) and [Washington Crossing Historic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Crossing_Historic_Park) were named to commemorate the event.

## Geography

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 622 square miles (1,610 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 604 square miles (1,560 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 18 square miles (47 km<sup>2</sup>) (2.8%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-5]

The southern third of the county between [Philadelphia](50585.md) and [Trenton, New Jersey](57843.md), often called Lower Bucks, resides in the [Atlantic Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coastal_Plain); it is flat and near sea level, and is the county's most populated and industrialized area.

Bucks County shares a western border with [Montgomery County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Pennsylvania), and also borders Philadelphia to the southwest, and [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_County,_Pennsylvania) and [Lehigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_County,_Pennsylvania) Counties to the north. From north to south, it is linked to [Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_County,_New_Jersey), [Hunterdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunterdon_County,_New_Jersey), [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_County,_New_Jersey) and [Burlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_County,_New_Jersey) Counties in [New Jersey](21648.md) by bridges.

[Tohickon Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohickon_Creek) and [Neshaminy Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neshaminy_Creek) are the largest [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) in Bucks County. Tohickon Creek empties into the river at [Point Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Pleasant,_Pennsylvania) and Neshaminy at [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon,_Pennsylvania) ([Bristol Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania)).

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county had a population of 646,538. The median age was 44.4 years. 20.3% of residents were under the age of 18 and 19.8% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 96.2 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 94.2 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-12]

As of the 2020 census, the racial makeup of the county was 82.0% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans), 4.1% [Black or African American](2154.md), 0.2% [American Indian and Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 5.4% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), <0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 2.6% from some other race, and 5.7% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race comprised 6.2% of the population.[^cite_note-Census2020PL-13]

The 2020 census reported that 89.7% of residents lived in urban areas, while 10.3% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-14]

The 2020 census recorded 245,587 households in the county, of which 29.4% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 55.8% were married-couple households, 15.2% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 23.2% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 23.9% of all households were made up of individuals and 11.6% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-12]

The 2020 census recorded 255,632 housing units, of which 3.9% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 76.2% were owner-occupied and 23.8% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 0.9% and the rental vacancy rate was 5.3%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-12]

### 2000 census

As of the 2000 census, there were 218,725 households, and 160,981 families residing in the county. There were 225,498 housing units at an average density of 371 per square mile (143/km<sup>2</sup>). 20.1% were of [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people), 19.1% [Irish](775859.md), 14.0% [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_people), 7.5% [English](18803164.md) and 5.9% [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) ancestry.

There were 218,725 households, out of which 35.30% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 61.20% were married couples living together, 8.80% had a female householder with no husband present, and 26.40% were non-families. 21.50% of all households were made up of individuals, and 8.10% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.69 and the average family size was 3.17.

In the county, the population was spread out, with 25.70% under the age of 18, 7.00% from 18 to 24, 30.70% from 25 to 44, 24.30% from 45 to 64, and 12.40% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 38 years. For every 100 females there were 96.30 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 93.40 males.

The median income for a household in the county was \$59,727, and the median income for a family was \$68,727. Males had a median income of \$46,587 versus \$31,984 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$27,430. About 3.10% of families and 4.50% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 4.80% of those under age 18 and 5.50% of those age 65 or over.

### Population growth

Growth began in the early 1950s, when [William Levitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Levitt) chose Bucks County for his second "Levittown". Levitt bought hundreds of acres of woodlands and farmland, and constructed 17,000 homes and dozens of schools, parks, libraries, and shopping centers. By the time the project was completed, the population of Levittown had swelled to nearly 74,000 residents. At the time, only whites could buy homes. This rule however, was soon overturned. Other planned developments included [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon,_Pennsylvania) and [Fairless Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairless_Hills). This rapid sprawl continued until the mid-1960s.

In the 1970s, the county experienced a second growth spurt as developers expanded in previously underdeveloped townships, including [Middletown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middletown_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania), [Lower Makefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Makefield_Township,_Pennsylvania), [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania), and [Newtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtown_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania) townships. [Tract housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tract_housing), office complexes, shopping centers, and sprawling parking lots continued to move more and more towards Upper Bucks, swallowing horse farms, sprawling forests, and wetlands. [Oxford Valley Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Valley_Mall) was constructed in Middletown, and became a retail nucleus in the county.

In the late 20th century, growth somewhat stabilized as development was completed in the county's historically underdeveloped areas, leaving little new area to be further developed.

Bucks County areas along the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) have surpluses of abandoned industry, so many municipalities have granted building rights to luxury housing developers. As the regions that began the suburban boom in Bucks County, such as Levittown, have aged, commercial strips and other neglected structures have been torn down and replaced with new shopping plazas and commercial chains. With rising property values, areas with older construction are undergoing a renaissance, and Central and Upper Bucks have continued to experience rapid growth, with many municipalities doubling their populations since the late 20th century.

## Economy



Aerial view of [Levittown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levittown,_Pennsylvania), c. 1959

The boroughs of [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol,_Pennsylvania) and [Morrisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrisville,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania) were prominent industrial centers along the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) during [World War II](32927.md). [Suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbs) development accelerated in Lower Bucks in the 1950s with the opening of [Levittown, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levittown,_Pennsylvania), the second such "Levittown" designed by [William Levitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Levitt).

Among Bucks' largest employers in the twentieth century were [U.S. Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel) in [Falls Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania), and the Vulcanized Rubber & Plastics and Robertson Tile companies in Morrisville. [Rohm and Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohm_and_Haas) continues to operate several chemical plants around Bristol. [Waste Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_Management,_Inc) operates a [landfill](211285.md) in [Tullytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullytown,_Pennsylvania) that is the largest receptacle of out-of-state waste in the US (receiving much of New York City's waste following the closure of Fresh Kills landfill in [Staten Island, New York](127062.md) 40 miles (64 km) away).

Bucks is also experiencing rapid growth in [biotechnology](4502.md), along with neighboring [Montgomery County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Pennsylvania). The [Greater Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Philadelphia) area consistently ranks in the top 10 geographic clusters for biotechnology and biopharma.[^cite_note-24] It was projected that by 2020, one out of four people in Bucks County would work in biotechnology.

### Tourism



Schofield Ford Covered Bridge over [Neshaminy Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neshaminy_Creek) in [Tyler State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_State_Park_\(Pennsylvania\)). Bucks County has 12 [covered bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covered_bridge), ten of which are still open to highway traffic, and two of which are located in parks and open to non-vehicular traffic.



[New Hope Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope_Railroad) in [New Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope,_Pennsylvania)

The county's northern regions, colloquially referred to as Upper Bucks, are known for their natural scenery, farmland, colonial history, and proximity to major urban areas, including [Philadelphia](50585.md), [New York City](645042.md), [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading,_Pennsylvania), and [Atlantic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City), each of which is within a two-hour driving radius.

Bucks County is home to twelve [covered bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covered_bridges). Ten are still open to vehicular traffic; two others, located in parks, are open only to non-vehicular traffic. All Bucks County bridges use the [Town truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_truss) design. Schofield Ford Bridge, in [Tyler State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_State_Park_\(Pennsylvania\)), was reconstructed in 1997 from the ground up after arsonists destroyed the original in 1991.[^cite_note-25]

Popular attractions in Bucks County include the shops and studios of [New Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope,_Pennsylvania), [Peddler's Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peddler's_Village) (in [Lahaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaska,_Pennsylvania)), [Washington Crossing Historic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Crossing_Historic_Park), [New Hope Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope_Railroad), Bucks County River Country and Bucks County Playhouse Theater (in New Hope). Rice's Market near Lahaska is a popular destination on Tuesday mornings. [Quakertown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakertown,_Pennsylvania) Farmer's Market (locally called "Q-Mart") is a popular shopping destination on weekends. The county seat of Doylestown has the trifecta of concrete structures built by [Henry Chapman Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Chapman_Mercer), including the [Moravian Pottery and Tile Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Pottery_and_Tile_Works), the [Mercer Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_Museum) and [Fonthill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fonthill_\(house\)), Mercer's personal home.

Southern Bucks, colloquially referred to as Lower Bucks, is home to two important [shopping malls](235723.md), [Neshaminy Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neshaminy_Mall), [Oxford Valley Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Valley_Mall), and [Sesame Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Place_Philadelphia), a family [theme park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park) based on the *[Sesame Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Street)* television series. Also within Lower Bucks County is [Parx Casino and Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parx_Casino_and_Racing) in [Bensalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bensalem_Township,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania), a [casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino) and [thoroughbred horse racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_horse_racing) track. The casino was built on the grounds of what was originally Philadelphia Park Racetrack. The complex includes the thoroughbred horse racing track, expansive casino, a dance club, numerous dining options, and the [Xcite Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xcite_Center).

## Arts and culture

### Fine and performing arts

Many artists and writers based in [New York City](645042.md) have called Bucks County home, settling mainly in the small stretch between [Doylestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doylestown,_Pennsylvania) and [New Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope,_Pennsylvania) and along the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River).

The county boasts many local [theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater) companies, including the long-established and recently reopened [Bucks County Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucks_County_Playhouse) in New Hope, Town and Country Players in Buckingham, ActorsNET in Morrisville, and the Bristol Riverside Theatre, a professional Equity theater in Bristol. The Bucks County Symphony, founded in 1953, performs in Doylestown throughout the year and the Bucks County Gilbert & Sullivan Society, founded in 2009, performs a Gilbert & Sullivan operetta with full orchestra each June.

The *[Wild River Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_River_Review)*, an online magazine that publishes in-depth reporting, works of literature, art, visual art, reviews, interviews, and columns by and about contemporary artists, photographers, and writers, is based out of Doylestown.

## Sports

### Rugby league

The [Bucks County Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucks_County_Sharks) [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) team played in the [AMNRL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMNRL) from 1997 to 2010.[^cite_note-35] They returned to play in the AMNRL in 2011, until the league's fold in 2014, when they subsequently joined the [USARL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USARL).[^cite_note-36]

### Little League

The county has a considerable history of producing [Little League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_League) baseball contenders. Since its inception in 1947, four of the seven Pennsylvania teams to compete in the [Little League World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_League_World_Series) in [Williamsport, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsport,_Pennsylvania) have come from Bucks County: [Morrisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrisville,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania) ([1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Little_League_World_Series)), [Levittown American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levittown,_Pennsylvania) ([1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Little_League_World_Series) and [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Little_League_World_Series)), and [Council Rock-Newtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtown,_Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania) ([2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Little_League_World_Series)). Two of these squads, Morrisville and Levittown (1960), went on to win the World Series title. In 2007, Council Rock Northampton won the PA State championship, and lost in the finals of regionals.

### PIAA

The county is a part of [PIAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Interscholastic_Athletic_Association)'s District I, and has seen many schools capture multiple state titles.

### American Legion baseball

In 1996, Yardley Western Post 317 won the American Legion National Championship.

Bristol Legion Post 382 won the 2011 American Legion State Championship.

### Horse racing

[Parx Casino and Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parx_Casino_and_Racing), formerly Philadelphia Park Racetrack and Keystone Racetrack, the former home of [Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_of_Thoroughbred_Racing) contender [Smarty Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smarty_Jones), is in [Bensalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bensalem,_Pennsylvania).

Like most of the Philadelphia suburbs, Bucks County was once a stronghold for the [Republican Party](32070.md). However, in recent years it has become more of a [swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_vote) county, like Pennsylvania at large.

In presidential elections, Bucks County has been caught up in the overall [Democratic](5043544.md) trend that has swept the Philadelphia area, although the trend in Bucks has been less pronounced than in Delaware and Montgomery counties. It went Democratic in every presidential election from 1992 to 2020, though by a margin of less than 5% in each except for 2008, with three of those eight elections decided by less than 2% (1992, 2012, and 2016). In 2024, [Donald Trump](4848272.md) flipped the county with a victory margin of 0.07% or 291 votes. Prior to this Republicans won the county all but thrice between 1896 and 1988 (except in 1912, 1936, and 1964). Unlike most other suburban Philadelphia counties, Bucks County consistently voted Democratic during the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) era, only voting Republican twice between 1856 and 1892, in 1860 and 1872.

The executive government is run by a three-seat board of commissioners, one member of which serves as chairperson. Commissioners are elected through [at-large voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality-at-large_voting) and serve four-year terms. In cases of vacancy, a panel of county judges appoints members to fill seats. The current commissioners are Diane M. Ellis-Marseglia (D) (chairwoman), Robert "Bob" J. Harvie Jr. (D) (Vice-chairman), and [Gene DiGirolamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_DiGirolamo) (R). The current terms expire in January 2028.[^cite_note-49] In 2012, four county employees were sentenced for compensating public employees for political work on behalf of Republican candidates.[^cite_note-50]

Bucks County is represented in the U.S. Congress by [Pennsylvania's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_1st_congressional_district),[^cite_note-51] formerly numbered as the [8th District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_8th_congressional_district). The district is represented by [Brian Fitzpatrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Fitzpatrick_\(American_politician\)) (R), making this district one of 18 nationwide to be held by a Republican and won by [Joe Biden](145422.md) in 2020.

In the 2016 elections, Democrats [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) (president), [Josh Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Shapiro) (attorney general), and [Joe Torsella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Torsella) (state treasurer) won Bucks County, while Republicans [Pat Toomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Toomey) (U.S. senator), Brian Fitzpatrick (U.S. representative), and John Brown (auditor general) won Bucks County in their respective races.[^cite_note-52]

The 2024 elections were mired in controversy in Bucks County, culminating in widespread condemnation of Bucks County Commissioner Diane Ellis-Marseglia (D), who was filmed on video stating that the "rule of law doesn't matter anymore in this country" in the context of a decision made by the county's Board of Elections to violate the PA State Supreme Court's ruling regarding which provisional ballots can and cannot be counted.[^cite_note-\:0-53]

In the early 2020s, school board elections in Bucks County became increasingly politicized amid ongoing culture-war debates over education policy.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] In recent elections, Democratic candidates made significant gains, reclaiming control of several school boards after sweeping Republican victories in 2021 that had led to controversial conservative policies.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

## Climate

### Piedmont Region

According to the [Trewartha climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification) system, the [Piedmont (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(United_States\)) section of Bucks County, which is located roughly northwest of [U.S. Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_1), has a Temperate Continental Climate with hot and slightly humid summers, cold winters and year-around precipitation (*Dcao*). Dcao climates are characterized by at least one month having an average mean temperature ≤ 32.0 °F (0 °C), four to seven months with an average mean temperature ≥ 50.0 °F (10 °C), at least one month with an average mean temperature ≥ 72.0 °F (22 °C) and no significant precipitation difference between seasons. According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system, the climate is a hot-summer, wet all year, [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfa*). During the summer months in the Piedmont, episodes of extreme heat and humidity can occur with [heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) values > 102 °F (39 °C). The average wettest month is July which corresponds with the annual peak in [thunderstorm](70807.md) activity.

During the winter months, episodes of extreme cold and wind can occur with [wind chill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_chill) values < −16 °F (−27 °C). The [plant hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_hardiness_zone) at Haycock Mountain, elevation 968 ft (295 m), is 6b with an average annual extreme minimum air temperature of −4.6 °F (−20 °C).[^cite_note-USDA-63] The average seasonal (Nov-Apr) snowfall total is between 26 and 36 inches (66 and 91 centimetres) depending on elevation and distance from the Atlantic Ocean. The average snowiest month is February which correlates with the annual peak in [nor'easter](402179.md) activity. Some areas of the Piedmont farther south and along the river below New Hope are in hardiness zone 7a, as is the Atlantic Coastal Plain region of Bucks.

### Atlantic Coastal Plain Region

According to the [Trewartha climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification) system, the [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain) section of Bucks County, which is located roughly southeast of [U.S. Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_1) has a Temperate Oceanic Climate with hot and slightly humid summers, cool winters and year-around precipitation (*Doak*). Doak climates are characterized by all months having an average mean temperature > 32.0 °F (0 °C), four to seven months with an average mean temperature ≥ 50.0 °F (10 °C), at least one month with an average mean temperature ≥ 72.0 °F (22 °C) and no significant precipitation difference between seasons. According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), this region has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*). During the summer months in the Atlantic Coastal Plain, episodes of extreme heat and humidity can occur with [heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) values > 110 °F (43 °C). The average wettest month is July which corresponds with the annual peak in [thunderstorm](70807.md) activity. During the winter months, episodes of extreme cold and wind can occur with [wind chill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_chill) values < −7 °F (−22 °C). The [plant hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_hardiness_zone) in Andalusia, Bensalem Twp, elevation 16 ft (4.9 m), is 7a with an average annual extreme minimum air temperature of 3.0 °F (−16 °C).[^cite_note-USDA-63] The average seasonal (Nov-Apr) snowfall total is between 24 and 26 inches (61 and 66 centimetres) depending on elevation and distance from the Atlantic Ocean. The average snowiest month is February which correlates with the annual peak in [nor'easter](402179.md) activity.

## Official seal

The traditional seal of Bucks County, Pennsylvania takes its design from the inspiration of the county's founder, [William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn). The center of the seal consists of a shield from the Penn family crest with a tree above and a flowering vine surrounding it in symmetric flanks. The seal has a gold-colored background and a green band denoting Penn as the county's first proprietor and governor.

In 1683, Penn's council decreed that a tree and vine be incorporated into the emblem to signify the county's abundance of woods. The seal was used in its official capacity until the Revolutionary War. The county government has since used the official Pennsylvania state seal for official documents. Today, the Bucks County seal's use is largely ceremonial. It appears on county stationery and vehicles as a symbol of the county's heritage. The gold emblem is also the centerpiece of the official Bucks County flag, which has a blue background and gold trim.
