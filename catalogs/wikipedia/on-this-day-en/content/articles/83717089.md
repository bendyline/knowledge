**Yu Deng** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 邓煜; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Dèng Yù*, born June 1989) is a Chinese mathematician and a professor of mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He is known for his contributions to [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [fluid dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_dynamics), and [probabilistic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_analysis_of_algorithms). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](10859.md) in 2026 for his derivation of the [Boltzmann equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_equation), his derivation of wave kinetic equations from nonlinear dispersive systems, and his development of probabilistic approaches to [non-linear dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_dynamics).

## Early life and education

Deng was born in June 1989[^cite_note-Cepelewicz-2026-1] in [Kaifeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaifeng), [Henan](166408.md), China.[^cite_note-2] His father was a [software engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineer), and his mother was a [gastroenterologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenterologist).[^cite_note-Cepelewicz-2026-1] He was raised in [Shenzhen](83368.md), Guangdong, and began participating in mathematics competitions in primary school.[^cite_note-3]

As a child, Deng excelled in tournaments of the Chinese strategy board game [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_\(game\)).[^cite_note-Searcy-2026-4] In 2001, while in middle school, he competed professionally but failed to qualify for the national Go tournament in China. He later recalled, "Either Go or math. Since I lost, I decided to move my focus to math."[^cite_note-Cepelewicz-2026-1] As a first-year student at [Shenzhen Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Senior_High_School), he won first prize at the 2006 [Chinese Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Mathematical_Olympiad).[^cite_note-5] He then represented China in the 2006 [International Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Olympiad), where he won a gold medal at age 16.[^cite_note-Searcy-2026-4]

Granted exemption from the [Gaokao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaokao), Deng was admitted to [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University) in 2007, the same year as [Hong Wang](79379843.md).[^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-2026-6] He was an undergraduate there for two years until June 2009, when he transferred to the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](18879.md) (MIT), where he graduated with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics in 2011.[^cite_note-Yu_Deng-2025-7] As an undergraduate at MIT, Deng developed an interest in partial differential equations and, after working on a problem given to him by [Gigliola Staffilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigliola_Staffilani), published a paper on the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Schr%C3%B6dinger_equation) in the journal *Analysis & Partial Differential Equations*.[^cite_note-Quanta2026-8]

In 2015, Deng earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^cite_note-Yu_Deng-2025-7] As a doctoral student at Princeton, Deng won a Jacobus Fellowship, the university's highest honor for graduate students.[^cite_note-9] His doctoral dissertation, completed under [Alexandru D. Ionescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_D._Ionescu), was titled, "Long time behavior of some nonlinear dispersive equations".[^cite_note-Yu_Deng-2025-7] Ionescu recalled Deng as "brilliant from the beginning...even as a graduate student, without experience...he was very good at getting at the essence of the thing, picking up what matters in a problem."[^cite_note-Quanta2026-8]

## Career

After receiving his doctorate, Deng was an instructor at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at New York University from 2015 to 2018. He became an assistant professor of mathematics at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) in 2018 and a full professor there in April 2024.[^cite_note-Yu_Deng-2025-7] In September 2024, Deng joined the faculty of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) as an associate professor of mathematics and was promoted to a full professorship in June 2025.[^cite_note-Yu_Deng-2025-7]

## Academic research

Deng works in [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation) and [mathematical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physics), particularly on probabilistic and combinatorial methods for nonlinear wave and particle systems. With [Andrea Nahmod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Nahmod) and Haitian Yue, he developed tools known as random tensors to study rough random initial data for the two-dimensional [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Schr%C3%B6dinger_equation), proving invariance of the associated [Gibbs measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_measure). With Zaher Hani at the University of Michigan, he subsequently used combinatorial expansions of interacting waves to rigorously derive the wave kinetic equation from the Schrödinger equation over the time scales predicted by physics.[^cite_note-Quanta2026-8]

Deng and his collaborators adapted these methods to systems of [hard spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_sphere). They derived[^cite_note-10] the [Boltzmann equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_equation) from Newtonian particle dynamics over long time scales, overcoming the repeated-collision problem that had restricted earlier results to very short times. For a periodic-box model, their work combined with earlier Boltzmann-to-[Navier–Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_equations) limits to establish a rigorous passage from microscopic particle motion to macroscopic fluid behaviour in a major case of [Hilbert's sixth problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_sixth_problem).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## Awards

In 2021, Deng received a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in recognition of outstanding early-career research.[^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-2026-6] In 2025, he was awarded the [Oberwolfach Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberwolfach_Prize) for "his outstanding achievements in analysis and applied mathematics".[^cite_note-14] In 2026, he was awarded the [Clay Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Research_Award) for "remarkable derivation of the Boltzmann equation for long times, starting from a system of hard spheres".[^cite_note-15]

On 23 July 2026, Deng was awarded the [Fields Medal](10859.md) alongside [Hong Wang](79379843.md), [John Pardon](48651594.md), and [Jacob Tsimerman](48497888.md) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](627842.md) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md).[^cite_note-nytimes-16] He and Hong Wang are the first Chinese citizens to be awarded the Fields Medal.[^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-2026-6]

> For his work in partial differential equations, including the rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics for rarefied gases, the derivation of wave kinetic equations from nonlinear dispersive systems, and probabilistic approaches to nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics.

— [International Mathematical Union](14868.md) short citation, [Fields Medal](10859.md)
