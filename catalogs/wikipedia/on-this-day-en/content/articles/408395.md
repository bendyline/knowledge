The **Barbados Labour Party** is a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) in [Barbados](3455.md). It has served as the [governing party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governing_party) of Barbados from 1954 to 1961, 1976 to 1986, 1994 to 2008, and since 2018.&#x20;

The current [Party Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leader) is [Prime Minister](450163.md) [Mia Amor Mottley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Amor_Mottley), [SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_counsel), [MP](98066.md) while the Chairman is [Reginald Farley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Farley), [FB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Freedom_of_Barbados) and General-Secretary is [Dr. Jerome Walcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Walcott), FB, [FRCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Royal_Colleges_of_Surgeons). [Santia Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santia_Bradshaw), MP is [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deputy_prime_ministers_of_Barbados) and Leader of Government Business in [The House of Assembly](3629496.md), [The Parliament of Barbados](2555282.md).&#x20;

Former party leaders include [Sir Grantley Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantley_Herbert_Adams), [Dr. Hugh Gordon Cummins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Gordon_Cummins), [Sir Harold Bernard St. John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Bernard_St._John), [Tom Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.M.G._\(Tom\)_Adams), [Sir Henry Forde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_de_Boulay_Forde) and [Professor Owen Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Arthur), [PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)).&#x20;

Like Barbados' other major party, the [Democratic Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Labour_Party_\(Barbados\)) or the "Dems", the BLP has been broadly described as a [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) [social-democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy) party, with local politics being largely personality-driven and responsive to contemporary issues and the state of the economy. However, the party distinguishes itself by being rooted in [Asquithian Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._H._Asquith) policies, including a focus on trade as a way of bolstering economic growth over the creation of social services.[^cite_note-auto-1]

The BLP is a former observer member of the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International).[^cite_note-2]

## History

Originally called the **Barbados Progressive League** until 1944, the party was founded on 31 March 1938 at the home of James Martineau. During the first meeting, Chrissie Brathwaite and [Grantley Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantley_Adams) were elected as chairman and vice-chairman, respectively. Adams had entered the House of Assembly in 1934 partly through his deconstruction of the labour-focused efforts of the [Charles Duncan O'Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Duncan_O'Neal)'s Democratic League,[^cite_note-3] but this new party turned to organizing the political movement brought on by the [unrest of 1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indian_labour_unrest_of_1934%E2%80%9339) that he had earlier opposed. As such, their objectives included many of the league's original goals, such as [adult suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), free education, and better housing and health care.[^cite_note-4]

The BLP first participated in general elections in 1940. In 1994, [Owen Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Arthur) became the prime minister as leader of the Barbados Labour Party. In the [2003 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Barbadian_general_election) the BLP won 23 out of the 30 seats. The number increased to 24 in 2006, when in an almost unprecedented development the leader of the opposition, after a bitter and tumultuous internal battle within his own party, resigned the post and joined the governing party.



Grantley Adams House, the party's current headquarters, Bridgetown

The Barbados Labour Party governed from 1994 to 2008, which was commonly called the "Owen Arthur Administration". Prime Minister Arthur was chosen from among leaders around the globe to deliver the [William Wilberforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wilberforce) lecture on the 200th anniversary of the [Abolition of the Atlantic Slave Trade Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Trade_Act_1807).

The party lost power in the [2008 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Barbadian_general_election), winning 10 seats against 20 for the [Democratic Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Labour_Party_\(Barbados\)) (DLP).[^cite_note-5] After the election, Arthur stepped down as BLP leader and was replaced by former deputy prime minister [Mia Mottley](15337866.md) in a leadership election against [Attorney-General of Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Barbados) [Dale Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Marshall_\(politician\)). Mottley also became opposition leader.[^cite_note-6]

In the summer of 2008 Hamilton Lashley, MP for St. Michael South East, resigned from the party to become an independent candidate in the [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_\(Barbados\)). He was thereafter given a job by the DLP, the party he had belonged before crossing the floor to the BLP, as a consultant on poverty. This move by the member reduced to nine the number of seats the Barbados Labour Party had in the House.

After a decade in opposition, the BLP returned to power [on 25 May 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Barbadian_general_election) under Mia Mottley, who became Barbados's first female prime minister.[^cite_note-7] The party originally won all of the seats in the House of Assembly, but Bishop [Joseph Atherley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Atherley), the MP for St. Michael West, became an independent MP (later founder and leader of the [People's Party for Democracy and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Democracy_and_Development)[^cite_note-8]) and the [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_opposition) on 2 June 2018.[^cite_note-9]

In January 2022, the BLP obtained a landslide victory, winning all 30 legislative seats, in the first general [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Barbadian_general_election) since Barbados became a republic in 2021.[^cite_note-10]

In February 2024, BLP MP [Ralph Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Thorne) left the party, crossing the floor and becoming Leader of the Opposition. He joined the DLP shortly thereafter, becoming the party's leader its the first MP since 2018.[^cite_note-11]

In the [2026 Barbadian general election](71208378.md) on 11 February, the BLP again won all 30 seats in the House of Assembly.[^cite_note-12]

## Branches

The women's branch of the Barbados Labour Party is called the Women's League. The youth branch is called the League of Young Socialists.
