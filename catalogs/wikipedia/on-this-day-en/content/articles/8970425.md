The **European** **Commissioner for Enlargement[^cite_note-Note_1-1]** is a member of the [European Commission](9974.md) responsible for overseeing the [accession process of prospective new member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_European_Union) of the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) and leading the [Directorate-General for Enlargement and the Eastern Neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate-General_for_Neighbourhood_and_Enlargement_Negotiations) (DG ENEST).

As of 2025, there are nine official [candidate countries for EU membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_enlargement_of_the_European_Union): [Albania](738.md), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Georgia](48768.md), [Moldova](19260.md), [Montenegro](20760.md), [North Macedonia](23564616.md), [Serbia](29265.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md). In addition, [Kosovo](17391.md) is recognized as a potential candidate.[^cite_note-2] Bosnia and Herzegovina was most recently granted candidate status in December 2022.[^cite_note-3]

Since 2024, the Commissioner’s responsibilities have been limited strictly to enlargement policy and the Eastern Neighbourhood, following the creation of a separate Directorate-General for the Middle East, North Africa and the Gulf (DG MENA), led by the newly created Commissioner for the Mediterranean. Previously, the role also covered relations with the EU’s southern neighbourhood through the [European Neighbourhood Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Neighbourhood_Policy).

The current Commissioner is [Marta Kos](77809600.md), who has held the office in the [von der Leyen II Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_Commission_II) since 1 December 2024.

## History

[Günter Verheugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCnter_Verheugen) (1999–2004) was the first Commissioner to hold responsibility for Enlargement. His tenure oversaw the preparations for the historic [2004 enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union), which brought ten new countries into the European Union: [Cyprus](5593.md), the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Estonia](28222445.md), [Hungary](13275.md), [Latvia](17514.md), [Lithuania](17675.md), [Malta](19137.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), and [Slovenia](27338.md).

[Olli Rehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olli_Rehn) (2004–2010) succeeded Verheugen, and supervised the [accession of Romania and Bulgaria in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_enlargement_of_the_European_Union), the granting of candidate status to [Croatia](5573.md) (2004) and [North Macedonia](23564616.md) (2005), and the opening of accession talks with [Montenegro](20760.md) following its [independence in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Montenegrin_independence_referendum). He was also active in negotiations related to [Turkey](11125639.md)’s candidacy and the status of [Kosovo](17391.md).

[Štefan Füle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0tefan_F%C3%BCle) (2010–2014) followed Rehn. Füle’s most notable achievement was overseeing the [accession of Croatia in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_enlargement_of_the_European_Union), making it the EU’s 28th member state. His mandate also placed strong emphasis on the [Eastern Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Partnership) and the EU’s relations with countries on its eastern borders.

[Johannes Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Hahn) (2014–2019) took office as the enlargement agenda slowed. During his term, accession negotiations with the [Western Balkans](4829.md) remained the central focus, while relations with Turkey became increasingly strained.&#x20;

[Olivér Várhelyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliv%C3%A9r_V%C3%A1rhelyi) (2019–2024) served during a period when the enlargement process regained strategic importance, particularly in light of geopolitical tensions following [Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). Under his tenure, [Ukraine](31750.md), [Moldova](19260.md), and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) were granted candidate status in 2022.

[Marta Kos](77809600.md) (2024–present) was appointed on 1 December 2024.&#x20;
