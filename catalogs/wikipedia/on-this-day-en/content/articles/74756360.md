The **Ottawa Charge** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Charge d'Ottawa*) are a professional [ice hockey](14790.md) team based in [Ottawa](22219.md) that competes in the [Professional Women's Hockey League](74699992.md) (PWHL). They are one of the league's six charter franchises. The Charge play home games at the [Canadian Tire Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Tire_Centre) in [Kanata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanata,_Ontario).

## History

### Founding and first two seasons

On August 29, 2023, it was announced that one of the PWHL's first six franchises would be located in Ottawa.[^cite_note-1] [Michael Hirshfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hirshfeld), former executive director of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) Coaches' Association, was named the team's general manager, and [TD Place Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD_Place_Arena), its home venue.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] On September 15, [Carla MacLeod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_MacLeod), a former member of the [Canadian national team](1329942.md) and the head coach of the [Czech women's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_women's_national_ice_hockey_team), was named Ottawa's first head coach.[^cite_note-4] Each PWHL team was permitted three signings during the free-agency period, ahead of the [2023 PWHL Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PWHL_Draft). Ottawa signed Canadian national team players [Emily Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Clark_\(ice_hockey\)), [Brianne Jenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brianne_Jenner), and [Emerance Maschmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerance_Maschmeyer) on September 5, 2023.[^cite_note-sn20230905-5] Each signed a three-year deal lasting through to the 2025–26 season.[^cite_note-6] Next, 15 players were selected in the league's September 18 draft, with Ottawa's first pick being [American national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_ice_hockey_team) member [Savannah Harmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_Harmon).[^cite_note-7] On October 17, it was announced that [Haley Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haley_Irwin) and [Cassea Schols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassea_Schols?action=edit\&redlink=1) would be assistant coaches and [Pierre Groulx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Groulx) would be the goaltending coach.[^cite_note-8]

The team colours and jerseys were officially revealed on November 14, 2023, as red, 'storm' grey, and white.[^cite_note-9]



Ottawa's [Hayley Scamurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayley_Scamurra) celebrating a goal in 2024.

Ahead of the start of the inaugural 2023–24 season, Ottawa announced that Jenner would serve as team captain, with Clark and [Jincy Roese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jincy_Roese) serving as alternate captains.[^cite_note-10] The first game in franchise history took place on January 2, 2024, when Ottawa hosted [PWHL Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PWHL_Montreal) at the TD Place Arena. The game set a new attendance record for a professional women's hockey game at 8,318.[^cite_note-\:0-11] [Hayley Scamurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayley_Scamurra) scored the first goal in franchise history to give the hosts a 1–0 lead; however, Montreal would go on to win in overtime with a 3–2 score.[^cite_note-\:0-11] Ottawa secured its first win at its second game, a 5–1 win against [PWHL Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PWHL_Toronto) on January 13.[^cite_note-12] Its first win at home came on January 23, also against Toronto.[^cite_note-13] Ottawa would go on to miss the inaugural PWHL playoffs, eliminated from contention with a 5–2 loss against Toronto in the last game of the season.[^cite_note-14] However, despite missing the playoffs, Ottawa led the PWHL in attendance during its inaugural season.[^cite_note-\:1-15] Ottawa's finish meant they were awarded the second overall selection in the [2024 PWHL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_PWHL_draft).[^cite_note-16]

The 2024–25 season marked a major breakthrough for the team in their second PWHL campaign. Finishing third in the regular season with a 12–2–4–12 record, they surged into the playoffs and upset the top-seeded [Montréal Victoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montr%C3%A9al_Victoire) in a dramatic semifinal series that included a four-overtime thriller. Led by rookie goaltender Gwyneth Philips, who posted a stellar .952 save percentage and was named Ilana Kloss Playoff MVP,[^cite_note-17] Ottawa advanced to their first Walter Cup Final. Despite taking Game 1, the Charge fell to the [Minnesota Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Frost) in four tightly contested games that all ended in overtime. The season showcased Ottawa's rise as a gritty, defensively strong contender with a bright future.

## Future

On November 7, 2025, [Ottawa City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_City_Council) approved the proposal to demolish [TD Place Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD_Place_Arena) and build a new arena as part of the Lansdowne 2.0 redevelopment project. This plan garnered much criticism from the Charge and the PWHL due to a significant reduction in seating capacity from 8,500 to 5,850 seats.[^cite_note-18] In comparison, the average fan attendance for Charge home games during the [2024–25 PWHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_PWHL_season) was 6,768, rising to 8,348 for weekend regular season games.[^cite_note-\:2-19]

PWHL executives [Jayna Hefford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayna_Hefford) and [Amy Scheer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Scheer) delegated to city council on October 29 regarding their opposition to the plan.[^cite_note-20] In an interview with the [Ottawa Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Citizen), Scheer stated that they had "been having conversations for well over a year talking about capacity being an issue with both the Mayor and [OSEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Sports_and_Entertainment_Group)". Ottawa mayor [Mark Sutcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Sutcliffe) argued that increasing the capacity of the new arena would not be economically viable as the team is expected to outgrow it in short time. He suggested relocating to a larger arena in the metropolitan area, such as the [Canadian Tire Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Tire_Centre), but the league and fanbase had been adamant that the team remain in the central part of the city.[^cite_note-\:2-19] the team would eventually decide to make the move to the Canadian Tire Centre for the 2026-27 season.[^cite_note-21] With the planned construction of a new [arena at LeBreton Flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBreton_Flats_arena) a move has been speculated as the future of the Canadian Tire Centre is uncertain, but no deal has been made.

## Team identity



Uniform worn by [PWHL Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_PWHL_Ottawa_season) in the league's inaugural season



Inaugural logo as PWHL Ottawa

Like all charter PWHL franchises, Ottawa operated without unique branding for the league's inaugural season—the team wore a league-wide jersey template that featured the city's name diagonally on the front, and was known as PWHL Ottawa.[^cite_note-22] The team did have its own colour scheme, featuring red and grey. The league registered a trademark in October 2023 for the name Ottawa Alert, seemingly in homage to the early 20th-century women's "world series" champions the [Ottawa Alerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_women's_ice_hockey_history#Ottawa_Alerts).[^cite_note-23] However, Ottawa was ultimately given the name Charge when franchise names were unveiled by the PWHL in September 2024. The name Charge references Ottawa's motto, "Advance—Ottawa—En Avant".[^cite_note-\:02-24] *[The Hockey News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hockey_News)* reported that other names in contention for Ottawa included Advance—another clear reference to the motto—and Guard.[^cite_note-25] In addition to the Charge name, the league unveiled the team's logo, an in-motion unfinished circle that references "OC" initials; yellow was also added to the team's colour scheme.[^cite_note-\:02-24]
