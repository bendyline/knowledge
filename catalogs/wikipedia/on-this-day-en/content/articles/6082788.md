**Dahieh** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الضاحية الجنوبية, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'the southern suburb', French: *Banlieue Sud de Beyrouth, Dâhiye de Beyrouth*) is a predominantly [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims) suburb in the south of [Beirut](37428.md), in the [Baabda District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baabda_District) of [Lebanon](17771.md). It has a minority of [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Sunni_Muslims), [Christians](4796145.md), and a [Palestinian](23267.md) refugee camp with 20,000 inhabitants. It is a residential and commercial area with malls, stores and [souks](556904.md),[^cite_note-\:1-1] and comprises several towns and municipalities,[^cite_note-\:0-2] including [Ghobeiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghobeiry), [Haret Hreik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haret_Hreik), [Bourj el-Barajneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourj_el-Barajneh), [Ouzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouzai,_Lebanon?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Hay El-Saloum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_Al-Saloum?action=edit\&redlink=1). It is north of [Rafic Hariri International Airport](12869180.md), and the M51 freeway that links Beirut to the airport passes through it.

Dahieh is the Beirut stronghold of Lebanese political party and [paramilitary](146730.md) group [Hezbollah](13919.md), and it had large [auditoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium) in [Haret Hreik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haret_Hreik), [Hadath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadath,_Mount_Lebanon) and [Bourj el-Barajneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourj_el-Barajneh), where Hezbollah followers gathered on special occasions.[^cite_note-3] The area was severely bombed by Israel in the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) and in the [Israel–Hezbollah conflict (2023–present)](75005425.md). [Hassan Nasrallah](313436.md), the [Secretary-General of Hezbollah](9364927.md), was [ killed in an Israeli airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hezbollah_headquarters_strike) in 2024.[^cite_note-4]

## Demographics

Dahieh is home to one of the most densely populated communities in Lebanon. In 1986 the number of Shia living in Dahieh was estimated to be 800,000.[^cite_note-Odd_Karsten_Tveit_Goodbye_Lebanon-5]

## History

In the 14th century, there was a sizeable [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims) community at [Bourj Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourj_el-Barajneh). The community was first mentioned in a decree that the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) viceroy issued against the Shiites of Beirut and the surrounding areas in 1363, demanding that they stop practicing [Shia](26961.md) rituals.[^cite_note-6] In Ottoman [tapu tahrir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapu_tahrir) tax records of 1545, Bourj had a population of 169 households, 11 bachelors and one [imam](15240.md), all Shia Muslims. Shia of Bourj were also identified in [al-Duwayhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istifan_al-Duwayhi)'s writings in 1661, and the town was then known as Burj Beirut (lit. "the tower of Beirut").[^cite_note-Harris-7]

Prior to the start of the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) in 1975, Dahieh was one of the increasingly urbanized rural settlements outside of Beirut, with a mixed community of [Christians](4796145.md) and Shia Muslims. From 1920 to 1943 many Shia moved to Dahieh from [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md) and [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md), where the [French mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon) cracked down on Shiite anti-French rebels in June 1920. More Shiites arrived in the early 1960s escaping financial hardship and state neglect of the [countryside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countryside). By the start of 1975, 45% of Lebanese Shiites were living in [Greater Beirut](42710804.md).[^cite_note-Harris-7]

Dahieh's population further increased during the war. By 1976, around 100,000 Shia had been displaced from the [East Beirut canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Beirut_canton) following sectarian violence in the [Black Saturday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Saturday_\(Lebanon\)) and [Karantina massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karantina_massacre). [Ayatollah](63899.md) [Mohammad Hussein Fadlallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hussein_Fadlallah) was among them. Most of the newcomers were destitute, leading to solidarity and self-reliance.[^cite_note-Harris-7] More Shiites arrived in Dahieh following the [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_South_Lebanon_conflict) and [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War). [^cite_note-8] Unwilling to live under the Israeli [South Lebanon security belt administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_security_belt_administration), more Shiites moved out of their villages to Beirut. By 1986, an estimated 800,000 Shias were living in Dahieh, constituting the majority of Shia in Lebanon.[^cite_note-Odd_Karsten_Tveit_Goodbye_Lebanon-5]

### 2006 Lebanon war

See also: [Dahiya doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahiya_doctrine)



A crater in Dahieh in 2008, two years after the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War)

Hezbollah's television station, [Al-Manar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Manar), was targeted in the area.[^cite_note-\:1-1][^cite_note-9]

Hours after the 14 August 2006, ceasefire, Hezbollah pledged to reconstruct houses for the residents of Dahieh, and offered rent money for the period in which they were being built.[^cite_note-10]

On 22 September 2006, Hezbollah's leader [Hassan Nasrallah](313436.md) attended a mass rally in Dahieh declaring a "Divine Victory" against Israel. Apart from mentioning Hezbollah having 20,000 rockets at its disposal, he also went on to criticize Lebanon's central government, stating it should step down and form a unity government.[^cite_note-11] According to the Hezbollah's "Jihad al-Bina' " association,[^cite_note-\:3-12] the reconstruction of Dahieh started from 25 May 2007, the day of the anniversary of the 2000 Israeli pullout from Lebanon.[^cite_note-\:3-12]

### 2013 bombings

Main articles: [July 2013 Beirut bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2013_Beirut_bombing) and [August 2013 Beirut bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2013_Beirut_bombing)

On 9 July 2013, 53 people were wounded after a bomb exploded in a busy shopping street in the suburb; the blast came on a busy shopping day on the eve of the holy Muslim month of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan).[^cite_note-\:4-13] A faction of the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) (FSA) claimed responsibility; however, FSA spokesman Luay Miqdad condemned the attack, as well as another attack the following month.[^cite_note-\:4-13]

On 16 August 2013, a month after the first bomb, another car bomb blast hit the suburb.[^cite_note-\:2-14] At least 21 people were killed and 200 injured in the massive explosion, the majority of whom children.[^cite_note-\:2-14] A group linked to the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) calling itself the "Brigade of Aisha" claimed responsibility for the attack.[^cite_note-\:2-14]

### 2024 airstrikes

Main article: [September 2024 Lebanon strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2024_Lebanon_strikes)

On 27 September 2024, Israel launched airstrikes on the suburb, which led to the [assassination of Hassan Nasrallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hezbollah_headquarters_strike) as part of the attack, as confirmed on the following day.[^cite_note-15]

### 2026 airstrikes

Main article: [2026 Lebanon war](82549840.md)

An Israeli airstrike led to two fatalities on 27 March 2026 in the residential area of Tahwitat al-Ghadir.[^cite_note-naharnettwokilled-16]
