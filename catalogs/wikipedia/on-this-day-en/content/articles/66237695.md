The **2026 Pacific hurricane season** is the current [Pacific hurricane](3092184.md) season in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere). The season officially began on May 15, 2026, and will end on November 30, 2026. For the Central Pacific ([140°W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/140th_meridian_west) to [180°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180th_meridian)), the season began on June 1, 2026, and will end on November 30, 2026, with the most active period typically occurring between July and October. Those dates, adopted by convention, describe the period in which most [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis) occurs in the Pacific Ocean. With three [Category 5 Pacific hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_5_Pacific_hurricane) having formed this season, it is tied with the [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Pacific_hurricane_season), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Pacific_hurricane_season), and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pacific_hurricane_season) seasons as the season with the most Category 5 hurricanes.

The first system of the season, Tropical Storm Amanda, formed in the open eastern Pacific on June 2. In July, Hurricane Fausto became the first hurricane, while Hurricane Genevieve became the first major hurricane, and first Category 5 hurricane, of the season. In August, [Hurricane Lala](83950433.md) became the first hurricane to directly hit the [Big Island](70962.md) of Hawaii since [1871](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pacific_hurricanes_before_1900#1871) while also becoming Hawaii's third-wettest tropical cyclone on record. In September, [Lowell](84128118.md) became the second Category 5 hurricane of the season and the first hurricane to directly impact [Kauaʻi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauai_County,_Hawaii) since [Hurricane Iniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Iniki) in 1992. Later that month, [Hurricane Polo](84283796.md) became the record-tying third Category 5 hurricane of the season and the second-most-intense Pacific hurricane by central pressure on record.

In advance of each [Pacific hurricane](3092184.md) season, [forecasts of hurricane activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_seasonal_forecasting) are issued by forecasters from the United States [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](37876.md) (NOAA)'s [Climate Prediction Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Prediction_Center), and [Mexico](3966054.md)'s [Servicio Meteorológico Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Meteorol%C3%B3gico_Nacional_\(Mexico\)) (SMN). The forecasts include weekly and monthly changes in significant factors that help determine the amount of tropical storms, hurricanes, and major hurricanes within a particular season.

According to NOAA, the average Pacific hurricane season between 1991 and 2020 contained about 15 named storms, 8 hurricanes, and 4 major hurricanes (Category 3 and higher), as well as a median [accumulated cyclone energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accumulated_Cyclone_Energy) (ACE) index of 75.38–110.55 units.[^cite_note-Background-1] Broadly speaking, ACE is the measure of the power of a tropical or [subtropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_cyclone) multiplied by the length of time it existed. ACE is only calculated for full advisories on specific tropical or subtropical cyclones reaching wind speeds of 39 mph (63 km/h) or higher. NOAA typically describes a season as above-average, average, or below-average depending on the cumulative ACE index, but the number of tropical storms, hurricanes or major hurricanes can also be considered.

On April 22, 2026, Mexico's [National Meteorological Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Meteorol%C3%B3gico_Nacional_\(Mexico\)) (SMN) issued its first forecast for the Pacific hurricane season, predicting 18–21 named storms, 9–11 hurricanes and 4–5 major hurricanes.[^cite_note-SMN-3] On May 21, 2026, NOAA issued its forecast, indicating an above-normal season with 15–22 named storms, 9–14 hurricanes, and 5–9 major hurricanes.[^cite_note-NOAAUSAToday-2026-4] NOAA forecasted that these elevated numbers would materialize due to a strong [El Niño event](83914690.md) during the season.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-Index-5]

## Seasonal summary

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of the 2026 Pacific hurricane season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2026_Pacific_hurricane_season).





Hurricanes [Lowell](84128118.md) (left), Karina (center) and Marie (right) on September 3



Hurricanes [Nolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Nolo) (left), Odalys (center), and [Polo](84283796.md) (center-right), with the disturbances that later became Rachel (far right) and Nineteen-E (southeast of Nolo) on September 25

Officially, the 2026 Pacific hurricane season began on May 15 in the Eastern Pacific, on June 1 in the Central Pacific, and both will end on November 30.[^cite_note-7] Altogether, twenty tropical cyclones have formed, seventeen in the Eastern Pacific and three in the Central Pacific, and all became named storms. Nine of the storms strengthened into hurricanes, with seven of them intensifying into major hurricanes (Category 3 or higher in the [Saffir-Simpson scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffir-Simpson_hurricane_wind_scale)). This season's ACE index is approximately 285.6 units, as officially calculated by the [National Hurricane Center](255317.md) (NHC) on October 3.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

The Pacific hurricane season was defined by an exceptionally strong start, with three named systems developing by early June—the most active opening to a season in the basin since [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Pacific_hurricane_season).[^cite_note-10] On June 2, Tropical Storm Amanda formed in the open Pacific, becoming the first tropical cyclone of the season.[^cite_note-11] Five days later, [Tropical Storm Boris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Boris_\(2026\)) formed off the southwestern coast of Mexico, followed by [Tropical Storm Cristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Cristina_\(2026\)) the next day off the Pacific coast of Nicaragua.[^cite_note-12] Boris caused damage in [southern Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Mexico) while Cristina brought torrential rain throughout [Central America](6121.md). In early July, Tropical Storm Douglas formed in the open Pacific, without affecting any land.[^cite_note-ep042026-13] Tropical Storm Fausto formed on July 19 and subsequently became the first hurricane of the season; Fausto crossed into the central Pacific a week later. On July 24, Hurricane Genevieve formed near the coast of Mexico and, a few days later, became the first major hurricane of the season. Genevieve ultimately became the first [Category 5 hurricane](3611403.md) in the basin since [Kristy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Kristy_\(2024\)) in [2024](66237692.md).[^cite_note-renamed_from_012_on_20261001001239-14]

In September, [Hurricane Lowell](84128118.md) became the first Pacific hurricane to reach Category 5 intensity twice since [Hurricane Ioke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ioke) in 2006.[^cite_note-m573-15]

## Systems

### Tropical Storm Amanda

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | June 2 – June 7 |
| Peak intensity | 50 mph (85 km/h) (1-min); 1003 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On May 31, the [National Hurricane Center](255317.md) (NHC) noted that an area of low pressure could form well to the southwest of the Baja California Peninsula.[^cite_note-16] The system quickly organized over time, and by June 2, the system was designated as Tropical Depression One-E after its cloud pattern had improved and scatterometer data identified a closed low-level circulation.[^cite_note-atcr-17][^cite_note-18] The next day, the depression strengthened into a tropical storm and was named *Amanda*, following deepening convection near its center.[^cite_note-19] Through June 3 to June 4, Amanda strengthened and organized slightly, though suffering from some [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] On June 5, Amanda began weakening and its center became completely exposed.[^cite_note-22] On June 6, Amanda weakened into a tropical depression in the face of shear and dry air, though warm waters allowed Amanda to continue producing some [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection).[^cite_note-23] Amanda continued to wane through June 7 as environmental shear and dry air increased.[^cite_note-24] By June 8, Amanda lacked enough convection to be considered a tropical cyclone, transitioning into a [remnant low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remnant_low).[^cite_note-atcr-17][^cite_note-25]

### Tropical Storm Boris

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | June 7 – June 9 |
| Peak intensity | 50 mph (85 km/h) (1-min); 1000 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Tropical Storm Boris (2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Boris_\(2026\))

On June 3, the NHC noted the possibility of an area of low pressure forming off the southwestern coast of Mexico.[^cite_note-\:1-26] On June 7, an [Oceansat-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceansat-3) pass had indicated that the disturbance had consolidated a closed low-level circulation, the low was classified as Tropical Depression Two-E at 15\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-27] Early the next day, the system acquired tropical storm-force wind and received the name *Boris*.[^cite_note-\:0-28] Most of the convection at this point was organized linearly with some banding around the center of the storm.[^cite_note-\:0-28] On June 9, Boris made landfall on the far southwestern coast of [Oaxaca](156896.md).[^cite_note-29] The rugged terrain proved unfavorable for this system, therefore transitioning it into a post-tropical cyclone, and then quickly dissipating afterward.[^cite_note-30]

One person died in [Manzanillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzanillo,_Colima), [Colima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colima), in rough seas.[^cite_note-\:2-31] Two additional people were swept away by rough surf in [Tecomán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecom%C3%A1n) and later found dead.[^cite_note-\:2-31][^cite_note-32] Commercial losses in Acapulco were estimated at Mex\$1.5 billion (US\$87.1 million).[^cite_note-33] In Oaxaca, a man drowned after his truck was swept away by an overflowing river. An outdoor theater worth Mex\$54 million (US\$3.16 million) was destroyed by swells.[^cite_note-34] As of July 2026, [Arthur J. Gallagher & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_J._Gallagher_&_Co.) estimated that losses totaled to US\$100 million.[^cite_note-\:20-35]

### Tropical Storm Cristina

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | June 8 – June 10 |
| Peak intensity | 50 mph (85 km/h) (1-min); 1002 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Tropical Storm Cristina (2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Cristina_\(2026\))

On June 3, the NHC noted the possibility of an area of low pressure forming off the western coast of [Central America](6121.md).[^cite_note-\:1-26] On June 8, after developing persistent deep convection, the low was classed as a tropical depression and was it designated Tropical Depression Three-E.[^cite_note-36] Soon, the depression intensified into a tropical storm, becoming named *Cristina*, with winds of 50 mph (85 km/h) and a pressure of 1002 mbar (hPa) based on [scatterometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scatterometer) data.[^cite_note-37] At the time while moving at an erratic movement, Cristina exhibited an exposed low-level circulation, a characteristic typical of tropical cyclones under the effects of high wind shear because of the interaction of a nearby and slightly stronger Tropical Storm Boris.[^cite_note-38] Cristina weakened into a tropical depression as a result. The trend continued, and on June 11, Cristina opened up into a [trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trough_\(meteorology\)) as the circulation was no longer well defined.[^cite_note-39] The trough merged with a low pressure system which later produced heavy rainfall [along the western Gulf of Mexico](83471857.md) coast.[^cite_note-40]

Prior to becoming a tropical depression, Cristina produced heavy rains that caused flooding in [San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula), [Honduras](13394.md), killing a 60-year-old man and disrupting traffic.[^cite_note-41] In [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_\(canton\),_Costa_Rica), Costa Rica, five people were left missing after heavy winds attributed to Cristina capsized two boats.[^cite_note-42] Across [El Salvador](9356.md), 58 damaged or flooded homes, 11 affected vehicles, 38 fallen trees, 34 blocked roads and 5 landslides were reported.[^cite_note-43] In [Acajutla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acajutla), residents and business owners estimated that losses totaled to US\$150,000.[^cite_note-44] Heavy rainfall related to Cristina, combined with the effects of the local rainy season, resulted in five deaths and three injuries in [Guatemala](17238567.md); two deaths occurred each in [Momostenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momostenango) and [Casillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casillas,_Guatemala) and a child died in [Senahú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senah%C3%BA).[^cite_note-45] In [Chiapas](6787.md), heavy rainfall from Cristina caused flooding. A man was found dead after being swept away by an overflowing river.[^cite_note-46]

### Tropical Storm Douglas

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | July 1 – July 3 |
| Peak intensity | 40 mph (65 km/h) (1-min); 1003 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On June 21, the NHC began monitoring a disturbance off the southwestern coast of Mexico and noted the possibility of tropical development.[^cite_note-47] The disturbance increased in chances for development as it showed signs of further organization as it moved into open waters.[^cite_note-48] The disturbance eventually had a 90% chance of development according to the NHC.[^cite_note-49] By July 1, the low pressure system obtained enough organization to be classified as Tropical Depression Four-E and had winds of 35 mph (55 km/h) and a pressure of 1005 mbar (29.67 inHg).[^cite_note-ep042026-13] Later, it strengthened into a tropical storm and received the name *Douglas*.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The system meandered slowly over the open waters, eventually reaching peak winds of 40 mph (65 km/h) and a minimum central pressure of 1003 mbar (29.61 inHg) over the open waters,[^cite_note-52] which it maintained until July 3. The system weakened into a remnant low with only a little amount of convection and cloud tops near the circulation mainly due to cooler sea surface temperatures.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-\:6-54] The remnants of the system continued moving generally to the northwest towards cooler waters in the open Eastern Pacific Ocean,[^cite_note-\:6-54] before dissipating on July 5.

### Tropical Storm Elida

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | July 14 – July 20 |
| Peak intensity | 70 mph (110 km/h) (1-min); 990 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On July 7, at 06\:00 UTC, the NHC began monitoring a disturbance off the coast of southern Mexico with a low chance for development.[^cite_note-55] Over the next few days, the disturbance became better organized under favorable conditions for [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis). On July 14 at 18\:00 UTC, the system consolidated into Tropical Depression Five-E,[^cite_note-56] and twelve hours later, the depression was upgraded to Tropical Storm Elida.[^cite_note-57] Though some dry air and mid-level wind wind shear hindered the storm, it continued to strengthen.[^cite_note-58] It attained its peak intensity on July 18 with winds of 70 mph (115 km/h) and a pressure of 990 mbar (29 inHg).[^cite_note-59] The storm then weakened as it moved over colder waters as well as experiencing more dry air and wind shear. The system stopped producing significant convection on July 20 and became post-tropical.[^cite_note-60]

Elida impacted [Clarion Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarion_Island) with strong winds and rough seas throughout July 16.[^cite_note-61] Swells related to the remnants of Elida as well as the further distant Hurricanes Fausto and Genevieve on July 25 impacted produced significant swells on the California coast. A total of 464 water rescues took place in [San Diego](28504.md) while another 160 rescues took place in [Oceanside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanside,_California).[^cite_note-KUSI1-62] The [Los Angeles County Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Fire_Department) performed a record 1,950 rescues on July 25.[^cite_note-63] Further north, more than 25 rescues occurred at beaches in the [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_California) area that same day.[^cite_note-64] A lifeguard who was caught on video performing one of these rescues in Santa Cruz was invited to the [White House](33057.md) to receive a high civilian honor.[^cite_note-65]

### Hurricane Fausto

| Category 2 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | July 19 – July 29 |
| Peak intensity | 105 mph (165 km/h) (1-min); 967 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

The NHC began monitoring an area for development on July 12,[^cite_note-66] with a tropical wave being located offshore of southern Mexico by July 16.[^cite_note-67] The tropical wave developed into a broad low on July 18.[^cite_note-\:5-68] The low gained sufficient organization and developed into Tropical Depression Six-E at 03\:00 UTC on July 19.[^cite_note-69] The depression continued to organize and strengthened to Tropical Storm Fausto at 21\:00 UTC later that day.[^cite_note-70] Fausto strengthened slowly over the next two days, with persistent northerly shear counteracting an otherwise conducive environment.[^cite_note-71] Despite this, Fausto continued to organize, and strengthened into a hurricane at 03\:00 UTC on July 22, becoming the first hurricane of the season.[^cite_note-72] Thereafter, Fausto struggled to strengthen for a couple days due to resurgent wind shear and dry air intrusions.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Despite a marginal environment, the storm reorganized and strengthened again on July 24, becoming a Category 2 hurricane.[^cite_note-75] Fausto continued moving west, and weakened back to a Category 1 hurricane due to drier air and cooler sea surface temperatures on July 26.[^cite_note-76] On July 28, Fausto weakened to a tropical storm under further unfavorable conditions,[^cite_note-77] ultimately degenerating into a remnant low the following day, devoid of sufficient convection.[^cite_note-78]

Fausto impacted the [Revillagigedo Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revillagigedo_Islands) with strong winds and rough seas throughout July 20.[^cite_note-79] Significant swells from Fausto also affected the California coast in conjunction with the remnants of Elida and the developing Genevieve.[^cite_note-KUSI1-62]

### Hurricane Genevieve

| Category 5 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | July 24 – August 2 |
| Peak intensity | 160 mph (260 km/h) (1-min); 924 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

A tropical wave that moved off the west coast of Africa on July 13 crossed Central America and exited into the Eastern Pacific by July 21.[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] A low-pressure area formed on July 22 to the south of Mexico.[^cite_note-81] It developed into a tropical depression at 06\:00 UTC on July 24, and intensified into Tropical Storm Genevieve six hours later. Just over a day later, Genevieve attained hurricane status,[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] amid low wind shear and warm waters. It moved west-northwestward due to a powerful ridge to its northeast.[^cite_note-82] Genevieve [rapidly intensified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_intensification), becoming a major hurricane at 12\:00 UTC on July 26,[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] the first major hurricane of the season.[^cite_note-83] It was quickly upgraded to a Category 4 hurricane[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] as it cleared out a warm and symmetric eye.[^cite_note-84] Based on several satellite estimates, the NHC upgraded Genevieve to a Category 5 hurricane at 06\:00 UTC on July 27, with peak winds of 160 mph (255 km/h) and a minimum central pressure of 924 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) (27.32 [inHg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inches_of_Mercury)).[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] Soon after its peak, Genevieve began an eyewall replacement cycle, as the eye became cloud-filled and replaced by an outer eyewall.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] On July 28, Genevieve fell to a Category 3 hurricane[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] after being affected by dry air and wind shear.[^cite_note-88] The hurricane unexpectedly re-intensified to a Category 4 hurricane on July 29.[^cite_note-TCR072026-80] After this brief resurgence, Genevieve resumed weakening due to the dry air and wind shear, and its eye collapsed.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] The deepest convection became displaced to the south and east of its center.[^cite_note-92] At 00\:00 UTC on July 31, Genevieve was downgraded to a tropical storm. Genevieve continued to weaken and degenerated to a post-tropical cyclone at 18\:00 UTC on August 2.[^cite_note-TCR072026-80]

Genevieve generated swells that caused rough surf and rip current conditions along the western coast of [Baja California peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_peninsula) and parts of Southwestern Mexico.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Genevieve produced blustery conditions on Clarion Island, with a peak sustained wind of 63 mph (101 km/h) and a peak wind gust of 86 mph (138 km/h) being observed.[^cite_note-95] Genevieve also generated swells that caused coastal flooding in Southern California, leading to the cancellation of two [Pacific Surfliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Surfliner) trains, as well as delays on [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)).[^cite_note-KTLA81-96] City officials in [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_California) constructed [sand berms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berm) on local beaches to prevent flooding from increased tides.[^cite_note-97] A High Surf advisory was issued for [Southern California](62520.md) beaches from between July 29 and August 1 due to the threat of dangerous surf conditions.[^cite_note-98] Several water rescues occurred from rough ocean conditions.[^cite_note-KTLA81-96]

&#x20;The NHC began monitoring a tropical wave south of Baja California on August 8.[^cite_note-99] The system slowly organized over the next few days, developing a well-defined low on August 11.[^cite_note-100] On August 13, the disturbance was designated Tropical Depression Eight-E at 03\:00 UTC after developing persistent deep convection and a closed circulation.[^cite_note-101] The cyclone strengthened to a tropical storm six hours later and was named *Hernan*.[^cite_note-102] Hernan peaked in intensity soon thereafter with a minimum central pressure of 1,003 mbar (29.6 inHg) and 1-minute sustained winds of 45 mph (75 km/h) by 21\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-103] Strong shear then exposed the storm's center,[^cite_note-104] weakening it to a tropical depression a day later.[^cite_note-105] On August 15, Hernan degenerated to a remnant low.[^cite_note-106]

|  |  |
| Duration | August 13 – August 28 |
| Peak intensity | 130 mph (215 km/h) (1-min); 947 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Hurricane Lala](83950433.md)

On August 12, the NHC designated [Potential Tropical Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_Tropical_Cyclone) One due to a tropical disturbance east-southeast of Hawaii.[^cite_note-PTC_One-C_Advisory_Number_1-107] A day later, the NHC redesignated the system as Tropical Storm Lala, after increased convection and consolidation of the circulation.[^cite_note-108] The storm strengthened while moving towards Hawaii, but was deterred slightly by dry air intrusions..[^cite_note-Tropical_Storm_Lala_7-109] At 19\:00 UTC on August 15, Lala attained hurricane status.[^cite_note-110] At 03\:00 UTC on August 16, Lala passed just south of the Big Island with winds of 80 mph (130 km/h).[^cite_note-111] Land interaction quickly weakened Lala back to a tropical storm, but it regained hurricane status on August 18 after passing the island chain.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] Lala rapidly intensified, attaining peak winds of 130 mph (215 km/h) on August 19, with a minimum pressure of 947 mbar (28.0 inHg).[^cite_note-114] A trough to its north turned Lala to the north, bringing it through the [Papahanaumokuakea Marine National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papahanaumokuakea_Marine_National_Monument) on August 21. By that time, it had weakened due to dry air and cooler waters.[^cite_note-115] Lala fell to tropical storm status on August 23. It turned back to the northwest due to a building ridge.[^cite_note-116] Stronger wind shear caused the storm to weaken on August 27.[^cite_note-117] On August 28, Lala transitioned into a post-tropical cyclone.[^cite_note-118] The system crossed the international date line later that day, with the [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center) (JTWC) marking it as a subtropical system, while the [Japan Meteorological Agency](457886.md) (JMA) classified it as an extratropical low.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

A 93-year-old woman was found deceased in her home in [Nāʻālehu](110484.md) following the storm.[^cite_note-121] In addition, one man was killed and two others were injured, including one critically, as a result of a head-on car accident in [Hawaiian Ocean View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Ocean_View,_Hawaii) due to inclement weather caused by Lala's passage.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] A few days after Lala's passage, an unidentified man, believed to be in his 60s, was found deceased near [Hawaiʻi Belt Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawai%CA%BBi_Belt_Road) in Nāʻālehu in a pasture, with his death being attributed to Lala.[^cite_note-124] Elsewhere, another woman was hospitalized with moderate injuries after she was swept 100 yards away by floodwaters after her home was knocked off its foundation in [Waiʻōhinu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wai%CA%BB%C5%8Dhinu,_Hawaii).[^cite_note-125]

### Tropical Storm Moke

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | August 20 – August 25 |
| Peak intensity | 50 mph (85 km/h) (1-min); 994 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

A tropical depression formed about 960 miles (1,540 km) east-southeast of [Hilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilo,_Hawaii) over the Central Pacific on August 20,[^cite_note-126] with the initial intensity being set at 35 mph (55 km/h) based on an earlier ASCAT pass.[^cite_note-127] The next day, based on data obtained by [recon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_hunters), the storm was upgraded to a tropical storm, and received the name *Moke*.[^cite_note-z739-128] On August 22, Moke reached peak winds of 50 mph (85 km/h), as recorded by recon.[^cite_note-129] On August 23, Moke passed south of the island of Hawaii as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-130] Moke encountered unfavorable conditions and on August 25 at 09\:00 UTC, it degenerated into a remnant low.[^cite_note-131]

The NHC noted that although the storm was expected to cause some wind, rain and surf impacts in the Hawaiian Islands, no tropical storm watches or warnings were expected due to the storm's compact nature.[^cite_note-z739-128] Moke threatened Hawaii just a few days after the passage of Lala. Members of the [Hawaii Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Army_National_Guard) and [Hawaii Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Air_National_Guard) were deployed to the southern region of the Big Island to begin storm preparations for Moke in conjunction with ongoing recovery efforts from Lala.[^cite_note-132] Rainfall from the storm reached 4.21 in (107 mm) near [Lahaina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii).[^cite_note-WPCMAX-133]

### Hurricane Karina

| Category 4 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | August 27 – September 7 |
| Peak intensity | 145 mph (230 km/h) (1-min); 940 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Late on August 21, the NHC began noting the potential for an area of disturbed weather to form well southwest of [Mexico](3966054.md) over the next several days and undergo [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis).[^cite_note-144] Five days later, an area of low pressure formed in association with a [tropical wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wave) well offshore Southwestern Mexico and began to consolidate.[^cite_note-145] At 21\:00 UTC on August 27, the system having already attained gale-force winds was upgraded into Tropical Storm Karina.[^cite_note-146] At 09\:00 UTC on August 30, Karina was upgraded to a hurricane with winds of 80 mph (130 km/h) and a pressure of 980 mbar (29 inHg).[^cite_note-147] Moving over warm waters near 84 °F (29 °C), Karina rapidly intensified, becoming a major hurricane at 00\:00 UTC on August 31,[^cite_note-148] then reaching Category 4 strength later that same day, with sustained winds of 145 mph (235 km/h) and a pressure of 942 mbar (27.8 inHg).[^cite_note-149] After briefly weakening, Karina became an [annular hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annular_tropical_cyclone) the following day, allowing the storm to maintain higher intensity over cooler waters, and slowing its weakening rate.[^cite_note-150] By September 2, it began to restrengthen, eventually reaching its peak intensity with sustained winds of 145 mph (235 km/h) and a slightly lower central pressure of 940 mbar (28 inHg).[^cite_note-151] Drier air and marginal SSTs eventually worked to weaken the cyclone, and Karina dropped below major hurricane strength on September 3 as it neared the Central Pacific basin.[^cite_note-152] On September 4, at 21\:00 UTC, Karina rapidly fell below hurricane status as it encountered more southwesterly shear and more dry air.[^cite_note-153] The trend continued and, on September 7, Karina degenerated into a post-tropical cyclone, and was ultimately absorbed by the nearby weakening [Hurricane Lowell](84128118.md) early on September 9.

Karina impacted the uninhabited Revillagigedo Islands with rough seas and strong winds.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

### Hurricane Lowell

| Category 5 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | August 27 – September 11 |
| Peak intensity | 160 mph (260 km/h) (1-min); 922 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Hurricane Lowell](84128118.md)

At 00\:00 UTC on August 27, after gaining some organization and a medium chance for development, an area of low pressure was noted by the NHC.[^cite_note-156] At 21\:00 UTC on August 27, scatterometer data revealed that the disturbance had developed gale-force winds and was upgraded into Tropical Storm Lowell.[^cite_note-157] Lowell slowly strengthened over the next few days and organized despite easterly wind shear.[^cite_note-158] On September 1 at 15\:00 UTC, Lowell attained hurricane strength,[^cite_note-159] and rapidly intensified into a major hurricane by 00\:00 UTC on September 2.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] The storm continued rapidly intensifying late that day, becoming the second Category 5 hurricane of the season by 21\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-162] Lowell became the first Category 5 hurricane in the Central Pacific since [Hurricane Walaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Walaka) in 2018.[^cite_note-p021-163] Lowell weakened below Category 5 status the next day due to an increase in easterly shear.[^cite_note-164] By September 4, at 09\:00 UTC, Lowell further weakened to a Category 3 hurricane due to an ongoing eyewall replacement cycle.[^cite_note-165] Later that day, Lowell began to go under another phase of rapid intensification, and reintensified into a Category 5 hurricane by the following day.[^cite_note-166] Soon after, Lowell weakened back to Category 4 due to increasing wind shear.[^cite_note-167] The next day, Lowell weakened further into a Category 3.[^cite_note-168] The next day, Lowell dropped below major hurricane status due to rising wind shear and dropping sea surface temperatures.[^cite_note-169] On September 9, Lowell weakened to a tropical storm due to moderate wind shear and dry air.[^cite_note-170] On September 11, Lowell degenerated into a [post-tropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-tropical_cyclone).[^cite_note-171]

### Hurricane Marie

| Category 2 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | September 1 – September 8 |
| Peak intensity | 100 mph (155 km/h) (1-min); 967 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On September 1, the NHC designated a low-pressure system as Tropical Storm Marie.[^cite_note-172] Marie steadily intensified over the Eastern Pacific over the next day, becoming a high-end tropical storm late on September 2.[^cite_note-173] On September 3 at 03\:00 UTC, Marie improved its structure and intensified to a Category 1 hurricane with winds of 80 mph (130 km/h).[^cite_note-174] Over the next 24 hours, Marie slowly and steadily intensified, struggling due to dry air, and it became a Category 2 hurricane on September 4, at 03\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-175] Marie held its intensity for 24 hours, with dry air mostly restricting the opportunity for further intensification, however the hurricane deepened to 967 mbar (28.6 inHg) that evening.[^cite_note-176] Early the following day, Marie had weakened to high-end Category 1 intensity.[^cite_note-177] Cooler sea surface temperatures continued to restrict Marie from strengthening as the system fell below hurricane status. By September 8, Marie transitioned into a post-tropical cyclone.

Marie impacted the [Revillagigedo Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revillagigedo_Islands) with rough seas and tropical-storm and hurricane-force winds.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] Marie also generated swells that caused rough surf and rip current conditions along parts of Southwestern Mexico, Baja California peninsula, and Southern California.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181] Small vessels were prohibited from leaving ports in [San Jose del Cabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_del_Cabo). Classes were also cancelled in [Los Cabos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Cabos) and several streets were flooded.[^cite_note-182] High waves in [Long Beach, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_California) forced 20 homes to evacuate.[^cite_note-183]

### Tropical Storm Norbert

At 12\:00 UTC on September 8, the NHC upgraded an area of low pressure to a high chance of developing into a tropical depression.[^cite_note-184] At 21\:00 UTC on September 9, scatterometery data revealed a circulation that was slightly elongated but closed, with max winds supporting 35 mph (55 km/h), prompting the designation of Tropical Depression *Fourteen-E*.[^cite_note-185] On the next day at 09\:00 UTC, the NHC upgraded the system to Tropical Storm *Norbert*.[^cite_note-186] On September 11, Norbert reached winds of 65 mph (105 km/h) and a pressure of 996 mbar (29.4 inHg).[^cite_note-187] Afterward, dry air and wind shear caused the storm to weaken throughout September 12.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] At 15\:00 UTC on September 15, the NHC declared Norbert as a remnant low due to lacking organized deep convection.[^cite_note-190]

Norbert brought rough seas and strong winds to the Revillagigedo Islands.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]

### Hurricane Nolo

| Category 4 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | September 13 – present |
| Peak intensity | 155 mph (250 km/h) (1-min); 929 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Hurricane Nolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Nolo)

On September 8, the NHC began monitoring an area of interest well south of the Baja California peninsula. Two days later, a broad low pressure area developed, and the system began to become better organized despite moderate wind shear. On September 13 at 21\:00 UTC, the consolidating low formed into Tropical Depression *Fifteen-E*.[^cite_note-193] Unfavorable wind shear eventually overtook the tropical depression, causing it to open up into a trough early on September 16.[^cite_note-194] However, the NHC continued to monitor the system for potential regeneration.[^cite_note-195] Early on September 23, the remains of the system regenerated into a tropical depression in the central Pacific.[^cite_note-TD15E_Back-196] Late that day, it became a tropical storm and was assigned the name *Nolo*.[^cite_note-197] At 3\:00 UTC on September 25, Nolo strengthened into a Category 1 hurricane as it moved north-northwestward.[^cite_note-198] It continued steadily strengthening, becoming a Category 2 hurricane late that day.[^cite_note-199] Dry air intrusions caused Nolo to weaken back into a Category 1 hurricane on September 26 as it passed south of Hawaii's Big Island, moving westward.[^cite_note-200] It restrengthened into a high-end Category 2 hurricane on September 27, continuing its westward movement.[^cite_note-201] By 09\:00 UTC on September 28, Nolo had become an annular hurricane, rapidly intensifying to Category 4 strength as a ridge began steering it west-northwestward.[^cite_note-202]

A hurricane watch was issued late on September 23 for [Hawaiʻi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawai%CA%BBi_County,_Hawaii) in anticipation of Nolo's impacts.[^cite_note-203] This was followed by a tropical storm warning the next day.[^cite_note-204] Governor [Josh Green](41279962.md) declared a [state of emergency](298608.md) for the state.[^cite_note-205] The state also provided hotel rooms for some in affected areas to evacuate to.[^cite_note-206] Hawaiʻi County schools and offices closed. Flooding from Nolo's heavy rains forced the closure of the [Kohala Mountain Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohala_Mountain_Road). [Spencer Beach Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_M._Spencer#Park) recorded 17.25 in (438 mm) of rain.[^cite_note-207] Over 16,000 people lost power in Hawaii as Nolo approached.[^cite_note-208]

### Hurricane Odalys

| Category 4 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |

On September 20 at 09\:00 UTC, the NHC upgraded an area of low pressure to Tropical Storm Odalys after the system exhibited an increase in convective organization.[^cite_note-209] Several days later on September 22 at 09\:00 UTC, amid improved structural organization, the NHC upgraded Odalys into a hurricane.[^cite_note-210] Odalys further intensfied into the sixth major hurricane of the season by 09\:00 UTC on September 25, with winds of 115 mph (185 km/h).[^cite_note-211] Later that day at 12\:00 UTC, the NHC upgraded Odalys to Category 4 hurricane, with winds of 130 mph (210 km/h) and a pressure of 946 mb (946 hPa).[^cite_note-212] Afterward, increasing southwesterly wind shear caused the inner core to decay, and the storm dropped below major hurricane intensity.[^cite_note-213] Strong wind shear, dry air, and cool waters caused Odalys to rapidly weaken and transition into a post-tropical cyclone on September 27.[^cite_note-214] Remnant moisture from Odalys, as well as [Hurricane Polo](84283796.md), partially contributed to severe thunderstorms across the [Southwest United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_United_States) on September 28–29.[^cite_note-215]

### Hurricane Polo

| Category 5 hurricane (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | September 20 – September 30 |

On September 20, Tropical Depression Seventeen-E formed off the southwest coast of Mexico.[^cite_note-216] Early on the following day, the NHC upgraded the system to Tropical Storm *Polo*. Under favorable conditions, such as 31 °C (88 °F) waters off the coast of Mexico and limited wind shear, Polo began explosively intensifying, becoming a hurricane by 18\:00 UTC[^cite_note-217] and the season's third Category 5 hurricane by 12\:00 UTC the following day.[^cite_note-218] Only a few hours later, it reached its initial peak intensity with winds of 180 mph (285 km/h) and a pressure of 892 mbar (26.34 inHg).[^cite_note-219] An eyewall replacement cycle then caused it to weaken to Category 4, but it regained Category 5 intensity by 00\:40 UTC on September 24[^cite_note-220] after completing the eyewall replacement cycle. At least five vehicles were flooded.[^cite_note-222] Polo brought hurricane-force winds and dangerous swells to the Revillagigedo Islands.[^cite_note-223]
