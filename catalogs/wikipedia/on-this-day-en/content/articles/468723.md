**Asir**,[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] officially the **Aseer Province**,[^cite_note-3] is a [province](468738.md) of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) in [southern Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia). It has an area of 76,693 square kilometres (29,611 sq mi), and an estimated population of 2,024,285 (in 2022).[^cite_note-4] [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha) is its seat and second-largest city, after [Khamis Mushait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamis_Mushait).[^cite_note-5] Asir is bounded by the [Mecca Province](468731.md) to the north and west, [Al-Baha Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Baha_Province) to the northwest, [Riyadh Province](23631923.md) to the northeast, [Najran Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran_Province) to the southeast, and [Jazan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazan_Province) and the [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate) of [Yemen](350939.md) to the south.

## Etymology

The origin of the name "Asir" remains a subject of debate among historians. One theory, proposed by [Al-Masudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Masudi) in his work *[The Meadows of Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meadows_of_Gold)*, suggests that the region was originally known as the *Land of [Azd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azd)*.[^cite_note-6]

Another popular hypothesis suggests that the name "Asir" derives from the Arabic term *ʿUsrah* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عُسرة, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Hardship'). This theory comes from the fact that the region's terrain is rugged, which can be challenging to navigate.[^cite_note-7]

A third perspective proposed by [Fuad Hamza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuad_Hamza) in his book *Fi bilad **ʿ**Aseer*, links the name to the historical prominence of [Banu Asir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Asir).[^cite_note-\:0-8] As the tribe gained power and influence, its name came to encompass the surrounding territories and tribes.[^cite_note-\:0-8] This association was reinforced by the close ties between the Asir tribe and many of the ruling emirs in the region, including those from the Al Yazid, Al Muthami, and Al A'ad families.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

## History

### Ancient history

In 25 B.C., [Aelius Gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelius_Gallus) marched his legions south from [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) on an expedition of 1,300 miles (2,100 kilometres), to take control of the ancient overland trade routes between the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and what is now [Hadhramaut](325752.md) in [Yemen](350939.md). The [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) wanted control of those routes because they were desperate for money and hoped to raise some by capturing [Marib](3604484.md), capital of [Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba), and taking control of the trade in [incense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense) – then a priceless commodity – and other valuable [aromatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatic_compound). As it turned out, however, the expedition was a disaster and little information about Asir emerged.[^cite_note-9]

### Modern history

See also: [Emirate of Asir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Asir) and [Saudi–Yemeni war (1934)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi%E2%80%93Yemeni_war_\(1934\))

When the [First Saudi state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Saudi_state) was destroyed by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1818, the Asiris continued to fight the [Ottoman Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Egypt) forces in their region tenaciously. With the withdrawal of the Ottomans in 1840, the dynasty of [Al-Ayedh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ayedh?action=edit\&redlink=1), also of Mughayd, took control of the Asir highlands. The Al-Ayedh generally allied themselves to the Saudis, who had re-established their dynasty in 1824, but did not formally enter under their command. As the Al-Ayedh attempted to expand into the [Tihamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihamah) lowlands (present-day [Jazan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazan_Province)), the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) felt provoked to invade and occupy the highlands. They defeated and executed the leader of Al Ayedh in 1872 and established a *mutasarrifiyya* (a sub-governorate) in [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha) attached to the [Yemen vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_vilayet). Their rule, however, seldom extended far from the isolated forts where their troops were garrisoned.[^cite_note-a-10]

In about 1906, [Muhammad ibn Ali al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Ali_al-Idrisi), a descendant of [Ahmad ibn Idris al-Fasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Idris_al-Fasi), began to establish political control of Asir. After negotiations with [Italy](14532.md), which had interests nearby in [Somalia](27358.md), the [Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Asir) forces of Muhammad came into conflict with [Ottoman forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army_\(1861%E2%80%931922\)) in [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha). The Idrisis were defeated in 1911 by [Hashemites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemites) forces under [Hussein bin Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_bin_Ali,_King_of_Hejaz), the [Sharif of Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_of_Mecca), then still loyal to the Ottomans, but the tide turned when Muhammad ibn Ali concluded a secret military alliance with Great Britain (by then at war with the Ottomans) in 1915, and Sharif Hussein later switched sides and joined the British against the Ottomans.

After the end of [World War I](4764461.md), Muhammad ibn Ali became ruler of an internationally recognized sovereign state, the [Emirate of Asir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Asir), until he died in 1920. The territories of the emirate reached from [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha) in the north to [Hodeidah](769308.md) in the south. Muhammad's successors were, however, unable to resist the growing power of [Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud), who began annexing Asir and its neighboring regions after Muhammad's death, initially intervening under the pretext of mediating between the Al-Ayedh of Asir and the Idrisis. The Saudis took control of the regional capital [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha) in 1920, and incorporated the rest of Asir by 1923.[^cite_note-a-10] [Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud) later successfully fought off a rival claim for the region by the Zaydi [Imam](15240.md) of the neighboring [Kingdom of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yemen) in 1934.

By 1920, however, [Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud), the founder of the modern Saudi state, had begun to recoup the losses of the [House of Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saud), and to unify most of the [peninsula](47858.md) under his rule. As part of this campaign, he sent his [Bedouin](61770.md) warriors also known as the [Ikhwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikhwan) to occupy Asir, and the ruler of the region, Hasan Al Idrissi, had to leave.[^cite_note-jlt81-11] Therefore, he asked for protection from [Imam Yahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Muhammad_Hamid_ed-Din), the ruler of Yemen and went there.[^cite_note-jlt81-11] From then on Asir has been controlled by the House of Saud, a situation formalized in 1934 with the signing of the [Treaty of Taif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi%E2%80%93Yemeni_war_\(1934\)) between Saudi Arabia and Yemen. Even then the region was still largely unknown to the West. In 1932, [St John Philby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John_Philby), one of the first Europeans to explore and map the peninsula, did enter Asir, but as he did not publish his observations until 1952, the area remained one of the blank spots on the world's map.[^cite_note-12] In 1935 Asir was made a separate governorate.[^cite_note-13]

Since 2014, hundreds of Ethiopian refugees trying to cross the [Saudi Arabia–Yemen border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia%E2%80%93Yemen_border) have been killed, with reports of women being [raped](7980471.md), by the [Saudi Border Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Directorate_of_Border_Guard).[^cite_note-\:1-14] According to a 2023 [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) report, at least several hundred Ethiopian refugees had been killed between March 2022 and June 2023 while crossing the border "in a pattern that is widespread and systematic."[^cite_note-\:1-14] The Saudis have also used [explosive weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_weapon).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Economy

Historically, Asir was known for producing [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [alfalfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfalfa), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senna_\(plant\)), and [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense).[^cite_note-Prothero83-17][^cite_note-Prothero84-18][^cite_note-Prothero86-19] Wheat was grown in the summer and [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame) has been grown in wetter areas of the region.[^cite_note-Prothero84-18][^cite_note-Prothero85-20] [Straw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw) was used to make mats, hats, and [baskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basket) while tribes in the area also wove tents from straw.[^cite_note-Prothero99-21]

### Modern development projects

In 2019, the Saudi government launched an infrastructure development project in ʿAsir Region. The project is expected to cost more than 1 billion Saudi Riyals. The provided projects will include health care, transportation and municipal services.[^cite_note-arabnews.com-22] The project is in line with the [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md) to diversify non-petroleum income and activate new resources in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-arabnews.com-22]

In 2023, [Crown Prince](40373823.md) [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md) launched [Soudah Peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soudah_Peaks) Project to develop a tourism destination in Saudi Arabia's highest peak, [Jabal Soudah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Soudah).[^cite_note-23] Soudah Peaks is a planned luxury mountain tourism destination located at 3,015 meters above sea level.

## Education

See also: [List of universities and colleges in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Saudi_Arabia) and [Education in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Saudi_Arabia)

The Asir Province is served by several institutions of higher education, the most prominent being [King Khalid University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Khalid_University) (KKU). Established in 1998 through a royal decree that merged the satellite campuses of [Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Muhammad_ibn_Saud_Islamic_University) and [King Saud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Saud_University), The main campus is located in [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha), with satellite campuses in cities such as [Khamis Mushait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamis_Mushait), [Muhayil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhayil), and [Bisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisha). The university offers a wide range of undergraduate and graduate programs across fields like medicine, engineering, computer science, humanities, Islamic studies, and business. It enrolls both male and female students.[^cite_note-24]

Primary and secondary education in the province is overseen by the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Saudi_Arabia\)), which operates a comprehensive network of public schools. These schools serve students from the elementary to high school levels and are distributed across urban centers, mountainous villages, and rural areas, ensuring widespread educational access throughout the province.

In addition to academic institutions, the province hosts numerous technical and vocational education centers managed by the [Technical and Vocational Training Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_and_Vocational_Training_Corporation) (TVTC).[^cite_note-25]

## Geography

See also: [Asir Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asir_Mountains)



[Habala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habala) Valley

The Asir Province is situated on a high plateau that receives more rainfall than the rest of the country and contains the country's highest [peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhorm_Mountain), which rise to almost 3,000 metres (9,800 ft) at [Jabal Soudah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Soudah) near [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha). Though data is exceedingly sparse and unreliable, the average annual rainfall in the highlands probably ranges from 300–500 millimetres (12–20 in). It falls in two rainy seasons, the chief one being in March and April, with some rain in the summer. Temperatures are very extreme, with diurnal temperature ranges in the highlands the greatest in the world. It is common for afternoon temperatures to be over 30 °C (86 °F), yet mornings can be extremely frosty and [fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fog) can cut visibility to near zero percent. As a result, there is much more natural vegetation in ʿAsir than in any other part of Saudi Arabia, with sheltered areas even containing areas of dense coniferous forests, though more exposed ridges still are very dry. ʿAsir is home to many farmers who chiefly grow wheat and fruit crops. Irrigation has greatly expanded production in modern times. [Asir National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asir_National_Park) was established in 1981, and extends from the Red Sea coast through the western foothills to the Asir escarpment.[^cite_note-26]

## Transportation

See also: [Transport in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Saudi_Arabia) and [List of airports in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Saudi_Arabia)

### Road Network

- [Highway 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_10_\(Saudi_Arabia\)) – connects Abha to [Najran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran_Province) in the east.

Several ongoing projects aim to improve access to remote villages and tourism sites across the [Sarawat Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawat_Mountains).

### Air Transport

The province is primarily served by [Abha International Airport](9419420.md), which offers domestic flights to major Saudi cities and limited international routes. The airport is undergoing expansion and modernization in line with national development goals.[^cite_note-29]

### Public Transport

Public transportation is available in urban centers like Abha and Khamis Mushait, though it remains limited. Ride-hailing services such as [Careem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Careem) and [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) operate in the province.

### Future Projects

The [Asir Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asir_Development_Authority?action=edit\&redlink=1) has announced strategic transportation initiatives under [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md), including:

- Development of mountain cable car systems for tourism and local mobility

- Improved connectivity between airports, urban centers, and tourist destinations

- Environmentally friendly transport systems designed to preserve the province’s natural environment.
