**Rail transport in Ukraine** is a major [transport mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_of_transport) in Ukraine. Most railway infrastructure in Ukraine is owned by the [government of Ukraine](1372585.md) through [Ukrzaliznytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrzaliznytsia) (Public [JSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock_company) 'Ukrainian Railways'), a [joint-stock company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock_company) which has a de facto country-wide monopoly on passenger and freight transport by rail.

The first railway tracks were constructed in present-day western Ukraine by [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), but most lines were built under the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) or [the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_the_Soviet_Union), resulting in a track web dominated by [1520 mm gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_ft_and_1520_mm_gauge_railways). Part of the rail network in [eastern Ukraine](13065940.md) was privatized in the late 1990s, creating the biggest private railway company in the country, [Lemtrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemtrans), which focuses on freight transport. Since the outbreak of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) in 2014, intensified by the February 2022 full-scale [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), major parts of the Ukrainian rail network have been occupied by Russia-aligned military forces, while the Ukrainian government and Ukrzaliznytsia have undertaken considerable efforts to repair and transform the network for better vital logistical connections with the [European Union](9317.md) (which mostly has [standard-gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway)) and [Moldova](19260.md).

## History

### Before Ukrainian independence

#### Early railway systems

Further information: [Grand Crimean Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Crimean_Central_Railway)

In January 1855, during the [Siege of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_\(1854%E2%80%931855\)), part of the wider [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), construction of a railway line between [Balaklava Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaklava_Bay) and [British Army](4887.md) positions around [Sevastopol](51587.md) started. By April, 240 tons of freight were transported daily over the route. It was the first railway line in the territory of modern Ukraine.[^cite_note-2]

#### In Austria-Hungary

Further information: [Galician Railway of Archduke Charles Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Railway_of_Archduke_Charles_Louis), [Imperial Royal Austrian State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Royal_Austrian_State_Railways), and [Hungarian State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_State_Railways)



Prince [Leon Sapieha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Sapieha) in 1878

The first regular railway line in the lands of modern Ukraine was constructed as part of the [Galician Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Railway_of_Archduke_Charles_Louis) between 1859 and 1861, connecting [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl) and [Lviv](49968.md). On 4 November 1861, the first passenger train arrived to [Lviv Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Railway_Station). In the following years, with the support of prince [Leon Sapieha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Sapieha), a line connecting Lviv with [Chernivtsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi), the capital of [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bukovina) was constructed. Inaugurated on 1 September 1866, it would be later continued towards [Iasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iasi) and [Suceava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suceava). In 1869 another line connecting Lviv with [Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brody) and the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) border was opened; four years later, railway transit to the Russian side became possible after the opening of [Radyvyliv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radyvyliv) station. On 28 December 1870 Lviv was connected to [Ternopil](512798.md) via [Krasne railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasne_railway_station), and next year the line reached [Pidvolochysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pidvolochysk) and [Volochysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volochysk), establishing a link to [Odesa](55545.md).[^cite_note-loc-3]

During the early 1870s, construction started on a railway line across the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains), connecting Lviv and [Pest](36787.md) via the [Volovets Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volovets_Pass). In September 1873, a railway link between Lviv and [Stryi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryi) was opened, and in May 1875 the [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria) capital received connection with [Stanislaviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk). However, construction on the southern part of the line, passing across the Carpathians, was hindered by financial and political issues. Finally, in 1883 the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisleithania) government accepted an obligation to build the section of the line between Stryi and [Beskyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskydy_Tunnel), and next year [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transleithania) authorities agreed to construct the section from [Mukachevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukachevo), with both segments of the route meeting at the station of [Lavochne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavochne). Five [railway tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_tunnel) and several bridges were built between [Volovets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volovets) and Beskyd. On 4 April 1887 the Carpathian route was finally inaugurated.[^cite_note-loc-3]



Rail viaduct in Vorokhta

In 1893 the construction of another line across the Carpathians started in order to connect Stanislaviv with Hungarian territory. Led by [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) specialists, the project envisioned the railway to cross the [Prut](30876764.md) valley in the areas of [Deliatyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliatyn), [Mykulychyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykulychyn), [Tatariv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatariv?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Vorokhta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorokhta); on the southern side of the mountains, it was to run through [Körösmezö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasinia) and reach [Bocsko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhiv). 9 bridges and three tunnels were built along the route, which was inaugurated in 1898. In 1901 the construction of a railway between Lviv and [Sambir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambir) started along the [Dniester](376633.md) river. From Sambir, the line was continued to [Turka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turka,_Ukraine), crossing the Carpathian valleys over two bridges. In 1905 a connection between [Sianky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sianky) and [Uzhok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhok_Pass) was established.[^cite_note-loc-3]

In general, Ukrainian lands ruled by Austria-Hungary had a much denser railway network compared to Russian-ruled Ukraine.[^cite_note-ency-4] Between 1890 and 1912 the length of passenger railways in Galicia increased from 1,375 to 2,325 kilometers.[^cite_note-loc-3] The total length of the railway system in Galicia, Bukovina and [Transcarpathia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcarpathia) by 1914 reached 3,700 kilometers.[^cite_note-ency-4]

#### In the Russian Empire

Further information: [History of rail transport in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transport_in_Russia) and [Struve Railroad Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struve_Railroad_Bridge)



[Railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_railway_station) in [Yekaterinoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav) (modern Dnipro) in 1909

The first railway line in Ukrainian lands under the rule of the Russian Empire was opened in 1865 and connected Odesa with [Balta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balta,_Ukraine). In 1868-1870 the route was expanded to [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk) and Kyiv, establishing a link to [Moscow](19004.md). The main purpose of railway construction during that period was the export of grain and other agricultural products. Later, railways also became important for the transportation of [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and [ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore).[^cite_note-ency-4]

During the 1870s, railway lines connected Moscow with [Kharkiv](2788558.md), [Lozova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozova), [Sevastopol](51587.md) and [Mykolaiv](901495.md).[^cite_note-ency-4] In 1873, a railway line between [Kivertsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kivertsi), [Kovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovel) and [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus) was inaugurated, connecting Odesa and [Kyiv](585629.md) with the [Baltics](3335.md).[^cite_note-loc-3] Another [important route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libava%E2%80%93Romny_Railway) connected [Romny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romny) with Libava (now [Liepāja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liep%C4%81ja), Latvia), contributing to the export of Ukrainian grain over the Baltics. In 1884 a railway line connected the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) with [Kryvyi Rih](624764.md). As of 1914, the total length of railways in Ukrainian parts of the Russian Empire reached 16,000 kilometers.[^cite_note-ency-4]

#### World War I and Interwar period

Further information: [History of rail transport in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transport_in_Poland) and [Rail transport in the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_the_Soviet_Union)



The history of railway development in present-day Ukraine

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), a number of new railway lines were established in [frontline areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_I\)) due to strategic considerations. [Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_1917) and [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence) led to chaotic conditions on the railways, which started to retain their functions only during the 1920s.[^cite_note-ency-4] Authorities of the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic) and the [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State) dedicated special attention to railway infrastructure, and several prominent political figures of Ukraine during that time, such as [Vsevolod Holubovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsevolod_Holubovych) and [Borys Butenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borys_Butenko), were [railway engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_engineer) by profession. After the establishment of the [Directorate of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate_of_Ukraine), its leader [Symon Petliura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symon_Petliura) issued a decree, according to which all railway workers in Ukraine received an equal status to military servicemen.[^cite_note-kad-5]

Following the [Polish-Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Ukrainian_War), Galician railways became the property of [Polish State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_State_Railways), meanwhile railways in Bukovina came under [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) jurisdiction. Despite this, the line between Lviv and Chernivtsi preserved its great importance for international trade during the [Interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period).[^cite_note-loc-3] In [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine), around 4,000 kilometers of railways were constructed between the wars, chiefly in the Donbas, [Dnipro](989795.md) and [Kryvbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvbas) industrial regions. Important routes established during that time connected Kharkiv, [Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipropetrovsk) and [Kherson](1025861.md), and [Krasnyi Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnyi_Lyman) with [Kupiansk](5156384.md). Another major line created a railway link between [Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv) and [Ovruch](2936682.md) in the [Polesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesia). As a result of modernization and expansion of the network, between 1913 and 1940 transportation of freight increased from 61,7 to 200 millions of tons, and the number of railway passengers grew from 49 to 243 million.[^cite_note-ency-4]

#### World War II and aftermath



Territorial subdivisions of Ukrainian Railways

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Ukraine's railways suffered severe damage, with over 9,200 kilometers of tracks and over 2,000 bridges being demolished. By 1948, the railway system had largely restored its function, but many railway lines, especially in Galicia, continued to be disused. Electrification of railways progressed slowly. During the postwar period, Ukrainian railways were subordinated to the [all-Union ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Railways_\(Soviet_Union\)) in Moscow and administratively subdivided into several directorates.[^cite_note-ency-4]

Notable educational establishments which produced railway specialists functioned in Kharkiv and Dnipropetrovsk, and large train factories were active in [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk) ([Luhanskteplovoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhanskteplovoz)), Kharkiv, [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk) ([Kryukiv Railway Car Building Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryukiv_Railway_Car_Building_Works)), [Dniprodzerzhynsk](1261408.md) and [Darnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darnytsia).[^cite_note-ency-4]

### Early Ukrainian independence era (1991–2014)

See also: [Ukrainian Railways](3149341.md)



Ukrainian Railways headquarters in Kyiv

On 24 September 1991, following the resolution of the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) (Ukraine's parliament) on separation from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), all railroad administration was temporarily passed to the South-Western Railways. According to the resolution, all assets located within the borders of the former [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) became property of [Ukraine](31750.md). To improve efficiency a special centralized administration was created. On 14 December 1991 the [Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ministers_of_Ukraine) issued declaration No. 356 *"In creation of the State Administration of Railroad Transportation in Ukraine"* which proclaimed Ukrzaliznytsia a government body in administration railroad transportation uniting the six state railroad companies.[^cite_note-6]

### Russo-Ukrainian War (2014–present)

#### War in Donbas era (2014–2022)

At the outbreak of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) in 2014, the Russian Federation annexed Crimea and Sevastopol, while large parts of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts were seized by Russia-backed separatists, severely disrupting rail transport throughout southern and eastern Ukraine.

As of 2015 the Ukrainian government transformed the railways into a [public joint-stock company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_joint-stock_company) named *Ukrainian Railways* ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): *Ukrainska Zaliznytsia*).[^cite_note-7]

#### Full-scale Russian invasion (early 2022 phase)

The 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) showed the crucial role of railways in both civilian and military logistics in the area. Given the lack of roads passable during [rasputitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasputitsa) and the Russian lack of vehicles capable of off-road operation, logistics relied heavily on rail transport. Railway nodes became an important target of Russian attacks to maintain their own supply lines and disrupt those of Ukraine.[^cite_note-8] At the same time, Ukraine's exports and imports were shifted even more to rail than in peacetime as Russia captured or cut off many important [Black Sea](3386.md) ports that usually handle a large share of Ukraine's external trade.[^cite_note-9] Railways were crucial in transporting refugees and European governments, and state railways organized special trains for humanitarian aid to and from Ukraine.[^cite_note-10] As airports were targets of Russian attacks and/or Ukrainian counter-attacks, trains were also used for diplomatic visits by foreign heads of state and government.[^cite_note-11]

#### 2022 rail freight crisis

Further information: [Economic impact of the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine § Commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine#Commodities)

Due to the Russian invasion, many of the country's Black Sea ports were blocked, prompting a crisis in the export of agricultural products that were normally shipped. Railway freight has become the most viable alternative, but the Ukrainian railway network has not been able to cope with the demand, mainly because of the railway [break-of-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_of_gauge) between Ukraine's Soviet-era [1520 mm gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_ft_and_1520_mm_gauge_railways) and the [standard-gauge railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) (1435 mm) of states west of its borders has created bottlenecks at [transloading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transloading) stations.[^cite_note-Aloisi-12][^cite_note-van_Hoorn-13] On 7 April 2022, Ukrzaliznytsia reported that 10,320 [wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_wagon) (about half of the total) were waiting at the [Ustyluh (Izov)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustyluh)–[Hrubieszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrubiesz%C3%B3w) border crossing on the [Linia Hutnicza Szerokotorowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linia_Hutnicza_Szerokotorowa) line, the main railway connection between Ukraine and Poland and the longest 1,520 mm gauge [railway of Poland](13011368.md).[^cite_note-Aloisi-12]

Efforts to quickly increase rail freight capacity have been launched, including construction of new large transloading stations near [Mostyska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostyska) and elsewhere, mainly in Poland.[^cite_note-van_Hoorn-13] As of 12 April 2022, the Mostyka station was planned to be completed by June 2022, with a transloading capacity of 50,000 metric tonnes of grain per month by July, and 100,000 tonnes by September.[^cite_note-van_Hoorn-13] Additional challenges included the need to increase the number of wagons, appropriate permits to let Ukrainian wagons ride in EU territory, and to increase the capacity of EU ports (such as [Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Gdansk), [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hamburg) and [Rotterdam](4410985.md)) that would have to take over the shipping role of the blocked Black Sea ports.[^cite_note-van_Hoorn-13]

Some railway stations have been damaged due to the war.[^cite_note-14]

#### Restoration and modernisation (late 2022–present)



An Ukrzaliznytsia train on a broad gauge track in [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m), Poland

By October 2022, operations were underway to repair damaged railway infrastructure, while mines were cleared and unexploded ordnance were defused or safely detonated and removed.[^cite_note-Hoorn_Balduk-15] The railways had proven to serve an essential role for Ukrainian military, cilivian and humanitarian logistics, and the recapture of Russian-occupied train tracks seriously disrupted the logistics of invading forces.[^cite_note-Hoorn_Balduk-15] Ukrainian Railways was also in the process of planning the replacement of its [1,520 mm gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_ft_and_1520_mm_gauge_railways) with the 1,435 mm [standard-gauge railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) for future integration with the [European Union](9317.md)'s rail network.[^cite_note-Hoorn_Balduk-15]

In early February 2022 (just before the Russian invasion began), upon returning from a visit to Poland, Infrastructure Minister [Oleksandr Kubrakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Kubrakov), his deputy [Mustafa Nayyem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Nayyem) and Ukrzaliznytsia CEO [Oleksandr Kamyshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Kamyshin) announced that Ukraine and Poland had agreed that a [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) link would be built between Kyiv and Warsaw through [Yahodyn railway station](79499133.md),[^cite_note-Ukrinform-16] reducing travel times by four hours. Ukrzaliznytsia also decided to remove all restrictions on rail transit to Poland beginning on 10 February 2022.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] After the Russian invasion caused a lot of damage to Ukrainian railway and airport infrastructure, the Polish government in October 2022 offered to help Ukraine's post-war reconstruction, as well as adding an extra high-speed rail link between [Lublin](74594.md) and [Lviv](49968.md) through [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87) and [Bełżec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be%C5%82%C5%BCec_\(village\)).[^cite_note-Ukrinform-16]

### Transition to the standard gauge

See also: [Track gauge conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_gauge_conversion)

Since the 2010s, and especially since the February 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, much urge has been given to plans to build new standard-gauge connections between the largest cities and EU countries, and switch over parts of the Ukrainian network from 1,520 mm to standard gauge.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] Although switching the entire network over quickly would not be realistic, the construction or renewal of standard-gauge border crossings were deemed viable options to be prioritised.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19]

In February 2021, plans were drafted by the Ministry of Infrastructure for building four standard-gauge sections with a total length of 2,000 km, with a top train speed of over 250 km/h.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] The first and longest of these would be an 896 km-long track between Kyiv and Lviv, onwards to the Ukrainian-Polish border.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] The plans also included standard-gauge rail between [Chernivtsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi) and [Siret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siret), Romania.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] Restoration of the [Mostyska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostyska)-1 to [Rodatychi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodatychi) standard-gauge track is under discussion.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19]

A long-term plan is to turn Lviv into a European standard rail hub, but that major project would have to be performed in several smaller stages.[^cite_note-RailTech_02-10-23-22]

In July 2023, the European Commission published the "Strategy for the EU integration of the Ukrainian and Moldovan rail systems".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202335-23] A principal conclusion and recommendation of the study is to develop a new backbone Standard gauge network in Ukraine, to be operated in conjunction with the existing 1520mm network. The new backbone network would focus on higher speed intercity transportation, while the 1520mm system would cater for lower-speed transport (local and regional passenger traffic and heavy bulk).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202335-23] The development of the backbone network would be implemented in a phased manner, from West to East.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202335-23]

In April 2024, Ukraine commenced building the Standard gauge track between [Chop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chop,_Ukraine) and [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod) (22 km).[^cite_note-u336-24] After its completion in September 2025, Uzhhorod became the first administrative center ([oblast capital](994124.md)) of Ukraine connected to the EU by the standard gauge.[^cite_note-EC_Chop_Uzhhorod-25][^cite_note-u336-24]

In July 2025, the Ukrainian Railways announced that the EU committed the funding for the Standard gauge track from Poland to Lviv.[^cite_note-26] This 80-kilometre standard-gauge line, which is scheduled for completion by the end of 2027, was estimated in November 2025 to cost 190 million euros, of which 73.3 million would be funded by the [European Union](9317.md) through the Connecting Europe Facility programme.[^cite_note-NFP_Sknyliv-27]

### Rolling stock

Further information: [Ukrainian Railways § Rolling stock](3149341.md#Rolling_stock)

Because of the difference in gauges, most of Ukraine's rolling stock cannot move across standard gauge rail into neighbouring countries to its west, namely Poland, Slovakia, Hungary, and Romania.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] Although replacing the [bogies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogie) would make wagons compatible with most Romanian and Bulgarian railways, '\[Ukrainian] grain wagons have a width of 3,224 mm, while the maximum allowable one in many European countries is 3,150 mm, and the axle load of \[Ukrainian] wagons is up to 23.5 tons with the maximum allowable 18–20 tons in many neighboring countries. Therefore, in Hungary, Slovakia, and Poland, basically only European wagons can be used.'[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] Specialised freight fleets at [Kovel railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovel_railway_station) compatible with the European network can move across the [Chełm–Kovel railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m%E2%80%93Kovel_railway), Ukraine's longest standard-gauge track into Poland, but as of 2022 had not been in use for almost 30 years.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19]

## Infrastructure

Railways in Ukraine use various gauge types. The broad gauge (1,520 mm), a legacy of Imperial Russian and Soviet times, dominates the landscape, and [narrow-gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railways_in_Ukraine) (750 mm) also exist, but plans are being made to construct, restore or switch to more standard-gauge railways (1,435 mm) for better train connections with the rest of Europe.[^cite_note-Moiseev-19] About half of railways in Ukraine are electrified (some of which have been damaged during the ongoing [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) and temporarily switched to diesel trains).[^cite_note-UZ_stats_2020-36]

The total length of Ukrainian rails of all gauges was 28,000 kilometres at the end of 1997.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202318-37] Around January 2022, the length of railways in Ukraine was 19,790 km in broad-gauge 1520 mm (excluding those located in temporarily occupied territory), about 350 km in standard-gauge 1435 mm, and about 400 km in narrow gauge 750 mm.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202318-37]

#### Beskydy Tunnel

The [Beskydy Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beskydy_Tunnel) was inaugurated in May 2018, serving as the most important rail link between Ukraine and the [pan-European railway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_rail_network), handling 60% of Ukraine-EU freight traffic. The project was supported by the [European Union](9317.md), the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank) (EIB), and the [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development](37963.md) (EBRD).[^cite_note-53] The new tunnel more than doubles the output on the line, to 100 trains from the previous 45, and allows a speed increase from 40 km/h to 70 km/h.[^cite_note-54]

#### Kyiv – Vasylkiv electrification project

In 2021 the electrification of the track section [Vasylkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasylkiv) 1 – [Vasylkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasylkiv) 2 is planned, which will enable the launch of electric suburban rail between Kyiv and [Vasylkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasylkiv). The project involves the reconstruction of the [Boyarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyarka) [electrical substation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_substation), track works and a new passenger platform.[^cite_note-cfts_2020-09-16-55]

#### Zhytomyr – Zviahel line upgrade

In 2021, the line between [Zhytomyr](1025103.md) and [Zviahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zviahel) is planned to be modernized and electrified.[^cite_note-cfts_2020-09-16-55][^cite_note-56]

#### Kyiv – Cherkasy electrification

The project envisages the electrification of the 30 km section between Taras Shevchenko ([Smila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smila)) and [Cherkasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherkasy) stations, which is the only non-electrified section between Cherkasy and [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv-Pasazhyrskyi_railway_station). The project will enable the launch of INTERCITY+ services between Kyiv and Cherkasy, reducing travel time from 3 hours 41 minutes to 2 hours and 45 minutes.[^cite_note-57] Ukrainian Railways plans to carry out service using [Skoda City Elephant EJ675](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UZ_Class_675) double decker electric multiple units.[^cite_note-58]

Rail transport used for [mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit) is usually administered by local government, typically city authorities; this includes trams, subway (metro), funicular and others. There are rapid transit systems in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_metro), [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_metro) and [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_metro) as well as tram systems among which the [Kryvyi Rih Metrotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryvyi_Rih_Metrotram) contains underground sections.

In mountainous regions various narrow gauge railways are owned and operated privately, sometimes in the form of [heritage railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_railway).

## European integration

Ukraine's 19,790km railway network operates on 1,520mm broad gauge, incompatible with the European Union's 1,435mm standard gauge. Freight moving westbound requires transshipment at border crossings, adding 18-24 hours processing time and €42-55 per tonne in transfer costs, totalling €982 million to €1.29 billion annually for 23.4 million tonnes of westbound cargo. The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development's €2.1 billion rail modernisation programme finances dual gauge installation on the 847km Kyiv-Lviv-Polish border corridor, enabling both gauge operations and reducing freight transit times by 40%.[^cite_note-gtinvest-transport-61]

Three technical solutions were assessed: dual gauge installation (€12.4 billion for 4,200km), complete standard gauge conversion (€18.7 billion for full network), and automated transshipment facility expansion (€3.2 billion). The dual gauge approach was selected based on cost-effectiveness and operational continuity during construction.[^cite_note-gtinvest-transport-61]
