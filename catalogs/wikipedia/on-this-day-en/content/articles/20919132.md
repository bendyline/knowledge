The coastline of the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) is the fourth longest in the world after the coastlines of [Canada](5042916.md), [Greenland](12118.md), and [Indonesia](14579.md). The Russian [fishing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry) has an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ) of 7.6 million km<sup>2</sup> including access to twelve seas in three oceans, together with the landlocked [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) and more than two million rivers.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

According to the [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO), in 2005 the Russian [fishing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry) harvested 3,190,946 [tonnes](31185.md) of fish from [wild fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_fisheries) and another 114,752 tonnes from [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture). This made Russia the ninth leading producer of fish, with 2.3 percent of the world total.[^cite_note-FAO_2005-4]

## Management

[Fisheries management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_management) is regulated by Russian federal laws. The federal law "On Fisheries and Protection of Aquatic Biological Resources" of December 2004 (referred to below as the *Law on Fisheries*) divides fisheries into three main categories" industrial, recreational, and subsistence fisheries of [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) groups. Industrial fisheries includes coastal fisheries. This definition has been challenged and is under review.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

The *Law on Fisheries* requires that [total allowable catch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_allowable_catch) (TAC) levels are set for fishery stocks. It defines these levels as the "scientifically justified annual catch of aquatic biological resources of particular species in a fishing area". However, the *Law on Fisheries* then goes on to state that industrial fisheries are not necessarily required to base their catch on TAC. The law does not explain this further, but calls for the federal government to issue a special TAC setting statute. Pacific salmon is the main stock that will probably not have TAC, but will have regulated [fishing effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_effort) instead.

The *Law on Fisheries* also gives a definition of a fishing unit area and sets general principles for their use. The compiling of lists of fishing unit areas is delegated to the regional authorities. The *Law on Fisheries* has gaps and its application is criticized by parliamentarians and stakeholders. It may be expected that in the coming years at least two new federal laws, "On Coastal Fisheries" and "On Aquaculture", will be considered by Russian legislators.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

Apart from TAC settings, fisheries are also regulated by the so-called *Fishing Rules* (Pravila rybolovstva). These rules are set separately for different geographical regions.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

The *Fishing Rules* specify seasonal closures, closed areas, restrictions on specific gears such as restricting mesh sizes, minimum catch sizes, and restricted levels of allowable [bycatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bycatch). Fisheries management has been changing since Soviet times, and further changes are likely.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

The government has mismanaged the fisheries, with frequent restructuring of the institutions responsible for fishery management and control. Starting in 1992, the fishery authority has been reorganized at least five times. The head of the fishery authority was replaced seven times, and not one of these heads was a fishery professional. The issues involved in regulating fishing capacity were never really recognized. However, consistent fishery policies are starting to be developed now.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

The extreme bureaucracy involved for a fishing vessel to make a port call and land fish results in coastal processing being bypassed. Instead, the seafood is just directly exported, unprocessed. Similarly, there are many bureaucratic difficulties in developing aquaculture. Getting a licence to use water and the necessary sanitary certificates is very time-consuming, although it does guarantee environmental and health safety.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3] Ships built, purchased, or serviced outside of the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md) face significant restrictions in landing catches caught in Russia's EEZ, and from 2022 will not be allocated any fishing quotas in Russian waters.[^cite_note-8]

### Artisanal



Fishing vessels off a jetty, believed to be [Kostroma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostroma) (Russia) Oil on canvas, 1839, by [Anton Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Ivanov-Goluboy)

There is no legally adopted term in Russia for [artisanal fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan_fishing). Artisanal or subsistence fishing usually refers to fishing mainly with traditional gear, with production delivered to the market but also used for subsistence. In Russia, the term covers also several kinds of fisheries classified as industrial, such as [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon), [chars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvelinus), [whitefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_whitefish), [navaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navaga), [flounders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flounder) and [greenling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenling) fisheries in the Baltic, the Arctic and the Far Eastern Seas. Subsistence fishing by [indigenous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_group) is also an issue. Indigenous fishers mainly work estuaries, lagoons and rivers (for [anadromous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadromous) fish). Legally, they are bound to use their catch for local consumption only. They are not allowed to sell their catch, but in reality, this is not always the case.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

In Russia, poverty contributes to poaching and other threats to fishery resources. Poverty can leave people depending on natural resources to feed themselves. There may be little perceived incentive to protect fish and other aquatic life and to use them in a sustainable way. Lack of awareness and lack of public involvement in managing local resources can result in poaching, [overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing), and other kinds of illegal activities. Poaching by private individuals feeds the industrial [IUU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUU) catch, and forms a vicious cycle.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

The social impacts of traditional fisheries has rarely been analysed. The yearly fishing cycle still dominates life in the [traditional fishing villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_fishing_village) of the [Pomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomor), dotted around the coast of the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea).[^cite_note-9] Fishing has similarly influenced the life style of many indigenous groups, such as among settlers around the Pacific Coast, north of Siberia, and around the big lakes. In the late 1960s, administrative decisions were made to abandon many coastal villages and resettle people in larger settlements. This has disrupted the traditional ways and is associated with alcohol abuse and increased poverty. There is now a slow movement towards reviving cultural traditions. To succeed, there must also be a re-establishment of the sustainable fisheries that allowed such fishing communities to flourish.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

### Recreational

[Recreational fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_fishing) occurs everywhere in Russia. The *Fishing Rules* do not distinguish recreational fishing from [artisan fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan_fishing), so both are regulated under the same rules. In some areas, tourist fishing is growing.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

In 1999, recreational and subsistence fishers took 4,300 tonnes, mostly [perches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perch) and [cyprinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprinid).[^cite_note-10] Later estimates are not available. The most significant recreational fishery by value is the [Kola Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_Peninsula) [Atlantic salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_salmon) fishery.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

- inland fisheries

- aquaculture

## Wild fisheries

### EEZ



Relief map of Russia

Russia's marine fisheries are based on twelve seas from three oceans which surround Russia, the landlocked [Caspian Sea](19653787.md), and the [high seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_seas) beyond Russia's [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ).

The three oceans are:[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

- the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic): with the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), [Black Sea](3386.md), [Baltic](3335.md), [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea) and [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea)

- the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean): with the [Kara Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Sea), [Laptev Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptev_Sea), [East Siberian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Siberian_Sea) and [Chuckchi Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuckchi_Sea)

- the [Pacific](23070.md): with the [Bering Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea), [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk) and [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan).

Marine capture fisheries in Russia's [territorial seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_sea), internal marine waters and the EEZ provided up to 75 percent of the total reported catch for 1996–2005.

### Catch profile

The officially recorded annual value of fisheries is about US\$5 billion, equivalent to 0.3 percent of GDP. The fishery sector has been stable in absolute terms in recent years, so its share of GDP has reduced as the general economy has expanded.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

### Inland fisheries



[Omul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omul) fish, endemic to [Lake Baikal](56865.md). Smoked and on sale at [Listvyanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listvyanka) market.

The biggest inland water is the landlocked [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). The biggest lakes are [Baikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal_Lake) (31,700 km<sup>2</sup>), [Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladoga_Lake) (19,100 km<sup>2</sup>) and [Onega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onega_Lake) (9,700 km<sup>2</sup>). Russia has more than 2 million rivers, the largest of which are, in order, [Severnaya Dvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dvina_River), [Pechora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechora_River), [Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River), [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), [Ob'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ob_River), [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)), [Yenisei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisei_River), [Lena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River), [Kolyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolyma_River), [Indigirka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigirka_River) and [Amur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_River).[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3] The most important inland fishing area is the Ob'–Irtysh River Basin (about 27 percent). Sixty species are caught in the inland fisheries of Russia.[^cite_note-11] In volume terms, [whitefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_whitefish) (Coregonidae), [cyprinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprinid), [zanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zander) and [perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perch) are most important. Set nets are the most common gear used in inland water commercial fisheries. [Seines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_fishing) are also used on big rivers and lakes, and small [trawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawl) on the big lakes. In 2005, the official catch in the inland waters was 72,000 tonnes.[^cite_note-ministry_2006-12]

In the past, [sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon) has been an important catch in the basin of the [Sea of Azov](78019.md) and the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md), and in Siberian Rivers and the [Amur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_River). Currently, sturgeon stocks are heavily depleted and under constant pressure from poaching. Inland fisheries are regulated by the *Law on Fisheries* discussed above. However, few provisions refer specifically to inland fisheries, although there are specific regulations for same catchments and river systems. These regulations specify closed areas, seasonal closures, gear restrictions, minimum mesh sizes and minimum catch size.[^cite_note-ministry_2006-12]

### Fishing fleet



The Russian [fishing trawler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler) *Sergey Makarevich* in the North Atlantic.



The [Russian](25391.md) midwater fishing trawler [SRT-129](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%A0%D0%A2-129) (Museum of the World Ocean in [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad))

According to the Russian State Marine Register, in 2002, the offshore [fishing fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_fleet) contained about 2,500 [fishing vessels](10079376.md), 366 transport vessels and 46 [factory ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_ship). Of the fishing vessels, 17 percent were longer than 64 metres ([o/a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_overall)), half were between 34 and 64 metres, and one-third were between 24 and 34 metres. Smaller boats are registered with the State Inspection of Small Size Fleet. In 2005, the marine small size fleet contained 2,491 boats, and the inland fleet contained 5,500 motor boats.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

Fishing gears used are:[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

- [Midwater trawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwater_trawling) – used by [processing trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_ship#Factory_stern_trawler) and [freezing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_ship#Freezing_trawler) for [redfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redfish) and [Alaska pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_pollock).

- [Bottom trawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_trawling) – restricted use by medium and large sized [trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler), for [demersal fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demersal_fish) such as [halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halibut), [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod), [redfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redfish), [flounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flounder).

- Shrimp trawls – used by specialised [shrimp trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler#Outrigger_trawlers)

- Bottom nets – used by small and midsize vessels for flounder, cod and halibut

- Bottom [seines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_fishing) – deployed by small vessels for flounder, cod, halibut and other demersal fishes.

- [Drift nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drift_net) – used by midsize vessels, mainly for [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon).

- Bottom [longlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longline_fishing) – used for halibut, cod and redfish.

- [Traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trap) and [pots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster_pot) – used by small and midsize vessels for [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp), [crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crab) and [whelks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whelk),

- Seines and pound nets – for [herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herring) and [whitefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_whitefish)

- [Dredges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_dredge) – operated from small vessels for [clams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clams).

- Small boats – used with salmon kiddles (basketwork traps), and for [skindivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-diving) harvesting [scallops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scallop), [sea urchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_urchin), [kelp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp) and [sea cucumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holothuroidea).

An important issue is the age of the Russian fishing fleet. About two-thirds of the fishing vessels do not conform to safety norms. Compared to 1990, by 2000 capital investment in the industry had decreased thirty percent and the number of specialists qualified in fishing, navigation and processing technologies had decreased 30 to 40 percent.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea) cod fishery is an example of the dominance of elderly and ineffective vessels.[^cite_note-15] Between 2002 and 2005, forty percent of effort in the [demersal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demersal_fish) fishery was by elderly freezing trawlers, which produced only twenty-five percent of the official catch. That is, they were 1.5 times less effective than the other vessels in the fleet. Equivalent modern trawlers are three to four times as effective. The low efficiencies of these elderly vessels also implicates them in involvements with [IUU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUU) catch.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

### Decline of stocks

According to the [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO), important stocks have declined as the result of:[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

- natural fluctuations: [Pacific pilchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_pilchard?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- a combination of natural fluctuations and [overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing): [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_herring) and [Pacific herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_herring), [Alaska pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_pollock), [capelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capelin) in the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea)

- overfishing and continuing [IUU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUU): [sturgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon), [Atlantic salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_salmon), [red king crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_king_crab), [sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holothuroidea)

- a combination of [marine pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution) and overfishing: [whitefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_whitefish) and Atlantic salmon in the [Pechora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechora) drainage basin, whitefish and sturgeon in the [Ob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ob_River) drainage basin, most of the stocks in the [Amur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_River) Basin

- ecosystem transformation due to the introduction of [invasive species](67554.md): [sprat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprat) in the [Black](3386.md) and the [Caspian Seas](19653787.md).

Aggravating factors surround the demand for seafood from East Asian markets, which encourage commercial fishermen to exhaust stocks in Russia's EEZ. Russian illegal exporters have well oiled links to importers in Japan, China and South Korea. Criminal groups and corruption magnifies the effect, as the short distances needed to transport seafood from south [Kurils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurils) and south [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin) to Japan. Huge fish processing developments in China built on cheap labour encourage the export of further unprocessed fish.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

## Aquaculture

Over sixty species of fish, [invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate) and [seaweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed) are commercially cultivated by [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture) or [fish farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farm) in Russia.[^cite_note-federal_2006-5] Aquaculture is based mainly on [buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ictiobus), [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_carp) and [silver carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_carp), [rainbow trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_trout), [scallops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scallop), [mussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussel) and [laminaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminaria). In 2007 there were 300 aquaculture enterprises.[^cite_note-16]

Aquaculture can be freshwater or marine ([mariculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariculture)):

- Freshwater aquaculture – occurs northwest of [European Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Russia) where a lot of [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout) are farmed, in the Far East, and south of Siberia. Production 2003 to 2006 was about 100,000 tonnes.

  [^cite_note-federal_2006-5]

- Mariculture – occurs mainly in [Primorye Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai) on the coast of the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan). In 2006, marine farms in Primorye covered 10,000 hectares, which produces 1,340 tonnes, mainly of Laminaria, blue mussel and the scallop *[Mizuhopecten yessoensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizuhopecten_yessoensis)*.

  [^cite_note-17]

Potential development areas for freshwater aquaculture include 960,000 hectares of agricultural water bodies, 143,000 hectares of ponds, plus other areas in big lakes and water reservoirs suitable for cage farming. The National Project on Agricultural Sector development (Federal Agency of Fishery, 2006) has set a target for 2020 of 1.4 million tonnes from freshwater aquaculture and 400 thousand tonnes from mariculture. The federal government is considering a subsidy of two-thirds of the credit needed to construct and modernise aquaculture facilities.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

## Research

In Soviet Union, the [Ministry of the Fishing Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Fishing_Industry) operated numerous [research institutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_institute) with R\&D focus on [oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography), [marine biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology), [fishery management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery_management), assessment of fishing resources, the development of fishing gear and [fish processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_processing) technologies. The ministry had its own [research ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_ship) which conducted research for the Soviet distant water fisheries.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

In the modern Russia, the research institutes, particularly responsible for the research in [fisheries science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_science), were coordinated by VNIRO, the central fishery institute in [Moscow](19004.md). In 2007, independent institutes became subordinate branches of VNIRO with different scientific focus. For instance, [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md)-based GIPRORYBFLOT works in fishing vessels technologies and fish processing, while the VIERH in Moscow does economic research.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

## Education

By 2007, five technological universities and four professional schools in Russia trained specialists in fisheries. The educational programs included navigation and marine engineering, fishery biology, fish processing, processing machinery, the economics of fisheries, and aquaculture.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

The faculties of Geography of [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) and [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) (MSU), the faculty of Biology of MSU, the [Far Eastern National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_National_University), [Kazan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_State_University) and [Perm State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University) considered the most important educational facilities for the fishing industry. Around 120 aquaculture specialists and numerous specialists in fish biology and fishery oceanography graduate each year.[^cite_note-FAO_profile-3]

## Industry

The fishing industry suffered from the [collapse of Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_Soviet_Union): the harvest dropped dramatically and only started to recover in the mid-1990s. The reasons for the decline in annual harvest included the decrease in stock due to aggressive Soviet fishing practices, changes in the use of the open sea fishing areas, low effectiveness, and problems in the value chain on the local market, including lack of regulations and unaffordable lending. Due to weak national currency, fishing companies had little incentive to deal with barriers on the local market and prioritized exports with payments in the Western currency.[^cite_note-Nilssen-2021-18]

To modernize the outdated Soviet fishing fleets, many private companies accepted bareboat charter contracts (BBC) with foreign investors, the [leasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leasing) contracts that locked Russian fishing companies into the delivery of catches to foreign management companies. The number of BBC contracts increased from 1994 onwards.[^cite_note-19] The absence of trade protectionism measures additionally incentivized Russian fishing companies to deliver fish directly to foreign buyers both in the Northwest and the Far East, providing no benefit to the Russian government in terms of taxes or currency. The domestic supply decreased, pushing up prices making Russia a lucrative market for foreign fish companies. From the 1990s to the mid-2000s, the import of fish to Russia increased from 424 thousand tonnes to nearly 1 million tonnes, while domestic supply decreased from 3.3 million tonnes to a record low level of 2.5 million tonnes.[^cite_note-Nilssen-2021-18]

In the 2000s, the Russian authorities addressed the issues of the decreasing volume and value of domestic seafood production and growing dependence on seafood exports. In 2003, the "Concept for Development of the Fishing Industry of the Russian Federation to 2020" was approved by the government. In 2009, the BBC contracts were effectively banned in the Russian [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone). The 2010 [Food Security Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_Security_Doctrine_\(Russia\)) set the food independence and [food security](216361.md) goals and defined the target proportion of seafood exports and imports. According to the document, the domestic fish catch in Russia should account for no less than 80% of the total seafood consumption. The program goals included renewing the fishing fleet and land-based processing industry by introducing the fishery quota. Basically, the government allocated 20% of the total allowable catch for the companies willing to invest in new vessels to be built on domestic shipyards.[^cite_note-Nilssen-2021-18]

By 2019, [Norebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norebo) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Норебо), owned by Vitaly Orlov, was the largest fishing holding in Russia. That year, its 16 companies totaled 58,2 billion rubles in revenue. It was the only fishing company on the [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes) list of the largest privately owned companies in Russia.[^cite_note-forbes-2019-20]

The other top ten of the largest fishing companies included Hydrostroy ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Гидрострой) owned by the [Federation Council](1268578.md) member [Alexander Verkhovskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Verkhovskiy) and the associates of [Roman Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich); SZRK ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): СЗРК, Северо-Западный рыболовный консорциум) owned by Gennady Mirgorodsky and Dmitry Ozersky; the companies of Igor Evtushok and Valery Ponomarev; the Russian Fishery Company owned by Gleb Frank; Salmonica owned by Nikita Kozhemyako; the companies of Maksim Petrushin and Sergey Popov; FEST Group ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Группа ФЭСТ) owned by Sergey Prutkov; FOR Group ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Группа ФОР) owned by [Ilya Klebanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Klebanov); and NBAMR ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): НБАМР) owned by the ex-governor of [Primorsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai) [Sergey Mikhailovich Darkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Mikhaylovich_Darkin).[^cite_note-forbes-2019-20]

### Export

Despite being one of the key global exporters of fish (specifically, [pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollock), [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod), and [herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herring)), Russia has a notable import dependency on [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon). The reasons include the underdeveloped [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture), light export regulations, and distorted production and consumption geography: 85% of salmon is harvested in the [Far East](11672.md) but consumed elsewhere, and selling fish domestically means dealing with expensive and unpredictable logistics.[^cite_note-21]
