# Paul Townend

Not to be confused with [Paul Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Townsend).



Paul Townend (right) riding Un De Sceaux at [Sandown Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandown_Park_Racecourse) in 2016

**Paul Townend** (born 15 September 1990) is an [Irish](775859.md) [jockey](284545.md) who competes in [National Hunt racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hunt_racing). From [Lisgoold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisgoold) in [County Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork), he has been attached to the yard of Irish trainer [Willie Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Mullins) since he was fifteen, beginning his career as an apprentice [flat racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_racing) jockey. Following the retirement of [Ruby Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Walsh) in 2019, Townend became the number one jockey to the [Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Mullins) yard.&#x20;

In [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cheltenham_Gold_Cup) he gave [Willie Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Mullins) his first [Cheltenham Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheltenham_Gold_Cup) winner with [Al Boum Photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Boum_Photo) and he repeated this success in 2020. He recorded his third and fourth win in the race on [Galopin Des Champs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galopin_Des_Champs) in 2023 and 2024, and his fifth win in 2026 with [Gaelic Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Warrior). He won the 2023 [Irish Grand National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Grand_National), the [2024 Aintree Grand National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Grand_National) and the [2026 Aintree Grand National](82918023.md) on [I Am Maximus](82927168.md) for Mullins. He is a seven-time [Irish jump racing Champion Jockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_jump_racing_Champion_Jockey) (2010/11, 2018/19, 2019/20, 2020/21, 2021/22, 2022/23 and 2024/25 seasons).

## Early life

Townend (born 15 September 1990)[^cite_note-HRI-1] comes from a racing background and grew up on a small farm in east County Cork.[^cite_note-Gloss-2][^cite_note-Biginterview-3] His father Tim trains [point-to-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-to-point_\(steeplechase\)) horses, his youngest sister [Jody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jody_Townend) is an amateur jockey, and a cousin is retired jockey [Davy Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Condon).[^cite_note-Biginterview-3][^cite_note-4] He attended primary school in the village of Lisgoold and then went on to [Midleton CBS Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midleton_CBS_Secondary_School).[^cite_note-Gloss-2] While at school, he competed in pony racing.[^cite_note-Biginterview-3] Having lost his mother Josephine to cancer, he left school after junior certificate and became an apprentice at the yard of trainer Willie Mullins, where his cousin was a jockey.[^cite_note-Gloss-2][^cite_note-5]

## Career

Townend started his racing career riding on the flat. His first win was on The Chip Chopman in an apprentice handicap at [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_Racecourse) on 22 June 2007, when he was sixteen. He rode ten winners that season, but switched to riding over fences as a conditional jockey in 2008 due to his weight.[^cite_note-Grand-6] In July 2008 he rode Indian Pace to victory in the [Galway Hurdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_Hurdle). There were no more wins until November 2008, when he rode Hurricane Fly to victory in the [Royal Bond Novice Hurdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bond_Novice_Hurdle) on his first ride in a Grade 1 for Mullins. He had been given the ride because Mullins' number one jockey was sidelined with an injury.[^cite_note-Biginterview-3] On New years Eve 2008 he rode out his claim by winning a treble at [Tramore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramore_Racecourse).[^cite_note-7] He had his first ride in the [Grand National](74730.md) in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Grand_National), coming eleventh on Irish Invader.[^cite_note-Grand-6] He rode his first [Cheltenham Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheltenham_Festival) winner in 2011, when What A Charm, trained by Arthur Moore, won the [Fred Winter Juvenile Novices' Handicap Hurdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Winter_Juvenile_Novices'_Handicap_Hurdle).[^cite_note-HRI-1] That season he became [Irish jump racing Champion Jockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_jump_racing_Champion_Jockey) for the first time.[^cite_note-Biginterview-3]

A bizarre incident in the [Growise Champion Novice Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dooley_Insurance_Group_Champion_Novice_Chase) at the 2018 [Punchestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punchestown_Festival) Festival cost Townend a 21 day ban for dangerous riding. Riding [Al Boum Photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Boum_Photo) for Mullins, he had mistakenly thought that the last fence was to be bypassed and carried out another horse.[^cite_note-8] The ban did not take effect until the start of the 2018/19 season, and Townend gained a treble the day after the incident.[^cite_note-9] In March 2019 Townend rode Al Boum Photo in the [Cheltenham Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheltenham_Gold_Cup), winning by two-and-a-half lengths to give both Townend and Mullins a first win in the race.[^cite_note-10] Later that month, Townend achieved his maiden century, riding his 100th Irish winner at [Clonmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonmel_Racecourse).[^cite_note-11] At the end of the 2018/19 season he was Irish jump racing Champion Jockey for the second time with 109 winner.[^cite_note-HRI-1] When Walsh retired in May 2019, Townend became number one jockey for Mullins.[^cite_note-12]

In 2020 Townend became top jockey at the Cheltenham Festival for the first time. He had 5 winners including a second win on Al Boum Photo in Gold Cup.[^cite_note-13] At the end of the 2019/20 season Townend was crowned Irish jump racing Champion Jockey for the third time. He held the title for the next five seasons.[^cite_note-HRI-1] He was top jockey at the Cheltenham festival in 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026.[^cite_note-HRI-1] He won the [Queen Mother Champion Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mother_Champion_Chase) three times. He won riding Energumene in 2023 and 2024, while in 2026 he won the race for the third time on Il Etait Temps. He achieved back-to-back victories in the Gold Cup on [Galopin Des Champs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galopin_Des_Champs), equalling [Pat Taaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Taaffe)'s record of four wins in the race in 2024 and breaking the record in 2026 with Gaelic Warrior. In 2024 he won the [Champion Hurdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champion_Hurdle) on State Man and won the race in 2026 on Lossiemouth.[^cite_note-HRI-1]

In April 2023, Townend won the [Irish Grand National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Grand_National) with [I Am Maximus](82927168.md), owned by [JP McManus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._P._McManus) and trained by Mullins.[^cite_note-14] The following year, the partnership won the [Aintree Grand National](74730.md).[^cite_note-15] It was Townend's thirteenth ride in the race. He had only completed the course on three occasions, coming eleventh on Irish Invader in 2009, sixteenth on Prince De Beauchene in 2014 and third on Gaillard Du Mesnil in 2023.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] In the [2025 Grand National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Grand_National), Townend again rode I Am Maximus, who finished second to [Nick Rockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Rockett). In [2026](82918023.md), I Am Maximus, with Townend riding, regained the Grand National, the first horse since [Red Rum](2545868.md) to do so.[^cite_note-18]

## Personal life

Townend lives in [Co Kilkenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Kilkenny) with his girlfriend Sophie Vard Ryan and three Shetland ponies.[^cite_note-Gloss-2]
