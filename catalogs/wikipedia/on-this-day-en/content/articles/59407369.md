The **Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency** (**CISA**) is a component of the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) responsible for [cybersecurity](7398.md) and [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) infrastructure protection. It oversees all levels of the federal government and coordinates with [U.S. states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._states) to improve cybersecurity against private and nation-state [hackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker).[^cite_note-zdnet-4] The CISA is headquartered in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia).

The agency began in 2007 as the **National Protection and Programs Directorate** (**NPPD**).[^cite_note-zdnet-4][^cite_note-aboutcisa-5] With the [Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency Act of 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybersecurity_and_Infrastructure_Security_Agency_Act_of_2018), CISA's footprint expanded to include roles protecting the [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_census), managing [National Special Security Events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Special_Security_Event), and the [U.S. response to the COVID-19 pandemic](63136490.md). It has also been involved in overseeing [5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G) network security, [securing elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_security), and strengthening the US grid against [electromagnetic pulses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_pulse) (EMPs).[^cite_note-6] The Office for Bombing Prevention leads the national [counter-IED effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-IED_efforts).[^cite_note-7]

## History

The National Protection and Programs Directorate (NPPD) was formed in 2007 as a component of the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md).[^cite_note-8] NPPD's goal was to advance the Department's [national security](240468.md) mission by reducing and eliminating threats to U.S. critical physical and cyber infrastructure. The NPPD oversaw five subordinate offices, including the [Federal Protective Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Federal_Protective_Service) (FPS) and the [Office of Biometric Identity Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Biometric_Identity_Management) (OBIM).[^cite_note-zdnet-4]

On November 16, 2018, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) signed into law the [Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency Act of 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybersecurity_and_Infrastructure_Security_Agency_Act_of_2018) (or CISA Act), which established CISA, effectively elevating NPPD to the level of a federal agency, thereby opening the door to an increased budget and authority.[^cite_note-zdnet-4][^cite_note-\:0-9] OBIM was moved from CISA to the [DHS Management Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHS_Management_Directorate), while the fate of FPS had not been determined at the time of CISA's creation.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-\:1-10] As of 2020, CISA assisted other government agencies, including state and local agencies, and private sector organizations in addressing cybersecurity issues, representing a major expansion of activities beyond those of NPPD.[^cite_note-11] Former NPPD under-secretary [Christopher Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_C._Krebs) was CISA's first Director, and former deputy under-secretary [Matthew Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Travis) was its first deputy director.[^cite_note-zdnet-4][^cite_note-\:1-10][^cite_note-12]

On January 22, 2019, CISA issued its first Emergency Directive (19-01: Mitigate DNS Infrastructure Tampering)[^cite_note-ed-19-01-13] warning that "an active attacker is targeting government organizations" using [DNS spoofing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNS_spoofing) techniques to perform [man-in-the-middle attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-in-the-middle_attacks).[^cite_note-cisa-blog-feb-19-14] Research group FireEye stated that "initial research suggests the actor or actors responsible have a nexus to Iran".[^cite_note-fireeye-feb-15]

In 2020, CISA created a website, titled *Rumor Control*, to rebut [disinformation](142690.md) associated with the [2020 United States presidential election](48410011.md).[^cite_note-16] On November 12, 2020, CISA issued a press release asserting, "There is no evidence that any voting system deleted or lost votes, changed votes, or was in any way compromised."[^cite_note-17] On the same day, Director Krebs indicated that he expected to be dismissed from his post by the Trump administration.[^cite_note-topcyber-18] Krebs was subsequently fired by President Trump on November 17, 2020[^cite_note-19] via tweet for his comments regarding the security of the election.[^cite_note-20] According to various reports and statistics, the scale and frequency of cyber-attacks have been steadily increasing in recent years. For example, the number of data breaches reported in 2020 alone reached a record high of 3,932, a 48% increase compared to the previous year, with over 37 billion records exposed globally, and also the average cost of a data breach in 2020 was estimated to be \$3.86 million, with an average time to identify and contain a breach of 280 days.[^cite_note-21]



Secretary of Homeland Security [Alejandro Mayorkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Mayorkas) at CISA's current headquarters in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), in 2021

On July 12, 2021, the [Senate](24909346.md) confirmed [Jen Easterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jen_Easterly) by a [voice vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_vote).[^cite_note-22] Easterly's nomination had been reported favorably out of [Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Homeland_Security_and_Governmental_Affairs) on June 16, but a floor vote had been reportedly [held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_hold) (delayed) by Senator [Rick Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Scott) over broader national security concerns, until the President or Vice President had visited the southern border with Mexico.[^cite_note-23] Easterly hired new staff to monitor online disinformation to enhance what she called the nation's "cognitive infrastructure" and utilized the existing rumor control website during the 2021 elections.[^cite_note-24]

In September 2022, CISA released its 2023–2025 CISA Strategic Plan, the first comprehensive strategy document since the agency's establishment in 2018.[^cite_note-25]

In 2025, CISA began dismantling parts of its organization at the direction of the Trump administration.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] A lapse in DHS funding in early 2026 forced the agency to scale back even more, resulting in the layoff of more than one-third of its staff since January 2025.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

In May 2026 it was reported that CISA had left exposed access keys to several important governmental functions.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

## Programs

The Continuous Diagnostics and Mitigations program provides cybersecurity tools and services to federal agencies.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

CISA issues "binding operational directives" that require federal government agencies to take action against specific cybersecurity risks.[^cite_note-36]

In March 2021, CISA assumed control of the [.gov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.gov) [top-level domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top-level_domain) (TLD) from the [General Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Services_Administration). CISA manages the approval of domains and operates the TLD [Domain Name System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_Name_System) nameservers. In April 2021, CISA removed the fee for registering domains.[^cite_note-37] In January 2023, [Cloudflare](32760230.md) received a \$7.2M contract to provide DNS registry and hosting services for the TLD.[^cite_note-38]

CISA provides incident response services to the federal executive branch and US-based entities.

CISA manages the [EINSTEIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_\(US-CERT_program\)) [intrusion detection system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrusion_detection_system) to detect malicious activity on federal government agency networks.

The [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_M._\(Mac\)_Thornberry_National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2021) granted CISA the authority to issue [administrative subpoenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_subpoena) in order to identify the owners of internet connected critical infrastructure related devices with specific vulnerabilities. In 2021, CISA issued 47 subpoenas.[^cite_note-39]

In 2021, CISA released a report that provided guidance for how to navigate and prevent [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware) incidents. This was due to a significant jump in recent attacks related to ransomware.[^cite_note-40]
