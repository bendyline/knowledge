Parliamentary elections were held in [Guinea](12176.md) on 31 May 2026. This was the first legislative election since the [2021 Guinean coup d'état](68651369.md), in which President [Alpha Condé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Cond%C3%A9) was deposed by General [Mamady Doumbouya](68651894.md).

Guinea presented the election as the final step in its post-coup [democratic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_transition), however major opposition parties were barred from running. Doumbouya's electoral coalition, the [Generation for Modernity and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_for_Modernity_and_Development), received 64% of the vote and 93 of the 147 seats in the [National Assembly](3623664.md); with its allies, the coalition received over 100 seats. The opposition boycotted the election, with observers noting low [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout).

## Background

Military general [Mamadi Doumbouya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamadi_Doumbouya) has led Guinea since the [2021 Guinean coup d'état](68651369.md), which deposed the previous president [Alpha Condé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Cond%C3%A9). [Amnesty International](18947898.md) has described a "climate of terror" following the coup, as the military junta has cracked down on opposition parties, civil society, and journalists.[^cite_note-1] By September 2025, more than 220 people had been killed for opposing the government.[^cite_note-2]

In September 2025, a [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Guinean_constitutional_referendum) passed, which extended the term limits of the [presidency](392253.md) and reformatted the Senate. In December 2025, Doumbouya was [elected president](79968772.md). Regional blocs [ECOWAS](36848.md) and the [African Union](10634933.md) lifted their sanctions on Guinea following the election, citing a transition to democracy.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] However, United Nations rights commissioner [Volker Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Turk) observed that the suppression of opposition parties and the media risked "undermining the credibility of the electoral process".[^cite_note-5]

On 6 March 2026, the [General Directorate of Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Directorate_of_Elections?action=edit\&redlink=1) dissolved 40 political parties, including the three main opposition parties in the country: [Union of Democratic Forces of Guinea](28077599.md) (UDFG), [Rally of the Guinean People](2726867.md), and [Union of Republican Forces](28077785.md).[^cite_note-6] The opposition initiated a [boycott](18591996.md) of the legislative election in response.[^cite_note-Reuters-7] UDFG's exiled leader and former prime minister, [Cellou Dalein Diallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellou_Dalein_Diallo), called for "direct resistance".[^cite_note-8] Several ministers of the [National Council of the Transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_the_Transition) controversially had their candidacies approved, despite the council's charter prohibiting its members from running for office.[^cite_note-ISS-9]

On 10 April 2026, Doumbouya announced the postponement of the elections to 31 May 2026 from 24 May 2026.[^cite_note-10] The campaign period opened on 1 May and ended on 28 May.[^cite_note-CampaignPeriod-11] Local elections take place at the same time.[^cite_note-CampaignPeriod-11] The opposition [Democratic Front of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Front_of_Guinea?action=edit\&redlink=1) said one of its candidates was attacked by hooded individuals in [Mamou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamou) on 30 May.[^cite_note-12]

## Electoral system

The [2025 constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Guinean_constitutional_referendum) approved a bicameral legislature. The lower chamber, the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Guinea), will be directly elected. It will have 147 seats: 98 constituency seats and 49 national seats allocated to provide [proportional representation](23298.md) using a [closed list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_list).[^cite_note-guinee360-13] Of the 98 constituency seats, 21 are elected by [first-past-the-post](261709.md) in single-member constituencies (including 4 elected by overseas diaspora voters), and 76 are elected by [plurality block voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_block_voting) in 29 constituencies ranging in size from two to five seats.

Following the legislative election, two-thirds of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Guinea) will be indirectly elected and one-third of the Senate will be appointed by the president per the new constitution.[^cite_note-visionguinee-14][^cite_note-15]

## Analysis

Doumbouya's [landslide victory](41053306.md) was expected by observers, given that all the main opposition parties were banned in March.[^cite_note-19] It further consolidated Doumbouya's political power, with GMD and its allies winning over 100 seats.[^cite_note-Reuters-7][^cite_note-Bloomberg-20] The election was presented as the end of the [democratic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_transition) process initiated by Doumbouya following the 2021 coup.[^cite_note-ISS-9][^cite_note-Bloomberg-20] The Guinean Supreme Court is set to confirm the results by mid-June.[^cite_note-GSC-17]

The conduct of the election was peaceful and [monitored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_monitoring) by [ECOWAS](36848.md) and other countries.[^cite_note-Turnout-21] Observers noted that there was low [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout).[^cite_note-Turnout-21][^cite_note-Diallo-22][^cite_note-France24-23] Contributing factors include the banning of opposition parties, widespread boycotts from the opposition and their supporters, and a lack of voter confidence following the 2025 presidential election and constitutional referendum.[^cite_note-Diallo-22] The election took place four days after [Eid al-Adha](358226.md); many Guineans (Guinea is 80% Muslim) travelled far away from the constituencies that they were registered to vote in and had yet to return, including 10,000 pilgrims that had embarked on the [Hajj](23487999.md).[^cite_note-Diallo-22][^cite_note-France24-23]

Following the election, several opposition parties initiated a legal appeal contesting the results. The Democratic Front of Guinea and Avenir Guinée Nouvelle allegedly found irregularities between their tallies and the official results; Gbessia d'Abord joined the legal action because the General Directorate of Elections did not provide them with the data for 11 polling stations.[^cite_note-LegalAction-24] The [West Africa Network for Peacebuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa_Network_for_Peacebuilding) denounced functional failures in the electoral efforts, as well as "proven cases of corruption, pressure, and administrative interference".[^cite_note-25] An Avenir Guinée Nouvelle candidate claimed that his name on the official tally was incorrectly swapped with that of another party member's, causing confusion at the polling station.[^cite_note-LegalAction-24]
