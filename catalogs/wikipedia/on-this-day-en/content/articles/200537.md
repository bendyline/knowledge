The **Saladin**, **Salah ad Din**, or **Salah Al-Din Governorate** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): محافظة صلاح الدين, *Muḥāfaẓat Ṣalāḥ al-Dīn*) is one of [Iraq](7515928.md)'s 19 [governorates](222800.md), north of [Baghdad](4492.md). It has an area of 24,363 square kilometres (9,407 sq mi), with a population of 1,774,542 people in 2024. It is made up of 8 districts, with the capital being [Tikrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikrit). Before 1976 the governorate was part of [Baghdad Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad_Governorate).

The governorate is named after [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin) or Salah ad-Din. This governorate is largely [Sunni Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Iraq) and is also where [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein), former [president of Iraq](209174.md), was born, in the village of [Al-Awja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Awja). Salah Al-Din governorate, a traditional stronghold of Saddam and his [Al-Bu Nasir tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bu_Nasir_\(Iraqi_tribe\)) that is located in the heart of the [Sunni Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Triangle), has been a centre of [insurgencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(2003%E2%80%932011\)), tribal rivalries, and political and sectarian violence since the [2003 U.S.-led Coalition invasion of Iraq](201936.md) including the [rise of ISIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Islamic_State_offensive_in_Iraq).[^cite_note-4]

## History

Saladin Governorate contains a number of important religious and cultural sites. [Samarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarra), the governorate's largest city, is home to both the [Al-Askari Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Askari_Shrine) (an important religious site in [Shia Islam](26961.md) where the 10th and 11th [Shia Imams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamah_\(Shia_doctrine\)) are buried), and the [Great Mosque of Samarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Samarra) with its distinctive Malwiya minaret. It also contains an old [Zengid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zengid) mosque.

Samarra was the capital of the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) in the 9th century CE, and today [Abbasid Samarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Samarra) is a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).&#x20;

The ancient [Neo-Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire) Assyrian city of [Assur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assur) is located in [Al-Shirqat District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shirqat_District) on the banks of the [Tigris River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris_River). Other sites in the governorate include the Crusader Dome (القبة الصلبية) north of Samarra and the Al-\`Ashaq Palace (قصر العاشق).

In January 2014, there were plans announced by Prime Minister [Nouri al-Maliki](4723094.md) to make the Tuz Khurmatu district into a new governorate due to its [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmen) majority.[^cite_note-5] However, these plans were not implemented.[^cite_note-6]

### Autonomy

In October 2011, the governorate's administration declared itself a semi-autonomous region, explaining that the declaration was in response to the [central government](11752991.md)'s "domination over the provincial council authorities".[^cite_note-Mcclatchy-7] Saladin, which is a largely [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) governorate, is also hoping that by declaring themselves an autonomous region within Iraq, it will entail them to a larger portion of government funding.[^cite_note-Mcclatchy-7] The council cited "[article 119 of Iraq's constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Iraq#Regions)" in its call for autonomy, which states that "one or more governorates shall have the right to organize into a region" if one third of the Provincial Council members or one tenth of the voters request to form a region".[^cite_note-8]
