During the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md) and the ensuing [internet blackout](82063825.md) and [massacres](82036668.md) in Iran, many people across the [Iranian diaspora](9573713.md) held solidarity rallies worldwide against the [Islamic Republic](50913538.md).[^cite_note-iranintl-english-2][^cite_note-IranInternational-1] Some protesters and diaspora members have faced [transnational threats, violence, and even death](#Violence_towards_diaspora_protesters) amid the rallies.[^cite_note-dw-secretservices-3][^cite_note-aj-transnational-4] The diaspora-led rallies against the Islamic Republic have continued during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-\:9-5][^cite_note-\:10-6][^cite_note-\:11-7]

After the start of the [Iran war](82533582.md) on 28 February, [celebratory rallies](#Following_the_Israeli–United_States_strikes_on_Iran) against the Islamic Republic were held worldwide, led by the Iranian diaspora.[^cite_note-global-celebrations-8][^cite_note-9] This mirrored reactions reported among some Iranians in Iran.[^cite_note-\:6-10][^cite_note-\:7-11][^cite_note-\:8-12][^cite_note-auto1-13]

Several thousand protesters gathered in Berlin in February 2026, with about 10,000 attending a rally at the Brandenburg Gate organized by the [MEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Mujahedin_of_Iran), calling for an end to Iran's clerical regime and condemning its violent crackdown on recent anti-government protests. The MEK event featured prominent European and American political figures, with participants demanding free elections.[^cite_note-14]

[Reza Pahlavi](613947.md) called for a [global day of action](#Global_day_of_action) on 14 February, prompting large diaspora rallies in cities including [Los Angeles](18110.md), [Toronto](64646.md), and [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-\:2-15] The Munich rally attracted more than 250,000 participants, while the Toronto and Los Angeles rallies each drew an estimated 350,000 attendees.[^cite_note-\:1-16][^cite_note-iranian-internal-reaction-17][^cite_note-largest-european-18][^cite_note-\:3-19] Analysts noted that Pahlavi's level of support remains difficult to assess, with some questioning whether participants were expressing support for monarchy or opposition to the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Beginning in January 2026, solidarity rallies against the Islamic Republic were held worldwide in many countries.[^cite_note-44]

By 20 January, the country with the most rallies was Germany with 27 rallies in the ten days between 11 and 20 January. The US and Australia held 23 rallies each during this period, Canada held 19, and the UK held 12.[^cite_note-IranInternational-1] While the number of protesters across many of the rallies is unclear, by 1 February, a rally in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto,_Ontario) was reported to have 150,000 in attendance with "zero incidents" according to Toronto police,[^cite_note-150000-attendance-45] and a rally in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrangeles) was reported to have 60,000 in attendance.[^cite_note-IranInternational-1][^cite_note-iranintl-english-2][^cite_note-46]

While the protests in Iran were suppressed with deadly force, the worldwide solidarity rallies across the diaspora continued.[^cite_note-\:0-40][^cite_note-\:1-16] Despite [isolated incidents of violence reported in some locations](#Violence_towards_diaspora_protesters), most diaspora rallies were largely peaceful overall, with police in several cities reporting orderly conduct and many participants giving flowers to officers as a gesture of gratitude, as well as cleaning up litter in the streets at the end of the rallies.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### Global day of action



Global day of action for the Iranian people in David Lam Park, Vancouver on 14 February



Massive 50-metre flag being raised in Munich on 14 February

[Reza Pahlavi](613947.md) declared 14 February as a "global day of action," and called for major diaspora solidarity rallies in [Los Angeles](18110.md), [Toronto](64646.md), and [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-\:4-51][^cite_note-\:5-52] Iranians from neighboring countries, states, and cities drove, bussed, and flew in for the Munich, Toronto, and Los Angeles rallies.[^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-largest-european-18][^cite_note-53] Over 250,000 attended the 14 February rally in Munich, making it the largest ever Iran-focused demonstration in European history to date, while the Toronto and Los Angeles rallies each drew 350,000 in attendance.[^cite_note-\:1-16][^cite_note-iranian-internal-reaction-17][^cite_note-largest-european-18] The 14 February rally in [London](17867.md) also drew 50,000, and the rally in [Vancouver](32706.md) drew 45,000.[^cite_note-iranian-internal-reaction-17] Thousands of people took part in solidarity rallies elsewhere worldwide, including in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), [Lisbon](18091.md), [Tel Aviv](31453.md), and cities across [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-australia-global-day-55]

The same day, the [62nd Munich Security Conference](82337810.md) was held, where Reza Pahlavi was invited to participate and speak before giving a speech at the Munich rally.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] U.S. Senator [Lindsey Graham](363279.md) also attended the Munich rally, holding a [Lion and Sun flag](82020140.md) and telling the crowd, "The people of Iran are making history, and this is the most magnificent moment in Iranian history," while stressing that he would help the Iranian people rid themselves of the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-58]

Iranian state media downplayed the diaspora rallies, while many Iranians within Iran stated the diaspora rallies gave them a renewed sense of hope and unity.[^cite_note-iranian-internal-reaction-17] Following the Munich rally, Iran summoned Germany's ambassador in Tehran.[^cite_note-summon-59]

### Following the Israeli–United States strikes on Iran

See also: [Reactions to the 2026 Iran war](82535697.md)



Iranian diaspora in the US celebrated the death of Khamenei in [New York City](645042.md) on 1 March 2026

Following the start of the [Iran war](82533582.md), many members of the Iranian diaspora held celebratory rallies worldwide amid the American and Israeli strikes on Iran and the [assassination of Khamenei](82537558.md), echoing the cheerful sentiment of many in Iran.[^cite_note-\:6-10][^cite_note-\:7-11][^cite_note-\:8-12][^cite_note-auto1-13][^cite_note-global-celebrations-8][^cite_note-Sinaiee-2026-60]

Amid the war, members of the Iranian diaspora also celebrated the 17 March [Chaharshanbe Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaharshanbe_Suri) festivities worldwide ahead of the 20 March [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz) holiday.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] The diaspora rallies against the Islamic Republic also continued during the Nowruz festivities.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Diaspora-led rallies supporting the overthrow of the Islamic Republic have continued amid the war.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

On 29 March, a rally and march from US Congress towards the White House was held in [Washington DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_DC) in support of regime change in Iran; the rally was called for by Pahlavi.[^cite_note-69] After the [8 April ceasefire](82888027.md), rallies continued to be held worldwide against the Islamic Republic, with protesters voicing opposition to any potential deal with the Islamic Republic during international rallies on 11 April and onwards, including in Australia,[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Austria,[^cite_note-72] Canada,[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Finland,[^cite_note-76] France,[^cite_note-77] Germany,[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Japan,[^cite_note-\:14-80][^cite_note-81] New Zealand,[^cite_note-82] Scotland,[^cite_note-83] South Korea,[^cite_note-\:14-80] Spain,[^cite_note-84] Sweden,[^cite_note-85] Switzerland,[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] and the United States.[^cite_note-89] Separately, on 30 April 2026, diaspora protests were held outside the 2026 [FIFA Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Congress) in [Vancouver](32706.md), calling on [FIFA](11049.md) to ban the [Iranian team](272865.md) from the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) and stating that it represents the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) instead of the people of Iran; over 200 Iranians also protested outside [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in [Los Angeles](18110.md) on 7 June before Iran's opening 2026 World Cup match, waving [Lion and Sun flags](82020140.md) after [FIFA banned the flag again](83181783.md#Protests_and_massacres_in_Iran).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

[Maryam Rajavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Rajavi), the leader of the France–Albania based [National Council of Resistance of Iran](1181356.md), which is the political wing of the [People's Mojahedin Organization of Iran](285632.md) (MEK), announced the formation of a rival transitional government and rejected both the Islamic Republic and the Iran Prosperity Project on the [social media site X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_\(website\)).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

and [protests in London, England against the strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Iranian_protests_during_the_2026_Israeli%E2%80%93United_States_strikes_on_Iran#United_Kingdom) being organized The organizers suggested the decision was influenced by a telephone conversation between the French and Iranian foreign ministers shortly before the ban. Organizers expected around 100,000 participants, and the rally was intended to protest the recent increase in executions in Iran.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

## Protester demands

### Regime change in Iran

Protesters called for the international community to take "immediate action" against the Iranian government's actions in suppressing protesters during the [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md),[^cite_note-IranWire-106][^cite_note-Mohabat-32] while expressing support for the Iranian people.[^cite_note-Mohabat-32]

A study has indicated that members of the [Iranian diaspora](9573713.md) were deeply affected by [the massacres](82036668.md) despite being far away physically and that it had a major effect on their lives as a whole.[^cite_note-107]

At many of the rallies, protesters replaced the flag of the Islamic Republic with the [Lion and Sun flag](82020140.md), the Iranian flag prior to the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), and accompanied it with the flag of the host country.[^cite_note-IranInternational-1] [Slogans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_slogans_against_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran) included "Free political prisoners", "Long live the king", "Death to Khamenei", "king Reza Pahlavi" and "This is the final battle—Pahlavi will return", while some demonstrators who reject both forms of government have been heard shouting "Neither Shah nor Mullah".[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-IranWire-106] Protesters' signs condemned the "New Holocaust," a "genocide in the making," and "terror".[^cite_note-109] Protesters also called for regime change and [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism_in_Iran) democracy.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

Another concern cited by protesters is the [internet blackout](82063825.md), which they say is a method used by the government to prevent protesters from within the country from effectively communicating, while covering up a deadly repression of protesters.[^cite_note-113] During the internet blackout in Iran, Iranian diaspora internet users ran applications to share part of their bandwidth in an attempt to help users inside Iran circumvent the blackout.[^cite_note-psiphon-114]

Across the Iranian diaspora as well as Iranians inside Iran, calls grew for American military strikes on Iran.[^cite_note-115] Likewise, amid the 26 February [negotiations](82251337.md#Pleas_against_talks) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), members of the Iranian diaspora protested against the Islamic Republic outside the [United Nations Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_at_Geneva).[^cite_note-116] During the 28 February [Israeli–United States strikes on Iran](82546969.md), members of the Iranian diaspora held [celebratory rallies worldwide](#Following_the_Israeli–United_States_strikes_on_Iran), echoing the cheerful sentiment of many in Iran.[^cite_note-\:6-10][^cite_note-\:7-11][^cite_note-\:8-12][^cite_note-telegraph-cheer-117]

On 24 February, Iranian protesters in London urged the UK to designate the [IRGC](1285472.md) as a [terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization).[^cite_note-118]

On 18 February, it was reported that Australia had granted permanent residency and health qualifications to Hanieh Safavi, the daughter of Iranian major general [Yahya Rahim Safavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Rahim_Safavi), a sanctioned adviser to Supreme Leader [Ali Khamenei](385653.md) and a former senior figure in the IRGC who was reportedly involved in Iran's [nuclear](721807.md) and ballistic missile programs, drawing criticism from members of the Iranian diaspora and opposition lawmakers.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] A parliamentary petition was subsequently lodged by [Iranian Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Australians), demanding the Australian government "deny all visas, including refugee visas, to IR (Islamic Republic) officials, IRGC members, and their immediate family," while a separate [change.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change.org) petition was launched calling for Safavi's deportation.[^cite_note-145]

## Violence towards diaspora protesters

As the Iranian diaspora held solidarity rallies worldwide, some reports of violence and threats towards them emerged.[^cite_note-iranintl-english-2][^cite_note-intimidation-abroad-146] *[Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle)* reported that Iranian exiles were being targeted internationally by the Iranian government's secret services.[^cite_note-dw-secretservices-3]

During protests in the United Kingdom, some British-Iranian activists and community groups reported harassment, threats, and intimidation by pro-monarchist activists. A petition called on the UK government to investigate supporter groups of [Reza Pahlavi](613947.md) and protect Iranian, Iraqi, Kurdish, and other communities from intimidation and threats of violence.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

On 11 January 2026, a [U-Haul](1480329.md) truck was used to [ram into a crowd](44318569.md) of protesters opposed to the Iranian government during solidarity rally in support of the [Iranian protesters](81976180.md) in [Westwood, Los Angeles](374055.md), California. The vehicle plowed into a crowd estimated at more than 3,000 people.[^cite_note-\:12-149][^cite_note-ktla-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-\:02-152] No serious injuries were reported.[^cite_note-auto-153][^cite_note-auto2-154] The truck displayed a sign saying: "No [Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah). No [Regime](50913538.md). USA: Don't Repeat [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Iranian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). No [Mullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah)."[^cite_note-\:02-152] The sign appeared to reference the [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Iranian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) U.S.-backed coup that ousted Prime Minister [Mohammad Mosaddegh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Mosaddegh), strengthened the shah's rule, and helped pave the way for Iran's 1979 Islamic Revolution. According to the [Associated Press](18935732.md), some people within the Iranian diaspora in the United States support replacing Iran's present government but are not in favor of reinstating the monarchy.[^cite_note-155] The man responsible was later identified as Calor Madanescht, who said he intended to join the protest but was attacked by pro-Shah demonstrators after parking his truck. He claimed he drove away to escape the assault and protect himself.[^cite_note-auto-153]

Madanescht was booked into the [Los Angeles County Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Jail) with \$0 bail, and faced a [misdemeanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdemeanor) charge for [reckless driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reckless_driving).[^cite_note-calor-156] He was released the following day on his own recognizance.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-jj2-158] Authorities allege Madanescht deliberately drove the truck into the protest area.[^cite_note-jj2-158] Investigators said they do not view the incident as terrorism or politically driven, and said the driver does not have a known criminal record.[^cite_note-jj2-158] The [FBI](11127.md) was present and working alongside LAPD as investigators assessed the motive, and the case was to be presented to the [Los Angeles City Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_City_Attorney).[^cite_note-jj2-158][^cite_note-calor-156] However, the LA County District Attorney's Office declined to prosecute Madanescht, therefore sending the case back to the city attorney's office.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160]

On 11 January, Turkey barred Iranians from protesting outside the Iranian consulate in [Istanbul](3391396.md) and cordoned off the area with police.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

During the 16 January protests in [London](17867.md), violent clashes ensued, resulting in several protesters being injured and hospitalised, four Metropolitan Police officers being hospitalised, and 14 arrests for offences including violent disorder, assault of an emergency worker, criminal damage, and trespass on diplomatic premises.[^cite_note-uk-iranians1-163][^cite_note-uk-iranians2-164]

After the 17 January protests in [Hamburg](13467.md), two protesters were seriously injured after a [stabbing](618009.md) attack, including one critically, leading to three suspects being arrested.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-ec-167][^cite_note-168] The suspects were reportedly Afghan nationals, and authorities stated the attack was premeditated.[^cite_note-ec-167]

On 18 January, following threats from the Islamic Republic, Armenia barred Iranians from protesting in [Yerevan](34352.md) and arrested protesters.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

Iranians abroad were also the targets of online cybersecurity threats and [phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phishing) scams at the time of the protests amid the [domestic internet blackout](82063825.md).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

On 21 January, it was reported that [Turkey](11125639.md), which [does not require visas for Iranian citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_requirements_for_Iranian_citizens), had struck an agreement with the Islamic Republic to arrest and potentially return protesters and refugees who challenge the Iranian government.[^cite_note-turkey-173] In cooperation with Turkish President [Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan)'s government, the Islamic Republic has in the past abducted several of its opponents from Turkey and later executed them in Iran.[^cite_note-turkey-173]

On 23 January, it was reported that Iranians fleeing the massacres faced violent repression at the Turkish border.[^cite_note-174] By late January, reports emerged of Iranians facing deportation from Turkey.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

On 24 January, *[The Telegraph](23797577.md)* reported that [Iranian dissidents in the United Kingdom](15158276.md) were receiving [death threats](13262036.md) via phone calls and messages from Islamic Republic supporters, threatening to kill them in the UK.[^cite_note-177]

Iranian protesters were also targeted and harassed by pro-Palestinian protesters on 31 January in [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-178]

On 27 January, it was reported that Germany's federal interior ministry had warned members of the [Iranian diaspora in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranians_in_Germany) of intimidation, harassment, and possible cyber attacks by Iranian state actors.[^cite_note-germany-diaspora-warning-179]

On 28 January, a federal judge in [Manhattan](45470.md) sentenced Carlisle Rivera to 15 years in prison for his role in a plot led by the Islamic Republic to kill Iranian-American dissident [Masih Alinejad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masih_Alinejad) in 2024.[^cite_note-alinejad-carlisle-180] This follows plots by the Islamic Republic in hiring criminals, including Russian mobsters, Mexican cartel hitmen, and a Canadian [Hells Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hells_Angels), to carry out violent acts to silence critics abroad.[^cite_note-alinejad-carlisle-180]

On 6 February, Canadian police declared the disappearance of [Masoud Masjoudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lawsuits_involving_X_Corp.#Defamation_and_conspiracy_cases_in_Canada_by_Masood_Masjoody), an Iranian dissident residing in Canada who protested the influence of the Islamic Republic in Canadian universities, to be a targeted crime, while speculation arose of his suspicious death.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

A separate rally in [Berlin](3354.md) on 7 February was hosted by the [MEK](285632.md), where some non-Iranian rally-goers stated it was their first time attending an Iran demonstration and that the rally had been promoted to them.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184] Former U.S. Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) addressed the rally in Berlin by video.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]

*[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that the Islamic Republic was monitoring and threatening diaspora journalists and their families over their coverage of the protests.[^cite_note-187]

Iranians abroad also faced intimidation and threats for their online discourse and opinions.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-intimidation-abroad-146] An independent research group stated the Islamic Republic had coordinated a large social media influence operation aimed at shaping global narratives.[^cite_note-social-media-influence-operation-189] Likewise, another investigative watchdog stated that pro-government editors were active during the crackdown across [Wikipedia](5043734.md) and other services in [deleting content and sanitizing articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_negationism) related to the [Islamic Republic's human rights](3156692.md) record.[^cite_note-190]

On 14 February, ahead of the "[global day of action](#Global_day_of_action)" in Toronto, 56-year-old Michael David Holland of [Burlington, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Ontario) was arrested after threatening to target the rally with a firearm to prevent the gathering.[^cite_note-191] Another 26-year-old man from Toronto, Seyed Vorudi, was also arrested for an alleged hate-motivated assault, facing two charges for assault and intent to wear a disguise.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194] Vorudi was previously charged in October 2024 for willful promotion of hatred towards Jewish and LGBTQ communities.[^cite_note-195]

On 23 February, [Persian-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) network [Manoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoto) halted its London-based live broadcast again due to a potential threat warning received from the UK's counterterrorism police perceived to be linked to the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-196]

### Violence after 28 February strikes

Following the 28 February [Israeli–United States strikes on Iran](82546969.md), foreign security officials warned of [attacks from sleeper cells abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactions_to_the_2026_Iran_conflict#Attacks_abroad).[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] On 1 March, the [Austin bar mass shooting](82545863.md) was carried out by an American man of [Senegalese](27278.md) descent wearing a shirt that read "Property of Allah" and an undershirt bearing the flag of the Islamic Republic of Iran.[^cite_note-200] The same day, a local gym owned by an Iranian-Canadian anti-regime activist located outside [Toronto](64646.md) was shot with 17 bullets, hours after [Khamenei's death](82537558.md).[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203] [Los Angeles mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Mayor) [Karen Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Bass), whose [city hosts the largest Iranian community outside Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrangeles), stated the [LAPD](218985.md) would be providing extra security in sensitive areas of the city in the wake of the airstrikes.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]

In March 2026, it was reported that the Islamic Republic-linked [Handala Hack Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handala_Hack_Team) placed a [\$](18717338.md)250,000 bounty for the beheading of Iranian-American lawyer and activist Elica Le Bon and Iranian-Canadian former politician and activist [Goldie Ghamari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldie_Ghamari) after leaking their home addresses to its [Jalisco New Generation Cartel](35006872.md) partners.[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207]

On 4 March, "a small number of isolated incidents" were reported in [Manchester](20206.md) between diaspora demonstrators and members of the Islamic Centre of Manchester during the latter's candlelit vigil for Khamenei.[^cite_note-208]

[After not singing the national anthem](82634971.md) of the Islamic Republic of Iran ahead of their opening game of the [Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) in Australia against South Korea as a form of silent protest, the [Iranian women's football team](9896170.md) was reportedly forced to sing the national anthem prior to their second game against Australia on 5 March, with threats to the players' family members if they did not.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] After the team's last group stage match against the Philippines on 8 March, members of the team gave what appeared to be [SOS](145605.md) hand signals from the bus as they were leaving, leading to protests and growing calls for Australia to offer the team refuge after the players were accused of being wartime traitors by Iranian state media for not singing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic in their opening game amid the [Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

On 6 March, London-based Persian-language television network, [Iran International](58555280.md), was declared a "legitimate target" for attack by a former Islamic Republic spokesperson.[^cite_note-215] Two years prior, Iran International journalist [Pouria Zeraati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pouria_Zeraati) was stabbed outside his London home following months of threats towards the station's journalists.[^cite_note-216] On 17 March, British politician [Jenny Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Chapman) stated the UK will attend to threats against Iran International.[^cite_note-217]

Also on 6 March, British counterterrorism police [arrested](82646443.md#United_Kingdom) four people suspected of spying on Jews in London for the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

The same day, a federal jury in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) convicted Asif Merchant, a 48-year-old Pakistani man allegedly trained by the [IRGC](1285472.md), of a murder-for-hire plot and attempting to kill politicians in the United States, likely in retaliation for the [assassination of Qasem Soleimani](62726668.md).[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221]

In [Moscow](19004.md), police detained members of the diaspora who celebrated the death of Khamenei.[^cite_note-222]

On 6 March at [Washington Square Park](745249.md) in Manhattan, clashes occurred between Khamenei supporters and diaspora counter protesters, leading to multiple arrests being made by the [NYPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Police_Department).[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] In [Birmingham, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_England), Islamic Republic supporters carried pictures of Khamenei while chanting "[Allahu Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahu_Akbar)" before burning and stomping on the Israeli flag, leading to clashes with diaspora counter protesters as well.[^cite_note-226] In London, clashes occurred between diaspora celebrants and demonstrators in support of the Islamic Republic near the [Islamic Centre of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Centre_of_England), leading to one arrest being made the following day.[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-guardian-7march-95]

During the 7 March protests in London against the strikes, which were organized by groups including the [Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Nuclear_Disarmament), [Stop The War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_the_War_Coalition), the [Palestine Solidarity Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Solidarity_Campaign), the [Muslim Association of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Association_of_Britain), the Palestinian Forum in Britain, and Friends of Al-Aqsa, four people were arrested in total for suspicion of inciting racial hatred, possession of an offensive weapon, racially aggravated public order offences, and violent disorder in relation to an incident the day prior near the Islamic Centre of England.[^cite_note-guardian-7march-95]

On 9 March, an Iranian anti-[IRGC](1285472.md) protester was stabbed and bottled in the [North Finchley](453539.md) suburb of London.[^cite_note-228]

Also on 9 March, the Islamic Republic threatened to seize the assets of expatriates and diaspora Iranians along with other legal penalties if they cooperate with nations abroad.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] On 10 March, the Islamic Republic said that they will go after critics within and outside Iran with seizure of property, arrest, and death, raising fears of another crackdown.[^cite_note-232] On 21 March, the Islamic Republic's intelligence ministry stated it had submitted documents related to fifteen Iranians living abroad to the judiciary for review of asset confiscation.[^cite_note-233] On 5 April, it was reported that Tehran's prosecutor ordered the identification and seizure of assets and bank accounts of more than one hundred Iranian expatriates, including actors, athletes, and staff of media outlets [Iran International](58555280.md) and [Manoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoto).[^cite_note-234]

On 18 March, a Swedish citizen was executed in Iran by the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-235] Days prior, an Iranian-Austrian was [arrested in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_hostage_diplomacy).[^cite_note-236] The month prior, [a detained British couple](79261557.md) were sentenced by the [Islamic Revolutionary Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Revolutionary_Court) to ten years in prison.[^cite_note-237]

On 19 March, the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) said it shut down websites that spread Islamic Republic [propaganda](33693903.md), threatened dissidents abroad, and took credit for hacks.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239] On 20 March, the [FBI](11127.md) warned that cyber actors linked to the Islamic Republic's intelligence services were using the [social media service Telegram](41501305.md) to target dissidents and journalists abroad with [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware) attacks.[^cite_note-240]

Also on 20 March, the Netherlands tightened security for Iranian dissidents after a man of Iranian descent who is critical of the Islamic Republic was ambushed and shot at his home in [Schoonhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoonhoven); a 27-year-old man was later arrested in [Dortmund](149349.md), Germany in connection with the shooting.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

On 21 March in Birmingham, England's [Victoria Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Square,_Birmingham), Iranians demonstrating in a planned protest against the Islamic Republic were attacked with a [zombie knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombie_knife) by a group of teenagers; the four teenagers were subsequently arrested on suspicion of violent disorder following the melee.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246]

On 23 March, an Iranian-linked militant group claimed responsibility for [arson of Jewish ambulances in northern London](82760663.md).[^cite_note-247]

On 25 March, [CNN](62028.md) released a video showing an Iranian diaspora-owned shop in London being vandalized for reportedly having the [Lion and Sun flag](82020140.md), one of a series of vandalism incidents targeting Iranian businesses in [North London](380308.md).[^cite_note-248]

On 28 March, it was reported that Iranian expatriates in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) were having their residency visas canceled and were being repatriated to Iran via Afghanistan.[^cite_note-249] On 1 April, the UAE barred most Iranian nationals from entering or transiting through the country, exempting [Golden Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE_Golden_Visa) holders.[^cite_note-250]

Also on 28 March, a foiled bomb attack outside of a [Bank of America](347756.md) in [Paris](22989.md) was linked to Islamic Republic-linked proxy operatives.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252]

On 29 March, [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) reported on transnational threats facing the Iranian diaspora, adding that Iranian Americans living in exile feared retribution.[^cite_note-aj-transnational-4]

On 4 April, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported that the Islamic Republic was detaining family members and threatening to seize the assets of Iranian opposition figures in exile to silence them.[^cite_note-253]

On 15 April, an attempted [arson](184695.md) attack at the London studios of [Iran International](58555280.md) led to three arrests by the [Metropolitan Police](192450.md).[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255]

On 18 April, an Iranian man described to be an opponent of the Iranian government was assaulted in London.[^cite_note-256]

On 23 April, [Reza Pahlavi](613947.md) was splattered with red liquid in Berlin.[^cite_note-257]

On 27 April, Canadian politician [Costas Menegakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costas_Menegakis) stated Iranian Canadians live in fear of intimidation from the Islamic Republic on Canadian soil, and called for the identification and removal of IRGC operatives in Canada.[^cite_note-258]

Also on 27 April, a memorial in London dedicated to victims of the [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md) was targeted in a suspected arson attack.[^cite_note-259]

On 29 April, [two Jewish men were stabbed in Golders Green, London](83065835.md) as part of the [2026 London antisemitic attacks](83012228.md) and the broader [antisemitic incidents during the Iran war](82646443.md); a British national born in [Somalia](27358.md) was arrested, and the Iran-linked [Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamia](82697292.md) claimed responsibility.[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261]

On 5 May, pro-Islamic Republic graffiti was reported in [Los Angeles](18110.md) as a form of intimidation against the diaspora community, while in a separate incident, a nearby memorial dedicated to slain Iranian protesters was vandalized.[^cite_note-262]

During a diaspora protest against the Islamic Republic on 10 May in [Berlin](3354.md), [Kowsar Eftekhari, who had been shot and blinded in the eye during the 2022 Iranian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_injuries_during_2022_Iranian_protests#Kosar_Eftekhari), was attacked by pro-Palestinian counter-protesters who shouted "[Allahu Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahu_Akbar)".[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264]

On 9 June, a judge in Iran stated the ongoing confiscation of exiled Iranians' assets would weaken the diaspora anti-regime protests.[^cite_note-265]

## Reactions

Notable figures in the Iranian diaspora voiced their reactions and opinions to the protests in Iran. [Maryam Rajavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Rajavi), co-leader of the [People's Mojahedin Organisation of Iran](285632.md) (MEK), said that protesters have "struck fear into a weakened enemy", warning security forces who obey orders to crackdown on the protests that "the courts of a free Iran are awaiting (you)."[^cite_note-mekrajavi-266]

[Gissou Nia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gissou_Nia), a human rights lawyer and Iran expert, stated that while the economic crisis was the catalyst for the protest, the slogans and behaviour of the protesters show a profound dissatisfaction with the Iranian government and a desire to overthrow it.[^cite_note-Noll-267]

Iranian dissident and [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_by_country) laureate [Shirin Ebadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirin_Ebadi) condemned Iranian security forces for directly shooting unarmed protesters in [Fasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasa), calling it a human rights violation. In a message shared on Instagram, Ebadi stressed that using live ammunition against unarmed civilians cannot be justified. She urged authorities to immediately stop the violence, ensure proper medical care for those injured, and investigate who authorised the use of live fire against protesters in Fasa.[^cite_note-268] During the [internet blackout](82063825.md), she claimed the Iranian government may carry out a massacre under cover of a sweeping communications blackout, encouraging Western governments to speak out immediately.[^cite_note-269]

[Masih Alinejad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masih_Alinejad), an Iranian-American journalist and human rights activist, stated that President Donald Trump's message to Iran gave people a "strong message" of hope, and that he is on the right side of history.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272] After [the massacres](82036668.md), Alinejad denounced [the US-Iran negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_buildup_in_the_Middle_East_during_the_2026_United_States-Iran_crisis#Negotiations), stating the Islamic Republic shoots protesters and [tortures for confessions](81976180.md#Internal_propaganda_and_coercion).[^cite_note-273]

[Goldie Ghamari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldie_Ghamari), a Canadian-Iranian activist and former politician, stated that "Iranians are more determined than ever to overthrow the Islamic Republic", "it's life or death", "Iranians have nothing left to lose, and these are the true freedom fighters of the 21st century", and that "the Iranian national revolution is anti-Islam, anti-Islamic, anti sharia law; it's pro-revolution, pro-[shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah)".[^cite_note-274]

British-Iranian actress and activist [Nazanin Boniadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazanin_Boniadi) urged [Hollywood](418334.md) to stand with Iran's protesters.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276]

[Ali Karimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Karimi), a former footballer for the [Iran national football team](272865.md), spoke at a January rally in Los Angeles and called for political change in Iran.[^cite_note-277] He also wrote an open letter to [FIFA](11049.md) and all its member football associations, calling on FIFA president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) to speak up on the protest deaths.[^cite_note-278]

[Kimia Alizadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimia_Alizadeh), Iran's first female Olympic medalist, attended the diaspora protests and stated [detained protesters in Iran were being executed](81976180.md#Executions).[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280]

Many Iranians within Iran have stated that the diaspora rallies abroad give them renewed hope.[^cite_note-iranian-internal-reaction-17]

After the start of the [Iran war](82533582.md), Iranians across social media began gathering cases of repression and neglect in Iran linked to government mismanagement, arguing that the suffering long predates the current war.[^cite_note-281]
