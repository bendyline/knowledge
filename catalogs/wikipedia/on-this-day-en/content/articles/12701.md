**Greenwich Mean Time** (**GMT**) is the [local mean time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_mean_time) at the [Royal Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich) in [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich), London, counted from [midnight](247844.md). At different times in the past, it has been calculated in different ways, including being calculated from [noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noon);[^cite_note-1] as a consequence, it cannot be used to specify a particular time unless a context is given. The term "GMT" is also used as [one of the names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Time) for the [time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone) [UTC+00\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+00\:00) and,[^cite_note-RMG-2] in UK law, is the basis for [civil time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_time) in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-IA1978-3][^cite_note-4]

Because of Earth's uneven [angular velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_velocity) in its [elliptical orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_orbit) and its [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt), noon (12\:00\:00) GMT is rarely the exact moment the Sun crosses the [Greenwich Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_meridian_\(Greenwich\))[^cite_note-6] and reaches its highest point in the sky there. This event may occur up to 16 minutes before or after noon GMT, a discrepancy described by the [equation of time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equation_of_time). Noon GMT is the annual average (the [arithmetic mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_mean)) moment of this event, which accounts for the word "mean" in "Greenwich Mean Time".[^cite_note-7]

Originally, astronomers considered a GMT day to start at noon,[^cite_note-8] while for almost everyone else it started at midnight. To avoid confusion, the name [Universal Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Time) was introduced in 1928 to denote GMT as counted from midnight.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcCarthySeidelmann200917-9][^cite_note-10] Today, Universal Time usually refers to [Coordinated Universal Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time) (UTC) or else to [UT1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Time);[^cite_note-AAO-UT-11] English speakers often use GMT as a synonym for UTC.[^cite_note-12] For navigation, it is considered equivalent to UT1 (the modern form of mean solar time at 0° longitude); but this meaning can differ from UTC by up to 0.9 s.[^cite_note-13] The term "GMT" should thus not be used for purposes that require precision.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiltonMcCarthy2013231–232-14]

The term "GMT" is especially used by institutional bodies within the [United Kingdom](31717.md), such as the [BBC World Service](37627.md), the [Royal Navy](26061.md), and the [Met Office](319671.md); and others particularly in Arab countries, such as the [Middle East Broadcasting Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Broadcasting_Centre) and Dubai-based [OSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSN).

## History

Main article: [History of longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_longitude)



The [Shepherd Gate Clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd_Gate_Clock) at the gates of the [Royal Observatory, Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich) is permanently kept on Greenwich Mean Time.

As the [United Kingdom](31717.md) developed into an advanced [maritime nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_nation), British mariners kept at least one [chronometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_chronometer) on GMT to calculate their [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) from the Greenwich meridian,[^cite_note-15] which was considered to have longitude zero degrees, by a convention adopted in the [International Meridian Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Meridian_Conference) of 1884. Synchronisation of the chronometer on GMT did not affect shipboard time, which was still solar time. But this practice, combined with mariners from other nations drawing from [Nevil Maskelyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevil_Maskelyne)'s method of [lunar distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_distance_\(navigation\)) based on observations at Greenwich, led to GMT being used worldwide as a standard time independent of location. Most [time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone) were based upon GMT, as an offset of a number of hours (and occasionally half or quarter hours) "ahead of GMT" or "behind GMT".

Greenwich Mean Time was adopted across the island of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) by the [Railway Clearing House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Clearing_House) in 1847 and by almost all railway companies by the following year, from which the term *[railway time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_time)* is derived. It was gradually adopted for other purposes, but a legal case in 1858 held "[local mean time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_mean_time)" to be the official time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHowse1997114-16] On 14 May 1880, a letter signed by "Clerk to Justices" appeared in *The Times*, stating that "Greenwich time is now kept almost throughout England, but it appears that Greenwich time is not legal time. For example, our polling booths were opened, say, at 8 13 and closed at 4 13 p.m."[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] This was changed later in 1880, when Greenwich Mean Time was legally adopted throughout the island of Great Britain. GMT was adopted in the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man) in 1883, in [Jersey](15693.md) in 1898 and in [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailiwick_of_Guernsey) in 1913. [Ireland](147575.md) adopted GMT in 1916, supplanting [Dublin Mean Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Mean_Time).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMyers2007-19] Hourly [time signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_signal) from Greenwich Observatory were first broadcast by [shortwave radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortwave_radio) on 5 February 1924 at 17\:30\:00 UTC,[^cite_note-20] providing a rival accurate time-source to the [time ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_ball) at the Greenwich Observatory.[^cite_note-21]

The daily rotation of the Earth is irregular (see [ΔT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%94T_\(timekeeping\))) and has a slowing trend; therefore [atomic clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_clock) constitute a much more stable timebase. On 1 January 1972, GMT as the international civil time standard was superseded by [Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time), maintained by an ensemble of atomic clocks around the world. [*Universal Time* (*UT*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Time), a term introduced in 1928, initially represented mean time at Greenwich determined in the traditional way to accord with the originally defined [universal day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Meridian_Conference); from 1 January 1956 (as decided by the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) in [Dublin](8504.md) in 1955, at the initiative of [William Markowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Markowitz)) this "raw" form of UT was re-labelled *UT0* and effectively superseded by refined forms UT1 (UT0 equalised for the effects of [polar wandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_wandering))[^cite_note-22] and UT2 (UT1 further equalised for annual seasonal variations in Earth rotation rate).

> Indeed, even the Greenwich meridian itself is not quite what it used to be—defined by "the centre of the transit instrument at the Observatory at Greenwich". Although that instrument still survives in working order, it is no longer in use and now the meridian of origin of the world's longitude and time is not strictly defined in material form but from a statistical solution resulting from observations of all time-determination stations which the [BIPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Weights_and_Measures) takes into account when co-ordinating the world's time signals. Nevertheless, the line in the old observatory's courtyard today differs no more than a few metres from that imaginary line which is now the prime meridian of the world.&#x20;

— Howse, D. (1997). *Greenwich time and the longitude*. London: Philip Wilson.

## Ambiguity in the definition of GMT

Historically, GMT has been used with two different conventions for numbering hours. The long-standing astronomical convention, dating from the work of [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy), was to refer to noon as zero hours (see [Julian day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_day)). This contrasted with the civil convention of referring to midnight as zero hours dating from the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). The latter convention was adopted on and after 1 January 1925 for astronomical purposes, resulting in a discontinuity of 12 hours, or half a day. The instant that was designated as "December 31.5 GMT" in 1924 almanacs became "January 1.0 GMT" in 1925 almanacs. The term *Greenwich Mean Astronomical Time* (*GMAT*) was introduced to unambiguously refer to the previous noon-based astronomical convention for GMT.[^cite_note-23] The more specific terms *UT* and *UTC* do not share this ambiguity, always referring to midnight as *zero hours*.

## GMT in legislation

### United Kingdom

Legally, the civil time used in the UK is called "Greenwich mean time" (without capitalisation), with an exception made for those periods when the [Summer Time Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Time_Act_1972) orders an hour's shift for daylight saving. The [Interpretation Act 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretation_Act_1978), section 9, provides that whenever an expression of time occurs in any Act, the time referred to shall (unless otherwise specifically stated) be held to be Greenwich mean time.[^cite_note-IA1978-3] Under subsection 23, the same rule applies to deeds and other instruments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMyers2007-19]

During the experiment of 1968 to 1971, when the British Isles did not revert to Greenwich Mean Time during the winter, the all-year [British Summer Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Summer_Time) was called *British Standard Time* (*BST*).

In the UK, UTC+00\:00 is disseminated to the general public in winter and [UTC+01\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00) in summer.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcCarthySeidelmann200917-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHowse1997157-24]

BBC radio stations broadcast the "six pips" of the [Greenwich Time Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Time_Signal). It is named from its original generation at the [Royal Greenwich Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich) but is calibrated to UTC. If announced (such as near the start of summer time or of winter time), announcers on domestic channels declare the time as *GMT* or *BST* as appropriate. As the BBC World Service is broadcast to all time zones, the announcers use the term "Greenwich Mean Time" consistently throughout the year.

### Other countries

Several countries define their local time by reference to Greenwich Mean Time.[^cite_note-AMANO-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeagoSeidelmannAllen2011-26] Some examples are:

- [Belgium](3343.md): Decrees of 1946 and 1947 set legal time as one hour ahead of GMT.

  [^cite_note-AMANO-25]

- [Ireland](14560.md): "Standard Time" ([Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *Am Caighdeánach*) is defined as being one hour in advance of GMT.

  [^cite_note-IST-27]

  &#x20;"Winter Time" (*Am Geimhridh*) is defined as being the same as GMT.

  [^cite_note-IWT-28]

  [^cite_note-29]

- [Canada](5042916.md): Interpretation Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-21, section 35(1). This refers to "standard time" for the several provinces, defining each in relation to "Greenwich time", but does not use the expression "Greenwich mean time". Several provinces, such as [Nova Scotia](21184.md) (Time Definition Act. R.S., c. 469, s. 1), have their own legislation which specifically mentions either "Greenwich Mean Time" or "Greenwich mean solar time".
