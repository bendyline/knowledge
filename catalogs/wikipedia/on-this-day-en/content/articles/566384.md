**Duhok** ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): دهۆک, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kurdish): *Dihok*;[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): دهوك, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Dohūk*;[^cite_note-5] [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language): ܒܝܬ ܢܘܗܕܪܐ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Syriac): *Beth Nohadra*,[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] [Lishanid Noshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lishanid_Noshan_language): דוהוך, romanized: *Dohok*[^cite_note-8]) is a city in [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md), Iraq and the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) city of [Duhok Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duhok_Governorate).

## Name

The city of Duhok received its name from the [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages) words 'du' (two) and 'hok' (lump) as a tax payment of two lumps from the basket of each passing caravan that often carry wheat and barley.[^cite_note-dhk-pti_DuhokCity-9] According to a tradition presented by Sasson Nahum, Duhok was initially named *Duhok-e [Dasinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasini)*, signifying "Duhok of the [Yezidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis)". However, after a massacre of the [Yezidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yezidis), the town was abandoned, leading to the settlement of Muslims, Christians, and Jews in the area.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

## Demographics

The city is home to diverse ethnic groups, with [Kurds](17068.md) forming the majority, while other minorities include [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis), [Armenians](387816.md), and Arabs.[^cite_note-11] The city also hosts tens of thousands of [refugees](45547.md) from [Syria](7515849.md), mostly [Syrian Kurds](3821855.md), and [internally displaced persons (IDPs)](427408.md), most of whom are Yazidis and [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians) who fled after [ISIS](9087364.md) took control of [Sinjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjar) and [Mosul](200475.md), Iraq.[^cite_note-auto-12][^cite_note-13]

According to the [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Kurdistan_Region), as of March 2024, the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) hosts 631,174 IDPs, with 40% of them living in [Duhok Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duhok_Governorate). Additionally, there are 251,475 [Syrian](8862873.md) refugees, of which 131,700 (as of 2022) reside in Duhok Governorate.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-auto-12]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Duhok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Duhok).

One of the earliest written references to Duhok appears in the late 15th-century Persian chronicle *[Kitab-i Diyarbakriyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitab-i_Diyarbakriyya)* by [Abu Bakr Tihrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_Tihrani), which mentions Duhok in accounts of [Aq Qoyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aq_Qoyunlu) military campaigns.[^cite_note-15]

According to historical sources, Duhok served as the center of the [Yazidi principality of Dasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikhan_principality) (Sheikhan) until the 16th century, with the mountain overlooking the city known as "Mount Dasin". Following the foundation of the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran) in 1501 and its subsequent expansion into Kurdistan, the region came under Safavid authority after the capture of [Diyarbakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbak%C4%B1r) and [Mosul](200475.md) in 1508. The Mir of [Bahdinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahdinan), Hasan ibn Seyf al-Din, was commissioned by [Shah Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I) to assist in the capture of Duhok fortress from the [Dasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daseni) tribe, after which the city passed to the control of the Bahdinan emirate.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] As observed by [Evliya Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_%C3%87elebi) in *[Seyahatnâme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyahatn%C3%A2me)* (Book of Travels), Bahdinan was divided into: [Akre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akre), [Zaxo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaxo), Shixoyi, Duhok, Zibari, and [Muzuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzuri_\(tribe\)).[^cite_note-18]

In 1820, Rich described Duhok as a small town comprising 300 houses, serving as the principal site for the Doski tribe, accompanied by eighty additional villages. The missionary [Henry Aaron Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Aaron_Stern) (1851) observed Dohuk's diverse population, which included [Jewish](25955086.md) residents. Stern further noted that the *kiahya*, or village mayor, was an [Assyrian Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Christian) of [Chaldean Catholic](698821.md) affiliation. By 1859, [Rabbi](51273.md) Yehiel found two *[minyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyan)* of Jews in the area. The Muslim and Assyrian Christian communities comprised around a hundred households. Stern also mentioned the large [Yazidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidi) population at the time, as well as the fact that it was initially called "Dohuk-e-Dasiniya", i.e. Duhok of the Yazidis, potentially implying that it was founded or heavily influenced by [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis).[^cite_note-\:0-10]

In 1929, the settled population reached approximately 3,500 inhabitants, with Kurds forming the majority. Among the 550 households, 65 were Assyrian Christian, and 30 were Jewish.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

### Modern times

The [University of Duhok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Duhok?action=edit\&redlink=1) was founded on 31 October 1992.[^cite_note-VerifyUOD-19]

In March 2024, an [ancient Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) archaeological site was vandalized with Islamist slogans. This incident drew criticism from the [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) community, in line with the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md)'s ongoing failure to preserve their ancient artifacts. Notably, in 2016, the [Halamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halamata_Cave) reliefs were spray-painted with the Kurdish flag and subsequently stolen.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

On 1 April 2025, during the [Kha b-Nisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kha_b-Nisan) (Assyrian New Year) parade in downtown Duhok, a man armed with an axe attacked [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians) celebrating the event while shouting Islamist slogans, injuring a 17-year-old boy and a 75-year-old woman.[^cite_note-22] The assailant, later identified as a [Syrian Kurdish](3821855.md) national affiliated with the [Islamic State](9087364.md), was quickly subdued by nearby Assyrians.[^cite_note-\:1-23] The incident was described as a terrorist attack by the Assyrian deputy governor.[^cite_note-\:1-23]

## Culture

The culture of the city of Duhok reflects the city's diverse ethnic and religious composition, alongside significant [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians) and [Yazidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis) communities.[^cite_note-24]

### Festivals and celebrations

#### Newroz (Kurdish New Year)

[Newroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newroz_as_celebrated_by_Kurds) is celebrated annually on the 21st of March and marks the first day of spring. The festival of Newroz holds deep cultural significance for the [Kurds](17068.md), symbolizing freedom and resistance.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

In Duhok, Newroz is celebrated with large public gatherings, traditional [Kurdish music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_music) (most notably songs of [Hesen Zîrek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesen_Z%C3%AErek)), folk dancing such as [halparke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdis_dance?action=edit\&redlink=1), and family picnics. Residents wear traditional [Kurdish clothes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_clothes) and prepare festive meals to share with family and neighbors.[^cite_note-\:2-27]

The celebration of Newroz is connected to Duhok's history. During the [Kurdish uprisings of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Kurdish_uprisings), the people of Duhok, alongside [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga), liberated the city and won against the [Ba’athist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athist_regime_in_Iraq) on 14 March 1991. This victory paved the way for the lighting of the Newroz flame seven days later, on 21 March 1991.[^cite_note-28]

In 2025, thousands of residents celebrated Newroz after the postponed due to overlapping with [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) and [Eid-Al Fitr](154944.md). Families pitched tents in valleys and mountains across the city, joining in traditional dancing, music, and meals. Large crowds were recorded in Duhok and the governorate as locals embraced the spring weather.[^cite_note-\:2-27]

#### Akitu (Assyrian New Year)

Duhok hosts major celebrations for the [Akitu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akitu) festival, marking the [Assyrian New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_New_Year). The festival, dating back to the [5th millennium BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_millennium_BC), is celebrated annually on the 1st of April. The festive procession kicks off in front of the Virgin Mary Church in Duhok, with participants marching to the celebration venue. During the march, attendees wave flags and banners, perform traditional folk dances, and showcase Assyrian heritage and customs.[^cite_note-\:3-29]

In 2025, thousands of [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians) from Iraq and across the [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_diaspora) took part of the celebrations in Duhok. [Ninos Odisho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninos_Odisho?action=edit\&redlink=1), a member of the organizing committee, stated that the event aimed to send a clear message to the Assyrians who remain rooted in their homeland.[^cite_note-\:3-29]

#### Duhok Provincial Festival

In May 2025, Duhok hosted the 11th edition of its annual provincial festival, drawing thousands from the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md). The festival turned the city into a showcase of heritage, culture, and pride. Local folklore troupes performed traditional dabka dances, while singers echoed melodies rooted in the region's multiethnic legacy.[^cite_note-\:4-30]

The festival also honored the area's rich folklore, with dozens of ensembles presenting scenes from daily life, weddings, and rituals across the diverse communities of Duhok. [Government departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_departments), [NGOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGOs), and private companies erected pavilions across the Nowruz Square to display projects and local initiatives.[^cite_note-\:4-30]

#### Kurdish food festival

In October 2025, Duhok hosted a festival showcasing traditional [Kurdish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_cuisine), coinciding with [World Food Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Food_Day). Participants across the governorate of Duhok displayed a wide range of Kurdish food products and handmade crafts reflecting the region's cultural identity.[^cite_note-\:5-31]

[Khalil Mahmoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalil_Mahmoud?action=edit\&redlink=1), mayor of the [Semel District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semel_District), stated that "the goal of the festival was to introduce younger generations to the traditional Kurdish cuisine and preserve the region’s culture." Participants prepared traditional dishes such as [dolma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolma), sour kubba, yogurt kubba, and rice with chickpeas.[^cite_note-\:5-31]

### Music

Duhok has a rich musical heritage dating to the 1970s. The Tipa Duhok band, a band in Duhok, became one of the most famous bands in Iraq. Its song "Nergiz-Nergiz" was popular throughout Iraq.[^cite_note-\:6-32] The band kept continuing until 2014, where due to the [war with ISIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Iraq_\(2013%E2%80%932017\)), the [federal government of Iraq](11752991.md) had to cut Kurdistan's budget, hence the band was forced to suspend its work.[^cite_note-\:6-32]

Notable musicians from Duhok include Kurdish musician and composer [Dilshad Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilshad_Said), as well as Dastor (Mohammed Salah), a Kurdish singer known for his work in rap and pop music, who has also produced more than 100 tracks.[^cite_note-\:7-33][^cite_note-34]

Music in Duhok has gone through many phases. The period from 2003 to 2013 is considered the golden age of music for Duhok. The rise of [ISIS](9087364.md) and financial difficulties in the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) negatively affected the music industry.[^cite_note-\:7-33]

### Arts and galleries

The Duhok Gallery was opened in 1998 by the Directorate of Culture in Duhok. The gallery, located in the city centre, has showcased many public and personal exhibitions since its opening.[^cite_note-35]

In January 2019, the gallery hosted its 14th annual exhibition, showcasing over a hundred new pieces of art created by artists across the region. Artists from Kurdish cities, such as Duhok, [Erbil](24534207.md), [Sulaymaniyah](653537.md), [Halabja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halabja), and [Kirkuk](199292.md) participated in the exhibition.[^cite_note-36]

### Museums

Duhok is home to many museums preserving the region's cultural heritage. One of these museums is the Duhok Cultural Museum, located inside the Duhok Directorate of Culture and Arts building. The museum was founded in 1998 by Rafaat Rajab, who was behind its success until his death in 2019. The museum features many artifacts representing the culture of coexistence in the region. [Kurds](17068.md), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians), [Christians](18337522.md), [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis), and [Arabs](2185.md) all have a unique cultural history represented by these artifacts.[^cite_note-\:8-37] The exhibits in the museum mainly feature the old, agricultural life that was present back then in the region. There were [herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb) that were used for medicine and food, traditional clothes, [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery), [carpentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentry), and household items that were in use for a long time. Many collectors of old artifacts helped the museum by donating their well-preserved items. Some of the items are even more than 500 years old.[^cite_note-\:8-37]

In [Khanke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanke), a town south of Duhok, a local resident named Ivan Haji single-handedly founded a museum, spending over six years collecting thousands of precious artifacts that tell the story of his community's heritage. The museum features Kurdish textiles, vintage cameras, old phones, typewriters, traditional instruments, intricately woven bags, and old farming tools.[^cite_note-38]

## Archaeology

Seven kilometers southwest of Duhok, [Halamata Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halamata_Cave) is an [archaeological site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) containing the Assyrian [relief carvings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_carving) known as the Maltai Reliefs, associated with the northern canal system built by the Assyrian king [Sennacherib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sennacherib) (r. 704–681 BC) to carry water to his capital city of [Nineveh](21699.md)".[^cite_note-39]

In 2020, researchers discovered in the Balyuz hills, ten kilometers west of Duhok City, an ancient tablet with [Greek inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) which dates back to 165 BC. The inscriptions refer to [Demetrius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_Soter), the region's ruler during that time.[^cite_note-40]

## Climate

According to the [Köppen-Geiger climate classification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification_system), Duhok, like most of [Upper Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mesopotamia), has a hot-summer [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa*) featuring sweltering, virtually rainless summers and cool to cold, wet winters.[^cite_note-\:11-41] [Precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) falls in the cooler months, being heaviest in late winter and early spring.[^cite_note-42] The city experiences around two or three snowy days yearly, with more severe falls in the uplands. Summers are virtually rainless, with rain returning in late autumn.[^cite_note-\:11-41]

## Transport

### Road networks

Duhok is connected to major cities of the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) via a network of highways and regional roads. The ninth cabinet of the Kurdistan Region has prioritized road development, with significant development across the governorate, with 212 road and bridge projects completed in 2024.[^cite_note-\:9-45][^cite_note-46]

One of these projects is The Ba’adre-Etit Strategic Highway Project, a 26-kilometer road project in the [Duhok Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duhok_Governorate). Once this project finishes, the entire Erbil-Duhok road will be a modern dual carriageway, improving connectivity and supporting economic, trade, and tourism development across the region.[^cite_note-47]

Another project is the Duhok-Semel Road Project, which was completed in January 2025. This road connects the city of Duhok to the [Semel District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semel_District). The road serves as an example for necessary infrastructure development and has provided convenience for those moving between the places of Duhok and [Semel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simele).[^cite_note-\:9-45]

The [Development Road Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Road) is a major road planned to span 1,200 kilometers, beginning at the [Faw Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faw_Peninsula) in [Basra](57580.md) and ending at the [Iraq-Turkey border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq-Turkey_border). The [Duhok Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duhok_Governorate) is key in this project, with the route passing through the [Zakho District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakho_District), which is administered by the governorate.[^cite_note-48] [Erbil](24534207.md) and [Baghdad](4492.md) have agreed on the most efficient route for the project, which will run from Basra to Duhok and then to [Turkey](11125639.md). The selected route also runs from [Rabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabia,_Iraq) to [Faysh Khabur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faysh_Khabur) and then to the Turkish border. The project is scheduled to begin construction in 2026.[^cite_note-49]

### Public transportation

Public transportation in Duhok mainly consists of [taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis) and shared [vans](63679.md) (locally known as "sarbis").[^cite_note-\:10-50] In May 2025, Duhok introduced its first [electric taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_taxi), with the government waiving additional registration fees to encourage more electric vehicles.[^cite_note-51]

[Buses](4146.md) are also available, however are mostly available for [inter-city travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus_service), with regular services operating from [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Mardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardin), and [Cizre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cizre) in [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

There are no [metros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_train) or [tramways](30733.md) currently operating in Duhok, however, an academic study has proposed a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) system to help reduce [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) in the city.[^cite_note-\:10-50][^cite_note-55]
