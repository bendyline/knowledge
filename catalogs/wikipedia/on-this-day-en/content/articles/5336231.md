**Enos Stanley Kroenke** ([/ˈkrɒŋki/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); born July 29, 1947) is an American [billionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire) real estate magnate and sports team owner. Through his company [Kroenke Sports & Entertainment](5336313.md), he owns [Arsenal F.C.](2174.md) of the English [Premier League](11250.md), [Arsenal W.F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) of the English [Women's Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Super_League), the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) of the [NFL](21211.md), the [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) of the [NBA](22093.md), the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) of the [NHL](21809.md), the [Colorado Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rapids) of [MLS](71802.md), and the [Colorado Mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Mammoth) of the [National Lacrosse League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lacrosse_League).

The Nuggets and Avalanche franchises are held in the name of his wife, [Ann Walton Kroenke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Walton_Kroenke), to evade NFL rules that forbid a team owner from having teams in other markets.[^cite_note-2] Ann is the daughter of [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) co-founder [James "Bud" Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_%22Bud%22_Walton).

Kroenke's holding company for sports teams has been controversial as in 2016, he moved the St. Louis Rams to Los Angeles, which later led to a lawsuit which incurred legal costs for the entire league.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] In 2021, Kroenke was involved in a failed effort to end the traditional European soccer system by creating a closed [European Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Super_League) which would have included Arsenal and several other teams.

In the five major U.S. sports, Kroenke's teams have won multiple championships under his ownership (either partial or full), with the Rams winning one [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowls), the Avalanche winning the [Stanley Cup](66968.md) twice, the Nuggets winning an [NBA Championship](890793.md), and the Rapids winning an [MLS Cup](483246.md).[^cite_note-SK_rings-5][^cite_note-SK_MLS-6] The Colorado Mammoth have also won two National Lacrosse League Championships while he was the owner. Kroenke has also found success in European football, as [Arsenal](2174.md) won the [Premier League](11250.md) in the [2025/2026](77711894.md) season and [Arsenal W.F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) won the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) in the [2024/2025 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League).

## Early life and education

Kroenke grew up in [Mora, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mora,_Missouri), an unincorporated community with a population of about two dozen, where his father owned Mora Lumber Company.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-Kroenke-8] His first job was sweeping the floor at his father's lumber yard.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] At Cole Camp (Missouri) High School, he played baseball, basketball and ran track.[^cite_note-11]

## Business career

### Real estate

Kroenke married [Ann Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Walton_Kroenke), a Walmart heiress, in 1974. In 1983, he founded the Kroenke Group, a real estate development firm which has built shopping centers and apartment buildings and has developed many of his plazas near [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) stores.[^cite_note-Kroenke-8]

Kroenke is also the chairman of [THF Realty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THF_Realty), a real estate development company specializeing in suburban development. He founded it in St. Louis, Missouri, in 1991. In 2016, THF's portfolio was valued at more than \$2 billion, including more than 100 projects totaling 20 million square feet, primarily in retail shopping centers.[^cite_note-portfolio-12]

Kroenke was already wealthy in his own right, but became even more so when Ann inherited a major stake in Walmart upon the death of her father, Walmart cofounder [Bud Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud_Walton), in 1995.[^cite_note-13]

Kroenke is a major owner of working ranches, owning a total of 848,631 acres (343,429 ha) in 2015 when *[The Land Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Land_Report)* magazine ranked him as the United States' ninth-largest landowner.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In 2016, he acquired the [Waggoner Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waggoner_Ranch) in [Texas](29810.md), the largest ranch within one fenceline.[^cite_note-16] As of 2026, Kroenke is the largest private landowner in the U.S., owning over 2.7 million acres across the American West and Canada.[^cite_note-17]

### Other ventures

In 2006, Kroenke in partnership with the money manager Charles Banks, acquired [Screaming Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screaming_Eagle_Winery_and_Vineyards), a [winery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winery) in [Napa Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napa_County,_California). In 2009, Banks said he was no longer involved with Screaming Eagle.[^cite_note-ws06-18]

In 2017, Kroenke came under fire for launching a British outdoor sports television channel that shows hunting programs that include killing elephants, lions, and other vulnerable African species.[^cite_note-19]

## Professional sports

### Kroenke Sports and Entertainment

Main article: [Kroenke Sports & Entertainment](5336313.md)

Founded in 1999, Kroenke Sports & Entertainment owns [Ball Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_Arena) in Denver, home of the Nuggets, Avalanche, and Mammoth, and co-owns [Dick's Sporting Goods Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick's_Sporting_Goods_Park) in [Commerce City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_City,_Colorado), home of the Rapids. Both venues were built by his development company. In 2004, Kroenke launched a competitor to FSN Rocky Mountain (now known as [AT\&T SportsNet Rocky Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_SportsNet_Rocky_Mountain)), [Altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_Sports_and_Entertainment), a [regional sports network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_sports_network) that became the official broadcaster for both of Kroenke's teams on launch. Kroenke also established TicketHorse, a ticket company that provides in-house sales for all his teams.

### Denver Nuggets and Colorado Avalanche



Kroenke (center) at the Nuggets championship parade

In 2000, Kroenke became full owner of both the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) and the [National Hockey League](21809.md)'s [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md), purchasing the teams from [Charlie Lyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Lyons)'s *Ascent Entertainment Group*.

### Colorado Crush

In 2002, Kroenke partnered with Denver Broncos owner [Pat Bowlen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Bowlen) and former Bronco quarterback [John Elway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Elway) to become part owner of the [Arena Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Football_League_\(1987%E2%80%932008\))'s [Colorado Crush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Crush).[^cite_note-20]

### Colorado Mammoth and Colorado Rapids

In 2004, Kroenke purchased the [National Lacrosse League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lacrosse_League)'s [Colorado Mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Mammoth) and [Major League Soccer](71802.md)'s [Colorado Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rapids) from [Phil Anschutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Anschutz).[^cite_note-21]

### St. Louis Rams

On April 13, 1995, Kroenke helped [Georgia Frontiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Frontiere) move the [National Football League](21211.md)'s [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Los_Angeles_Rams) from [Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim,_California) to [St. Louis](27687.md) by purchasing a 30% share of the team.[^cite_note-22]

In 2010, two years after Frontiere's death, Kroenke exercised his [right of first refusal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_first_refusal) to purchase the remaining interest in the Rams from her estate.[^cite_note-23] On August 25, 2010, he became full owner of the Rams by unanimous consent of the NFL. To gain approval from NFL owners, Kroenke agreed to turn over control of the Denver Nuggets and Colorado Avalanche to his son, Josh, by the end of 2010, and to give up his majority stake in both teams in December 2014. The NFL does not allow its owners to hold majority control of major-league teams in other NFL markets.[^cite_note-RamsOwner-24] On October 7, 2015, the NFL approved transfer of his ownership stake of the Avalanche and Nuggets to his wife, [Ann Walton Kroenke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Walton_Kroenke).[^cite_note-25]

#### Stadium lease issues

In April 2010, as Kroenke was trying to gain full ownership of the team, and while knowing of an escape clause in the Rams' lease at the Edward Jones Dome, he said, "I'm going to attempt to do everything that I can to keep the Rams in St. Louis."[^cite_note-26]

In February 2013, the Rams and the City of St. Louis went to arbitration over a clause in the Rams' lease stating their stadium must be among the NFL's best. The arbitrators agreed with the Rams, enabling the team to break their original lease and assume a year-to-year lease agreement.[^cite_note-27]

In 2015, Kroenke said he was willing to work with Missouri officials and give them a "complete understanding" of the stadium situation. On November 30, 2015, he met with Missouri Governor [Jay Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Nixon) at Rams Park in [Earth City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_City,_Missouri).[^cite_note-28]

### Los Angeles Rams

#### Move to Los Angeles

On January 5, 2015, the Kroenke Group announced it would team with [Stockbridge Capital Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockbridge_Capital_Group) to build [a 70,000-seat NFL stadium](45525802.md) and venue in [Inglewood, California](107640.md). To persuade Kroenke to keep the Rams in St. Louis, the city offered to build an open-air stadium, later named [National Car Rental Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Car_Rental_Field), in the north riverfront area of downtown.[^cite_note-29]

In his pitch to fellow NFL owners, Kroenke said St. Louis was no longer a viable market for the Rams or the NFL.[^cite_note-30] NFL commissioner [Roger Goodell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Goodell) said the St. Louis funding did not meet the NFL's criteria.[^cite_note-31] St. Louis officials countered Kroenke was misrepresenting the city where St. Louis was being misrepresented at the owners' meetings.[^cite_note-32]

The [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Oakland_Raiders) and [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_San_Diego_Chargers) had complained about the aging amenities of their stadiums ([Oakland Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Coliseum) in [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California) and [Qualcomm Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SDCCU_Stadium) in [San Diego](28504.md), respectively), and had proposed [a stadium in Carson, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_Stadium) in competition with Kroenke's Inglewood proposal.

On January 4, 2016, all three teams applied to move to Los Angeles for the [2016 NFL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NFL_season).[^cite_note-33] The next day, the Rams and Kroenke released their proposal for the move. Some of the Rams' conclusions were disputed by [Mayor of St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_St._Louis) [Francis Slay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Slay) (in a letter to Goodell),[^cite_note-34] the [St. Louis Regional Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Regional_Chamber),[^cite_note-35] and *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*.[^cite_note-36] Others said the city would be better off not paying for a new stadium.[^cite_note-37]

On January 12, the NFL, after discussions led by [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md) owner [Jerry Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Jones), approved the Rams' application to move from St. Louis back to Los Angeles by a 30–2 vote; the Chargers would have a one-year option to join them.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In 2017, the Chargers announced they would move to Los Angeles for the 2017 season, with the intention to first play in Carson then move into Kroenke's [Inglewood Stadium](45525802.md), pending completion. The Raiders subsequently [announced a move to Las Vegas, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders_relocation_to_Las_Vegas).

On April 12, 2017, it was reported the City of [St. Louis](27687.md), [St. Louis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_County,_Missouri), and the [Regional Convention and Sports Complex Authority](452735.md) had sued the [NFL](21211.md) and all 32 NFL clubs (including Kroenke) and sought damages and restitution of profits.[^cite_note-40] On July 12, 2017, the Rams filed motions seeking to dismiss the case for failure to state a claim, dismiss the case for lack of personal jurisdiction, and appeal to have the case determined through arbitration rather than in front of a St. Louis-based jury. The motions in the case were decided by Judge Christopher McGraugh. On April 20, 2020, the Supreme Court denied Kroenke's and the NFL's appeal of the lawsuit.[^cite_note-41] On November 24, 2021, it was announced the NFL, Kroenke, and the various St. Louis parties had agreed to a \$790 million settlement to end the lawsuit before it would have gone to trial in 2022.[^cite_note-42] Some legal experts believe the lawsuit was not dismissed because Kroenke lied about his true intentions with the Rams, the NFL did not follow its own relocation guidelines created after the league failed to stop the Raiders' move to Los Angeles in the early 80s, and Kroenke/the league allowed St. Louis to spend money planning a [replacement stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Car_Rental_Field) when they had no intention of making a deal to lease it.[^cite_note-43]

### Arsenal



Stan and Josh Kroenke carrying the Premier League trophy on the final day of the 2025/26 season

Kroenke owns [Arsenal Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ownership_of_Arsenal_football_clubs), which owns football clubs [Arsenal](2174.md) of the English [Premier League](11250.md) and [Arsenal Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) of the English [WSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Women's_Super_League). Arsenal already had a connection to Kroenke's Colorado Rapids when, in 2007, Granada Ventures, a subsidiary of [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_plc), sold its 9.9% stake in Arsenal Holdings to Kroenke's KSE UK.[^cite_note-44] Kroenke bought more shares in the club, taking his total stake up to 12.19%.[^cite_note-45] The club's board initially expressed skepticism a bid would be in its best interests,[^cite_note-46] but gradually warmed to him as part of counteracting [Alisher Usmanov](13020970.md)'s rival bid.

By June 2008, the board had prepared to let Kroenke take over,[^cite_note-47] and on September 19, 2008, it was officially announced Kroenke had joined the board of directors.[^cite_note-48] Kroenke had a beneficial interest in, and controlled voting rights, over 18,594 shares, representing 29.9% of the issued shares. At the 29.99% threshold, an offer for all remaining shares must be made.[^cite_note-49]

On April 10, 2011, it was reported Kroenke was in advanced talks to complete the takeover.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The next day, it was announced he had increased his stake to 62.89% by purchasing the stakes of [Danny Fiszman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Fiszman) and Lady [Nina Bracewell-Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Bracewell-Smith), and agreed to make an offer for the rest of the club at £11,750 per share, valuing the club at £731M.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In August 2018, Kroenke made an offer which was accepted of around £600M, valuing the total shareholding at £1.8 billion, to [Alisher Usmanov](13020970.md), to bring his ownership of shares beyond 90% and forcing the purchase of the remaining shares.[^cite_note-54]

In April 2021, Arsenal was announced as a founding member of the [European Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Super_League), which would have effectively ended the pyramid system of European soccer and placed Arsenal in a closed league without prospects for meritocratic relegation and promotion. Arsenal and the five other English clubs involved backed out within two days after a strong backlash. After the aborted attempt to end the European soccer system, Arsenal fans protested and called for the Kroenke family to sell the club.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] The Kroenke family released a statement saying they would not do so.[^cite_note-59]

In 2026, Arsenal were the champion of the English Premier League, their first title in 22 years.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

### Los Angeles Gladiators

In 2017, Kroenke Sports and Entertainment developed a new e-sports team franchise, the [Los Angeles Gladiators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Gladiators), in the newly founded [Overwatch League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwatch_League); their inaugural season began on December 6.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] The team folded in October of 2023 after the Overwatch League dissolved.

### Los Angeles Guerrillas

In 2019, Kroenke Sports and Entertainment developed its second e-sports team, the [Los Angeles Guerrillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Guerrillas) of the [Call of Duty League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty_League). They, along with the Los Angeles Gladiators of the Overwatch League, are based in Hollywood Park, next door to where the Rams play.[^cite_note-64] The team was sold to Gentle Mates out of France in December of 2024.

### Acquisition of the Los Angeles Angels

On September 1, 2026, it was reported Kroenke Sports & Entertainment entered a formal agreement to buy the [Los Angeles Angels](1360083.md), with Stan Kroenke and his ownership group set to become the controlling owners of the team.[^cite_note-65] The deal is set to close in the first quarter of 2027, per MLB's approval.[^cite_note-66]

## Personal life

On a ski trip to [Aspen, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen,_Colorado), Kroenke met his wife, Ann Walton, daughter of Bud Walton, who co-founded Walmart with his brother Sam. As an heiress to the Walmart fortune, Ann is worth \$9.1 billion as of 2023.[^cite_note-Forbes-67] They married in 1974.[^cite_note-Kroenke-8]

Kroenke is of German descent and was raised [Lutheran](23371382.md).[^cite_note-Kroenke-8][^cite_note-68] He is popularly known as "Silent Stan" because he rarely gives interviews.[^cite_note-RamsOwner-24]

Kroenke, a Republican, donated at least \$300,000 to Republican campaigns between 2000 and 2013, and donated \$1 million to Donald Trump's inauguration fund in 2017. During the [2016 U.S. presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), Kroenke donated \$100,000 to the [Hillary Victory Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Victory_Fund), possibly related to concerns around Walmart's labor practices circulating as a campaign issue.[^cite_note-politics1-1]. He subsequently donated \$1 million to [Donald Trump's inaugural committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump#Presidential_Inaugural_Committee).[^cite_note-Guardian-69][^cite_note-sportsillustrated-70][^cite_note-usatoday-71]
