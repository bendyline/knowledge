**Jubail** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجبيل, *Al Jubayl*) is an industrial city in the [Eastern province](468721.md) on the Persian Gulf of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). It is the third most-populous city in the province, after the capital, [Dammam](479214.md), and [Al Hofuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofuf). With a total population of 474,679 as of 2022, it is Saudi Arabia’s [11th most-populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Saudi_Arabia) city. It is home to the Middle East's largest and world's fourth largest petrochemical company [SABIC](1929210.md). It has the world's largest IWPP (Independent Water and Power Project) producing 2,743.6 MW of electricity and 800,000 m<sup>3</sup> of water daily.&#x20;

Jubail comprises the Old Town of Al Jubail (Jubail Balad), which was a small fishing village until 1975, and the Industrial Area. Jubail Industrial City is the largest [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) project in the world today. In 1975, the Saudi government designated Jubail as the site for a new industrial city, with rapid expansion and [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) arising. The new industrial and residential areas were named Madīnat al Jubayl aṣ Ṣinā\`īyah (Jubail Industrial City). The 2005 Census Report for Jubail Industrial City estimates the population at 224,430 residents.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## History



[Tanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_\(leather\)) in Jubail, 1934

The town of Al-Jubail, on the Persian Gulf coast of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, has ancient roots. Human habitation dates back at least 7,000 years, when the people of [Dilmun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilmun) — whose civilization radiated up and down the coast of the Persian Gulf — established a settlement there. Archaeological place: Crease Tower or [Tuwayah Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tawiya_Tower) was constructed around 1928. It was key source of water for Jubail. In September 1933, Jubail gained a measure of fame as the landing site for the first team of geologists to explore for oil in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-2]

The origins of Jubail date back to the early Islamic period when it was known as 'Aynayn ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عينين), meaning "two springs."[^cite_note-3] The settlement was known for its sweet water springs, [date palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) groves, and its status as a cultural and literary center.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

At some point, the original settlement of 'Aynayn was abandoned. The modern town of Jubail was established around 1911 and became a center for fishing and pearling. The town came under [Saudi rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Saudi_Arabia) in 1913 when King '[Abd al-Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud) regained the [al-Hasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahsa_Oasis) Province.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

### 1930s

Jubail experienced economic challenges during the 20th century. The global [economic depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_depression) and the rise of Japan’s [cultured pearl industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultured_pearl) in the 1930s caused a decline in the traditional pearling trade, which had been a key industry for the town. At its peak, Jubail had supported over 200 pearling boats, but this number rapidly dwindled as cultured pearls became dominant.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

The arrival of oil exploration teams in the 1930s briefly revitalized the town. Jubail was chosen as a base for early geological surveys due to its harbor, freshwater springs, and favorable terrain. On September 23, 1933, geologists from [Standard Oil Company of California](290527.md), including Robert Miller, Schuyler "Krug" Henry, and [Karl Twitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Twitchell), arrived in Jubail to conduct exploratory work. However, as oil exploration activities shifted to [Dhahran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhahran) and [Khobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khobar), Jubail reverted to being a minor fishing center.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

### Modern Development

Prior to its industrial transformation, Jubail was primarily a fishing community. As of 1972, the town boasted a fishing fleet of approximately 160 boats, with catches including shrimp and small sharks that were renowned as far as [Kuwait](7515890.md) and [Riyadh](51554.md).[^cite_note-\:1-4]

Jubail’s transformation began in the early 1970s when the Saudi government initiated plans to develop it into a major industrial city. This initiative was part of a broader strategy to diversify the Kingdom's economy and leverage its vast natural gas reserves. Jubail was selected as the site for a massive industrial complex due to its strategic location and existing infrastructure.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

The city's redevelopment included the construction of [refineries](339530.md), [petrochemical plants](2293201.md), [steel mills](1143008.md), aluminum facilities, and [desalination plants](156787.md). To support this industrialization, extensive infrastructure was developed, including new ports, roads, pipelines, and an [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubail_Airport). Thousands of workers were relocated to Jubail to operate the facilities, and the city was redesigned to accommodate the growing population.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

Today, Jubail is home to the Jubail Industrial City, one of the world's largest petrochemical industrial hubs, managed by the [Royal Commission for Jubail and Yanbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_Jubail_and_Yanbu). It is a cornerstone of Saudi Arabia's industrial and economic strategy, contributing significantly to the country's non-oil exports.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

Iran targeted Jubail for strikes in the [2026 Iran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_War).[^cite_note-5]

## Jubail Industrial City

Main article: [Jubail Industrial City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubail_Industrial_City)

It is the world's largest industrial city established in 1975. It covers 1,016 square kilometers and includes industrial complexes, major harbour and port facilities. It contributes to about 7% of Saudi Arabia's GDP.[^cite_note-6]

[Bechtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechtel) began work on the Jubail Industrial City project more than 40 years ago and is still working in Jubail now. Bechtel has managed the Jubail project since it began in the mid-1970s, and in 2004 the Royal Commission for Jubail and Yanbu asked the company to manage Jubail II, a \$3.8 billion expansion of the city's industrial and residential areas.

## Transportation

### Highway

Jubail is directly connected with other cities by two major highways; [Dhahran-Jubail Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhahran-Jubail_Highway) and [Abu Hadriyah Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hadriyah_Highway).

One ongoing project is the Jubail-Qassim (Buraidah) Expressway 500 km (310 mi), which will reduce the distance between Jubail and Qassim (Buraidah) to around 331 km (206 mi) from the current 831 km (516 mi).

### Railway

A branch of the [Saudi Landbridge Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Landbridge_Project) railway is proposed to connect Jubail to [Dammam](479214.md).

### Seaport

There are two seaports in Jubail—the Jubail Commercial Seaport and the King Fahd Industrial Seaport. As of 2011, Jubail ranks 92nd in the world in terms of Total Cargo Volume with 44,700 tons.[^cite_note-7]

### Airport

The city is served by [King Fahd International Airport](2212468.md).

[Jubail Naval Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubail_Naval_Airport?action=edit\&redlink=1) is an airfield 25 kilometres (16 mi) west of Jubail City, near the industrial area. Originally constructed by the Royal Commission of Jubail and Yanbu as part of Jubail project, it was meant to be used for commercial aviation until it was decided to utilize the large nearby [King Fahd International Airport](2212468.md). Thus, it was handed over to the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Saudi_Arabia\)) and has been used as a base for the naval aviation group/eastern fleet of the [Royal Saudi Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Saudi_Navy) since then. The terminal in Dammam Airport is about 60 km drive from the suburbs of Jubail, 80 km from the city center and 100 km from the Royal Commission neighborhoods.

However it was recently announced that the airport will be opened for private aviation operations starting September 2014. A project to renovate the airfield was undertaken since some of the airfield's infrastructure was incomplete, as a result of the previous change for the use of airport from commercial to military. To date, the project and renovations have not been completed.

In addition, two other airfields are located in the city; [Abu Ali Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ali_Airport) is located in the Island of Abu Ali while the other is located in [King Abdulaziz Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Naval_Base).
