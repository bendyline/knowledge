**Kosti** (also **Kusti**, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): كوستي) is one of the major cities in [Sudan](27421.md) that lies south of [Khartoum](17320.md), the capital of [Sudan](27421.md), and stands on the western bank of the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) river opposite [Rabak](14063078.md), the capital of the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile_\(state\)) state and is connected to it by a bridge. The city is served by Kosti Railway Station and [Rabak Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabak_Airport). The population of Kosti as of 2012 was estimated at 345,068 people.

## History

Kosti was founded shortly after 1899 by the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Greeks) merchant Konstantinos "Kostas" Mourikis, who arrived in Sudan along with his brother following the [Anglo-Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_invasion_of_Sudan) victory over the indigenous [Mahdist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdist_Sudan) state.[^cite_note-2] He set up a store on the White Nile, where pilgrims from [West Africa](67393.md) to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) and Southern trade routes crossed. The settlement soon grew to a town and was named after "Kostas", illustrating the important role played by the [Greeks in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Sudan), especially in the field of trade.[^cite_note-3] In 1911, the [Goz Abu Goma Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goz_Abu_Goma_Bridge) connecting Rabak and Kosti was completed

In May 2023, [clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Kosti_clashes) between Nuba and Hausa groups led to the deaths of 29 people in Kosti.

### Displacement site

Kosti received large numbers of [displaced people](427408.md) from the early 1980s, mainly from southern Sudan, Darfur, Kordofan and elsewhere in White Nile State. In 1992, the IDP camps were relocated from Combo to Goz Al Salam and Allaya, about 4 to 6 km southeast of Kosti. Most residents of the two camps belonged to southern ethnic groups, including the Dinka, Shilluk, Nuer, Latoka and Bari.[^cite_note-proint-4]

Many residents had lost their former [agro-pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agro-pastoralism) livelihoods and were generally unable to obtain land for cultivation near Kosti. Some travelled to central and eastern Sudan for seasonal agricultural work, while others relied on fishing, growing rice for sale, petty trade, domestic work and casual labour. In 2002, Dan Kuch, Goz Es Salam and Liya were identified as particularly vulnerable IDP camps in Kosti. IDPs in White Nile State faced [malnutrition](258979.md), [waterborne diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterborne_diseases), a lack of [sanitation services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WASH) and inadequate schools, health centres and trained personnel.[^cite_note-proint-4]

A 2002 UN report stated that Kosti had about 3,000 street children, the second-largest concentration in Sudan after Khartoum. It linked prolonged displacement among the city’s IDP population to family disintegration and relative increases in orphans, female-headed households and unemployment.[^cite_note-proint-4]

Since early March 2026, Goz Al Salam’s population has grown by more than 3,000 to approximately 30,000.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-loofut-6] The camp remains overcrowded, with shortages of shelter, food and other essential supplies. Water, sanitation and hygiene services have not kept pace with new arrivals, placing further strain on limited facilities.[^cite_note-loofut-6]

## Economy

The large [Kenana Sugar Factory](83012015.md) is located south of Rabak, east of the city.

Kosti is home to the [El Imam El Mahdi University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Imam_El_Mahdi_University), established in 1994 as a public university funded by the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research. It is named in honor of [Muhammad Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ahmad), the leader of the Mahdia revolution in Sudan (1885-1898).[^cite_note-7]

## Climate

Kosti has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) (*BWh*) according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). Like most of Sudan, the city has a very dry climate. The climate stays hot throughout the year, even into November temperatures still break 100 °F (38 °C).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]
