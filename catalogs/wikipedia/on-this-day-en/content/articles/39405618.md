# List of Mount Everest records

This article is about the list of records established on Mount Everest. For the record label, see [Everest Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everest_Records).



From [Kala Patthar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_Patthar), west of Everest looking the South West face primarily



Mount Everest from [Gokyo Ri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokyo_Ri), showing a little more of the North face



[Tashi and Nungshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashi_and_Nungshi_Malik) were the first twins to summit Mount Everest together.

This article lists different records related to [Mount Everest](42179.md). One of the most commonly sought after records is a "summit", meaning to reach the highest elevation point on Mount Everest.

### Nepali records



[Chhurim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhurim) summited twice in May 2012, the first woman to summit twice in a season.

Many Mount Everest records are held by Nepali, especially those from the Sherpa region.

- On 11 May 2011, [Apa Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apa_Sherpa) successfully reached the summit of Everest for the twenty-first time, breaking his own record for the most successful ascents.

  [^cite_note-135]

  &#x20;He first climbed Mount Everest in 1989 at the age of 29.

  [^cite_note-136]

  - [Phurba Tashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phurba_Tashi) Sherpa (also 21 times)

  - [Kami Rita Sherpa](54159070.md) (24 times by 2019)

- One famous Nepalese female mountaineer was [Pasang Lhamu Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasang_Lhamu_Sherpa), the first Nepali female climber to reach the summit of Everest, but who died during the descent. Another well-known woman Sherpa was the two-time Everest summiter [Pemba Doma Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemba_Doma_Sherpa), who died after falling from [Lhotse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhotse) on 22 May 2007.

  [^cite_note-137]

- Nepali mountaineer [Lhakpa Sherpa](50511396.md), the first Nepali female climber to reach the summit of Everest and descend from it, stood atop Everest 7 times by 2016 and 8 times by 2017, the most times for woman. She successfully completed her 11th record-breaking ascent of Everest in May 2026.

  [^cite_note-138]

  [^cite_note-139]

  [^cite_note-140]

- Nima Jangmu Sherpa, 28, made the historic ascent on Mount Kanchenjunga on 23 May 2018 morning and became the only woman in the world to climb Nepal's three highest peaks (Mount Everest -14 May 2018, Mount Lhotse - 29 April 2018, Mount Kanchenjunga – 23 May 2018) above 8,000 metres in a single season within 25 days. She has also become the only Nepali woman to stand atop the world's third highest peak, Mount Kanchenjunga.

  [^cite_note-141]

Other examples of noted Nepali Everest mountaineers:

- [Temba Tsheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temba_Tsheri)

- Nima Chhamzi Sherpa

- [Nimdoma Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimdoma_Sherpa)

- [Ming Kipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Kipa)

- [Chhurim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhurim)

*About names: Many Nepalese are only given one name, and are often named for a day of the week*

One source of confusion was in record keeping was that the South side was essentially closed in 2014 due to an avalanche disrupting the Khumbu Icefall. So most of the summiters summited from the North side, except for a small group that flew a helicopter *over* the Khumbu Icefall, even though it was not open. This is why in 2016 [Adrian Ballinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Ballinger) said on Instagram "9 Sherpa using supplemental oxygen became the first to summit on the South Side of Everest in the past 3 years" in 2016.[^cite_note-cnn.com-142] The full Nepal route to the summit was not open in 2014 or in 2015, but there was a group that summited from the south side in 2014 as part of a Chinese team that used a helicopter to reach that area.[^cite_note-cnn.com-142]
