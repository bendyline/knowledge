A contemporary [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) strategic bomber, the [Rockwell B-1B Lancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_B-1B_Lancer)

A **strategic bomber** is a medium-to-long-range [bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber) aircraft designed to drop large amounts of [air-to-ground weaponry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-ground_weaponry) onto a distant target for the purposes of debilitating the enemy's capacity to wage war. Unlike [tactical bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_bombing), [penetrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetrator_\(aircraft\)), [fighter-bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter-bomber), and [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft), which are used in [air interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction) operations to attack enemy combatants and military equipment, strategic bombers are designed to fly into enemy territory to destroy strategic targets (e.g., [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics), [military installations](843566.md), factories, etc.). In addition to [strategic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing), strategic bombers can be used for tactical missions. There are currently only three countries that operate strategic [heavy bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_bomber): the [United States](3434750.md), [Russia](25391.md) and [China](5405.md).

The modern strategic bomber role appeared after [strategic bombing was widely employed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II), and [atomic bombs were first used](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) during [World War II](32927.md). Nuclear strike missions (i.e., delivering [nuclear](21785.md)-armed [missiles](1544116.md) or [bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb)) can potentially be carried out by most modern [fighter-bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter-bomber) and [strike fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_fighter), even at intercontinental range, with the use of [aerial refueling](237949.md), so any nation possessing this combination of equipment and techniques theoretically has such capability. Primary delivery aircraft for a modern strategic bombing mission need not always necessarily be a heavy bomber type, and any modern aircraft capable of nuclear strikes at long range is equally able to carry out [tactical missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_bombing) with [conventional weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_weapon). An example is [France](5843419.md)'s [Mirage IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_IV), a small strategic bomber replaced in service by the [ASMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-Sol_Moyenne_Port%C3%A9e)-equipped [Mirage 2000N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000N/2000D) fighter-bomber and [Rafale](33731380.md) [multirole fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft).

As of 2025, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) is the largest operator with 130 strategic bombers in active service, between the [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md), [B-1 Lancer](4952.md), and [Northrop B-2 Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_B-2_Spirit). The [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) is second with 120 [Xi'an H-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an_H-6) bombers, and the [Russian Air Force](1526308.md) third, with 119 bombers between its [Tu-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95), [Tu-22M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M), and [Tu-160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-160).[^cite_note-directory-1]

## History

### First and Second World Wars



The [Sikorsky Ilya Muromets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Ilya_Muromets) was designed by [Igor Sikorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Sikorsky) as the first ever [airliner](51215.md), but it was turned into a bomber by the [Imperial Russian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Air_Force).

The [first strategic bombing efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_I) took place during [World War I](4764461.md) (1914–18), by the [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) with their [Sikorsky Ilya Muromets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Ilya_Muromets) bomber (the first heavy four-engine aircraft), and by the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) using [Zeppelins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin) or long-range multi-engine [Gotha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothaer_Waggonfabrik) [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft). Zeppelins reached [England](9316.md) on bombing raids by 1916, forcing the British to create extensive defense systems including some of the first [anti-aircraft guns](146640.md) which were often used with searchlights to highlight the enemy machines overhead. Late in the war, [American](3434750.md) fliers under the command of Brig. Gen. [Billy Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Mitchell) were developing multi-aircraft "mass" bombing missions behind German lines, although the [Armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Armistice_at_Compi%C3%A8gne) ended full realization of what was being planned.

Study of strategic bombing continued in the interwar years. Many books and articles predicted a fearful prospect for any future war, paced by political fears such as those expressed by [British Prime Minister](24150.md) [Stanley Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Baldwin) who told the [House of Commons](13828.md) early in the 1930s that "[the bomber will always get through](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_bomber_will_always_get_through)" no matter what defensive systems were undertaken. It was widely believed by the late 1930s that [strategic "terror" bombing of cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bombing_of_cities) in any [war](33158.md) would quickly result in devastating losses and might decide a conflict in a matter of days or weeks. But theory far exceeded what most air forces could actually put into the air. Germany focused on short-range tactical bombers. [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain)'s [Royal Air Force](25679.md) began developing four-engine long-range bombers only in the late 1930s. The [U.S. Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Corps) ([*Army Air Forces*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) as of mid-1941) was severely limited by small budgets in the late 1930s, and only barely saved the [B-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-17_Flying_Fortress) bomber that would soon be vital. The equally important [B-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) first flew in 1939. Both aircraft would constitute the bulk of the bomber force for [USAAF strategic bombing in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II#US_bombing_in_Europe) and [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) [day bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_bomber) units more generally.

At the start of [World War II](32927.md), so-called "strategic" bombing was initially carried out by [medium bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_bomber) [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft) which were typically twin-engined, armed with several defensive guns, but only possessed limited [bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb)-carrying capacity and [range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_\(aeronautics\)). Both [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) and the [US](3434750.md) were developing larger two- and four-engined designs, which began to replace or supplement the smaller aircraft by 1941–42. After American entry into the war in December 1941, the U.S. [8th Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Air_Force) began to develop a [daylight bombing capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II#US_bombing_in_Europe) using improved [B-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-17_Flying_Fortress) and [B-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) four-engine aircraft. In order to assemble the formations to carry out these bombing campaigns, [assembly ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_ships) were used to quickly form defensive [combat boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_box). The [RAF](25679.md) [concentrated its efforts on night bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II#The_British_later_in_the_war). But neither force was able to develop adequate [bombsights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombsight) or tactics to allow for often-bragged "pinpoint" accuracy. The post-war U.S. [Strategic Bombing Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Bombing_Survey) studies supported the overall notion of strategic bombing, but underlined many of its shortcomings as well. Attempts to create pioneering examples of "[smart bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition#Radio-controlled_weapons)" resulted in the [Azon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azon) ordnance, deployed in the [European Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Theater_of_Operations_United_States_Army) and [CBI Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Burma_India_Theater) from B-24s.



The only operational strategic bomber with the *[Luftwaffe](17885.md)* in [World War II](32927.md) was the troubled [Heinkel He 177](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinkel_He_177).

Following the untimely death of the top German advocate for strategic bombing, *General* [Walther Wever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Wever_\(general\)) in early June 1936, the focus of Nazi Germany's [Luftwaffe](17885.md) bomber forces, the so-named *[Kampfgeschwader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampfgeschwader)* (bomber wings) became the battlefield support of the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)) as part of the general [Blitzkrieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitzkrieg) form of warfare, carried out with both medium bombers such as the [Heinkel He 111](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinkel_He_111), and *[Schnellbombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnellbomber)* such as the [Junkers Ju 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_88)A. Support for the [Ural bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_bomber) project before the start of WW II dwindled after Wever's death, with the only aircraft design that could closely match the Allied bomber force's aircraft – the [Heinkel He 177](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinkel_He_177)A, originated in early November 1937, deployed in its initial form in 1941–42, hampered by a [RLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Air_Ministry) requirement for it to also perform medium-angle [dive bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dive_bombing), not rescinded until September 1942 – unable to perform either function properly, with a powerplant selection and particular powerplant installation design features on the 30-meter wingspan *Greif*, that led to constant problems with engine fires. The trans-Atlantic ranged *[Amerika Bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerika_Bomber)* program started in March 1942 sought to ameliorate the lack of a long-range bomber for the *Luftwaffe*, but led only to three [Messerschmitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Me_264)- and two [Junkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_390)-built prototypes ever flying, and no operational "heavy bombers" for strategic use for the Third Reich beyond the roughly one thousand He 177s built.

By the end of the Second World War in 1945, the "heavy" bomber, epitomized by the British [Avro Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lancaster) and American [Boeing B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress) used in the [Pacific Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean_theater_of_World_War_II), showed what could be accomplished by [area bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_Bombardment) of Japan's cities and the often small and dispersed factories within them. Under Major General [Curtis LeMay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_LeMay), the U.S. [20th Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Air_Force), based in the [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands), undertook low-level [incendiary bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incendiary_bomb) missions, results of which were soon measured in the number of square miles destroyed. The [air raids on Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Japan) had withered the nation's ability to continue fighting, although the Japanese government delayed surrender until [atomic bombs were dropped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) on [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima) and [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki) in August 1945.

### The Cold War and its aftermath

During the [Cold War](325329.md), the United States and United Kingdom on one side and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) on the other kept strategic bombers ready to [take off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeoff) on short notice as part of the [deterrent](735661.md) strategy of [mutually assured destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutually_assured_destruction) (MAD). Most strategic bombers of the two [superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower) were designed to deliver [nuclear weapons](21785.md). For a time, some squadrons of [Boeing B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md) bombers were [kept in the air around the clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Chrome_Dome), [orbiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loiter_\(aeronautics\)) some distance away from their [fail-safe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fail-safe) points near the Soviet border.

The British produced three different "[V bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_bomber)" for the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) which were designed and designated to be able to deliver British-made nuclear bombs to targets in European Russia. These bombers would have been able to reach and destroy cities such as Kiev or Moscow before American strategic bombers. While they were never used against the Soviet Union or its allies, two V bomber types, the [Avro Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Vulcan) and the [Handley Page Victor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handley_Page_Victor), were used in the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) towards the end of their operational lives.

The Soviet Union produced hundreds of unlicensed copies of the American [Boeing B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress), which the [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) called the [Tupolev Tu-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-4). The Soviets later developed the [jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft)-powered [Tupolev Tu-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-16) "Badger".

The People's Republic of China produced a version of [Tupolev Tu-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-16) on license from the Soviet Union in the 1960s, which they named the [Xian H-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xian_H-6).

During the 1960s France produced its [Dassault Mirage IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_IV) nuclear-armed bomber for the [French Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_Force) as a part of its independent nuclear strike force, the *[Force de Frappe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_de_dissuasion)*, using French-made bombers and [IRBMs](337326.md) to deliver French-made [nuclear weapons](21785.md). Mirage IVs served until mid-1996 in the bomber role, and to 2005 as [reconnaissance aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft).

The French Republic limited its strategic armaments to a squadron of four [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion) [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarines), with 16 [SLBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine-launched_ballistic_missile) tubes apiece. France also maintains an active force of supersonic [fighter-bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter-bomber) carrying [ASMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-Sol_Moyenne_Port%C3%A9e) stand-off nuclear [missiles](19594.md), with [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) 3 speed and a range of 500 kilometers. These missiles can be delivered by the [Dassault Mirage 2000N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000N/2000D) and [Rafale](33731380.md) [fighter-bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter-bomber); the Rafale is also capable of [refueling](237949.md) others in flight using a [buddy refueling pod](237949.md#Probe-and-drogue_2).



A [USAF](32090.md) [B-2 Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_B-2_Spirit) [stealth bomber.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_aircraft)

Newer strategic bombers such as the [Rockwell B-1 Lancer](4952.md), the [Tupolev Tu-160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-160), and the [Northrop Grumman B-2 Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_B-2_Spirit) designs incorporate various levels of [stealth technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_technology) in an effort to avoid detection, especially by [radar](25676.md) networks. Despite these advances earlier strategic bombers, for example the [B-52](18933037.md) last manufactured in 1962 and the [Tupolev Tu-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95), remain in service and can also deploy the latest air-launched [cruise missiles](6590.md) and other "[stand-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standoff_missile)" or [precision guided weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) such as the [JASSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-158_JASSM) and the [JDAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munition).

The [Russian Air Force](1526308.md)'s new [Tu-160M2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-160) strategic bombers are expected to be delivered on a regular basis over the course of 10 to 20 years. The [Tu-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95) and Tu-160 bombers will be periodically updated, as was done during the 1990s with the [Tu-22M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M) bombers.

Strategic bombers of the [Cold War](325329.md) were primarily armed with nuclear weapons. During the post-1940s [Indochina Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina_Wars), and also since the end of the Cold War, modern bombers originally intended for [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing) use have been exclusively employed using [non-nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_weapon), [high explosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_explosive) weapons. During the [Vietnam War](32611.md), [Operation Menu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Menu), [Operation Freedom Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Freedom_Deal), [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), [military action in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)), and the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), American B-52s and B-1s were mostly employed in [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_bombing) roles. During the [Soviet-Afghan war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet-Afghan_war) in 1979–88, [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) Tu-22Ms carried out several mass air raids in various regions of [Afghanistan](737.md).

As of 2025, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) is the largest operator with 130 strategic bombers in active service, between the [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md), [B-1 Lancer](4952.md), and [Northrop B-2 Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_B-2_Spirit). The [Russian Air Force](1526308.md) is second with 119 bombers between its [Tu-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95), [Tu-22M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M), and [Tu-160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-160).[^cite_note-directory-1]

## Notable strategic bombers

### Nomenclature

Bombers listed below were used in the main or represented a shift in long-range bomber design (Maximum bomb load). In practice, bomb loads carried are dependent on factors such as the distance to target and the individual type, size or weight of bombs used.

Nomenclature for size classification of aircraft types used in [strategic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing) varies, particularly since the time of World War II due to sequential technological advancements and changes in [aerial warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare) strategy and tactics. The [B-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress), for example was a benchmark aircraft of the [heavy bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_bomber) type at end of [World War II](32927.md) due to its size, range and load carrying ability; as the [Cold War](325329.md) began, it became an intercontinental [range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_\(aeronautics\)) strategic bomber with the development of new techniques, such as [aerial refueling](237949.md) (which also greatly extended the range of other medium- to long-range [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber), [fighter-bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter-bomber) and [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft)).

During the 1950s the U.S. [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) also briefly brought back the outdated term "[medium bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_bomber)" to distinguish its [Boeing B-47 Stratojets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-47_Stratojet) from somewhat larger contemporary [Boeing B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md) "[heavy bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_bomber)" in bombardment wings; older [B-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress) and [B-50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-50_Superfortress) heavy bombers were also redesignated as "medium" during this period.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] SAC's [nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenclature) here was purely semantic and bureaucratic, however as both the B-47 and B-52 strategic bombers were much larger and had far greater performance and load-carrying ability than any of the World War II-era heavy or medium bombers.

Other aircraft such as the twin-[jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine) [US](3434750.md) [FB-111](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-111_Aardvark#FB-111A_/_F-111G), [Douglas A-3 Skywarrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-3_Skywarrior) and [France](5843419.md)'s [Dassault Mirage IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_IV) had nominal warloads of less than 20,000 lb (9,100 kg), and were significantly smaller in size and gross weight compared with their strategic bomber contemporaries, based on which they might be classified as medium bombers. In the [nuclear](21785.md) strike role, France would replace its Mirage IVs beginning in the late 1980s with the even smaller, single-engine [Mirage 2000N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000N/2000D) [fighter-bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter-bomber), a further example of advancing technologies and changing tactics in [military aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aviation) and [aircraft design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering). France's newer twin-engine [Dassault Rafale](33731380.md) [multirole fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft) also has nuclear strike capability.

### Future

- [Xian H-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xian_H-20). An under-development stealth bomber by China. Planned to be deployed in 2025.

- [Northrop Grumman B-21 Raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_B-21_Raider). An under-development stealth bomber by the United States, with a goal of supplanting the current Rockwell B-1 Lancer and Northrop Grumman B-2 Spirit.

- [Tupolev PAK DA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_PAK_DA). An under-development stealth bomber by Russia, with a goal of supplanting a portion or all of the current Tupolev Tu-95. Planned to be deployed in 2027.
