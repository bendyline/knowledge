On January 7, 2026, **Renée Nicole Macklin Good**, a 37-year-old American woman, was fatally shot by [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) agent Jonathan Ross[^cite_note-5] in [Minneapolis](6097240.md), Minnesota, during [Operation Metro Surge](82105033.md). Good was in her car stopped sideways in the street, which led Ross to circle her vehicle on foot. Other agents approached, and one ordered her to get out of the car while reaching through her open window. Good briefly reversed, then began moving forward and to the right, into the direction of traffic. At this point, Ross was at the front-left of the vehicle which was turning away, when he fired three shots, killing her. The killing sparked national protests and multiple investigations.

[Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) officials, Acting Attorney General [Todd Blanche](74006584.md), and President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) defended the shooting, saying the agent acted in [self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense), that Good ran him over, and that the agent was recovering in a hospital. Their accounts of the shooting were contested by eyewitnesses, journalists,[^cite_note-Lum-2026b-6] [federal prosecutors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_prosecutors),[^cite_note-\:0-7] and [Democratic Party](5043544.md) lawmakers, some of whom called for criminal proceedings against Ross.[^cite_note-Barnett-2026-8][^cite_note-The_Guardian-2026-9] The president and federal officials were criticized for espousing conclusions before any investigation had occurred. Minneapolis mayor [Jacob Frey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Frey) and Minnesota governor [Tim Walz](2216593.md) called on ICE to end their presence in the city.

The killing sparked widespread protests in Minneapolis[^cite_note-Mitchell-2026-10] and [in cities across the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_U.S._immigration_enforcement_protests) including Chicago, [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_and_New_York_City_during_the_second_Trump_presidency#January_7,_2026), Los Angeles, San Francisco, Seattle, and Washington, D.C. Marches in Minneapolis prompted the closing of public schools and the deployment of more police officers. Federal agents used [tear gas](1202646.md) and [pepper spray](157302.md) against protesters, and Governor Walz placed the [National Guard](4562088.md) on standby.

Leaders of the [Department of Justice](52563.md) (DOJ)'s [Civil Rights Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Civil_Rights_Division) declined to open a constitutional investigation, which led more than a dozen federal prosecutors in Minneapolis and Washington to resign in protest. Minnesota attorney general [Keith Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Ellison), along with the cities of Minneapolis and [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota), filed suit against the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) to halt ICE deployments. The incident intensified national debate over immigration enforcement and renewed calls to [abolish ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE).

As part of the Trump administration's [sweeping deportation efforts](78314324.md) during his second presidency, ICE agents have been increasingly involved in violent confrontations with migrants and US citizens as part of the agency's shift to more aggressive immigration enforcement.[^cite_note-11] On January 6, DHS announced what it called the largest immigration enforcement operation ever carried out, sending 2,000 agents to the [Minneapolis–Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul) metropolitan area. The surge included Homeland Security Investigations officers focused on [suspected fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020s_Minnesota_fraud_scandals#2025_developments). Saint Paul City Council member Molly Coleman described the first day of the action as "unlike any other day we've experienced".[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Luscombe-2026b-13] An eyewitness to the shooting said, "People in our neighborhood have been terrorized by ICE for six weeks."[^cite_note-Colorado_Public_Radio-2026-14] Good's killing was the ninth time in five states and Washington, D.C., that ICE agents had opened fire on people since September 2025.[^cite_note-Lum-2026a-15] At least four other people have died during federal deportation operations since the enforcement surge began in 2025.[^cite_note-16]

### Renée Good

**Renée Nicole Macklin Good**[^cite_note-22] ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Ganger**; April 2, 1988 – January 7, 2026)[^cite_note-Thomas-2026b-23] was a 37-year-old [US citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_citizen).[^cite_note-Swanson-2026-24] She was a writer and poet[^cite_note-Walsh-2026-25][^cite_note-Cho-2026-26] who lived in Minneapolis with her partner[^cite_note-27] and a six-year-old child from her second marriage.[^cite_note-Biesecker-2026b-28][^cite_note-Walsh-2026-25][^cite_note-Thomas-2026b-23] Originally from [Colorado Springs, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado), she graduated with a degree in English from [Old Dominion University](308123.md).[^cite_note-Wertheimer-2026-29][^cite_note-Maher-2026-30] According to a neighbor, Good had previously lived in [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), before relocating to Canada along with her partner and family following Trump's victory in the [2024 US presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_presidential_election). Later, she moved to Minneapolis.[^cite_note-Johnson-2026b-31] Good had been married twice. She and her first husband were married from 2009 to 2016 and had two children. She had one child with her second husband. Her second husband, a military veteran, died in 2023 at the age of 36.[^cite_note-Maher-2026-30][^cite_note-Biesecker-2026b-28][^cite_note-Musa-2026-32]

Federal officials declined to identify the shooter, but the day after the shooting, agent **Jonathan Ross** was identified as the agent who shot Good by the *[Minnesota Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Star_Tribune)*, [CNN](62028.md), and *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*, which cited court records for his identity.[^cite_note-Sawyer-2026-2][^cite_note-Chapman-2026-3][^cite_note-Sacchetti-2026-4]

Ross has been married twice. His first marriage began shortly before his deployment to Iraq and ended in a divorce filed before he returned from deployment. He remarried in 2015. Ross graduated in 2007 with a degree in business administration from [Anderson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_University_\(Indiana\)).[^cite_note-Agent_Who_Shot_Renee_Good_Was_Trained_to_Track_and_Apprehend_Fugitives-35]

Ross served in the [Indiana National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_National_Guard) from 2002 to 2008, and was a machine gunner in the [Iraq War](5043324.md) from 2004 to 2005.[^cite_note-Warnecke-2026-36] He worked for the [US Border Patrol](567453.md) from 2007 to 2015.[^cite_note-Foley-2026-37] Court documents listed his start date with ICE as 2016.[^cite_note-Sweet-2026-38] He was in the Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO) unit of ICE at the time of the shooting.[^cite_note-Adamczeski-2026-39] Ross testified in December that he was "a firearms instructor, an active shooter instructor...a field intelligence officer, and...a member of the [SWAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAT) team, the St. Paul Special Response Team".[^cite_note-40]

On June 17, 2025, Ross was injured during a traffic stop in [Bloomington, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Minnesota). While trying to arrest someone inside a car, Ross broke a window and reached in to unlock the door. The driver then accelerated, dragging Ross some 50 yards (45 m).[^cite_note-Sawyer-2026-2][^cite_note-Fields-41][^cite_note-Foley-2026-37] The incident was mentioned by DHS secretary [Kristi Noem](27663933.md) and Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md) in press briefings after Ross shot Good.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### Shooting

Keeping his phone in his left hand, Ross drew his gun, leaned forward, and fired three shots at Good in the moving SUV in under one second, all hitting her, at 09\:37\:13 a.m.[^cite_note-ST2026-01-10-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-Bogel-Burroughs-2026-65] He remained upright on the left of the SUV as it passed.[^cite_note-Bogel-Burroughs-2026-65][^cite_note-Inal-2026-1][^cite_note-Kim-2026b-61][^cite_note-Pattee-2026-70] According to separate video analyzes by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* and [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\)), Ross fired the first shot at the SUV's windshield and the second and third shots through the driver's side window of the departing SUV.[^cite_note-Bogel-Burroughs-2026-65][^cite_note-Inal-2026-1]

### Post-shooting



Federal agents on [Portland Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_and_Portland_Avenues_\(Minneapolis\)) in Minneapolis on the day Renée Good was killed

After the shooting, the SUV continued down the street until it crashed into a parked car and light pole.[^cite_note-Burke-2026-71][^cite_note-Lum-2026a-15][^cite_note-Cox-2026-62][^cite_note-72] As it did so, the recording made by Ross captured a male voice saying "fucking bitch". According to voice and video analysis by *The New York Times*, it was Ross speaking.[^cite_note-Agent_Who_Shot_Renee_Good_Was_Trained_to_Track_and_Apprehend_Fugitives-35][^cite_note-Video_Analysis_of_ICE_Shooting_Sheds_Light_on_Contested_Moments-73] Ross approached the crashed SUV and returned to his colleagues. He told other agents to call [911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/911_\(emergency_telephone_number\)) and remained on scene for more than a minute.[^cite_note-Davis-2026-74][^cite_note-Minnesota_Public_Radio-2026-59][^cite_note-Schulberg-2026-60][^cite_note-Lum-2026b-6] A nearby resident said that, after he heard Good's SUV smash, he went outside and saw Becca Good "covered in blood" and sitting in front of the building, crying, "You guys just killed my wife."[^cite_note-Harvey-2026-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-Luscombe-2026a-52] Becca Good was also seen sobbing "I made her come down here, it's my fault", and saying "they just shot my wife".[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

The ICE agents blocked a bystander who attempted to provide medical care. The bystander asked the agents, "Can I check a pulse?" An agent replied, "No, back up now!" The bystander said, "I'm a physician!" An agent replied, "I don't care." Another agent said [EMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services) was on the way, and another said they had their own medics on scene. A woman asked, "Where are they?"[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-Lum-2026b-6][^cite_note-Inal-2026-1] *The New York Times* reported in their analysis of a video of the incident that after the shooting that "several agents, including the agent who opened fire, \[got] in their vehicles and \[drove] off, apparently altering the active crime scene".[^cite_note-Lum-2026b-6]

At 09\:43\:14 a.m., six minutes after the first shot was fired, EMS and firefighters arrived, began removing Good from her vehicle, and attempted to render aid.[^cite_note-Inal-2026-1] *The* *New York Times* obtained records showing Good had an irregular pulse when the emergency workers arrived, and a short time later, no pulse.[^cite_note-83] Good had four gunshot wounds: two in the chest, one in the forearm, and one in the head. Her pupils were dilated and blood was pouring from her ear.[^cite_note-DayStarTribune-84] By 09\:45\:30 a.m., Good was carried to Portland Avenue and 34th Street, where she began receiving [CPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPR), eight minutes after being shot. Seven minutes later, around 09\:52 a.m., Good was placed into an ambulance.[^cite_note-Inal-2026-1] She was taken to [Hennepin County Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hennepin_County_Medical_Center), where she was pronounced dead.[^cite_note-Colorado_Public_Radio-2026-14] Good's pet dog in the back seat was unharmed.[^cite_note-85]

Good's family commissioned an independent autopsy; it found that she suffered a graze wound and separate gunshot wounds to her left forearm and right breast which would not have been "immediately life-threatening", and that a temple wound indicated that a bullet had gone through her head and likely killed her.[^cite_note-86]

### Subsequent events

ICE continued conducting operations in Minneapolis, extending south into neighboring [Richfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richfield,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Romanucci & Blandin, the same firm who represented [George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd)'s family after [his murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd), was hired to represent Good's family and estate. In a statement, the firm said that Good's family does not want her to be "used as a political pawn, but rather as an agent of peace for all".[^cite_note-89]

## Investigations



ICE and Border Patrol agents at the scene of the shooting

On January 8, the head of the [Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Bureau_of_Criminal_Apprehension) disclosed that the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI) had revoked their access to evidence of the shooting, reversing an earlier agreement that a joint investigation would be undertaken by the two agencies.[^cite_note-90] Minnesota's [public safety commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Public_Safety) said "it would be extremely difficult, if not impossible" for local investigation to continue "without cooperation from the federal government".[^cite_note-Karnowski-2026a-91]

Minneapolis's chief county prosecutor [Mary Moriarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Moriarty) said her office was "exploring all options to ensure a state level investigation can continue".[^cite_note-Karnowski-2026b-92] On January 9, she and Minnesota attorney general [Keith Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Ellison) asked residents to submit, per *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, "videos, photos and eyewitness accounts of the shooting and the events leading up to it". The officials said it was too early to tell if enough evidence could be gathered to decide whether to charge the shooter.[^cite_note-De_Avila-2026-93]

ICE policy requires agents to wear active body cameras to record enforcement and interactions for review in serious incidents.[^cite_note-Santana-2026b-56] *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* reported on January 9 that the ICE agents at the scene were equipped with body-worn cameras, but that footage had not yet been released to the public;[^cite_note-Ensor-2026-58] [MPR News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPR_News) reported on January 8 that it was not known whether or not Ross had been wearing one.[^cite_note-Stroozas-2026-94] The [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported on January 9 that DHS "has not responded to questions about whether the officer who opened fire or any of the others who were on the scene were wearing body cameras".[^cite_note-Santana-2026b-56]

The prevailing view among prosecutors in the US attorney’s office in the [District of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Minnesota) was that Good was not attempting to hit Ross with her vehicle and that shooting was unjustified. Prosecutors also viewed stopping the bystander from attempting to provide medical care as another potential criminal act. Following standard protocol, they opened an investigation into whether Good's civil rights had been violated. On January 8, a warrant to conduct a forensic analysis of Good's car was signed off by a judge but not acted on by the FBI. DOJ officials told the prosecutors to take the warrant back and replace it with one that investigated Good as a domestic terrorist.[^cite_note-\:0-7] Former acting US attorney [Joseph H. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_H._Thompson_\(attorney\)) and five other Minnesota federal prosecutors then resigned their positions due to "the Justice Department's push to investigate the widow of \[Renée Good] and the department's reluctance to investigate the shooter" according to *The New York Times*.[^cite_note-resign2-95]

On January 12, *The New York Times* reported that in addition to investigating Ross's actions, federal investigators at the FBI and DOJ were also tasked with analyzing any connections that Good might have had to activist groups protesting the Trump administration's immigration policies. The DOJ's Civil Rights Division did not open a civil rights investigation.[^cite_note-96] This decision by Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights [Harmeet Dhillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmeet_Dhillon), combined with previous decisions, led to the division's criminal section chief, principal deputy chief, deputy chief, and acting deputy chief, among other prosecutors in the office, resigning on January 13.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-Civil_Rights_Division_resign-98] Additionally, former acting US attorney [Joseph H. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_H._Thompson_\(attorney\)) and five other federal prosecutors in the [District of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Minnesota) resigned their positions due to "the Justice Department's push to investigate the widow of \[Renée Good] and the department's reluctance to investigate the shooter" according to *The New York Times*.[^cite_note-resign-99] DOJ officials announced that along with criminal investigations into Good's widow, the department would probe into Walz and Frey for an alleged conspiracy to impede federal officers from carrying out their duties.[^cite_note-KashiwagiMST-100] On January 23, *The New York Times* reported that Tracee Mergen, a supervisor in the FBI's Minneapolis field office, resigned after FBI leadership pushed her to abandon a civil rights inquiry into Ross.[^cite_note-101]

Trump appointee Daniel N. Rosen, US attorney for Minnesota, sent an internal email instructing prosecutors to "say nothing" to law enforcement and the media about Good's killing, and that because of its sensitive nature, only his designees could speak to investigators.[^cite_note-102] US deputy attorney general [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) stated on *[Fox News Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News_Sunday)* that the DOJ would not be opening a criminal investigation into Ross for his killing of Good,[^cite_note-KashiwagiMST-100][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] claiming it unnecessary as "everybody can watch the videos and see that \[Ross] got attacked with a car that was trying to take his life" and added: "\[The Department of Justice doesn't] just go out and investigate every time an officer is forced to defend himself against somebody putting his life in danger. We never do."[^cite_note-105]

On March 24, 2026, the state of Minnesota, Hennepin County, and the Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension filed suit against the Department of Justice and the Department of Homeland Security, alleging that the defendants were withholding investigative evidence about Good's killing, [Alex Pretti's killing](82203651.md), and the [shooting of Julio Sosa-Celis](82105033.md#Shooting_of_Julio_Cesar_Sosa-Celis), to shield the federal officers involved.[^cite_note-106] On May 6, 2026, federal agencies turned over evidence related to the shooting of Renée Good to a federal judge, following an April court order in a separate case involving Roberto Carlos Munoz-Guatemala. State officials noted that this did not provide Minnesota access to the evidence and accused federal agencies of continuing to withhold it from state investigators.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] On July 13, 2026, Minnesota prosecutors announced that they had obtained the hard drives of previously withheld evidence and that Good's car was in the possession of state and local investigators.[^cite_note-109] The hard drives contain "federal evidence like body camera footage and statements from immigration enforcement agents about the killings" of Good and Pretti.[^cite_note-110]

## Aftermath



Vigil in Minneapolis for Renée Good

[Minneapolis Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Public_Schools) (MPS) canceled class for the remainder of the week, citing safety concerns after the shooting and ICE's use of [pepper spray](157302.md) and [pepper balls](1448999.md) against students (allegedly for interfering with an arrest) at [Roosevelt High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_High_School_\(Minnesota\)).[^cite_note-Johnson-2026a-111][^cite_note-Shockman-2026-112] Faculty at the high school said that armed ICE officers had handcuffed two members of the staff.[^cite_note-Shockman-2026-112] On January 9, MPS announced it would offer the option of [remote learning](8997.md) until February 12.[^cite_note-Santana-2026a-113]

On January 10, three Minnesota congressional Democrats, [Angie Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angie_Craig), [Kelly Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Morrison), and [Ilhan Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar), were blocked from [overseeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_oversight) an ICE facility near Minneapolis. According to attorneys for congressional Democrats, DHS secretary [Kristi Noem](27663933.md) had on January 8 reinstated a seven-day waiting period for congressional oversight of immigration detention facilities, restoring a policy which had been ordered blocked for review by a judge in December. The attorneys asked the judge for an emergency hearing on whether the restored policy violated her order.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

An online fundraiser campaign started by Good's family was closed on January 9 after reaching more than \$1.5 million in donations. The family stated that the money would be placed in a trust fund for the family.[^cite_note-Stevenson-2026-116] In response, a fundraising campaign in support of the shooter was established by Clyde Emmons of [Mount Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Forest,_Michigan), Michigan. As of January 12, this campaign raised \$375,000.[^cite_note-117] An online account known for making antisemitic posts and proliferating the [white genocide conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_genocide_conspiracy_theory) started another online fundraiser for Ross on [GiveSendGo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GiveSendGo), raising about \$300,000. The fundraiser initially said the shooting was "the direct result of anti-American traitors like Minneapolis Mayor [Jacob Frey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Frey) (who is Jewish) fanning the flames of resistance". The parenthetical comment on Frey's religion was later removed from the fundraiser.[^cite_note-LapinJTA-118]

The killing brought renewed attention to other incidents of [killings by immigration agents](82067135.md), including an off-duty ICE officer's fatal shooting of [Keith Porter Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Porter_Jr.) a week earlier.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-Alessandrini-2026-120] In the days following the attack, the personal information of thousands of alleged ICE agents [was leaked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Homeland_Security_employee_data_leak); DHS assistant secretary Tricia McLaughlin called the act "the disgusting [doxxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doxxing) of our officers" in a statement to *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)*.[^cite_note-121]

### Dispute whether the vehicle struck Ross

DHS secretary Kristi Noem claimed shortly after the shooting that the ICE agent who fired the shots had been treated at a hospital having been "hit by the vehicle" and then released.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-Beitsch-2026-124] Reports published by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* said there were "no visible sign in the videos" of injuries to ICE officers in the incident[^cite_note-Luscombe-2026b-13] and the officer "was not in the pathway of her car when he began firing".[^cite_note-125] [CNN](62028.md) said that one video from further down the street "seemed to show" Good's SUV "making contact" with him, but that another video from immediately behind Good's SUV showed Ross pointing his pistol at Good while moving outwards from the front of her SUV and to the driver's side.[^cite_note-126] An analysis by *The New York Times* concluded that while the grainy, low-resolution video from further down the street appeared to show Ross being struck by Good's SUV, the video from behind Good's SUV showed that Ross was not run over and instead crossed to the left of the SUV, opened fire while its wheels were pointed to the right away from him, and continued shooting as she drove past.[^cite_note-Lum-2026b-6] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* said Ross moved out of the way as he fired his first shot, then fired twice from the side of the vehicle, and that the videos do not "clearly show whether the agent is struck", a conclusion also reached by [BBC News](1139893.md) and [Bellingcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingcat).[^cite_note-Davis-2026-74][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-Venkatraman-2026-128]

On the afternoon of January 9, a new video became available, apparently from Ross's cellphone.[^cite_note-Mangan-2026-64] In their analysis of Ross's video, *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* said, "the front corner of the car clips him, turning him sideways",[^cite_note-Ensor-2026-58] and *[The Telegraph](23797577.md)* said he was "almost knocked off his feet as Good lurches the SUV towards him".[^cite_note-129] *[The Straits Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times)*, *The New York Times,* *The Washington Post*, CNN, and the [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported that Ross's video did not record any contact between him and Good's SUV because his camera jerked up toward the sky.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-Santana-2026b-56][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-Devine-2026-54] Noem alleged that Ross was struck and injured by the vehicle. US officials claimed Ross "suffered internal bleeding" to his torso, but did not provide the extent of the bleeding or other details.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] *The Star Tribune* noted that "there is no documentation in the incident reports that \[Ross] required any medical attention on the scene after killing Good."[^cite_note-DayStarTribune-84]

### Protests

Main article: [2026 U.S. immigration enforcement protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_U.S._immigration_enforcement_protests)

Anti-ICE protest in [Manhattan](45470.md) the day after Good was killed

At a press conference, Governor Walz announced he had begun preparing the [Minnesota National Guard](4562088.md).[^cite_note-Date-2026-137] By the evening, the crowd at the location where Good was killed grew to thousands, including members of the [Minneapolis City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_City_Council).[^cite_note-Mitchell-2026-10] Protesters also gathered in many other cities across the country.[^cite_note-161]



"ICE Out" buttons worn by celebrities during the 2026 awards season in protest of [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement)



"Be Good" buttons distributed as part of the #BeGood campaign honoring [Renee Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Renee_Nicole_Good) and protesting ICE

On January 8, a crowd of people barricaded the street in Minneapolis where Good was killed to hold a vigil in her memory.[^cite_note-162] City workers removed the barricades shortly after but preserved a memorial established.[^cite_note-Turtinen-2026-163] More protests occurred elsewhere, including in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York);[^cite_note-Mroziak-2026-164] [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina);[^cite_note-165] [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina);[^cite_note-Daniely-2026-145] [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri);[^cite_note-Lowe-2026-166] and Los Angeles, California.[^cite_note-Pulliam-2026-167] Over 1,000 protests under the banner "ICE Out For Good" were planned for the following weekend, with an estimated tens of thousands marching in Minneapolis on January 10.[^cite_note-Hickman-2026-168] By January 11, city officials said 30 people were arrested at weekend protests and one police officer sustained minor injuries.[^cite_note-169]

On January 23, an ["ICE Out of Minnesota" citywide protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Minnesota_ICE_protests) (originally imagined as a general strike) against ICE presence and violence was held.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

## Response

### Congressional

[House](19468510.md) minority leader [Hakeem Jeffries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakeem_Jeffries) demanded a criminal investigation. Other Democrats in Congress, such as [Senate](24909346.md) minority leader [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer), also called for investigations,[^cite_note-The_Guardian-2026-9] and [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna) said the agent "needs to be arrested, and he needs to be prosecuted. He needs to be put on trial".[^cite_note-FortinskyHill-178] [Ken Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Martin), chair of the [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee), accused the FBI of a cover-up when it was announced that the [Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Bureau_of_Criminal_Apprehension) had their access to the investigation revoked.[^cite_note-179] Democratic representative [Robin Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Kelly) (Illinois) introduced [articles of impeachment](70046548.md) for DHS Secretary Kristi Noem on January 14, 2026, citing obstruction of [congressional oversight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_oversight) of ICE facilities, violations of public trust through warrantless arrests without [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process), and of [self-dealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dealing) for personal benefit.[^cite_note-180] It is unexpected to clear the Republican-controlled House, though it has accrued more than 70 signatures as of January 15. DHS representatives have dismissed the articles of impeachment as "silly", and one anonymous House Democrat said: "There's a frustration with all these impeachments ... It's all performative bullshit."[^cite_note-181]

There have been calls to [abolish ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE) from various political candidates and representatives to rein in the federal law enforcement agency amid heightened scrutiny;[^cite_note-182] however, some political strategists have warned that this could be a repeat of the political backlash that [progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) previously faced with the "[defund the police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defund_the_police)" movement.[^cite_note-183] On January 15, 2026, Democratic representative [Shri Thanedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Thanedar) of Michigan introduced the Abolish ICE Act, which would end federal funding for ICE and abolish the agency 90 days after enactment; he accused it of being "beyond reform".[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] A [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov)/*[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* poll taken between January 9–12, 2026, indicated that 46% of Americans support abolishing ICE, while 43% oppose it,[^cite_note-186] and the issue is expected to be a centerpiece of the [2026 United States elections](70357296.md).[^cite_note-187]

On February 3, 2026, Renée Good's brothers, Luke and Brent Ganger, spoke in a public forum at the US Capitol at the invitation of Congressional Democrats.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]

### Executive agencies



DHS's [Tricia McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_McLaughlin_\(spokeswoman\)) (photographed Jan 2025) accused Good of [domestic terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism).

DHS spokesperson [Tricia McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_McLaughlin_\(government_official\)) said that an ICE officer "fearing for his life" had shot Good in [self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense) after she attempted to run them over in what McLaughlin described as "an act of [domestic terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism)".[^cite_note-O'Donovan-2026-45] After the release of camera phone footage taken by Ross, McLaughlin said, "The American people can watch this video with their own eyes and ears and judge for themselves."[^cite_note-Vargas-2026-191][^cite_note-Mangan-2026-64][^cite_note-Penzenstadler-192]

DHS secretary Kristi Noem said that Good "attacked \[ICE] and those surrounding them and attempted to run them over and ram them with her vehicle. An officer of ours acted quickly and defensively shot to protect himself and the people around him."[^cite_note-193] The next day, Noem announced [Operation Salvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Salvo), which would increase ICE presence in New York City.[^cite_note-194] Three days later, on January 11, journalist [Jake Tapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Tapper) challenged Noem on her claim that Good had attempted to run over ICE officers, saying: "That's not what happened. We all saw what happened." Noem responded, "It absolutely is what happened."[^cite_note-State_of_the_Union-2026-195]

Leaders in the [Civil Rights Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Civil_Rights_Division) of the Justice Department declared that they would not investigate the ICE agent involved in this incident for any [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law_of_the_United_States) violations, even though it is customary for them to take the lead in such cases. Deputy Attorney General [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) said publicly that the use of deadly force can sometimes be justified.[^cite_note-196] On January 13, twelve or more federal prosecutors in Minneapolis and Washington, including former acting US attorney [Joseph H. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_H._Thompson_\(attorney\)) of the [District of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Minnesota) and members of the [Civil Rights Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Division), resigned their positions over the Department of Justice's actions or lack thereof in investigating this case.[^cite_note-resign-99][^cite_note-Civil_Rights_Division_resign-98]

### ICE agents

Noah Hurowitz of *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* said that Federal agents have repeatedly "invoked Good's death" and "referred to civilians learning their lesson" in multiple encounters in Minneapolis.[^cite_note-197] A legal observer arrested by ICE on January 11 reported that "the ICE agent who had pepper sprayed into the vents of my car said 'you guys gotta stop obstructing us, that's why that lesbian bitch is dead.' "[^cite_note-MoiniMPR-198][^cite_note-199][^cite_note-GrantTNR-200]

The exclamation "fucking bitch" on footage of the shooting brought renewed attention to an instance from the previous October of an immigration agent shooting a woman and telling her to "Do something, bitch."[^cite_note-GrantTNR-200]

### Local officials

Speaking at a press conference on the afternoon of the killing, Minnesota governor [Tim Walz](2216593.md) called it "the consequences of governance designed to generate fear, headlines and conflict" and added "we do not need any further help from the federal government".[^cite_note-201] Walz announced that he was ordering the state's [National Guard](37988.md) to adopt a state of preparedness.[^cite_note-202] In response to DHS statements that Good was [weaponizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_as_a_weapon) her SUV to run over an ICE agent, Minneapolis mayor Jacob Frey noted that the video footage of the incident did not appear to suggest this, stating: "Having seen the video myself, I want to tell everybody directly that is bullshit" and "To ICE, get the fuck out of Minneapolis."[^cite_note-Burke-2026-71] The [Minneapolis Police Department](13087466.md) activated [mutual aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_aid_\(emergency_services\)) from surrounding jurisdictions and bolstered local law enforcement resources as a precautionary response to any potential civil unrest.[^cite_note-203] Walz proclaimed January 9, 2026, to be "Renee Good Day".[^cite_note-Nodrum-2026-204] Minneapolis police chief [Brian O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_O'Hara) criticized the actions of ICE agents and called Good's killing "predictable and preventable".[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206]

*[Politico](9172097.md)* reported that the killing kicked off a series of efforts by state legislators nationwide to limit cooperation with and increase oversight of ICE.[^cite_note-207] On January 12, the Monday following the shooting, Minnesota attorney general [Keith Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Ellison), alongside the cities of [Minneapolis](6097240.md) and St. Paul, announced a lawsuit against the DHS, seeking to end the deployment of ICE agents to the state.[^cite_note-Chidi-2026-208][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

## Federal lawsuit

On October 1, 2026, ten months after the killing, relatives of Good filed two federal lawsuits in Minnesota federal court. The lawsuit accused ICE agent Jonathan Ross of creating a dangerous situation by placing himself in front of Good's vehicle, as well as using excessive and unjustified force. The first lawsuit was filed under the [Federal Tort Claims Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Tort_Claims_Act), allowing plaintiffs to seek damages for negligent deaths. In a statement, Good's lawyers said "the day of accountability has arrived for the United States' rogue agencies, [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) and [Customs and Border Protection](757040.md), and the unjustified and excessive use of force -- let alone deadly force -- used on people exercising their rights under the Constitution's [Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights)"[^cite_note-238]

White House senior advisor [Stephen Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Miller), former DHS Secretary [Kristi Noem](27663933.md), Border Czar [Tom Homan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Homan), and ICE agent Jonathan Ross were named as defendants in the second lawsuit.[^cite_note-239]

### Conduct of federal agents and liability

Speaking to the [Associated Press](18935732.md) (AP), criminology professor Geoff Alpert questioned whether there was any law enforcement training which would permit the use of a firearm in one hand while filming with the other. Law professor John P. Gross said that Ross "casually filming" the encounter demonstrated that Good was not perceived as a threat.[^cite_note-Santana-2026b-56] *Washington Post* columnist [Monica Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Hesse) wrote that Good backed up her car to conduct a multipoint turn on the icy street after waving other motorists ahead to allow space for her to pull onto the street.[^cite_note-240]

Legal experts and former ICE officials interviewed by *[Politico](9172097.md)* and *[The New York Times](30680.md)* said that the Trump administration's rush to assign blame, mount partisan attacks, and deny responsibility damaged public trust in ICE and the credibility of any federal investigation.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-2026-01-10_NYT-242] Legal analyst [Ian Millhiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Millhiser) found it unlikely that the federal government would charge the shooter with a crime, but said it may be possible for state prosecutors to lay homicide charges against him. The previous year, the Supreme Court ruled in *[Martin v. United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_v._United_States)* that federal officers are protected from prosecution only to the extent that they acted in a "necessary and proper" fashion in the discharge of their official duties.[^cite_note-Millhiser-2026-243] Similarly, law professor Rachel Moran listed ICE actions in the aftermath of the shooting, such as the use of pepper spray, as possible grounds for assault charges.[^cite_note-DewanNYT-244]

It was not immediately clear whether Good was legally obligated to comply with ICE's orders. Legal analysts interviewed by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* said the issue was multifactorial, encompassing the question of whether ICE was engaged in lawful activity, and also whether Good was blocking their cars.[^cite_note-DewanNYT-244]

### Government policy and statements

DHS policy directives instruct its officers to "avoid intentionally and unreasonably placing themselves in positions in which they have no alternative to using [deadly force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadly_force)". ICE directs its officers to use deadly force "only when an officer has [probable cause](146751.md) that a detainee poses an imminent danger of death or serious physical injury".[^cite_note-Chang-2026-245] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* noted that previous DHS [self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense) assertions in cases of seemingly excessive force "have been repeatedly debunked", with evidence in federal court showing that "[Border Patrol](567453.md) agents involved in such confrontations" [in Chicago the previous year](81030411.md) "lied under oath and exaggerated the threat from protesters in order to justify their aggression".[^cite_note-The_Economist-2026-246] According to *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)*, the incident historically would have been investigated by the DHS Office for Civil Rights and Civil Liberties "to review policies, training, and oversight procedures to try to prevent anything like it from happening again", but the Office was disabled early the previous year along with two other DHS oversight offices.[^cite_note-247] [Radley Balko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radley_Balko) wrote that the administration's refusal to investigate themselves or support other investigations "is the very definition of a cover-up. It's just being done in plain sight."[^cite_note-248]

### Militarization of border police agencies

*The* *New York Times* said Ross's career trajectory, which took him from the [US war campaign in Iraq](5043324.md), to border patrol in Texas, and then immigration enforcement in Minnesota, was emblematic of a broader effort by the federal government to [militarize its police apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarization_of_police) at the border in the decades after the September 11 attacks.[^cite_note-Agent_Who_Shot_Renee_Good_Was_Trained_to_Track_and_Apprehend_Fugitives-35] [David Wallace-Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wallace-Wells) contextualized the shooting as an instance of [imperial boomerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_boomerang), writing: "Here we are, with an Iraq veteran in tactical gear, surrounded by comrades swarming a car partially blocking his way, firing point-blank at its driver. In the immediate aftermath, sympathetic [nativists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_in_United_States_politics) justified the shooting by describing a Minneapolis taken over by Somali refugees, but also by pointing to the victim's divorce and sexuality, the [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice) curriculum at her child's elementary school and the obstinateness of liberal white women."[^cite_note-WallaceWellsNYT-249] [Michelle Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Goldberg) wrote, "It's as if the right is speedrunning the [Martin Niemöller poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_They_Came) that begins, 'First they came for the Communists.' ICE's invasion of Minneapolis started with the demonization of Somali immigrants. It took only weeks for conservative demagogues to direct their venom toward the middle-class women of the Resistance." Goldberg also wrote that what made Good's killing "such a shock is that we're not used to seeing law enforcement violence against middle-class white mothers".[^cite_note-250]
