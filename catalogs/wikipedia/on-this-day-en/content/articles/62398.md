The **Belmont Stakes** is an American [Grade I stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_stakes_race) [race](67745.md) for three-year-old [Thoroughbreds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbreds) run at [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park) in [Elmont, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmont,_New_York). It is run over the worldwide classic distance of 1+1⁄2 miles (12 furlongs; 2,414 metres). [Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_\(horseracing\)) and [geldings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelding) carry a weight of 126 pounds (57 kg); [fillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filly_\(horseracing\)) carry 121 pounds (55 kg). The race, nicknamed "The Test of the Champion",[^cite_note-1] "The Test of Champions"[^cite_note-2] and "The Run for the Carnations", is the traditional third and final leg of the [Triple Crown](19116721.md) and will be the second race in the [Thoroughbred Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_Championship_Series?action=edit\&redlink=1). It is usually held on the first or second Saturday in June, five weeks after the [Kentucky Derby](17135.md) and three weeks after the [Preakness Stakes](62370.md). The 1973 Belmont Stakes and Triple Crown winner [Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_\(horse\)) holds the track record (which is also a world record on dirt) of 2\:24.

The race covers one full lap of Belmont Park, known as "The Championship Track" because nearly every major American champion in racing history has competed on the racetrack. Belmont Park, with its large, wide, sweeping turns and long homestretch, is considered one of the fairest racetracks in America.[^cite_note-3] Despite the distance, the race tends to favor horses with tactical speed: relatively few winners close from far behind the early leaders.[^cite_note-4]

The attendance at the Belmont Stakes is among the [American thoroughbred racing top-attended events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_thoroughbred_racing_top_attended_events). The 2004 Belmont Stakes drew a television audience of 21.9 million viewers, and had the highest household viewing rate since 1977 when [Seattle Slew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Slew) won the Triple Crown.[^cite_note-5]

The [158th](80257691.md) Belmont Stakes was run on Saturday, June 6, 2026, at [Saratoga Race Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratoga_Race_Course) and was won by [Golden Tempo](83090873.md).

## History

### 1867–1929: Early years



[August Belmont Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Belmont_Trophy), presented annually to the winning owner since 1926.

The first Belmont Stakes was held at [Jerome Park Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Park_Racetrack) in [the Bronx](3338.md), built in 1866 by stock market speculator [Leonard Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Jerome) (1817–1891) and financed by [August Belmont Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Belmont_Sr.) (1816–1890), for whom the race was named. The first race in 1867 saw the [filly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filly) [Ruthless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthless_\(horse\)) win, while the following year was won by [General Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Duke_\(horse\)).[^cite_note-PastWinners-6] The first post parade in the [United States](3434750.md) was at the 14th Belmont, in 1880. The race continued to be held at Jerome Park until 1890, when it was moved to the nearby facility, [Morris Park Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park_Racecourse).[^cite_note-Belmont_Stakes_Records-7] The 1895 race was almost not held because of new laws that banned bookmaking in New York: it was eventually rescheduled for November 2.[^cite_note-8] The race remained at Morris Park Racecourse until the May 1905 opening of the new [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park), 430-acre (1.7 km<sup>2</sup>) racetrack in Elmont, New York, on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), just outside the New York City borough of [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens).[^cite_note-Belmont_Stakes_Records-7] When anti-gambling legislation was passed in New York State, Belmont Racetrack was closed, and the race was cancelled in 1911 and 1912.[^cite_note-9]

The first winner of the [Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Triple_Crown_of_Thoroughbred_Racing) was [Sir Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Barton), in 1919, before the series was recognized as such.[^cite_note-10] In 1920, the Belmont was won by the great [Man o' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_o'_War), who won by 20 lengths, setting a new stakes and American record, while under a stout pull.[^cite_note-11]

Starting in 1926, the winner of the Belmont Stakes has been presented with [August Belmont Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Belmont_Trophy). The owner may keep the trophy for one year, and also receives a silver miniature for permanent use.[^cite_note-Belmont_Stakes_Records-7]

### 1930–2000: Evolution of the Triple Crown series

Main article: [Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing (United States)](19116721.md)



1940 winner Bimelech in the winner's circle

The term Triple Crown was first used when [Gallant Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallant_Fox) won the three races in 1930, but the term did not enter widespread use until 1935 when his son [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_\(horse\)) repeated the feat. Sir Barton was then honored retroactively.[^cite_note-12] Since 1931, the order of Triple Crown races has been the Kentucky Derby first, followed by the Preakness Stakes, and then the Belmont Stakes. Prior to 1931, the Preakness was run before the Derby eleven times. On May 12, 1917, and again on May 13, 1922, the Preakness and the Derby were run on the same day. On eleven occasions, the Belmont Stakes was run before the Preakness Stakes.[^cite_note-13] The date of each event is now set by the [Kentucky Derby](17135.md), which is always held on the first Saturday in May. The [Preakness Stakes](62370.md) is currently held two weeks later; and the Belmont Stakes is held three weeks after the Preakness (five weeks after the Derby). The earliest possible date for the Derby is May 1, and the latest is May 7; the earliest possible date for the Belmont is thus June 5, and the latest is June 11.[^cite_note-14] In 2020, due to the cancellation of the original dates due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the order changed with the Belmont first on June 20, the Kentucky Derby on September 5 and the Preakness on October 3—all with no spectators—before the Triple Crown races resumed their normal schedule in 2021.

In 1937, War Admiral became the fourth Triple Crown winner after winning the Belmont in a new track record time of 2\:28 3/5.[^cite_note-15] In the 1940s, four Triple Crown winners followed: [Whirlaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlaway) in 1941, [Count Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Fleet) in 1943, [Assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_\(horse\)) in 1946 and [Citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_\(horse\)) in 1948. Count Fleet won the race by a then-record margin of twenty-five lengths.[^cite_note-16] He also set a stakes record of 2\:28 1/5, a record tied by Citation. In 1957, the stakes record was smashed when [Gallant Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallant_Man) ran the Belmont in 2\:26 3/5 in a year when the Triple Crown series was split three ways.[^cite_note-17]

The Belmont Stakes race was held at [Aqueduct Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_Racetrack) from 1963 to 1967, while the track at Belmont was restored and renovated.

The largest crowd of the 20th century was in 1971 with over 80,000 people, supplemented by the city's [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinos) community, there to cheer on their new hero, [Cañonero II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1onero_II), the [Venezuelan](32374.md) colt who had won the [Kentucky Derby](17135.md) and [Preakness Stakes](62370.md) and was poised to win the U.S. [Triple Crown](19116721.md). However, due to a foot infection that had bothered the horse for several days, Cañonero II failed to win the Triple Crown when he struggled across the finish line in 4th place behind [Pass Catcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pass_Catcher), ridden by [Walter Blum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Blum). Despite this loss, Cañonero II was named the winner of the first [Eclipse Award for Outstanding Three-Year-Old Male Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_Award_for_Outstanding_Three-Year-Old_Male_Horse).[^cite_note-18]

On June 9, 1973, [Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_\(horse\)) won the Belmont Stakes by thirty-one lengths in a record time of 2\:24, becoming a Triple Crown champion, ending a 25-year gap between [Citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_\(horse\)), the Belmont and Triple Crown winner in 1948. Secretariat's record still stands as the fastest running of the Belmont Stakes and an American record for 1+1⁄2 miles on the dirt.[^cite_note-19] In 1977, [Seattle Slew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Slew) became the first horse to win the Triple Crown while undefeated. Affirmed was the last winner of the Triple Crown in the 20th century, taking the Belmont Stakes in 2\:26 4/5 on June 10, 1978. Ridden by eighteen-year-old Steve Cauthen, Affirmed defeated rival Alydar with Jorge Velásquez in the saddle. At the time the race was the third-slowest start and the third-fastest finish with the quarter in 25, the half in 50, 3/4 in 1\:14, the mile in 1\:37 2/5.[^cite_note-20]

In 1988, Secretariat's son [Risen Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risen_Star) won the Belmont in 2\:26 2/5, then the second-fastest time in the history of the race. The next year, [Easy Goer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Goer) lowered the mark for second-fastest time to 2\:26. Easy Goer also holds a [Beyer Speed Figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyer_Speed_Figure) of 122 for the race, the best of any Triple Crown race since these ratings were first published in 1987.[^cite_note-21]

### 2001–present: Recent years



The crowd packs the facility when a Triple Crown is on the line

For three years in a row, horses came to the Belmont Stakes with a Triple Crown on the line only to fail. In 2002, Belmont Park hosted what was then the largest crowd in its history when 103,222 saw [War Emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Emblem) lose to longshot Sarava after stumbling at the start. In 2003, 101,864 watched [Funny Cide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funny_Cide) finish third behind [Empire Maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Maker). In 2004, the attendance record was shattered when 120,139 people saw [Smarty Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smarty_Jones) upset by [Birdstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdstone).[^cite_note-22]

In 2007, [Rags to Riches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rags_to_Riches_\(horse\)) became the first filly to win the race since Tanya in 1905. Three more failed Triple Crown bids followed: in 2008, [Big Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brown_\(horse\)) lost to [Da' Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da'_Tara); in 2012, [I'll Have Another](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'll_Have_Another) was withdrawn due to injury; and in 2014, [California Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Chrome) was beaten by [Tonalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonalist). This fueled debate about whether the series needed to be changed, for example by lengthening the period between races.[^cite_note-23]

[American Pharoah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Pharoah) won the 2015 race, becoming the 12th horse in history to win the Triple Crown and the first in 37 years. The crowd that year was limited for the first time, to 90,000.[^cite_note-24] His time of 2\:26.65 was the sixth-fastest in Belmont Stakes history, and the second-fastest time for a Triple Crown winner.[^cite_note-25] In 2018, Justify became the 13th Triple Crown winner and only the second horse to do so while undefeated.[^cite_note-26]

[Justify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justify_\(horse\)) won the [150th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Belmont_Stakes) Belmont Stakes in 2018 to become the 13th Triple Crown champion.

The 152nd running of the Belmont Stakes took place without in-person fans on June 20, 2020,[^cite_note-27] delayed from June 6 as a result of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). Leading to the announcement, [NYRA](673662.md) considered various dates after evaluating the spring-summer meeting at Belmont Park and after the other three majors set their respective dates: the Kentucky Derby moving to September 5, the Preakness Stakes moving to October 3, and the Breeders' Cup Classic slated for November 7. Governor [Andrew Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo) of New York announced on May 16 that horse racing tracks statewide, including Belmont Park, would be permitted to resume races without in-person fans on June 1.[^cite_note-28] The 2020 contest also marked the first time in history that the Belmont Stakes served as the Triple Crown's opening leg, with its running length shortened accordingly to 1+1⁄8 miles (9 furlongs),[^cite_note-29] its shortest distance since 1894.[^cite_note-30]

#### Temporary move to Saratoga

The [New York Racing Association](673662.md) held both the [2024 Belmont Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Belmont_Stakes) and the [2025 Belmont Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Belmont_Stakes) at [Saratoga Race Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratoga_Race_Course) because of ongoing construction and renovations at Belmont Park.[^cite_note-Bonesteel-31] The purse of the race was increased to US\$2 million, with the winner receiving \$1,200,000. It was run at a shorter distance of 1+1⁄4 miles (2.0 km) instead of the usual 1+1⁄2 miles (2.4 km) due to the smaller size of the Saratoga dirt track.[^cite_note-Bonesteel-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

On June 13, 2025, it was announced that the 2026 Belmont Stakes would also be held at Saratoga Race Course.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-amny_com-35]

The 2026 Belmont Stakes was the final running held at Saratoga Race Course before the race will return to its rebuilt home at Belmont Park in 2027.[^cite_note-36]

#### Introduction of Thoroughbred Championship Series

On August 5, 2026, the NYRA and [Churchill Downs Incorporated](3648388.md) announced the [Thoroughbred Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_Championship_Series?action=edit\&redlink=1) (TCS), a six-race series for three-year-old thoroughbreds that is set to begin in 2027. The Belmont Stakes was designated as the second race of the new series, skipping over the Preakness Stakes in the process.[^cite_note-37] The TCS has called the future of the Triple Crown into jeopardy as result.[^cite_note-38]

## Locations

Over the years the race has been held at five racetracks in the state of New York:

- 1867–1889: [Jerome Park Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Park_Racetrack) (23 times)

- 1890–1904: [Morris Park Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park_Racecourse) (15 times)

- 1905–1910: 1st [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park) (6 times)

- 1911–1912: Not held due to [Hart-Agnew Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart-Agnew_Law)

- 1913–1962: 1st Belmont Park (56 times)

- 1963–1967: [Aqueduct Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_Racetrack) (5 times)

- 1968–2023: 2nd Belmont Park (56 times)

- 2024–2026: [Saratoga Race Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratoga_Race_Course) (3 times)

The purse for the first running in 1867 was \$1,500 added,[^cite_note-39] meaning the purse was supplemented by nomination and entry fees. This made the total purse \$2,500, with the winner receiving \$1,850. The purse increased sharply in the Roaring Twenties, from Man O'War's earnings of \$7,950 in 1920 to Gallant Fox's take of \$66,040 in 1930. Purses declined as a result of the Great Depression, with War Admiral earning only \$28,020 in 1937, then began to recover. Throughout the sixties and early seventies, the value to the winner was roughly \$100,000, depending on the added money generated by entry fees (larger fields thus leading to higher prize money). The purse was repeatedly raised in the eighties and nineties, reaching \$500,000 added, with the winner receiving roughly \$400,000.[^cite_note-PastWinners-6] In 1998, the purse was changed to \$1,000,000 guaranteed, with the winner receiving \$600,000. In 2014, the purse was raised to \$1,500,000.[^cite_note-40]

With one exception, the race has been run at a level weight of 126 pounds (with a 5-pound allowance for fillies) since 1900. The 126 pounds comes from the [English Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Classic_Races), where the standard weight is 9 stone, with one stone equaling 14 pounds. In 1913, the Belmont was run as a handicap with the winner carrying only 109 pounds compared to the runner-up carrying 126 pounds. Races run prior to 1900 had varied weight conditions.[^cite_note-PastWinners-6]

The first post parade in the United States was at the 14th Belmont, in 1880. Before 1921, the race was run in the clockwise tradition of English racing. Since then, the race has been run in the American, or counter-clockwise, direction.

## Traditions



The winner's blanket, made of white carnations

The Belmont Stakes is traditionally called "The Test of the Champion" because it is by far the longest of the Triple Crown races (1.5 miles–a full lap around the enormous Belmont main track). It is also one of the longest first-class races on dirt in the United States. Most three-year-olds are unaccustomed to the distance, and lack the experience, if not the [stamina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endurance), to maintain a winning speed for so long. In a long race such as the Belmont, positioning of the horse and the timing of the move to chase for the lead can be critical.

Unlike the Kentucky Derby or Preakness Stakes, a notable recording artist does not sing the [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star_Spangled_Banner) during the pre-race ceremony.

It is also known as "The Run for the Carnations" because the winning horse is draped with a blanket of [white carnations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianthus_caryophyllus) after the race, in similar fashion to the blanket of [roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose) and [black-eyed Susans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudbeckia) for the Derby and Preakness, respectively.

### Trophy

The winning owner is ceremonially presented with the silver winner's [trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Belmont_Trophy), designed by [Paulding Farnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulding_Farnham) for [Tiffany and Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_and_Co.) It was first presented to [August Belmont Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Belmont_Jr.) in 1896 and donated by the Belmont family for annual presentation in 1926.

### Official song

Despite the fact that the Belmont Stakes is the oldest of the Triple Crown races, its traditions have been more subject to change. Until 1996, the post parade song was "[The Sidewalks of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sidewalks_of_New_York)". From 1997 to 2009, the song was changed to a recording by [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra) of the "[Theme from *New York, New York*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_from_New_York,_New_York)" in an attempt to appeal to younger fans.[^cite_note-41] In 2010, the song was changed to [Jay-Z](162870.md)'s "[Empire State of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_of_Mind)" sung by [Jasmine V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_V)[^cite_note-42] before reverting to "Theme from *New York, New York*" from 2011[^cite_note-43] through the present. This tradition is similar to the singing of the state song at the post parades of the first two Triple Crown races: "[My Old Kentucky Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Old_Kentucky_Home)" at the Kentucky Derby and "[Maryland, My Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland,_My_Maryland)" at the Preakness Stakes.[^cite_note-Belmont_Stakes_Records-7] The change of song gave rise to "the myth of Mamie O'Rourke", a reference to a character in the lyrics of "The Sidewalks of New York." Before [American Pharoah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Pharoah) won the Triple Crown in 2015, some claimed that changing the official Belmont song "cursed" the Triple Crown and was why no horse had won since [Affirmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmed) in 1978. Others note that there was no Triple Crown winner between 1979 and 1996, even though "Sidewalks" was still played.[^cite_note-44]

### Official drink

> **Belmont Breeze** *Single Serving* Ingredients [^cite_note-45]:
>
> - 1 1/2 oz. bourbon whiskey
>
> - 2 oz. lemonade
>
> - 1 oz. pomegranate juice
>
> - Lemon wedge, orange zest, or cherry (for garnish)

Along with the change of song in 1997, the official drink was also changed, from the "White Carnation" to the "Belmont Breeze."[^cite_note-46] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reviewed both cocktails unfavorably, calling the Belmont Breeze "a significant improvement over the nigh undrinkable White Carnation" despite the fact that it "tastes like a refined trashcan punch".[^cite_note-47]

In 2011, the Belmont Breeze was again changed to the current official drink known as the "Belmont Jewel."

While the origin of the white carnation as the official flower of the Belmont Stakes is unknown, traditionally, pure white carnations stand for love and luck. It takes approximately 700 "select" carnations imported from [Colombia](5222.md) to create the 40-pound blanket draped over the winner of the Belmont Stakes. The NYRA has long used The [Pennock Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennock_Company?action=edit\&redlink=1), a wholesale florist based in [Philadelphia](50585.md) to import the carnations used for the mantle.[^cite_note-48]

## Trivia

- [James G. Rowe Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_G._Rowe_Sr.) and [George M. Odom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Odom) have each won the Belmont Stakes as a jockey and a trainer.

- On 5 June 1993, thoroughbred racing's all-time leading female jockey, [Julie Krone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Krone), became the first woman to win a Triple Crown race when she rode to victory in the Belmont Stakes aboard [Colonial Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Affair).

- In 1984, [Sarah Lundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_A._Lundy) became the first female trainer to saddle a horse in the Belmont Stakes, sending out Minstrel Star, who finished eleventh.

- The 2004 race had the biggest attendance in the park's history with 120,139.

- [Sarava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarava), at odds of 70–1, upset [War Emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Emblem)'s bid for the Triple Crown.

- Braulio Baeza has the distinction of winning three Belmont Stakes over three different surfaces. He won in his Belmont Stakes debut on 65 to 1 long-shot Sherluck in 1961 at the old Belmont Park, won in 1963 on Chateaugay when the race was run at Aqueduct, and won in 1969 on Arts and Letters at the new Belmont Park.

- Prior to the 2016 running, [bay horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_\(horse\)) had the most victories with 56. [Chestnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut_\(coat\)) were close behind with 54 wins, followed by 33 dark bay/browns. Only three gray/[roan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roan_\(horse\)) horses had won

- Fifteen Belmont Stakes winners have sired at least one Belmont winner. Leading this list is [Man o' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_o'_War), who sired three subsequent winners — [American Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Flag_\(horse\)), [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_\(horse\)) and Triple Crown winner [War Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Admiral).

- Twenty-three horses missed their chance at a Triple Crown by not winning the Belmont. Eight of these finished second: Pensive (1944), Tim Tam (1958), Forward Pass (1968), Majestic Prince (1969), Sunday Silence (1989), Silver Charm (1997), Real Quiet (1998), and Smarty Jones (2004). Five finished third: Northern Dancer (1964), Spectacular Bid (1979), Pleasant Colony (1981), Charismatic (1999), and Funny Cide (2003). Four finished fourth: Kauai King (1966), Canonero II (1971), Alysheba (1987), and California Chrome (2014). Carry Back (1961) finished seventh, War Emblem (2002) finished eighth and Big Brown (2008) did not finish. Finally, three Derby/Preakness winners did not race in the Belmont: Burgoo King (1932), Bold Venture (1936) and I'll Have Another (2012), though I'll Have Another was injured and was scratched the day before his Belmont Stakes in 2012.

## Fillies in the Belmont

Only 24 [fillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filly) have run in the Belmont; three of which have won:

- [1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1867_Belmont_Stakes) – [Ruthless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthless_\(horse\))

- 1905 – [Tanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanya_\(horse\))

- [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Belmont_Stakes) – [Rags to Riches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rags_to_Riches_\(horse\))

This gives them a respectable 13% win rate when entered.[^cite_note-55] For context, three fillies have won the Kentucky Derby while six have won the Preakness Stakes. On average, fillies have won between 2% and 3% of the Triple Crown races, with similar numbers for [geldings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelding); while about 95% of these races have been won by colts. The last filly as of June 11, 2022, to run in the Belmont was in 2022 when Nest ran second (placed) behind the winner her stable mate [Mo Donegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Donegal).

## Sire lines

Winners of the Belmont Stakes can be connected to each other due to the practice of [arranging horse breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_breeding_theories) based on their previous success. All of the horses can be traced back to the three foundational sires, with [Godolphin Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godolphin_Arabian) the ancestor of 18 winners, [Byerley Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byerley_Turk) the ancestor of 14 winners, and [Darley Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darley_Arabian) the ancestor of 125 winners, including all winners since 2009.

The [Mr Prospector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_Prospector) direct sire line has produced 3 of the last 4 Belmont Stakes winners, most recently [Golden Tempo](83090873.md) in 2026.
