**Nebraska** ([/nəˈbræskə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e4/En-us-Nebraska.ogg/En-us-Nebraska.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e4/En-us-Nebraska.ogg/En-us-Nebraska.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*nə-BRASK-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-19] is a landlocked [state](18618239.md) in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) region of the United States. It borders [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) to the north; [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) to the west; [Colorado](5399.md) to the southwest; [Kansas](16716.md) to the south; and [Missouri](19571.md) to the southeast and [Iowa](26810748.md) to the east, both across the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River). Nebraska is the [16th-largest state by land area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area), with just over 77,347 square miles (200,330 km<sup>2</sup>). As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 1,961,504,[^cite_note-2020_Census_(State)-4] and was estimated to be 2,018,006 in 2025,[^cite_note-QF-20] it is the [38th-most populous state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) and the [eighth-least densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States_by_population_density). Nebraska's [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) is [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska), and its [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Nebraska) is [Omaha](46159.md), which is on the Missouri River.

Nebraska was admitted into the United States in 1867, two years after the end of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). The [Nebraska Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Legislature) is unlike any other American legislature in that it is [unicameral](204304.md), and its members are elected without any official reference to [political party affiliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties_in_the_United_States). Nebraska is one of only two states ([Maine](19977.md) being the other) that divide electoral college votes by district, and is not winner-take-all state-wide.[^cite_note-Electoral_College_Vote_Distribution-21]

Nebraska is composed of two major land regions: the [Dissected Till Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissected_Till_Plains) and the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains). The Dissected Till Plains region consists of gently rolling hills and contains the state's largest cities, Omaha and Lincoln. The Great Plains region, occupying most of western Nebraska, is characterized by treeless [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie). Eastern Nebraska has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) while western Nebraska is primarily [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate). The state has wide variations between winter and summer temperatures; the variations decrease in southern Nebraska. Violent [thunderstorms](70807.md) and [tornadoes](37530.md) occur primarily during spring and summer, and sometimes in autumn. The [Chinook wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_wind) tends to warm the state significantly in the winter and early spring.

Indigenous peoples, including [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_people), [Missouria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouria), [Ponca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponca), [Pawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawnee_people), [Otoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otoe_tribe), and various branches of the [Lakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakota_people) ([Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux)) tribes, lived in the region for thousands of years before [European discovery and exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery). The state is crossed by many historic trails, including that of the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition). The completion of the [Transcontinental Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad) through Nebraska and passage of the [Homestead Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts) led to rapid growth in the population of [American settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_pioneer) in the 1870s and 1880s and the development of a large agriculture sector for which the state is known to this day.

## Etymology

Nebraska's name is the result of [anglicization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation) of the archaic [Otoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwere_language) *Ñí Brásge* (contemporary Otoe: *Ñíbrahge*, pronounced [\[ɲĩbɾasꜜkɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)), or the [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha%E2%80%93Ponca_language) *Ní Btháska* (Omaha–Ponca pronunciation: [\[nĩbɫᶞasꜜka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)), meaning 'flat water', after the [Platte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platte_River) which flows through the state.[^cite_note-22]

## History

Main article: [History of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nebraska)



Nebraska in 1718, [Guillaume de L'Isle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_de_L'Isle) map, with the approximate area of the future state highlighted

### Early history

[Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) lived in the region of present-day Nebraska for thousands of years before [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas). The historic tribes in the state included the [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_people), [Missouria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouria), [Ponca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponca), [Pawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawnee_people), [Otoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otoe_tribe), and various branches of the [Lakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakota_people) ([Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux)), some of which migrated from eastern areas into the region. When European exploration, trade, and settlement began, both [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonial_empire) and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) sought to control the region. In the 1690s, Spain established trade connections with the [Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_people), whose territory then included western Nebraska. By 1703, France had developed a regular trade with native peoples along the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) in Nebraska, and by 1719 had signed treaties with several of these peoples. After war broke out between the two countries, Spain [dispatched an armed expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villasur_expedition) to Nebraska under Lieutenant General Pedro de Villasur in 1720. The party was attacked and destroyed near present-day [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Nebraska) by a large force of Pawnee and Otoe, both allied with the French. The massacre ended Spanish exploration of the area for the remainder of the 18th century.[^cite_note-Hanson-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] In 1762, during the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), France ceded the [Louisiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)) to Spain. This left Britain and Spain competing for dominance along the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River); by 1773, the British were trading with the native peoples of Nebraska. Spain dispatched two trading expeditions up the Missouri River in 1794 and 1795; the second, under James Mackay, established the first European settlement in Nebraska near the mouth of the Platte River. Later that year, Mackay's party built a trading post, dubbed Fort Carlos IV (Fort Charles), near present-day [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer,_Nebraska).[^cite_note-Hanson-23][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### American settlement and statehood

In 1819, the United States established [Fort Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Atkinson_\(Nebraska\)) as the first U.S. Army post west of the Missouri River, just east of present-day [Fort Calhoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Calhoun,_Nebraska). The army abandoned the fort in 1827 as migration moved farther west. European-American settlement was scarce until 1848 and the [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush). On May 30, 1854, the U.S. Congress created the [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Territory) and [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Territory) territories, divided by the [Parallel 40° North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_parallel_north), under the [Kansas–Nebraska Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas%E2%80%93Nebraska_Act).[^cite_note-Establishment-29] The Nebraska Territory included parts of the modern-day states of [Colorado](5399.md), [North Dakota](21651.md), [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota), [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming), and [Montana](19978.md).[^cite_note-30] The territorial capital of Nebraska was [Omaha](46159.md).[^cite_note-31]



[Homesteaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts) in central Nebraska in 1888

### Late 19th century

In the 1860s, after the U.S. government forced many of the [American Indian tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_\(Native_American\)) to cede their lands and settle on [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation), it opened large tracts of land to agricultural development by [European immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emigration) and [American settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_pioneer). Under the [Homestead Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Act), thousands of settlers migrated into Nebraska to claim free land granted by the federal government. Because so few trees grew on the [prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairies), many of the first farming settlers built their [homes of sod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sod_house), as had Native Americans such as the Omaha. The first wave of settlement gave the territory a sufficient population to apply for statehood.[^cite_note-32] Nebraska became the 37th state on March 1, 1867, and the capital was moved from Omaha to the center at Lancaster, later renamed [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska) after the recently assassinated President of the United States, [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln). The battle of [Massacre Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_Canyon), on August 5, 1873, was the last major battle between the [Pawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawnee_people) and the [Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

During the 1870s to the 1880s, Nebraska experienced a large growth in population. Several factors contributed to attracting new residents. The first was that the vast prairie land was perfect for cattle grazing. This helped settlers to learn the unfamiliar geography of the area. The second factor was the invention of several farming technologies. New agricultural innovations such as barbed wire, windmills, and the steel plow, combined with fair weather, enabled settlers to transform Nebraska into prime farming land. By the 1880s, Nebraska's population had soared to more than 450,000 people.[^cite_note-35] The [Arbor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbor_Day) holiday was founded in [Nebraska City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_City,_Nebraska) by territorial governor [J. Sterling Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Sterling_Morton). The [National Arbor Day Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Arbor_Day_Foundation) is still headquartered in Nebraska City, with some offices in Lincoln.

In the late 19th century, African Americans migrated from the South to Nebraska as part of the [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_\(African_American\)). Eventually, they moved primarily to [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha) which offered working-class jobs in [meat packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_packing_industry), the railroads and other industries. Omaha has a long history of [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Movement_in_Omaha,_Nebraska) activism. Blacks encountered discrimination from other Americans in Omaha and especially from recent European immigrants who were also competing for the same jobs as well.[^cite_note-36]

### 20th century

In 1912, African Americans founded the Omaha chapter of the [National Association for the Advancement of Colored People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Colored_People) to work for improved conditions in the city and state. During the [Omaha Race Riots in 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_race_riot_of_1919), many white rioters protested throughout Omaha in response to an incident in which a black man was accused of sexually assaulting a white woman. [The Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) also greatly affected the residents of Nebraska. From 1930 to 1936 the [Dust Bowl](59749.md), a period of dust storms caused by drought, destruction of topsoil by farming practices, and other factors affected Nebraska along with much of the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States). In 1934, voters passed an initiative to make Nebraska's legislature a unicameral body, and the first [unicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Legislature) session was held in 1937. [Kay Orr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_A._Orr) was elected Nebraska's first female governor on November 4, 1986.[^cite_note-37]

A map of Nebraska

The state is bordered by [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) to the north; [Iowa](26810748.md) to the east and [Missouri](19571.md) to the southeast, across the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River); [Kansas](16716.md) to the south; [Colorado](5399.md) to the southwest; and [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) to the west. It is the only triply [landlocked state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_state) in the United States.[^cite_note-38] The state has [93 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Nebraska) and is split between two [time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone), with the majority of the state observing [Central Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Time_Zone) and the [Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Panhandle) and surrounding counties observing [Mountain Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone).

Three rivers cross the state from west to east:

- The [Niobrara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobrara_River) flows through the northern part of the state, and

- The [Platte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platte_River), formed by the confluence of the [North Platte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Platte_River) and the [South Platte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Platte_River), runs through the state's central portion

- The [Republican River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_River) runs across the southern part.

&#x20;The first [Constitution of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nebraska) in 1866 described Nebraska's boundaries as follows:

> The State of Nebraska shall consist of all the territory included within the following boundaries, to-wit: Commencing at a point formed by the intersection of the western boundary of the State of Missouri, with the fortieth degree of north latitude; extending thence due west along said fortieth degree of north latitude, to a point formed by its intersection with the twenty-fifth degree of longitude west from Washington \[the Southern border]; thence north along said twenty-fifth degree of longitude, to a point formed by its intersection with the forty-first degree of north latitude; thence west along said forty-first degree of north latitude to a point formed by its intersection with the twenty-seventh degree of longitude west from Washington; thence north along said twenty-seventh degree of west longitude, to a point formed by its intersection with the forty-third degree of north latitude \[the Western border, which is the Panhandle]; thence east along said forty-third degree of north latitude to the Keya Paha river; thence down the middle of the channel of said river, with its meanderings, to its junction with the Niobrara River; thence down the middle of the channel of said Niobrara River, and following the meanderings thereof to its junction with the Missouri River \[the Northern border]; thence down the middle of the channel of said Missouri River, and following the meanderings thereof to the place of beginning \[the Eastern border, which is the Missouri River].[^cite_note-39]

The description given for the northern border is no longer accurate, since the [Keya Paha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keya_Paha_River) and the [Niobrara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobrara_River) no longer form the boundary of the state of Nebraska. Instead, Nebraska's northern border now extends east along the forty-third degree of north latitude until it meets the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) directly.

Nebraska is composed of two major land regions, the [Dissected Till Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissected_Till_Plains) and the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains). The easternmost portion of the state was scoured by [Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age) [glaciers](12463.md); the Dissected Till Plains were left after the glaciers retreated. The Dissected Till Plains is a region of gently rolling hills; [Omaha](46159.md) and [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska) are in this region. The Great Plains occupy most of western Nebraska, with the region consisting of several smaller, diverse land regions, including the [Sandhills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhills_\(Nebraska\)), the [Pine Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Ridge_\(region\)), the [Rainwater Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainwater_Basin), the [High Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Plains_\(United_States\)) and the [Wildcat Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildcat_Hills). [Panorama Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama_Point), at 5,424 feet (1,653 m), is Nebraska's highest point; though despite its name and elevation, it is a relatively low rise near the [Colorado](5399.md) and [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) borders. A former tourist slogan for the State of Nebraska was "Where the West Begins", but this has since been changed to "Honestly, it's not for everyone".[^cite_note-40] Locations given for the beginning of the "West" in Nebraska include the Missouri River, the intersection of 13th and O Streets in Lincoln (where it is marked by a red brick star), the [100th meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100th_meridian_west), and [Chimney Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimney_Rock_National_Historic_Site).

### Climate

Further information: [Climate change in Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Nebraska)



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Nebraska, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](42778673.md)



Winter at [Scotts Bluff National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotts_Bluff_National_Monument)

Two major climatic zones are represented in Nebraska. The eastern two-thirds of the state has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfa*), although the southwest of this region may be classed as a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*) using the −3 °C or 26.6 °F near the Kansas state line, analogous to the predominantly [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) climate of Kansas and [Oklahoma](22489.md). Western Nebraska, including the [Nebraska Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Panhandle) and adjacent areas bordering Colorado have a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) (Köppen *BSk*). The entire state experiences wide seasonal variations in both temperature and precipitation. Average temperatures are fairly uniform across Nebraska, with hot summers and generally cold winters. However, [chinook winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_wind) from the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) provide a temporary moderating effect on temperatures in the state's western portion during the winter.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Thus, average January maximum temperatures are highest at around 43 °F or 6.1 °C in southwestern [Dundy County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundy_County,_Nebraska), and lowest at about 30 °F or −1.1 °C around [South Sioux City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sioux_City,_Nebraska) in the northeast.

Average annual precipitation decreases east to west from about 31.5 inches (800 mm) in the southeast corner of the state to about 13.8 inches (350 mm) in the Panhandle. Humidity also decreases significantly from east to west. Snowfall across the state is fairly even, with most of Nebraska receiving between 25 and 35 inches (0.64 and 0.89 m) of snow each year.[^cite_note-43] Nebraska's highest-recorded temperature was 118 °F (48 °C) in [Minden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minden,_Nebraska) on July 24, 1936. The state's lowest-recorded temperature was −47 °F (−44 °C) in [Camp Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Clarke_Bridge_Site) on February 12, 1899.

Nebraska is located in [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley). [Thunderstorms](70807.md) are common during both the spring and the summer. Violent thunderstorms and [tornadoes](37530.md) happen primarily during those two seasons, although they also can occur occasionally during the autumn.

### Settlements

Map of state: mostly one to twenty-five people per square mile, with density increasing as one moves eastward

Population density in Nebraska

Eighty-nine percent of the cities in Nebraska have fewer than 3,000 people. Nebraska shares this characteristic with five other Midwestern states: [Kansas](16716.md), [Oklahoma](22489.md), [North Dakota](21651.md) and [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota), and [Iowa](26810748.md). Hundreds of towns have a population of fewer than 1,000. Regional population declines have forced many rural schools to consolidate.

Fifty-three of Nebraska's 93 counties reported declining populations between 1990 and 2000, ranging from a 0.06% loss ([Frontier County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_County,_Nebraska)) to a 17.04% loss ([Hitchcock County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchcock_County,_Nebraska)).



Omaha, Nebraska's largest city

More urbanized areas of the state have experienced substantial growth. In 2020, the city of [Omaha](46159.md) had a population of 486,051 and in 2024, the city's estimated population was 489,265, a 0.7% increased from the 2020 census. In 2020, the city of [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska) had a population of 291,082 and in 2024, the city's estimated population was 300,619, a 3.3% increased from the 2020 census.



Lincoln, Nebraska's capital city

### 2024 estimate

As of the 2024 estimate, there were 2,005,465 people, 824,012 households, and \_ families residing in the state. The population density was 26.11 inhabitants per square mile (10.1/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 880,521 housing units at an average density of 11.46 per square mile (4.4/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the state was 86.5% White *(75.1% NH White)*, 5.7% African American, 1.8% Native American, 3.1% Asian, 0.2% Pacific Islander, \_% from some other races and 2.7% from two or more races. Hispanic or Latino people of any race were 13.6% of the population.

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2024 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress) (AHAR), there were an estimated 2,720 [homeless people in Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_Nebraska).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### 2020 census



Largest Non-Hispanic Native American ancestry by county and numbers of people reporting "Native American Alone"

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), there were 1,961,504 people, 773,312 households, and 493,998 families residing in the state.[^cite_note-54] The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 25.54 inhabitants per square mile (9.9/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 844,278 housing units at an average density of 10.99 per square mile (4.2/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the state was 78.41% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 4.92% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.18% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 2.70% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.08% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(U.S._Census\)), 5.36% from some other races and 7.35% from two or more races. [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) people of any race were 11.97% of the population.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) of Nebraska is approximately 3.1 miles west of the village of [Garrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison,_Nebraska) ([Butler County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_County,_Nebraska)).[^cite_note-57]

According to the 2016 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), 10.2% of Nebraska's population were of [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) origin (of any race): [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American) (7.8%), [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans) (0.2%), [Cuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_American) (0.2%), and other Hispanic or Latino origin (2.0%). The largest ancestry groups were: [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) (36.1%), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) (13.1%), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) (7.8%), [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_American) (4.7%), [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_American) (4.3%), and [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_American) (3.5%).

Nebraska has the largest [Czech American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Americans) and non-Mormon [Danish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Americans) population (as a percentage of the total population) in the nation. In recent years, Nebraska has become home to many refugee communities. The state legislature lifted the ban in December 2016.[^cite_note-65]

Mexico, India, China, Guatemala, and El Salvador are top countries of origin for Nebraska's immigrants.[^cite_note-66]

The religious affiliations of the people of Nebraska are predominantly Christian, according to a 2014 survey by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center). At the 2020 Public Religion Research Institute survey, 73% of the population identified as Christian.[^cite_note-80] At the 2014 Pew Research Center's survey, 20% of the population were religiously unaffiliated; in 2020, the Public Religion Research Institute determined 22% of the population became religiously unaffiliated. The 2023–24 Pew Research Center's Religious Landscape Study found that 74% of Nebraskans identified as Christians and 21% were religiously unaffiliated.

The largest single denominations by number of adherents in 2010 were the [Roman Catholic Church](606848.md) (372,838), the [Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod) (112,585), the [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](248255.md) (110,110) and the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) (109,283).\[^cite_note-[www.thearda.com-81](http://www.thearda.com-81/)]

## Taxation

Nebraska has a [progressive income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_tax). The portion of income from \$0 to \$2,400 is taxed at 2.56%; from \$2,400 to \$17,500, at 3.57%; from \$17,500 to \$27,000, at 5.12%; and income over \$27,000, at 6.84%. The standard deduction for a single taxpayer is \$5,700; the personal exemption is \$118.[^cite_note-82]

Nebraska has a state sales and use tax of 5.5%. In addition to the state tax, some Nebraska cities assess a city sales and use tax, in 0.5% increments, up to a maximum of 1.5%. [Dakota County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_County,_Nebraska) levies an additional 0.5% county sales tax.[^cite_note-83] Food and ingredients that are generally for home preparation and consumption are not taxable.[^cite_note-84] All [real property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_property) within the state of Nebraska is taxable unless specifically exempted by statute. Since 1992, only depreciable [personal property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_property) is subject to tax and all other personal property is exempt from tax. [Inheritance tax](5824627.md) is collected at the county level. As of July 2025, the state's gasoline tax is 49.70 cents per gallon.[^cite_note-\:0-85]

In 2025, the [Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis) estimated Nebraska's [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) was \$198 billion.[^cite_note-86] Nebraska's [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income_in_the_United_States) was \$75,858 in 2025, ranked 19th in the nation.[^cite_note-87] Nebraska has a large agriculture sector, and is a major producer of [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef), [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [corn (maize)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans), and [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum_bicolor).[^cite_note-88] Other important economic sectors include [freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight) transport (by rail and truck), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), [telecommunications](33094374.md), [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), and [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance).

### Industry

[Kool-Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kool-Aid) was created in 1927 by [Edwin Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Perkins_\(inventor\)) in the city of [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings,_Nebraska), which celebrates the event the second weekend of every August with Kool-Aid Days,[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] and Kool-Aid is the official soft drink of Nebraska.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] *[CliffsNotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CliffsNotes)* were developed by [Clifton Hillegass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton_Hillegass) of [Rising City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_City,_Nebraska). He adapted his pamphlets from the Canadian publications, *[Coles Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coles_\(bookstore\))*.

[Omaha](46159.md) is home to [Berkshire Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway), whose [chief executive officer](52234.md) (CEO), [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett), was ranked in March 2009 by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine as the [second-richest person in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World's_Billionaires). The city is also home to [Mutual of Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_of_Omaha), [West Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Corporation), [Valmont Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmont_Industries), [Woodmen of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_of_the_World), [Kiewit Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiewit_Corporation), [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad), and [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)). [Ameritas Life Insurance Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameritas_Life_Insurance_Company), [Nelnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelnet), [Sandhills Publishing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhills_Publishing_Company), Duncan Aviation, and [Hudl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudl) are based in [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska). [The Buckle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckle_\(store\)) is based in [Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kearney,_Nebraska). [Sidney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney,_Nebraska) is the national headquarters for [Cabela's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabela's), a specialty retailer of outdoor goods now owned by [Bass Pro Shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_Pro_Shops). [Grand Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Island,_Nebraska) is the headquarters of [Hornady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornady), a manufacturer of [ammunition](18940621.md).

The world's largest [train yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_yard), [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad)'s [Bailey Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_Yard), is in [North Platte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Platte,_Nebraska). The [Vise-Grip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locking_pliers) was invented by William Petersen in 1924, and was manufactured in [De Witt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Witt,_Nebraska) until the plant was closed and moved to China in late 2008.[^cite_note-95]

Lincoln's Kawasaki Motors Manufacturing is the only Kawasaki plant in the world to produce the [Jet Ski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Ski_\(brand\)), [all-terrain vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-terrain_vehicle) (ATV), and [MULE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_MULE) product lines. The facility employs more than 1,200 people.

The [Spade Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spade_Ranch_\(Nebraska\)), in the [Sandhills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhills_\(Nebraska\)), is one of Nebraska's oldest and largest beef cattle operations.

### Energy

See also: [List of power stations in Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Nebraska)

Nebraska has been the nation's second-largest producer of ethanol [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel). It has few fossil-fuel resources except for crude oil from the [Niobrara Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobrara_Formation) which underlays a portion of the state's western region. It hosts one uranium leach mining operation near its northwest border with Wyoming. It has an abundance of renewable generation resources, including untapped biomass generation potential from its productive agriculture industry. It has been a top-ten state for per-capita energy consumption due in large part to its energy-intensive agriculture, meat packing, and food processing industries.[^cite_note-eiapro-96]

Nebraska is the only state in the US where all [electric utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_utility) are [publicly owned](204768.md).[^cite_note-97] Half of its electricity is generated from coal and the fastest-growing source in recent years has been wind. Nebraska has no [renewable portfolio standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_portfolio_standard) while supporting [net metering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_metering).[^cite_note-eiapro-96]

## Law and government

Main article: [Government of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nebraska)



[Treemap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treemap) of the popular vote by county, 2016 presidential election

[The Government of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nebraska) operates under the framework of the [Nebraska Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nebraska), adopted in 1875,[^cite_note-100] and is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial.

### Executive branch

Further information: [Governor of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Nebraska)

The head of the executive branch is [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Nebraska) [Jim Pillen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Pillen) ([Republican](32070.md)).[^cite_note-101] The Governor of Nebraska is the [head of government](37570.md) of the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of Nebraska as provided by the fourth article of the [Constitution of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nebraska). Other elected officials in the executive branch[^cite_note-102] are [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Nebraska) [Joe Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Kelly_\(attorney\)),[^cite_note-103] [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Attorney_General) [Mike Hilgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Hilgers),[^cite_note-104] [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Nebraska) [Bob Evnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Evnen),[^cite_note-105] [State Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Treasurer) [John Murante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Murante),[^cite_note-106] and [State Auditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Auditor) [Mike Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Foley_\(Nebraska_politician\)).[^cite_note-107] All elected officials in the executive branch serve four-year terms.

### Legislative branch

Further information: [Nebraska Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Legislature) and [Nebraska State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_State_Capitol)

Nebraska is the only state in the United States with a 'single-house' [unicameral](204304.md) legislature.[^cite_note-Unicameral_History-108] Although this house is officially known simply as the "[Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Legislature)", and more commonly called the "Unicameral", its members call themselves "senators". Nebraska's Legislature is also the only [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_\(United_States\)) in the United States that is officially [nonpartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-partisan_democracy). The senators are elected with no party affiliation next to their names on the ballot, and members of any party can be elected to the positions of speaker and committee chairs.[^cite_note-109] The Nebraska Legislature adopts and revises state legislation and can also override the governor's veto with a three-fifths majority,[^cite_note-110] in contrast to the two-thirds majority required in some other states.

When Nebraska became a state in 1867, its legislature consisted of two houses: a House of Representatives and a Senate. For years, U.S. Senator [George Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Norris) (Senator 1913–1943) and other Nebraskans encouraged the idea of a unicameral legislature and demanded the issue be decided in a [referendum](51582.md). Norris argued:[^cite_note-Unicameral_History-108]

> The constitutions of our various states are built upon the idea that there is but one class. If this be true, there is no sense or reason in having the same thing done twice, especially if it is to be done by two bodies of men elected in the same way and having the same jurisdiction.

&#x20;Unicameral supporters also argued that a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) legislature had a significant undemocratic feature in the committees that reconciled House and Senate legislation. Votes in these committees were secretive, and would sometimes add provisions to bills that neither house had approved.[^cite_note-111] Nebraska's unicameral legislature today has rules that bills can contain only one subject,[^cite_note-112] and must be given at least five days of consideration. In 1934, due in part to the budgetary pressure of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), Nebraska citizens ran a state initiative to vote on a constitutional amendment creating a unicameral legislature, which was approved, which, in effect, abolished the House of Representatives (the lower house).[^cite_note-Unicameral_History-108]

The Legislature meets in the third [Nebraska State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_State_Capitol) building, built between 1922 and 1932. It was designed by [Bertram G. Goodhue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertram_Goodhue). Built from [Indiana limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Limestone), the capitol's base is a cross within a square. A 400-foot (122 m) domed tower rises from this base. The Sower, a 19-foot (5.8 m) bronze statue representing agriculture, crowns the building.

### Judicial branch

Further information: [Courts of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Nebraska)

The judicial system in Nebraska is unified, with the [Nebraska Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Supreme_Court)[^cite_note-113] having administrative authority over all the courts within the state.[^cite_note-Judicial_Branch-114] Nebraska uses the [Missouri Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Plan) for the selection of judges at all levels, including [county courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_County_Courts) (as the lowest-level courts)[^cite_note-115] and twelve [district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_District_Courts),[^cite_note-116] which contain one or more counties. The [Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Court_of_Appeals) hears appeals from the district courts,[^cite_note-117] [juvenile courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Juvenile_Courts),[^cite_note-118] and workers' compensation courts.[^cite_note-119]

### Federal representation

Further information: [Nebraska's congressional delegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_congressional_delegations)



The [Nebraska State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_State_Capitol) in [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska)

Nebraska is represented in the [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) by [Republican](32070.md) [Deb Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb_Fischer),[^cite_note-Federal_Representatives-120] who was first elected in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Nebraska).[^cite_note-121] Nebraska's other Senate seat is currently held by [Pete Ricketts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Ricketts), who took office on January 23, 2023.[^cite_note-122]

Nebraska has three representative seats in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md).[^cite_note-Federal_Representatives-120] Until the next election, Nebraska's representatives are [Mike Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flood_\(politician\)) (R) of the [1st district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_1st_congressional_district),[^cite_note-123] [Don Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bacon) (R) of the [2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_2nd_congressional_district),[^cite_note-124] and [Adrian Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Smith_\(politician\)) (R) of the [3rd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_3rd_congressional_district).[^cite_note-125]

Nebraska is one of two states ([Maine](19977.md) is the other) that allow for a split in the state's allocation of [electoral votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_\(United_States\)) in [presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-Electoral_College_Vote_Distribution-21] Under a 1991 law, two of Nebraska's five votes are awarded to the winner of the statewide popular vote, while the other three go to the highest vote-getter in each of the state's three [congressional districts](449478.md).

### Politics

Further information: [Political party strength in Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_Nebraska)

For most of its history, Nebraska has been a solidly [Republican](32070.md) state. Republicans have carried the state in all but one presidential election since [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_United_States_presidential_election): the [1964 landslide election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election) of [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson). In the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election), [George W. Bush](3414021.md) won the state's five electoral votes by a margin of 33 percentage points (making Nebraska's the fourth-strongest Republican vote among states) with 65.9% of the overall vote; only [Thurston County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurston_County,_Nebraska), which is majority-[Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), voted for his [Democratic](5043544.md) challenger [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry). In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), the state split its electoral votes for the first time: Republican [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) won the popular vote in Nebraska as a whole and two of its three congressional districts; the second district, which includes the city of Omaha, went for Democrat [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama). Since then, the state has split its electoral vote twice, with the second district going for the Democratic Party candidates [Joe Biden](145422.md) in [2020](48410011.md) and [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) in [2024](59568546.md).

Despite the current Republican domination of Nebraska politics, the state has a long tradition of electing [centrist](39619035.md) members of both parties to state and federal office; examples include [George W. Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Norris) (who served a few years in the Senate as an independent), [J. James Exon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._James_Exon), [Bob Kerrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Kerrey), and [Chuck Hagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Hagel). Voters have tilted to the right in recent years, a trend evidenced when Hagel retired from the Senate in 2008 and was succeeded by conservative Republican [Mike Johanns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johanns) to the U.S. Senate, as well as with the 2006 re-election of [Ben Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Nelson), who was considered the most conservative Democrat in the Senate until his retirement in 2013. Johanns retired in 2015 and was succeeded by [Ben Sasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Sasse), while Nelson retired in 2013 and was succeeded by [Deb Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb_Fischer), both conservative Republicans.

Though its politics are generally conservative, the state also has a history of progressive reform. Nebraska was the first U.S. state to [outlaw sexual assault within a marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marital_rape_in_the_United_States), in 1975.[^cite_note-Polisi-126] In 1980, it became the first U.S. state to [divest from South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinvestment_from_South_Africa) to protest the racist system of [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid).[^cite_note-Lansing1981-127]

Former President [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) was born in Nebraska but moved away shortly after birth. [Illinois](14849.md) native [William Jennings Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan) represented Nebraska in Congress, served as [U.S. Secretary of State](32293.md) under President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson), and unsuccessfully ran for president three times. Former Vice President [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney) was born in [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska) but moved to [Casper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper,_Wyoming), [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming).
