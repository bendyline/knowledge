**Politics of Solomon Islands** takes place within the framework of a [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy). [Solomon Islands](265083.md) is an independent [Commonwealth realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), where [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the [government](12229.md) and a [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system) parliament.

The head of state, the [King or Queen of Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Solomon_Islands), is represented by the [Governor-General](1017894.md). The [head of government](37570.md) is the [Prime Minister](4282815.md). Constitutional safeguards include freedom of speech, press, worship, movement, and association. The [Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is independent of the executive and the legislature.

The [King of Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Solomon_Islands) is represented in [Solomon Islands](265083.md) by the [Governor-General of Solomon Islands](1017894.md) who acts on the advice of the prime minister and the cabinet. The [Governor-General of Solomon Islands](1017894.md) is elected by parliament, but formally appointed by His Majesty the King.

Solomon Islands governments are characterized by weak political parties and highly unstable parliamentary coalitions. They are subject to frequent votes of no confidence, and government leadership changes frequently as a result. Cabinet changes are common.

The [Prime Minister](4282815.md), elected by [Parliament](4503022.md), chooses the other members of the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)). Each ministry is headed by a cabinet member, who is assisted by a permanent secretary, a career public servant, who directs the staff of the ministry. The cabinet consists of the Prime Minister and ministers of executive departments. They answer politically to the House of Assembly.

### Attorneys-General of Solomon Islands

As described in the [Constitution of the Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Solomon_Islands) (1978), the Attorney-General is appointed by the Judicial and Legal Service Commission and acts as the principal legal adviser to the Government. The Attorney General of Solomon Islands may serve in an advisory capacity to the parliament, but does not possess any voting rights.[^cite_note-1]

## Legislative branch



Parliament house in Honiara.



[National Parliament of Solomon Islands](4503022.md) (2008) in Honiara

The [National Parliament](4503022.md) has 50 members, elected for a four-year term in single-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency). Solomon Islands have a [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party) system, with numerous [parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties) in which no one party often has a chance of gaining power alone. Political parties must work with each other to form [coalition governments](6036.md).

Parliament may be dissolved by a majority vote of its members before the completion of its term. Parliamentary representation is based on single-member constituencies. [Suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage) is universal for citizens over age 18.

## Administrative divisions

Further information: [Provinces of Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Solomon_Islands)

For local government, the country is divided into 10 administrative areas, of which [nine are provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Solomon_Islands) administered by elected provincial assemblies, and the 10th is the city of [Honiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honiara), administered by the Honiara City Council.

## Political history

Solomon Islands governments are characterized by weak political parties and highly unstable parliamentary coalitions. They are subject to frequent [votes of no confidence](208101.md), and government leadership changes frequently as a result. Cabinet changes are common.

The first post-independence government was elected in August 1980. Prime Minister [Peter Kenilorea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kenilorea) was head of government until September 1981, when he was succeeded by [Solomon Mamaloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Mamaloni) as the result of a realignment within the parliamentary coalitions. Following the November 1984 elections, Kenilorea was again elected Prime Minister, to be replaced in 1986 by his former deputy [Ezekiel Alebua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel_Alebua) following shifts within the parliamentary coalitions. The next election, held in early 1989, returned Solomon Mamaloni as Prime Minister. [Francis Billy Hilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Billy_Hilly) was elected Prime Minister following the national elections in June 1993, and headed the government until November 1994 when four Cabinet Ministers were allegedly bribed by a foreign logging company to shift their parliamentary loyalties and bring Solomon Mamaloni back to power.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The national election of 6 August 1997 resulted in [Bartholomew Ulufa'alu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_Ulufa'alu)'s election as Prime Minister, heading a coalition government, which christened itself the [Solomon Islands Alliance for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands_Alliance_for_Change). In June 2000, an insurrection mounted by militants from the island of [Malaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaita) resulted in the brief detention of Ulufa’alu and his subsequent forced resignation. Prior to this Ulufa'alu had requested Australian intervention to stabilise the deteriorating situation in Solomon Islands, which was refused. [Manasseh Sogavare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasseh_Sogavare), leader of the [People's Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Progressive_Party_\(Solomon_Islands\)), was chosen Prime Minister by a loose coalition of parties. New elections in December 2001 brought [Sir Allan Kemakeza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Allan_Kemakeza) into the Prime Minister's chair with the support of a coalition of parties. Bartholomew Ulufa’alu was Leader of the Opposition.

Kemakeza attempted to address the deteriorating law and order situation in the country, but the prevailing atmosphere of lawlessness, widespread [extortion](162449.md), and ineffective police prompted a formal request by the Solomon Islands Government for outside help. In July 2003, [Australian](4689264.md) and [Pacific Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Island) police and troops arrived in the Solomon Islands under the auspices of the Australian-led [Regional Assistance Mission to Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Assistance_Mission_to_Solomon_Islands) (RAMSI). The mission, consisting of a policing effort, military support, and a large development component, largely restored law and order to Honiara and the other provinces of Solomon Islands. Efforts are now underway to identify a donor base and reestablish credible systems of governance and financial management.

In the 2006 legislative election Kemakeza's People's Alliance Party suffered a major defeat, losing more than half its seats. However Deputy Prime Minister Synder Rini succeeded in gaining the support of enough Independent Members of Parliament to form government. This resulted in rioting in the capital of Honiara. Much of the violence was directed at Chinese businessmen who were accused of influencing the election result. Reinforcements to RAMSI stabilised the situation, but not before serious damage was done to the nation's already fragile economy. Rini resigned shortly before a motion of no confidence was due to take place, and was succeeded by Manasseh Sogavare, a former Prime Minister.

The inability of RAMSI to oversee a peaceful election has raised serious doubts about the success of the mission.

In 2006, [riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Assistance_Mission_to_Solomon_Islands#Riots_following_2006_general_election) broke out following the election of [Snyder Rini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snyder_Rini) as Prime Minister, destroying part of Chinatown and displacing more than 1,000 Chinese residents; the large Pacific Casino Hotel was also totally gutted.[^cite_note-McKinnon2009-26] The commercial heart of Honiara was virtually reduced to rubble and ashes.[^cite_note-27] Three [National Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Parliament_of_the_Solomon_Islands) members, [Charles Dausabea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dausabea), [Nelson Ne'e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Ne'e), and [Patrick Vahoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Vahoe),[^cite_note-28] were arrested during or as a result of the riots. The Regional Assistance Mission to Solomon Islands (RAMSI), the 16-country Pacific Islands Forum initiative set up in 2003 with assistance from Australia, intervened, sending in additional police and army officers to bring the situation under control. A vote of no confidence was passed against the Prime Minister. Following his resignation, a five-party Grand Coalition for Change Government was formed in May 2006, with Manasseh Sogavare as Prime Minister, quelling the riots and running the government. The military part of [RAMSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Assistance_Mission_to_Solomon_Islands) was withdrawn in 2013 and rebuilding took shape.[^cite_note-Doing-29]

In 2009, the government is scheduled to set up a [Truth and Reconciliation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_Reconciliation_Commission_\(Solomon_Islands\)), with the assistance of [South African](17416221.md) Archbishop [Desmond Tutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Tutu), to "address people’s traumatic experiences during the five-year ethnic conflict on Guadalcanal".[^cite_note-RNZI_41836-30][^cite_note-31]

The government continues to face serious problems, including an uncertain economic outlook, [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), and [malaria](20423.md) control. At one point, prior to the deployment of RAMSI forces, the country was facing a serious financial crisis. While economic conditions are improving, the situation remains unstable.

### 2021 unrest



Prime Minister [Manasseh Sogavare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasseh_Sogavare) with [Taiwanese President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_President) [Tsai Ing-wen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsai_Ing-wen) in July 2016

Main article: [2021 Solomon Islands unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Solomon_Islands_unrest)

In 2019, the central government under Prime Minister [Manasseh Sogavare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasseh_Sogavare) withdrew recognition of the [Republic of China (Taiwan)](25734.md) and established relations with the mainland [People's Republic of China](5405.md). [Malaita Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaita_Province), however, continued to be supported by Taiwan and the [United States](3434750.md), the latter sending US\$25 million of aid to the island in 2020.[^cite_note-32] The premier of Malaita Province, [Daniel Suidani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Suidani), also held an independence referendum in 2020 which the national government has dismissed as illegitimate.[^cite_note-33] Rising unemployment and poverty, worsened by the border closure during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), have also been cited as a cause of the unrest.[^cite_note-34] Chinese businesses were also accused of giving jobs to foreigners instead of locals.[^cite_note-\:3-35]

The protests were initially peaceful,[^cite_note-\:02021-36] but turned violent on 24 November 2021 after buildings adjoining the [Solomon Islands Parliament Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands_Parliament_Building)[^cite_note-37] were burnt down. Schools and businesses were closed down as police and government forces clashed with protesters. Violence escalated as [Honiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honiara)'s [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Honiara) was looted.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Most of the protesters came from [Malaita Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaita_Province).[^cite_note-\:1-40][^cite_note-\:2-41]

[Australia](4689264.md) responded to the unrest by deploying [Australian Federal Police](341221.md) and [Australian Defence Force](67538.md) personnel following a request from the Sogavare government under the [Australia–Solomon Islands Bilateral Security Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Solomon_Islands_Bilateral_Security_Treaty).[^cite_note-Andrews-42] [Papua New Guinea](22943.md), [Fiji](10707.md) and [New Zealand](4913064.md) also sent peacekeepers.[^cite_note-AFPviaF24-43][^cite_note-44]

### Since 2024

In May 2024, [Jeremiah Manele](10375180.md) was elected as the Solomon Islands new prime minister to succeed Manasseh Sogavare.[^cite_note-45]

## Land ownership

Land ownership is reserved for Solomon Islanders. At the time of [independence](62450.md), citizenship was granted to all persons whose parents are or were both [British](31717.md) protected persons and members of a group, tribe, or line indigenous to Solomon Islands. The law provides that resident [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate), such as the [Chinese](5405.md) and [Kiribati](16674.md), may obtain citizenship through [naturalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization).

Land generally is held on a family or village basis and may be handed down from mother or father according to local custom. The islanders are reluctant to provide land for nontraditional economic undertakings, and this has resulted in continual disputes over land ownership.

## Military

No military forces are maintained by Solomon Islands, although the [Royal Solomon Islands Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Solomon_Islands_Police_Force) (RSIP) of nearly 500 includes a border protection element. The police also have responsibility for fire service, [disaster relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_relief), and [maritime surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_surveillance). The police force is headed by a commissioner, appointed by the Governor General and responsible to the prime minister.
