**Tuscany** ([/ˈtʌskəni/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*TUSK-ə-nee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Italian](14708.md): *Toscana* [\[tosˈkaːna\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian)) is a [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Italy) [Italy](14532.md). With a population of 3,659,222 in an area of 22,987.04 square kilometres (8,875.35 square miles), it is the [9th most populous and 5th-largest region of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy#Regions).[^cite_note-population-3][^cite_note-area-2] The capital and largest city is [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence).

Tuscany is known for its landscapes, history, artistic legacy, and influence on [high culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_culture). It is regarded as the birthplace of the [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance)[^cite_note-7] and of the foundations of the Italian language. The prestige established by [Tuscans'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_dialects) use in literature by [Dante Alighieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri), [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch), [Giovanni Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Boccaccio), [Niccolò Machiavelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Machiavelli), and [Francesco Guicciardini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Guicciardini) led to its adoption as the basis for elaboration of the language of culture throughout Italy.[^cite_note-8] It has been home to many figures influential in the history of art and science, and contains well-known museums such as the [Uffizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uffizi) and the [Palazzo Pitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Pitti). Tuscany is also known for [its wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_wine), including [Chianti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chianti), [Vino Nobile di Montepulciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vino_Nobile_di_Montepulciano), [Morellino di Scansano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morellino_di_Scansano), [Brunello di Montalcino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunello_di_Montalcino), and white [Vernaccia di San Gimignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernaccia_di_San_Gimignano). Having a strong linguistic and cultural identity, it is sometimes considered "a nation within a nation".[^cite_note-9]

Tuscany is the second-most-popular [Italian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) for travelers in Italy, after [Veneto](43780.md).[^cite_note-10] The main tourist spots are [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), [San Gimignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gimignano), [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena) and [Lucca](46169.md).[^cite_note-ilgiunco.net-11] [Castiglione della Pescaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castiglione_della_Pescaia) is the most visited seaside destination in the region,[^cite_note-ilgiunco.net-11] with seaside tourism accounting for approximately 40% of tourist arrivals. The [Maremma region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maremma), the [Chianti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chianti_\(region\)), [Versilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versilia), and [Val d'Orcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_d'Orcia) are also internationally renowned and particularly popular among travelers.

Eight Tuscan localities have been designated [World Heritage Sites](44940.md): the [historic Centre of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Florence) (1982); the [Cathedral square of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_dei_Miracoli) (1987); the historical centre of [San Gimignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gimignano) (1990); the historical centre of [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena) (1995); the historical centre of [Pienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pienza) (1996); the [Val d'Orcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_d'Orcia) (2004), the [Medici Villas and Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici_villas) (2013), and [Montecatini Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montecatini_Terme) as part of [the Great Spa Towns of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Spa_Towns_of_Europe) (2021). Tuscany has over 120 protected [nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve), making Tuscany and its capital Florence popular tourist destinations. In 2018, Florence alone had over 5 million arrivals, making it the world's 51st [most visited city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_international_visitors).[^cite_note-12]

## History

Main article: [History of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tuscany)

### Apennine, Proto-Villanovan and Villanovan culture

Main articles: [Apennine culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_culture), [Proto-Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Villanovan_culture), and [Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanovan_culture)



Cinerary urns of the [Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanovan_culture)

The pre-Etruscan history of the area in the middle and late [Bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) parallels that of the [archaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_Greece) [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarkerRasmussen20005-13] The Tuscan area was inhabited by peoples of the so-called [Apennine culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_culture) in the [second millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_millennium_BC) (c. 1400 – c. 1150 BC) who had trading relationships with the [Minoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization) and [Mycenaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece) civilizations in the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarkerRasmussen20005-13] and, at the end of the Bronze Age, by peoples of the so-called [Proto-Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Villanovan_culture) (c. 1100 – c. 900 BC) part of the central European [Urnfield culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urnfield_culture) system. Following this, at the beginning of the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), the [Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanovan_culture) (c. 900 – c. 700 BC), regarded as the oldest phase of [Etruscan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization),[^cite_note-Neri-14][^cite_note-Bartolonivillanoviana-15] saw Tuscany, and the rest of Etruria, taken over by [chiefdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdom).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarkerRasmussen20005-13] [City-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state) developed in the late Villanovan (paralleling Greece and the Aegean) before "[Orientalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalizing_period)" occurred.[^cite_note-Smith2014-16]

### Etruscans

Main article: [Etruscan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization)



The *[Chimera of Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimera_of_Arezzo)*, Etruscan bronze, 400 BC

The [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) (Latin: *Tusci*) created the first major [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization) in this region, large enough to establish a transport infrastructure, to implement agriculture and mining and to produce vibrant art.[^cite_note-Jones_2-17] The Etruscans lived in the area of [Etruria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruria) well into prehistory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarkerRasmussen20005-13] The civilization grew to fill the area between the Arno and [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber) from the tenth century BCE, reaching its peak during the seventh and sixth centuries B.C., finally succumbing to the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) by the first century BCE.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarkerRasmussen20001-18] From the Etruscans, Tuscany took the name of Etruria, Tuscia for the Romans and subsequently Tuscania and Tuscany. The areas of north-western Tuscany were inhabited by the ancient [Ligures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligures). In northwestern Tuscany, the area between the Arno and Magra rivers was culturally aligned with the Etruscans in the early Iron Age, and came under Ligurian control in the late Iron Age.[^cite_note-19]

One reason for the eventual demise of this civilization was the increasing absorption by surrounding cultures, including the adoption of the Etruscan upper class by the Romans.[^cite_note-Jones_2-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarkerRasmussen20001-18]

### Romans

Soon after absorbing Etruria (to the north, northeast, east, and a strip to the south), Rome established the cities of [Lucca](46169.md), [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena), and [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), endowed the area with new technologies and development, and ensured peace.[^cite_note-Jones_2-17] These developments included extensions of existing roads, the introduction of aqueducts and sewers, and the construction of many buildings, both public and private. However, many of these structures have been destroyed by erosion due to weather.[^cite_note-Jones_2-17]

The Roman civilization in the West of the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) and later [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) collapsed in the fifth century, and the region fell briefly to [barbarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian) migrating through the Empire from Eastern Europe and Central Asia of the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) (Eastern – [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) and Western – [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths)), then was re-conquered by the revived [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire) (later [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire)) under the Emperor [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian). In the years following 572, the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) arrived and designated [Lucca](46169.md) the capital of their subsequent [Tuscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscia).[^cite_note-Jones_2-17]

### Medieval period

See also: [Duchy of Tuscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Tuscia) and [March of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Tuscany)



[Battle of Montaperti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Montaperti), 1260

[Pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim) travelling along the [Via Francigena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Francigena) between [Rome](25458.md) and France brought wealth and development during the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_period).[^cite_note-Jones_2-17] The food and shelter required by these travellers fuelled the growth of communities around churches and taverns.[^cite_note-Jones_2-17] The conflict between the [Guelphs and Ghibellines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines), factions supporting the [Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papacy) or the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in central and northern Italy during the 12th and 13th centuries, split the Tuscan people.[^cite_note-Jones_2-17] The two factions gave rise to several powerful and rich [medieval communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_commune) in Tuscany: [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo), [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [Lucca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Lucca), [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), and [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena).[^cite_note-Jones_2-17] Balance between these communes was ensured by the assets they held: Pisa, a port; Siena, banking; and Lucca, banking and silk.[^cite_note-Jones_3-20] But by the time of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), Florence had become the cultural capital of Tuscany.[^cite_note-Jones_3-20]

One family that benefitted from Florence's growing wealth and power was the ruling [Medici family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici_family). Its scion [Lorenzo de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_de'_Medici) was one of the most famous of the Medici. The legacy of his influence is visible today in the prodigious expression of art and architecture in Florence. His famous descendant [Catherine de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_de'_Medici) married Prince Henry (later [King Henry II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II_of_France)) of France in 1533.

The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) epidemic hit Tuscany starting in 1348.[^cite_note-21] It eventually killed 70% of the Tuscan population.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] According to Melissa Snell, "Florence lost a third of its population in the first six months of the plague, and from 45% to 75% of its population in the first year."[^cite_note-24] In 1630, Florence and Tuscany were once again ravaged by the [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1629%E2%80%9331_Italian_plague).[^cite_note-25]

### Renaissance

Further information: [The Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Renaissance) and [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance)



&#x20;*[Primavera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primavera_\(Botticelli\))* (1482) by [Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Botticelli)



Hanging and burning of [Girolamo Savonarola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Savonarola) in [Piazza della Signoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_della_Signoria) in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) 1498 — painting depicting Renaissance Florence

Tuscany, especially [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), is regarded as the birthplace of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). Though "Tuscany" remained a linguistic, cultural, and geographic conception rather than a political reality, in the 15th century, Florence extended its dominion in Tuscany through the annexation of Arezzo in 1384, the purchase of Pisa in 1405, and the suppression of a local resistance there (1406). [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno) was bought in 1421 and became the harbour of Florence.

From the leading city of Florence, the republic was from 1434 onward dominated by the increasingly monarchical [Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici) family. Initially, under [Cosimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosimo_de'_Medici), [Piero the Gouty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_di_Cosimo_de'_Medici), [Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_de'_Medici) and [Piero the Unfortunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_the_Unfortunate), the forms of the republic were retained and the Medici ruled without a title, usually without even a formal office. These rulers presided over the [Florentine Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_Renaissance). There was a return to the republic from 1494 to 1512, when first [Girolamo Savonarola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Savonarola) then [Piero Soderini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Soderini) oversaw the state. Cardinal Giovanni di Lorenzo de' Medici retook the city with Spanish forces in 1512, before going to Rome to become [Pope Leo X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_X). Florence was dominated by a series of papal proxies until 1527 when the citizens declared the republic again, only to have it taken from them again in 1530 after a siege by an Imperial and Spanish army. At this point [Pope Clement VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII) and [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) appointed [Alessandro de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_de'_Medici) as the first formal hereditary ruler.

The Sienese commune was not incorporated into Tuscany until 1555, and during the 15th century, Siena enjoyed a cultural 'Sienese Renaissance' with its own more conservative character. Lucca remained an independent republic until 1847 when it became part of [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany) by the will of its people. [Piombino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piombino) and other strategic towns constituted the tiny [State of the *Presidi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Presidi) under Spanish control.

### Modern era

See also: [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany)



Map of the [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany)



Memorial to the victims of the [Sant'Anna di Stazzema massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Anna_di_Stazzema_massacre), in which 560 locals were murdered by [Nazi Germans](21212.md) and [Italian Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Brigades) in 1944

In the 16th century, the [Medicis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Medici), rulers of Florence, annexed the [Republic of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Siena), creating the [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany). The Medici family became extinct in 1737 with the death of [Gian Gastone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Gastone_de'_Medici,_Grand_Duke_of_Tuscany), and Tuscany was transferred to [Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), [Duke of Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Lorraine) and husband of Austrian Empress [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa), who let the country be ruled by his son. The dynasty of the Lorena ruled Tuscany until 1860, except during the [Napoleonic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_period), when most of the country was annexed to the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire). After the [Second Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italian_War_of_Independence), a revolution evicted the last Grand Duke, and after a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite), Tuscany became part of the new [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy). From 1864 to 1870 Florence became the second capital of the kingdom.

Under [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), the area came under the dominance of local [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fascist_Party) leaders such as [Dino Perrone Compagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Perrone_Compagni) (from Florence), and [Costanzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costanzo_Ciano) and [Galeazzo Ciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeazzo_Ciano) (from [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno)). Following the fall of Mussolini and the armistice of 8 September 1943, Tuscany became part of the Nazi-controlled [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic) and was conquered almost totally by the Anglo-American forces during the summer of 1944.

Following the end of the Social Republic and the transition from the Kingdom to the modern Italian Republic, Tuscany once more flourished as a cultural centre of Italy. Since the establishment of the regional government in 1970, Tuscany has always been ruled by centre-left governments.

## Geography

Roughly triangular in shape, Tuscany borders the regions of [Liguria](71864.md) to the northwest, [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md) to the north, [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche) and [Umbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria) to the east, and [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio) to the south and southeast. The [comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune) (municipality) of [Badia Tedalda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badia_Tedalda), in the Tuscan [Province of Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Arezzo), has an exclave named Ca' Raffaello within Emilia-Romagna.

Tuscany has a western coastline on the [Ligurian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Sea) and the [Tyrrhenian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea), among which is the [Tuscan Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_Archipelago), of which the most significant island is [Elba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elba). Tuscany has an area of approximately 23,000 square kilometres (8,900 sq mi). Surrounded and crossed by major mountain chains and with few (but fertile) plains, the region has a relief dominated by hilly country used for agriculture. Hills make up nearly two-thirds (66.5%) of the region's total area, covering 15,292 square kilometres (5,904 sq mi), and mountains (of which the highest are the [Apennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_Mountains)), a further 25%, or 5,770 square kilometres (2,230 sq mi). Plains occupy 8.4% of the total area—1,930 square kilometres (750 sq mi)—mainly around the valley of the [Arno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arno). Many of Tuscany's most significant cities lie on the banks of the Arno, including the capital, [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [Empoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empoli), and [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa). To the north of Florence lies the [Mugello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugello) valley, a green and fertile area surrounded by the Apennine mountains, known for its rolling hills,[^cite_note-26] medieval villages, and the Mugello Circuit, one of Italy’s most famous racing tracks.

The climate is fairly mild in the coastal areas, and harsher and rainy in the interior, with considerable fluctuations in temperature between winter and summer,[^cite_note-ite1geo-27] giving the region a soil-building active freeze-thaw cycle, in part accounting for the region once having served as a key breadbasket of [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic).[^cite_note-28]

As of 2026, the population is 3,659,222, of which 48.8% are male, and 51.2% are female. Minors make up 13.6% of the population, and seniors make up 27.1%.[^cite_note-population-3]

The population density of Tuscany, with 159.2 inhabitants per square kilometre (412/sq mi), is below the national average (195.1/km<sup>2</sup> or 505/sq mi). This is due to the low population density of the provinces of Arezzo, Siena, and especially Grosseto (47.7/km<sup>2</sup> or 124/sq mi). The highest density is found in the province of Prato (714.1/km<sup>2</sup> or 1,850/sq mi), followed by the provinces of Pistoia, Livorno, Florence and Lucca, peaking in the cities of Florence (more than 3,535/km<sup>2</sup> or 9,160/sq mi), Livorno, Prato, Viareggio, Forte dei Marmi and Montecatini Terme (all with a population density of more than 1,000/km<sup>2</sup> or 2,600/sq mi). The territorial distribution of the population is closely linked to the socio-cultural and, more recently, economic and industrial development of Tuscany.[^cite_note-ite1geo-27][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-area-2]

Accordingly, the least densely populated areas are those where the main activity is agriculture, unlike the others where, despite the presence of several large industrial complexes, the main activities are connected with tourism and associated services, alongside many small firms in the leather, glass, paper and clothing sectors.[^cite_note-ite1geo-27]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Tuscany)

The [President of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Tuscany) is the [head of government](37570.md). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in the [Regional Council of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Tuscany), while [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the Regional Government led by the President, who is directly elected by the people. The current Statute, which regulates the functioning of the regional institutions, has been in force since 2005.

Tuscany is a stronghold of the centre-left [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)) (PD), forming with [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md), [Umbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria) and [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche) the so-called Italian political "[Red Quadrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_belt_\(Italy\))". Since 1970, Tuscany has been continuously governed by left-wing governments.

## Culture

See also: [List of museums in Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Tuscany)

Tuscany has an immense cultural and artistic heritage, expressed in the region's churches, palaces, art galleries, museums, villages, and piazzas. Many of these artifacts are found in the main cities, such as [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) and [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena), but also in smaller villages scattered around the region, such as [San Gimignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gimignano).

### Art



[Michelangelo's *David*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_\(Michelangelo\))

Tuscany has a unique artistic legacy, and Florence is one of the world's most important water-colour centres, even so that it is often nicknamed the "art palace of Italy" (the region is also believed to have the largest concentration of Renaissance art and architecture in the world).[^cite_note-35] Painters such as [Cimabue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimabue) and [Giotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto), the fathers of Italian painting, lived in Florence and Tuscany, as well as Arnolfo and [Andrea Pisano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Pisano), renewers of architecture and sculpture; [Brunelleschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Brunelleschi), [Donatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatello) and [Masaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaccio), forefathers of the Renaissance; [Ghiberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Ghiberti) and the [Della Robbias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Della_Robbia), [Filippo Lippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Lippi) and [Angelico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Angelico); [Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botticelli), [Paolo Uccello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Uccello), and the universal genius of [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) and [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The region contains numerous museums and art galleries, many housing some of the world's most precious works of art. Such museums include the [Uffizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uffizi), which keeps Botticelli's *[The Birth of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birth_of_Venus)*, the [Palazzo Pitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Pitti), and the [Bargello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargello), to name a few. Most of the frescos, sculptures, and paintings in Tuscany are held in the region's abundant churches and cathedrals, such as [Florence Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Cathedral), [Siena Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena_Cathedral), [Pisa Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa_Cathedral) and the [Collegiata di San Gimignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiata_di_San_Gimignano).

### Art schools

Main articles: [Florentine painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_painting), [Sienese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sienese_School), and [Lucchese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucchese_School)



A painting from the [Sienese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sienese_School) by [Pietro Lorenzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Lorenzetti)

In the medieval period and the Renaissance, four main Tuscan art schools competed against each other: the [Florentine School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_School), the [Sienese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sienese_School), the Pisan School, and the [Lucchese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucchese_School).

- The Florentine School refers to artists in, from, or influenced by the [naturalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_\(arts\)) style developed in the 14th century, largely through the efforts of [Giotto di Bondone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto_di_Bondone), and in the 15th century the leading school of the world. Some of the best known artists of the Florentine School are [Brunelleschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Brunelleschi), [Donatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatello), [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo), [Fra Angelico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Angelico), [Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botticelli), [Lippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Lippi), [Masolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masolino_da_Panicale), and [Masaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaccio).

- The Sienese School of painting flourished in [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena) between the 13th and 15th centuries and for a time rivaled Florence, though it was more conservative, being inclined towards the decorative beauty and elegant grace of late [Gothic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art). Its most important representatives include [Duccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duccio), whose work shows Byzantine influence; his pupil [Simone Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Martini); [Pietro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Lorenzetti) and [Ambrogio Lorenzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrogio_Lorenzetti); [Domenico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_di_Bartolo) and [Taddeo di Bartolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taddeo_di_Bartolo); and [Sassetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_di_Giovanni) and [Matteo di Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_di_Giovanni). Unlike the naturalistic Florentine art, there is a mystical streak in Sienese art, characterized by a common focus on miraculous events, distortions of time and place, and often dreamlike coloration, with less attention to proportions. In the 16th century, the Mannerists [Beccafumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi) and [Il Sodoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Sodoma) worked there. While Baldassare Peruzzi was born and trained in Siena, his major works and style reflect his long career in Rome. The economic and political decline of Siena by the 16th century, and its eventual subjugation by Florence, largely checked the development of Sienese painting, although it also meant that many Sienese works in churches and public buildings were not discarded or destroyed by new paintings or rebuilding. Siena remains a remarkably well-preserved Italian late-Medieval town.

- The Lucchese School, also known as the School of Lucca and as the Pisan-Lucchese School, was a school of painting and sculpture that flourished in the 11th and 12th centuries in the western and southern part of the region, with an important centre in [Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra). The art is mostly anonymous. Although not as elegant or delicate as the Florentine School, Lucchese works are remarkable for their monumentality.

### Language

Main article: [Tuscan dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_dialect)

Apart from Tuscan being the standard model for Italy's national language (historically preferred over other languages of the peninsula), the [Tuscan dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_dialect) (*dialetto toscano*) is spoken in Tuscany. The Italian language is actually literary Tuscan itself, specifically the [Florentine dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_dialect), only commonly referred to as Italian for political and nationalist reasons. It became the language of culture for all the people of Italy, thanks to the prestige of the masterpieces of [Dante Alighieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri), [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch), [Giovanni Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Boccaccio), [Niccolò Machiavelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Machiavelli), and [Francesco Guicciardini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Guicciardini). It would later become the official language of all the [Italian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historic_states_of_Italy) and of the Kingdom of Italy, when it was formed. Many Tuscan terms are also common in the [Central Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Italian) dialects of Umbria and some parts of [Emilia Romagna](162715.md).[^cite_note-38]

### Music

Main article: [Music of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Tuscany)

See also: [Music of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Florence)



[Giacomo Puccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Puccini)

Tuscany has a rich ancient and modern musical tradition, and has produced numerous composers and musicians, including [Giacomo Puccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Puccini) and [Pietro Mascagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Mascagni). Florence is the main musical centre of Tuscany. The city was at the heart of much of the Western musical tradition. It was there that the [Florentine Camerata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_Camerata) convened in the mid-16th century and experimented with setting tales of [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology) to music and staging, resulting in the first operas, fostering the further development of the operatic form, and the later developments of separate "classical" forms such as the [symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony).

There are numerous musical centres in Tuscany. [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo) is indelibly connected with the name of [Guido d'Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_d'Arezzo), the 11th-century monk who invented modern [musical notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_notation) and the [do-re-mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solf%C3%A8ge) system of naming notes of the scale; [Lucca](46169.md) hosted possibly the greatest Italian composer of [Verismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verismo), [Giacomo Puccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Puccini) together with [Alfredo Catalani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Catalani), while [Pietro Mascagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Mascagni) was born in [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno); and [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena) is well known for the [Accademia Musicale Chigiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Musicale_Chigiana), an organization that currently sponsors major musical activities such as the Siena Music Week and the Alfredo Casella International Composition Competition. Other important musical centres in Tuscany include [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) and [Grosseto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosseto).

### Literature



Tuscan poet and literary figure [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch)

Several famous writers and poets are from Tuscany, most notably Florentine author [Dante Alighieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri). Tuscany's literary scene particularly thrived in the 13th century and the Renaissance.

In Tuscany, especially in the Middle Ages, popular love poetry existed. A school of imitators of the Sicilians was led by [Dante da Maiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_da_Maiano), but its literary originality took another line – that of humorous and satirical poetry. The democratic form of government created a style of poetry that stood against the medieval mystic and chivalrous style.

Another type of poetry also began in Tuscany. Guittone d'Arezzo made art abandon chivalry and [Provençal](48503.md) forms for national motives and Latin forms. He attempted political poetry, and although his work is often obscure, he prepared the way for the Bolognese school. [Bologna](21069333.md) was the city of science, and philosophical poetry appeared there. [Guido Guinizelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Guinizelli) was the poet after the new fashion of the art. In his work, the ideas of [chivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chivalry) are changed and enlarged. Only those whose heart is pure can be blessed with true love, regardless of class. He refuted the traditional credo of [courtly love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtly_love), for which love is a subtle philosophy only a few chosen knights and princesses could grasp. Love is blind to blasons but not to a good heart when it finds one: when it succeeds it is the result of the spiritual, not physical affinity between two souls. Guinizzelli's democratic view can be better understood in the light of the greater equality and freedom enjoyed by the city-states of the center-north and the rise of a middle class eager to legitimise itself in the eyes of the old nobility, still regarded with respect and admiration but dispossessed of its political power.

Guinizelli's *[Canzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canzone)* make up the bible of [Dolce Stil Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_Stil_Novo), and one in particular, "Al cor gentil" ("To a Kind Heart") is considered the manifesto of the new movement which would bloom in Florence under [Cavalcanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Cavalcanti), Dante and their followers. His poetry has some of the faults of the school of d'Arezzo. Nevertheless, he marks a great development in the history of Italian art, especially because of his close connection with Dante's [lyric poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyric_poetry).

In the 13th century, there were several major [allegorical poems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory). One of these is by [Brunetto Latini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunetto_Latini), who was a close friend of Dante. His *Tesoretto* is a short poem, in seven-syllable verses, rhyming in couplets, in which the author professes to be lost in a wilderness and to meet with a lady, who represents Nature, from whom he receives much instruction. We see here the vision, the allegory, the instruction with a moral object, three elements which we shall find again in the *Divine Comedy*. [Francesco da Barberino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_da_Barberino), a learned lawyer who was secretary to bishops, a judge, and a [notary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notary_public), wrote two little allegorical poems, the *Documenti d'amore* and *Del reggimento e dei costumi delle donne*. The poems today are generally studied not as literature, but for historical context. A fourth allegorical work was the *Intelligenza*, which is sometimes attributed to Compagni but is probably only a translation of French poems.[^cite_note-39]

In the 15th century, [humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism) and publisher [Aldus Manutius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldus_Manutius) published the Tuscan poets Petrarch and Dante Alighieri (*[Divine Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Comedy)*), creating the model for what became a standard for modern Italian.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Tuscan food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_food)

See also: [Tuscan wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_wine)



An assortment of Tuscan foods: various wine and cheese, and different sorts of salamis and hams

Simplicity is central to [Tuscan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_food). [Legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume), bread, cheese, vegetables, mushrooms, and fresh fruit are used. [Olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) is made from Moraiolo, [Leccino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leccino) and Frantoiano olives. White [truffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truffle) from [San Miniato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miniato) appear in October and November. Beef of the highest quality comes from the [Chiana Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiana_Valley), specifically, a breed known as [Chianina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chianina) used for [Florentine steak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_steak). The indigenous [Cinta Senese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinta_Senese) breed of pork is also produced.[^cite_note-40]

Wine is a famous and common produce of Tuscany. The red wine [Chianti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chianti) is perhaps the most well-known internationally. Due to the many British tourists who come to the area where Chianti wine is produced this specific area has been nicknamed "[Chiantishire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiantishire)".

### Postage stamps

Main article: [Postage stamps and postal history of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps_and_postal_history_of_Tuscany)

Between 1851 and 1860, the [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany), an independent Italian state until 1859 when it joined the [United Provinces of Central Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Central_Italy), produced two postage stamp issues which are among the most prized [classic stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_stamp) issues of the world, and include the most valuable Italian stamp. The Grand Duchy of Tuscany was an independent Italian state from 1569 to 1859 but was occupied by France from 1808 to 1814. The Duchy comprised most of the present area of Tuscany, and its capital was Florence. In December 1859, the Grand Duchy officially ceased to exist, being joined to the duchies of [Modena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Modena_and_Reggio) and [Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Parma) to form the United Provinces of Central Italy, which was annexed by the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia) a few months later in March 1860. In 1862 it became part of Italy and joined the Italian postal system.

## Economy

The gross domestic product (GDP) of the region was 117.5 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 6.7% of Italy's economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 31,400 euros or 104% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 106% of the EU average.[^cite_note-41]

### Agriculture



Vineyards in the [Chianti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chianti) region

The subsoil in Tuscany is relatively rich in mineral resources, with iron ore, copper, mercury, and [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) mines, the famous *soffioni* ([fumarole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumarole)) at [Larderello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larderello), and the vast marble mines in [Versilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versilia). Although its share is falling all the time, agriculture still contributes to the region's economy. In the region's inland areas cereals, potatoes, olives, and grapes are grown. The swamplands, which used to be marshy, now produce vegetables, rice, tobacco, beets, and sunflowers.[^cite_note-ite1geo-27]

### Industry

One of the traditional foundations of the industrial sector is mining, given the abundance of underground resources. Also of note are textiles and clothing, chemicals and pharmaceuticals, steel and metalworking, glass and ceramics, and printing and publishing. Smaller areas specialising in manufacturing and craft industries are found in the hinterland: the leather and footwear area in the south-west part of the province of Florence, the hot-house plant area in [Pistoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistoia), the ceramics and textile industries in the [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato) area, scooters, and motorcycles in [Pontedera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontedera), and the processing of timber for the manufacture of wooden furniture in the [Cascina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascina) area. The heavy industries (mining, steel, and mechanical engineering) are concentrated along the coastal strip ([Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno) and [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) areas), where there are also important chemical industries. Also of note are the marble ([Carrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrara) area) and paper industries ([Lucca](46169.md) area).[^cite_note-ite1geo-27]

### Tourism

In Tuscany 80% of tourism demand is concentrated in cities of art and seaside resorts; the rest is divided between the countryside, hills, and mountains. In 2019 the municipalities with the relatively higher percentage of presences, in descending order, are: [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), [Montecatini Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montecatini_Terme), [Castiglione della Pescaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castiglione_della_Pescaia), [San Vincenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vincenzo,_Tuscany), [Orbetello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbetello), [Grosseto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosseto), [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena), [Bibbona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibbona), [Viareggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viareggio), [Capoliveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capoliveri). In the Tuscan tourist areas, together with the Florentine area, the Etruscan Coast holds the relatively largest share of presences, immediately followed by [Maremma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maremma) ([Monte Argentario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Argentario), [Giglio Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giglio_Island), [Talamone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talamone), [Capalbio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capalbio)), Terre di Pisa and [Elba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elba).[^cite_note-44]

Tuscany has many small and picturesque villages; 29 of them have been selected by *[I Borghi più belli d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Borghi_pi%C3%B9_belli_d'Italia)* (English: The most beautiful Villages of Italy),[^cite_note-45] a non-profit private association of small Italian towns of strong historical and artistic interest[^cite_note-46] that was founded on the initiative of the Tourism Council of the National Association of Italian Municipalities.[^cite_note-47]

### Fashion

See also: [Fashion in Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_in_Florence) and [Fashion designers of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_designers_of_Florence)



The [Via de' Tornabuoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_de'_Tornabuoni) in Florence, the city's top fashion and shopping street, contains some of the world's most luxurious clothing and jewelry houses, such as [Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_\(jeweler\)), [Ferragamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Ferragamo_S.p.A.), [Gucci](844136.md), [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace) and [Bulgari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgari).

The fashion and textile industry are the pillars of the Florentine economy. In the 15th century, Florentines were working with luxury textiles such as wool and silk. Today the greatest designers in Europe utilize the textile industry in Tuscany, and especially Florence. A large plant of production is happening in the city of [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato).

Italy has one of the strongest textile industries in Europe, accounting for approximately one-quarter of European production. Its turnover is over 25 billion euros. It is the third largest supplier of clothing after China and Japan. The Italian fashion industry generates 60% of its turnover abroad.[^cite_note-48]
