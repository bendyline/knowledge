[Gubernatorial elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_United_States#State_elections) were held in the United States on November 4, 2025, in [New Jersey](21648.md) and [Virginia](32432.md). Both states had last elected governors in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_gubernatorial_elections), with both incumbents being ineligible to seek re-election due to term limits in their respective state constitutions.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

In the [2024 United States presidential election](59568546.md), both states voted for Democratic nominee [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) by a 5-6% margin.[^cite_note-3] New Jersey in particular had a large swing to the right in 2024, with [Donald Trump](4848272.md) losing the state by only 6%, and had narrowly re-elected Democrat [Phil Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Murphy) in 2021, leading some to expect a close race.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Ultimately, Democratic nominees [Mikie Sherrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikie_Sherrill) and [Abigail Spanberger](58910530.md) won their races in New Jersey and Virginia, respectively, by wide margins and outperformed Harris.[^cite_note-6] This is the first time since 1985 that every race in this cycle of governorships was decided by double digits.&#x20;

## Partisan composition

Heading into the election, there were 27 Republican governors and 23 Democratic governors in the United States.

The gubernatorial seats were up in the moderately blue states of Virginia and New Jersey where there was one Republican and one Democratic seat. Republicans were defending one governorship in a state that [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) won in 2024 (Virginia). Democrats were defending one governorship in a state that Harris won (New Jersey).

## Election predictions

Several sites and individuals publish predictions of competitive seats. These predictions looked at factors such as the strength of the [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent) (if the incumbent is running for re-election), the strength of the candidates, and the partisan leanings of the state (reflected in part by the state's [Cook Partisan Voting Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Partisan_Voting_Index) rating). The predictions assigned ratings to each seat, with the rating indicating a party's predicted advantage in winning that seat.

Most election predictors use:

- "tossup": no advantage

- "tilt" (used by some predictors): advantage that is not quite as strong as "lean"

- "lean": slight advantage

- "likely": significant, but surmountable, advantage

- "safe" or "solid": near-certain chance of victory

[Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_Jersey) [Phil Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Murphy) was elected to a second term in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_New_Jersey_gubernatorial_election) with 51.2% of the vote. He was [term-limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits_in_the_United_States) by the [New Jersey Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Constitution) and couldn't seek re-election for a third consecutive term. [Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey) [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Jersey_City,_New_Jersey) [Steven Fulop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Fulop), former [New Jersey Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Senate) president [Stephen Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Sweeney), U.S. Representatives [Josh Gottheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Gottheimer) and [Mikie Sherrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikie_Sherrill), and [Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey) [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Newark,_New_Jersey) [Ras Baraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Baraka) ran for the Democratic nomination.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Sherrill-19] Ultimately, Sherrill won the Democratic nomination on June 10. Former state Assemblyman [Jack Ciattarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Ciattarelli), who was the Republican nominee in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_New_Jersey_gubernatorial_election), was the Republican nominee.

Sherrill won the election, becoming the second female governor of New Jersey when several media outlets called the race for her over Ciattarelli on November 4, 2025.[^cite_note-\:0-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In addition, she won three consecutive terms for her party for the first time in the state since 1961.[^cite_note-\:0-20] Sherrill outperformed expectations after Republicans outperformed in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_New_Jersey_gubernatorial_election) & [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_New_Jersey), winning by approximately 14 points against Ciattarelli.[^cite_note-23]

Spanberger won the election by over 15 points, becoming the first [female governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_female_governors_in_the_United_States) in Virginia's history.[^cite_note-28]
