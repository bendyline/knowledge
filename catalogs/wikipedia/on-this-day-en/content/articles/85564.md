The **Isle of Oléron** or **Oléron Island** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *île d'Oléron* [\[il dɔleʁɔ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French), or simply *Oléron* [\[ɔleʁɔ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Poitevin-Saintongese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poitevin-Saintongese): *ile d'Olerun* or *ilâte d'Olerun*; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Uliaros insula* [\[uliˈaːros ˈinsula\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin)) is an island off the [Atlantic](698.md) coast of France (due west of [Rochefort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochefort,_Nouvelle-Aquitaine)), on the southern side of the [Pertuis d'Antioche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertuis_d'Antioche) strait.

1686 map of Oléron

1686 map of Oléron

It is the second largest island of [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France), after [Corsica](5714828.md), with a length of 30 km (19 mi) and a width of 8 km (5 mi). It has an area of 174 km<sup>2</sup> (67 sq. mi.) and more than 21,000 permanent inhabitants.

## History

Oléron has been known since the 1st century, where [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) refers to it in his [Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_\(Pliny\)) as Uliaros (*"in aquitanico sinu Vliaros")*.[^cite_note-1]



Château Fournier, located in Saint-Georges d'Oléron, bears witness to the island's wine-growing past. Today, it has been transformed into a retirement home.

Towards the end of the 3rd century, the Roman emperor [Probus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probus_\(emperor\)) extended the privilege of owning vineyards and producing wine to all Gauls, and this led to a culture of winemaking developing on the island.[^cite_note-2] This lasted until the end of the 19th century, when [the arrival of phylloxera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_French_Wine_Blight) decimated almost all the vines. The vineyards did not recover, and grape production today is mainly for Cognac *bois ordinaires*.[^cite_note-3]

In the 7th and 8th centuries, the island, along with [Ré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9), formed the *Vacetae Insulae* or Vacetian Islands, according to the *[Cosmographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aethicus_Ister)*.[^cite_note-4] Vaceti was another name for the [Vascones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascones); the reference is seen as evidence of Basque (Gascon) control of the islands by that time.

It was at Oléron in about 1152 to 1160 that [Eleanor of Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_of_Aquitaine) introduced the first ['maritime' or 'admiralty' laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_law) in that part of the world: the [Rolls of Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls_of_Ol%C3%A9ron). In 1306, [Edward I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) granted the island to his son, [Edward II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_II), as part of the [Duchy of Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Aquitaine).

In 1793, during the French Revolution, the villages of the isle of Oléron were renamed. The Château d'Oléron became "Equality", Saint-Trojan became "La Montagne", Dolus became "Sans-Culotte", Saint-Pierre became "La Fraternité" and Saint-Georges became "L'Unité". The overall territory of Oléron became **the island of Liberty**.[^cite_note-5] A few years later, the original names were given back to the villages and towns.

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the island was occupied by German forces and fortified. It was liberated by [Free French Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_Forces) in an [amphibious assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault) code-named [Operation Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royan_pocket) on 29 April 1945. The [French cruiser *Duquesne*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_cruiser_Duquesne_\(1925\)) fired 550 heavy shells at the German artillery batteries, and the garrison surrendered on the following day.[^cite_note-6]

The isle of Oléron is known today for the quality of its [oyster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster) production.[^cite_note-7]

On 5 November 2025, a driver [deliberately drove](44972591.md) into a crowd of pedestrians and cyclists while shouting "[Allahu Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takbir)" in [Dolus-d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolus-d'Ol%C3%A9ron), injuring at least five people, including two critically.[^cite_note-8]

## Geography and climate



Map of Oléron

The island has an area of about 174 km<sup>2</sup> (67 sq mi). It is a fertile and well cultivated island on the Atlantic coast of France, that is on the [Bay of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay).

The climate is generally mild (maritime temperate) with sufficient but not excessive rainfall, but with probably from 3 to 15 days of intense heat in the summer months of July and August, mostly grouped.[^cite_note-oleron_tourist_office-9]

Oléron is located on the sunniest part of the French Atlantic coast, receiving more than 2100 hours of sunshine per year.[^cite_note-10]

## Administration

Administratively, the island belongs to the [Charente-Maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charente-Maritime) *[département](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9partement)*, in the [Nouvelle-Aquitaine](45092904.md) *[région](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9gion)*. The island is divided into 8 [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_in_France):

- [La Brée-les-Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Br%C3%A9e-les-Bains)

- [Le Château-d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Ch%C3%A2teau-d'Ol%C3%A9ron)

- [Dolus-d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolus-d'Ol%C3%A9ron)

- [Saint-Denis-d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Denis-d'Ol%C3%A9ron)

- [Saint-Georges-d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Georges-d'Ol%C3%A9ron) (includes [Boyardville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyardville))

- [Saint-Pierre d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Pierre_d'Ol%C3%A9ron)

- [Saint-Trojan-les-Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Trojan-les-Bains)

The island has about 22,000 inhabitants.

## Transportation



Île d'Oléron bridge, seen from Le Château d'Oléron

Since 1966, the island has been connected to the mainland by a road bridge, the [Oléron bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ol%C3%A9ron_bridge?action=edit\&redlink=1). With a length of 2,862 m (9,390 ft) between [abutments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abutment), it was the [longest bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bridges_by_length) in France at the time of construction. It is now the third one, after the [Saint-Nazaire bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Nazaire_Bridge) and the [Île de Ré bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_de_R%C3%A9_bridge). It has been toll-free since 1991.

To get to the island, it is possible to arrive at the stations of [Surgères](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surg%C3%A8res), [Saintes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saintes,_Charente-Maritime) or Rochefort, then take the bus. On the island itself, the easiest way to get around is by car or by bicycle. During the last ten years, a network of 110 kilometers (68 miles) of bicycle paths have been built. These bicycle lanes are mostly car-free.

## Tourism



[Le Château-d'Oléron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Ch%C3%A2teau-d'Ol%C3%A9ron), 1703 military mock-up.

As a large Atlantic island only 3 kilometres (2 miles) off the Aquitanian coast of France, Oléron is a popular tourist destination. Several companies operate boat trips from the towns of Boyardville and Saint-Denis to the nearby [Île d'Aix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_d'Aix), [La Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rochelle), and past the [Fort Boyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Boyard_\(fortification\)). The port towns are frequently visited by tourists, especially the village of La Cotiniere. This village is the base for a hundred trawlers who sell their fish every day at 5 am and 4 pm. La Cotiniere was the first fishing port in the department of Charente-Maritime, and the 8th of France.[^cite_note-14]
