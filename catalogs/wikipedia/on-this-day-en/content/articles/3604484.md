**Marib** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مَأْرِب, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Maʾrib*; [Old South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_South_Arabian_script): **𐩣𐩧𐩨/𐩣𐩧𐩺𐩨** *Mryb/Mrb*) is the capital city of [Marib Governorate](3604391.md), Yemen. It was the capital of the ancient kingdom of *[Sabaʾ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba%E2%80%99)* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): سَبَأ),[^cite_note-Cite_quran|27|6|e=93|s=ns-1][^cite_note-Cite_quran|34|15|e=18|s=ns-2] which some scholars believe to be the ancient [Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba) of [biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical) fame.[^cite_note-Sheba-01-3] It is about 120 kilometres (75 miles) east of Yemen's modern capital, [Sanaa](18691665.md), and is in the region of the [Sarawat Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawat_Mountains).[^cite_note-Burrows2010-4] In 2005 it had a population of 16,794. However, in 2021, it had absorbed close to a million refugees fleeing the [Yemeni Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-\:0-5]

The ancient city of Mirwah is one of the most important places for [Arabian archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_the_Arabian_Peninsula), and in 2023, was added by the [UNESCO](21786641.md) to its [World Heritage Site List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site_List), as one of the [Landmarks of the Ancient Kingdom of Saba, Marib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmarks_of_the_Ancient_Kingdom_of_Saba,_Marib).

## History

### Ancient

Main article: [Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba)



The [Barran Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barran_Temple), a relic of the [Sabaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans) era



Stone-carved female figure wearing a strap and a necklace, 3rd–2nd millennium BC, [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), 27.5 x 14.3 x 14.3 cm, in the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art) (New York City)

The Sabaean kingdom was based around Marib, with territory in northern Yemen. The Sabaean kings made their capital at Marib, and built great irrigation works such as the [Marib Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marib_Dam), whose ruins are still visible. The Marib Dam supported a flourishing culture for more than a thousand years. They also built castles and temples in the area, including, most notably the temples of Almaqah at [Awwam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awwam) and [Barran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barran_Temple). Saba was known for dealing in the lucrative [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense) and [myrrh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrrh) trade.[^cite_note-Sheba-01-3][^cite_note-6]

In 25 BC, [Aelius Gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelius_Gallus) of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) led [an expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romans_in_Arabia#Aelius_Gallus_expedition) to Marib, laying siege to the city. He suffered major losses and was forced to retreat to [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_\(Roman_province\)).[^cite_note-7]

### 20th century

#### Old city

The site of ancient Marib was largely abandoned during the 20th century. Although a small village remains, the multi-story [mud-brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud-brick) buildings of the historic city are largely in [ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins). The [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_history) town of Marib is about 3.5 kilometres (2.2 miles) north of the center of the ancient city.

#### Modern Marib Dam

In 1982, floods ravaged the country. In response, the [President of the U.A.E.](9480572.md), [Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh) [Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayed_bin_Sultan_Al_Nahyan), financed the construction of the [current dam of Marib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marib_Dam#Current_dam) in 1984.[^cite_note-GulfNews2015Yemen-8][^cite_note-National2015Ma’ribDam-9][^cite_note-KhaleejTimes2013-10] The Sheikh himself was reportedly descended from people who migrated from the area of Marib to what is now the [U.A.E.](69328.md)[^cite_note-Burrows2010-4]

### Yemeni Civil War (2014–present)

During the [Yemeni civil war](46215853.md), Marib and the surrounding [Marib Governorate](3604391.md) came under attack by the [Houthis](17240332.md) movement rebelling against the government of [Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdrabbuh_Mansour_Hadi). The tribes of Marib repelled the Houthis with help from the [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen).

According to the [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md)-based *[The National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_\(Abu_Dhabi\))* newspaper, "With 80 per cent of the province's population Sunni and only one of the five main tribes supportive of the Zaidi Shiite Houthis, tribal fighters managed to repel the attack. As a result, the Houthis control only about 20 per cent of Marib and the oil fields remained under Hadi's control. Many of the tribes in Marib, and in neighbouring al-Jawf and Shabwa provinces, are loyal to [Al-Islah Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Islah_\(Yemen\)). According to two tribal chiefs, there are 8,000 Yemeni forces and tribal fighters based in Marib united against the Houthis. Some are directly loyal to President Hadi, others to Saudi Arabia, and a large number to the [al-Islah Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Islah_\(Yemen\)), an Islamist group. The entire First Armoured Brigade, considered a military wing of al-Islah, based in Sanaa, was transferred to Marib in 2014 to defend the province. Other sections of Yemen's military remained loyal to Ali Abdullah Saleh, the former president overthrown by Arab Spring protests who has now sided with the Houthis against Hadi. After the Saudi-led coalition joined the war in March 2015 and drove the Houthis from most of Yemen's southern provinces in July 2015, the focus shifted to Marib, known as the gateway to Sana'a, where the strong support base made it a natural location for an attack in the north.[^cite_note-TN_2015_Ma’rib-11]

According to [Al-Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic), by 7 April 2015, Houthi forces had been expelled from the majority of [Marib Governorate](3604391.md) by Saudi-backed tribesmen. The governor of Marib told Al-Jazeera that forces allied to President Abd-Rabbu Mansour Hadi and the Gulf coalition were "perusing the last pockets of Houthis" in the province.[^cite_note-12]

The city of Marib is just 173 kilometres (107 miles) from the capital, and the province adjoins the predominantly Sunni provinces of Al Jawf, Al-Baydha and Shabwa, where the Houthis' control is unlikely to hold if attacked. In particular, Al-Jawf to the north would provide a route towards the Houthi's Saada stronghold. The coalition began moving supplies to Marib in March 2015, using land routes from Saudi Arabia through Hadramout and Shabwa provinces. In August 2015, coalition forces started flying more reinforcements to Marib using a small airport in the tiny town of Safer, 60 km east of Marib city. Loyalist military sources said further reinforcements including tanks, armoured vehicles, rocket launchers and Apache helicopters arrived August 2015. The town serves as a base for the state-run [Safer Exploration and Production Operations Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_Oil_and_Gas_Corporation) and other foreign companies working in Yemen's vital energy sector. The main gas pipeline south also runs through the town, which is controlled by the pro-Hadi military commander Abdullah Al-Shaddadi. The nearest Houthi presence was in Baihan in Shabwa province, 50 kilometres (31 miles) away."[^cite_note-WP_2015_Ma’rib-13][^cite_note-TN_2015_Ma’rib-11]

On 4 September 2015, 52 [Emirati](69328.md), ten [Saudi](349303.md), five Bahraini servicemen of the [Arab coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) and scores of pro-Hadi Yemeni soldiers were killed by a Houthi [ballistic missile attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2015_Marib_Tochka_missile_attack) against a military base in Safer, Marib.[^cite_note-BBC_News-14][^cite_note-Melissa_Gray,_CNN-15]

More than 100 Yemeni government soldiers were killed in a [drone](337755.md) and [missile](38025.md) [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2020_Marib_attack) on a [military camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_camp) near Marib on 18 January 2020, leaving over a hundred more soldiers injured and killing at least 5 civilians. The [Houthis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement) were suspected and accused of carrying out the attack, although they denied responsibility.[^cite_note-reuters-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-bbc-18][^cite_note-voa-19][^cite_note-20]

Five foreign [mine clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_clearance) experts were killed in an explosion inside the Saudi Project for Landmine Clearance MASAM on 20 January 2019. One of the experts was a resident of South Africa of British descent. Other members of the team were from [Colombia](5222.md) and unspecified countries in Africa.[^cite_note-21]

On 27 May 2020, a Houthi missile attack targeted the headquarters of the army command of the Saudi-backed government in Marib Governorate killing eight soldiers including the Chief of Staff of the [Republic of Yemen Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Yemen_Armed_Forces), Lt. Gen. [Sagheer bin Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagheer_Hamoud_Aziz)'s son and nephew.[^cite_note-22]

On 22 February 2021, the Houthis launched [an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marib) on Marib Governorate in late February with the aim of capturing Marib city. After making steady advances in the governorate, the Houthis launched a three direction assault on the city with occasional ballistic strikes. According to the [International Organization for Migration](36893.md) (IOM), over 140,000 displaced refugees from western Marib fled fearing the Houthis' advance.[^cite_note-23]

On 30 November 2021, Marib was described as "the city at the heart of Yemen's dirty war" by the [BBC News](1139893.md). It has absorbed close to a million refugees fleeing the war in other parts of Yemen.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

## Oil refinery

The [Yemen Oil Refining Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_Oil_and_Gas_Corporation) opened a [refinery](195137.md) in Marib in 1986, which produces 10,000 barrels (1,600 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil per day (2009). In November 2009, the company announced an agreement with [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea)'s Shinhan to expand and upgrade the refinery to produce 25,000 barrels (4,000 m<sup>3</sup>)/day.[^cite_note-24]

Marib is the start of the [Marib-Ra's Isa oil pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marib-Ra's_Isa_oil_pipeline) (438 km (272 mi)), with a capacity of 200,000 barrels (32,000 m<sup>3</sup>) per day. In addition, oil derivatives markets have witnessed relative stability in Marib, as fuel products are produced and refined locally and cover local needs in the governorate. This stands in sharp contrast to other areas around Yemen which at times suffer from a shortage of oil derivatives and wild price fluctuations. In Marib, petrol and cooking gas are almost always available at official prices, while gas tankers leave the governorate to the capital Sana’a and other governorates on a daily basis.[^cite_note-25]

The kingdom of Saba is known to have existed in the region of Yemen. By 1000 BC caravan trains of camels journeyed from [Oman](22316.md) in south-east [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) to the Mediterranean. As the [camel drivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_driver) passed through the [deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) of Yemen, experts believe that many of them would have called in at Marib. Dating from at least 1050 BC, and now barren and dry, Marib was then a lush [oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis) teeming with [palm trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_tree) and [exotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species) plants. Ideally placed, it was situated on the trade routes and with a unique dam of vast proportions. It was also one of only two main sources of [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense) (the other being East Africa), so Saba had a virtual monopoly. Marib's wealth accumulated to such an extent that the city became a byword for riches beyond belief throughout the Arab world. Its people, the [Sabeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans) – a group whose name bears the same etymological root as Saba – lived in [South Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia) between the tenth and sixth centuries BC. Their main temple – [Mahram Bilqis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahram_Bilqis), or temple of the moon god (situated about three miles (5 km) from the capital city of Marib) – was so famous that it remained [sacred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred) even after the collapse of the Sabean civilisation in the sixth century BC – caused by the rerouting of the spice trail. By that point the dam, now in a poor state of repair, was finally breached. The [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) system was lost, the people abandoned the site within a year or so, and the temple fell into disrepair and was eventually covered by sand. Saba was known by the [Hebrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrews) as Sheba *\[Note that the collapse of the dam was actually in 575 C.E., as shown in the timeline in the same article in the History Files, and attested by MacCulloch (2009)].*
