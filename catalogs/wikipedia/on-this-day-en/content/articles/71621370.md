The **Government of National Stability** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حكومة الاستقرار الوطني, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Ḥukūmat al-istiqrār al-waṭanī*) is a [rival government](80144294.md) and [quasi-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-state) that controls eastern [Libya](17633.md).[^cite_note-6] It was formed on 3 March 2022, is led by [Osama Hammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_Hammad) and supported by the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)) and the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md). Since its inception, the government has claimed power over Libya in [competition](80144294.md) with the Tripoli-based [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) led by [Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Dbeibeh), with the [Libyan Political Dialogue Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Political_Dialogue_Forum) coordinating the ceasefire agreement.

The Government of National Stability is a nominal civilian government in control of eastern Libya. However, *[de facto](43594.md)* control in this area is held by the Libyan National Army and its commander [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md), who controls the government and the region through a [military dictatorship](146728.md).[^cite_note-Brooke-Holland-7][^cite_note-Oxford-8]

As of May 2025 the [United Nations](31769.md) recognises the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) led by [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Libya) [Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Dbeibeh) as the legitimate government of Libya.[^cite_note-9]

## History

On 21 September 2021, the House of Representatives (HoR), which controls eastern Libya, passed a no-confidence motion against the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)).[^cite_note-10]

On 10 February 2022, the House of Representatives selected [Fathi Bashagha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathi_Bashagha) as Prime Minister-designate, after HoR Speaker [Aguila Saleh Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguila_Saleh_Issa) announced the only other candidate, Khalid Al-Baybas, withdrew his candidacy.[^cite_note-\:1-11] However, Al-Baybas denied withdrawing from the race.[^cite_note-12] Prime Minister of the Government of National Unity [Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Dbeibeh) rejected Bashagha's appointment as Prime Minister, stating that he will only hand over power after a national election.[^cite_note-\:1-11] [LNA](35457665.md) leader [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) welcomed Bashagha's appointment.[^cite_note-13]

On 1 March, the House of Representatives voted to give confidence to Bashagha's Government of National Stability.[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-15] According to HoR Speaker Saleh, 92 out of 101 attending members voted for the new government.[^cite_note-\:0-14] The [High Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_of_State_\(Libya\)) rejected "unilateral" steps by the HoR and regards the HoR decision to grant confidence to a new government a violation of the Libyan Political Agreement.[^cite_note-16] The Government of National Unity refused to transfer powers to the Bashagha government.[^cite_note-17] The [United Nations](31769.md) has voiced concerns over the vote due to reports on lack of transparency and procedure, and acts of intimidation prior to the HoR session.[^cite_note-18]

Bashagha and his cabinet were sworn in at the House of Representatives headquarters in [Tobruk](405871.md) on 3 March.[^cite_note-\:22-19][^cite_note-20]

In mid-2022, the [two governments](80144294.md) functioned in parallel, holding [dual power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_power).[^cite_note-21]

There were [clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Tripoli_clashes) between supporters of the two governments starting in May 2022,[^cite_note-22] which escalated on 27 August 2022.[^cite_note-23]

In May 2023 Bashagha was replaced by Finance Minister [Osama Hamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_Hamada), citing poor performance.[^cite_note-24]

A coalition of lawmakers is putting pressure on the House of Representatives in November 2025 to remove President Aguila Saleh from his position as president.[^cite_note-25]
