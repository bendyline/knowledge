The **Government of Pakistan**[^cite_note-2] (constitutionally known as the **Federal Government**[^cite_note-3] and commonly the **Centre**[^cite_note-4]) is the [national authority](12229.md) of the [Islamic Republic of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Pakistan), a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic) [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic) located in [South Asia](21566765.md), consisting of [four provinces](446017.md) and [one federal territory](15294.md). The territories of [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md) and [Azad Kashmir](2745.md) are also considered and administered as part of the country, but have separate systems and are not part of the federation.[^cite_note-5]

Under the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan), there are three primary branches of a government: *the legislative*, whose powers are vested in a [bicameral Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan); *the executive*, consisting of the [president](419460.md), aided by the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Pakistan) which is headed by the [prime minister](461896.md); and *the judiciary*, with the [Supreme Court](843857.md).[^cite_note-gop1-6]

Effecting the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system) for governing the state, the government is mainly composed of the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch), [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_branch), and [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_branch) branches, in which all powers are vested by the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) in the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan), the prime minister and the [Supreme Court](843857.md).[^cite_note-Government_of_Pakistan-7] The powers and duties of these branches are further defined by acts and amendments of the Parliament, including the creation of executive institutions, departments and courts inferior to the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-Government_of_Pakistan-7] By constitutional powers, the president promulgates ordinances and passes bills.

The president acts as the ceremonial [figurehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) while the [people-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Pakistan) prime minister acts as the [chief executive](52234.md) (of the executive branch) and is responsible for running the federal government. There is a bicameral Parliament with the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan) as a [Lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) and the [Senate](761025.md) as an upper house. The most influential officials in the Government of Pakistan are considered to be the [Federal Secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Secretary), who are the highest ranking [bureaucrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Services_of_Pakistan) in the country and run cabinet-level ministries and divisions. The judicial branch systematically contains an apex [Supreme Court](843857.md), [Federal Shariat Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Shariat_Court), [High courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_Pakistan) of [five provinces](446017.md), district, [anti-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti_Terrorism_Court_of_Pakistan), and the green courts; all inferior to the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-Government_of_Pakistan-7]

The full name of the country is the *Islamic Republic of Pakistan*. No other name appears in the Constitution, and this is the name that appears on money, in treaties, and in legal cases. The "Pakistan Government" or "Government of Pakistan" are often used in official documents representing the federal government collectively.[^cite_note-Government_of_Pakistan-7] Also, the terms "Federal" and "National" in government institutions or program names generally indicate affiliation with the federal government. As the [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government) is in [Islamabad](51153.md), "Islamabad" is commonly used as a [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonym) for the federal government.[^cite_note-Government_of_Pakistan-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Federal law and Constitution

The [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) established and constituted the federal government of [four provinces](446017.md) of federation of nation-state, known as [State of Pakistan](23235.md). The [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) reads as:

> The Federal Government is Subject to the Constitution. The executive authority of the Federation shall be exercised in the name of the President by the Federal Government, consisting of the Prime Minister and the (Federal) Ministers, which shall act through the Prime Minister, who shall be the chief executive of the Federation.\
> In the performance of his functions under the Constitution, the Prime Minister may act either directly or through the (Federal) Ministers.

— [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan): Part III: The Federation of Pakistan— Chapter 3: The Federal Government, Article 196–197, source[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Pakistan-10]

The basic [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(common_law\)) and [criminal laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_in_Pakistan) governing the citizens of Pakistan are set down in major parliamentary legislation (a term [inherited from the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_heritage_of_Pakistan) [United Kingdom](31717.md)), such as the [Exit Control List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_Control_List), the [Pakistan Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Penal_Code), and the now repealed [Frontier Crimes Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Crimes_Regulations).[^cite_note-11] By the [Article 246th](http://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/constitution/part12.ch3.html) and [Article 247th](http://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/constitution/part12.ch3.html) to the constitution, the Islamic *[Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga)* (or [*Panchayat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj)) system has become an institution for local governance.[^cite_note-Chapter_3:_Tribal_Areas.-12][^cite_note-13] The 1950s reforms in the government administration, the constitutional law and jurisprudence in Pakistan have been greatly influenced by the United States Of America ' legal system. Since the 1970s, the traditional [jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga)-based law has also been in place in a few areas, and has influenced the country's judicial development.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Branches of government

### Legislative branch

Main article: [Parliament of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan)

The legislative branch, being a bicameral, has two houses, which combined are known as the Parliament of Pakistan ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu): مجلسِ شوریٰ پاکستان, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language): [Consultative Council of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan):

- The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan), presided by [Speaker of the National Assembly of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Pakistan), deputed by the [Deputy Speaker of the National Assembly of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Speaker_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Pakistan), is the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) and has 336 members (according to new census and emerging of FATA). 266 general members are elected directly by the people, while 60 seats are reserved for women and 10 seats for religious minorities.

- The [Senate](761025.md), presided by [Chairman of the Senate of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Senate_of_Pakistan), deputed by [Deputy Chairman of the Senate of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chairman_of_the_Senate_of_Pakistan). is the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) and has 96 senators elected indirectly by members of respective provincial assemblies for the term of six years.

The Parliament enjoys [parliamentary supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_sovereignty). All the Cabinet ministers, as well as the prime minister, must be members of Parliament (MPs), according to the constitution. The prime minister and the Cabinet ministers are jointly accountable to the Parliament. If there is a policy failure or lapse on the part of the government, all the members of the cabinet are jointly responsible. If a [vote of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no_confidence) is passed against the government, then the government collapses and a new one must be formed.

### Executive branch

By general definition, the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch) of government is the one that has sole [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority) and [responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_responsibility) for the daily administration of the [state bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Civil_Services). The division of power into separate branches of government is central to the republican idea of the [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers). The separation of powers system is designed to distribute authority away from the executive branch – an attempt to preserve individual liberty in response to tyrannical leadership throughout history.

#### Prime Minister and Cabinet

The [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md) ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu): وزیراعظم; lit: *Wazir-e- Azam*), is the executive [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_Government) of Pakistan, constitutionally designated as the [Chief Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Executive) (CE).[^cite_note-Govt._Pakistan-16] after the retirement of the Prime Minister, an interim (or Caretaker) Prime Minister is appointed by the president of Pakistan after consulting incumbent Prime Minister and the [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Pakistan\)) of the national assembly.

The Prime Minister must be a Muslim citizen of Pakistan above 25 years of age. There have been 20 different [Prime Ministers of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Pakistan), with 24 different tenures, where only three people ([Benazir Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto), [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif), and [Shehbaz Sharif](6502561.md)) to become Prime Minister of Pakistan more than once. None of them complete their 5 years tenure. Pakistan also has had 4 [Caretaker Prime Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caretaker_Prime_Minister_of_Pakistan).

The Prime Minister makes key appointments on various important positions, including;

- The [federal secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Secretary) as head of cabinet- level ministries

- The [chief secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Secretary_\(Pakistan\)) of the provinces

- Key administrative and military personnel in the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md)

- The chairmen of large public sector organisations and corporations such as [NHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_Authority), [TCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_Corporation_of_Pakistan), [PNSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_National_Shipping_Corporation), etc.

- The chairmen and other members of the federal commissions and public institutions

- Ambassadors and High Commissioners to other countries

The [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Pakistan) can have a maximum of 11 percent (50 members including the Prime Minister) of the total strength of the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-17] Each Cabinet member must be a member of Parliament (MP).[^cite_note-Information_and_Mass-Media_Broadcasting_Ministry_of_Pakistan-18] The Minister can be from the either house of the Parliament, but maximum 1/4th of the Ministers can be from the Senate. The [Cabinet Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Pakistan) chair the Cabinet and are further assisted by the [Cabinet Secretary of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_of_Pakistan), whose appointment comes from the [Civil Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Superior_Services_of_Pakistan) of Pakistan. Other Ministers are [Ministers of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministers_of_State), junior members who report directly to one of the Cabinet Ministers, often overseeing a specific aspect of government.[^cite_note-Information_and_Mass-Media_Broadcasting_Ministry_of_Pakistan-18]

Once appointed by the prime minister, all Cabinet Ministers are officially confirmed to their appointment offices by the president in a special oath of ceremony.[^cite_note-Information_and_Mass-Media_Broadcasting_Ministry_of_Pakistan-18][^cite_note-19]

The [president of Pakistan](419460.md), officially the **President of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan**, is the ceremonial [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) of [Pakistan](23235.md) and the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Article_411-21]

The office of president was created upon the [proclamation of Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1956) on 23 March 1956. The then serving [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Pakistan), [Major-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major-General) [Iskander Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander_Mirza), assumed office as the first president. Following the [1958 *coup d'etat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Pakistani_coup_d'etat), the office of [prime minister](461896.md) was abolished, leaving the Presidency as the most powerful office in the country. This position was further strengthened when the [1962 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1962) was adopted. It turned Pakistan into a [Presidential Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Republic), giving all executive powers to the president. In 1973, the [new Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) established [Parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy) and reduced president's role to a ceremonial one. Nevertheless, the [military takeover in 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fairplay) reversed the changes. The [8th Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan) turned Pakistan into a [semi-presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_republic) and in the period between 1985 and 2010, the executive power was shared by president and prime minister. The [18th Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighteenth_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan) in 2010 restored [Parliamentary Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Democracy) in the country, and reduced presidency to a ceremonial position.[^cite_note-22]

The constitution prohibits the president from directly running the government.[^cite_note-23] Instead, the executive power is exercised on his behalf by the [prime minister](461896.md) who keeps him informed on all matters of internal and [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_Pakistan), as well as all legislative proposals.[^cite_note-24] The Constitution however, vests the president with the powers of granting [pardons, reprieves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemency), and the [control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military) over the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee); however, all appointments at higher commands of the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_military) must be made by the president on a "required and necessary" basis, upon [consultation and approval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) from the prime minister.[^cite_note-25]

The president is indirectly elected by the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_\(Pakistan\)) for a five-year term. The Constitution requires the president to be a "[Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) of not less than forty five (45) years of age". The president resides in an estate in [Islamabad](51153.md) known as [Aiwan-e-Sadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiwan-e-Sadar) (President's House). In his absence, the [chairman of Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Senate_of_Pakistan) exercises the responsibilities of the post, until the actual president resumes office, or the next office holder is [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Pakistan).

There have been a total of [13 presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_Pakistan). The first president was [Iskander Ali Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander_Ali_Mirza) who entered office on 23 March 1956. The current office holder is [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md), who took charge on 10 March 2024, following his victory in the [2023 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_presidential_election).[^cite_note-26]

### Judicial branch

Main article: [Judiciary of Pakistan](4988685.md)

Pakistan's independent judicial system began under the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), and its concepts and procedures resemble those of [Anglo-Saxon countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_countries). Institutional and judicial procedures were later changed, in 1950s, under the influence of American legal system to remove the fundamental rights problems.[^cite_note-Chapter_3:_Tribal_Areas.-12] The judiciary consists of the [Supreme Court of Pakistan](843857.md), Provincial [High Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_Pakistan), [District Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Courts_of_Pakistan), [Anti-terrorism courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti_Terrorism_Court_of_Pakistan), [*Sharia* courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Shariat_Court), and Environmental courts all over the country; Supreme Court being the superior court.[^cite_note-Government_of_Pakistan-7] The Supreme Court of Pakistan consists of a Chief Justice, and [Senior Justices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Justices_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_Pakistan) appointed by the president after consultation with the [Chief Justice of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Pakistan). The Constitution does not fix the number of justices of the Supreme Court, though it can be fixed by Parliament through an act signed by the president.[^cite_note-Part_VII:_The_Judicature-27]

#### Judicature transfer

The Constitution grants powers to the Supreme Court to make judicature transfers.[^cite_note-Part_VII:_The_Judicature-27] Although the proceedings in the Supreme Court arise out of the judgement or orders made by the subordinate courts, the Supreme Court reserves the right to transfer any case, appeal or proceedings pending before any High Court to any other High Court.[^cite_note-Part_VII:_The_Judicature-27]

#### Supreme Judicial Council

Misconduct of judges is highly intolerable as is mentioned in the constitution. Under the mainframe of the [Supreme Judicial Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Judicial_Council_of_Pakistan) [Article 209](http://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/constitution/part7.ch4.html) an inquiry into the capacity or conduct of a Judge, who is a member of the council, may be conducted.

## Civil service

Main articles: [Central Superior Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Superior_Services) and [Pakistan Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Administrative_Service)

The civil service of Pakistan is the permanent bureaucracy of the Government of Pakistan. The civil servants are the permanent officials of the government, occupying a respected image in the [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_society). Civil servants come from different cadres (e.g. [Pakistan Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Administrative_Service), [Police Service of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Service_of_Pakistan) etc.) after passing the [CSS examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Superior_Services). Not all the employees of the Government of Pakistan are civil servants; other employees of the Government of Pakistan come from the scientific institutions, state-owned corporations and commissioned military science circles.

In the [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy), the ultimate responsibility for running the administration rests with the elected representatives of the people who are the ministers. These ministers are accountable to the legislatures which are also elected by the people on the basis of universal adult suffrage. The cabinet and its ministers are expected to lay down the policy guidelines, and the civil servants are responsible for implementing and enforcing it.

### Federal secretaries

Main article: [Federal Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Secretary)

The [federal secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Secretary) are the most senior, experienced, and capable officials in the country. Each ministry/division has its Secretary to oversee and enforce the public policy matters.

The secretaries, who are basic pay scale [(BPS)-22 grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_22) officers, are largely considered to be the most powerful officials in the country.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Due to the importance of their respective assignments, there are twelve specific federal secretaries which are considered to be the most vital in the Government of Pakistan. These include the [Secretary Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Establishment_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for civil service matters), [Secretary Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for trade), [Secretary Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for Cabinet Division), [Secretary to the Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Secretary_to_the_Prime_Minister_of_Pakistan) (responsible for Prime Minister's Office), [Secretary Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for law and order), [Secretary Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for the country's treasury), [Secretary Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for foreign relations), [Secretary Maritime Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for ports and shipping), [Secretary Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for the electricity and power sector), [Secretary Planning and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_and_Development_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for development projects), [Secretary Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_Secretary_of_Pakistan) (responsible for the petroleum sector) and [Secretary Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Secretary_of_Industries_and_Production) (responsible for industrial development).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Management of major crisis situations in the country and coordination of activities of the various Ministries in such situations are the functions of the [Cabinet Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretariat_\(Pakistan\)). Appointment for the chairman of the [FPSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Public_Service_Commission), the prestigious body responsible for the recruitment of elite bureaucrats, is made by the president after consulting the prime minister, according to Article 242 of the Constitution.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-32]

## Elections and voting system

Main articles: [Elections in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Pakistan), [Politics of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Pakistan), and [Election Commission of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_Pakistan)

Since 1947, Pakistan has an asymmetric [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government), with elected officials at the national (federal), provincial, tribal, and local levels. Constitution has set the limit of government for five years, but if a [Vote of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no_confidence) movements takes place in the parliament (and prelude of movements are proved at the Judicial branch), the government falls and immediately replaced with [caretaker government](240683.md) initiated by the president (consultation of Prime Minister also required to make such move), in regards to Article 58 of the constitution.[^cite_note-58_Dissolution_of_the_National_Assembly.-33]

There has been four times that the [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan) has been in effect, and controversially [approved by the supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_necessity).[^cite_note-Information_and_Mass-Media_Broadcasting_Ministry_of_Pakistan-18] Through a general election where the leader of the majority winning party is selected to be the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-Information_and_Mass-Media_Broadcasting_Ministry_of_Pakistan-18] All members of the federal legislature, the Parliament, are directly elected. Elections in Pakistan take place every five years by universal adult suffrage.[^cite_note-Information_and_Mass-Media_Broadcasting_Ministry_of_Pakistan-18]

## Administration and governments

### Provincial and Local governments

Main articles: [List of provincial governments of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_governments_of_Pakistan), [Local government in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Pakistan), and [Administrative units of Pakistan](446017.md)

There are four [provincial governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Governments_of_Pakistan) that rule the four provinces of the state. The [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_in_Pakistan) heads the provincial government. All [provincial assemblies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Balochistan) are [unicameral](204304.md), elected for five years.[^cite_note-Chapter_3:_The_Provincial_Governments-34] The [Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Pakistan) appointed by President after consulting the Prime minister, act only as representatives of federal government in the province and do not have any part in running the government.

The provincial governments tend to have the greatest influence over most [Pakistanis](28171281.md)' daily lives. The [Local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Pakistan) functions at the basic level.[^cite_note-National_Bureau_of_Reconstruction-35] It is the third level of government, consisting *[Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga)* in rural tribal areas.[^cite_note-Part_XII:_Miscellaneous-36]

### Taxation and budget

Main articles: [Taxation in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Pakistan), [Tax on cash withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_on_cash_withdrawal), and [Foreign trade of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_trade_of_Pakistan)

Pakistan has a complex taxation system of more than 70 unique [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax) administered by at least 37 tax collection institutions of the Government of Pakistan.[^cite_note-Horrigan_2010-09-26-37] Taxation is a debated and controversial issue in public and political science circle of the country, and according to the [International Development Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Development_Committee), Pakistan had a lower-than-average tax take.[^cite_note-BBC_Pakistan_Bureau-38] Only 0.57% of Pakistanis, or 768,000 people out of a population of 190 million pay income tax.[^cite_note-BBC_Pakistan_Bureau-38]

The [Finance Minister of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Minister_of_Pakistan) presents the annual federal budget in the Parliament in the midst of the year, and it has to be passed by both houses of the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-39] The budget is preceded by an economic survey which outlines the broad direction of the budget and the economic performance of the country for the outgoing financial fiscal year.[^cite_note-geo-40]

### National Finance Commission program overview

Main article: [National Finance Commission Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Finance_Commission_Award)

Constituted under the [Article 160](http://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/constitution/part6.ch1.html) of the [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) by the Constitution, the [National Finance Commission Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Finance_Commission_Award) (NFC) program is a series of planned [economic programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_reforms_in_Pakistan) to take control of [financial imbalances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_payments_crisis) and equally manage the financial resources for the four provinces to meet their expenditure liabilities while alleviating the [horizontal fiscal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_Fiscal_Imbalance) imbalances.[^cite_note-autogenerated2-41]

According to stipulations and directions of the Constitution, the [provisional governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Governments_of_Pakistan) and Federal government compete to get higher share of the program's revenues in order to stabilise their own financial status.[^cite_note-Social_Policy_and_Development_Centre,-42]
