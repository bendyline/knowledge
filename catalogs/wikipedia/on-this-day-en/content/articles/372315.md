**Moscow Oblast**[^cite_note-7], informally known as **Podmoskovye**[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-OffNm-9] is a [federal subject](701438.md) of [Russia](25391.md) (an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)). With a population of 8,524,665 ([2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\))) living in an area of 44,300 square kilometers (17,100 sq mi),[^cite_note-area2-10] it is one of the most densely populated regions in the country[^cite_note-Dens-11] and is the [second most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federal_subjects_of_Russia_by_population) federal subject.[^cite_note-2010Census-12] The oblast has no official [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center); its public authorities are located in [Moscow](19004.md) and [Krasnogorsk](2399024.md) (the [Moscow Oblast Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Oblast_Duma) and the local government), and also across other locations in the oblast.[^cite_note-AdmCtr-13]

Located in [European Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Russia) between latitudes [54°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_parallel_north) and [57° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_parallel_north) and longitudes [35°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_meridian_east) and [41° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41st_meridian_east), Moscow Oblast borders [Tver Oblast](463952.md) in the northwest, [Yaroslavl Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslavl_Oblast) in the north, [Vladimir Oblast](463919.md) in the northeast and east, [Ryazan Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan_Oblast) in the southeast, [Tula Oblast](480930.md) in the south, [Kaluga Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluga_Oblast) in the southwest, and [Smolensk Oblast](480938.md) in the west. The oblast mostly surrounds the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of Moscow, which is not part of the oblast, but rather a separate federal subject in its own right. The oblast is highly [industrialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation), with the major industries being [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy), [oil refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_refining), and [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), along with food, energy, and chemical industries.

## Geography



Map of Moscow Oblast and the federal city of Moscow

### Relief

The oblast is mostly flat, with some hills with a height of about 160 meters (520 ft) in the western and extensive lowlands in the eastern part. From the southwest to northeast, the oblast is crossed by the border of the Moscow glacier to the north of the common ice-[erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion_\(geology\)) form with [moraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) ridges, and to the south are only erosional landforms. The western and northern parts of the oblast contain the Moscow Uplands. Their average height peaks at about 300 meters (980 ft) near [Dmitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitrov) and the upper point of 310 meters (1,020 ft) lies near the village of [Shapkino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapkino,_Mozhaysky_District,_Moscow_Oblast) in [Mozhaysky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozhaysky_District,_Moscow_Oblast). The northern part of the Moscow Uplands is steeper than the southern part. The uplands contain lakes of glacial origin, such as Lakes Nerskoye and Krugloye. To the north of the Moscow Uplands lies the alluvial Verhnevolzhsk Depression; It is marshy and flat with the height varying between about 120 meters (390 ft) and 150 meters (490 ft).[^cite_note-14]

To the south stretches a hilly area of the Moskvoretsko-Oksk plain. Its greatest height of 254 meters (833 ft) lies in the area of [Tyoply Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyoply_Stan_District), within the Moscow city limits. The plain has clearly defined river valleys, especially in the south parts, and occasional [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) relief, mostly in [Serpukhovsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhovsky_District). In the extreme south, after the Oka River, lies the [Central Russian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Russian_Upland). It contains numerous gullies and ravines and has average height above 200 m with the maximum of 236 m near [Pushchino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushchino).[^cite_note-15]

Most of the eastern part of Moscow Oblast is taken by the vast [Meshchera Lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshchera_Lowlands) with much wetland in their eastern part. Their highest hill peaks at 214 meters (702 ft) but the average heights are 120–150 meters (390–490 ft). Most lakes of the lowlands, such as Lakes Chyornoye and Svyatoye, are of glacial origin. Here lies the lowest natural elevation of the region, the water level of Oka River at 97 meters (318 ft).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Geology and minerals

#### Geology



Dolomite



Gneiss

Moscow Oblast is located in the central part of the [East European craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_European_craton). Like all cratons, the latter is composed of the crystalline basement and sedimentary cover. The basement consists of [Archaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archean) and [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic) rocks and the cover is deposited in the [Palaeozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeozoic), [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) and [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic) eras. The lowest depth of the basement (1,000 meters (3,300 ft)) is to the south of [Serebryanye Prudy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serebryanye_Prudy), in the very south area of the oblast, and the largest (4,200 meters (13,800 ft)) is to the east of [Sergiyev Posad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiyev_Posad), in the northeast region.[^cite_note-18]

[Tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_period) deposits are almost absent within the oblast. Significantly more abundant are deposits of the [Carboniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous) and [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) periods. In the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) period, a sea was covering Moscow Oblast, as evidenced by [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) deposits and a variety of sands. Cretaceous sediments are most common in the north of the oblast. The sea was wider in Jurassic than in Cretaceous period. Typical Jurassic deposits, in the form of black clay, are found within and around the city of Moscow and in the valley of the [Moscow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_River). Carboniferous deposits in Moscow Oblast are represented by [dolomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), and [marl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marl). Coal deposits rich in organic remains occur in the south, especially in Serpukhovsky District, and in the western regions. [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian) deposits were also found within the region.[^cite_note-19]

[Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) deposits are widely distributed in Moscow Oblast; their thickness decreases from the northwest to southeast. It is believed[^cite_note-20] that there were four glaciations in the area. The first occurred in the Lower [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) and spread to the east–west part of the Oka River valley, it left almost no trace in the region. In the [Middle Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene), there were two powerful glaciations. The Dnieper glacier covered a large part of the Russian Plain, whereas the Moscow glaciation stopped just south of the present city of Moscow. The last glaciation, the Valdai glaciation, occurred in the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene); it did not directly affect the territory of Moscow Oblast, but left traces in the form of [fluvioglacial deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvioglacial_deposits), mainly in the north area. The glaciers left behind a [moraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) [loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loam) with pebbles and boulders of various rocks, such as [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite), [gneiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gneiss), [quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzite), [dolomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone). Its thickness varies between a few meters at watersheds and 100 m at moraine ridges.[^cite_note-21]

#### Minerals

Moscow Oblast is rich in minerals. Sands from the sediments of different periods (mainly Quaternary and Cretaceous) are of high quality and are widely used in construction. Quartz sand (milled [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz)) is used in the glass industry, their production is conducted from the end of 17th century near [Lyubertsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyubertsy). Much of the production is currently halted due to environmental concerns, and only the Yeganovskoye field is being exploited; its silica sand reserves are 33 million tonnes and annual production reaches 675,000 tonnes.[^cite_note-22] Sand and gravel deposits are abundant within the Smolensk-Moscow Upland. [Sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) deposits are developed in [Klinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klinsky_District) and [Dmitrovsky Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitrovsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast).

There are numerous clay deposits within the oblast; fusible clay is excavated in [Sergiyev Posad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiyev_Posad). The Yeldiginskoye field near the village of [Sofrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofrino) has reserves estimated at 30 million cubic meters; its annual production reaches 600,000 cubic meters (21,000,000 cu ft). Refractory white clay occurs in the eastern region, in the Carboniferous and Jurassic sediments, and is extracted from the 14th century near [Gzhel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gzhel). The largest (Kudinovskoye) deposit is near the town of [Elektrougli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektrougli) with the reserves of 3 billion tonnes. Also widespread are loams which are used in brick manufacture and [limestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) ("white stone"). The famous [Myachkovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myachkovo,_Moscow_Oblast) deposit of [carboniferous limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous_limestone) provided material that went for cladding of such buildings in Moscow as the [Bolshoi Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshoi_Theatre). The mining in Myachkovo had been stopped and currently, limestone is provided by the quarries of [Podolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsky_District), [Voskresensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskresensky_District,_Moscow_Oblast), and [Kolomensky Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomensky_District). The latter district also provides marble-like limestone.[^cite_note-23]

Other industrial minerals of Moscow Oblast are dolomite, limestone [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff), and [marl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marl); mostly in the southern and eastern parts. Dolomite is used in the cement industry. Its mining is concentrated mainly near [Shchyolkovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shchyolkovo), the reserves exceed 20 million tonnes and the annual production is about 650 tonnes.[^cite_note-24]

Phosphates are produced in the Yegorevskoye and Severskoye fields. Meshchera and Verkhnevolzhsk Lowlands are rich in [peat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat). The largest mines are "Ryazanovskoe" (840,000 tonnes per year) and "Radovitsky moss" (760,000 tonnes per year), both around [Yegoryevsk](2350904.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] There are deposits of [brown coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_coal) beyond the Oka River, but they have no commercial value. There are also minor deposits of titanium and [iron ore](145352.md) in Serpukhovsky and [Serebryano-Prudsky Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serebryano-Prudsky_District).

Salts of potassium salt are being developed around Serpukhov and Yegoryevsk. There are also numerous mineral springs near Zvenigorod, Klin, and Serpukhov. They include surface springs and reservoirs at the depth of 300–500 meters (980–1,640 ft). Deeper, at 1–1.5 kilometers (0.62–0.93 mi) there is a large sea of salt extending beyond Moscow Oblast. Waters with the salt concentration up to 300 g/L are used in the local food industry and spas.[^cite_note-27]

### Climate



In winter

The climate of Moscow Oblast is [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental), with clearly expressed seasonality – short but warm summers and long, cold winters; the continentality increases from northwest to southeast. The period of the average temperature below 0 °C (32 °F) lasts 130–150 days, beginning in early or mid-November and ending in late March (or very early April). The average annual temperature varies from +3.5 °C (38.3 °F) to +5.5 °C (41.9 °F). The coldest months are January and February with the average temperature of −9 °C (16 °F) in the west and −12 °C (10 °F) in the east. With the arrival of arctic air, the temperature drops to below −20 °C (−4 °F) that may last up to twenty days during the winter, with the temperatures reaching −45 °C (−49 °F). The minimum temperature of −54 °C (−65 °F) was observed in [Naro-Fominsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naro-Fominsk). Thaws often occur in December and February due to the Atlantic, and rarely the Mediterranean [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone). The thaws usually last several days, and their total number from November to March can reach fifty. Snow starts accumulating in November, though sometimes in late October or early December, and disappears in mid-April (sometimes in late March). The snow depth is 25–50 centimeters (9.8–19.7 in) and the soil freezes to 65–75 centimeters (26–30 in). The warmest month is July with the average temperature of +18.0 °C (64.4 °F) in the northwest and +20.0 °C (68.0 °F) in the southeast. The maximum temperature of +40 °C (104 °F) was recorded in [Kolomna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomna) during [2010 Northern Hemisphere summer heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Northern_Hemisphere_summer_heat_waves). The average annual rainfall is 450–650 millimeters (18–26 in), the precipitation is maximal in the northwestern and minimal in the southeastern regions. The summer precipitation is usually 75 millimeters (3.0 in), but severe droughts occur once in 25–30 years, with less than 5 millimeters (0.20 in) of rain over June–August.[^cite_note-brit-28][^cite_note-29]

### Rivers and lakes



The [Shosha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shosha_River) near the *[selo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village#Russia)* of [Mikulino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikulino,_Moscow_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1)

There are more than three hundred rivers with the length above 10 kilometers (6.2 mi) in Moscow Oblast. All rivers are calm and have well-developed valleys and [floodplains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodplain). They are mostly fed by melting snow and the flood falls on April–May. The water level is low in summer and increases only with heavy rain. The rivers freeze over from late November until mid-April. The only navigable rivers are the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River), the [Oka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oka_River), and the [Moskva River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moskva_River).[^cite_note-wr-31]

Most rivers belong to the basin of the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River), which itself only crosses a small part in the north of Moscow Oblast, near the border with [Tver Oblast](463952.md). The second largest river of the region is the Oka. The northern part of Moscow Oblast includes such Volga [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) as the [Shosha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shosha_River), the [Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lama_River), the [Dubna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubna_River_\(Volga_basin\)), the [Sestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestra_River_\(Moscow_Oblast\)), and the Yakhroma. On the south flow the tributaries of the Oka, including the [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_River_\(Russia\)), the [Protva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protva_River), and the [Lopasnya Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopasnya_River). The Moskva River, which almost entirely flows within the oblast, also belongs to the Oka basin. The eastern and northeastern regions, including much of Meschersk Depression, are irrigated by the tributaries of the [Klyazma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klyazma_River), which itself is a main tributary of the Oka.[^cite_note-wr-31]

The [Moscow Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Canal) crosses the northern part of Moscow Oblast through the Ikshinskyoe, Klyazminskoye, Pyalovskoye, and Pestovskoye Reservoirs. In the basin of the Moskva River, there are also Ozerninskoye, Mozhayskoye, Istrinskoye, and Ruza Reservoirs, providing Moscow with drinking water.[^cite_note-wr-31]

There are about 350 lakes in the oblast, almost all are shallow (5–10 m) and many are of glacial origin. The largest are [Lake Dubovoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Dubovoye?action=edit\&redlink=1) (11.8 square kilometers (4.6 sq mi)) and [Svyatoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Svyatoye) (11.6 square kilometers (4.5 sq mi)) whereas the deepest (32 meters (105 ft)) is Lake Glubokoye in [Ruzsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzsky_District). There are also many marshes, especially within the Meshchersk and Verkhnevolzhsk lowlands.[^cite_note-32]

### Soils



A typical landscape of Moscow Oblast

The oblast is dominated by relatively infertile [podsol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podsol) soils which require fertilizers for commercial agriculture. On the hills there is more [loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loam) and the low-lying areas have more of bog, sandy loam and sand. [Chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem) is scarce and occurs only south of the Oka River. Gray forest soils are spread between the Oka, Moskva, and Klyazma Rivers, mostly in [Ramensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramensky_District) and Voskresensky Districts. Marshy soils are common in Meshchersk and Verkhnevolzhsk lowlands. Valleys of large rivers are rich in alluvial soils. In general, soils are heavily polluted with chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and household and industrial waste, especially around Moscow, [Orekhovo-Zuyevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orekhovo-Zuyevo), [Noginsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noginsk), and Voskresensk.

### Flora



The small highway ring around Moscow

Moscow Oblast lies within the zone of forests and steppes with forests covering over 40% of the region. [Coniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer) (mainly pine) trees dominate the northern (Verkhnevolzhsk lowlands) and western parts (Mozhaysky, Lotoshinsky, and Shakhovsky Districts). Forests of Meshchora consist primarily of pine; in waterlogged lowlands, there are individual [alder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alder) forests. Central and eastern regions have coniferous-deciduous forests with the main tree species of [Norway spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picea_abies), [Scots pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_sylvestris), [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch), and [aspen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus_tremula) often mixed with bushes of [hazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corylus_avellana). To the south lies the subzone of broad-leaved forests of [oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur), [lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilia_cordata), [maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple) and [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm). Moscow-Oka Upland is the transition zone which is dominated by spruce, for example, in the upper reaches of the [Lopasnya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopasnya_River). Valleys of the Oka are covered in pine forests of the steppe type and the far south regions (Serebryano-Prudsky and partially Serpukhovsky Districts) are cultivated steppes with occasional lime and oak groves.[^cite_note-33]

The intensive cutting of Moscow region forests in the 18–19th centuries reduced them and changed their species; conifers were replaced by [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch) and [aspen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen). There is almost no logging nowadays and the forests are being restored, especially around Moscow.

[Swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp) are prevalent in the eastern areas, such as [Shatursky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatursky_District) and [Lukhovitsky Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukhovitsky_District). The natural floodplain meadows are almost gone. The number of native plant species is reduced, but some introduced foreign species flourish, such as [sugar maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_saccharum). Native species include [water caltrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_caltrop) and [lady's slipper orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypripedium_calceolus).

### Environment

The ecological situation in the Moscow Oblast is serious. The areas adjacent to Moscow, and industrial zones in the east and south-east regions are heavily polluted. Most contamination originates from emissions from [Kashira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashira_Power_Plant) and [Shatura Power Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatura_Power_Station) and disposal of household and industrial waste. For example, the Timohovskaya dump is one of the largest in Europe; other objects of concern are aging oil storage tanks, and nuclear waste in the [Sergiyevo-Posadsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiyevo-Posadsky_District). Contamination level is highest in Moscow, Voskresensk and Klin, high in [Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzerzhinsky,_Moscow_Oblast), [Kolomna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomna), [Mytishchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytishchi), [Podolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsk), [Serpukhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhov), [Shchyolkovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shchyolkovo), and [Elektrostal](1998724.md), and low in Prioksko-Terrasny Biosphere Reserve.[^cite_note-ekoport-35] The major contaminants are [formaldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaldehyde) and [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol) in Moscow; [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia) and [hydrogen fluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fluoride) in Voskresensk; formaldehyde in Klin, Kolomna, Mytishchi and Podolsk, phenol in Serpukhov. The most polluted rivers are Moscow, Oka and Klyazma. In the Moscow area and in major cities (in particular, in Podolsk, Orekhovo-Zuyevo, Serpukhov, [Lukhovitsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukhovitsy) and Stupino) also heavily polluted are groundwaters.[^cite_note-ekoport-35]

## History

See also: [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow) and [Moscow Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Governorate)



Section of the panorama of the Battle of Borodino by [Franz Roubaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Roubaud) (1912)

The territory of what is now Moscow Oblast had been inhabited for more than twenty thousand years. Numerous mounds and settlements from [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) were discovered there. Up to the 9–10th centuries, the Moskva River basin and adjacent lands were inhabited by [Finnic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnic_peoples). [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) populated the area only in the 10th century. In mid-12th century, the lands became part of [Vladimir-Suzdal Principality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir-Suzdal). Several important cities were founded around that time, including [Volokolamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volokolamsk) (1135), Moscow (1147), [Zvenigorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvenigorod) (1152), and [Dmitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitrov) (1154). In the first half of the 13th century, the entire Vladimir-Suzdal Principality, including the Moscow area, was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Rus') by the Mongols.[^cite_note-36]



[Sergius of Radonezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergius_of_Radonezh) blesses Dmitry Donskoy, before the Battle of Kulikovo

In the 13th century, the land around Moscow was part of [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow), which subsequently was the center of the unification of Russian lands, in particular the Mongol raids. In 1380, from Kolomna the prince [Dmitry Donskoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Donskoy) led his troops to defeat the Mongols at the [Battle of Kulikovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kulikovo). The southern part of Moscow Oblast was then part of the [Principality of Ryazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Ryazan); it was attached to Moscow only in the 1520.[^cite_note-37]

In 1708, [Moscow Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Governorate) was established by the decree of [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great); the area included most of the present Moscow Oblast. The [Battle of Borodino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Borodino), which decided the outcome of the [French invasion of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Russia) was fought in 1812 near [Mozhaysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozhaysk).

Industries developed in Moscow Oblast in the 17–19th centuries.[^cite_note-brit-28] They were centered in [Bogorodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noginsk), [Pavlovsky Posad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlovsky_Posad), and [Orekhovo-Zuyevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orekhovo-Zuyevo) and were dominated by textile production. The first railway in Russia was constructed in the Moscow Oblast in 1851, connecting Moscow and [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), and in 1862 the line to [Nizhny Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Novgorod) was opened.

In the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), *Central Industrial Oblast* was established on January 14, 1929.[^cite_note-Established-1] It included the abolished [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Governorate), [Ryazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan_Governorate), [Tver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tver_Governorate), [Tula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tula_Governorate), [Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Governorate), and [Kaluga Governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluga_Governorate). The oblast was divided into ten [okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okrug) and had the administrative center in Moscow. On June 3, 1929, the area was renamed *Moscow Oblast* and on July 30, 1930, the division into ten okrugs was abolished.[^cite_note-h1-38][^cite_note-h2-39]

Parts of the then bulky *Moscow Oblast* were gradually transferred to other divisions. In particular, twenty-six districts became part of Kalinin Oblast in January 1935, and another seventy-seven districts were separated in September 1937 as [Tula](480930.md) and [Ryazan Oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan_Oblast). [Borovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borovsky_District), [Vysokinichsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vysokinichsky_District?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Maloyaroslavetsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloyaroslavetsky_District), [Ugodsko-Zavodsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugodsko-Zavodsky_District?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Petushinsky Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petushinsky_District) were transferred in 1944 to Kaluga and Vladimir Oblasts.[^cite_note-h1-38][^cite_note-h2-39]

In 1941 and 1942, one of the most significant military operations of [World War II](32927.md)—the [Battle of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow)—was fought in the Moscow Oblast. Germany reached Solnechnogorsky, Klinsky, Istrinsky, Lobninsky, Khimkinsky, Naro-Fominsky, Volokolamsky, Kolomensky, Kashirsky, Serybryano-Prudsky Districts and others.

According to the [Constitution of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia), adopted in December 1993, Moscow Oblast is one of the 83 [federal subjects of Russia](701438.md).

## Economy

### Industry



[Soyuz-TMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-TMA)-6 spacecraft developed by [Energia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.P._Korolev_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation_Energia)

In terms of industrial production, Moscow Oblast is second in Russia, after the city of Moscow. The industry of the Oblast relies on imported raw materials, strong scientific and technological base and highly skilled workforce; it is closely linked with the industry of Moscow.

Well developed are machinery and metalworking. There are plants for the thermal and nuclear power engineering ([ZiO-Podolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZiO-Podolsk?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Podolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsk)), nuclear fuel ([TVEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVEL) in [Elektrostal](1998724.md)), space and missile ([Energia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.P._Korolev_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation_Energia) in [Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korolyov_\(city\)), [Lavochkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavochkin) in [Khimki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khimki), NGO engineering in [Reutov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reutov), FTSDT "Union" in [Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzerzhinsky,_Moscow_Oblast) – development of solid rocket fuel, etc., IBC "Horizon" in [Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzerzhinsky_\(town\)) – power plants for aircraft, etc.); locomotives ([Kolomna factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomna_Locomotive_Works)), metro cars ([Metrowagonmash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrowagonmash) in [Mytischi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytischi)), electric trains (Demikhovsky Engineering Works), cars ([SeAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeAZ)), buses (Likinsky bus plant in [Likino-Dulyovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likino-Dulyovo)); agricultural machines, excavators and cranes ([Lyubertsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyubertsy), [Dmitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitrov), [Balashikha](1998682.md)); stainless steel ([Elektrostal](1998724.md)), cables ([Podolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsk)), optical devices (Krasnogorsky plant, Lytkarino Optical Glass Factory).

There are many defense enterprises, such as Russian Center for demonstrations of weapons, military equipment and technology in [Krasnoarmeysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoarmeysk,_Moscow_Oblast); [Kamov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamov), [Phazotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phazotron), [Bazalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazalt), [NPP Zvezda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPP_Zvezda), [MKB Fakel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKB_Fakel), [MKB Raduga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKB_Raduga), National Research Institute of Aviation Systems, Krasnozavodsk Chemical Plant, [Tikhomirov Scientific Research Institute of Instrument Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhomirov_Scientific_Research_Institute_of_Instrument_Design), Moscow Research Institute "Agat", [Dolgoprudnenskoe Scientific Production Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgoprudnenskoe_Scientific_Production_Plant), and many others.

Chemical industry of the Oblast produces acids ([Shchyolkovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shchyolkovo)), mineral fertilizers (plants named "Phosphates" and "Mineral fertilizers" in [Voskresensk](482247.md)), synthetic fibers ([Serpukhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhov) and [Klin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klin,_Klinsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast)), plastics ([Orekhovo-Zuyevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orekhovo-Zuyevo)), varnishes and paints (Sergiyev Posad, Odintsovsky paint factories), pharmaceuticals ([Staraya Kupavna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Kupavna)). ### Energy



The largest source of electricity in the Moscow region – [Kashira Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashira_Power_Plant)

In 1999, Moscow Oblast consumed 15.4 billion m<sup>3</sup> of natural gas, 3.32 million tonnes of oil, 2.13 million tonnes of coal and 8.5 billion kWh of electricity. Electricity for the Oblast is provided by the [Kashirskaya thermal power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashirskaya_thermal_power_plant) (TPP, 1910 MW), Dzerzhynskaya TPP No 22 (1300 MW), [Thermal Power Plant 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_Power_Plant_27) (1100 MW), [Shatura Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatura_Power_Station) (1100 MW), Zagorskaya [hydroelectric power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydroelectricity) (1200 MW), Elektrogorsk TPP (623 MW) and several smaller plants. Major new energy project in the region is the construction of Zagorsk hydroelectric plant with the capacity of 840 MW. The deficit of energy is provided by powerlines connecting the region with Saint Petersburg, [Volga Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Hydroelectric_Station) and other energy suppliers.[^cite_note-41]

### Agriculture

Agriculture has a relatively minor role in the economy of the Oblast. Only 25% of land is cultivated and another 15% are used for other activities such as [livestock](25160767.md) farming. Agriculture is the least developed in the northern, eastern and western border regions. In the southern region, especially south of the Oka River, more than 50% of land is used in agriculture. [Horticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horticulture) is typical for the southern region with most of the sown area (more than 3/5) occupied by forage crops. Large areas are reserved for grains, especially wheat, [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oats) and [rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye), and significant role plays potato. Greenhouses are very common and [Moskovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moskovsky,_Moscow_Oblast) city hosts the largest greenhouse complex in Europe. Also grown are [flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowers) and [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom). [Livestock](25160767.md) farming predominates over the crop, and is primarily aimed at the production of milk and meat. In addition to cattle, commonly bred are pigs and chickens.[^cite_note-brit-28]

The economic crisis of the 1990s in Russia had severely affected the agriculture of Moscow Oblast. In particular, in the 2000s, as compared with 1970–80s, the grain production has fallen by more than 3 times; potatoes by 2.5 times; vegetables, livestock and poultry by 30%; milk by 2 times and eggs by 4 times.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### Transport



The gateway to the [Moscow Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Canal) in [Yakhroma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakhroma)

Moscow Oblast has a dense transport network, including roads, railways and waterways along the largest rivers, lakes and reservoirs. Land routes are radially diverging from Moscow and crossed by one railway and [two highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKAD) rings. Neither railways nor roads, built for the most part many years ago, can cope with the steadily mounting traffic flows. About half of the roads are overloaded and three quarters do not meet modern requirements. Insufficient width of the roads and frequent repairs cause traffic jams.[^cite_note-44]

Moscow Oblast has the highest density of railways in Russia. Eleven major radial lines originate in Moscow and run through the Oblast; the total length of the railways reaches 2,700 km. Almost all railroads are electrified. The largest rail hubs are [Orekhovo-Zuyevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orekhovo-Zuyevo) and [Bekasovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bekasovo,_Moscow_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1). Regular navigation is carried on the rivers Volga, Oka and Moscow, as well as on the [Moscow Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Canal). Major river ports are in Serpukhov and Kolomna. Also well-developed is pipeline transport. There are two major oil lines, two natural gas rings and numerous radial lines connecting Moscow with the largest gas producing regions of the country.[^cite_note-45]

Moscow and Moscow Oblast have several international passenger [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport), namely [Sheremetyevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheremetyevo_International_Airport) (with two terminals), [Vnukovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vnukovo_International_Airport), [Domodedovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodedovo_International_Airport) and [Ostafyevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostafyevo_International_Airport). There is also [Bykovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bykovo_Airport) Airport, which is used for freight. ## Government and awards



The [RKA Mission Control Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RKA_Mission_Control_Center) in [Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korolyov,_Moscow_Oblast)

Moscow Oblast was awarded three [Orders of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin), on 3 January 1934, 17 December 1956 and 5 December 1966.

The highest executive organ is the Government of Moscow Oblast. Eighteen ministries act as the executive bodies of state authority.[^cite_note-46] The powers, tasks, functions and competence of the Government are defined by the Charter of the Moscow Region. The [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor) of the Moscow Oblast will be elected with the term of 5 years.[^cite_note-ustav-47] The Regional Duma of Moscow Oblast was formed on 12 December 1993. It consists of 50 deputies also serving a 5-year term.[^cite_note-ustav-47]

[Sergey Shoygu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Shoygu) was elected as Governor of Moscow Oblast in April 2012 by the Moscow Oblast Duma.[^cite_note-48] Shoygu left office after only six months with his appointment when he was appointed as [Minister of Defence](7875884.md) by [Vladimir Putin](32817.md). [Andrei Vorobyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Yuryevich_Vorobyov) was appointed as acting governor and won a full term to the office in the [2013 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Oblast_gubernatorial_election,_2013).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

## Science

Moscow Oblast has a high density of scientific research institutions, especially related to engineering and military technologies. The latter started developing in the region in 1930–1940s in [Zhukovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhukovsky_\(city\)) (aeronautical engineering), [Klimovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimovsk) (development of small arms), [Reutov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reutov) (Missile Engineering), [Fryazino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryazino) (microwave electronics)[^cite_note-51] and [Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korolyov_\(city\)) (space technology). They were later joined by famous centers for basic sciences in [Troitsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troitsk,_Moscow_Oblast), [Chernogolovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernogolovka) (physics and chemistry), [Dubna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubna) and [Protvino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protvino) (nuclear physics) and [Pushchino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushchino) (biology). Moscow Oblast hosts [Mission Control Centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Control_Center) for spacecraft (in Korolyov) and military satellites ([Krasnoznamensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoznamensk,_Moscow_Oblast)), as well as a number of test sites.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

## Culture and recreation



[Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Lavra_of_St._Sergius)



Wooden church

Moscow Oblast has numerous therapeutic and [recreational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation) facilities located mainly in western, northwestern and northern parts, and also near Moscow. Of great importance for recreation are forests, which occupy over 40% of the region, as well as horticultural activities. The region has the highest number (over 1 million) of [dachas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacha) with associated individual gardens. Also numerous are manor complexes, such as those in [Abramtsevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abramtsevo,_Sergiyevo-Posadsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast), [Muranovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muranovo), Ostafievo, historical towns ([Vereya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereya,_Naro-Fominsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast), [Volokolamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volokolamsk), [Dmitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitrov), [Zaraysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaraysk), [Zvenigorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvenigorod), [Istra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istra,_Istrinsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast), [Kolomna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomna), [Sergiyev Posad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiyev_Posad), [Serpukhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhov), etc.), monasteries ([Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Lavra_of_St._Sergius), [Joseph-Volokolamsk Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-Volokolamsk_Monastery), [Savvino-Storozhevsky Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savvino-Storozhevsky_Monastery), [Nikolo-Ugresh monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolo-Ugresh_monastery), etc.), and museums ([Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov) museum in [Melikhovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melikhovo), [Tchaikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky) museum in Klin, Serpukhov Historical and Art Museum, etc.). The oldest surviving building is the [Kamenskoye Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenskoye_Church).

Administratively, the oblast is divided into 38 [cities/towns under oblast jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) and 36 [administrative districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion), consisting of 46 [towns of district significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_district_significance), 72 [urban-type settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban-type_settlement), and 6,119 rural localities.

As of 2011, Moscow Oblast is [municipally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_divisions_of_Russia) subdivided into 38 urban okrugs and 36 [municipal districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion), which consist of 114 urban settlements and 193 rural settlements.



Center of [Volokolamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volokolamsk) in 2003

The three largest cities of the oblast are [Balashikha](1998682.md) (215,494), [Khimki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khimki) (207,425), and [Podolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsk) (186,961).[^cite_note-2010Census-12] Most other towns have ten to fifty thousand people. The smallest town is [Vereya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereya,_Naro-Fominsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast) in [Naro-Fominsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naro-Fominsky_District) with a population of 4,957 ([2002 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Russian_census))[^cite_note-PopCensus-69]. Among the urban-type settlements, the largest is Nakhabino (36,546) followed by [Tomilino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomilino) (30,605).[^cite_note-2010Census-12] The oldest populated place in the oblast is [Volokolamsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volokolamsk), first mentioned in 1135; slightly younger towns are Zvenigorod (1152), Dmitrov (1154), and Kolomna (1177).

The city of [Baikonur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur) in [Kazakhstan](16642.md) also belongs administratively to the oblast, as part of Odintsovsky District.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

The most intensive formation of towns occurred in 1938–1940. The youngest towns are [Golitsyno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golitsyno,_Moscow_Oblast) and [Kubinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubinka). They existed for quite some time, but were granted town status only in 2004. Some recent towns separated from the other towns, such as [Yubileyny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubileyny,_Moscow_Oblast) and [Peresvet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peresvet).

New projects have been announced at the beginning of the 21st century. One of them is [Rublyovo-Arkhangelsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rublyovo-Arkhangelsk?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is designed for 30,000 inhabitants with high income and is called by the media the "city for millionaires".[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Another is "Great Domodedovo, 30 kilometers (19 mi) south of the [Moscow Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Ring_Road), which is designed for 450,000 residents.[^cite_note-74] The new city A101 was designed for 300,000 residents in 2009 and the sale of its land in [Leninsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast) has already begun; the city's construction is planned to take thirty-five years.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

A part of Moscow Oblast's former territory, mainly to the southwest of the city of Moscow, was merged with the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of [Moscow](19004.md) on July 1, 2012.[^cite_note-Merger-78]

The housing stock of the oblast is approximately 125 million square meters. Almost all the houses are equipped with water supply, sewerage, gas,[^cite_note-79] central heating and hot water. However, the telephone network is underdeveloped in rural areas. In the competition for the most comfortable city of 2006 in the Moscow Oblast the winner was [Kolomna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomna) followed by [Balashikha](1998682.md) (for cities with population over 100,000) and [Vidnoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidnoye,_Moscow_Oblast) (<100,000) and then by [Mytishchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytishchi) and [Noginsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noginsk).
