**Honolulu** ([/ˌhɒnəˈluːluː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/02/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Honolulu.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Honolulu.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/02/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Honolulu.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Honolulu.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*HON-ə-LOO-loo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-8] Hawaiian: [\[honoˈlulu\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hawaiian)) is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_places_in_Hawaii) of the U.S. state of [Hawaii](13270.md), located in the Pacific Ocean. It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of the [consolidated City and County of Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_County,_Hawaii), situated along the southeast coast of the island of [Oʻahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%CA%BBahu). The population of Honolulu was 350,964 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-10] dropping to an estimated 341,868 by 2025.[^cite_note-HonoluluQuickFacts-11] The Urban Honolulu metropolitan area had an estimated population of just under 1 million residents in 2024 and is the [56th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the nation.[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-6]

The area's geography and [Honolulu Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Harbor) have long made it desirable as a port, accounting for the city's growth and importance in the [Hawaiian archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands) and the broader Pacific region. Honolulu has been the capital of Hawaii since 1845, first as seat of the independent [Hawaiian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Kingdom) until the [kingdom's overthrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overthrow_of_the_Hawaiian_Kingdom) in 1893, then of the [U.S. territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Hawaii) established after [annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newlands_Resolution) in 1898, and since 1959 of the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Admission_Act) of Hawaii. The city gained worldwide recognition following the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan)'s [attack on nearby Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) on December 7, 1941, which prompted the [entry of the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_States_during_World_War_II) into [World War II](32927.md); the harbor remains a major [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) base, hosting the [United States Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Fleet), the world's largest naval command.[^cite_note-12]

Honolulu is the westernmost and southernmost major U.S. city and state capital. It is also a hub for business, commerce, finance, hospitality, and military defense in [Oceania](22621.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The city is characterized by a mix of various [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_culture), [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture), and [Pacific cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanian_culture), reflected in its diverse demography, cuisine, and traditions. Honolulu's favorable tropical climate, rich natural scenery, and extensive beaches make it a popular global destination for tourists. With nearly 1.5 million visitors in 2024, Honolulu is among the ten most visited cities in the United States.[^cite_note-15] However, since the beginning of the 21st century, [climate change](5042951.md) has significantly impacted Honolulu and its tourism economy, contributing to increasing [drought](37335.md) and water shortages, and [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) as well as beach and hotel flooding in Downtown Honolulu. These factors are compounded by an aging and declining population, with an exodus emigrating to the [U.S. mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._mainland).

## Etymology

*Honolulu* is [Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_language) for 'sheltered harbor'[^cite_note-Honolulu.gov-16] or 'calm port';[^cite_note-17] this former meaning is also reflected in the [related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_languages) [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language) [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate) toponym [Whangaruru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whangaruru) in the tip of New Zealand's [North Island](62046.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Its old name, *Kou*, roughly encompasses the area from Nuʻuanu Avenue to Alakea Street and from Hotel Street to Queen Street, which is the heart of the present downtown district.[^cite_note-20]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Honolulu).

Evidence of the first settlement of Honolulu by the original [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia) migrants to the archipelago comes from oral histories and artifacts. These indicate that there was a settlement where Honolulu now stands in the 11th century.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] After [Kamehameha I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamehameha_I) conquered Oʻahu in the [Battle of Nuʻuanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nu%CA%BBuanu) at [Nuʻuanu Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu%CA%BBuanu_Pali), he moved his royal court from the [Island of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_\(island\)) to [Waikiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikiki) in 1804. His court relocated in 1809 to what is now [downtown Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Honolulu). The capital was moved back to [Kailua-Kona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailua-Kona,_Hawaii) in 1812.

In November 1794, Captain [William Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterworth_Squadron) of Great Britain was the first foreigner to sail into what is now [Honolulu Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Harbor).[^cite_note-23] More foreign ships followed, making the port of Honolulu a focal point for merchant ships traveling between North America and Asia. The settlement grew from a handful of homes to a city in the early 19th century after [Kamehameha I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamehameha_I) chose it as a replacement for his residence at Waikiki in 1810.[^cite_note-Daws-24]

In 1845, [Kamehameha III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamehameha_III) moved the permanent capital of the [Hawaiian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Kingdom) from [Lahaina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii) on [Maui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maui) to Honolulu.[^cite_note-Daws-24] He and the kings who followed him transformed Honolulu into a modern capital, erecting buildings such as [St. Andrew's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_Church_of_Saint_Andrew_\(Honolulu\)), [ʻIolani Palace](469613.md), and [Aliʻiōlani Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali%CA%BBi%C5%8Dlani_Hale). At the same time, Honolulu became the islands' center of commerce, with descendants of American missionaries establishing major businesses downtown.[^cite_note-25]

Despite the turbulent history of the late 19th century and early 20th century—such as the [overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overthrow_of_the_Hawaiian_Kingdom) in 1893, Hawaii's annexation by the U.S. in 1898, a [large fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Honolulu#Great_Honolulu_Chinatown_Fire_of_1900) in 1900, and the Japanese [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) in 1941—Honolulu remained the Hawaiian Islands' capital, largest city, and main airport and seaport.[^cite_note-26]

An economic and tourism boom following statehood brought rapid economic growth to Honolulu and Hawaii. Modern air travel brings, as of 2007, 7.6 million visitors annually to the islands, with 62.3% entering at [Honolulu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_International_Airport).[^cite_note-27] Today, Honolulu is a modern city with numerous high-rise buildings, and Waikiki is the center of the tourism industry in Hawaii, with thousands of hotel rooms.&#x20;

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the Urban Honolulu CDP has an area of 68.4 square miles (177.2 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 7.9 square miles (20.5 km<sup>2</sup>), or 11.56%, is water.[^cite_note-28]

Honolulu is the most remote major U.S. city and one of the most isolated major cities in the world.[^cite_note-\:0-29] The closest location in mainland U.S. is the [Point Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Arena,_California) Lighthouse in northern [California](5407.md), at 2,045 nautical miles (2,353 mi; 3,787 km).[^cite_note-30] ([Nautical vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_vessel) require some additional distance to circumnavigate [Makapuʻu Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makapu%CA%BBu_Point).) The closest major city is [San Francisco](49728.md), California, at 2,083 nautical miles (2,397 mi; 3,858 km).[^cite_note-\:0-29] Some islands off the Mexican coast and part of the [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands) of [Alaska](624.md) are slightly closer to Honolulu than the mainland.

The [volcanic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_field) of the [Honolulu Volcanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Volcanics) is partially inside the city.[^cite_note-31]

The [antipode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipodes_on_Earth) of Honolulu is in [Botswana](3464.md), [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa).

### Neighborhoods

- [Downtown Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Honolulu) is Hawaii's financial, commercial, and governmental center. On the waterfront is [Aloha Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha_Tower), for many years Hawaii's tallest building. The tallest building is now the 438-foot (134 m) [First Hawaiian Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hawaiian_Center), on King and Bishop Streets. The downtown campus of [Hawaiʻi Pacific University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Pacific_University) is also there.

- [The Arts District Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arts_District_Honolulu), both downtown and in Chinatown, is on Chinatown's eastern edge. It is a 12-block area bounded by Bethel & Smith Streets and Nimitz Highway and Beretania Street—home to numerous arts and cultural institutions. It is within the [Chinatown Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Honolulu), which includes the former Hotel Street Vice District.

  [^cite_note-32]

- The [Capitol District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Capital_Historic_District) is the eastern part of Downtown Honolulu. It is the current and historic center of Hawaii's state government, incorporating the [State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_State_Capitol), [ʻIolani Palace](469613.md), [Honolulu Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Hale) (City Hall), State Library, and the statue of King [Kamehameha I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamehameha_I), along with numerous government buildings.

- [Kakaʻako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakaako) is a light-industrial district between Downtown and Waikiki that has seen a large-scale redevelopment effort in the past decade. It is home to two major shopping areas, [Ward Warehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Centers) and [Ward Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Centers). [The Howard Hughes Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Howard_Hughes_Corporation) plans to transform Ward Centers into [Ward Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Village) over the next decade. The [John A. Burns School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Burns_School_of_Medicine), part of the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi_at_M%C4%81noa), is also there. A memorial to the [*Ehime Maru* Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehime_Maru_and_USS_Greeneville_collision) victims is at the [Kakaʻako Waterfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaka'ako_Waterfront_Park).

- [Ala Moana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala_Moana) is a district between [Kakaʻako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakaako) and [Waikiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikiki) and the home of [Ala Moana Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala_Moana_Center), the "world's largest open-air shopping center" and Hawaii's largest shopping mall.

  [^cite_note-33]

  &#x20;Ala Moana Center has over 300 tenants and is very popular with tourists. Also in Ala Moana is the Honolulu Design Center and [Ala Moana Beach Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala_Moana_Beach_Park), Honolulu's second-largest park.

- [Waikiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikiki) is Honolulu's tourist district, between the [Ala Wai Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala_Wai_Canal) and the Pacific Ocean next to [Diamond Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Head,_Hawaii). Numerous hotels, shops, and nightlife opportunities are along Kalākaua and Kūhiō Avenues. It is a popular location for visitors and locals alike and attracts millions of visitors every year. Most of Oʻahu's hotel rooms are in Waikiki.

- [Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoa), [Mōʻiliʻili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moiliili,_Hawaii), and [Makiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makiki) are residential neighborhoods in adjacent areas just inland of downtown and Waikiki. Mānoa Valley is home to the main campus of the [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi).

- Nuʻuanu and [Pauoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauoa,_Hawaii) are upper-middle-class residential districts inland of downtown Honolulu. The [National Memorial Cemetery of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Memorial_Cemetery_of_the_Pacific) is in [Punchbowl Crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punchbowl_Crater), fronting Pauoa Valley.

- [Pālolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palolo,_Hawaii) and [Kaimukī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaimuki) are neighborhoods east of Mānoa and Makiki, inland from Diamond Head. Pālolo Valley parallels Mānoa and is a residential neighborhood. Kaimukī is primarily a residential neighborhood with a commercial strip centered on Waiʻalae Avenue running behind Diamond Head. [Chaminade University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaminade_University) is in Kaimukī.

- [Waiʻalae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waialae,_Hawaii) and [Kāhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81hala,_Hawaii) are upper-class districts of Honolulu directly east of Diamond Head, with many high-priced homes. Also in these neighborhoods are the [Waialae Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waialae_Country_Club) and the five-star [Kahala Hotel & Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahala_Hotel_&_Resort).

- [East Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Honolulu,_Hawaii) includes the residential communities of [ʻĀina Haina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aina_Haina,_Hawaii), Niu Valley, and [Hawaiʻi Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Kai,_Hawaii). These are considered upper-middle-class neighborhoods. The upscale gated communities of Waiʻalae ʻIki and Hawaiʻi Loa Ridge are also there.

- [Kalihi and Pālama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalihi) are working-class neighborhoods with a number of government housing developments. Lower Kalihi, toward the ocean, is a light-industrial district.

- [Salt Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake,_Hawaii) and Āliamanu are (mostly) residential areas built in extinct [tuff cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_cone) along the western end of the Honolulu District, not far from [Honolulu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_International_Airport).

- [Moanalua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanalua) is two neighborhoods and a valley at the western end of Honolulu, and home to [Tripler Army Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripler_Army_Medical_Center).

- [Kamehameha Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamehameha_Heights,_Hawaii) is a northern suburb.

### Climate

Honolulu experiences a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_classification) *BSh*), with a mostly dry summer season, due to a [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) effect.[^cite_note-35] Despite temperatures that meet the tropical threshold of all months having a mean temperature of 64.4 °F (18.0 °C) or higher, the city receives too little precipitation to be classified as tropical.

Temperatures vary little throughout the year, with average high temperatures of 80–90 °F (27–32 °C) and average lows of 65–75 °F (18–24 °C). Nevertheless, there are slight seasons. The "winter" months from December to March can occasionally see lows fall below 67 °F (19 °C), whereas the "summer" from June to September can get a limited number of hot days achieving 90 °F (32 °C) or higher. This occurs on an average of only 32 days annually,[^cite_note-NOAA-36][^cite_note-37] with lows in the upper 50s °F (14–15 °C) once or twice a year. The highest recorded temperature was 95 °F (35 °C) on September 19, 1994, and August 31, 2019.[^cite_note-NOAA-36] The lowest recorded temperature was 52 °F (11 °C) on February 16, 1902, and January 20, 1969.[^cite_note-NOAA-36]

The annual average rainfall is 16.41 inches (417 millimeters),[^cite_note-NOAA-36] which mainly occurs from October through early April, with very little rainfall in the summer. However, both seasons experience a similar number of rainy days. Light showers occur in summer, while heavier rain falls during winter. Honolulu has an average of 278 sunny days and 89.2 rainy days per year.

Although the city is in the tropics, [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane) are quite rare. The last recorded hurricane that hit near Honolulu was Category 4 [Hurricane Iniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Iniki) in 1992. [Tornadoes](37530.md) are also uncommon and occur about every 15 years. [Waterspouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterspout) off the coast are also uncommon, hitting about every five years.[^cite_note-38]

Honolulu falls under the [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA) 12b Plant [Hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone).[^cite_note-39]

The average temperature of the sea ranges from 75.7 °F (24.3 °C) in March to 80.4 °F (26.9 °C) in September.[^cite_note-40]

The population of Honolulu is 350,964 as of the [2020 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), making it the 55th most populous city in the U.S. The city's population was 337,256 at the [2010 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census).[^cite_note-Census_2010-48]

The residential neighborhood of [East Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Honolulu,_Hawaii) is considered a separate census-designated place by the Census Bureau but is generally considered part of [Honolulu's urban core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_metropolitan_area). The population of East Honolulu was 50,922 as of 2020, increasing Honolulu's core population to over 400,000.[^cite_note-53]

In terms of race (including Hispanics in the racial counts), 54.8% were Asian, 17.9% were European, 1.5% were African American, 0.2% were Native American or Alaska Native, 8.4% were Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander, 0.8% were from "some other race", and 16.3% were from two or more races. Separately, Hispanic and Latino residents of any race made up 5.4% of the population.[^cite_note-Census_2010-48] In 1970, the Census Bureau reported Honolulu's population as 33.9% white and 53.7% Asian and Pacific Islander.[^cite_note-54]

Asian Americans are the majority of Honolulu's population, accounting for over 52%. The Asian ethnic groups are Japanese (19.9%), Filipino (13.2%), Chinese (10.4%), Korean (4.3%), Vietnamese (2.0%), Indian (0.3%), Laotian (0.3%), Thai (0.2%), Cambodian (0.1%), and Indonesian (0.1%).

Pacific Islander Americans are 8.4% of Honolulu's population. The Pacific Islander ethnic groups are people solely of Native Hawaiian ancestry (3.2%), Samoan Americans made up 1.5% of the population, Marshallese people make up 0.5%, and Tongan people comprise 0.3%. People of Guamanian descent made up 0.2% of the population and numbered 841.[^cite_note-Census_2010-48]

Metropolitan Honolulu, which encompasses all of [Oahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oahu) island, had a population of 953,207 as of the 2010 U.S. census and 1,016,508 in the 2020 U.S. census, making it the 54th-largest metropolitan area in the United States.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

## Economy



With symbolic native-styled architectural features, [First Hawaiian Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hawaiian_Center) is the tallest office building in Hawaii and home to a [Honolulu Museum of Art Spalding House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Museum_of_Art_Spalding_House) gallery.

The largest city and airport in the Hawaiian Islands, Honolulu acts as a natural gateway to the islands' large tourism industry, which brings millions of visitors and contributes \$10 billion annually to the local economy. Honolulu's location in the Pacific also makes it a large business and trading hub, particularly between [the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient) and [the West](21208200.md). Other important aspects of the city's economy include military defense, research and development, and manufacturing.[^cite_note-econ-57]

Among the companies based in Honolulu are:

- [Alexander & Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_&_Baldwin)

- [American Savings Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Savings_Bank)

- [Bank of Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Hawaii)

- [Central Pacific Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pacific_Bank)

- [First Hawaiian Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hawaiian_Bank)

- [Hawaii Medical Service Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Medical_Service_Association)

- [Hawaii Pacific Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Pacific_Health)

- [Hawaiian Electric Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Electric_Industries)

- [Matson Navigation Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matson_Navigation_Company)

- [The Queen's Health Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_Health_Systems)

[Hawaiian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Airlines),[^cite_note-58] [Island Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Air_\(Hawaii\)),[^cite_note-Island_Air-59] and [Aloha Air Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha_Air_Cargo) are headquartered in the city.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-CDPMap-61] Until it dissolved, [Aloha Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha_Airlines) was headquartered in the city.[^cite_note-62] At one time [Mid-Pacific Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Pacific_Airlines) had its headquarters on the property of [Honolulu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_International_Airport).[^cite_note-63]

In 2009, Honolulu had a 4.5% increase in average rent, maintaining it in the second most expensive rental market among 210 U.S. metropolitan areas.[^cite_note-2nd_most_expensive_rents-64] Similarly, the general cost of living, including gasoline, electricity, and most foodstuffs, is much higher than on the U.S. mainland, because the city and state have to import most goods.[^cite_note-\:0-29] One 2014 report found that cost of living expenses were 69% higher than the U.S. average.[^cite_note-65] According to a 2024 report from the Department of Business, Economic Development & Tourism, Honolulu experienced a 1.5 percentage point higher CPI-U compared to the 3.2% U.S. average increase in the first half of 2024.[^cite_note-66]

Banks based on the U.S. mainland have a minimal presence in Hawaii; residents typically use [Hawaiian-based banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._national_banks_of_Hawaii). First Hawaiian Bank is Hawaii's largest and oldest bank,[^cite_note-Hill_2008-67] headquartered at the [First Hawaiian Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hawaiian_Center), the state's tallest office building.[^cite_note-Emporis.com-68]

[Bishop Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Museum)

The [Bishop Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Museum) is Honolulu's largest museum. It has the state's largest collection of natural history specimens and the world's largest collection of Hawaiiana and Pacific culture artifacts.[^cite_note-69] The [Honolulu Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Zoo) is Hawaii's main [zoological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology) institution, while the [Waikiki Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikiki_Aquarium) is a working [marine biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology) laboratory. The Waikiki Aquarium partners with the [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi) and other universities worldwide. Established for appreciation and [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany), Honolulu is home to several gardens: [Foster Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_Botanical_Garden), [Liliʻuokalani Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lili%CA%BBuokalani_Botanical_Garden), [Walker Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_Estate), among others.

Established in 1900, the [Honolulu Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Symphony) is the second-oldest U.S. symphony orchestra west of the Rocky Mountains. Other classical music ensembles include the [Hawaii Opera Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Opera_Theatre). Honolulu is also a center for [Hawaiian music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_music). The main music venues include the [Hawaii Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Theatre), the [Neal Blaisdell Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Blaisdell_Center) Concert Hall and Arena, and the [Waikiki Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikiki_Shell). Honolulu also includes several venues for live [theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater), including the [Diamond Head Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Head_Theatre) and [Kumu Kahua Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumu_Kahua_Theatre).

The [Honolulu Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Museum_of_Art) has Hawaii's largest collection of Asian and Western art. It also has the largest collection of Islamic art, housed at the [Shangri La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri_La_\(Doris_Duke\)) estate. Since the merger of the Honolulu Academy of Arts and The Contemporary Museum, Honolulu (now called the [Honolulu Museum of Art Spalding House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Museum_of_Art_Spalding_House)) in 2011, the museum is also the state's only [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art) [museum](37585.md). The contemporary collections are housed at main campus ([Spalding House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spalding_House)) in [Makiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makiki) and a multi-level gallery in [downtown Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Honolulu) at the [First Hawaiian Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hawaiian_Center). The museum hosts a film and video program dedicated to arthouse and world cinema in the museum's Doris Duke Theatre, named for the museum's historic patroness [Doris Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Duke).[^cite_note-70]

The [Hawaii State Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_State_Art_Museum) (also downtown) has pieces by local artists as well as traditional [Hawaiian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_art). The museum is administered by the [Hawaii State Foundation on Culture and the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_State_Foundation_on_Culture_and_the_Arts).



Aerial view of [Diamond Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Head,_Hawaii)

Honolulu also annually holds the [Hawaii International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_International_Film_Festival) (HIFF). It showcases some of the best films from producers all across the Pacific Rim and is the largest "East meets West" style film festival of its sort in the United States.

## Sports

Honolulu's tropical climate lends itself to year-round activities. Honolulu has three large [road races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_running): the [Great Aloha Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Aloha_Run), [Honolulu Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Marathon), and [Honolulu Triathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Triathlon?action=edit\&redlink=1). [Ironman Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironman_Hawaii) was first held in Honolulu. It was the first ever Ironman triathlon event and is also the world championship. The Waikiki Roughwater Swim race is held annually off the beach of Waikiki on a 2.384 miles (3.837 km) course.[^cite_note-71]

Fans of spectator sports in Honolulu generally support the [football](18951490.md), volleyball, basketball, [rugby union](25405.md), [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league), and baseball programs of the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi_at_M%C4%81noa).[^cite_note-72] High school sporting events, especially football, are especially popular. Honolulu has no professional sports teams, with any prospective teams being forced to conduct extremely long travels for away games in the continental states. It was the home of the [Hawaii Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Islanders) ([Pacific Coast League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coast_League), 1961–87), [The Hawaiians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hawaiians_\(WFL\)) ([World Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_League), 1974–75), [Team Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Hawaii) ([North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%9384\)), 1977), and the [Hawaiian Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islanders) ([af2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Af2), 2002–04).

The [NCAA](60706.md) football [Hawaii Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Bowl) is played in Honolulu. Honolulu also hosted the [NFL](21211.md)'s annual [Pro Bowl](274117.md) every year from 1980 to 2016, with exception of the 2010 and 2015 editions, which were played in [Miami Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Life_Stadium) and [Glendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Phoenix_Stadium), respectively.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] From 1993 to 2008, Honolulu hosted [Hawaii Winter Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Winter_Baseball), featuring minor-league players from [Major League Baseball](38776.md), [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball), [Korea Baseball Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Baseball_Organization), and [independent leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_baseball).

Honolulu Little League baseball teams have been successful in the [Little League World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_League_World_Series), winning the championship in 2018 and 2022.[^cite_note-75] In 2024, Honolulu was the official home of the [Pokémon World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_World_Championships).

## Government



Completed in 1928, [Honolulu Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Hale) is the city and county seat.

[Rick Blangiardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Blangiardi) was elected mayor of Honolulu County on August 8, 2020, and began serving as the county's 15th mayor on January 2, 2021. The municipal offices of the [City and County of Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_County_of_Honolulu), including [Honolulu Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Hale), the seat of the city and county, are in the [Capitol District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Capital_Historic_District), as are the Hawaii state government buildings.[^cite_note-Historic_Honolulu-79]

The Capitol District is in the Honolulu [census county division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_county_division) (CCD), the urban area commonly regarded as the "City" of Honolulu. The Honolulu CCD is on the southeast coast of Oʻahu between [Makapuu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makapuu) and [Halawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halawa,_Hawaii). The division boundary follows the Koʻolau crestline, so Makapuʻu Beach is in the Ko'olaupoko District. On the west, the division boundary follows Halawa Stream, then crosses [Red Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hill_Underground_Fuel_Storage_Facility) and runs just west of [Aliamanu Crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake,_Hawaii), so that [Aloha Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha_Stadium), [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor) (with the [USS Arizona Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Arizona_Memorial)), and [Hickam Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickam_Air_Force_Base) are all in the island's Ewa CCD.[^cite_note-Honolulu_CCD-80]

The [Hawaii Department of Public Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Department_of_Public_Safety) operates the Oʻahu Community Correctional Center, the jail for the island of Oʻahu, in Honolulu CCD.[^cite_note-81]

The [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) operates post offices in Honolulu. The main Honolulu Post Office is by the international airport, at 3600 Aolele Street.[^cite_note-82] [Federal Detention Center, Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Detention_Center,_Honolulu), operated by the [Federal Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Prisons), is in the CDP.[^cite_note-83]

### Research institutions

Honolulu is home to three international affairs research institutions. The [Pacific Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Forum_CSIS), one of the world's leading Asia-Pacific policy research institutes and one of the first U.S. organizations to focus exclusively on Asia, has its main office on Bishop Street in downtown Honolulu. The [East–West Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Center) (EWC), an education and research organization established by Congress in 1960 to strengthen relations and understanding among the peoples and nations of Asia, the Pacific, and the U.S., is headquartered in [Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi_at_M%C4%81noa), Honolulu. The [Asia-Pacific Center for Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Center_for_Security_Studies) (APCSS), a [U.S. Department of Defense](7279897.md) institute, is based in Waikiki. [APCSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Center_for_Security_Studies) addresses regional and global security issues and supports the [U.S. Pacific Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Command) by developing and sustaining relationships among security practitioners and national security establishments throughout the region.

### Highways



Aerial view of H-1 (looking east) from Daniel K. Inouye International Airport heading into downtown Honolulu

Honolulu has been ranked as having the nation's worst traffic congestion, beating former record holder [Los Angeles](18110.md). Drivers spend on average over 58 hours per year on congested roadways.[^cite_note-102] The following [freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway), part of the [Interstate Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) serve Honolulu:

-  [Interstate H-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_H-1). Its western terminus is at [Kapolei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapolei,_Hawaii), where the roadway continues onto Farrington Highway. The H-1 passes [Hickam Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickam_Air_Force_Base) and [Daniel K. Inouye International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_K._Inouye_International_Airport), and runs through Pearl City before reaching downtown Honolulu. After continuing eastward through Makiki and Kaimuki, it ends at Waialae/Kahala as the roadway continues onto Kalanianole Highway.

-  [Interstate H-201](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_H-201)—known as the Moanalua Freeway and sometimes referred to by its former number, Hawaii State Route 78—connects two points along H-1: [Aloha Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha_Stadium) and [Fort Shafter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Shafter). Close to H-1 and Aloha Stadium, H-201 has an exchange with the western terminus of [Interstate H-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_H-3) to the windward side of Oʻahu ([Kaneohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaneohe,_Hawaii)). This complex of connecting ramps, some directly between H-1 and H-3, is in [Halawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halawa,_Hawaii).

-  [Interstate H-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_H-2) connects with the H-1 freeway at a junction near Waipahu and Pearl City. It passes Schofield Barracks before ending at Wahiawa, where it connects to the [North Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_\(Oahu\)).

-  [Interstate H-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_H-3) connects to H-1 at a junction near Hālawa, runs from Hālawa through the Ko'olau Range via the [Tetsuo Harano Tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuo_Harano_Tunnels) to Kaneohe, and ends at [Marine Corps Base Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Hawaii).

Other major highways that link Honolulu CCD with other parts of the Island of Oʻahu are:

-  [Pali Highway, (State Route 61)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Route_61), which crosses north over the Koʻolau range via the Pali Tunnels to connect to [Kailua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailua,_Hawaii) and [Kaneohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaneohe,_Hawaii) on the windward side of the Island.

-  [Likelike Highway, (State Route 63)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Route_63), which also crosses the Koʻolau mountains to Kaneohe via the Wilson Tunnels.

-  Kalanianaole Highway, State Route 72, which runs eastward from Waialae/Kahala to [Hawaiʻi Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawai%CA%BBi_Kai,_Hawaii) and around Oʻahu's east end to [Waimanalo Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waimanalo_Beach,_Hawaii).

- [Kamehameha Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamehameha_Highway), State Routes 80, 83, 99 and 830, which runs westward from near [Hickam Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickam_Air_Force_Base) to [Aiea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiea,_Hawaii) and through the center of the island and the North Shore, ending in [Kaneohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaneohe,_Hawaii).

- [Farrington Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrington_Highway), State Route 93, which runs along western leeward Oʻahu from Kaena Point through Waianae and Makaha before the start of the H-1. State Route 930 starts east-to-west in the north shore from Wailua to Kaena Point.

Like most major American cities, the Honolulu metropolitan has heavily congested traffic during rush hours, especially to and from the western suburbs of [Kapolei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapolei,_Hawaii), [ʻEwa Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BBEwa_Beach,_Hawaii), [Aiea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiea,_Hawaii), [Pearl City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_City,_Hawaii), [Waipahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waipahu,_Hawaii), and [Mililani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mililani,_Hawaii).

There is a Hawaii Electric Vehicle Demonstration Project (HEVDP).[^cite_note-103]

### Public transit

See also: [Skyline (Honolulu)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyline_\(Honolulu\)) and [TheBus (Honolulu)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheBus_\(Honolulu\))



A Skyline train departing [Hālawa station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C4%81lawa_station)

Honolulu is constructing a 20-mile (32 km) rail transit line that will connect Honolulu with cities and suburban areas near [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor) and in the Leeward and West Oʻahu regions. Approved in 2010 by public referendum, [Skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyline_\(Honolulu\)) aims to alleviate [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) for West Oʻahu commuters while aiding the westward expansion of the metropolitan area.[^cite_note-HART-104] The project has been criticized for its high cost, delays, and potential environmental impacts, but the line is expected to have large ridership. The line's first segment connects East Kapolei and Aloha Stadium and opened on June 30, 2023.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] The opening of the first phase of Skyline was delayed until 2023, as HART canceled the initial bids for the first nine stations, rebid the work as three packages of three stations each, and allowed more time for construction in the hope that increased competition on smaller contracts would drive down costs;[^cite_note-107] initial bids ranged from \$294.5 million to \$320.8 million, far surpassing HART's \$184 million budget.[^cite_note-108]

Electric street railways were operated in Honolulu by the now-defunct Honolulu Rapid Transit Company before World War II. Predecessors to the Honolulu Rapid Transit Company were the [Honolulu Rapid Transit and Land Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Rapid_Transit_and_Land_Company) (began 1903) and Hawaiian Tramways (began 1888).[^cite_note-109]

Established by former Mayor [Frank F. Fasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_F._Fasi) as the replacement for the Honolulu Rapid Transit Company (HRT), Honolulu's [TheBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheBus_\(Honolulu\)) system was honored in 1994–95 and 2000–01 by the [American Public Transportation Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Public_Transportation_Association) as "America's Best Transit System". TheBus operates 107 routes serving Honolulu and most major cities and towns on Oʻahu. TheBus comprises a fleet of 531 buses, and is run by the [nonprofit corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit_corporation) Oʻahu Transit Services in conjunction with the city Department of Transportation Services. As of 2006, Honolulu was ranked fourth for highest per-capita use of [mass transit in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-NTD_2006-110]

The island also features TheHandi-Van,[^cite_note-111] for riders who require para-transit operations. To be eligible for this service, riders must meet the requirements of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). TheHandi-Van has a fare of \$2 and is available from 4am to 1am. There is a 24-hour service within 3/4 of a mile of TheBus route 2[^cite_note-112] and route 40.[^cite_note-113] TheHandi-Van comprises a fleet of 160 buses. The parantransit branch also runs Human Services Transportation Coordination (HSTCP), which mainly provides transportation for people with disabilities, older adults, and people with limited incomes, assisted by the Committee for Accessible Transportation (CAT). Both organizations work together to provide transportation for elderly and persons with disabilities.

### Bicycle sharing

Since June 28, 2017, [Bikeshare Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikeshare_Hawaii?action=edit\&redlink=1) administers the bicycle sharing program in Oʻahu while Secure Bike Share operates the *Biki* system. Most *Biki* stations are between Chinatown/Downtown and Diamond Head, but a 2018 expansion added stations toward the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa Campus, Kapiʻolani Community College, Makiki, and Kalihi area.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

### Modal characteristics

According to the 2016 American Community Survey (five-year average), 56% of Urban Honolulu residents commuted to work by driving alone, 13.8% carpooled, 11.7% used public transportation, and 8.7% walked. About 5.7% commuted by bike, taxi, motorcycle or other forms of transportation, while 4.1% worked at home.[^cite_note-118]

The city of Honolulu has a high percentage of households without a motor vehicle. In 2015, 16.6% of Honolulu households were car-free, which increased slightly to 17.2% in 2016; by comparison, the United States national average was 8.7% in 2016. Honolulu averaged 1.4 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8.[^cite_note-119]

## Public safety

The [Honolulu Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Police_Department) is the primary law enforcement agency for the city and county of Honolulu and serves all of Oʻahu. The department has a mixed fleet of marked and unmarked patrol cars along with a subsidized vehicle program. Marked vehicles are white with blue stripes and white lettering reading HONOLULU POLICE. The Honolulu Police Department lets officers of a certain rank purchase a private vehicle for police use. Subsidized vehicles are unmarked but have a blue roof light.[^cite_note-120] Subsidized vehicles can be any make, model, or color, but must follow department rules and guidelines. Honolulu Police and Hawaii County Police on the Big Island are the only departments in the state or the nation with subsidized vehicles. All Hawaii Police keep their cruise blue lights on while on patrol.[^cite_note-121]

The [Honolulu Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Fire_Department) provides firefighting services and first responder level emergency medical services on Oʻahu. Emergency medical services at higher levels are provided by the Honolulu Emergency Medical Services. Fire trucks in Honolulu are yellow.[^cite_note-Honolulu_Fire_Services-122]
