**Christianity in Nigeria** represents one of several [religious traditions in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Nigeria), including [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Nigeria) and [Traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions).

Christianity arrived to Nigeria in the 15th century through [Augustinian](144980.md) and [Capuchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin) friars from Portugal. By 2020, it accounted for an estimated 45% of the Nigerian population, two-thirds of which are [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism).[^cite_note-1] As such, it remains the second-largest religion in Nigeria, behind a 50–55% Muslim majority.[^cite_note-2] According to the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), in 2011, Nigeria had the largest Christian population of any country in Africa, with more than 80 million people in Nigeria belonging to various [denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination).[^cite_note-Pew2011-3] Christianity is the majority religion in the southern and [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Belt) regions of Nigeria.[^cite_note-4] The 2024-2025 Afrobarometer found that the majority of Nigerian Christians were mostly [non-denominational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Christianity).[^cite_note-g779-5]

### Catholic Church



Saint Agnes Catholic church in Maryland, Lagos

The [Catholic Church](606848.md) has seen an increase of followers in Nigeria. In 2020, there were an estimated 32 million baptised Catholics in Nigeria.[^cite_note-13] The archdioceses of the [Catholic Church in Nigeria](414103.md) are [Abuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Abuja), [Benin City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Benin_City), [Calabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Calabar), [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Ibadan), [Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Jos), [Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Kaduna), [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Lagos), [Onitsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Onitsha), and [Owerri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Owerri).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Cardinal [Francis Arinze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Arinze) is a [Catholic cardinal](6221.md) from Nigeria.[^cite_note-16]

### Anglican Church of Nigeria

The ecclesiastical provinces of the [Church of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Nigeria) are [Aba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Aba), [Abuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Abuja), [Bendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Bendel), [Enugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Enugu), [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Ibadan), [Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Jos), [Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Kaduna), [Kwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Kwara), [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Lagos), [Lokoja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Lokoja), [The Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_the_Niger), [Niger Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Niger_Delta), [Ondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Ondo) and [Owerri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Owerri).[^cite_note-Site_of_the_Church_of_Nigeria-17] Its [primate](24672.md), since 2020, is [Henry Ndukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ndukuba).[^cite_note-Site_of_the_Church_of_Nigeria-17] The Church of Nigeria claimed about 18 million members in 2016, with an estimated 2 million members being active.[^cite_note-Christianity_Today-18]

### The Apostolic Church Nigeria

[The Apostolic Church Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Apostolic_Church_Nigeria) is a Pentecostal Christian denomination in Nigeria, affiliated with the [Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Church_\(1916_denomination\)). Its headquarters is in Lagos. It had 4.5 million members in 2016.[^cite_note-19]

### Assemblies of God

The [General Council of the Assemblies of God Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Council_of_the_Assemblies_of_God_Nigeria) has its origins in the Nigerian Church of Jesus Christ and a partnership with the [Assemblies of God USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God_USA) in 1934. The council was founded in 1964. It had 16,300 churches and 3.6 million members as of 2019.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Assemblies_of_God_Church,_Obalende_2019-21]

### Church of Christ in Nigeria

The Church of Christ in Nations (COCIN), formerly [Church of Christ in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ_in_Nigeria), is a Christian denomination in Nigeria. It was founded in 1904. Its headquarters is in Jos, Plateau State. It used to have the name of Ekklesiyar Kristi A Nigeria. It is estimated to have over 8,000,000 members.[^cite_note-22]

### Evangelical Church Winning All

The [Evangelical Church Winning All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Church_Winning_All) has about 6000 congregations and 6 million members.[^cite_note-ECWA_History-23] It was founded by SIM, a missions organization established in Nigeria in 1893.[^cite_note-ECWA_History-23]

### Evangelical Reformed Church of Christ

The [Evangelical Reformed Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Reformed_Church_of_Christ) was formed in Nasarawa State on 8 July 1916. The church has approximately 1.5 million members.[^cite_note-24]

### Lutheran Church of Christ in Nigeria

The [Lutheran Church of Christ in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church_of_Christ_in_Nigeria) (LCCN) is a major Lutheran denomination in Nigeria, a member of the Lutheran World Federation (LWF). It was established as an independent church in 1913 from the Sudan United Mission, Danish Branch (SUMD), known today as Mission Afrika. The LCCN now has an estimated 2,200,000 members in over 2,400 congregations nationwide.[^cite_note-25]

### Methodist Church Nigeria

The [Methodist Church Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church_Nigeria) is one of the largest Methodist denominations in the world and one of the largest Christian churches in Nigeria, with around two million members in 2000 [congregations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_\(congregation\)). It has seen exponential growth since the turn of the millennium.[^cite_note-26]

### Nigerian Baptist Convention

The [Nigerian Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Baptist_Convention) had about 6.5 million baptized members in 2008.[^cite_note-\:0-27] The Baptist Mission was started by Thomas Jefferson Bowen in 1850. It currently has thirty five conferences in different ecclesiastical in Nigeria. It has its headquarter in Dugbe, Ibadan, Oyo State.[^cite_note-\:0-27]

### Presbyterian Church of Nigeria

The [Presbyterian Church of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_of_Nigeria) has almost 4 million members in thousands of congregations mainly in [Nigeria](21383.md), but has regional Presbytery in [Togo](30138.md) as well as in [Benin](3459.md). It was founded in the mid-1800s, by ministers of the [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland). It is a member of the [World Communion of Reformed Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Communion_of_Reformed_Churches).[^cite_note-28]

### Redeemed Christian Church of God

The [Redeemed Christian Church of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redeemed_Christian_Church_of_God) ([RCCG](http://www.rccg.org/)) is a Pentecostal mega church and denomination founded in [Lagos](85232.md), Nigeria. The General overseer (most senior pastor) is [Enoch Adeboye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enoch_Adeboye), ordained in 1981. In 2008, it had 14,000 churches and 5 million members in Nigeria.[^cite_note-29]

### QIC-United Evangelical Church

The [QIC-United Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QIC-United_Evangelical_Church) (Founded as Qua Iboe Church) is a Christian denomination in Nigeria. It has existed since 1887.\[1] It has more than 1,000 congregations and 2,000,000 members.

### The Seventh-day Adventist Church

The [Seventh-day Adventist Church in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventist_Church_in_Nigeria) as of 2016 has close to 250,000 members throughout Nigeria divided into three different unions.[^cite_note-30]

### Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

[Within Nigeria, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_in_Nigeria) also has a growing presence. As of December 2025, the church claimed more than 200,000 members in the country[^cite_note-newsroom-31] and had established 880 congregations.[^cite_note-newsroom-31]

The church announced the creation of a new Owerri mission in Nigeria in 2016.[^cite_note-32]

### Other

In 1970, 87,000 [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) were present in Nigeria,[^cite_note-33] which grew to more than 360,000 by 2014.[^cite_note-34]

The [New Apostolic Church in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Apostolic_Church_in_Nigeria) reported 300,000 members in 2016.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

[Aladura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladura) is a classification of churches that abide by a Christian religious denomination or trend inspired by activities of progressive church elements, J.B. Sadare, D.O. Odubanjo, I.O. Sanya and others in 1918.[^cite_note-history-of-aladura-37] The denomination has over 3 million adherents worldwide. The Aladura movement started at Ijebu-Ode, Nigeria in 1918.[^cite_note-history-of-aladura-37] This movement later metamorphosed to Living Faith Church Worldwide (whose headquarters is the Faith Tabernacle) and to the Christ Apostolic Church. The [Church of the Lord (Aladura)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Lord_\(Aladura\)) is an African Initiated Church founded by Josiah Olunowo Ositelu in 1925, and inaugurated in 1930 in Ogere Remo, Ogun State, Nigeria. Ositelu was born on 15 May 1900 at Ogere, ijebu-Remo, Ogun State in Nigeria.

Since the 1990s, there has been significant growth in many other churches, independently started in Africa by Africans, particularly the evangelical Protestant ones. These include the mostly charismatic and Pentecostal denominations such as [Mountain of Fire and Miracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_of_Fire_and_Miracles), [Christ Embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Embassy), Streams of Joy International, [Celestial Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_Church_of_Christ) and Dominion City. These churches have further many millions of members and followers in Nigeria.[^cite_note-38] Estimates of Pentecostals and Charismatics in the country reach up to 60 million.[^cite_note-v233-39][^cite_note-Balogun_2022_p._225-40]

## National Church of Nigeria



The [National Church of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Church_of_Nigeria)

The [National Church of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Church_of_Nigeria) (previously known as the **Nigerian Ecumenical Centre** and officially known as the **National Christian Centre**) is a non-denominational church building of the [Christian Association of Nigeria](13255449.md), the umbrella body of many of Nigeria's Christian denominations.[^cite_note-41] The church is located in [Abuja](56823.md).

## Freedom of religion

Nigeria was ranked sixth on [Open Doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Doors)’ 2023 World Watch List, an annual ranking of the 50 countries where Christians face the most extreme persecution.[^cite_note-42] In 2022, the country was ranked seventh.

The ruins of a [Catholic church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_church) in [Akpanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akpanta?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nigeria](21383.md), after being attacked by [Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_people) [jihadists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadists) in 2025.

Multiple organizations such as International Committee on Nigeria (ICON) have called the killings a silent genocide.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The Catholic *[Crisis Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_Magazine)* has claimed that Christians are routinely denied land to build churches, that Christian students are denied Christian religious curricula in the primary and secondary levels, and instead are forced to study Islam and that they are denied jobs and promotions in government parastatals as well as the right to seek public office.[^cite_note-45] According to *The World Watch List* for 2024, 3,100 Christians were killed and 2,830 Christians were kidnapped in Nigeria.[^cite_note-46] In May 2025 a series of attacks carried out by Fulani herders killed up to 36 Christians.[^cite_note-47]

In November 2025, US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) threatened US military action in Nigeria over treatment of Christians.[^cite_note-48]

In Nigeria, the [Boko Haram insurgency](27868253.md) aims to establish an [Islamic state](2646438.md).[^cite_note-49] [University of Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Johannesburg) law professor Werner Nicolaas Nel has noted that this has resulted in the persecution of Christians in Nigeria.[^cite_note-Nel2020-50] There has been a tendency of "mischaracterisation of the situation as civil conflict".[^cite_note-Nel2020-50] The number of Nigerian Christians killed for their identification as Christians accounts for 70% of Christians being killed for their faith worldwide.[^cite_note-51] The academic *Journal of African Studies and Sustainable Development* published a paper in 2020 that noted that since 2015, over 12,000 Christians have been killed in Nigeria.[^cite_note-52] Christian human rights organisations, such as [Global Christian Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Doors) have provided higher figures, such as documenting in 2023 that 52,250 Christians were murdered for their faith in the previous fourteen years.[^cite_note-53] In 2025, this garnered international attention with United States president Donald Trump vowing military action in Nigeria if the attacks against Christians did not subside; on Christmas Day 2025, the United States targeted ISIS cells in Nigeria.[^cite_note-Connor2025-54][^cite_note-55]
