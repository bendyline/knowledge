The **Syrian Democratic Forces** (**SDF**)[^cite_note-60] was a [Kurdish](3821855.md)-led coalition of [left-wing](18499.md)[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] ethnic militias and rebel groups, which served as the official military wing of the [Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria) (DAANES; also unofficially known as Rojava) from 2015 to 2026.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Founded on 10 October 2015, the stated mission of the SDF was to create a [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular), [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), and [federalized Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalization_of_Syria). The SDF was opposed by Turkey, who viewed the group as an extension of the [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK), which is internationally designated a terrorist group.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]&#x20;

Formed as a rebel alliance in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) with [American](3434750.md) support,[^cite_note-68] the SDF was composed primarily of [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Kurds), [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_people), and [Assyrian/Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Syria), as well as some smaller [Armenian](387816.md), [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Turkmen), and [Chechen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechens) forces. It is militarily led by the [People's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Protection_Units) (YPG), a [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) militia which is designated as a terrorist group by both Turkey and [Qatar](8238258.md). The SDF also includes several ethnic militias and various factions of the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition)'s [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army).&#x20;

Opponents of the SDF included various [Islamist](15012.md), [Syrian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_nationalist), and pro-Turkish forces involved in the civil war. Major enemies included [al-Qaeda](1921.md) affiliates, the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL), the [Syrian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Army) (TFSA), the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md), and their allies. Up to 2019, the SDF focused primarily on ISIL,[^cite_note-69] successfully driving them from important strategic areas such as [Al-Hawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hawl), [Shaddadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaddadi), [Tishrin Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tishrin_Dam), [Manbij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij), [Tabqa](10513014.md), [Euphrates Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Dam), [Mansoura Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansoura_Dam), and ISIL's former capital of [Raqqa](858965.md).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] In March 2019, the SDF announced the total territorial defeat of ISIL in Syria, with the SDF [taking control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baghuz_Fawqani) of the last stronghold in [Baghuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghuz).[^cite_note-76] After that, the SDF was increasingly involved in combatting the TFSA and Turkish presence in northern Syria. In January 2026, the [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) [launched an offensive](82106178.md) against the SDF in [northeastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria), leading to the capture of Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor, ending in a [14-point ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#January_2026_agreement) negotiated through the US as a basis for integration.&#x20;

On 10 March 2025, the SDF [agreed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#10_March_agreement) to integrate into Syrian state institutions under the [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government). In January 2026, the [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) [launched an offensive](82106178.md) against the SDF in [northeastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria). On 18 January, a [14-point ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#January_2026_agreement) with the SDF, negotiated through the US envoy [Tom Barrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Barrack), was announced, under which the SDF started integrating into the Syrian government, and the governorates of [Raqqa](5317143.md) and [Deir ez-Zor](7445749.md) immediately handed over to the government, together with the administration of [prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner-of-war_camp) for [Islamic State](9087364.md) members, all border crossings and oil fields. On 25 August 2026, SDF commander-in-chief [Mazloum Abdi](59386296.md) declared the SDF's dissolution, and its complete integration into the [Syrian Army](3423927.md).&#x20;

## History

### Establishment

#### Foundation

The establishment of the SDF was announced on 11 October 2015 during a press conference in [al-Hasakah](7573335.md).[^cite_note-Kurdish_Question-2015-77] The alliance built on longstanding previous cooperation between the founding partners.[^cite_note-78] While the [People's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Protection_Units) (Yekîneyên Parastina Gel, YPG) and the [Women's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Protection_Units) (Yekîneyên Parastina Jin, YPJ) had been operating throughout the regions of the [Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria), the other founding partners were more geographically focused.&#x20;

Geographically focused on the [Euphrates Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Region) were the YPG's partners in the [Euphrates Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Volcano) joint operations room, several mainstream Syrian rebel factions of the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army), who had helped defend the Kurdish town of [Kobanî](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koban%C3%AE) during the [Siege of Kobanî](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Koban%C3%AE). [Liwa Thuwwar al-Raqqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_Thuwwar_al-Raqqa) had been expelled by the [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) and ISIL from the city of [Raqqa](858965.md) for its alliance with the YPG. The group participated in the [capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Abyad_offensive) of [Tell Abyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Abyad) from the Islamic State.&#x20;

Geographically focused on the [Jazira Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazira_Region) in northeast Syria were the [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) [Syriac Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Military_Council) (Mawtbo Fulhoyo Suryoyo, MFS) and the [al-Sanadid Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Sanadid_Forces) of the Arab [Shammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shammar) tribe, both of whom had cooperated with the YPG in fighting ISIL since 2013.[^cite_note-79] The MFS is further politically aligned with the YPG via their shared secular ideology of [democratic confederalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_confederalism), which in the Assyrian community is known as the *[Dawronoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawronoye)* movement.[^cite_note-80]&#x20;

Geographically focused on the Manbij Region was the [Army of Revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Revolutionaries) (Jaysh al-Thuwar, JAT), originally an alliance within the FSA of several groups of diverse ethnic and political backgrounds, who had in common that they had been rejected by the mainstream [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) for their [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism), anti-[Islamist](15012.md) views and affiliations.&#x20;

In 2017, then-Commander of [U.S. Special Operations Command](45204749.md) [Raymond Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_A._Thomas) stated that they were aware of Turkey's concerns about the YPG's ties to the PKK since 2015. For this reason, it was necessary for YPG to change their name and incorporate the word "democratic". Thomas explained that this is how the name Syrian Democratic Forces came to be.[^cite_note-81]&#x20;

#### Political representation

On 10 December 2015, after a two-day conference, the [Syrian Democratic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Democratic_Council) was established. Human rights activist [Haytham Manna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haytham_Manna) and [Ilham Ehmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Elham_Ehmed) were elected co-chairman/woman at its founding.[^cite_note-82] The Assembly establishing the Syrian Democratic Council was made up of 13 members of different ethnic, economic and political backgrounds.&#x20;

#### Ethnic makeup

Initially, the mostly Kurdish YPG/YPJ comprised the majority of SDF and dominated it organizationally.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAustin_Holmes20192–3-84] However, it has had smaller minorities of other communities involved in it from the beginning, including Christians, Yezidis, Turkmen and Arabs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAustin_Holmes20192–3,_7–8-85]&#x20;

The participation of thousands of Arab troops fighting under the SDF, in its [military councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Democratic_Forces_military_councils) as well as various militias and rebel groups, during the battle for Raqqa in 2015 initially made local Arab groups see that Arabs and the SDF could work together. This encouraged increased Arab membership at the time.[^cite_note-86] By 2017, the SDF was reported by the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) to have an Arab majority.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] A 2019 [Wilson Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_International_Center_for_Scholars) study also suggested that a majority of the Syrian Democratic Forces' personnel are Arabs. The study was based on a representative survey with 391 SDF fighters; of the total respondents, 68.7% were Arabs, 17.2% Kurds, 12.5% Christians, 0.9% Yezidis, and 0.6% Turkmens. By comparison, the SDF itself estimated at the time that 50–70% of its troops were Arabs, 30–50% Kurds, 5% Christians, 2% Yezidis, and 2% Turkmens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAustin_Holmes20199-89] Arabs comprised more than half of the SDF by 2019, and possibly earlier.[^cite_note-90]&#x20;

Unofficial figures, quoted in *Al Majalla* in 2021, similarly indicated a sizeable Arab majority, 65,000 out of 100,000 total. The Arab membership of the SDF may relate to the pay levels and conscription going on within the Arab majority Northeast Syria region, as the SDF has a mandatory 1 year of service for all men living in its area of control.[^cite_note-Mahmud-2023-54]&#x20;

#### 2015

At the time of its founding in late 2015, *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* described the SDF as "essentially a subsidiary of the Kurdish YPG".[^cite_note-93] At the end of October, the [al-Shaitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shaitat) tribal militia, the Desert Hawks Brigade, joined the SDF to fight ISIL in the southern countryside of [Hasakah Governorate](1429110.md).[^cite_note-94] In November, the FSA group [Euphrates Jarabulus Battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Jarabulus_Battalions) announced its accession to the SDF.[^cite_note-95] In December, members of the [Deir ez-Zor Governorate](7445749.md)-based Arab tribe al-Shaitat joined the SDF, sending fighters to [al-Shaddadah](39348357.md).[^cite_note-Ethnic_Maps_of_Kurd-2016-96]&#x20;

#### 2016

### Integration into Syrian government (2024–2026)

Main articles: [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government), [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md), and [Syrian peace process § 10 March agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#10_March_agreement)

Syrian president [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) announced the 14-point agreement on the ceasefire and the integration of the SDF, which was signed by SDF Commander-in-Chief [Mazloum Abdi](59386296.md)

#### 2026 northeastern Syria offensive

Main article: [2026 northeastern Syria offensive](82106178.md)

In January 2026, the [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) launched an offensive against the SDF in [northeastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria). On 18 January, a 14-point ceasefire agreement with the SDF, negotiated through the US envoy [Tom Barrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Barrack), was announced, under which the SDF is set to be integrated into the Syrian government, and the governorates of [Raqqa](5317143.md) and [Deir ez-Zor](7445749.md) immediately handed over to the government, together with the administration of [prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner-of-war_camp) for [Islamic State](9087364.md) members, all border crossings and oil fields.[^cite_note-HN-224]&#x20;

According to the consensus reached between SDF Commander Mazloum Abdi and Syrian presidential envoy Ziad al-Ayesh, the majority of SDF members will be reorganized into three brigades and integrated into the [60th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Division_\(Syria\)), led by Awad al-Jassem. In addition, a military brigade from Kobani will also be incorporated into a division in Aleppo Governorate.[^cite_note-225]&#x20;

On 20 August, during the centenary celebrations of the city of [Qamishli](524369.md) Abdi declared that the integration of the military, security, and administrative institutions into the Syrian state had been completed and that non-Syrian fighters affiliated with the SDF had withdrawn.[^cite_note-\:022-226][^cite_note-dissolved22-227][^cite_note-\:2-228]&#x20;

On 25 August, Abdi, alongside YPJ commander Newroz Ahmed, and co-president of the DAANES Executive Council, [Îlham Ehmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Elham_Ehmed), travelled to Damascus for a day of discussions, following which Abdi gave a press conference in which he declared the integration of the SDF into the Syrian state and its formal dissolution.[^cite_note-229] Numerous SDF units were not included in the deal and sidelined, as the Syrian Army refused to integrate the [Women's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Protection_Units) (YPJ) or any pro-SDF Arab units. Syrian Defense Minister [Murhaf Abu Qasra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murhaf_Abu_Qasra) claimed that allowing women to serve in the army was "against Islam" despite the anti-discrimination articles of the [Constitutional Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Declaration_of_the_Syrian_Arab_Republic) and the fact that all neighboring Muslim states allowed women in their militaries.[^cite_note-230] The YPJ subsequently held talks with the [Syrian Ministry of Interior](34679004.md) which demonstrated greater willingness to include armed women in its security forces.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232]&#x20;

## War crimes

See also: [Human rights in the Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria § Syrian Democratic Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_Democratic_Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria#Syrian_Democratic_Forces), and [People's Protection Units § Allegations concerning violations of international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Protection_Units#Allegations_concerning_violations_of_international_law)

On 15 March 2017, a video surfaced that showed members of the Northern Sun Battalion reportedly torturing an ISIL fighter, who had been captured while planting mines. One of these mines had reportedly killed nine fighters of the battalion, leading five others to take revenge on the ISIL militant. The Manbij Military Council condemned the act and announced that the involved Northern Sun Battalion fighters would be held for trial for violating the [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions).[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] The five accused were arrested on 17 March.[^cite_note-235]&#x20;

On 24 April 2024, [Amnesty International](18947898.md) reported that there had been large-scale human rights violations of more than 56,000 people, including 30,000 children and 14,500 women, held indefinitely in at least 27 detention facilities for those with "perceived IS affiliation". According to the report among those held in this detention system are hundreds of [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis), [Syrians](8862873.md), [Iraqis](4184636.md), and foreign citizens from nearly 74 other countries. The report described the trials as "flawed", and many of the people in these camps were detained without charges. Detainees are held in inhumane conditions and subjected to torture including severe beatings, [stress positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_position), and electric shocks with thousands having been forcibly disappeared. Women were also subjected to [sexual](731637.md) and [gender based violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_based_violence) by SDF security members as well as attacks by other prisoners for perceived "moral" infractions.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-2024-236] Amnesty International's secretary general [Agnès Callamard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agn%C3%A8s_Callamard) has accused the US government of playing a central role in the creation and maintenance of this system.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-2024-236]&#x20;

The [Syrian Network for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Network_for_Human_Rights) (SNHR) found that the SDF was responsible for the deaths of 274 children and 859 children were forcibly removed or forcibly disappeared by SDF in its annual report on violations against children in 2024. It was also reported that the SDF had attacked 48 schools and kindergartens and 12 health institutions.[^cite_note-237] Another report published by the same organization stated that the SDF was responsible for the deaths of 287 women and that 983 women have been unlawfully imprisoned by SDF.[^cite_note-238] Another report said that the SDF had imprisoned 61 teachers for not following the curriculum and expelled 550 teachers for refusing to join the army.[^cite_note-239] In another report published by SNHR, it was found that 3,417 people were arrested by the SDF and that no news was heard from them again. One of them, Syrian Kurdish opposition politician Amin Aisa al Ali, was detained by SDF forces without any legal justification. He was tortured and died in custody. His body was later returned to his family.[^cite_note-240] In a report published on 6 February 2025, the organisation determined that the SDF had killed 65 civilians, including one child and two women, with sniper fire in the city of [Aleppo](159244.md) in two months.[^cite_note-241]&#x20;

### Ethnic cleansing and forced displacement allegations

In June 2015, [Amnesty International](18947898.md) released a report accusing the YPG of forcibly displacing and destroying the homes of civilians.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-2015-242] The [US State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_State_Department) reacted by stating they would 'scrutinise' Amnesty International's accusations and that it had to determine if there was "any veracity to the claims", but showed concern by calling for any administrator in the area to rule "with respect for all groups regardless of ethnicity".[^cite_note-\:0-243] The report makes accusations of looting, coercing civilians to join their armed forces and the forced targeted displacement of 1400 families in the [Turkman villages of Hammam al-Turkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Turkmen#By_the_YPG), 800 Turkmen from Mela Berho and [Suluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suluk,_Syria) residents. The report offers unnamed witness testimony from reported victims, cross referenced with photo and video evidence, media reports, and satellite imagery to substantiate these reports.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-2015-242]&#x20;

The Turkish government claimed that the YPG was carrying out [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) of non-Kurdish populations as part of a plan to join the [Jazira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazira_Region) and [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_Region) regions into a single territory.[^cite_note-244]&#x20;

In a report published by the [United Nations](31769.md)' [Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Syrian Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_International_Commission_of_Inquiry_on_the_Syrian_Arab_Republic) on 10 March 2017, the Commission refuted Amnesty International's reports of ethnic cleansing, stating that "though allegations of 'ethnic cleansing' continued to be received during the period under review, the Commission found no evidence to substantiate reports that YPG or SDF forces ever targeted Arab communities on the basis of ethnicity".[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]&#x20;

In interviews, YPG spokespersons acknowledged that a number of families were in fact displaced. However, they placed the number at no more than 25,[^cite_note-248] and stated military necessity. They stated that the family members of terrorists maintained communications with them, and therefore had to be removed from areas where they might pose a danger. They further reported that ISIL was using civilians in those areas to plant car bombs or carry out other attacks on the YPG.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-2015-242]&#x20;

### Child soldiers

In June 2014, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) criticized the YPG for accepting minors into their ranks,[^cite_note-249] picking up on multiple earlier reports of teenage fighters serving in the YPG. A report by the [United Nations](31769.md) [Secretary General](162415.md) stated that 24 minors under age of 18 had been recruited by the YPG, with 124 having been recruited by the Free Syrian Army and 5 by the Syrian Arab Army.[^cite_note-250] In response, the YPG and YPJ signed the [Geneva Call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Call) *Deed of Commitment* protecting children in armed conflict, prohibiting sexual violence and against gender discrimination in July 2014,[^cite_note-251] and Kurdish security forces (YPG and [Asayish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asayish_\(Syria\))) began receiving human rights training from Geneva Call and other international organisations with the YPG pledging publicly to demobilize all fighters under 18 within a month and began to enact disciplinary measures against commanders of the units that had involved in corruption and accepting recruit under age of 18 to their ranks.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] In October 2015 the YPG demobilized 21 minors from the military service in its ranks.[^cite_note-254]&#x20;

In response to reports issued by international organisations such as Human Rights Watch,[^cite_note-255] the general command of the SDF issued a military order prohibiting the recruitment of children.[^cite_note-256] On 29 June 2019, Abdi, as representative of the SDF, signed the action plan of the [United Nations](31769.md) aiming to prevent the enlistment of child soldiers in the armed forces. The action plan was signed to address the inclusion of the YPG in the SDF.[^cite_note-257]&#x20;
