**Kure** (呉市, *Kure-shi*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[kɯꜜ.ɾe, kɯ.ɾeꜜ.ɕi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese), locally [\[kɯ.ɾeꜜ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-1]) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Japan) in the [Hiroshima Prefecture](179797.md), Japan. As of 30 April 2023, the city had an estimated population of 208,024 in 106,616 households and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 590 persons per km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-Kure-hp-2] The total area of the city is 352.80 square kilometres (136.22 sq mi). With a strong industrial and naval heritage, Kure hosts the second-oldest naval [dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dockyard) in Japan and remains an important base for the [Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force](2370744.md).

## History

The area of Kure is part of ancient [Aki Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_Province), and the port of Kure was an important seaport for [Hiroshima Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_Domain) in the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period).

The [Kure Naval District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure_Naval_District) was first established in 1889, leading to the construction of the [Kure Naval Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure_Naval_Arsenal) and the rapid growth of steel production and shipbuilding in the city. Kure was formally incorporated on October 1, 1902. From 1889 until the end of the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War), the city served as the headquarters of the [Kure Naval District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure_Naval_District).

Kure dockyards recorded a number of significant engineering firsts including the launching of the first major domestically built capital ship, the battlecruiser [*Tsukuba*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cruiser_Tsukuba) (1905)[^cite_note-3] and the launching of the largest battleship ever built, the [*Yamato*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_battleship_Yamato) (1940).[^cite_note-4]

During the Pacific War, Kure acted as the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy)'s single-largest naval base and arsenal. Most of the city's industry and workforce were employed in the service of the naval installations, munitions factories and associated support functions. In the later stages of the conflict Kure came under sustained aerial bombardment culminating in the [bombing of Kure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Kure) in June and July 1945.

From February 1946 until the end of Japan's postwar occupation in 1952, military establishments in Kure served as the operational headquarters for the [British Commonwealth Occupation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth_Occupation_Force).

Since 2005, Kure has attracted attention as a tourism center with the [Yamato Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Museum) hosting a 1\:10 scale model of the *Yamato* alongside a waterfront JMSDF museum of Japanese naval history.

The city continues as a major maritime center hosting both the dockyards of [Japan Marine United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Marine_United) and numerous shore-based facilities of the JMSDF including training centers and a major hospital. The city serves as the home port of an Escort Flotilla (Destroyers), a Submarine Flotilla and the Training Squadron of the JMSDF Regional Kure District.

### Historic timeline

- July 1, 1889 — [Kure Naval District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure_Naval_District) established.

- 1895 — Kure naval shipyard established, initially as a subsidiary of the [Onohama Shipyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onohama_Shipyards) in Kobe.

- October 1, 1902 — The towns of Washō and Futagawa and the villages of Miyahara and Sōyamada merge to form the city of Kure.

- November 10, 1903 — [Kure Naval Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure_Naval_Arsenal) established.

- December 27, 1903 [Kure rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure_Line) opens providing direct rail access to [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima)

- April 1, 1928 — The towns of Kegoya, Yoshiura, and Aga merge into Kure.

- April 21, 1941 — The town of Nigata and the village of Hiro incorporated into Kure.

- March 19, 1945 — US Navy aircraft [attack Japanese warships at Kure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Kure_\(March_1945\))

- May 5, 1945 — Bombing of Hiro Naval Arsenal.

- June 22, 1945 — Bombing of Kure Naval Arsenal.

- July 1, 1945 — Kure Air Raid.

- July 24–28, 1945 — Battle of Kure, American bombers [attack the remaining fleet in Kure Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Kure_\(July_1945\)).

- July 1, 1954 — Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force founded.

- October 1, 1956 — The town of Tennō and the village of Shōwa in [Aki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_District,_Hiroshima), and the village of Gōhara in [Kamo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamo_District,_Hiroshima) merge into Kure.

- November 1, 2000 — Kure becomes a [Special City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_cities_of_Japan)

- April 1, 2003 — The town of [Shimokamagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimokamagari,_Hiroshima) (from [Aki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_District,_Hiroshima)) was merged into Kure.

- April 1, 2004 —The town of [Kawajiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawajiri,_Hiroshima) (from [Toyota District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_District,_Hiroshima)) was merged into Kure.

- March 20, 2005 — The towns of [Ondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondo,_Hiroshima), [Kurahashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurahashi,_Hiroshima) and [Kamagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamagari,_Hiroshima) (all from [Aki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_District,_Hiroshima)), and the towns of [Yasuura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuura,_Hiroshima), [Toyohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyohama,_Hiroshima) and [Yutaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutaka,_Hiroshima) (all from [Toyota District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_District,_Hiroshima)) were merged into Kure.

- April 1, 2016 — Kure officially became a [Core city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_city) with increased local autonomy

## Government

Kure has a [mayor-council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council) form of government with a directly elected mayor and a [unicameral](204304.md) city council of 31 members. Kure contributes five members to the Hiroshima Prefectural Assembly. In terms of national politics, the city is part of the Hiroshima 5th district of the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_Japan) of the [Diet of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_of_Japan).

## Geography

JMSDF submarine flotilla in Kure



Exterior view of the [Yamato Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Museum) and adjacent JMSDF Kure Museum

Kure is located 20 kilometres (10 mi) south-east of Hiroshima city and faces the [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea). Surrounded by steep hillsides to the north, the two major commercial and industrial centers of the city are bisected by Mount Yasumi 497 m (1,631 ft). The city is next to the [Setonaikai National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setonaikai_National_Park). As well as densely populated urban and industrial centers, the city also incorporates sparsely inhabited outlying islands such as [Kurahashi-jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurahashi-jima), Shimo-kamagari, Kami-kamagari and Toyoshima.

### Primary and secondary education

Kure has 37 public elementary schools, 25 public junior high schools and one public high school operated by the city government, and seven public high school operated by the Hiroshima Prefectural Board of Education. There are also one private middle school and three price high schools. The prefecture also operates two special education schools for the disabled.
