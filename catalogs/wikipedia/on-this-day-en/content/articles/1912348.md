**Kentucky State University** (**KSU**, and **KYSU**) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) [historically black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_colleges_and_universities) [land-grant university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university) in [Frankfort, Kentucky](57834.md), United States. Founded in 1886 as the **State Normal School for Colored Persons**, and becoming a land-grant college in 1890, KSU is the second-oldest state-supported institution of higher learning in Kentucky.[^cite_note-KSUHistory-1]

## History

Kentucky State University was chartered in May 1887 as the [State Normal School for Colored Persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school), only the second state-supported institution of higher learning in Kentucky.[^cite_note-KSUHistory-1] During the euphoria of Frankfort's 1887 centennial celebration, the city donated \$1,500 towards the purchase of land for a new college on a bluff overlooking Frankfort.[^cite_note-3]

The new school formally opened on October 11, 1887, with three teachers, 55 students, and John H. Jackson as president.[^cite_note-KSUHistory-1] Recitation Hall (now Jackson Hall), the college's first permanent building, was erected in that year.

KSU became a [land-grant college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university) in 1890 following the passage of the [Morrill Land-Grant Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrill_Land-Grant_Acts), and the departments of [home economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_economics), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), and [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) were added to the school's curriculum.[^cite_note-KSUHistory-1][^cite_note-\:0-4] The school produced its first graduating class of five students in the spring of that year. A high school was organized in 1893. This expansion continued into the 20th century in both name and program. In 1902, the name was changed to Kentucky Normal and Industrial Institute for Colored Persons. The name was changed again in 1926 to **Kentucky State Industrial College for Colored Persons** (**KSIC**).

In 1929, the high school was discontinued by president [Rufus B. Atwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_B._Atwood), since students were now entering college with a high school education.[^cite_note-\:0-4] In 1938, the school was named the **Kentucky State College for Negroes**.[^cite_note-KSUHistory-1] The term "for Negroes" was dropped in 1952.

The civil engineering program was started in 1942 after the [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP) threatened a lawsuit on behalf of a black student who wanted to attend the engineering program at the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky).[^cite_note-5]

Dr. [Martin Luther King Jr.](20076.md) delivered the commencement speech at the 1957 [graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) ceremonies titled, "Facing the Challenge of a New Age".[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

In 1960, the first white student enrolled.[^cite_note-\:0-4] **Kentucky State College** became a university in 1972, renamed Kentucky State University.[^cite_note-\:0-4]\


On December 9, 2025, a [self-defense shooting occurred at the university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kentucky_State_University_shooting), leaving one dead and one in critical condition. The shooter, a father of a student at the university, was initially charged with murder and first-degree assault, but on December 23, the grand jury accepted the shooter's self-defense claim and decided not to return an indictment.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] This is the second shooting to occur at the university, the previous incident having occurred just a few months earlier on August 17.[^cite_note-13]

Students are divided into five colleges, four associate degrees, 55 undergraduate degrees, and six postgraduate programs.[^cite_note-KSUQuickFacts20132014-17][^cite_note-KSUAcademicPrograms-18]

- College of Agriculture, Food Science, and Sustainable Systems

- College of Arts and Sciences

- College of Business and Computer Science

- College of Professional Studies

The university also offers five liberal study degrees through the Whitney Young School (WYS) of Honors and Liberal Studies, which consists of a Honors Program, an Integrative Studies Program, and an International Studies Program.[^cite_note-19] The degrees include Africana Studies and Liberal Studies.[^cite_note-KSUAcademicPrograms-18]

### Demographics

As of 2024, Kentucky State University was host to 1,932 undergraduate students. African Americans comprised 81% of the undergraduate student body.[^cite_note-CPE-20]

### Library

The Paul G. Blazer Library,[^cite_note-21] constructed in 1960,[^cite_note-22] houses a collection of more than 700,000 items includes extensive reference, periodical, and circulating collections of materials such as books, videos, microforms, sound recordings, and others, to aid students in their course work and research.[^cite_note-23] It is named after [Paul G. Blazer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_G._Blazer), a strong supporter of education[^cite_note-book_1992-24] who was the founder and [CEO](52234.md) of [Ashland Oil and Refining Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland_Inc.) in [Ashland, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland,_Kentucky).

### Pawpaw program

KYSU has the world's largest pawpaw (*[Asimina triloba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asimina_triloba)*) research planting.[^cite_note-25] The research program was started in 1990 with the aim of developing pawpaw as a new tree-fruit crop for Kentucky.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Pawpaw is the largest native fruit in the United States and has very few diseases compared to other orchard crops. KYSU is the site of the [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA) [National Clonal Germplasm Repository](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Clonal_Germplasm_Repository) for *Asimina&#x20;*&#x73;pecies[^cite_note-28] and the pawpaw orchards at KYSU contain over 1,700 trees. Research activities include germplasm collection and variety trials, and efforts are directed towards improving propagation, understanding fruit ripening and storage, and developing orchard management practices. Cultivation is best in [hardiness zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) 5-9 and trees take 7–8 years from seedling to fruiting. KYSU has created the three cultivars 'KSU-Atwood', 'KSU-Benson', and 'KSU-Chappell', with focus on better flavors, higher yields, vigorous plants, and low seed-to-pulp ratios.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-ccd-cp-14-31][^cite_note-kysu-propagation-32][^cite_note-kysu-cultiv2009-33]

## Athletics

Main article: [Kentucky State Thorobreds and Thorobrettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_State_Thorobreds_and_Thorobrettes)

Kentucky State University teams participate as a member of the [Division II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_II) [Southern Intercollegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Intercollegiate_Athletic_Conference). The school's mascot are the Thorobreds. Men's sports include baseball, basketball, cross country, football, golf, and indoor and outdoor track and field; while women's sports include basketball, cross country, indoor and outdoor track and field, softball, and volleyball.[^cite_note-KSUThorobreds-34] Kentucky State's main rivals include [Tennessee State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_State_Tigers_and_Lady_Tigers), [West Virginia State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_State_Yellow_Jackets), and [Central State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_State_Marauders_and_Lady_Marauders).

The men's basketball team was national champions in 1970, 1971, and 1972 at the NAIA level.

The Exum Center, the university's athletic and recreational complex, was named after William Exum, the first African-American varsity football player at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Badgers_football).[^cite_note-35] Exum was hired as head of KSU's Physical Education department in 1949, and later made head of the Athletics department. He then became manager of the United States Track and Field teams at the [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics) and [1976 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Summer_Olympics). Exum retired from KSU in 1980.

## Mighty Marching Thorobreds

Kentucky State University's marching band is named the Mighty Marching Thorobreds (MMT). MMT is one of the largest student organizations on campus with over 200 members. MMT has several notable performances including the [Honda Battle of the Bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Battle_of_the_Bands) in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia) and the National Battle of the Bands in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas). MMT is accompanied by the K-Rettes danceline and Silk Flag Corps.[^cite_note-36]

## Presidents

As of December 2025, Kentucky State University has had 18 permanent presidents. Four presidents have served multiple terms, with each term counting as a separate presidency. John Henry Jackson (1886–1898, 1907–1910), James Shelton Hathaway (1900–1907, 1910–1912), Green Pinckney Russell (1912–1923, 1924–1929) and Raymond M. Burse (1982–1989, 2014–2016) all served as multiple-term presidents. As an example, KSU considers Jackson as its first and fourth president.[^cite_note-past_presidents-40] This list will following the numbering system as given by Kentucky State University in their "Timeline of KSU History".[^cite_note-41]
