**Sylhet Division**[^cite_note-16] ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): সিলেট বিভাগ, pronounced [\[sileʈ bibʱaɡ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bengali) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2f/LL-Q9610\_%28ben%29-Titodutta-%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B2%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%9F.wav/LL-Q9610\_%28ben%29-Titodutta-%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B2%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%9F.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2f/LL-Q9610_%28ben%29-Titodutta-%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B2%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%9F.wav/LL-Q9610_%28ben%29-Titodutta-%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B2%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%9F.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the northeastern [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Bangladesh) of [Bangladesh](3454.md). It covers an area of approximately 12,000 km<sup>2</sup> (4,600 sq mi), and shares international borders with the [Indian states](375986.md) of [Meghalaya](326019.md), [Assam](186162.md) and [Tripura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura) to the north, east and south respectively—while domestically it adjoins the divisions of [Chattogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Division) to the southwest and [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Division) and [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh_Division) to the west. Sylhet's [eponymous capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet) is the [fifth-largest urban agglomeration in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Bangladesh).&#x20;

Prior to [Partition in 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), it included [Karimganj subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj_district) (presently in [Barak Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Valley), Assam, India). However, Karimganj (including the [thanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thana) of [Badarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badarpur,_Assam), [Patharkandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patharkandi) and [Ratabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratabari_Assembly_constituency)) was inexplicably severed from Sylhet by the [Radcliffe Boundary Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Line). According to [Niharranjan Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niharranjan_Ray), it was partly due to a plea from a delegation led by [Abdul Matlib Mazumdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Matlib_Mazumdar).[^cite_note-\:1-17]

## Etymology



Sirote was a name used by Europeans in the 1700s

The Sylhet Division is named after its headquarters, the city of [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet). Sylhet is the [anglicisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation_\(linguistics\)) of শিলহট (*Śilhôṭ*), one of the archaic native names for the city. The local name is generally thought to be directly derived from শ্রীহট্ট (*Śrīhaṭṭa*), the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) name of the city.[^cite_note-18] The city of Śrīhaṭṭa takes its name from *[Śrīhaṭṭanātha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattanath)*, the [tutelary deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutelary_deity) of the Nātha dynasty who promoted the early settlement of [Nāthas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nath) in the Surma and [Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Valley) valleys between the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, founding the Śrīhaṭṭa *[janapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janapada)* and establishing *Śrīhaṭṭanātha* idols across the region.[^cite_note-19] The later [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) [rajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja) of Sylhet, such as [Gour Govinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gour_Govinda), continued to pay tribute to the deity as *Hāṭkeśvara* or *Haṭṭanātha* as evident from the [Devipurana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas) and copper-plate inscriptions.[^cite_note-achyut-20]

## History

Main articles: [History of Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sylhet) and [Undivided Sylhet district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undivided_Sylhet_district)



The Assam Province's Sylhet District contained Karimganj.

In 1874, the current Sylhet Division, which included [Karimganj District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj_District), was entirely known as the 'Sylhet district'. On 16 February 1874, Sylhet was separated from mainland Bengal to be made a part of the [non-regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-regulation_province) [Chief Commissioner's Province of Assam (Northeast Frontier Province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Commissioner's_Province_of_Assam) in order to facilitate Assam's commercial development.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The people of Sylhet submitted a memorandum to the Viceroy protesting the inclusion in Assam.[^cite_note-23] The protests subsided when the Viceroy, [Lord Northbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Baring,_1st_Earl_of_Northbrook), visited Sylhet to reassure the people that education and justice would be administered from Bengal,[^cite_note-24] and when the people in Sylhet saw the opportunity of employment in tea estates in Assam and a market for their produce.[^cite_note-25] In 1905, Sylhet district rejoined Bengal as a part of the new *Surma Valley Division* of [Eastern Bengal and Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_and_Assam). In 1912, the then Sylhet district was once again moved to the newly created [Assam Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Province) alongside the other districts of the Surma Valley Division. Historically, the entire Sylhet region was a single district within the *Surma Valley and Hill Districts* Division as part of the Assam Province.[^cite_note-26]

During the partition of India in 1947, a plebiscite was held to determine whether the Sylhet region would remain in India or join East Pakistan. [Abdul Matlib Mazumdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Matlib_Mazumdar) led a delegation advocating for the region to remain with India. However, due to demands from the Muslim League and support from Assam's political leaders at the time,[^cite_note-27] the plebiscite resulted in Sylhet's transfer to Pakistan by a narrow margin. Allegations of electoral fraud and irregularities were raised, but the results stood.[^cite_note-28] However, the district's [Karimganj subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj_district) was given to India by Cyril Radcliffe, after apparently being pleaded by a delegation led by Abdul Matlib Mazumdar. The four other subdivisions ([North Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sylhet), [South Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sylhet), [Habiganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habiganj_District) and [Sunamganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunamganj_District)) joined the [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan); subsequently forming [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal)'s 'Sylhet district' in the Chittagong division.[^cite_note-\:1-17]

Following the [independence of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Bangladesh) in 1971, Sylhet became part of the new nation. In 1984, the four subdivisions of Sylhet District were upgraded to districts as part of [Chief Martial Law Administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Martial_Law_Administrator) [Hussain Muhammad Ershad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Muhammad_Ershad)'s decentralisation programme. The four districts remained part of [Chittagong Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Division) until 1995, when they formed the new Sylhet Division.[^cite_note-29]

The Sylhet Division has a "friendship link" with the city of [St Albans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Albans), in the United Kingdom. The link was established in 1988 when the [St Albans District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Albans_District_Council) supported a housing project in Sylhet as part of the International Year of Shelter for the Homeless. Sylhet was chosen because it is the area of origin for the largest ethnic minority group in St Albans.[^cite_note-30]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Sylhet)



The Mulnicherra Estate is the oldest tea garden in South Asia

The area has over 150 tea gardens out of a total of 166 tea estates located all over Bangladesh.[^cite_note-Bangladesh_Tea_Association-31]

The plantations, or gardens, were mostly developed during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), the [Malnicherra Tea Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malnicherra_Tea_Estate) being established in Sylhet in the year 1854. The plantations were started by the British, and the managers still live in the white timber houses built during the Raj. The bungalows stand on huge lawns. The service and the lifestyle of managers are still unchanged.[^cite_note-Bangladesh_Tea_Association-31]

Numerous projects and businesses in the city and in large towns have been funded by Sylhetis living and working abroad. As of 1986, an estimated 95 percent of ethnic [British Bangladeshis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Bangladeshi) originated from or had ancestors from the Sylhet region.[^cite_note-32]

Sylhet has also benefited from tourism. There are many natural landmarks people tend to visit, such as the [Keane Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keane_Bridge), [Ali Amjad's Clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Amjad's_Clock), [Lalakhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalakhal), [Jaflong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaflong), [Madhabkunda waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhabkunda_waterfall), [Ratargul Swamp Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratargul_Swamp_Forest), [Hakaluki Haor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakaluki_Haor), [Lawachara National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawachara_National_Park), [Tanguar Haor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanguar_Haor) and [Bichnakandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bichnakandi).[^cite_note-33] Sylhet is also considered to be the spiritual capital of Bangladesh, due to the resting place of [Shah Jalal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jalal), a Sufi saint who spread Islam in Bangladesh, along with hundreds of his disciples.[^cite_note-34] The [Sylhet Shahi Eidgah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Shahi_Eidgah) is a famous place where Eid prayers take place and it is one of the largest Eidgahs in Bangladesh, built by [Farhad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farhad_Khan) during the reign of Mughal emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb).[^cite_note-35] There are numerous hotels and resorts, particularly in [Sreemangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sreemangal_Upazila) and [Bahubal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahubal_Upazila).[^cite_note-36]

## Governance



[Osmani International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmani_International_Airport)&#x20;



Sylhet Railway station

In 1995, Sylhet split from Chittagong Division and was declared as the 6th division of the country. The Sylhet Division is overseen by the Divisional Commissioner, the current Divisional Commissioner is Md. Mashiur Rahman.[^cite_note-37] The Sylhet Division is divided into four districts ([Habiganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habiganj_District), [Moulvibazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulvibazar_District), [Sunamganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunamganj_District) and [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_District)) and further divided into 35 [upazilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upazila) (sub-districts). These upazilas are further divided into 323 [union parishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_parishad). Each union is roughly divided into 9 wards before going to village-level. There are roughly 10,185 villages in the Division. The Division hosts 19 [Municipal corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation) known as [pourashavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pourashava), and one city corporation in [Sylhet city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_City_Corporation).[^cite_note-38] It also has 19 [Parliamentary constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Parliamentary_constituencies_in_Bangladesh).[^cite_note-39] The headquarters of the Sylhet Division is the city of Sylhet in [Sylhet Sadar Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Sadar_Upazila), Sylhet District. Pre-partition Sylhet's [Karimganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj_district) has been governed by India since 1947.[^cite_note-40]

## Geography



The highest peak in the region is [Kala pahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_pahar) located in the Longla Ridge (Hararganj-Singla range).



[Hakaluki Haor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakaluki_Haor) is the largest [haor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haor) in Bangladesh.

Geographically the region is surrounded by hillocks (known as *tilla*s) from all three sides except its western plain boundary with the rest of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal). In the south of the region ([Habiganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habiganj_District), [Moulvibazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulvibazar_District)), eight hill ranges enter the plains of Sylhet running uniformly from the west to the east. They are: Raghunandan, Dinarpur-Shatgaon, Balishira, Bhanugach-Rajkandi, Hararganj-Singla, Patharia, Pratapgarh-Duhalia and Sorrispur-Siddheswar hill ranges. At the centre of the region is also an isolated range known as the Ita Hills.[^cite_note-heads-42]

The region is considered one of the most picturesque and archaeologically rich regions in South Asia. It is home to three [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park); the [Lawachara National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawachara_National_Park), [Khadim Nagar National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadim_Nagar_National_Park) and [Satchari National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satchari_National_Park), as well as numerous smaller parks and forests such as the [Ratargul Swamp Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratargul_Swamp_Forest), [Rema-Kalenga Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rema-Kalenga_Wildlife_Sanctuary). Its burgeoning economy has contributed to the regional attractions of landscapes filled with fragrant orange and pineapple gardens as well as tea plantations. The region has a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Am*) bordering on a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cwa*) at higher elevations. The rainy season from April to October is hot and humid with very heavy showers and thunderstorms almost every day, whilst the short dry season from November to February is very warm and fairly clear. Nearly 80% of the annual average rainfall of 4,200 millimetres (170 in) occurs between May and September.[^cite_note-43]

The [physiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiography) of the division consists mainly of hill soils, encompassing a few large depressions known locally as "[beels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beel)" which can be mainly classified as [oxbow lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbow_lake), caused by tectonic subsidence primarily during [the earthquake of 1762](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1762_Arakan_earthquake).[^cite_note-heads-42]

Geologically, the division is complex having diverse sacrificial geomorphology; high topography of [Plio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliocene)-[Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) age such as the [Khasi and Jaintia Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_Hills) and small hillocks along the border. At the centre there is a vast low laying flood plain of recent origin with saucer shaped depressions, locally called *[haors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haor)*. There are many haors in the region and the largest ones include [Hakaluki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakaluki_Haor), Kawadighi, [Tanguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanguar_Haor) and Hail. Available limestone deposits in different parts of the region suggest that the whole area was under the ocean in the [Oligo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligocene)-[Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene). In the last 150 years, three major earthquakes hit the city, at a magnitude of at least 7.5 on the [Richter Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richter_Scale), the last one took place in 1918, although many people are unaware that Sylhet lies on an earthquake prone zone.[^cite_note-ds10Feb2006-44]

### Flora and fauna

The region is home to the [Asian elephant](379035.md) and the [One-horned rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-horned_rhinoceros), mostly towards the south. Tigers and leopards were once found throughout the region. Other notable fauna include the [Sambar deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambar_deer), [Indian hog deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_hog_deer), [Sylhet hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hara_jerdoni) and [Sylhet roofed turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_roofed_turtle).[^cite_note-allen-45]

The Asian elephant were once found in small numbers in places such as Chapghat, Bhanugach, Chamtolla, Mahram and the Raghunandan hills. More abundantly they are found near streams in Singla and Langai.[^cite_note-heads-42]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Sylhet)

### Language



The "Nagari Chattar" (Nagari Square), built near Surma river in the city of [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet), consists of the [Sylheti Nagri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylheti_Nagri) script.

The official language of Sylhet is [Standard Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), which is used in education and all government affairs in the division. [Sylheti dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylheti_dialect) is the most widely spoken in the division. The [Adivasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi) and tea labourers brought over during the British colonial rule also have their own native languages such as [Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_language), [Kuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_language), [Laiunghtor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laleng?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language), [Bishnupriya Manipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupriya_Manipuri_language), [Hajong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajong_language), [Garo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_language), [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language), [Kurmi creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurmi), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), [Bhumij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_language) and [Tripuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_language).[^cite_note-musahar-46]

### Architecture

See also: [Bengali architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_architecture), [Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture), and [Mughal architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture)

The intense building of mosques which took place during the Sultanate era indicates the rapidity with which the locals converted to Islam. Today, mosques are present in most Muslim-inhabited villages. Bengali mosques are normally covered with several small domes and curved brick roofs decorated with [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta). [Ponds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponds) are often located beside a mosque.[^cite_note-Grabar1989-47]

[Faujdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faujdar) [Farhad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farhad_Khan) built [Sylhet Shahi Eidgah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Shahi_Eidgah) in the 1660s under the reign of [Mughal Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Emperor) [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb). It stands as the largest [eidgah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidgah) of the region.[^cite_note-48]

In 1872, Nawab Moulvi Ali Ahmed Khan of [Prithimpassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithimpassa) constructed [Ali Amjad's Clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Amjad's_Clock), named after his son, in [Sylhet City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_City).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] In 1936, a bridge was constructed across the [Surma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surma_River) known as the [Keane Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keane_Bridge). These two historic landmarks are known as the *gateway to Sylhet city*.[^cite_note-52]

## Demography



[Bengali Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslim) primary school students in [Srimangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srimangal).



A young [Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_people) boy in a remote village.

The division's population is over 12 million and [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis) make up a large majority of the region's population. The tribal and [Adivasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi) population tend to live in secluded rural areas of the region primarily near the hills and tea gardens. They are made up of several ethnic groups such as the [Bishnupriya Manipuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupriya_Manipuri_people), [Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_people), [Lalengs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laleng?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tripuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_people), [Meiteis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people), [Garos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_people), and [Kukis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people). In the nineteenth century, the British brought over indigenous peoples from other parts of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) to work as tea garden labourers such as the [Kurmis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurmi), [Musahars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musahar), [Bauris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauris), [Beens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beens), [Bonaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaz), [Sabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabar_people) and [Bhumij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_people) amongst others.[^cite_note-bhumij-53]

### Religion

| Religion |  | Percent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Bangladesh) |  | 86.22% |
| [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Bangladesh) |  | 13.51% |
| [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Bangladesh) |  | 0.23% |
| Others |  | 0.04% |

[Islam](6037917.md) is the largest religion in the whole region practised by the [Bengali Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslims). [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) is the largest denomination with majority following the [Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi) school of law although some also follow the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) and [Hanbali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanbali) [madhhabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab).[^cite_note-ourbang-54] There are significant numbers of people who follow [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) ideals similar to the [Barelvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelvis), the most influential is the teachings of [Abdul Latif Chowdhury Fultali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_Chowdhury_Fultali) of [Zakiganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakiganj_Upazila) – a descendant of one of the disciples of [Shah Jalal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jalal).[^cite_note-BDUK-55] The revivalist [Deobandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deobandi) movement is also popular in the region with [Jamia Tawakkulia Renga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamia_Tawakkulia_Renga_Madrasah) being a notable centre and many are part of the [Tablighi Jamaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablighi_Jamaat). [Haji Shariatullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haji_Shariatullah)'s [Faraizi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraizi_movement) was very popular during the British period and [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) is adopted by some upper-class families.[^cite_note-56] The [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya) community is mostly concentrated in [Selbaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmapasha_Upazila), which was the ancestral home of Ahmad Toufiq Choudhury, the leader of Ahmadiyya Muslim Jama'at Bangladesh.[^cite_note-zikr-57][^cite_note-58]

There is a very small minority of [Shia Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Muslims) who gather every year during [Ashura](488563.md) for the [Mourning of Muharram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourning_of_Muharram) processions. Places of procession include the [Prithimpasha Nawab Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithimpassa_Family) in [Kulaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulaura), home to a royal Shia family, as well as [Rajtila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamalganj_Upazila).

[Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) is the second largest religion practised by the [Bengali Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindus) as well as majority of the [Bishnupriya Manipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupriya_Manipuri_people), [Beens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beens), [Bhumij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_people), [Bonaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaz), [Sabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabar_people), [Lalengs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laleng?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Tripuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_people) population. Sylhet has the largest concentration of Hindus in Eastern Bengal and is a part of the [Shakta pitha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakta_pitha).

Other minority religions include Christianity (including the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Sylhet) and [Sylhet Presbyterian Synod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Presbyterian_Synod)), [Ka Niam Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_people#Khasi_mythology), [Sanamahism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamahism), [Songsarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_people#Religion) as well as animism. In the early 20th century, there were over a hundred [Marwaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwari_people) from [Rajasthan](26291.md) that were living in Sylhet, mostly as merchants and followed [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism).[^cite_note-allen-45]<sup>: 90 </sup>

There was a presence of [Sikhism](27964.md) in Sylhet after [Guru Nanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Nanak)'s visit in 1508 to spread the religion. [Kahn Singh Nabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahn_Singh_Nabha) has stated that in memory of Nanak's visit, *Gurdwara Sahib Sylhet* was established. This Gurdwara was visited twice by [Tegh Bahadur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegh_Bahadur) and many [hukamnamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukamnama) were issued to this temple by [Guru Gobind Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Gobind_Singh). In 1897, the gurdwara fell down after the [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1897_Assam_earthquake).[^cite_note-59]
