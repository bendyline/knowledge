**Balochistan** ([/bəˈlɒtʃɪstɑːn, bəˌlɒtʃɪˈstɑːn, -stæn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English))[^cite_note-8] is a [province](446017.md) of [Pakistan](23235.md). Located in the [southwestern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan) of the country, Balochistan is the largest province of Pakistan by land area but is also the [least populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Pakistan). It is bordered by the Pakistani provinces of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) to the north-east, [Punjab](24751.md) to the east, and [Sindh](28972.md) to the southeast; shares international borders with [Iran](11715031.md) to the west and [Afghanistan](203570.md) to the north; and is bound by the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the south. Balochistan is an extensive [plateau](538005.md) of rough terrain divided into basins by ranges of significant height and ruggedness. It has a large deep-sea port, [Gwadar Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_Port), lying in the Arabian Sea.

Although it constitutes approximately 44% of Pakistan's land area, only 5% of it is arable, and it is noted for an extremely dry [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate).[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-9][^cite_note-Government_of_Balochistan-10] The agriculture and livestock make up about 47% of Balochistan's economy.[^cite_note-Government_of_Balochistan-10]

## Etymology

The name "[Balochistan](8431743.md#Etymology)" means "the land of the [Baloch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people)".[^cite_note-11] The meaning of the [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) "Baloch" (also spelt Baluch) is unclear.[^cite_note-\:0-12] Until the times of [Arab conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_conquests) in the 7th century CE, Baloch people lived in [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province) in present-day [Iran](14653.md). They started migrating eastwards into [Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makran) in what would later came to be known as Balochistan by the time of [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_dynasty) in the 11th century.[^cite_note-\:0-12]

## History

Main article: [History of Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Balochistan)

### Ancient



Map showing the sites and extent of the Indus Valley civilisation in Pakistan, including [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh).

Much of present-day Balochistan province occupies the southeastern most portion of the [Iranian plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau). The [Kach Gandava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach_Gandava) Plain in the northeast was the setting for the earliest known farming settlements in the pre-[Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) era, the earliest of which was [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh), dated around 7000 BCE.[^cite_note-13]

Although an indigenous population existed during the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and under the empire of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), the Baloch people themselves did not appear in the region until the 14th century CE.[^cite_note-\:1-14] From the first millennium BCE onwards, the region was divided into several provinces of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), namely [Maka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maka_\(satrapy\)), which certainly corresponded to Makran, and [Zarang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarang).[^cite_note-\:0-12] The Greek authors during the time of Alexander the Great in the 4th century BCE described the region as the inhospitable [Gedrosian desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedrosian_desert), with scarce vegetation and water.[^cite_note-\:0-12] Alexander crossed the Gedrosian desert after returning from Indian subcontinent. According to the ancient Greek writers, the population of Gedrosia consisted of [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryans) ethnic groups like the Arbies and [Oreitae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreitae), and Iranian Myci;[^cite_note-\:0-12] as well as [Ichthyophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyophagi) ("Fish-eaters"), sometimes identified with the modern [Med people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Med_people).[^cite_note-15] [Seleucus I Nicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucus_I_Nicator) was forced to cede Gedrosia along with other northwestern regions to the Mauryan emperor [Chandragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya) in 302 BCE.[^cite_note-16]

The next significant information about Makran comes from the [Sasanian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_period).[^cite_note-\:0-12] A king of Makran paid homage to Sasanian Shah [Narseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narseh) (r. 293 – 303) upon his accession. Sasanians divided the region into several units, including Makran, [Tugran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turan_\(Sasanian_province\)) (modern [Kalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalat,_Balochistan)), [Paradan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradan) (probably modern [Kharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharan_\(princely_state\))) and [Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hind_\(Sasanian_province\)). Tugran was well-populated by people speaking a non-Iranian language, possibly the [Brahui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahui_people) of today.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The Brahuis, whose language belong to [Dravidian family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_language), are considered the remnants of the earliest people of the region.[^cite_note-Andrew_Dalby_-_Hindu_origin_of_Brahui_people2-17] During the Sasanian period Makran and Qayqan (present-day Kalat) had a substantial population of [Zuṭṭ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zu%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%AD) or [Jat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jats) [dromedary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary)-men, many of whom were resettled by Sasanians in the southern regions (present-day Iraq) in the times of [Bahram V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_V) (r. 420 – 438).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 142 </sup>

Makran was under a dynasty of Hindu Rais who had a capital at [Aror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aror) in Sindh before the Muslim conquests. It was captured by Persians during the reign of Rai Siharas II, but [Chach of Aror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chach_of_Aror) recovered Makran in 631 CE.[^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 133–134 </sup> According to the [*Chachnama*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chach_Nama), Chach went with his army and marked the border of his kingdom with Kerman by planting [date-trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) along the banks of a small stream, and branded the following words there, "This is the boundary as established in the days of Chach, son of Salaji, son of Basij, King of Sind, when it came into his possession."[^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 133–134 </sup> The exact border thus marked is uncertain, although according to [André Wink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Wink) it was probably beyond [Tiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tis,_Iran).[^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 133–134 </sup> A large population of Makran was [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) before Muslim conquests, as mentioned by the Chinese pilgrim [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang).[^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 134–135 </sup>

### Medieval

The early [Arab geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_geographers) regarded Makran a part of [al-Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_\(caliphal_province\)).[^cite_note-\:23-20]<sup>: 129 </sup> An Arab army [invaded Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rasil) in 644 during the tenure of caliph [Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar) (r. 634 – 644). It defeated the king of Sind and marched almost until the Indus River, but after hearing the reports of poor economic conditions and water scarcity in the region, the caliph ordered the troops to not cross the river.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The territory of Qayqan in Sind was first conquered by Muslims during the reign of caliph [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali) in 659, but in 667 the Muslim governor Abdullah ibn Sawar al-Abdi was killed in a rebellion and Qayqan was lost.[^cite_note-21] According to the 9th century geographical treatise *[Futuh al-Buldan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futuh_al-Buldan)*, the people of Qayqan were Zuṭṭ.[^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 158 </sup> In the following centuries the region attracted a large number of [Kharijites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharijites).[^cite_note-\:0-12] A Kharijite breakaway state was established under the Arab [Ma'danid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'danid_dynasty) in Makran in the 9th century, whose rulers adopted the Sanskrit title of [*maharaj*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaj).[^cite_note-22]

In the 11th century the [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) brought much of Makran under their control. Over the next three centuries the foreign influence over Makran weakened and the region again became relatively autonomous. The Italian traveller [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo) mentioned the existence of an independent Muslim state in [Kij-Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kech_District) in the 13th century, describing it as the last kingdom in India.[^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 133 </sup> According to Marco Polo, the ruler of Makran was known as [*malek*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik). Food was abundantly produced and the people were involved in trade as well as agriculture.[^cite_note-\:0-12]

#### Baloch migrations

Baloch people are first mentioned in the historical sources at [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province), in the 10th century CE.[^cite_note-\:22-19]<sup>: 142–143 </sup> Although Baloch presence was already noted in Makran by 11th century, large-scale Baloch migrations occurred only by the turn of 14th century.[^cite_note-\:0-12] Evidence of migrations comes from Baloch epic poetry as well as [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) sources. According to Baloch folklore, [Mir Jalal Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Jalal_Khan) led forty-four Baloch tribes from Persia during the migration. The descent of five major Baloch tribal divisions, namely [Rind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rind_\(tribe\)), [Lashar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashari), [Jatoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatoi_\(clan\)), Hoth and Korai, is traditionally traced from him.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The folklore does not mention several prominent tribes of today,[^cite_note-23] which according to the anthropologist [Brian Spooner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Spooner_\(anthropologist\)) suggests that either they are branches of the aforementioned tribes, or were not assimilated into the Baloch yet.[^cite_note-\:0-12]

### Pre-modern

During the era of Baloch chief and folk hero [Mir Chakar Rind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Chakar_Rind), another large wave of Baloch people moved to the [Punjab Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Plain) in the 15th century.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The epic poetry of the period gives much details regarding the intertribal [Rind-Lashari War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rind-Lashari_War). In 1620, Kij-Makran was taken over by the [Buledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buledi) tribe, which later came to rule all of Makran. Buledis were followers of [Zikrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zikrism), a [Mahdist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi) sect, as were the [Gichkis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gichki), who ousted them in 1740.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The Brahui Qambrani dynasty established the [Khanate of Kalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kalat) towards the end of 17th-century in the Kalat highlands. The Khan of Kalat [Abdullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Khan_Ahmadzai) died fighting against the [Kalhora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalhora_dynasty) Amirs of Sindh in 1731. His successor, Muhabat Khan was awarded the Kach Gandava Plains, then ruled by the Sindhi Kalhora dynasty, by [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) during the later's invasion of [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in 1739 as blood compensation for the death of Abdullah Khan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpooner1988Nāder_also_gave_them_the_lowland_plains_of_Kacchi_(then_ruled_by_the_Kalhoras_of_Sind)_as_blood_compensation_for_the_death_of_Mīr_ʿAbd-Allāh_Khan.-24] [Mir Nasir Khan I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Nasir_Khan_I), the greatest Khan of Kalat, expanded his rule over all of Balochistan. He established a standing army recruited from the tribes of [Sarawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawan) and [Jhalawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhalawan), and an administrative machinery. He pledged allegiance to [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani), who granted him Quetta in 1751. Nasir Khan undertook nine campaigns against Gichkis of Makran, forcing them to recognise his suzerainty, as well as against the rulers of [Kharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharan_\(princely_state\)) and [Lasbela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Bela_\(princely_state\)).[^cite_note-\:0-12] The Khanate declined after his death in 1795.[^cite_note-25]

### Colonial



A historical sketch of [Bolan Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolan_Pass), Balochistan, Pakistan

In 1876, [Baluchistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_\(Chief_Commissioner's_Province\)) became one of the [presidencies and provinces of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India),[^cite_note-Henige19702-26] when [Robert Sandeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Groves_Sandeman) negotiated the [Treaty of Kalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kalat), which brought the Khan of Kalat's territories, as well as the [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) of [Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makran_\(princely_state\)), Kharan, and Las Bela, under British protection, even though they retained internal independence.[^cite_note-27] After facing defeat in the [Second Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Afghan_War), the [Afghan Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Yaqub_Khan) ceded the districts of Quetta, [Pishin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pishin_District), [Harnai](14609670.md), and Sibi to the British under the terms of [Treaty of Gandamak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gandamak) in May 1879. On 1 April 1883, the British took control of the [Bolan Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolan_Pass), south-east of Quetta, from the Khan of Kalat. In 1887, small additional areas of Balochistan were declared British territory.[^cite_note-28] Two devastating earthquakes occurred in Balochistan during British colonial rule: the [1935 Quetta earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Quetta_earthquake), which devastated Quetta, and the [1945 Balochistan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_Balochistan_earthquake) with its epicentre in the Makran region.[^cite_note-29]



[Quetta Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetta_Railway_Station)

In British India, Baluchistan consisted of a Chief Commissioner's province and princely states (including Kalat, Makran, Las Bela, and Kharan) that became a part of Pakistan.[^cite_note-30] During the time of the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), three pro-Congress parties were still active in Balochistan's politics apart from Balochistan's [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_League_\(Opposition\)), including the [Anjuman-i-Watan Baluchistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjuman-i-Watan_Baluchistan), which [opposed partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_the_partition_of_India).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The province's [Shahi Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga) (the grand council of tribal elders[^cite_note-33]) and the non-official members of the [Quetta Municipality](93764.md),[^cite_note-CheemaRiemer19902-34] according to the Pakistani version,[^cite_note-rafisheikh2-35]<sup>: 80 </sup> agreed to join Pakistan unanimously on 29 June 1947;[^cite_note-CheemaRiemer19902-34] however, the political scientist Salman Rafi Sheikh states that the Shahi Jirga was stripped of its members from the Kalat State before the vote.[^cite_note-rafisheikh2-35]<sup>: 81 </sup> The then-president of the Balochistan Muslim League, [Qazi Muhammad Isa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazi_Mohammad_Isa), informed [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah) that "Shahi Jirga in no way represents the popular wishes of the masses" and that members of the Kalat State were "excluded from voting; only representatives from the British part of the province voted including the leased areas of Quetta, Nasirabad Tehsil, Nushki and Bolan Agency."[^cite_note-rafisheikh2-35]<sup>: 81 </sup> Following the referendum, on 22 June 1947 the Khan of Kalat [Ahmad Yar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Yar_Khan) received a letter from members of the Shahi Jirga, as well as [sardars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar) from the leased areas of Baluchistan, stating that they, "as a part of the Baloch nation, were a part of the Kalat state too" and that if the question of Baluchistan's accession to Pakistan arise, "they should be deemed part of the Kalat state rather than (British) Balochistan".[^cite_note-rafisheikh2-35]<sup>: 82 </sup> This has brought into question whether an actual vote took place, says Sheikh.[^cite_note-rafisheikh2-35]<sup>: 82 </sup>

### After independence

Lasbela, whose ruler belonged to the [Jamote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamote_people) family,[^cite_note-36] was first of the four princely states to accede to Pakistan on 7 March 1948. It was followed by accession of Makran and Kharan on 17 March; the accession of all three was accepted by the Pakistani government on the same day.[^cite_note-37] According to Dushka H. Saiyid, Ahmad Yar Khan lost all of his bargaining chips with the accession of Kharan, Las Bela, and Makran, leaving Kalat as a land-locked island.[^cite_note-\:03-38] Salman Rafi Sheikh largely concurs with Saiyid's assessment: multiple other Kalat sardars were preparing to accede to Pakistan and Ahmad Yar Khan would have hardly any territory left, if he did not accede.[^cite_note-rafisheikh2-35]<sup>: 80 </sup> Initially aspiring for independence,[^cite_note-CheemaRiemer19902-34] the Khan of Kalat finally acceded to Pakistan on 27 March 1948 following a period of negotiations with Pakistan.[^cite_note-39] The signing of the Instrument of Accession by Ahmad Yar Khan led his brother, [Prince Abdul Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Abdul_Karim), to revolt against his brother's decision due to their family rift[^cite_note-Qaiser_Butt-20132-40] in July 1948.[^cite_note-41] The Prince indulged in terrorist activities without any assistance from others.[^cite_note-Siddiqi20122-42] Princes Agha Abdul Karim Baloch and Muhammad Rahim refused to lay down arms, leading the Dosht-e Jhalawan in unconventional attacks on the army until 1950.[^cite_note-Qaiser_Butt-20132-40] [Baluchistan States Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_States_Union) was established in 1952 with Khan of Kalat as its head. Jinnah and his successors allowed Yar Khan to retain his title until the province's dissolution in 1955 into [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan).

[Gwadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar), a region of Balochistan, was a colony of [Oman](22316.md) for more than a century, from 1783 until 1958, when it was [purchased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_Purchase) by Pakistan.[^cite_note-43] Many people in this region are therefore Omani.[^cite_note-44] In 1970, the prior four provinces were restored, resulting in the establishment of Balochistan province.

[Insurgencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_conflict) by Baloch separatists took place in [1948–1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Balochistan_conflict), [1958–1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Balochistan_conflict), [1962–1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Balochistan_conflict), and [1973–1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Balochistan_conflict), with an ongoing insurgency by autonomy-seeking Baloch groups since 2003.[^cite_note-Hussain-4-25-132-45][^cite_note-bbc-22-2-142-46] While many Baloch support the demand for autonomy, the majority is reportedly not interested in seceding from Pakistan.[^cite_note-47]

At a press conference on 8 June 2015 in Quetta, the provincial Home Minister [Sarfraz Bugti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarfraz_Bugti) accused India's Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) of openly supporting terrorism. Bugti implicated India's [Research and Analysis Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_Analysis_Wing) (RAW) of being responsible for recent attacks at military bases in Smangli and Khalid, and for subverting the [China–Pakistan Economic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Pakistan_Economic_Corridor) (CPEC) agreement.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

## Geography



[Astola Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astola_Island)

Balochistan is situated in the southwest of Pakistan and covers an area of 347,190 square kilometres (134,050 mi<sup>2</sup>). It is Pakistan's largest province by area, constituting 44% of Pakistan's total landmass. The province is bordered by [Afghanistan](737.md) to the north and northwest, Iran to the south-west, [Punjab](24751.md) and [Sindh](28972.md), and [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) and the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas) to the northeast. To the south lies the [Arabian Sea](2747.md). Balochistan is located in the southeastern part of the Iranian plateau. It borders the geopolitical regions of the [Middle East](19323.md) and Southwest Asia, Central Asia, and [South Asia](21566765.md). Balochistan lies at the mouth of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) and provides the shortest route from seaports to Central Asia. Its geographical location has placed the otherwise desolate region in the scope of competing for global interests for all of recorded history.

The capital city, [Quetta](93764.md), is located in a densely populated portion of the [Sulaiman Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaiman_Mountains) in the northeast of the province. It is situated in a river valley near the Bolan Pass, which has been used as the route of choice from the coast to Central Asia, entering through Afghanistan's [Kandahar](17260.md) region. The British and other historic empires have crossed the region to invade Afghanistan by this route.[^cite_note-51]

Balochistan is rich in non-renewable and renewable resources; it is the second-largest supplier of natural gas in Pakistan. The province's renewable and human resource potential has not been systematically measured or exploited. Local inhabitants have chosen to live in towns and have relied on sustainable water sources for thousands of years.

### Climate

The climate of the upper highlands is characterised by very cold winters and hot summers. In the lower highlands, winters vary from extremely cold in northern districts of [Ziarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziarat), [Quetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetta_District), [Kalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalat,_Balochistan), [Muslim Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Bagh), and [Khanozai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanozai), where temperatures can drop to −20 °C (−4 °F), to milder conditions closer to the Makran coast. Winters are mild on the plains, with temperatures never falling below freezing point. Summers are hot and dry, especially in the arid zones of Chagai and Kharan districts. The plains are also very hot in summer, with temperatures reaching 50 °C (122 °F). The record high temperature, 53 °C (127 °F), was recorded in Sibi on 26 May 2010,[^cite_note-52] exceeding the previous record, 52 °C (126 °F). Other hot areas include [Turbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbat) and [Dalbandin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalbandin). The desert climate is characterised by hot and very arid conditions. Occasionally, strong windstorms make these areas very inhospitable.

## Government and politics

Main article: [Government of Balochistan, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Balochistan,_Pakistan)

In common with the other provinces of Pakistan, Balochistan has a parliamentary form of government. The ceremonial head of the province is the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Balochistan_\(Pakistan\)), who is appointed on the advice of the [Chief Minister of Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Balochistan) by the [President of Pakistan](419460.md). The Chief Minister, the province's chief executive, is normally the leader of the largest political party or alliance of parties in the provincial assembly.



Balochistan Governor's House, Quetta

The [unicameral](204304.md) [Provincial Assembly of Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Balochistan) comprises 65 seats, of which 11 are reserved for women, and 3 are reserved for non-Muslims. The judicial branch of government is carried out by the [Balochistan High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_High_Court), which is based in Quetta and headed by a Chief Justice.

Besides the dominant Pakistan-wide political parties (such as the [Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf](1651925.md), [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)), and the [Pakistan Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Peoples_Party)), Balochistan nationalist parties (such as the [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(Pakistan\)) and the [Balochistan National Party (Mengal)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_National_Party_\(Mengal\))) have been prominent in the province.[^cite_note-Hussain-4-25-13-53]

Balochistan's population density is low due to the mountainous terrain and scarcity of water. In March 2012, preliminary census figures showed that the population of Balochistan, not including the districts of Khuzdar, Kech and Panjgur, had reached 13,162,222, an increase of 139.3% from 5,501,164 in 1998. The population constituted 6.85% of Pakistan's total population. This was the largest increase in population in any province of Pakistan during that time period, almost thrice the national increase of 46.9%.[^cite_note-POP-60][^cite_note-census1998-61][^cite_note-2012Census1-62] Official estimates of Balochistan's population grew from approximately 7.45 million in 2003 to 7.8 million in 2005.[^cite_note-worldbankpop-63] The [2023 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census) enumerated a population of 14,894,402.

As per a 2025 *[Dawn News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_News)* report, based on data compiled by research organisation [Population Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Council), [UK Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_International_Development) and the [United Nations Population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Population_Division), Balochistan had an annual growth rate of 3.20%. According to demographic projections, if the province's current [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) of 4 children per woman remains unchanged, Balochistan's population could more than double to approximately 35 million by 2050.[^cite_note-DawnPop-64]

According to the preliminary results of the [2023 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), the languages with the most native speakers in the province are [Balochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_language), spoken by 39.91% of the population (an increase of 4% compared to the 2017 census), and [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) whose share is at 34.34%.[^cite_note-2023census-65]

The [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) mainly inhabit the north of Balochistan and form the majority in [Quetta](93764.md). Baloch on the other hand are found throughout Balochistan, but most highly concentrated in the west and south of the province.

[Brahui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahui_language) is spoken by 17.22% mainly in the central part of Balochistan. Other languages include [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language) (3.81%), [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language) (2.19%), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (0.59%), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) (0.53%) and others at (1.5%).

Balochi forms the majority in 21 districts and Pashto forms majority in 9 districts of Balochistan.[^cite_note-66] Brahui has majority in 4 districts. In the [Lasbela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasbela_District), [Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hub_District) districts and in [Kachhi plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kacchi_Plain) region a large minority of the population speaks [Lasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasi_dialect) and [Siraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siraiki_dialect),[^cite_note-brit-67] which are dialects of [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language).[^cite_note-68]

According to the Ethnologue, households speaking [Balochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_language), whose primary dialect is Makrani constitutes 13%, Rukhshani 10%, Sulemani 7%, and Khetrani 3% of the population. Other languages spoken are Lasi, Urdu, Punjabi, Hazargi, Sindhi, Saraiki, Dehvari, Dari, Tajik, Hindko, Uzbek, and Hindki.[^cite_note-yb2008-69]

The 2005 census concerning [Afghans in Pakistan](22663356.md) showed that a total of 769,268[^cite_note-70] Afghan refugees were temporarily staying in Balochistan. However, there are probably fewer Afghans living in Balochistan today as many refugees repatriated in 2013. As of 2015, there are only 327,778 registered Afghan refugees according to the UNHCR.[^cite_note-71]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Balochistan, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Balochistan,_Pakistan)

The economy of Balochistan is largely based upon agriculture, livestock, fisheries, production of natural gas, coal, and other minerals.[^cite_note-Ethnic_Subnationalist_Insurgencies_in_South_Asia:_Identities,_Interests_and_Challenges_to_State_Authority-93]

Though agriculture and livestock play a dominant role in the provincial economy by contributing 47% of its GDP. It faced extensive damage due to the [2022 Pakistan floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistan_floods). The floods killed around 500,000 of Balochistan's livestock and damaged cultivation and agricultural output in 32 out of 35 districts of the province. The [Lasbela district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasbela_District) was the worst hit as the floods washed away four-fifths of the homes, crops, and livestock.[^cite_note-94] Due to the floods and severe drought conditions, the province faces food insecurity and is 85% dependent on the [Sindh](28972.md) and [Punjab](24751.md) provinces for the supply of wheat.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

Furthermore, with the exception of [Quetta](93764.md), Balochistan has been called a "neglected province where a majority of the population lacks amenities."[^cite_note-dawn-22-3-12-97][^cite_note-kupecz-98] Although the province is rich in natural resources capable of uplifting its economy, most of them have not been fully utilised for the welfare of the population and are yet to be explored or developed.[^cite_note-99]

Since the mid-1970s, the province's contribution to Pakistan's GDP has dropped from 4.9 to 3.7%,[^cite_note-100] and as of 2007, it had the highest poverty rate and infant and maternal mortality rate, and the lowest literacy rate in comparison to other provinces,[^cite_note-101] factors some allege have contributed to the insurgency.[^cite_note-kupecz-98] However, in the seventh NFC awards, Punjab province and the Federal contributed to increase Baluchistan share more than its entitled population-based share.[^cite_note-102] In Balochistan, poverty is increasing. In 2001–2002, poverty incidences were at 48% and by 2005–2006, these were at 50.9%.[^cite_note-The_Political_Economy_of_Conflict_in_South_Asia-103] According to a report on Dawn, the rate of multidimensional poverty in Balochistan had risen to 71% by 2016.[^cite_note-104]

Several major development projects, including the construction of a new deep-sea port at the strategically important town of [Gwadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar),[^cite_note-pakboi-105] are in progress in Balochistan. The port is projected to be the hub of an energy and trade corridor to and from China, the Middle East, and the Central Asian republics. The [Mirani Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirani_Dam) on the [Dasht River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasht_River), 50 kilometres (31 mi) west of Turbat in the [Makran Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makuran_Division), is being built to provide water to expand agricultural land use by 35,000 km<sup>2</sup> (14,000 sq mi) where it would otherwise be unsustainable.[^cite_note-mirani-106] In the district of Lasbela, there is an oil refinery owned by Byco International Incorporated (BII), which is capable of processing 120,000 barrels of oil per day. A power station is located adjacent to the refinery.[^cite_note-107] Several cement plants and a marble factory are also located there.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] One of the world's largest [ship-breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_breaking) yards is located on the coast.[^cite_note-111]

### Natural resource extraction

Balochistan's share of Pakistan's national income has historically ranged between 3.7% to 4.9%.[^cite_note-112] Since 1972, Balochistan's gross income has grown in size by 2.7 times.[^cite_note-113] Outside Quetta, the resource extraction infrastructure of the province is gradually developing but still lags far behind other parts of Pakistan.

The agreements for royalty rights and ownership of mineral rights were reached during a period of unprecedented natural disasters, economic, social, political, and cultural unrest in Pakistan. The negotiations were widely considered to be insufficiently transparent.[^cite_note-114]
