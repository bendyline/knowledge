**Beit Lid** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بيت ليد) is a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) town in the [Tulkarm Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulkarm_Governorate) in the northeastern [West Bank](33209.md), located a 10 kilometers southeast of [Tulkarm](851834.md) and west of [Nablus](233369.md). According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics), the town had a population of 5,606 inhabitants in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] In 1922, it had 653 inhabitants,[^cite_note-census1922-3] which rose to 1,807 in 1961.[^cite_note-Census1961-4]

## History

[Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) ceramics have been found here.[^cite_note-5]

The village mosque has a large gate with a triple [arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch). An inscription over the [lintel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lintel) is a construction text, commemorating the building of the mosque. It dates from the late [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)), or early Ottoman period.[^cite_note-6]

### Ottoman era

Beit Lid, like all of [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) was incorporated into the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1517. In the 1596 [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter), it was part of the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* ("subdistrict") of Jabal Sami, part of the larger [Sanjak of Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Nablus). It had a population of 64 households, all [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). The inhabitants paid a fixed tax rate of 33,3% on agricultural products, including wheat, barley, summer crops, olive trees, goats and beehives, in addition to occasional revenues and a press for olive oil or grape syrup; a total of 17,310 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e). Half of the revenues went to a [Waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf).[^cite_note-7]

In 1838, [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) passed it in the distance,[^cite_note-8] and placed it in the *Wady esh-Sha'ir* administrative region, west of [Nablus](233369.md).[^cite_note-Robinson129-9] In 1863 [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) noted it as a considerable village which occupied the top of a high hill; containing a thousand inhabitants. The houses were crudely built.[^cite_note-10]

In the 1860s, the Ottoman authorities granted the village an agricultural plot of land called Ghabat Beit Lid in the former confines of the Forest of Arsur (Ar. Al-Ghaba) in the coastal plain, west of the village. This formed the foundation for the village of [Khirbat Bayt Lid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbat_Bayt_Lid), founded by some Beit Lid's residents.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

In 1870/1871 (1288 [AH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Hegirae)), an Ottoman census listed the village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiyah)* (sub-district) of Wadi al-Sha'ir.[^cite_note-13]

In 1882, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* (SWP) described Beit Lid as “A village of small size, built on a hill rising 600 feet above the valley south of it. The houses are of stone, and supplied by a [well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_well) on the south-east, lower down. A few olives grow round the village.”[^cite_note-14]

### British Mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine), Beit Lid's population was 653, all Muslims,[^cite_note-census1922-3] increasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 738 residents, still all Muslim, in a total of 171 houses.[^cite_note-census1931-15]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) the population of Beit Lid was 960 Muslims,[^cite_note-DoS1945-16] and the land area was 16,752 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam), according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-17] 1,044 dunams were for citrus and bananas, 3,264 dunams were plantations and irrigable land, 4,473 used for cereals,[^cite_note-18] while 22 dunams were built-up (urban) land.[^cite_note-19]

### 1948-1967

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Beit Lid came under [Jordanian](7515964.md) rule.

In 1961, the population of Beit Lid was 1,807.[^cite_note-Census1961-4]

### Post-1967

After the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Beit Lid has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md).

[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) is a major economic sector in Beit Lid, with olives, almonds, figs, grapes and grains being the primary crops grown on 16,753 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam). Until recently, agriculture employed most of the working males of the town, however, many residents are employed in small business professions, such as construction, blacksmithing and electricity. Many in Beit Lid that worked inside [Israel](9282173.md), have been unemployed since the [Second Intifada](71717.md) and the difficult conditions surrounding it.[^cite_note-PR-20]

There are four schools (two primary and two secondary), four kindergartens, a clinic operated by the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) and founded in 1964. Beit Lid contains four [mosques](19894.md), including Masjid al-Qadim, Masjid [Fatima Zahra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_Zahra), Masjid as-Salaam and Masjid Salah ad-Din.[^cite_note-PR-20]
