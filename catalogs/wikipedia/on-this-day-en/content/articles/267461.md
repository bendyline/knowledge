**Surigao del Norte** ([Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language): *Probinsya nan Surigao del Norte*; [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Amihanang Surigao*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Hilagang Surigao*), officially the **Province of Surigao del Norte**, is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga) [region](216786.md) of [Mindanao](20546.md). The province was formerly under the jurisdiction of Region 10 ([Northern Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mindanao)) until 1995. Its capital is [Surigao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City), the most populous in the province.[^cite_note-4] The province comprises two major islands—[Siargao](2855427.md) and [Bucas Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucas_Grande)—in the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea), plus a small area at the northeastern tip of mainland Mindanao and other surrounding minor islands and islets. This mainland portion borders [Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Norte) – between the Municipality of Alegria in Surigao del Norte and the Municipality of Kitcharao in Agusan del Norte; and the province of [Surigao del Sur](267469.md), to the south.

Surigao del Norte is the second northernmost of the Mindanao provinces and is an important commerce and transportation hub between [Visayas](72418.md) and Mindanao. Numerous ferries cross the [Surigao Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_Strait) between Surigao and the island of [Leyte](194750.md) transporting vehicles, cargoes, and passengers between [Liloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liloan,_Southern_Leyte) in [Southern Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Leyte) and Surigao City.

## Etymology

See also: [List of Philippine provincial name etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_provincial_name_etymologies)

There are two presumptions on the original meaning of "Surigao" among linguists, depending on the original root word. If the root word was taken to be *sulig* ("sprout" or "spring up"), then Surigao may have derived from *suligao* ("spring water"), likely referring to the Surigao River (known as "Suligaw" in [Mandaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaya_language)) that empties at the northern tip of the island of [Mindanao](20546.md).[^cite_note-sur3-5][^cite_note-sur1-6] Early historical accounts record the name of the river as Suligao, Surigao, or Zurigan.[^cite_note-taw2-7]

Another possibility is that it is derived from Visayan "*surogao"* or "*suyogao*," meaning "water current". From *suyog* (also *sulog* or *surog*), "current". Other Visayan words derived from the same root include [Sinulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinulog), [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu), and [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taus%C5%ABg_people) (Suluk).[^cite_note-8]

### Spanish colonial era

Rajah Siawi and Rajah Kulambo, members of the [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maginoo) of the [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_people) and [Butuanon people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuanon_people), respectively, were encountered by the [Magellan expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition) in 1521 on the island of [Limasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limasawa) (which was a hunting ground for the rulers). [Antonio Pigafetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Pigafetta) describes them as being tattooed and covered in gold ornaments. Pigafetta also records the name of the Surigao region as "Calagan".[^cite_note-Nowell-9]<sup>: 141–142 </sup>

The explorer [Ruy López de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_L%C3%B3pez_de_Villalobos) headed an expedition to Surigao in 1543, an attempt at subjugation which failed because of the resistance of the natives. At that time the island of Mindanao was given the name Caesarea Caroli, in honor of [Carlos V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of Spain. [Jesuit missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) visited Butuan in 1597 but did not make much progress in Christianizing the people. It was not until 1609, when a full-dress expedition was launched, that Spanish authority was imposed to Tandag. In 1622, the arrival of the Recollect missionaries in Tandag commenced the establishment of a permanent mission in Surigao.

The area of Surigao del Norte was once part of an ancient district referred to as "Caraga" during the [Spanish colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521%E2%80%931898\)) named after its chief inhabitants, the [Caraga tribe or Caragans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_language) who were largely concentrated in a settlement called [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga,_Davao_Oriental). The ancient district of Caraga, which was established in 1609 comprised all of the present day provinces of Surigao del Norte, [Dinagat Islands](3185248.md), [Surigao del Sur](267469.md), the northern part of [Davao Oriental](296986.md) and eastern [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental). The seat of government was at [Tandag](877402.md) until it was transferred to the [town of Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City) in 1848. Six Spanish military districts were established in Mindanao in 1860 and the areas of present-day Surigao del Norte, Surigao del Sur, Dinagat Islands, [Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Norte) and [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur) (collectively called Agusan back then), including the territory lying between present day [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan_Bay) and Caraga bays, formed the third district called the "East District" which was changed in 1870 to "Distrito de Surigao". By the end of the Spanish rule in 1897, Agusan had been organized as a single politico-military comandancia named "Butuan", within the administrative jurisdiction of Distrito de Surigao.

#### Surigao during the Philippine Revolution

On December 23, 1898, the last Spanish Governor of Surigao turned his authority over to a provisional Filipino "Junta Provincial" Peacefully. The Spanish Government along with the Spanish community soon left Surigao by the next day on December 24 leaving behind Spanish missionaries. They were sent off with the sound of music and goodwill by the locals.[^cite_note-\:1-10] The Junta Provincial elected Jantoy Gonzalez to serve as the Provisional head while waiting for the arrival of the new authority from Luzon that was sent by Aguinaldo.

> **First and Official Flag Raising in Mindanao**. Father Alberto Masoliver, S.J., during his stint as the parish priest of Surigao in 1898, kept a compilation of his diaries, entitled *Diario de la Casa de Surigao*. His diaries are currently stored in the Jesuit archives at Centro Borja, [Sant Cugat del Valles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Cugat_del_Valles), Spain. He thus wrote on December 26, 1898, *"Before 10\:00 AM, the tri-color was hoisted from Casa Real seat of provincial government) and the Tribunal … without any formalities … without anybody's attention being drawn…..these people have no idea of what it is all about, and if they have… how peaceful and without malice they are."*. Surigao then was the *cabecera* and the seat of government of the province of Caraga which had jurisdiction over Surigao, Butuan and Cagayan de Misamis ([Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro)). Alejandro (Jantoy) Gonzalez was the president of the Junta Provincial of Surigao during the time of the first flag hoisting.

The New Authority under Aguinaldo's Supreme Revolutionary Government arrived in Surigao by January 12, 1899, they were the brothers Simon and Wenceslao Gonzales, children of Jantoy Gonzales.

#### Confiscation of Church Estate

Immediately after the arrival of the Gonzalez Brothers, they carried the orders of the Malolos Leadership which included the imprisonment of Spanish Missionaries, Confiscation of Church funds and other church properties.

### American Occupation

#### Garcia Coup

&#x20;By March 24, 1899, a new military and political figure appeared in Surigao. Don Prudencio Garcia staged a coup which resulted in the imprisonment of the Gonzalezes (Jantoy, Simon, and Wencaslao), and were killed shortly after in Cortez (now [Surigao del Sur](267469.md)).[^cite_note-\:1-10]

> Finally, on April 2, 1899, at 12 midnight they were put in a boat, well handcuffed, and shipped out to the Pacific, and upon arriving at Cortes they shot them down. They had been guarded by soldiers and officials who were all personal enemies of the Gonzalezes. In Cortes they were put under the custody of a mestizo, Hilarion Marinez, whom the Gonzalezes had maltreated by words and deeds. He avenged himself by having them fusilated. That was the Tragic end of our enemies ("Apuntes..." p. 116)

The news of what led to the deaths of the Gonzalezes did not reach Aguinaldo as they were given false information to their deaths. Aguinaldo was given a message from an "unknown correspondent" which stated

> "General Simon Gonzales and his brother Wencaslao, the Governor of Surigao has been assassinated for collecting excessive contribution in money for the war, and their successors have also been assassinated by the Moros who form a greater part of the population and who have revolted against Filipino rule" (Philippine Insurgent Record 1039.5 in Taylor)

the letter stated that the Gonzales were assassinated, and that the Moros were blamed for the deaths of the predecessors despite having barely any Moros in Surigao by that time.

#### Narciso v. Garcia

After the execution of the Gonzalezes, Hermenegildo Narciso (a former ally of Garcia) claimed the position of governor. By around July 1899, an estimated 400 men armed against Garcia took position outside the Provincial Capital ([Surigao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City)) with 80 men in the trenches near the exit of the town. On July 8, 1899, Garcia returned to Surigao from Banganga with all the honors befitting a general. Governor Narciso (who was still in Manila) was expected to return to Surigao to take full control of the Provincial Junta.

July 6, 1899, Soldiers (Garcia) began defending the Government house and Military Quarters in Provincial Capital, and at 6 o'clock Garcia's Soldiers and Narciso's soldiers had a shooting exchange. By 7 o'clock Garcia prepared to leave Surigao via sea. The soldiers of Narciso began their offensive when the boat began embarking equipment. They took position behind Acacia trees, and once they were discovered both sides exchanged fire. Garcia's soldiers retreated to the port and those who were not able to embark the boat with General Garcia were forced into hiding or surrender. The area of the conflict is located at the present day Surigao Luneta Park and alongside the street of Borromeo. The Province of Surigao would then be engulfed in a military rivalry between Governor Narciso and General Garcia, with Surigao and Butuan held by Narciso while Placer, Cantilan, and Tandag being held by Garcia. This military rivalry would be put to a close by the American occupation of the province.[^cite_note-\:1-10]

#### Division of Surigao

Distrito de Surigao [became a chartered province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\)) on May 15, 1901 (*[Philippine Commission Act 127](165100.md#History)*). The territorial expanse of the Surigao province was further reduced in 1907 when the politico-military commandancia of Butuan, then a sub-province of Surigao, was created into a separate province and officially named it [Agusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_\(province\)) with present-day [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan) as its capital.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-SurigaodelNorte-11]

### Philippine independence

On September 18, 1960, through *[Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) 2786* dated June 19, 1960, the province of [Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\)) was divided into the present-day provinces of *Surigao del Norte* and *[Surigao del Sur](267469.md)*. Agusan province was later dissolved and divided into the present-day provinces of [Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Norte) (retains present day Butuan as capital) and [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur) in 1967 by *[Republic Act No. 4979](165100.md#History)*.[^cite_note-Chan_Robles-RA2786-12] The [town of Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City) became a city on August 30, 1970, pursuant to *RA 6134*.[^cite_note-ChanRobles-RA6134-13]

### Contemporary

[Dinagat Islands](3185248.md) had been a part of the [First District of Surigao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Surigao_del_Norte) until becoming a province on its own on December 2, 2006, with the approval of *[Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) No. 9355*,[^cite_note-SenateGovPH-RA9355-14] the Charter of the Province of Dinagat Islands, in a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Dinagat_Islands_creation_plebiscite). Between February 2010 and March 2011, the islands were part again of Surigao del Norte after declaring *RA 9355* unconstitutional by the Supreme Court, then were reinstated as a separate province.

## Geography



Mainland Surigao del Norte agricultural landscape

Surigao del Norte is an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) province situated at the rim of the Asian continental shelf. It is located at the northeastern tip of [Mindanao](20546.md) and faces the [Philippine Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Trench). The province is bounded by the [Dinagat Islands](3185248.md) on the north, east by the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea), south by the provinces of Agusan del Norte and Surigao del Sur, and on the west by the [Surigao Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_Strait).

The province, with an area of 1,972.93 square kilometres (761.75 sq mi),[^cite_note-nscb-1] comprises the mainland situated at the northeastern tip of Mindanao and several offshore islands, the largest of which include [Siargao](2855427.md) and [Bucas Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucas_Grande). The mainland is generally mountainous.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-SurigaodelNorte-11]

The province has many caves and tunnels in its islands. Some are half-submerged in water most of the time and could be accessed only during low tides like the Sohoton Cove at Bucas Grande Island.

Its biggest islands are usually mountainous and rich in minerals. Nonoc Island has one of the world's largest deposits of [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel). The smaller ones either rest on sand and gravel or have a limestone base bonded by boulders, reefs and sandbars. Some islets which include those in [Del Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Carmen,_Surigao_del_Norte) in Siargao Island are a cluster of rock formations jutting out from the sea covered with shrubs and coconut trees.

### Climate

Surigao del Norte has no pronounced dry season, receiving maximum rainfall from November to January.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-SurigaodelNorte-11] It is among the top 20 most vulnerable provinces to [climate change in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-15]

### Administrative divisions



Political divisions of Surigao del Norte

Surigao del Norte comprises 20 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_municipality) and the [component city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines) of Surigao City, organized into [two legislative districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Surigao_del_Norte) and further subdivided into 335 [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay). The 1st legislative district comprises nine municipalities on the islands of [Siargao](2855427.md) and [Bucas Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucas_Grande) (plus smaller islands), with 136,092 inhabitants in 2020; the 2nd legislative district comprises Surigao City and eleven other municipalities on the mainland of Mindanao, with 398,544 inhabitants in 2020. Surigao City has the most number of Barangays with 54, while the Municipalities of Burgos and San Benito has the least number of Barangays each with six.

The population of Surigao del Norte in the 2024 census was 565,644 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–Caraga-17] with a density of 290 inhabitants per square kilometre or 750 inhabitants per square mile.

The inhabitants of the province is mostly of pure/mixed [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people) stock, with some people of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) and [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_of_Arab_descent) heritage. [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino) and [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_of_American_descent) ancestries are also evident, albeit a minor percentage.

Some 95.80% of the people speak [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language) as a [parent language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language). [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) and its dialect [Boholano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boholano_dialect) are also present. [Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_language) is spoken by a small percentage. A majority are able to speak [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) as second languages. A small minority of the population are also speakers of [Kamayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamayo_language) and [Manobo languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo_languages), as well as [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language), [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language) and [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language) languages.

## Flora and fauna



Coconut trees in the hills west of [Tubod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubod,_Surigao_del_Norte)

Mangroves are the major vegetation in the coastal areas of the province, which form interminable marine forests covering 175 square kilometres (68 sq mi) along the coasts of the mainland and the islands of Siargao and Bucas Grande. The province is host to a variety of marine ecosystems like the lush seagrass beds and coral reefs which are relatively healthy and intact, supporting a rich diversity of marine flora and fauna.

Over 23 different species are caught the whole year round. Marlin, tuna, lapu-lapu, mollusks, crabs, even squids, stingrays and octopuses can be bought from fish vendors and fishermen.

The "magcono" (ironwood) forests of Surigao del Norte are one of the watershed's resources. By nature, they are slow-growing and adapted to a mineralized soil.

## Transport

Air transport to and from the province is served by [Siargao Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siargao_Airport). The airport provides air services to Manila, Cebu and Clark which operated by [Cebgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebgo), [PAL Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAL_Express) and [Sunlight Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunlight_Air).

Since 2025, the province has also become accessible by sea through the Jubang International Cruise Port, with passenger services operated by [2GO Travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2GO_Travel).

## Culture

Surigao is home to the [Mamanwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamanwa) ethnic tribe. Their dances are showcased in a local festival called "Bonok-Bonok", held at the feast of [San Nicolas de Tolentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Nicolas_de_Tolentino) which is held annually on September 10. The Bonok-Bonok depicts the native folks' merry-making to show gratitude to God for bountiful harvest and good health.

A collection of ancient archaeological diggings like burial coffins, jars and Chinese ceramics unearthed in Panhutungan, Placer, is on public display at the Surigaonon Heritage Mini-Museum located at the Boulevard in [Surigao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City).

## Tourist attractions

Surigao City, the provincial capital, is dubbed as the "Gateway to Mindanao". The ferry landing terminal in Lipata links Mindanao to [Luzon](69375.md) through [Eastern Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Visayas). It annually showcases its distinct cultural heritage through the Bonok-Bonok Maradjao Karadjao Festival.

On the northern side of the province are [Siargao](2855427.md) and [Bucas Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucas_Grande) Islands, known for their long stretch of sugar-fine beaches, vast mangrove forest, and deep waters teeming with marine life.

The Panhutongan and Amoslog archeological excavations in Placer would give a glimpse of the origins of the province and its people.

## Notable people

- [Erlinda Lavandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlinda_Lavandia?action=edit\&redlink=1) – considered as one of the greatest javelin throwers in the history of the Southeast Asian Games. In 1977, Kuala Lumpur SEA Games, she won the gold medal on a record throw of 45.22 meters. In 1979 in Jakarta, she improved the SEA Games record at 45.78 meters and broke the record two years later at 46.20 meters in Manila Sea Games.
