The **2026 WNBA season** is the 30th and current season of the [Women's National Basketball Association](57721.md) (WNBA). The league expanded from 13 to 15 teams with the addition of the [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire) and [Toronto Tempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Tempo), first announced in 2024.[^cite_note-1] This followed the addition of the [Golden State Valkyries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Valkyries) in 2025.

In October 2025, the WNBA announced that the [Chicago Sky](2717051.md) would host the 2026 WNBA All-Star Game and related events for the second time in franchise history. The All-Star game was played at the [United Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Center) on Saturday, July 25, while the three-point contest and Skills Challenge took place on Friday, July 24, at [Wintrust Arena](40651728.md).[^cite_note-all-star2-2][^cite_note-3]

This will be the last season for the [Connecticut Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Sun) in [Uncasville, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncasville,_Connecticut) after [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets) owner [Tilman Fertitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilman_Fertitta) purchased the franchise with the intention to relocate the team to [Houston](13774.md) to revive the [Houston Comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Comets).[^cite_note-4]

## Offseason

### Collective bargaining agreement

The current WNBA [Collective Bargaining Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Bargaining_Agreement) (CBA), signed in 2020, was originally to run through 2027, but the [Women's National Basketball Player's Association](68077461.md) (WNBPA) opted out in October 2024, accelerating its termination to October 31, 2025.[^cite_note-optout-5]

The previous CBA was signed in January 2020, covering the 2020–2027 seasons. The agreement introduced increased salaries, maternity and family benefits, and improved travel conditions.[^cite_note-6]

#### Timeline

- October 2024: The WNBPA officially notified the league of its intent to opt-out of the existing CBA.

  [^cite_note-optout-5]

- Spring 2025: Preliminary discussions began between league representatives and the union.

  [^cite_note-7]

- July 2025: The WNBPA issued a public statement claiming that the league has "failed to meet players' priorities" in the early stages of negotiations.

  [^cite_note-8]

- October 2025: Tensions escalated after [Minnesota Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Lynx) forward [Napheesa Collier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napheesa_Collier) criticized league leadership, prompting WNBA Commissioner [Cathy Engelbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathy_Engelbert) to respond that she will "do better" in rebuilding trust with players.

  [^cite_note-9]

- October 30: It was announced that the WNBA and WNBPA had reached an agreement for a 30-day extension to continue negotiations.

  [^cite_note-10]

- November 30: It was announced that the WNBA and WNBPA had reached an agreement to a 6-week extension pushing back the expiration deadline for the current CBA until January 9, 2026.

  [^cite_note-11]

- December 18: The WNBPA announced that the union had authorized the executive committee to "call a strike when necessary" as players continue negotiations with the league over a new CBA.

  [^cite_note-12]

- February 23, 2026: The WNBA told the WNBPA and its teams that a new CBA must be agreed to by March 10 for the 2026 schedule not to be impacted.

  [^cite_note-13]

- March 18: The WNBA and WNBPA reached a verbal agreement on the terms of a new CBA.

  [^cite_note-\:0-14]

- March 23: WNBA players unanimously approved the new CBA.

  [^cite_note-15]

- March 24: The WNBA Board of Governors ratified the new CBA.

  [^cite_note-16]

The regular season began as scheduled on May 8.[^cite_note-\:0-14]

## Drafts

### Draft lottery

On November 7, 2025, the [WNBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA) announced that the 2026 draft lottery would be broadcast on Sunday, November 23, on [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN).[^cite_note-17]

The [Dallas Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Wings) won the first overall pick in the draft lottery for the second consecutive year.[^cite_note-18]

### Expansion draft

Main article: [2026 WNBA expansion draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_WNBA_expansion_draft)



[Toronto Tempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Tempo) players in a [pick and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pick_and_roll) action on May 1, 2026, ahead of their inaugural 2026 WNBA season.

An expansion draft was held on April 3. The [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire) and [Toronto Tempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Tempo) selected players for their respective debut rosters. The Fire and Tempo took turns picking one player from each of the thirteen existing teams except for the [Chicago Sky](2717051.md). However, Portland chose not to pick a player from the [Las Vegas Aces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Aces) nor did Toronto draft anyone from the [Seattle Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Storm).[^cite_note-19]

## Media coverage

### United States

This is the first season of new 11-year media rights agreements with the [ESPN family of networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_on_ESPN), [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_on_NBC), [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_on_Prime_Video).[^cite_note-media-82] At a combined value of approximately \$2.2 billion over the length of these three agreements, it is a substantial increase from the league's previous broadcast contracts.[^cite_note-83]

Due to NBC's deal being signed in 2024 before [NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal) spun-off most of its domestic cable networks to [Versant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versant) in January 2026, the league signed a separate 11-year agreement for expanded coverage on [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Sports_\(2025%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-USA_agreement-84][^cite_note-85]

This is also the first season of new multi-year deals with [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_on_CBS) and [Scripps Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Sports).[^cite_note-CBS_agreement-86][^cite_note-ION_agreement-87][^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

- ESPN networks will air 30 games (with all 30 games available to stream on [ESPN DTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_DTC)) . Twelve games will air on ABC on Saturday or Sunday afternoons, while 18 games will air on ESPN on Tuesday and Sunday nights, in addition to Saturday and Sunday afternoons. ABC will air the [2026 WNBA All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_WNBA_All-Star_Game) in primetime, while ESPN will air the Skills Challenge and 3-Point Contest.

  [^cite_note-89]

  &#x20;In the playoffs, ESPN Networks will air two first round series and one semifinal series.

  [^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

- NBC Sports will air 22 games. Seven games will air on NBC and stream on Peacock, all on Sundays, with two games in primetime (inheriting the *Sunday Night Basketball* banner introduced earlier for the NBA) and the rest in the afternoon. Fifteen games will air on [NBCSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCSN) and [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_\(streaming_service\)), primarily on Monday nights. During the playoffs, NBC Sports will air one semifinal series. Peacock will stream all games of the WNBA Finals, with Games 1 and 4 also on NBC.

  [^cite_note-90]

  [^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

  [^cite_note-91]

- USA Network will air 48 regular season games, primarily on Monday and Wednesday nights. In addition, to end the regular season, USA Network will air nine games over the season's final eight days.

  [^cite_note-92]

  &#x20;During the playoffs, USA Network will broadcast one first round series and all games except 1 and 4 of the WNBA Finals. Select games on USA Network will stream on Peacock.

  [^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

- Amazon Prime Video will air 30 regular season games, primarily on Thursday nights, as well as the championship game of the [WNBA Commissioner's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Commissioner's_Cup).

  [^cite_note-93]

  &#x20;During the playoffs, Prime Video will air one first round series.

  [^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

- CBS Sports will air 20 regular season games on [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) and [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+). Unlike the previous agreement, [CBS Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_Network) will no longer air games. On CBS, eight games will air in primetime on Saturday nights. The remaining games will air on Saturday and Sunday afternoons.

  [^cite_note-CBS_agreement-86]

- [Ion Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Television) will air 50 Friday night games. Select games will air in regional windows with out-of-market games on WNBA League Pass.

  [^cite_note-ION_agreement-87]

  [^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

- [NBA TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_TV) will air 15 games.

  [^cite_note-wnbaan-88]

### Canada

- This will be the first season of a multi-year deal for [Bell Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Media) to air games on [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) and [CTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTV_Television_Network) in Canada, including almost all Toronto Tempo games and almost all other major WNBA events.

  [^cite_note-94]

  [^cite_note-95]

- As in past seasons, [NBA TV Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_TV_Canada) continues to carry selected games not covered by the Bell Media and Prime Video deals.

  [^cite_note-sbjbell-96]
