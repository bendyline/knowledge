**Kurds in Sweden** ([Swedish](26689.md): *Kurder i Sverige*; [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): *Kurdên Swêdê*) may refer to people born in or residing in [Sweden](5058739.md) who are of [Kurdish](17068.md) origin.&#x20;

Most [Kurdish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) in Sweden live in the capital [Stockholm](26741.md) or in [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-2] A majority of Kurdish political refugees choose Sweden as their host country and therefore they have a cultural presence in Sweden.[^cite_note-3]&#x20;

## Chronological Kurdish arrivals to Sweden

- 1975: [Huddinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddinge) becomes the first municipality offering Kurdish as a primary language.

  [^cite_note-4]

- 1984: The education of Kurdish teachers in [Stockholm](26741.md) begins.

  [^cite_note-5]

- 1986-1990: Economical decline and the claimed mistreatment towards Kurdish people by the Turkish Republic led to immigration to Sweden.

  [^cite_note-6]

- 1991-1995: The crises in [Kuwait](7515890.md) due to Iraqi invasion, the war in [south Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kurdistan), and mass exodus from [Turkish Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Kurdistan) into Iran.

  [^cite_note-7]

## Political representation

In October 2019, thousands of Swedish Kurds staged a protest in [Stockholm](26741.md) over [Turkey's military operation in northeastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Turkish_offensive_into_north-eastern_Syria).[^cite_note-8] In Sweden there are several MP of Kurdish descent such [Amineh Kakabaveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amineh_Kakabaveh), [Gulan Avci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulan_Avci), [Lawen Redar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawen_Redar), [Sara Gille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Gille) or [Kadir Kasirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadir_Kasirga).[^cite_note-9] Kakabaveh was delivered the majority vote for that the Social Democrat [Magdalena Andersson](41178429.md) would become [Swedish Prime Minister](195846.md) in 2021.[^cite_note-10] As in May 2022 Sweden made an accession bid [to join NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO), Turkey demanded that Sweden ends its alleged support for the Kurdish [People's Defense Units](36497032.md) (YPG) and [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK).[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

## Controversies

### Honor killings

The 26-year-old Kurdish woman [Fadime Şahindal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadime_Sahindal) was murdered by her father in an [honour killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honour_killing) in 2002.[^cite_note-auto-12][^cite_note-13] Kurdish organizations were criticized by prime minister [Göran Persson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ran_Persson) for not doing enough to prevent honour killings.[^cite_note-auto-12] [Pela Atroshi was a Kurdish girl who was shot by her uncle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_killing_of_Pela_Atroshi) in a brutal honour killing.[^cite_note-14] The murder of Pela and Fadime gave rise to the formation of the human rights organization Never Forget Pela and Fadime (GAPF). GAPF is a politically and religiously independent and secular nonprofit organization working against honor-related violence and oppression. The organization's name is taken from Pela Atroshi and Fadime Sahindal which is Sweden's best-known and high-profile cases of honor killings.[^cite_note-15] The honor killing of Sara, an Iraqi Kurdish girl, was the first publicized honor killing in Sweden.[^cite_note-16] These three prominent cases of Sara, Pela and Fadime, brought the notion of honour killings into Swedish discourse.[^cite_note-auto-12]&#x20;
