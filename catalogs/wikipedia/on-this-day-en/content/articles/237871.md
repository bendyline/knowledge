# FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives

A color photograph of a man with a moustache wearing tinted glasses, a white undershirt, and a yellow overshirt in front of a white wall

On May 19, 1996, [Leslie Isben Rogge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Isben_Rogge) (pictured here in 1973) became the first person on the FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list to be apprehended due to the FBI's then-new home page on the [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet).

The **FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives** is a [most wanted list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_wanted_list) maintained by the [United States](3434750.md)' [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI). The list arose from a conversation held in late 1949 between [J. Edgar Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Edgar_Hoover), [Director of the FBI](5861065.md), and [William Kinsey Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kinsey_Hutchinson),[^cite_note-faq-1] [International News Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_News_Service) (the predecessor of the [United Press International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Press_International)) editor-in-chief, who were discussing ways to promote capture of the FBI's "toughest guys". This discussion turned into a published article, which received so much positive publicity that on March 14, 1950, the FBI officially announced the list to increase law enforcement's ability to capture dangerous [fugitives](406792.md).[^cite_note-History-2] The first person added to the list was Thomas J. Holden, a robber and member of the [Holden–Keating Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden%E2%80%93Keating_Gang) on the day of the list's inception.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-faq-1]

Individuals are generally only removed from the list if they are captured, dead, or if the charges against them are dropped; they are then replaced by a new entry selected by the FBI. In eleven cases, the FBI removed individuals from the list after deciding that they were no longer a "particularly dangerous menace to society". [Machetero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boricua_Popular_Army) member [Víctor Manuel Gerena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Manuel_Gerena), added to the list in 1984, was on the list for 32 years, which was longer than anyone else.[^cite_note-faq-1] Samuel Ramirez Jr. spent the shortest amount of time on the list, being listed for one hour and thirteen minutes in 2026.[^cite_note-4] The oldest person to be added to the list was [Eugene Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Palmer_\(fugitive\)) on May 29, 2019, at 80 years old. On rare occasions, the FBI will add a "Number Eleven" if that individual is extremely dangerous but the Bureau does not feel any of the current ten should be removed.[^cite_note-5] Despite occasional references in the media, the FBI does not rank their list; no suspect is considered "#1 on the FBI's Most Wanted List" or "The Most Wanted".[^cite_note-faq-1]

The list is commonly posted in public places such as [post offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_office). Some people on the list have turned themselves in.[^cite_note-8] On May 18, 1996, after surrendering at the U.S. embassy in Guatemala City, [Leslie Isben Rogge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Isben_Rogge) became the first person on the FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list to be apprehended due to the FBI's then-new home page on the internet.[^cite_note-9] The FBI maintains other lists of individuals, including the [FBI Most Wanted Terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Most_Wanted_Terrorists),[^cite_note-10] along with crime alerts, missing persons, and other fugitive lists.

On June 17, 2013, the list reached a cumulative total of 500 fugitives having been listed.[^cite_note-11] As of October 2, 2026, 543 fugitives had been listed, thirteen of them women (2%), and 504 of them were captured or located (93%), 164 (31%) of them due to public assistance.[^cite_note-faq-1][^cite_note-12]

## New additions

The [Criminal Investigative Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Criminal_Investigative_Division) (CID) at [FBI Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Edgar_Hoover_Building) calls upon all 56 Field Offices to submit candidates for the FBI's "Ten Most Wanted Fugitives" list.[^cite_note-FAQ-13] The nominees received are reviewed by special agents in the CID and the Office of Public Affairs.[^cite_note-FAQ-13] The selection of the proposed candidates is forwarded to the assistant director of the CID for their approval and then to the FBI's Director for final approval.[^cite_note-FAQ-13] This process takes some time, which is why [James Joseph "Whitey" Bulger Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitey_Bulger), who was arrested in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California), on June 22, 2011,[^cite_note-14] remained on the list until May 9, 2012,[^cite_note-15] despite no longer being at large. [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) similarly remained on the list for almost a year after his [death at the hands of U.S. forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Osama_bin_Laden) on May 2, 2011.[^cite_note-16]

On occasion, fugitives have been added to the list at the request of local law enforcement. For example, Bureau director [Clarence M. Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_M._Kelley) added [Twymon Myers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twymon_Myers) to the list in 1973 at the request of [New York City police commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Police_Commissioner) [Donald Cawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Cawley).[^cite_note-17]
