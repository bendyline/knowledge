Robin Hood Statue, Sherwood Forest

**Sherwood Forest** consists of the remnants of an ancient [royal forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_forest) in Nottinghamshire, in the [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands) region of England. It is associated with the [legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend) of [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood). The forest was proclaimed by [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) and mentioned in the [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) in 1086. The reserve has the highest concentration of ancient trees in Europe.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-3]

Today, Sherwood Forest [National Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_nature_reserve_\(United_Kingdom\)) encompasses 424.75 hectares (1,049.6 acres),[^cite_note-4] surrounding the village of [Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwinstowe) and the site of [Thoresby Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoresby_Hall). The reserve contains more than a thousand ancient oaks which are known to be more than 500 years old, with the [Major Oak](506201.md) being twice that age.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-3]

## Etymology

Sherwood originally was named Sciryuda in 958 AD, meaning the "woodland belonging to the shire". Its name is derived from its status as the [shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire) (or sher) wood of Nottinghamshire, which extended into several neighbouring counties (shires), bordered to the west by the [River Erewash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Erewash) and the [Forest of East Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_East_Derbyshire).[^cite_note-history-5]

Sherwood Forest is within an area which used to be called "Birch Lund", which is [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) in origin, now known as Birklands.[^cite_note-6]

## Prehistory

The area has been wooded since the end of the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period) (as attested by [pollen sampling cores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palynology)). This was about 10,000 years ago.[^cite_note-7]

Evidence of flint tools have shown use in Sherwood Forest by prehistoric hunter gatherers. During the Iron Age and Roman periods human habitation and farming was common. In the 9th century farming made an impact on Sherwood's landscape.[^cite_note-nottinghamshire.gov.uk-8]

## History



Royal Forests between 1327 and 1336

During [Roman Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) various camps were discovered in parts of Sherwood Forest. It is to this that two remains of [Roman villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_villa) were identified in nearby [Mansfield Woodhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Woodhouse) by [Major Hayman Rooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayman_Rooke) in 1787.[^cite_note-9]

Sherwood Forest was first recorded as being named Sciryuda in 958 AD.[^cite_note-nottinghamshire.gov.uk-8]

[King Edwin of Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria) in 633 AD was killed at [Hatfield Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_Chase) in a battle against his Mercian rival [King Penda of Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penda_of_Mercia) and his body was carried into the forest and buried/hidden in [St Mary's Church, Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Church,_Edwinstowe). His head was later buried in [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York) and his body in [Whitby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitby).[^cite_note-10]

The village of [Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwinstowe) takes its name from [King Edwin of Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

In 1066, in the invasion of England, [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) made Sherwood Forest a Royal Hunting Forest.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-auto1-14] When the [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) was compiled in 1086, the forest covered perhaps a quarter of Nottinghamshire (approximately 19,000 acres or 7,800 hectares) in woodland and heath subject to the [forest laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_forest).

The earliest notice of the forest at Sherwood was at the time of [Henry II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II_of_England) when [William Peverel the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Peverel_the_Younger) answered the plea of the forest, to which he profited and controlled the area.[^cite_note-15]

During the 12th and 13th centuries Christian Monastic Orders had established large estates within Sherwood Forest. Three Abbeys were founded [Rufford Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufford_Abbey), [Newstead Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newstead_Abbey) and [Thurgarton Priory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priory_Church_of_St_Peter,_Thurgarton).[^cite_note-16]

Sherwood Forest was frequently visited by the [Mercian Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Mercia).The forest became popular with [King John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_King_of_England) and [Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England). The remains of a [hunting lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_lodge) can be found at [Kings Clipstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Clipstone) named [King John's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_John's_Palace).[^cite_note-nottinghamshire.gov.uk-8] Prior to King John reluctantly signing the [Magna Carta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Carta) at [Runnymede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runnymede) in 1215, the [Forest Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Laws) came with much displeasure to the ruling classes of the forest.[^cite_note-17]



[King John's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_John's_Palace), Kings Clipstone

After the [dissolution of the monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries) by [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII) in 1536, the land of Sherwood was sold and granted into private ownership which was converted into house estates. [James I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_VI_and_I) in the 1600s visited the forest, as did [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England) and [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) brought back under control the management of Sherwood Forest.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-18] The oak trees from Sherwood Forest were used to build the roof of [St Paul's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_Cathedral) in London and [1st Viscount Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Viscount_Nelson)'s naval fleet.[^cite_note-19]

Sherwood Forest in 1623 had a narrow escape from a fire which broke out. The only record of this occurrence is written in a letter which is preserved in the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum).[^cite_note-20]

In the 17th and 18th centuries [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) and then [Queen Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne,_Queen_of_Great_Britain) sold large areas of Sherwood Crown Land to private owners who built the estates of [Thoresby Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoresby_Hall), [The former Clumber House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clumber_Park), [Welbeck Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welbeck_Abbey) and [Worksop Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop_Manor). These estates became known as [the Dukeries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dukeries). [Newstead Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newstead_Abbey) was converted into a country House and [Rufford Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufford_Abbey) was partially demolished and converted into a country House.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-18][^cite_note-21]

[Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield) anciently became pre-eminent in importance among the towns of the forest.[^cite_note-auto-22]

## Geology

Sherwood Forest is established over an area underlain by the [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) and [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic) age [New Red Sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Red_Sandstone).[^cite_note-23] The larger part of the Forest is found across the outcrop of pebbly [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) known as the Chester Formation. The regional dip is a gentle one to the east, hence younger rocks are found in that direction and older ones exposed to the west. The local [stratigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratigraphy) is (uppermost/youngest at top):

- [Mercia Mudstone Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia_Mudstone_Group)&#x20;

  - Tarporley Siltstone Formation ([siltstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siltstone), [mudstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudstone) and sandstones)&#x20;

    - including Retford Member (mudstones)

- [Sherwood Sandstone Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_Sandstone_Group)&#x20;

  - Chester Formation (pebbly sandstones)

  - Lenton Sandstone Formation

  - Edlington Formation (mudstones and sandstones)

    [^cite_note-24]

The sandstone is an [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) providing a local water supply. [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) deposits include river [sands and gravels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium), river terrace deposits and some scattered mid-[Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) glacial [till](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Till). There are 41 [local geodiversity sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionally_important_geological_site) within the Sherwood NCA; these are largely quarries and river sections.[^cite_note-25]

## Management and conservation

The Sherwood Forest Trust is a small charity that covers the ancient royal boundary and current [national character area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_character_area) of Sherwood Forest.[^cite_note-26] Its aims are based on conservation, heritage and communities but also include tourism and the economy.

Nottinghamshire County Council and [Forestry England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_England) jointly manage the ancient remnant of forest north of the village of Edwinstowe, providing walks, footpaths and a host of other activities.[^cite_note-27]

This central core of ancient Sherwood is a [Site of Special Scientific Interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_of_Special_Scientific_Interest) (SSSI) called [Birklands And Bilhaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birklands_And_Bilhaugh),[^cite_note-28] NNR[^cite_note-29] and [Special Area of Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Area_of_Conservation) (SAC).[^cite_note-30] It is a very important site for ancient oaks, wood pasture, invertebrates and fungi, as well as being linked to the legends of Robin Hood.

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) parts of Sherwood Forest were used extensively by the military for ammunition stores, [POW camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW_camp) and training areas.[^cite_note-31] Oil was produced at [Eakring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eakring#World_War_II_oil).[^cite_note-32] After the war, large ammunition dumps were abandoned in the forest and were not cleared until 1952, with at least 46,000 tons of ammunition in them.[^cite_note-33]

Part of the forest was opened to the public as a [country park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_park) in 1969 by Nottinghamshire County Council, which manages a small part of the forest under lease from the [Thoresby Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoresby_Hall). In 2002 a portion of Sherwood Forest was designated a [national nature reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_nature_reserves_in_England) by [English Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Nature). In 2007 [Natural England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_England) officially incorporated the Budby South Forest, Nottinghamshire's largest area of dry lowland heath, into the Nature Reserve, nearly doubling its size from 220 to 423 hectares (540 to 1,050 acres).[^cite_note-34]

A new Sherwood Forest Visitor Centre was authorised in 2015. In August 2018 the [Royal Society for the Protection of Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_for_the_Protection_of_Birds) (RSPB) opened the new development with a shop and café, having been granted permission to manage the woods in 2015. Part of an agreement with Natural England was that the land where the existing 1970s visitor centre was located would be restored to wood pasture.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Some portions of the forest retain many very old oaks, especially in the portion known as the [Dukeries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukeries), south of the town of [Worksop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop), which was so called because it used to contain four ducal residences, as well as a number of other country estates.

The [River Idle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Idle), a tributary of the [Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), is formed in Sherwood Forest from the confluence of several minor streams.

## Tourism



Visitor Centre

Sherwood attracts around 350,000 tourists annually, many from other countries.[^cite_note-38] Each August the nature reserve hosts a week-long Robin Hood Festival. This event recreates a medieval atmosphere and features the major characters from the Robin Hood legend. The week's entertainment includes [jousters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jousting) and strolling players dressed in medieval attire, in addition to a medieval encampment complete with [jesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jester), musicians, [rat-catchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat-catcher), [alchemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy) and [fire eaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_eating).[^cite_note-39]

The Sherwood Forest Art and Craft Centre is in the former coach house and stables of Edwinstowe Hall.[^cite_note-40] The centre contains art studios and a café and hosts special events, including craft demonstrations and exhibitions.

## Major Oak

Main article: [Major Oak](506201.md)



[Major Oak](506201.md) in 2014

Sherwood Forest is home to the [Major Oak](506201.md), an [oak](39696.md) tree between 800 and 1,000 years old, and since the [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era), its limbs have been partially supported by scaffolding. The Major Oak was identified by Major [Hayman Rooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayman_Rooke) in 1790. It is believed that the Major Oak took the name of Major [Hayman Rooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayman_Rooke).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The Major Oak used to be named the Cockpen Tree, after the cockfighting that once took place beneath it.[^cite_note-45] According to the legend, [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood) and his outlaws used this tree to hide from the [Sheriff of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriff_of_Nottingham).

The Major Oak was featured on the 2005 [BBC](19344654.md) TV programme *[Seven Natural Wonders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Natural_Wonders)* as one of the natural wonders of the [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Midlands).[^cite_note-46]

The Major Oak was confirmed to be dead in 2026. Saplings of the Major Oak have been planted in the US ambassadors' [Winfield House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfield_House) in London.[^cite_note-47]

## Parliament Oak



[Parliament Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_Oak), Peafield Lane

The [Parliament Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_Oak) is situated in Sherwood Forest near [Market Warsop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Warsop). It is reputed that [King John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_King_of_England) in 1212 and [King Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) in 1290 had impromptu parliaments at the tree.[^cite_note-48]

## Thynghowe

Main article: [Thynghowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thynghowe)

[Thynghowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thynghowe), an important [Danelaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danelaw) meeting place where people came to resolve disputes and settle issues, was lost to history until its rediscovery in 2005–06 by local history enthusiasts[^cite_note-49] amidst the old oaks of an area known as the Birklands. Experts believe it may also yield clues about the boundary of the ancient [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons) kingdoms of [Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia) and [Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria).

[English Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Heritage) inspected the site, confirming that it was known as 'Thynghowe' in 1334 and 1609.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Politics

The forest gives its name to the [Parliamentary constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Parliament_constituencies) of [Sherwood Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) (formerly Sherwood before the [2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Periodic_Review_of_Westminster_constituencies)).[^cite_note-52] This is represented by one member of parliament.
