The **Mediterranean Sea** ([/ˌmɛdɪtəˈreɪniən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*MED-ih-tə-RAY-nee-ən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is an intercontinental [sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea) situated between Europe, Asia, and Africa. It is surrounded by the [Mediterranean basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_basin) and almost completely enclosed by land: on the east by the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia), on the north by [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) in West Asia and [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe), and on the south by [North Africa](21714.md). To its west it is connected to the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) via the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) that separates the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) in [Europe](9239.md) from [Morocco](19291.md) in [Africa](5334607.md) by only 14 km (9 mi); additionally, it is connected to the [Black Sea](3386.md) through the [Bosporus](3705.md) strait that intersects Turkey in the northeast and the [Red Sea](26191.md) via the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in the southeast.

The Mediterranean Sea covers an area of about 2,500,000 km<sup>2</sup> (970,000 sq mi),[^cite_note-britannica-4] representing 0.7% of the global [ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean) surface; it includes fifteen [marginal seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_sea), including the [Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea), [Adriatic](23275478.md), [Tyrrhenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea), and [Marmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmara_sea). Geological evidence indicates that around 5.9 million years ago, the Mediterranean was cut off from the Atlantic and was partly or completely [desiccated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desiccation) over a period of some 600,000 years during the [Messinian salinity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis) before being refilled by the [Zanclean flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanclean_flood) about 5.3 million years ago.

The [history of the Mediterranean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Mediterranean_region) is crucial to understanding the origins and development of many modern societies; it is sometimes described as an "incubator of [Western civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_civilization)" and saw the emergence of some of the earliest and most advanced civilisations, including those of [Egypt](8087628.md), [Greece](12108.md), and the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent).[^cite_note-britannica-4] The Levant in the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean) was among the first regions in the world to display permanent human habitation as early as 12,000 BC. The Mediterranean Sea was an important route for [merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant), travellers, and migrants in [antiquity](252905.md), facilitating trade and cultural exchange between various peoples as well as colonisation and conquest. The [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) maintained [nautical hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Nostrum) over the sea for centuries and is the only state to have ever controlled all of its coast.

The Mediterranean Sea has an average depth of 1,500 m (4,900 ft) and the deepest recorded point is 5,109 ± 1 m (16,762 ± 3 ft) in the [Calypso Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_Deep) in the [Ionian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Sea). It lies between latitudes [30°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_parallel_north) and [46° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_parallel_north) and longitudes [6° W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_meridian_west) and [36° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_meridian_east). Its west–east length, from the Strait of Gibraltar to the [Gulf of Alexandretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Alexandretta), on the southeastern coast of [Turkey](11125639.md), is about 4,000 kilometres (2,500 mi). The north–south length varies greatly between different shorelines and whether only straight routes are considered. Also including longitudinal changes, the shortest shipping route between the multinational [Gulf of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Trieste) and the Libyan coastline of the [Gulf of Sidra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra) is about 1,900 kilometres (1,200 mi). The water temperatures are mild in winter and warm in summer and give name to the [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) type due to the majority of precipitation falling in the cooler months. Its southern and eastern coastlines are lined with hot deserts not far inland, but the immediate coastline on all sides of the Mediterranean tends to have strong maritime moderation.

The countries surrounding the Mediterranean and its marginal seas in clockwise order are [Spain](26667.md), [France](5843419.md), [Monaco](19261.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Slovenia](27338.md), [Croatia](5573.md), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Montenegro](20760.md), [Albania](738.md), [Greece](12108.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Palestine](241405.md) ([Gaza Strip](12047.md)), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Libya](17633.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), [Algeria](358.md), and [Morocco](19291.md); [Cyprus](5593.md) and [Malta](19137.md) are [island countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country) in the sea. In addition, [Northern Cyprus](271409.md) ([de facto state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition)) and two [overseas territories](207114.md) of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) ([Akrotiri and Dhekelia](1025144.md), and [Gibraltar](7607314.md)) also have [coastlines](5236.md) along the Mediterranean Sea. The drainage basin encompasses a large number of other countries, the [Nile](21244.md) being the longest river ending in the Mediterranean Sea.[^cite_note-5] The Mediterranean Sea encompasses [a vast number of islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Mediterranean), some of them of volcanic origin. The two largest islands, in both area and population, are [Sicily](27619.md) and [Sardinia](29376.md).

## History

Main article: [History of the Mediterranean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Mediterranean_region)

### Ancient civilizations



Greek (red) and Phoenician (yellow) [colonies in antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies_in_antiquity) c. the 6th century BC



The [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in 117 AD, the end of the reign of [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan)

Major ancient civilizations were located around the Mediterranean. The sea provided routes for trade, colonization, and war, as well as food (from fishing and the gathering of other seafood) for numerous communities throughout the ages.[^cite_note-23] The earliest advanced civilizations in the Mediterranean were the [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) and the [Minoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization), who traded extensively with each other. Around 1200 BC the eastern Mediterranean was greatly affected by the [Bronze Age Collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age_Collapse), which resulted in the destruction of many cities and trade routes.[^cite_note-24]

Two other notable Mediterranean civilizations in classical antiquity are the [Greek](12108.md) [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state) and [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia), both of whom extensively colonized the coastlines of the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-\:3-25]

[Darius I of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I_of_Persia), who conquered Ancient Egypt, built a canal linking the [Red Sea](26191.md) to the Nile, and thus the Mediterranean. Darius's canal was wide enough for two [triremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trireme) to pass each other with oars extended and required four days to traverse.[^cite_note-26]

Following the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) in the 3rd and 2nd centuries BC, the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) defeated the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians) to become the preeminent power in the Western Mediterranean region.[^cite_note-27] When [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) founded the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the Romans referred to the Mediterranean as *[Mare Nostrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Nostrum)* ("Our Sea").[^cite_note-28] For the next 400 years, the Roman Empire completely controlled the Mediterranean Sea and virtually all its coastal regions from Gibraltar to the Levant,[^cite_note-\:3-25] giving the sea the nickname "Roman Lake".[^cite_note-29]

### Middle Ages and empires

The [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) collapsed around 476 AD.[^cite_note-30] The east was again dominant as Roman power still existed on in the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) formed in the 4th century from the eastern half of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-31] During that time, another power arose in the 7th century, and with it the religion of [Islam](6037917.md), which soon swept across from the east; at its greatest extent,[^cite_note-32] the Arabs, under the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate), controlled the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), making a new phase of art and culture in the region.[^cite_note-33]



The port and fleet of [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) in the early 14th century, by [Quinto Cenni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinto_Cenni)

A variety of foodstuffs, spices and crops were introduced to the western Mediterranean's Spain and [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily) during Arab rule, via the commercial networks of the Islamic world. These include sugarcane,[^cite_note-spanish-food.org-34] rice,[^cite_note-35] cotton, alfalfa, oranges,[^cite_note-36] lemons,[^cite_note-37] apricots,[^cite_note-38] spinach,[^cite_note-39] eggplants,[^cite_note-40] carrots,[^cite_note-41] [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron)[^cite_note-42] and bananas.[^cite_note-43] The Arabs also continued extensive cultivation and production of olive oil (the Spanish words for 'oil' and 'olive'—*aceite* and *aceituna*, respectively—are derived from the Arabic *al-zait*, meaning 'olive juice'),[^cite_note-44] and pomegranates (the heraldic symbol of Granada) from classical [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) times.

&#x20;The [Arab invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) disrupted the trade relations between Western and Eastern Europe while disrupting trade routes with Eastern Asian Empires. This, however, had the indirect effect of promoting trade across the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). The export of grains from [Egypt](8087628.md) was re-routed towards the [Eastern world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world). Products from East Asian empires, like silk and spices, were carried from Egypt to ports like [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) by sailors and Jewish merchants. The [Viking raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_expansion) further disrupted the trade in western Europe and brought it to a halt. However, the [Norsemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) developed the trade from Norway to the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea), while also trading in luxury goods from [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) and the Mediterranean. The [Byzantines in the mid-8th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire_under_the_Isaurian_dynasty) retook control of the area around the north-eastern part of the Mediterranean. Venetian ships from the 9th century armed themselves to counter the harassment by Arabs while concentrating trade of Asian goods in Venice.[^cite_note-45]



The [Battle of Lepanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lepanto), 1571, ended in victory for the European [Holy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_League_\(1571\)) against the [Ottoman Turks](22278.md).

The [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) maintained trade relations with the [Italian city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_city-states) like [Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Amalfi) and [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) before the Crusades, according to the [Cairo Geniza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Geniza) documents. A document dated 996 mentions Amalfian merchants living in [Cairo](6293.md). Another letter states that the Genoese had traded with [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). The caliph [al-Mustansir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'ad_al-Mustansir_Billah) had allowed Amalfian merchants to reside in [Jerusalem](16043.md) about 1060 in place of the Latin [hospice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospice).[^cite_note-46]

The [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) led to the flourishing of trade between Europe and the *[outremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outremer)* region.[^cite_note-47] Genoa, Venice and [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa) created colonies in regions controlled by the Crusaders and came to control the trade with the Orient. These colonies also allowed them to trade with the Eastern world. Though the fall of the Crusader states and attempts at banning of trade relations with Muslim states by the Popes temporarily disrupted the trade with the Orient, it however continued.[^cite_note-48] Europe started to revive, however, when there was gradual centralization of state power in the [Renaissance of the 12th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_of_the_12th_century).[^cite_note-49]



The [bombardment of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Algiers_\(1816\)) by the Anglo-Dutch fleet in support of an ultimatum to release [European slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade), August 1816

[Ottoman](22278.md) power based in Anatolia continued to grow, and in 1453 extinguished the Byzantine Empire with the [Conquest of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Constantinople).[^cite_note-50] [Hayreddin Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Barbarossa), the Ottoman captain is a symbol of this domination with the victory of the [Battle of Preveza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Preveza) (1538), opening up [Tripoli](31232.md) and the eastern Mediterranean to Ottoman rule.[^cite_note-51] As the naval prowess of the European powers increased, they confronted Ottoman expansion in the region when the [Battle of Lepanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lepanto) (1571) damaging the power of the [Ottoman Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Navy). This was the last naval battle to be fought primarily between [galleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley).[^cite_note-52]

The [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) of [Northwest Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Africa) preyed on Christian shipping and coastlines in the Western Mediterranean Sea.[^cite_note-53] According to Robert Davis, from the 16th to 19th centuries, pirates captured 1 million to 1.25 million Europeans as slaves.[^cite_note-54]

The development of oceanic shipping began to affect the entire Mediterranean. Once, most of the trade between Western Europe and the East was [passing through the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean), but after the 1490s the development of a sea route to the Indian Ocean allowed the importation of Asian [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade) and other goods through the Atlantic ports of western Europe.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]



[Battle of the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Nile) during the [French campaign in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Egypt_and_Syria), August 1798

The sea remained strategically important. British mastery of [Gibraltar](7607314.md) ensured their influence in Africa and Southwest Asia. Especially after the naval battles of Abukir (1799, [Battle of the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Nile)) and [Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar) (1805), the British had for a long time strengthened their dominance in the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-58] Wars included [Naval warfare in the Mediterranean during World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare_in_the_Mediterranean_during_World_War_I)[^cite_note-59] and [Mediterranean theatre of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_theatre_of_World_War_II).[^cite_note-60]

With the opening of the lockless [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1869, the flow of trade between Europe and Asia changed fundamentally. The fastest route now led through the Mediterranean towards East Africa and Asia. This led to a preference for the Mediterranean countries and their ports like [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) with direct connections to Central and Eastern Europe experienced a rapid economic rise. In the 20th century, the 1st and 2nd World Wars as well as the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis) and the [Cold War](325329.md) led to a shift of trade routes to the European northern ports, which changed again towards the southern ports through European integration, the activation of the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) and free world trade.[^cite_note-61]

### 21st century and migrations

Further information: [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md), [List of migrant vessel incidents on the Mediterranean Sea](47418106.md), and [Timeline of the 2015 European migrant crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2015_European_migrant_crisis)



[Satellite image](1184557.md) of the Mediterranean Sea at night&#x20;

In 2013, the [Maltese](19137.md) president described the Mediterranean Sea as a "cemetery" due to the large number of migrants who drowned there after their boats capsized.[^cite_note-62] [European Parliament](9581.md) president [Martin Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Schulz) said in 2014 that Europe's migration policy "turned the Mediterranean into a graveyard", referring to the number of drowned refugees in the region as a direct result of the policies.[^cite_note-63] An Azerbaijani official described the sea as "a burial ground ... where people die".[^cite_note-64]

Following the [2013 Lampedusa migrant shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Lampedusa_migrant_shipwreck), the [Italian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letta_Cabinet) decided to strengthen the national system for the patrolling of the Mediterranean Sea by authorizing "[Operation Mare Nostrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mare_Nostrum)", a military and humanitarian mission in order to rescue the migrants and arrest the traffickers of immigrants. In 2015, more than one million migrants crossed the Mediterranean Sea into Europe.[^cite_note-65]

Italy was particularly affected by the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md). Between 2013 and 2018, over 700,000 migrants landed in Italy,[^cite_note-66] mainly sub-Saharan Africans.[^cite_note-67]

### Hydrography



Approximate extent of the Mediterranean drainage basin (dark green). [Nile](21244.md) basin only partially shown

The [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of the Mediterranean Sea is particularly heterogeneous and extends much further than the Mediterranean region.[^cite_note-76] Its size has been estimated between 4,000,000 and 5,500,000 km<sup>2</sup> (1,500,000 and 2,100,000 sq mi),[^cite_note-77] depending on whether non-active parts (deserts) are included or not.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-Margat_2004_p4-79][^cite_note-80] The longest river ending in the Mediterranean Sea is the [Nile](21244.md), which takes its sources in equatorial Africa. The basin of the Nile constitutes about two-thirds of the Mediterranean drainage basin[^cite_note-Margat_2004_p4-79] and encompasses areas as high as the [Ruwenzori Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruwenzori_Mountains).[^cite_note-81] Among other important rivers in Africa, are the [Moulouya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulouya_River) and the [Chelif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelif_River), both on the north side of the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains). In Asia, are the [Ceyhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceyhan_River) and [Seyhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyhan_River), both on the south side of the [Taurus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains).[^cite_note-UNEP-82] In Europe, the largest basins are those of the [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne), [Ebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebro), [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)), and [Maritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa).[^cite_note-UNECE-83] The basin of the Rhône is the largest and extends up as far north as the [Jura Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Mountains), encompassing areas even on the north side of the [Alps](981.md).[^cite_note-84] The basins of the Ebro, Po, and Maritsa, are respectively south of the [Pyrenees](24707.md), Alps, and [Balkan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Mountains), which are the major ranges bordering Southern Europe.

Total annual precipitation is significantly higher on the European part of the Mediterranean basin, especially near the Alps (the 'water tower of Europe') and other high mountain ranges. As a consequence, the [river discharges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)) of the Rhône and Po are similar to that of the Nile, despite the latter having a much larger basin.[^cite_note-UNEP-82] These are the only three rivers with an average discharge of over 1,000 m<sup>3</sup>/s (35,000 cu ft/s).[^cite_note-Margat_2004_p4-79] Among large natural fresh bodies of water are [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria) (Nile basin), [Lake Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Geneva) (Rhône), and the [Italian Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Lakes) (Po). While the Mediterranean watershed is bordered by other river basins in Europe, it is essentially bordered by [endorheic basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorheic_basin) or deserts elsewhere.

### Climate

Much of the Mediterranean coast has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate#Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate). The Mediterranean basin has a climate characterised by mild[^cite_note-\:6-98][^cite_note-\:7-99] wet winters,[^cite_note-\:7-99] and calm, dry and hot summers.[^cite_note-\:6-98][^cite_note-\:7-99] Although they are rare, [tropical cyclones occasionally form in the Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_tropical-like_cyclone), typically in September–November.[^cite_note-100]

## Seabed

The Mediterranean Sea has numerous [underwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_environment) [geological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology) features formed by the [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) of the [African Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_plate) beneath the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_plate).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] The sea is divided naturally into western and [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean) regions by the [Malta Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_Escarpment) that runs from the island of [Sicily](27619.md) to the [African](5334607.md) coast.[^cite_note-\:0-116][^cite_note-\:1-117][^cite_note-\:2-118][^cite_note-119]

Until the 1960s, the Mediterranean was believed to be the primary remaining portion of the earlier (200 million years old) [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) [Tethys Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethys_Ocean), which once encircled the [Eastern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Hemisphere). However, since the late 20th century, research using the theory of seafloor spreading has indicated that most of the current Mediterranean seafloor is not a portion of the Tethys sea floor.[^cite_note-\:0-116] Some researchers consider the Ionian Basin, to the east of the Malta Escarpment, to be the remnant of the Tethys Ocean.[^cite_note-127] Over the course of the last 44 million years, the continental plates of Africa and [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) have converged and receded, resulting in the current tectonically active basin and its surrounding mountain chains. According to the interpretation of geologic data, there are currently several major places where Africa and Eurasia collide, causing land submergence, mountain building, and volcanism.[^cite_note-\:0-116]

[Sediment cores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment_core) drilled in 1970 and 1975 led to theories that about 6 million years ago, the Mediterranean was around 3,000 metres (10,000 ft) below the current sea level and included arid deserts blanketed with evaporite salts. It was thought that Gibraltar's high ridges prevented Atlantic waters from entering until roughly 5.5 million years ago, when they broke through and flooded the Mediterranean. According to more recent seismic and microfossil research, the seafloor was never entirely dry. Rather, approximately 5 million years ago, the seafloor was made up of many basins with varying topography and sizes, spanning in depth from 200 to 1,520 metres (650 to 5,000 ft). Salts were likely accumulated on the bottom of highly salinised waters of widely differing depths. The uncertainty of the timing and nature of sea-bottom salt formation and evidence from later seismic research and [core samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_sample) has been the subject of intense scientific debate.[^cite_note-\:0-116]

### Malta Escarpment

The [Malta Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_Escarpment) is a 250-kilometre (160 mi) [undersea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_environment) [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) that stretches south from Sicily's eastern coast to the [Maltese islands](19137.md)' eastern coast and beyond, primarily formed due to tectonic activities.[^cite_note-\:0-116] There are more than 500 undersea canyons along the cliffs, which can reach heights of 3.5 kilometres (2.2 mi) in some locations. Rich biological communities may be found in the canyons, which also serve as channels for contaminants and nutrients due to underwater currents. These deep valleys are special due to the fact that they were not carved out by surface rivers. Underwater [landslides](37208.md) are among the natural hazards found on the Malta Escarpment. The [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta), [UK National Oceanography Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Oceanography_Centre_Southampton), [New Zealand National Institute of Water and Atmospheric Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Water_and_Atmospheric_Research), [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) and [Italy's Istituto Nazionale di Oceanografia e Geofisica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Oceanography_and_Applied_Geophysics) collaborated on a recent study financed by the [European Union](9317.md) that focused on the Escarpment.[^cite_note-\:1-117][^cite_note-128]

## Oceanography



Predominant surface currents for June

[Evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation) greatly exceeds [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) and river runoff in the Mediterranean, a fact that is central to the water circulation within the basin.[^cite_note-129] Evaporation is especially high in its eastern half, causing the water level to decrease and [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) to increase eastward.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPinet1996206-130] The average salinity in the basin is 38 PSU at 5 m (16 ft) depth.[^cite_note-tempandsal-131]

The temperature of the water in the deepest part of the Mediterranean Sea is 13.2 °C (55.8 °F).[^cite_note-tempandsal-131]

The net water influx from the Atlantic Ocean is ca. 70,000 m<sup>3</sup>/s (2.5 million cu ft/s) or 2.2×10<sup>12</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/a (7.8×10<sup>13</sup> cu ft/a).[^cite_note-132] Without this Atlantic water, the sea level of the Mediterranean Sea would fall at a rate of about 1 m (3 ft) per year.[^cite_note-133]

In [oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography), it is sometimes called the *Eurafrican Mediterranean Sea*, the *European Mediterranean Sea* or the *African Mediterranean Sea* to distinguish it from [mediterranean seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_sea_\(oceanography\)) elsewhere,[^cite_note-134] like the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), and the North-east Atlantic Ocean.[^cite_note-135]

### General circulation

[Water circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_circulation) in the Mediterranean can be attributed to the surface waters entering from the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic) through the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) (and also low salinity water entering the Mediterranean from the Black Sea through the [Bosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosphorus)). The cool and relatively low-salinity Atlantic water circulates eastwards along the [North African coasts](21714.md#Countries_and_territories). A part of the surface water does not pass the [Strait of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Sicily), but deviates towards [Corsica](5714828.md) before [exiting the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar#Inflow_and_outflow). The surface waters entering the [eastern Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_basin) circulate along the Libyan and Israeli coasts. Upon reaching the [Levantine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantine_Sea), the surface water, having warmed and increased its salinity from its initial Atlantic state, is now denser and sinks to form the Levantine Intermediate Waters (LIW). Most of the water found anywhere between 50 and 600 m (160 and 2,000 ft) deep in the Mediterranean originates from the LIW.[^cite_note-136]

LIW are formed along the coasts of Turkey and circulate westwards along the Greek and [south Italian coasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Italy#Boundaries). LIW are the only waters passing the Sicily Strait westwards. After the Strait of Sicily, the LIW circulate along the Italian, French and Spanish coasts before exiting the Mediterranean through the depths of the Strait of Gibraltar. Deep water in the Mediterranean originates from three main areas: the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md), from which most of the deep water in the eastern Mediterranean originates, the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea), and the [Gulf of Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Lion). Deep water formation in the Mediterranean is triggered by strong winter [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection) fuelled by intense cold winds like [Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_\(wind\)) and [Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(wind\)). When new deep water is formed, the older waters mix with the overlaying intermediate waters and eventually exit the Mediterranean. The [residence time of water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_cycle#Residence_times) in the Mediterranean is approximately 100 years, making the Mediterranean especially sensitive to [climate change](5042951.md) (see [below](#climate_change)).[^cite_note-137]

### Other events affecting water circulation

Being a semi-enclosed basin, the Mediterranean experiences transitory events that can affect the water circulation on short time scales. In the mid-1990s, the Aegean Sea became the main area for deep water formation in the eastern Mediterranean after particularly cold winter conditions. This transitory switch in the origin of deep waters in the eastern Mediterranean was termed Eastern Mediterranean Transient (EMT) and had major consequences on water circulation of the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

Another example of a transient event affecting the Mediterranean circulation is the periodic inversion of the North Ionian Gyre, which is an [anticyclonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticyclonic_rotation) [ocean gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_gyre) observed in the northern part of the [Ionian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Sea), off the Greek coast. The transition from anticyclonic to cyclonic rotation of this gyre changes the origin of the waters fuelling it; when the circulation is anticyclonic (most common), the waters of the gyre originate from the Adriatic Sea. When the circulation is cyclonic, the waters originate from the [Levantine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantine_Sea). These waters have different physical and chemical characteristics, and the periodic inversion of the North Ionian Gyre (called Bimodal Oscillating System or BiOS) changes the Mediterranean circulation and biogeochemistry around the Adriatic and Levantine regions.[^cite_note-141]

### Climate change

Because of the short residence time of its waters, the Mediterranean Sea is considered a hot-spot for climate change records,[^cite_note-Giorgi,_F._2006-142] registering indeed marked increases in temperature across the entire water column since the 1950s.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] According to climate projections, the decrease in precipitation over the region will lead to more evaporation, ultimately increasing marine salinity.[^cite_note-145] As a result of both temperature and salinity increases, the Mediterranean Sea is likely to become more stratified by the end of the 21st century, with notable consequences on water circulation and [biogeochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeochemistry). The stratification and warming have already led the eastern Mediterranean to become a net source of CO<sub>2</sub> to the atmosphere.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

Human-induced climate change appears to play a growing role in the development of [marine heatwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_heatwaves) that have become a prominent subject of research in recent years, particularly in the Mediterranean where a number of areas in both western and eastern sub-basins now experience peaks of temperatures, along with more frequent, more intense, more prolonged warming events than ever seen on record. These events, mainly driven by a combination of oceanic and atmospheric factors, are often triggered by high pressure systems that will reduce cloud cover and increase solar absorption by the sea surface. Their impacts on marine ecosystems, such as mass mortality in benthic communities, coral bleaching events, disruptions in fishery catches and shifts in species distributions, can be devastating.[^cite_note-148] Extreme warming can lead to [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss)[^cite_note-149] and presents an existential threat to some habitats[^cite_note-150] while making conditions more hospitable to invasive tropical species.[^cite_note-151]

## Biogeochemistry

A detailed description of the cycling of marine phytoplankton in the ocean's photic zone. Phytoplankton's role in photosynthesis, oxygen production, and marine food webs highlighted.

Cycling of marine phytoplankton

In spite of its great [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity), concentrations of [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll) and nutrients in the Mediterranean Sea are very low, making it one of the most [oligotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligotrophic) ocean regions in the world. The Mediterranean Sea is commonly referred to as an [LNLC (Low-Nutrient, Low-Chlorophyll) area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-nutrient,_low-chlorophyll_region). The Mediterranean Sea fits the definition of a [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) in which its nutrient contents are low, making it difficult for plants and animals to develop.

There are steep gradients in nutrient concentrations, chlorophyll concentrations and primary productivity in the Mediterranean. Nutrient concentrations in the western part of the basin are about double the concentrations in the eastern basin. The [Alboran Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alboran_Sea), close to the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar), has a daily [primary productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_productivity) of about 0.25 g C (grams of carbon) m<sup>−2</sup> day<sup>−1</sup> whereas the eastern basin has an average daily productivity of 0.16 g C m<sup>−2</sup> day<sup>−1</sup>.[^cite_note-Uitz_Stramski_Gentili_DOrtenzio_2012_p.-152] For this reason, the eastern part of the Mediterranean Sea is termed "ultraoligotrophic". The productive areas of the Mediterranean Sea are few and small. High (i.e. more than 0.5 grams of [Chlorophyll *a*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll_a) per cubic metre) productivity occurs in coastal areas, close to the river mouths which are the primary suppliers of dissolved nutrients. The [Gulf of Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Lion) has a relatively high productivity because it is an area of high vertical mixing, bringing nutrients to the surface waters that can be used by [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton) to produce Chlorophyll *a*.[^cite_note-Bosc,_E._2004-153]

Primary productivity in the Mediterranean is also marked by an intense seasonal variability. In winter, the strong winds and precipitation over the basin generate [vertical mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convective_mixing), bringing nutrients from the deep waters to the surface, where phytoplankton can convert it into [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_\(ecology\)).[^cite_note-154] However, in winter, light may be the limiting factor for primary productivity. Between March and April, spring offers the ideal trade-off between light intensity and nutrient concentrations in surface for a [spring bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_bloom) to occur. In summer, high atmospheric temperatures lead to the warming of the surface waters. The resulting [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) difference virtually isolates the surface waters from the rest of the water column and nutrient exchanges are limited. As a consequence, primary productivity is very low between June and October.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-Bosc,_E._2004-153]

Oceanographic expeditions uncovered a characteristic feature of the Mediterranean Sea biogeochemistry: most of the chlorophyll production does not occur on the surface, but in sub-surface waters between 80 and 200 metres deep.[^cite_note-156] Another key characteristic of the Mediterranean is its high nitrogen-to-phosphorus ratio (N\:P). [Redfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_C._Redfield) demonstrated that most of the world's oceans have an average N\:P ratio around 16. However, the Mediterranean Sea has an average N\:P between 24 and 29, which translates a widespread phosphorus limitation.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160]

Because of its low productivity, plankton assemblages in the Mediterranean Sea are dominated by small organisms such as [picophytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picophytoplankton) and [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria).[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-Uitz_Stramski_Gentili_DOrtenzio_2012_p.-152]

## Geology

See also: [Geology and paleoclimatology of the Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_and_paleoclimatology_of_the_Mediterranean_Basin)



A submarine [karst spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst_spring), called *vrulja*, near [Omiš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omi%C5%A1); observed through several ripplings of an otherwise calm sea surface.

The [geologic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology) of the Mediterranean Sea is complex. Underlain by [oceanic crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_crust), the sea basin was once thought to be a tectonic remnant of the ancient [Tethys Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethys_Ocean); it is now known to be a structurally younger basin, called the [Neotethys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotethys), which was first formed by the convergence of the [African Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Plate) and [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate) during the Late [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic) and Early [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic). Because it is a near-landlocked body of water in a normally dry climate, the Mediterranean is subject to intensive evaporation and the precipitation of [evaporites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporites). The [Messinian salinity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis) started about six million years ago (mya) when the Mediterranean became landlocked, and then essentially dried up. There are salt deposits accumulated on the bottom of the basin of more than a million cubic kilometres—in some places more than three kilometres thick.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

Scientists estimate that the sea was last filled about 5.3 million years ago (mya) in less than two years by the [Zanclean flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanclean_flood). Water poured in from the Atlantic Ocean through a newly breached gateway now called the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) at an estimated rate of about three orders of magnitude (one thousand times) larger than the current flow of the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River).[^cite_note-GCetal2009-164]

The Mediterranean Sea has an average depth of 1,500 m (4,900 ft) and the deepest recorded point is 5,267 m (17,280 ft) in the [Calypso Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_Deep) in the [Ionian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Sea). The coastline extends for 46,000 km (29,000 mi). A shallow submarine ridge (the [Strait of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Sicily)) between the island of [Sicily](27619.md) and the coast of [Tunisia](30188.md) divides the sea in two main subregions: the Western Mediterranean, with an area of about 850,000 km<sup>2</sup> (330,000 sq mi); and the Eastern Mediterranean, of about 1.65 million km<sup>2</sup> (640,000 sq mi). Coastal areas have submarine [karst springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst_spring) or *vrulja*s, which discharge pressurised groundwater into the water from below the surface; the discharge water is usually fresh, and sometimes may be thermal.[^cite_note-LaMoreaux2001-165][^cite_note-166]

### Messinian salinity crisis



Messinian salinity crisis before the [Zanclean flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanclean_flood)

Animation: Messinian salinity crisis

During [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) and [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic) times, as the northwest corner of Africa converged on Iberia, it lifted the Betic-Rif [mountain belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_belt) across southern Iberia and northwest Africa. There the development of the intramontane Betic and Rif basins created two roughly parallel marine gateways between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea. Dubbed the [Betic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betic_corridor) and Rifian corridors, they gradually closed during the middle and late Miocene: perhaps several times.[^cite_note-167] In the late Miocene the closure of the [Betic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betic_Corridor) triggered the so-called "[Messinian salinity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis)" (MSC), characterised by the deposition of a thick evaporitic sequence – with salt deposits up to 2 km thick in the Levantine sea – and by a massive drop in water level in much of the Basin. This event was for long the subject of acute scientific controversy, now much appeased,[^cite_note-168] regarding its sequence, geographic range, processes leading to evaporite facies and salt deposits. The start of the MSC was recently estimated astronomically at 5.96 mya, and it persisted for some 630,000 years until about 5.3 mya;[^cite_note-169] see Animation: Messinian salinity crisis, at right.

After the initial drawdown and re-flooding, there followed more episodes—the total number is debated—of sea drawdowns and re-floodings for the duration of the MSC. It ended when the Atlantic Ocean last re-flooded the basin—creating the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) and causing the [Zanclean flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanclean_flood)—at the end of the Miocene (5.33 mya). Some research has suggested that a desiccation-flooding-desiccation cycle may have repeated several times, which could explain several events of large amounts of salt deposition.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Recent studies, however, show that repeated desiccation and re-flooding is unlikely from a [geodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodynamic) point of view.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-GCV-173]

### Desiccation and exchanges of flora and fauna

See also: [Pleistocene megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_megafauna) and [Dwarf elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_elephant)

The present-day Atlantic gateway, the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar), originated in the early Pliocene via the [Zanclean Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanclean_Flood). As mentioned, there were two earlier gateways: the [Betic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betic_Corridor) across southern Spain and the Rifian Corridor across northern Morocco. The Betic closed about 6 mya, causing the Messinian salinity crisis (MSC); the Rifian or possibly both gateways closed during the earlier [Tortonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortonian) times, causing a "Tortonian salinity crisis" (from 11.6 to 7.2 mya), long before the MSC and lasting much longer. Both "crises" resulted in broad connections between the mainlands of Africa and Europe, which allowed migrations of flora and fauna—especially large mammals including primates—between the two continents. The [Vallesian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallesian) indicates a typical extinction and replacement of mammal species in Europe during Tortonian times following climatic upheaval and overland migrations of new species.[^cite_note-174]

Today, evaporation of surface seawater (output) is more than the supply (input) of fresh water by precipitation and coastal drainage systems, causing the salinity of the Mediterranean to be much higher than that of the Atlantic—so much so that the saltier Mediterranean waters sink below the waters incoming from the Atlantic, causing a two-layer flow across the Strait of Gibraltar: that is, an outflow [*submarine current*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar#Inflow_and_outflow) of warm saline Mediterranean water, counterbalanced by an inflow surface current of less saline cold oceanic water from the Atlantic. In the 1920s, Herman Sörgel proposed the building of a hydroelectric dam (the [Atlantropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantropa) project) across the Straits, using the inflow current to provide a large amount of hydroelectric energy. The underlying energy grid was also intended to support a political union between Europe and, at least, the Maghreb part of Africa (compare [Eurafrika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurafrique) for the later impact and [Desertec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertec) for a later project with some parallels in the planned grid).[^cite_note-175]

## Paleoclimate

Because of its latitude and its landlocked position, the Mediterranean is especially sensitive to astronomically induced climatic variations, which are well documented in its sedimentary record. Since the Mediterranean is subject to the deposition of [eolian dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eolian_dust) from the [Sahara](325363.md) during dry periods, whereas riverine [detrital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detritus_\(geology\)) input prevails during wet ones, the Mediterranean marine [sapropel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapropel)-bearing sequences provide high-resolution climatic information. These data have been employed in reconstructing astronomically calibrated time scales for the last 9 Ma of the Earth's history, helping to constrain the time of past [geomagnetic reversals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_reversal).[^cite_note-176]

## Biodiversity



Soft coral *[Eunicella cavolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunicella_cavolini)*

Unlike the vast multidirectional [ocean currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_current) in open [oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean) within their respective [oceanic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_zone); [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) in the Mediterranean Sea is stable due to the subtle but strong locked nature of currents which is favourable to life, even the smallest macroscopic type of [volcanic life form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_vent). The stable [marine ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ecosystem) of the Mediterranean Sea and [sea temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature) provides a nourishing environment for [life in the deep sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology) to flourish while assuring a balanced [aquatic ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_ecosystem) excluded from any external [deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sea) oceanic factors. It is estimated that there are more than 17,000 [marine species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life) in the Mediterranean Sea with generally higher marine biodiversity in [coastal areas](5236.md), [continental shelves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf), and decreases with depth.[^cite_note-177]

As a result of the drying of the sea during the [Messinian salinity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis),[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-\:4-179][^cite_note-\:5-180] the marine biota of the Mediterranean is derived primarily from the Atlantic Ocean. The North Atlantic is considerably colder and more nutrient-rich than the Mediterranean,[^cite_note-\:5-180][^cite_note-\:4-179] and the marine life of the Mediterranean has had to adapt to its differing conditions in the five million years since the basin was reflooded later.[^cite_note-\:4-179]

The [Alboran Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alboran_Sea) is a transition zone between the two seas, containing a mix of Mediterranean and Atlantic species.[^cite_note-181] The [Mediterranean monk seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_monk_seal) live in the Aegean Sea in Greece.[^cite_note-182]

There was a resident population of [orcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orca) in the Mediterranean until the 1980s, when they went extinct, probably due to long-term PCB exposure. There are still annual sightings of orca vagrants.[^cite_note-183]

See also: [Specially Protected Areas of Mediterranean Importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Protected_Areas_of_Mediterranean_Importance), [List of fishes of the Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fishes_of_the_Mediterranean_Sea), and [List of fishes of the Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fishes_of_the_Black_Sea)

## Environmental issues

For 4,000 years, human activity has transformed most parts of Mediterranean Europe, and the "humanisation of the landscape" overlapped with the appearance of the present Mediterranean climate. The image of a simplistic, environmental determinist notion of a Mediterranean paradise on Earth in antiquity, which was destroyed by later civilisations, dates back to at least the 18th century and was for centuries fashionable in archaeological and historical circles. Based on a broad variety of methods, e.g. historical documents, analysis of trade relations, [floodplain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodplain) sediments, [pollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollen), tree-ring and further archaeometric analyses and population studies, [Alfred Thomas Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Thomas_Grove)'s and [Oliver Rackham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Rackham)'s work on "The Nature of Mediterranean Europe" challenges this common wisdom of a Mediterranean Europe as a "Lost Eden", a formerly fertile and forested region, that had been progressively degraded and desertified by human mismanagement. The belief stems more from the failure of the recent landscape to measure up to the imaginary past of the [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics) as idealised by artists, poets and scientists of the early modern [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment).[^cite_note-Yale-184]



The [thermonuclear bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_bomb) that fell into the sea recovered off [Palomares, Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palomares,_Almer%C3%ADa), 1966

The historical evolution of climate, vegetation and landscape in southern Europe from prehistoric times to the present is much more complex and underwent various changes. For example, some of the deforestation had already taken place before the Roman age. While in the Roman age large enterprises such as the [latifundia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latifundium) took effective care of forests and agriculture, the largest depopulation effects came with the end of the empire. The major deforestation might have taken place in modern times—the later usage patterns were also quite different e.g. in southern and northern Italy. Also, the climate has usually been unstable and there is evidence of various ancient and modern "[Little Ice Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ice_Age)",[^cite_note-185] and plant cover accommodated to various extremes and became resilient to various patterns of human activity.[^cite_note-Yale-184]

Even Grove considered that human activity could be the cause of climate change. Modern science has been able to provide clear evidence of this. The wide ecological diversity typical of Mediterranean Europe is predominantly based on human behaviour, as it is and has been closely related to human usage patterns.[^cite_note-Yale-184] The diversity range was enhanced by the widespread exchange and interaction of the longstanding and highly diverse local agriculture, intense transport and trade relations, and the interaction with settlements, pasture and other land use. The greatest human-induced changes, however, came after [World War II](32927.md), in line with the "1950s syndrome"[^cite_note-186] as rural populations throughout the region abandoned traditional subsistence economies. Grove and Rackham suggest that the locals left the traditional agricultural patterns and instead became scenery-setting agents for tourism. This resulted in more uniform, large-scale formations. Among further current important threats to Mediterranean landscapes are overdevelopment of coastal areas, abandonment of mountains and, as mentioned, the loss of variety via the reduction of traditional agricultural occupations.[^cite_note-Yale-184]

### Natural hazards



[Stromboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromboli) volcano in Italy

The Mediterranean region is one of the most geologically active maritime areas of the globe, sitting on a complex tectonic boundary zone between the European and African plates. The geology of the region, with the presence of plate boundaries and active faults, makes it prone to quite frequent earthquakes, tsunamis and submarine landslides with can have devastating consequences in densely populated coastal areas. In addition climate change now intensifies the frequency and impacts of storm surges and coastal flooding, putting additional human lives and property at risk.[^cite_note-187]

[Earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquakes) are relatively frequent in the Mediterranean Basin, starting in the Mediterranean 70 million years ago. They are still ongoing. From the boundary of tectonic plates, the Mediterranean territory is called two distinct parts: the Eastern Mediterranean (from Italy to Turkey), which is characterised by intense seismicity with earthquakes whose magnitude can rise to 7.5 Richter with more than 350 recorded tsunamis, and the Western Mediterranean. During the 20th century, 198,548 earthquake victims were recorded.[^cite_note-188]

[Volcanic eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_eruptions) are not uncommon either and left their mark in historical and archaeological records. The largest include the [Thera eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thera_eruption), dated around 1600 BC, and the eruptions of [Mount Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius) in 217 BC and AD 79 - the latter famously known for the destruction and the burying of the ancient cities of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) and [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum).[^cite_note-mcguire2003-189] In the same region the [Phlegraean Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlegraean_Fields) west of Naples constitute one of the most significant volcanic systems in the world, still very active. In the same general area, volcanoes like Mt. Etna and Stromboli are considered in a state of permanent activity, with frequent eruptions and lava emissions through the past 1500 years.

[Tsunamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsunamis), usually triggered by earthquakes, volcanic eruptions and submarine landslides, have caused a number of documented disasters in the Mediterranean Basin in the past 2500 years. Historical examples include the 365 and 1303 tsunamis in the Hellenic Arc, more recently the disastrous 1908 event that destroyed the cities of Messina and Reggio Calabria, and the large tsunami that occurred off the Algerian margin in 2003.

On the diplomatic front, the experience of coastal countries and regional authorities is leading to exchange at the international level with the cooperation of NGOs, states, regional and municipal authorities.[^cite_note-evch-190] The [Greek–Turkish earthquake diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek%E2%80%93Turkish_earthquake_diplomacy) is a quite positive example of natural hazards leading to improved relations between traditional rivals in the region after earthquakes in İzmit and Athens in 1999. The European Union Solidarity Fund (EUSF) was set up to respond to major natural disasters and express European solidarity to disaster-stricken regions within all of Europe.[^cite_note-191] The largest amount of funding requests in the EU relates to [forest fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_fire), followed by floods and earthquakes. Forest fires, whether human-made or natural, are a frequent and dangerous hazard in the Mediterranean region.[^cite_note-evch-190] [Tsunamis](31161.md) are also an often-underestimated hazard in the region. For example, the [1908 Messina earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Messina_earthquake) and tsunami took more than 123,000 lives in Sicily and Calabria and were among the deadliest natural disasters in modern Europe.

### Invasive species



The [reticulate whipray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reticulate_whipray) is one of the species that colonised the Eastern Mediterranean through the [Suez Canal](29323.md) as part of the ongoing [Lessepsian migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lessepsian_migration).

Invasive species have become a major component of the Mediterranean ecosystem and have serious impacts on the Mediterranean ecology, endangering a number of local and [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) Mediterranean species. A first look at some groups of marine species shows that over 70% of exotic [decapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapoda)[^cite_note-Briand-192] and some 2/3 of exotic fishes[^cite_note-193] found in the Mediterranean are of [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) origin, introduced from the Red Sea via the Suez Canal. This makes the Canal the first pathway of arrival of [alien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species) species into the Mediterranean. The impacts of some Lessepsian species have proven to be considerable, mainly in the Levantine basin of the Mediterranean, where they are replacing native species and becoming a familiar sight.

According to definitions by the [Mediterranean Science Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Science_Commission) and the [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature), and to [Convention on Biological Diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Biological_Diversity) (CBD) and [Ramsar Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention) terminologies, they are alien species, as they are non-native (non-indigenous) to the Mediterranean Sea, and are found outside their normal, non-adjacent area of distribution. When these species succeed in establishing populations in the Mediterranean Sea, compete with and begin to replace native species they are "Alien Invasive Species", as they are an agent of change and a threat to the native biodiversity. In the context of CBD, "introduction" refers to the movement by human agency, indirect or direct, of an alien species outside of its natural range (past or present). The Suez Canal, being an artificial (human-made) canal, is a human agency. Lessepsian migrants are therefore "introduced" species (indirect, and unintentional). Whatever wording is chosen, they represent a threat to the native Mediterranean biodiversity, because they are non-indigenous to this sea. In recent years, the Egyptian government's announcement of its intentions to deepen and widen the Canal[^cite_note-194] raised concerns from [marine biologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biologist), fearing that such an act will only worsen the invasion of Red Sea species into the Mediterranean, and lead to even more species passing through the Canal.[^cite_note-195]

#### Arrival of new tropical Atlantic species

In recent decades, the arrival of exotic species from the tropical Atlantic has become noticeable. In many cases this reflects an expansion – favoured by a warming trend of sub-tropical Atlantic waters, and also by a fast-growing maritime traffic – of the natural range of species that now enter the Mediterranean through the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar). While not as intense as [Lessepsian migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lessepsian_migration), the process is of importance and is therefore receiving increased levels of scientific coverage.[^cite_note-196]

### Sea-level rise

By 2100 the overall level of the Mediterranean could rise between 3 and 61 cm (1.2 and 24.0 in) as a result of the [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_global_warming).[^cite_note-197] This could have adverse effects on populations across the Mediterranean:

- Rising sea levels will submerge parts of [Malta](19137.md). Rising sea levels will also mean rising salt water levels in Malta's groundwater supply and reduce the availability of drinking water.

  [^cite_note-198]

- A 30 cm (12 in) rise in sea level would flood 200 square kilometres (77 sq mi) of the [Nile Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Delta), displacing over 500,000 [Egyptians](31912046.md).

  [^cite_note-199]

- [Cyprus](5593.md) wetlands are also in danger of being destroyed by the rising temperatures and sea levels.

  [^cite_note-200]

  [^cite_note-201]

Coastal ecosystems also appear to be threatened by [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise), especially enclosed seas such as the [Baltic](3335.md), the Mediterranean and the Black Sea. These seas have only small and primarily east–west movement [corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_corridor), which may restrict northward displacement of organisms in these areas.[^cite_note-202] Sea level rise for the next century (2100) could be between 30 and 100 cm (12 and 39 in) and temperature shifts of a mere 0.05–0.1 °C (0.09–0.18 °F) in the deep sea are sufficient to induce significant changes in species richness and functional diversity.[^cite_note-greenpeace.org-203]

### Pollution

[Pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution) in this region has been extremely high in recent years. The [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) has estimated that 650,000,000 t (720,000,000 short tons) of [sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage), 129,000 t (142,000 short tons) of [mineral oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_oil), 60,000 t (66,000 short tons) of mercury, 3,800 t (4,200 short tons) of lead and 36,000 t (40,000 short tons) of [phosphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) are dumped into the Mediterranean each year.[^cite_note-204] The [Barcelona Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Convention) aims to 'reduce pollution in the Mediterranean Sea and protect and improve the marine environment in the area, thereby contributing to its sustainable development.'[^cite_note-205]

Many marine species have been almost wiped out because of the sea's pollution. One of them is the [Mediterranean monk seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_monk_seal) which is considered to be among the world's most [endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered) [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal).[^cite_note-206]

The Mediterranean is also plagued by [marine debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_debris). A 1994 study of the [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed) using [trawl nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawl_net) around the coasts of Spain, France and Italy reported a particularly high mean concentration of debris; an average of 1,935 items per km<sup>2</sup> (5,010/sq mi).[^cite_note-nep.org/regionalseas/ma-207]

### Tourism



[Kemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemer) Beach in [Antalya](361527.md) on the [Turkish Riviera (Turquoise Coast)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Riviera). In 2019, Turkey [ranked sixth in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_rankings#Most_visited_destinations_by_international_tourist_arrivals) in terms of the number of international tourist arrivals, with 51.2 million foreign tourists visiting the country.

[^cite_note-WTO_Tourism_Highlights_2019_Edition-210]

The coast of the Mediterranean has been used for tourism since ancient times, as the Roman villa buildings on the [Amalfi Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalfi_Coast) or in [Barcola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcola) show. From the end of the 19th century, in particular, the beaches became places of longing for many Europeans and travellers. From then on, and especially after World War II, [mass tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_tourism) to the Mediterranean began with all its advantages and disadvantages. While initially, the journey was by train and later by bus or car, today the plane is increasingly used.[^cite_note-211]

Tourism is a significant source of income for several Mediterranean countries, including small coastal communities and islands independent of urban centres, despite geopolitical conflicts in the region. However, tourism has also played a major role in the [degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation) of the coastal and [marine environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_environment). Rapid development has been encouraged by Mediterranean governments to support the large numbers of tourists visiting the region, but this has caused serious disturbance to [marine habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_habitat) by erosion [^cite_note-212] and [pollution](#Pollution) in many places along the Mediterranean coasts.

Tourism often concentrates in areas of high natural wealth, causing a serious threat to the habitats of endangered species such as [sea turtles](142905.md) and [monk seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monk_seal). Reductions in natural wealth may reduce the incentive for tourists to visit.[^cite_note-greenpeace.org-203]

### Overfishing

Main article: [Overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing)

[Fish stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_stocks) levels in the Mediterranean Sea are alarmingly low.[^cite_note-213] The European Environment Agency says that more than 65% of all fish stocks in the region are outside safe biological limits. Some of the most important fisheries are threatened. There are clear indications that catch size and quality have declined, often dramatically, and in many areas, larger and longer-lived species have disappeared entirely from commercial catches.[^cite_note-214]

### Marine heatwaves

Main article: [Marine heatwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_heatwave)

A study showed that [climate change-related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_oceans) exceptional [marine heatwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_heatwave) in the Mediterranean Sea during 2015–2019 resulted in widespread mass sealife die-offs in five consecutive years.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216]
