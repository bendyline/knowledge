A **minibus**, **microbus**, **minicoach**, or **short bus** is a passenger-carrying [motor vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle) that is designed to carry more people than a [multi-purpose vehicle or minivan](20828.md), but fewer people than a [full-size bus](4146.md). In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the word "minibus" is used to describe any full-sized passenger-carrying [van](63679.md) or [panel truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panel_truck). Minibuses have a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of between 12 and 30. Larger minibuses may be called [midibuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midibus). Minibuses are usually front-engine vehicles with step-in access, though low-floor designs are especially common in Japan.[^cite_note-1]

## History

It is unknown when the first minibus vehicle was developed. For example, [Ford Model T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_T) vehicles were modified for passenger transport by early bus companies and entrepreneurs.[^cite_note-2] Ford produced a version during the 1920s to carry up to twelve people.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

In the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the production of minibuses began in the mid-1950s, among the first mass-produced minibuses were the RAF-10, [UAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAZ)-451B, and Start.[^cite_note-6] Since September 1961, the RAF-977D "Latvia" minibus began to be mass-produced.[^cite_note-7]

## Driving licence

Some countries may require an additional class of [driving licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_licence) over a normal private car licence, and some may require a full [commercial driving licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_driving_licence). The need for such a licence may depend on:

- Vehicle weight or size

- Seating capacity

- Driver age

- Intended usage

- Additional training (such as the [Minibus Driver Awareness Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minibus_Driver_Awareness_Scheme) in the UK)

### France

In France, if the vehicle has more than 9 seats, a D permit is required. If there are between 9 and 17 seats, the driver must have a D1 permit.[^cite_note-12]

### United Kingdom

In the UK, the holder of an ordinary car driving licence which was obtained prior to January 1997, once aged 21 years minimum, may drive a **Minibus** with a capacity of 16 passengers. Where the "ordinary car driving licence" is obtained after December 1996, they will have to take a separate test to drive a vehicle with a capacity of more than 8 passengers. However, there is an exemption for certain volunteer drivers, where the vehicle does not exceed 3500 kg GVW (or 4250 kg GVW if the vehicle is designed to be wheelchair accessible).[^cite_note-ROSPA-guidelines-13][^cite_note-GOV-regulations-14]

### United States

In the United States, if the vehicle has 15 or fewer passengers, no special license is required. If there are 16 or more passengers (including the driver), a commercial driver's license is required (the specific type varies by state).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Ontario, Canada

A driving licence issued in [Ontario](22218.md), Canada, for an equivalent of a [UK class B or class B-auto driving licence (in the case of Ontario, a class G licence)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uk_driving_license), allows its holder to drive vehicles with:

- [11 tonnes maximum authorized mass, including trailers with 4.6 tonnes MAM](http://www.mto.gov.on.ca/english/dandv/driver/classes.shtml)

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;6 tonnes MAM in certain cases)

- passenger seating capacity of [9 or less](https://web.archive.org/web/20131203003448/http://www.drivetest.ca/EN/licencing/Pages/Licencing-Requirements.aspx)

Anyone wanting to drive a vehicle in Ontario, with the same MAM limits as for class G vehicles, with fewer than 25, but at least 10, passenger seats, must obtain a short bus licence (Class F in Ontario). This will allow, for example, its holder to drive 12- and 15-passenger vans] that [Transport Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Canada) defines as large passenger vans. A separate class of driving license (Class C) is required for full-size school buses and school buses.[^cite_note-19]
