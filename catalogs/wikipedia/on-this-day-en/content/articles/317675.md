**Engelberg** (lit.: *mountain of angel(s)*) is a village resort and a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Switzerland) in the [canton](180752.md) of [Obwalden](317636.md) in [Switzerland](26748.md). Alongside the central village of Engelberg, the municipality encompasses additional settlements, including **Grafenort**, **Oberberg** and **Schwand**.

The municipality of Engelberg is an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave), entirely encircled by the neighboring cantons of [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern), [Nidwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Nidwalden) and [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri).

Engelberg is a mountain resort in [Central Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Switzerland). In the Middle Ages, the area garnered recognition for its Benedictine monastery, known as [Engelberg Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberg_Abbey). As time progressed, particularly from the 19th century onwards, Engelberg became a well-known mountain resort. The city of [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucerne) serves as the nearest major urban center.

While the official language of Engelberg is [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Language), the predominant spoken language is the local variation of the [Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German_\(linguistics\)) dialect.

## History

Engelberg's earliest mention dates to 1122, denoted as Engilperc, concurrent with the founding of the Abbey. Prior to this, the [Alpine pasture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_pasture) of [Trüebsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%BCebsee) was already collectively utilized by the locals.[^cite_note-HDS-2]

In August 1815, as [Nidwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Nidwalden) hesitated in accepting the [Federal Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Treaty), Engelberg promptly expressed its support. Federal troops entered Nidwalden, ultimately prompting their acceptance of the treaty on 18 August 1815. Consequently, Engelberg was incorporated into the [canton of Obwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Obwalden).[^cite_note-HDS_NW-3]

The mid-19th century ushered in Engelberg's emergence as a vacation destination, known for its therapeutic mineral water, milk serum and fresh air. The efforts of families such as Cattani, Hess, and Odermatt led to the establishment of a number of hotels, cementing the region's status as a tourist destination. Related developments included the construction of a broader road between 1872 and 1874, as well as the inauguration of the [Stansstad-Engelberg electric railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzern-Stans-Engelberg-Bahn) in 1898.[^cite_note-HDS-2]

Engelberg's reputation as a hub for hiking and other mountain activities grew toward the end of the 19th century. The winter season made its debut in 1903-1904. The [Gerschnialpbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerschnialpbahn), a funicular railway (unveiled in 1913), connected Engelberg to [Gerschni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerschni?action=edit\&redlink=1) with a subsequent cable car (inaugurated in 1927) extending the journey to Ober Trüebsee. The pre-World War I era was characterized by robust growth, as indicated by a substantial influx of visitors (165,922 visitor-nights in 1911). The expansion of roadways and the extension of the railway to Lucerne (achieved in 1964) enhanced the accessibility of Engelberg. Further advancements were marked by the opening of the higher segment of the Titlis cable car in 1967. Over time, Engelberg's appeal extended beyond tourism, hosting regular conferences. By the turn of the millennium, the tertiary sector, particularly tourism, contributed significantly to employment in Engelberg.[^cite_note-HDS-2]

## Geography



Aerial view from 1000 m by [Walter Mittelholzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mittelholzer) (1919)

Engelberg is situated within the [Uri Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Alps) mountain range.

Engelberg is surrounded by several mountain summits, such as [Titlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titlis) in the south (3,238 metres (10,623 ft)) above sea level), the [Walenstöcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walenst%C3%B6cke) (2,572 metres (8,438 ft)) and [Ruchstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruchstock) (2,813 metres (9,229 ft)) to the north, [Hahnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahnen) (2,606 metres (8,550 ft)) and [Wissberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wissberg) (2,627 metres (8,619 ft)) to the east, the [Engelberger Rotstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberger_Rotstock) (2,819 metres (9,249 ft)) and the [Wissigstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wissigstock) (2,887 metres (9,472 ft)) to the northeast, and the upper valley of the Engelberger Aa leading to the [Surenen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surenen_Pass) (2,291 metres (7,516 ft)) leading to the [Urner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) [Reusstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuss_\(river\)).[^cite_note-SwissTopo-4]

Engelberg has an area (as of the 2004/09 survey) of 74.87 km<sup>2</sup> (28.91 sq mi).[^cite_note-Arealstatistik-5] Of this area, about 27.1% is used for agricultural purposes, while 25.8% is forested. Of the rest of the land, 3.7% is settled (buildings or roads) and 43.5% is unproductive land. In the 2013/18 survey a total of 146 ha (360 acres) or about 1.9% of the total area was covered with buildings, an increase of 35 ha (86 acres) over the 1980/81 amount. Over the same time period, the amount of recreational space in the municipality increased by 34 ha (84 acres) and is now about 0.61% of the total area. Of the agricultural land, 685 ha (1,690 acres) is fields and grasslands and 1,424 ha (3,520 acres) consists of alpine grazing areas. Since 1980/81 the amount of agricultural land has decreased by 156 ha (390 acres). Over the same time period the amount of forested land has increased by 103 ha (250 acres). Rivers and lakes cover 78 ha (190 acres) in the municipality.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-SFOS2018-7]

The average altitude of Engelberg is 1,020 m (3,350 ft). However the village is surrounded by the Alps, creating very steep terrain. The highest point in the borders of the municipality is the [Titlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titlis). The Engelberg Valley ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Engelbergertal*) is drained by the [Engelberger Aa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberger_Aa), a tributary of [Lake Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lucerne). The valley is located southwards from the lake.[^cite_note-SwissTopo-4]

## Transport

The municipality of Engelberg is served by two stations on the [Luzern–Stans–Engelberg line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzern%E2%80%93Stans%E2%80%93Engelberg_railway_line). [Engelberg railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberg_railway_station) is located in the village and is the terminus of the line. [Grafenort station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafenort_railway_station) is to the north, one station away. Both stations are served by hourly [InterRegio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterRegio) trains from the city of [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucerne).[^cite_note-8]

A free bus system provides daytime transport within the village, with a network of seven routes during the winter season (from December through to April) and a single route during the summer season (from April until October).[^cite_note-9]

[Drahtseilbahn Engelberg–Hotel Terrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drahtseilbahn_Engelberg%E2%80%93Hotel_Terrasse), a funicular from the village to [Hotel Terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Terrace_\(Engelberg\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) operated from 1905 to 2008, with some interruptions. [Gerschnialpbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerschnialpbahn) opened in 1913.

## Tourism

The tourist activities in the village and surrounding area include skiing and other snow sports in the winter season, and hiking and mountain activities during the summer.

In the village itself the main sights are the Benedictine monastery [Engelberg Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberg_Abbey) which incorporates a cheese factory and demonstration shop, the Talmuseum showing the history of the area and Swiss rural life, and a number of old chapels.

The winter sports season generally lasts from December until April, although the high altitude glacier areas on the Titlis can sometimes be used (by advanced skiers) from October until May.[^cite_note-10] Snow coverage is generally reliable, although in recent years [artificial snow machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmaking) have been installed on some of the lower altitude runs in order to improve snow cover.



[Gross-Titlis-Schanze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross-Titlis-Schanze)

Engelberg hosts a round of the [ski jumping World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_jumping_World_Cup) at the [Gross-Titlis-Schanze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross-Titlis-Schanze) jump.



1 August parade in Engelberg

In common with the rest of Switzerland, there is a village celebration for [Swiss National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Day) on 1 August, with parades and events throughout the day.

Der Alpabzug

On the last Saturday in September the *Alpabzug* takes place, when the [cattle are brought from the mountain pastures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_transhumance) back to their winter barns in the village and valley.

There are three main mountain areas, accessible from the village, offering various activities in winter and summer. The cable cars generally run all year round, providing access for hikers and mountain bikers as well as skiers.

### Titlis

The [Titlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titlis) in the south of Engelberg at 3,238 metres (10,623 ft) above sea level is the highest summit of the range north of the [Susten Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susten_Pass), between the [Bernese Oberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Oberland) and [Central Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Switzerland).

The Titlis mountain massif is accessible by cable cars of the [Titlis Bergbahnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titlis_Bergbahnen?action=edit\&redlink=1). The cable car bottom station is also the central terminus of the village bus services. A funicular railway (dating from 1913) runs up to station *Gerschnialp* (1,267 m (4,157 ft)) and a wide Alpine pasture called Gerschni, with easy snow areas suitable for beginners and cross country ski trails, and a toboggan run leading back down to the valley station. In the summer there are two cheese dairies, with walking trails leading up to Ober Trüebsee and back down to the village, or level trails leading to Unter Trüebsee to the west.

The "Titlis Xpress" gondola lift, opened in 2015 to replace an older one dating from the 1970s, runs from the valley station (996 m (3,268 ft)) up to the middle station *Trübsee* (1,788 m (5,866 ft)) and on to *Stand* (2,428 m (7,966 ft)). This area provides more challenging skiing, on the lower slopes of the Titlis and via further chair lifts to the [Jochpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochpass) (2,207 m (7,241 ft)) and below the [Jochstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochstock) at 2,508 m (8,228 ft). A continuous ski piste leads down to Unter Trüebsee and back to the cable car valley station. In summer the lake is a destination for walkers, with rowing boats available on the lake and picnic places around it. Walking routes lead over the Jochpass to [Engstlenalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engstlenalp?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Melchsee-Frutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchsee-Frutt), or directly from Engleberg over the [Juchli Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juchli_Pass?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2,171 m (7,123 ft)) or [Storegg Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storegg_Pass?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1,742 m (5,715 ft))) into the [Melchtal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchtal).

The "Rotair" cable car ("the world's first rotating cable car") runs up to the *Kleintitlis* mountain station (3,028 m (9,934 ft)) where there is a restaurant and shops, an observation terrace and access to the glacier and summit. The high altitude glacier runs down from the peak are suitable for advanced skiers, with off-piste routes leading down to *Trüebsee* and the *Laubersgrat* ridge.

### Brunni

The [Brunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunni) mountain area, to the north of the village, is accessible from the cable car station which runs up to *Ristis* (1,600 m (5,200 ft)), with a further chair lift up to *Brunnihütte* (1,860 m (6,100 ft)). The ski runs here are of a beginner to medium standard, although sometimes not having so much snow cover as the Titlis side due to the south facing aspect. There is another toboggan run from *Brunnihütte* back down to *Ristis*. In summer there are a number of walking trails starting from here, including the *Walenpfad* leading to [Bannalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannalp) and the *Rot Grätli* ridge across the mountains to the north and northeast. There are also a number of prepared rock climbing routes ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *[Klettersteig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klettersteig)*).

### Fürenalp

At the eastern end of the Engelberg valley, there is a cable car up to [Fürenalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCrenalp?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1,840 m (6,040 ft)), passing over the *Fürenwand* rock climbing area. From the top station or from the valley, summer walking routes with views of the [Chli Spannort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chli_Spannort) (3,140 m (10,300 ft)) and [Gross Spannort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_Spannort) (3,198 m (10,492 ft)) mountain peaks lead to the [Surenenpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surenenpass) (2,291 m (7,516 ft)) in the east.

### Heritage sites

Engelberg is home to three sites that are Swiss [heritage sites of national significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_inventory_of_cultural_property_of_national_and_regional_significance); [Engelberg Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberg_Abbey) with its library, archives and music collection, the mansion (Herrenhaus) in Grafenort and the Holy Cross chapel in Grafenort.[^cite_note-11]

Engelberg has a population (as of December 2020) of 4,194.[^cite_note-Stat2020_OW-13] As of 2016, 26.2% of the population are resident foreign nationals. In 2015 a small minority (259 or 6.3% of the population) was born in Germany.[^cite_note-14] Over the last 6 years (2010-2016) the population has changed at a rate of 5.92%. The [birth rate](515898.md) in the municipality, in 2016, was 8.3, while the [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) was 5.8 per thousand residents.[^cite_note-SFOS2018-7]

As of 2016, children and teenagers (0–19 years old) make up 17.7% of the population, while adults (20–64 years old) are 61.4% of the population and seniors (over 64 years old) make up 20.9%.[^cite_note-SFOS2018-7] In 2015 there were 1,762 single residents, 1,808 people who were married or in a civil partnership, 230 widows or widowers and 297 divorced residents.[^cite_note-15]

In 2016 there were 1,925 private households in Engelberg with an average household size of 2.10 persons. In 2015 about 43% of all buildings in the municipality were single family homes, which is about the same as the percentage in the canton (44.4%) and less than the percentage nationally (57.4%).[^cite_note-16] Of the 1,302 inhabited buildings in the municipality, in 2000, about 48.5% were single family homes and 34.4% were multiple family buildings. Additionally, about 17.1% of the buildings were built before 1919, while 9.4% were built between 1991 and 2000.[^cite_note-Housing-17] In 2015 the rate of construction of new housing units per 1000 residents was 8.3. The vacancy rate for the municipality, in 2017, was 0.92%.[^cite_note-SFOS2018-7]

Most of the population (as of 2000) speaks German as their mother tongue (88.2%), with Serbo-Croatian being second most common (2.5%) and English being third (2.2%).[^cite_note-SFSO-18] As of 2000 the gender distribution of the population was 49.9% male and 50.1% female.

## Education

In Engelberg about 65.5% of the population (between age 25–64) have completed either non-mandatory [upper secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Switzerland#Secondary) or additional higher education (either university or a *[Fachhochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule)*).[^cite_note-SFSO-18]

## Business and industry

Engelberg is classed as a tourist community.[^cite_note-20]

As of  2014, there were a total of 2,547 people employed in the municipality. Of these, a total of 143 people worked in 56 businesses in the [primary economic sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy). The [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) employed 267 workers in 54 separate businesses, of which 6 businesses employed a total of 117 employees. Finally, the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) provided 2,137 jobs in 372 businesses.[^cite_note-21]

In 2016 a total of 8.3% of the population received social assistance.[^cite_note-SFOS2018-7] In 2011 the unemployment rate in the municipality was 1.1%.[^cite_note-22]

In 2015 local hotels had a total of 354,960 overnight stays, of which 67.1% were international visitors.[^cite_note-23]

In 2015 the average cantonal, municipal and church tax rate in the municipality for a couple with two children making [SFr](361362.md) 80,000 was 5.5% while the rate for a single person making SFr 150,000 was 11.1%, both of which are much lower than the average for the canton. The canton has a slightly higher than average tax rate for those making SFr 80,000 and one of the lowest for those making SFr 150,000. In 2013 the average income in the municipality per tax payer was SFr 88,070 and the per person average was SFr 45,328, which is greater than the cantonal averages of SFr 87,563 and SFr 38,842 respectively It is also greater than the national per tax payer average of SFr 82,682 and the per person average of SFr 35,825.[^cite_note-24]

Due to the risks of filming in the disputed region of [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir), many [Indian films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_India) requiring a Kashmir snowy mountain setting have been filmed in Engelberg in the recent past.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Climate

Between 1991 and 2020 Engelberg had an average of 151 days of rain or snow per year and on average received 1,568 mm (61.7 in) of [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)). The wettest month was July with 198 mm (7.8 in) of precipitation over 15.7 days. However June had the most precipitation days (15.9) but only 179 mm (7.0 in). The driest months of the year was February with 81 mm (3.2 in) of precipitation over 10.0 days.[^cite_note-MeteoSwiss-27]

This area has a long winter season, with little precipitation mostly in the form of snow, and low humidity. The [Köppen Climate System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_Climate_Classification) classifies the climate in Engelberg as Oceanic.[^cite_note-28] [MeteoSwiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeteoSwiss)'s classification is *Central Alpine northslope*.[^cite_note-MeteoSwiss-27]
