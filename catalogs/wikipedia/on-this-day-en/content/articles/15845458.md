The **China Coast Guard** (**CCG**; 中国海警局) is the [maritime security](8111405.md), [search and rescue](172599.md), and [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_law_enforcement) [service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [People's Armed Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Police) (PAP) of [China](5405.md). The [emergency number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_number) of the Coast Guard is 95110, which began operation in 2019.[^cite_note-2]

Between 2013 and 2023, the CCG confiscated a total of 9.875 tonnes of drugs, 21 billion [RMB](66958.md) worth of smuggled goods and 12 million tonnes of [stolen sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_theft), along with responding to over 53,000 emergency calls and handling 24,000 cases.[^cite_note-\:6-3]

## History

Prior to 2013, five [Chinese departments had maritime law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_law_enforcement_agencies_in_China) responsibilities leading to "waste, inefficiency, and disarray".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinson20151–2-4] In March 2013, the [National People's Congress](38878740.md) (NPC) authorized the creation of the CCG under the [State Oceanic Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Oceanic_Administration) (SOA). The CCG combined the SOA's [China Marine Surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Marine_Surveillance) (CMS), the [Ministry of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_\(China\))'s [China Fishery Law Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Fishery_Law_Enforcement?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CFLE), the [Ministry of Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_\(China\))'s (MPS)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinson20152–3-5] [People's Armed Police Border Defense Corps Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Defense_Corps_Coast_Guard_\(China\)?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinson20152–3-5][^cite_note-6] and the [General Directorate of Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Directorate_of_Customs_Anti-Smuggling_Bureau?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s Maritime elements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinson20152–3-5] Locally controlled and funded CMS and CFLE units remained separate.[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7] The [Ministry of Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_\(China\))'s [Maritime Safety Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Safety_Administration) (MSA) and [search and rescue](172599.md) organizations were not merged, possibly to reduce the difficulty of the reorganization or to allow the MSA to be continue to be differentiated from the more aggressive CCG for foreign relations purposes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinson201527–29-8] The CCG was officially created on 22 July 2013.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinson20153-9]

The 2013 CCG did not become an integrated service and the intended synergies failed to materialize. The founding organizations retained separate identities, missions, and cultures.[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7] The chain of command was unclear. Although technically subordinate to the SOA, the MPS had the authority to give "operational guidance" and [Meng Hongwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Hongwei), a vice-minister of the MPS, was appointed as head of the CCG. The MPS also staffed the nominally civilian CCG with personnel from its own paramilitary PAP.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiura2022289-10] By April 2018, the CCG was unable to resolve these problems.[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7]

The CCG was reorganized in 2018 to be a part of the Chinese armed forces. The PAP was subordinated only to the [Central Military Commission](214345.md) and received control of the CCG. By 2019, the CCG was commanded by Rear Admiral [Wang Zhongcai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhongcai) of the [People's Liberation Army Navy](24918.md) (PLAN). The SOA was abolished[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiura2022289-10] with most of its civilian researchers and administrators transferring to the [Ministry of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Natural_Resources_\(China\)), which "renounced any role in maritime law enforcement". Local CMS and CFLE units remained separate.[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7] On 22 June, the NPC authorized the transfer of the CCG to the PAP on 1 July;[^cite_note-11] The CCG's missions were unchanged by the reforms.[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7]

Legal reform followed the 2018 reorganization. When the CCG was created it continued to operate under the pre-2013 legal framework used by the separate organizations. In 2020, the CCG's role in maritime criminal cases was clarified.[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7] In January 2021, the NPC passed a law standardizing CCG operations and clarifying the authorization for the use of force;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiura2022290-12] the law permitted the use of lethal force to enforce Chinese territorial claims.[^cite_note-Law-13][^cite_note-14]

In June 2024, the CCG was authorized to detain foreign vessels and persons for up to 60 days.[^cite_note-\:1-15]

## Functions

The CCG duty is to perform regular patrols and reactive actions (such as Search and Rescue) on the coastal, near sea, and open ocean areas of its jurisdiction (and international waters). These actions include principally law enforcement tasks such as interdicting smuggling, illegal fisheries control, and protecting the environment (such as stopping [coral fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef_protection) and pollutant dumping).[^cite_note-16]

The CCG also serves as an armed border guard, protecting China's claimed maritime borders, which often leads to conflict and controversy. As a constituent part of the Chinese Armed Forces (being subordinate to the [PAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Police)), on wartime it would be placed under the operational control of the [People's Liberation Army Navy](24918.md), in which case it would be likely to play support roles and rear-area escort (like its [USCG](32223.md) counterparts, which is also a branch of the military, its ships are not equipped for full military combat).

### Law enforcement

The first set of duties of the CCG according to the China Coast Guard Law include seven law enforcement tasks:

1. fighting maritime violations and crimes,

   [^cite_note-17]

2. preserving maritime safety and security,

3. development and utilization of marine resources,

4. marine ecological and environmental protection,

   [^cite_note-18]

5. management of marine fishery resources,

   [^cite_note-19]

6. carrying out anti-smuggling tasks on the sea,

7. coordinating and guiding local maritime law enforcement.

### Maritime safety

Another set of responsibilities come from [maritime safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_safety). While maritime safety, [SAR](172599.md), and the enforcement of the rules of marine safety is the main remit of the [China Maritime Safety Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Maritime_Safety_Administration), and the leading organ in active SAR is the [China Rescue and Salvage Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Rescue_and_Salvage_Bureau), the CCG, as the main maritime law enforcement agency, is involved very often in rescue operations.[^cite_note-20] It also supports the CMSA in enforcing maritime safety rules and inspect ships suspected of presenting risks to navigation.

### International cooperation

International cooperation and coordination is one of the official tasks of the CCG. Part of this is cooperation with friendly nations for mutually beneficial tasks (such as cooperating with Russia in fishery operations, as part of the plan for the opening and operation of an Arctic passage).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] More critical is cooperation with neighboring states on matters of mutual interest, in particular fisheries and smuggling. The frequency of that cooperation often correlates with the state of bilateral relationships, but institutional connections do remain continuously active.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

In the 2000s and early 2010s, the Chinese Coast Guard (Before 2013, the Maritime Police and China Marine Surveillance) conducted periodic joint-training sessions with other navies in the North Pacific, including the US Coast Guard service.[^cite_note-25] The Chinese Coast Guard has also participated in the annual [North Pacific Coast Guard Agencies Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_Coast_Guard_Agencies_Forum) in Alaska, along with the US, Canadian, Japanese, South Korean, and Russian Coast Guards. As part of an exchange program, around 109 members of the Chinese Coast Guard service have served on [U.S. Coast Guard](32223.md) cutters.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]



Badge of the [People's Armed Police Border Defense Corps Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Defense_Corps_Coast_Guard_\(China\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)

The worsening of US-China relationships in the last few years (as of 2024), in particular the ongoing [conflict regarding the South China Sea](32005536.md) (in which the CCG is directly involved) have all but ended the co-training missions with the USCG, although the purely civilian [CMSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Maritime_Safety_Administration) still keeps a very close working relationship with its counterparts in the US and Japan.

### Territorial sovereignty and rights protection

As China's [claims of sovereign waters are extensive and overlap with several other countries](32005536.md), enforcing this doctrine has created a very large number of incidents and controversies involving the CCG.[^cite_note-\:2-28][^cite_note-\:1-15] These often escalate to skirmishes and tense brinkmanship in what has been called [grey-zone operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-zone_operations).[^cite_note-29] The CCG is at the forefront of these incidents (often alongside the [People's Armed Forces Maritime Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Forces_Maritime_Militia)).[^cite_note-30] The probable reason for that usage, according to international analysts[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] is that putting the paramilitary "White Hulls" (the CCG) and the "Blue Hulls" (the PAFMM) at the forefront avoids the dangerous escalation that would happen if the unambiguously military "Gray Hulls" (the [PLAN](24918.md)) were involved in an incident.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

The CCG is very active in patrolling those rights.[^cite_note-35] The result is a significant number of incidents of varying levels of tension.[^cite_note-\:2-28] In 2019, the United States issued a warning to China over aggressive and unsafe action by their Coast Guard and maritime militia.[^cite_note-36] In 2023, the Coast Guard used [water cannons](431462.md) on Philippine coast guard ships in contested waters.[^cite_note-37] In 2024, the PAFMM and CCG entered into a tense standoff with the Philippines over the [Second Thomas Shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Thomas_Shoal).[^cite_note-\:1-15][^cite_note-38] In 2026, Chinese researchers have proposed using drones in the air, on the water and underwater to defend islands and reefs in the South China Sea. The CCG Academy and [Dalian Maritime University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian_Maritime_University), would use these unmanned systems to watch the area and respond to threats, reducing the need for troops.[^cite_note-39]

## Organization

Following the 2018 reforms, the CCG hierarchy - from senior to junior - are the CCG Bureau in Beijing, the regional branch bureaus ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 海区分局; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *haiqu fenju*), the provincial-level bureaus ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 省级海警局; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *shengji haijing ju*), the municipal-level bureaus ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 市级海警局; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *shiji haijing ju*) and work stations ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 工作站; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *gongzuo zhan*).[^cite_note-jts_martinson_2021-7]

## Cooperation with other agencies

The CCG has direct intel sharing with the [General Administration of Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Administration_of_Customs).[^cite_note-pd_ccg_2013-40]

### International cooperation

The CCG has conducted joint patrols with the [Vietnam Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Coast_Guard).[^cite_note-53]
