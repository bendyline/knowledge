The **Dniester** ([/ˈniːstər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*NEE-stər*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-Merriam-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-7] is a [transboundary river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transboundary_river) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). It runs first through [Ukraine](31750.md) and then through [Moldova](19260.md) (from which it more or less separates the breakaway territory of [Transnistria](376581.md)), finally discharging into the [Black Sea](3386.md) on Ukrainian territory again.

## Names

The name *Dniester* derives from [Sarmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatian_language) *dānu nazdya* "the close river".[^cite_note-8] (The [Dnieper](380138.md), also of Sarmatian origin, derives from the opposite meaning, "the river on the far side".) Alternatively, according to [Vasily Abaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Abaev) *Dniester* would be a blend of [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian_language) *dānu* "river" and [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_language) *Ister*, the previous name of the river, literally Dān-Ister (River Ister).[^cite_note-9] The [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) name of Dniester, *Tyras* (Τύρας), is from [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian_language) *tūra*, meaning "rapid".

The names of the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)) and [Danube](21209639.md) are also from the same [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_languages) word *\*dānu* "river". Classical authors have also referred to it as *Danaster.* These early forms, without -*i*- but with -*a*-, contradict Abaev's hypothesis. [Edward Gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Gibbon) refers to the river both as the Niester and Dniester in his *[History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Decline_and_Fall_of_the_Roman_Empire)*.[^cite_note-10]

In [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), it is known as Дністе́р ([translit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian) *Dnister*), in [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language) as *Nistru*, in [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) as Днестр ([translit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian) *Dnestr*), in [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language) as Днестър ([translit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Bulgarian) *Dnestar*), in [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) as *Dniestr*, in [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language) as *Nester* נעסטער; in [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) as *Turla* ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): طورلا ، طورله), and in [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language) as *Dniestras*.

## Geography

The Dniester rises in [Ukraine](31750.md), near the city of [Turka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turka,_Ukraine), close to the border with Poland, and flows toward the [Black Sea](3386.md). Its course marks part of the border of Ukraine and [Moldova](19260.md), after which it flows through Moldova for 398 kilometres (247 mi), separating the main territory of Moldova from its breakaway region [Transnistria](376581.md). It later forms an additional part of the Moldova-Ukraine border, then flows through Ukraine to the Black Sea, where its [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) forms the [Dniester Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniester_Liman).

Along the lower half of the Dniester, the western bank is high and hilly while the eastern one is low and flat. The river represents the *de facto* end of the [Eurasian Steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe). Its most important tributaries are [Răut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%83ut) and [Bîc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%AEc).

## History

During the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), the Dniester River was the centre of one of the most advanced civilizations on earth at the time. The [Cucuteni–Trypillian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucuteni%E2%80%93Trypillian_culture) flourished in this area from roughly 5300 to 2600 BC, leaving behind thousands of archeological sites. Their settlements had up to 15,000 inhabitants, making them among the first large farming communities in the world.[^cite_note-11]

In antiquity, the river was considered one of the principal rivers of European [Sarmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatia), and it was mentioned by many Classical geographers and historians. According to [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) (iv.51) it rose in a large lake, whilst [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) (iii.5.17, 8.1 \&c.) places its sources in Mount Carpates (the modern [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains)), and [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) (ii) says that they are unknown. It ran in an easterly direction parallel with the Ister (lower [Danube](21209639.md)), and formed part of the boundary between [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia) and Sarmatia. It fell into the [Pontus Euxinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontus_Euxinus) to the northeast of the mouth of the Ister, the distance between them being 900 stadia – approximately 210 km (130 mi) – according to Strabo (vii.), while 210 km (130 mi) (from the *[Pseudostoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudostoma?action=edit\&redlink=1)*) according to [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) (iv. 12. s. 26). [Scymnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scymnus_of_Chios) (Fr. 51) describes it as of easy navigation, and abounding in fish. [Ovid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid) (*ex Pont.* iv.10.50) speaks of its rapid course.

Greek authors referred to the river as *Tyras* ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): ὁ Τύρας).[^cite_note-12] At a later period it obtained the name of *Danastris* or *Danastus*,[^cite_note-13] whence its modern name of Dniester (Niester), though the Turks still called it *Turla* during the 19th century.[^cite_note-14] The form Τύρις is sometimes found.[^cite_note-15]

It formed the northeastern border of the [Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire) in the 7th-10th centuries.[^cite_note-16] According to [Constantine VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_VII), the [Varangians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangians) used boats on their [trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks), along Dniester and Dnieper and along the Black Sea shore. The navigation near the western shore of Black Sea contained stops at Aspron (at the mouth of Dniester), then Conopa, Constantia (localities today in [Romania](25445.md)) and [Messembria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesebar) (today in [Bulgaria](3415.md)).

From the 14th century to 1812, part of the Dniester formed the eastern boundary of the [Principality of Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia).

Between the World Wars, the Dniester formed part of the boundary between Romania and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In 1919, on [Easter Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Sunday), the bridge was blown up by the [French Army](611740.md) to protect [Bender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bender,_Moldova) from the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik).[^cite_note-Kaba-17] During World War II, German and Romanian forces battled Soviet troops on the western bank of the river.

After the [Republic of Moldova](19260.md) declared its independence in 1991, the small area to the east of the Dniester that had been part of the [Moldavian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) refused to participate and declared itself the Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic, or [Transnistria](376581.md), with its capital at [Tiraspol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiraspol) on the river.

In Moldova, the Dniester Day ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Ziua Nistrului*) is celebrated every year in the last Sunday of May.[^cite_note-18]

## Eco-TIRAS International Environmental Association of River Keepers

**Eco-TIRAS International Environmental Association of River Keepers** was established in 1999 in [Moldova](19260.md). It was founded by environmental [NGOs](46539.md) from Moldova and [Ukraine](31750.md), specifically those operating within the Dniester River basin. The organization's primary goal is to promote the sustainable management of the Dniester River's water resources through the Integrated River Basin Management approach. Eco-TIRAS was registered with the [Ministry of Justice of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Moldova\)) on 14 January 2000. Its founder and executive director is ecologist [Ilia Trombițchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilia_Trombi%C8%9Bchi?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]
