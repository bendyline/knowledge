**Ulm** (German pronunciation: [\[ʊlm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/55/De-Ulm.ogg/De-Ulm.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/55/De-Ulm.ogg/De-Ulm.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the sixth-largest city of the southwestern German [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md), and with around 130,000 inhabitants, it is Germany's [60th-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population).

Ulm is located on the eastern edges of the [Swabian Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Jura) mountain range, on the upper course of the [River Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Danube), at the confluence with the small [Blau Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blau_\(Danube\)), coming from the [Blautopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blautopf) in the west. The mouth of the [Iller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iller) also falls within Ulm's city limits. The Danube forms the border with [Bavaria](3764.md), where Ulm's twin city [Neu-Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu-Ulm) lies. The city was part of Ulm until 1810, and Ulm and Neu-Ulm have a combined population of around 190000. Ulm forms an urban district of its own (*Stadtkreis Ulm*), and is the administrative seat of the [Alb-Donau-Kreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alb-Donau-Kreis), the district that surrounds it on three sides, but which the city itself is not a part of. Ulm is the overall 11th-largest [city on the river Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_on_the_river_Danube), and the third-largest German Danubian city after [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg) and [Ingolstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingolstadt).

Founded around 850, Ulm is rich in history and traditions as a former [free imperial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city). Ulm is an economic centre due to its varied industries, and is the seat of the [University of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm) (*Universität Ulm*), and of the Ulm University of Applied Sciences (*[Technische Hochschule Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_Ulm?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, THU). The city lies on the international railway corridor "[Main Line for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Line_for_Europe)", from [Paris](22989.md) to [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava) and [Budapest](36787.md), via [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), Ulm, [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg), [Munich](19058.md), [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), [Linz](55856.md) and [Vienna](55866.md). The [Ulm Minster](853115.md) (*Ulmer Münster*) is the second tallest church in the world (161.53 m or 529.95 ft).

In 1927, the then tiny village of Wiblingen was incorporated into Ulm, which led to that [Wiblingen Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiblingen_Abbey) with its monastic library and its True Cross reliquary (*Heilig-Kreuz-Reliquie*), that allegedly contains wood particles from the [True Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Cross), is now part of Ulm.

Famous personalities born in Ulm include [Johann Georg Niederegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_Niederegger?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1777–1859), [Karl Heinrich Kässbohrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Heinrich_K%C3%A4ssbohrer?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1864–1922), [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) (1879–1955), [Otto Kässbohrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_K%C3%A4ssbohrer) (1904–1989), [Hildegard Knef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_Knef) (1925–2002) and [Ursula Karven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Karven) (b.1964).

## Geography



View from [Ulm Minster](853115.md) towards Hirschstraße

Ulm lies at the point where the rivers [Blau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blau_\(Danube\)) and [Iller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iller) join the [Danube](21209639.md), at an altitude of 479 m (1,571.52 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level). Most parts of the city, including the old town, are situated on the northern bank of the Danube; only the districts of Wiblingen, Gögglingen, Donaustetten and Unterweiler lie on the southern bank. Across from the old town, on the other side of the river, lies the [twin city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_cities) of [Neu-Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu-Ulm) in the state of [Bavaria](3764.md), smaller than Ulm and, until 1810, a part of it (population c. 50,000).

Except for the Danube in the south, the city is surrounded by forests and hills which rise to altitudes of over 620 metres (2,034.12 feet), some of them part of the [Swabian Alb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Alb). South of the Danube, plains and hills finally end in the northern edge of the [Alps](981.md), which are approximately 100 kilometres (62 miles) from Ulm and are visible from the city on clear days.

The city of Ulm is situated in the northern part of the North Alpine [Foreland basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreland_basin), where the basin reaches the Swabian Alb. The [Turritellenplatte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turritellenplatte) of Ermingen ("Erminger Turritellenplatte") is a famous palaeontological site of [Burdigalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burdigalian) age.

## Neighboring communes



Ulm in the 1493 *[Nuremberg Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle)*

On the right (south-eastern) side of Danube and Iller there is the Bavarian district town [Neu-Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu-Ulm). On the left (north-western) side Ulm is almost completely surrounded by the Alb-Danube district. The neighbouring communes of Baden-Württemberg are the following: [Illerkirchberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illerkirchberg), [Staig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staig), [Hüttisheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%BCttisheim), [Erbach (Donau)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erbach_\(Donau\)), [Blaubeuren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaubeuren), [Blaustein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaustein), [Dornstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornstadt), [Beimerstetten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beimerstetten) and [Langenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langenau) as well as the eastern neighbouring community [Elchingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elchingen).

## Town subdivisions

The city is divided into 18 districts:

Ulm-Mitte, Böfingen, Donaustetten, Donautal, Eggingen, Einsingen, Ermingen, Eselsberg, Gögglingen, Grimmelfingen, Jungingen, Lehr, Mähringen, Oststadt, Söflingen (with Harthausen), Unterweiler, Weststadt, and Wiblingen.

Nine districts were integrated during the latest municipality reform in the 1970s: Eggingen, Einsingen, Ermingen, Gögglingen-Donaustetten, Jungingen, Lehr, Mähringen und Unterweiler. They have their own local councils which acquire an important consulting position to the whole city council concerning issues that are related to the prevailing districts. But at the end, final decisions can only be made by the city council of the entire city of Ulm.

## History

See also: [Free Imperial City of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Imperial_City_of_Ulm)



Ulm in 1572 by [Frans Hogenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Braun)

The oldest traceable settlement of the Ulm area began in the early [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period, around 5000 BC. Settlements of this time have been identified at the villages of Eggingen and Lehr, today districts of the city. In the city area of Ulm proper, the oldest find dates from the late Neolithic period. The earliest written mention of Ulm is dated 22 July 854 AD, when King [Louis the German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_the_German) signed a document in the King's palace of "Hulma" in the [Duchy of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia).[^cite_note-500px-5] The city was declared an [Imperial City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Free_City) (German: *Reichsstadt*) by [Friedrich Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Barbarossa) in 1181.

At first, Ulm's significance was due to the privilege of a *[Königspfalz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigspfalz)*, a place of accommodation for the medieval German kings and emperors on their frequent travels. Later, Ulm became a city of traders and craftsmen. One of the most important legal documents of the city, an agreement between the Ulm patricians and the trade guilds (German: *Großer Schwörbrief*), dates from 1397. This document, considered an early city constitution, and the beginning of the construction of an enormous church ([Ulm Minster](853115.md), 1377), financed by the inhabitants of Ulm themselves rather than by the church, demonstrate the assertiveness of Ulm's medieval citizens. Ulm blossomed during the 15th and 16th centuries, mostly due to the export of high-quality textiles. The city was situated at the crossroads of important trade routes extending to Italy. These centuries, during which many important buildings were erected, also represented the zenith of art in Ulm, especially for painters and sculptors like [Hans Multscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Multscher) and [Jörg Syrlin the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6rg_Syrlin_the_Elder). During the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation), Ulm became Protestant (1530). With the establishment of new trade routes following the discovery of the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) (16th century) and the outbreak and consequences of the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) (1618–1648), the city began to decline gradually. During the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) (1701–1714), it was alternately invaded several times by French and [Bavarian](3764.md) soldiers.



The capitulation of Ulm. General Mack and 23,000 Austrian troops surrendered to [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon).

In the wars following the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), the city was alternately occupied by French and Austrian forces, with the former ones destroying the city fortifications. In 1803, it lost the status of Imperial City and was absorbed into [Bavaria](3764.md). During the campaign of 1805, [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) managed to trap the invading Austrian army of [General Mack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Freiherr_Mack_von_Leiberich) and forced it to surrender in the [Battle of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ulm). In 1810, Ulm was incorporated into the [Kingdom of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) and lost its districts on the other bank of the Danube, which came to be known as [Neu-Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu-Ulm) (New Ulm).

In the mid-19th century, the city was [designated a fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortresses_of_the_German_Confederation) of the [German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) with huge military construction works directed primarily against the threat of a French invasion. The city became an important centre of industrialisation in southern Germany in the second half of the 19th century, its built-up area now being extended beyond the medieval walls. The construction of the huge minster, which had been interrupted in the 16th century for economic reasons, was resumed and eventually finished (1844–1891) in a wave of German national enthusiasm for the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages).

From 1933 to 1935, a [concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camp) primarily for political opponents of the regime was established on the Kuhberg, one of the hills surrounding Ulm. The Jews of Ulm, around 500 people, were first discriminated against and later persecuted; their synagogue was torn down during *[Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht)* in November 1938. Of 116 Jews deported from Ulm during World War II (45 were sent to [Theresienstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienstadt) on 22 August 1942), only four returned.[^cite_note-6] Approximately 25 Jews were living in Ulm in 1968.

[The sole RAF strategic bombing during World War II against Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Ulm_in_World_War_II) occurred on 17 December 1944, against the two large [lorry factories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Plan) of [Magirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meillerwagen)-Deutz and [Kässbohrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_K%C3%A4ssbohrer_Fahrzeugwerke), as well as other industries, barracks, and depots in Ulm. The Gallwitz Barracks and several military hospitals were among 14 [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) establishments destroyed.[^cite_note-7] The raid killed 707 Ulm inhabitants and left 25,000 homeless and after all the bombings, over 80% of the medieval city centre lay in ruins. The [Magirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magirus) factory hosted a subcamp of the [Dachau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp).[^cite_note-8]

Some parts of the city were rebuilt in the plain and simple style of the 1950s and 1960s, but most parts of the historic old town have been restored.[^cite_note-9] Due to its almost complete destruction in 1944, the Hirschstraße part of the city primarily consists of modern architecture. Ulm experienced substantial growth in the decades following World War II, with the establishment of large new housing projects and new industrial zones. In 1967, [Ulm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_University) was founded, which proved to be of great importance for the development of the city. Particularly since the 1980s, the transition from classical industry towards the high-tech sector has accelerated, with, for example, the establishment of research centres of companies like [Daimler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_AG), [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) and [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia) and a number of small applied research institutes near the university campus. The city today is still growing, forming a [twin city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_cities) of 170,000 inhabitants together with its neighbouring Bavarian city of [Neu-Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu-Ulm), and seems to benefit from its central position between the cities of [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and [Munich](19058.md) and thus between the cultural and economic hubs of southern Germany.

## Climate

Ulm has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfb*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dcbo*). Compared with the plains, the temperature fluctuates greatly, with the average temperature ranging from −1 °C (30 °F) in winter to 18 °C (64 °F) in summer, and precipitation is concentrated from May to August.

The Lübeck weather station has recorded the following extreme values:[^cite_note-sklima-11]

- Highest Temperature 37.4 °C (99.3 °F) on 27 July 1983.

- Warmest Minimum 20.6 °C (69.1 °F) on 1 July 1950.

- Coldest Maximum −16.8 °C (1.8 °F) on 7 January 1985.

- Lowest Temperature −25.5 °C (−13.9 °F) on 10 February 1956.

  [^cite_note-wetterzentrale-12]

- Highest Daily Precipitation 77.9 mm (3.07 in) on 2 July 1956.

- Wettest Month 237.5 mm (9.35 in) in July 1956.

- Wettest Year 928.1 mm (36.54 in) in 2002.

- Driest Year 501.4 mm (19.74 in) in 1949.

- Longest annual sunshine: 2,211.5 hours in 2022.

- Shortest annual sunshine: 1,401.5 hours in 1995.

## Economy



[Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George)'s Catholic church, Ulm

The city has very old trading traditions dating from medieval times and a long history of industrialisation, beginning with the establishment of a railway station in 1850. The most important sector is still classical industry (machinery, especially motor vehicles; [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics); [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals)). The establishment of the [University of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm) in 1967, which focuses on [biomedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedicine), the sciences, and engineering, helped support a transition to high-tech industry, especially after the crisis of classical industries in the 1980s.

## Ecology

In 2007 the City of Ulm was awarded the European Energy Award for its remarkable local energy management and its efforts to [combat climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_climate_change).[^cite_note-19] Examples of these efforts are a [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) power plant operated by the *Fernwärme Ulm GmbH* (10 [MW](21347693.md) electrical output), and the world's biggest [passive house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_house) office building, the so-called *Energon*, located in the "Science City" near the university campus. Moreover, the city of Ulm boasts the second largest [solar power](13690575.md) production in Germany.[^cite_note-20] For all new buildings, a strict energy standard (German KFW40 standard) has been mandatory since April 2008. Ulm Minster has been fully powered by renewables since January 2008.[^cite_note-21] Until the end of 2011 as a European pilot project a self-sustaining [data centre](579730.md) will be constructed in the west-city of Ulm.[^cite_note-22] There is a solar-powered ferry that crosses the Danube 7 days a week in summer.[^cite_note-23] The "Bündnis 100% Erneuerbare Energien" was founded in February 2010 with the aim of bringing together the people and organisations seeking to promote the transition to [100% renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_renewable_energy) in Ulm and Neu-Ulm by 2030.[^cite_note-24]

## Transportation



Tram in Ulm

Ulm is situated at the crossroads of the [A8 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_8) (connecting the principal cities of southern Germany, [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and [Munich](19058.md)), and the [A7 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_7) (one of the motorways running from northern to southern Europe).

[The city's railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_Hauptbahnhof) is served, among other lines, by one of the European train routes (Paris – [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) – [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) – Ulm – [Munich](19058.md) – [Vienna](55866.md) – [Budapest](36787.md)). Direct connections to [Berlin](3354.md) are also available.

Ulm's public transport system is based on several bus lines and [two tram lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Ulm). Several streets in the old town are for the use of pedestrians and cyclists only. Ulm was the first area to be served by the [Daimler AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_AG)'s [Car2Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car2Go) [carsharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsharing) service in 2008. However, the service in Ulm was discontinued at the end of 2014.

The nearest airports to Ulm are [Memmingen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memmingen_Airport), located 60 km (37 mi) to the south and [Stuttgart Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_Airport), located 80 km (50 mi) to the north west. [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) is also at a reasonable distance located 162 km (101 mi) east of Ulm.

## Education and culture



The Ulm Public Library

The [University of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm) was founded in 1967 and focuses on the sciences, medicine, engineering, and mathematics / economics. With about 10,000 students, it is one of the smaller universities in Germany.[^cite_note-25]

Ulm is also the seat of the city's [University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule) (German: *Fachhochschule*), founded in 1960 as a public school of engineering. The school also houses numerous students from around the world as part of an international study abroad programme.

In 1953, [Inge Aicher-Scholl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inge_Scholl), [Otl Aicher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otl_Aicher) and [Max Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Bill) founded the [Ulm School of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_School_of_Design) (German: Hochschule für Gestaltung – HfG Ulm), a design school in the tradition of the [Bauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus), which was, however, closed in 1968.[^cite_note-26]

Ulm's public library features over 480,000 print media. The city has a public theatre with drama, opera and ballet,[^cite_note-27] several small theatres,[^cite_note-28] and a professional philharmonic orchestra.[^cite_note-29]

### Historic

- [Ulm Minster](853115.md) (German: *Ulmer Münster*, built 1377–1891) with the world's second highest church [steeple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steeple) (161.53 m (529.95 ft) high and 768 steps). Choir stalls by Jörg Syrlin the Elder (1469–1474), famous sculpture *[Schmerzensmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmerzensmann)* ([Man of Sorrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_Sorrows)) by Hans Multscher (1429).

- The old *Fischerviertel* (fishermen's quarter) on the [River Blau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blau_\(Danube\)), with [half-timbered houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-timbered_houses), cobblestone streets, and picturesque footbridges. Interesting sights here are the *[Schiefes Haus Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiefes_Haus_Ulm?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (crooked house), a 16th-century house today used as a hotel, and the *Alte Münz* (Old Mint), a mediaeval building extended in the 16th and 17th centuries in [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) style.

- The remaining section of the city walls, along the river, with the 14th-century *Metzgerturm* (butchers' tower) (36 m (118.11 ft) high).

- The *[Rathaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_local_government)* ([Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hall)), built in 1370, featuring some brilliantly coloured murals dating from the mid-16th century. On the gable is an astronomical clock dating from 1520. Restored after serious damage in 1944.

- The *Krone* inn, a medieval complex of several houses (15th / 16th century, extensions from the 19th century), where German kings and emperors were accommodated during their travels.

- Several large buildings from the late Middle Ages / renaissance used for various purposes (especially storage of food and weapons), e.g. *Schwörhaus, Kornhaus, Salzstadel, Büchsenstadel, Zeughaus, Neuer Bau*.

- Ulm Federal Fortifications are the largest preserved fortifications and were built from 1842 to 1859 to protect from attacks by France.

- The historic district *Auf dem Kreuz*, a residential area with many buildings from before 1700.

- [Wiblingen Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiblingen_Abbey), a former [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) abbey in the suburb of Wiblingen in the south of Ulm. The church shows characteristics of late [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) and early [classicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classicism). Its library is a masterpiece of [rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo).

### Memorials

- Albert Einstein Memorial – A small memorial at the site of the house where [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) was born in *Bahnhofstraße*, between the present-day newspaper offices and the bank. The house itself and the whole district were destroyed in the [firebombing](153694.md) of 1944.

- Memorial to [Hans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Scholl) and [Sophie Scholl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Scholl) – A small memorial on the Münsterplatz in memory of these two members of the [Weiße Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei%C3%9Fe_Rose) (White Rose, a resistance group opposed to the Nazi regime), who spent their youth in Ulm. Their family's house near the memorial was destroyed in the firebombing of 1944.

- The Memorial to Deserters – Located near the university's botanical garden, it commemorates those who deserted from the Wehrmacht during World War II. It was originally erected on 9 September 1989, and was moved to its current location in July 2005. The Monument represents the idea: "Desertion is not reprehensible, war is".
