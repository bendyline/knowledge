**Peter Wilkinson Howitt** (born May 31, 1946) is a Canadian economist and the Lyn Crost Professor of Social Sciences Emeritus at [Brown University](4157.md). He is best known for his collaborative work with [Philippe Aghion](20746821.md) on [endogenous growth theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endogenous_growth_theory), the concept of [creative destruction](99656.md) in modern macroeconomics and the [Aghion–Howitt model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghion%E2%80%93Howitt_model).[^cite_note-nobel-4] In 2025, Howitt and Aghion were jointly awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](23535306.md) "for the theory of sustained growth through [creative destruction](99656.md)."[^cite_note-nobel-4]

## Early life and education

Howitt was born in [Guelph, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelph,_Ontario), in 1946.[^cite_note-5] He earned his BA in economics from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) in 1968, his MA from the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) in 1969, and his PhD from [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) in 1973 under [Robert W. Clower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Clower).[^cite_note-6]

## Academic career

Howitt returned to Canada after receiving his PhD and taught at the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) from 1972 to 1996.[^cite_note-Brown_News-7] He became a faculty member at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) in 1996 and joined Brown University in 2000, where he has remained since.[^cite_note-Brown_News-7] Since 2013, Howitt is Professor Emeritus at Brown University. Besides his university affiliations, he also has long-time connections with the [C. D. Howe Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._D._Howe_Institute), a nonpartisan think tank based in [Toronto, Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto,_Canada). Howitt started publishing reports of economic policy through the institute since 1986 and was a fellow-in-residence from 2011 to 2015.[^cite_note-gredeg-8][^cite_note-9]

He served as president of the [Canadian Economics Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Economics_Association) in 1993–1994 and was the editor of the *[Journal of Money, Credit, and Banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Money,_Credit,_and_Banking)* in the period 1997–2000.[^cite_note-mcgill-10]

## Honours and awards

Howitt was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 1992[^cite_note-mcgill-10] and of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society) in 1994.[^cite_note-11] In 2019, he and Aghion received the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award) in Economics, Finance and Management.[^cite_note-12] Howitt and Aghion were also awarded half of the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](23535306.md) in 2025 "for the theory of sustained growth through creative destruction", the other half going to [Joel Mokyr](10994991.md).[^cite_note-nobel-4]

## Personal life

Howitt is married to Pat Howitt who he credits for encouraging and supporting the collaboration with Aghion. [^cite_note-13]
