The **March 2026 No Kings protests** (also called **No Kings 3** and **No Kings Day 3.0**)[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] were a group of coordinated protests that took place on March 28, 2026. They were part of a series of [demonstrations](6787314.md) in the United States to protest the actions and policies of the [second Trump administration](78298108.md),[^cite_note-25] including the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md),[^cite_note-26] [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding_in_the_United_States), suppression of the [Epstein files](80415168.md), and [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) operations[^cite_note-27] that led to [shootings by immigration agents](82067135.md), most notably the killings of [Renée Good](82055905.md), [Keith Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Porter_Jr), and [Alex Pretti](82203651.md).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The organizers claim the protests included more than 3,300 organized events across the country that drew a combined estimated eight to nine million protestors.[^cite_note-30] It was the [largest single-day protest in American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protests_and_demonstrations_in_the_United_States_by_size).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-\:6-32][^cite_note-33]

## Background

The March 28 protests were preceded by [No Kings protests in June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_No_Kings_protests)[^cite_note-34] and [October 2025](81279597.md),[^cite_note-35] [a general strike in Minnesota on January 23](82198050.md) in response to [Operation Metro Surge](82105033.md),[^cite_note-36] and a [larger strike across the U.S. on January 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_30,_2026_protests_against_ICE),[^cite_note-37] and were coordinated by [Indivisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_movement) and [50501](79143789.md),[^cite_note-38] joined by various groups and organizations, including [Third Act Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Act_Movement)[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] and the [American Federation of Labor and Congress of Industrial Organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL-CIO) (AFL-CIO).[^cite_note-41]

When the protests were originally scheduled in response to the [killings of Renée Good](82055905.md) and [Alex Pretti](82203651.md) in January 2026, organizers described them as being opposed to immigration policies and authoritarianism of the Trump administration. After the 2026 Iran war began, organizers also described the protests as including opposition to "senseless war".[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Turnout

The protests were the [largest single-day protests in American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protests_and_demonstrations_in_the_United_States_by_size), surpassing the previous record held by [the October No Kings protest](81279597.md). Organizers estimated that more than eight million people participated in more than 3,300 organized events across the country, surpassing the previous No Kings protests' 5 million people in [June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_No_Kings_protests) and 7 million in October.[^cite_note-\:6-32] Roughly 2.3 percent of the US population participated in the protests,[^cite_note-45] which was short of organizers' goal of [3.5 percent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3.5%25_rule) of the population.[^cite_note-46]

## Locations and activities

Over 3,300 events took place in the United States nationwide.[^cite_note-\:3-47] A virtual protest also took place for those with disabilities or for those individuals who were unable to attend a live in person protest.[^cite_note-48]

Organizers said two-thirds of the RSVPs for protest events came from outside of major urban centers, including in conservative-leaning states like [Idaho](14607.md), [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming), [Montana](19978.md), [Utah](31716.md), [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) and [Louisiana](18130.md), as well as [swing states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state) like [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [Georgia](48830.md) and [Arizona](21883824.md).[^cite_note-\:3-47]

While most activities took place in the United States, a number of American [expatriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) organizations such as [Indivisible Abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_Abroad?action=edit\&redlink=1), local international chapters of Indivisible, and [Democrats Abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_Abroad) held protests in several international locations.[^cite_note-\:5-49]

### Alabama

22 demonstrations took place in Alabama, featuring thousands of protesters, including 700 in [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn,_Alabama), 7,000 in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama), 200 in [Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Alabama), 1,000 at the [Alabama State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_State_Capitol) in [Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery,_Alabama), and 700 in [Tuscaloosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscaloosa,_Alabama).[^cite_note-50]

### Alaska

About 25 protests were held in [Alaska](624.md),[^cite_note-51] some in below-freezing temperatures,[^cite_note-\:4-52] including in [Anchorage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchorage,_Alaska),[^cite_note-53] [Fairbanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairbanks,_Alaska),[^cite_note-54] [Ketchikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchikan,_Alaska),[^cite_note-55] [Kotzebue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotzebue,_Alaska),[^cite_note-56] [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer,_Alaska),[^cite_note-\:4-52] and [Juneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juneau,_Alaska).[^cite_note-57]

## Reaction

In a video that was retroactively set to private,

[^cite_note-314]

&#x20;on April 1, 2026, President Trump states "...They call me king now. Do you believe it? No king. I'm such a king, I can't get [a ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_State_Ballroom) approved. It's pretty amazing, right? I'm a king. If I was a king, we'd be doing a lot more. I'm doing a lot, but I could be doing a lot more if I was a king."

The Trump administration responses included "we do not think about the protest at all", that only the reporters covering the protests care about them,[^cite_note-315] and that the protests were "[Trump Derangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_derangement_syndrome) Therapy Sessions".[^cite_note-316]

During a speech at a White House Easter lunch on April 1, 2026, Trump said "On Palm Sunday, Jesus entered Jerusalem as crowds welcomed him with praise honoring him as king. They call me king now. Can you believe it?" He continued, "No kings. I'm such a king, I can't get a ballroom approved. It's pretty amazing, right? If I was a king, we'd be doing a lot more." The footage of the speech was later removed from the [White House website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehouse.gov).[^cite_note-317]
