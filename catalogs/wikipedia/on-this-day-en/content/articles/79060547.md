***Mr Nobody Against Putin*** is a 2025 [documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film) directed by [David Borenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Borenstein) and [Pavel Talankin](80453789.md). It follows Talankin in his job at a school in [Karabash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabash,_Chelyabinsk_Oblast), a poor mining town near the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains). While recording his students, Talankin also documents the [Putin](32817.md) administration's moves to control public perception during the ongoing [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md).[^cite_note-3]

The film had its world premiere at the World Cinema Documentary section of the [2025 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Sundance_Film_Festival) on 25 January, where it won the Special Jury Award. It was selected as the [Danish Oscar entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Danish_submissions_for_the_Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) for [Best International Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film),[^cite_note-4] and went on to win [Best Documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Documentary) at the [79th British Academy Film Awards](82230921.md) and [Best Documentary Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature_Film) at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md).[^cite_note-5] Reception was generally positive, but there were concerns – especially by Ukrainian viewers – about the way the film portrayed [opposition to Putin within Russia](38212364.md).[^cite_note-independent-2025-6] The film was also criticized for its use of covert footage, particularly of children, obtained without informed consent.[^cite_note-\:2-7]

## Background

*Mr Nobody Against Putin* was shot over a period of two years by Pavel "Pasha" Talankin, the videographer and events coordinator at [Karabash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabash,_Chelyabinsk_Oblast) Primary School #1 in [Chelyabinsk Oblast](475230.md).[^cite_note-\:0-8] Talankin began seriously documenting his activities after the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](70149799.md), when the government began requiring schools to hold regular "patriotic displays" and use state-written curriculum (such as *[Conversations about Important Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversations_about_Important_Things)*) to justify the invasion to students.[^cite_note-independent-2025-6] At the same time, the government instituted a requirement to upload footage of these displays to a state-run portal to prove compliance, allowing Talankin cover to film meetings, lessons, and visitors to the school without attracting suspicion.[^cite_note-\:1-9] He initially planned to resign in order to avoid supporting the Russian government; however, after getting in contact with director David Borenstein, he withdrew his resignation to keep gathering footage.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

On one occasion, mercenaries from the [Wagner Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) arrive at the school to give a lecture;[^cite_note-\:12-10] on another, Talankin disrupts the flag-raising ceremony by playing the US national anthem "[The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner)" performed by [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga) instead of the Russian anthem.[^cite_note-\:0-8] In parallel, the people surrounding Talankin are revealed — his mother, who works as a librarian at the same school; Pavel Abdulmanov, a pro-Putin history teacher; a student named Masha, whose brother fights and later dies in the war; and former students who are drafted into the army.[^cite_note-\:19-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-variety-20250125-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

Talankin secretly fled Russia during the summer of 2024, aided by his producers and Borenstein who worked to get him asylum in Europe, following evidence of police surveillance at his home, which appears in the film.[^cite_note-16]

## Production

*Mr Nobody Against Putin* was directed by [David Borenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Borenstein) and [Pavel Talankin](80453789.md). The co-production partners are [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF)/[Arte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte) and the [BBC](19344654.md), with additional support from the [Danish Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Film_Institute) and the [Czech Audiovisual Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Audiovisual_Fund?action=edit\&redlink=1). The film was produced by Borenstein, [Helle Faber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helle_Faber) for 'Made in Copenhagen' (Denmark), Radovan Síbrt, and Alžběta Karásková for PINK (Czech Republic).[^cite_note-variety-20250125-13] Post-production was done by Prague's UPP studio.

Lucie Kon is the BBC's commissioning editor and an executive producer, who oversaw the project and was crucial in providing the initial funding from the BBC. She also advised on the critical safety and security protocols required to get the sensitive undercover footage out of Russia. She facilitated the collaboration between the filmmakers and various international partners.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Release and reception

*Mr Nobody Against Putin* premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Sundance_Film_Festival) in January 2025. Beginning in March 2025, it has been screened at numerous film festivals around the world. The film premiered in Florida on 29 March 2025 at the [18th Gasparilla International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasparilla_International_Film_Festival) in Tampa.[^cite_note-19] In the United Kingdom, *Mr Nobody* first aired on [BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four) on 14 October 2025, as part of the *[Storyville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storyville_\(TV_series\))* strand:[^cite_note-bbc-release-20] [Kino Lorber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kino_Lorber) acquired North American rights in January 2026, setting a release date for January 22 in select theaters and streaming on the Kino Film Collection.[^cite_note-21]

The film's production process became the subject of public debate, particularly regarding ethical considerations. Because much of the footage was recorded covertly in a Russian school, critics raised concerns that students and colleagues were filmed without [informed consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informed_consent), including minors who could not meaningfully agree to participate. Some observers questioned whether the use of such material exposed participants to potential risks, especially given the political sensitivity of the subject matter.[^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-\:3-22]

These concerns were echoed in media discussions and among Russian-speaking audiences abroad. According to *[Meduza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meduza_\(website\))*, the documentary prompted disagreement within the emigre community, where some viewers praised the filmmaker's actions as courageous, while others criticized the project's ethics and questioned the author's motivations, describing him as potentially seeking recognition rather than solely documenting events.[^cite_note-\:4-23] Some have argued that the film filters events in Russia through a distinctly Western lens, shaped for the documentary circuit. Additionally, others have pointed out that certain moments appear staged or overblown, presenting Talankin as a heroic figure fighting against the odds.[^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-\:4-23]

More broadly, coverage in international media highlighted the inherent tension between the film's investigative value and the ethical risks of undercover filming in a school environment, with the project described as raising difficult questions about the boundaries of documentary practice under authoritarian conditions.[^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-\:3-22]

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), [100%](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_with_a_100%25_rating_on_Rotten_Tomatoes) of 52 critics' reviews are positive. The website's consensus reads: "Chronicling one whistleblower's uphill struggle with clear-eyed insight, *Mr Nobody Against Putin* is a powerful documentary that vividly illustrates the resilience of everyday people against institutional forces."[^cite_note-Rotten_Tomatoes-24] [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean), assigned the film a score of 80 out of 100, based on 13 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.[^cite_note-Metacritic-25]

*[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire)* published a positive review, giving the film a grade of "A-" and praising the documentary for its undercover footage of life inside Russia.[^cite_note-\:0-8] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* was similarly positive and praised Talankin for his bravery in capturing footage.[^cite_note-26] *[The Daily Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Beast)* and [*Variety*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\)) also published positive reviews.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-variety-20250125-13] *[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club)* was more critical, noting that the film's focus on a single school made it feel limited in scope and that several narrative threads were dropped as the film continued.[^cite_note-27] [Owen Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Matthews), writing for *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)*, was also critical, stating that the Russian government did not take as much offence to token acts of protest as *Mr Nobody* appears to portray, while also noting that the film shows more of the widespread [political apathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_apathy) and compliance that contributes to Putin's totalitarian rule than defiance – a problem raised by many Ukrainian viewers.[^cite_note-independent-2025-6]

### Russian government response

In February 2026, [Dmitry Peskov](9214683.md), Russian Presidential Press Secretary, told journalists that the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Administration_of_Russia) had not watched the work and that "it would be wrong to give any assessments without being familiar with the material".[^cite_note-28]

In March, the Russian [Presidential Council for Civil Society and Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Council_for_Civil_Society_and_Human_Rights) appealed to the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) and [UNESCO](21786641.md) Secretary General [Khaled Al-Anani](59419909.md) regarding *Mr. Nobody Against Putin*. The council claimed that the film may violate the rights of minors because it includes footage of schoolchildren used without parental consent, and asked the Academy to review whether the film complies with child-protection standards.[^cite_note-29]

In the same month, a court in [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) banned the documentary *Mr Nobody Against Putin* from being distributed in Russia. The charges cited the appearance of the [white-blue-white flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-blue-white_flag), considered a symbol of the [Freedom of Russia Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Russia_Legion), as "propaganda of extremism and terrorism". Prosecutors also claimed the film promotes "militarization", expresses a "negative attitude toward the war and the current government", and shows minors without parental consent.[^cite_note-30]
