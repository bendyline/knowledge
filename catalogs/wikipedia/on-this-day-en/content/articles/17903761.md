# Aviation biofuel



Refueling an [Airbus A320](164933.md) with [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) in 2011

An **aviation biofuel** (also known as **bio-jet fuel**,[^cite_note-IU4dec2020-1] **bio-aviation fuel** (BAF)[^cite_note-Doliente2020-2], **semi-synthetic jet fuel**[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-\:5-4], or **sustainable aviation fuel** (SAF)) is a [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) used to power [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft). The [International Air Transport Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association) (IATA) considers it a key element in reducing the [environmental impact of aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_aviation).[^cite_note-5] Aviation biofuel can be used to [decarbonize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbon_economy) otherwise hard-to-decarbonise medium and long-haul air travel via "drop-in-replacement" which does not require extensive modification to aircraft engine design. **Synthetic paraffinic kerosene** (SPK) refers to any non-petroleum-based hydrocarbon mixture designed as a blending component or complete replacement of kerosene [jet fuel](1205435.md).

Not all bio-fuels are produced in a sustainable manner. Typically sustainable aviation fuel is certified as being [sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable) by a third-party organisation and may benefit from emissions trading or carbon-credit schemes depending on local laws.

Biofuels are [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass)-derived fuels from plants, animals, or waste; depending on which type of biomass is used, they could lower CO<sub>2</sub> emissions by 20–98% compared to [conventional jet fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_A1).[^cite_note-Bauen2009-6] The first test flight using blended biofuel was in 2008, and in 2011, blended fuels with 50% biofuels were allowed on commercial flights. In 2023 SAF production was 600 million liters, representing 0.2% of global jet fuel use.[^cite_note-7] By 2024, SAF production was to increase to 1.3 billion liters (1 million tonnes), representing 0.3% of global jet fuel consumption and 11% of global renewable fuel production.[^cite_note-8] This increase came as major US production facilities delayed their ramp-up until 2025, having initially been expected to reach 1.9 billion liters.

Aviation biofuel can be produced from upgraded plant or animal sources such as *[Jatropha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatropha)*, [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae), [tallows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallows), waste oils, [palm oil](57561.md), [Babassu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babassu_oil), and *[Camelina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelina)* (bio-SPK); from solid [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) using [pyrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrolysis) processed with a [Fischer–Tropsch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer%E2%80%93Tropsch_process) (FT-SPK); with an [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(chemistry\))-to-jet (ATJ) process from waste fermentation; or from [synthetic biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_biology) through a [solar reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reactor). Pathways which use [electricity-derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolysis) hydrogen as a feedstock are called [Electrofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrofuels) or e-fuels.[^cite_note-9] Small piston engines can be modified to burn [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) as a replacement for [aviation gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avgas).

SAF technology faces significant challenges due to feedstock constraints. The oils and fats used in production of hydrotreated esters and fatty acids (HEFA), crucial for SAF production, are in limited supply as demand increases. Although advanced [e-fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrofuel) technology, which combines waste CO<sub>2</sub> with [clean hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_hydrogen), presents a promising solution, it is still under development and comes with high costs. To overcome these issues, SAF developers are exploring more readily available feedstocks such as [woody biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignocellulosic_biomass) and agricultural and municipal waste, aiming to produce lower-carbon jet fuel more sustainably and efficiently.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

### Technical development

The use of vegetable oils as a feedstock for aviation fuel has been studied for decades. Dunn (2001) highlighted the potential of vegetable oils as alternative jet fuel sources, which fundamentally underpins the modern HEFA pathway.[^cite_note-12]

By 2015, cultivation of [fatty acid methyl esters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid_methyl_ester) and [alkenones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkenone) from the algae, *Isochrysis*, was under research.[^cite_note-13]

By 2023, 90% of biofuel was made from oilseed and sugarcane which are grown for this purpose only.[^cite_note-14]

Some perspectives suggest that to overcome these economic challenges, SAF production should be integrated into biorefineries that also foster the production of renewable chemicals, thereby diversifying revenue streams.[^cite_note-Ambrosio2025-15]

### Development of Standard Specifications

By June 2011, the revised *Specification for Aviation Turbine Fuel Containing Synthesized Hydrocarbons* ([ASTM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASTM) D7566[^cite_note-\:1-3]) allowed commercial airlines to blend up to 50% biofuels with conventional jet fuel.[^cite_note-22] The safety and performance of jet fuel used in passenger flights is certified by [ASTM International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASTM_International).[^cite_note-ASTMsep2011-23] Biofuels were approved for commercial use after a multi-year technical review from [aircraft makers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer), [engine manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_turbofan_manufacturers) and [oil companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_companies).[^cite_note-24] Thereafter some airlines experimented with biofuels on commercial flights.[^cite_note-25] As of July 2020, seven annexes to D7566 were published, including various biofuel types:[^cite_note-26]

- Fischer-Tropsch Synthetic Paraffinic Kerosene (FT-SPK, 2009)

- Hydroprocessed Esters and Fatty Acids Synthetic Paraffinic Kerosene (HEFA-SPK, 2011)

- usHydroprocessed Fermented Sugars to Synthetic Isoparaffins (HFS-SIP, 2014)

- Fischer-Tropsch Synthetic Paraffinic Kerosene with Aromatics (FT-SPK/A, 2015)

- Alcohol to Jet Synthetic Paraffinic Kerosene (ATJ-SPK, 2016)

- Catalytic Hydrothermolysis Synthesized Kerosene (CH-SK, or CHJ; 2020).

A sub-percentage of fuel is required to come from [power-to-liquid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-to-X) technologies (essentially the same as the EU e-fuels sub-target), rising from 0.2% in 2028 to 3.5% in 2040.[^cite_note-\:4-28]

### Grants and Investments

In December 2011, the [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA) awarded US\$7.7 million to eight companies to develop [drop-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_good) sustainable fuels, especially from [alcohols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(chemistry\)), sugars, [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass), and [organic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_matter) such as [pyrolysis oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrolysis_oil), within its CAAFI and CLEEN programs.[^cite_note-40]

Dozens of companies received hundreds of millions in [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) from 2005 to 2012 to extract fuel oil from algae, some promising competitively-priced fuel by 2012 and production of 1 billion US gal (3.8 million m<sup>3</sup>) by 2012-2014.[^cite_note-Greentech19april2017-41] By 2017 most companies had disappeared or changed their [business plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_plan) to focus on other markets.[^cite_note-Greentech19april2017-41]

The 2022 [Inflation Reduction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_Reduction_Act) introduced the Fueling Aviation's Sustainable Transition (FAST) Grant Program. The program provides \$244.5 million in grants for SAF-related "production, transportation, blending, and storage."[^cite_note-42] In November, 2022, sustainable aviation fuels were a topic at [COP26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference).[^cite_note-43]

## Production

[Jet fuel](1205435.md) is a mixture of various [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon). The mixture is restricted by product requirements, for example, [freezing point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freezing_point) and [smoke point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_point). Jet fuels are sometimes classified as [kerosene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerosene_jet_fuel) or [naphtha](184342.md)-type. Kerosene-type fuels include Jet A, Jet A-1, JP-5 and JP-8. Naphtha-type jet fuels, sometimes referred to as "wide-cut" jet fuel, include Jet B and JP-4.

"Drop-in" biofuels are biofuels that are interchangeable with conventional fuels. Deriving "drop-in" jet fuel from bio-based sources is [ASTM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASTM) approved via two routes: blending and co-processing. ASTM has found it safe to blend in 50% SPK into regular jet fuels.[^cite_note-\:2-44][^cite_note-ASTMsep2011-23] Tests have been done with blending synthetic paraffinic kerosene (SPK) in considerably higher concentrations.[^cite_note-45]

### Alternative production routes

Several research initiatives and companies have reported work on technologies intended to produce [synthetic hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_hydrocarbons) and sustainable aviation fuels (SAF).

The SUN-to-LIQUID project (2016-2019) was a [European Union Horizon 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Horizon_2020)-funded research initiative that demonstrated the production of sustainable aviation fuel directly from sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide. The project utilised a solar thermochemical process involving a high-temperature solar reactor to produce synthesis gas (syngas), which was then converted into jet fuel through [Fischer-Tropsch synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer-Tropsch_synthesis). On June 13, 2019, researchers at the [IMDEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDEA) Energy Institute in Móstoles, Spain successfully demonstrated the complete production chain, marking a significant milestone in solar fuel technology. The project consortium included partners from seven European countries and Switzerland, led by Bauhaus Luftfahrt, and received support from the [Swiss State Secretariat for Education, Research and Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_State_Secretariat_for_Education,_Research_and_Innovation). While the demonstration proved the technical feasibility of producing drop-in aviation fuel from renewable sources without competing for agricultural land, the technology remained at an early stage with challenges related to scaling and economic viability requiring further development.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

Using [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae) to make jet fuel remains an [emerging technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_technology). Companies working on algae jet fuel include [Solazyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solazyme), Honeywell UOP, Solena, [Sapphire Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire_Energy), [Imperium Renewables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Renewables), and Aquaflow Bionomic Corporation. Universities working on algae jet fuel are [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) and [Cranfield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranfield_University). Major investors for algae-based SPK research are [Boeing](18933266.md), [Honeywell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell)/[UOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UOP_LLC), [Air New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand), [Continental Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Airlines), [Japan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Airlines), and [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric).

Alder Fuels developed a technology to convert [lignocellulosic biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignocellulosic_biomass), including forestry and agricultural residues, into a hydrocarbon-rich intermediate product called "greencrude" through [pyrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrolysis). This greencrude can subsequently be processed in conventional petroleum refineries using existing infrastructure to produce [drop-in aviation and transportation fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop-in_fuel). The company's process utilises waste biomass feedstocks that do not compete with food production, addressing one of the sustainability concerns associated with first-generation biofuels.[^cite_note-62]

Universal Fuel Technologies developed Flexiforming technology, a catalytic process designed to convert various feedstocks, including byproducts from existing renewable fuel production, into sustainable aviation fuel. The technology has feedstock flexibility, allowing for the processing of multiple biomass-derived inputs through a single conversion pathway.[^cite_note-63]

Arcadia eFuels developed a power-to-liquid facility at the port of [Vordingborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vordingborg), Denmark, utilising a process that combines water electrolysis powered by renewable electricity with carbon dioxide capture to produce synthetic aviation fuel. The process involves generating [green hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_hydrogen) through electrolysis, which is then combined with captured CO2 to create synthesis gas (syngas), subsequently converted to jet fuel via Fischer-Tropsch or similar gas-to-liquid processes.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Piston engines

Small piston engines can be modified to burn [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol).[^cite_note-SDSU2016-66] [Swift Fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_Fuel), a biofuel alternative to [avgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avgas), was approved as a test fuel by [ASTM International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASTM_International) in December 2009.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

### Technical challenges

A number of technical challenges and additional requirements exist for manufacturing, distribution, and use of SAF.

#### Aromatics and cycloalkanes

[Nitrile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrile)-based rubber materials expand in the presence of aromatic compounds found in conventional petroleum fuel. Pure biofuels without petroleum and paraffin-based additives may cause rubber seals and hoses to shrink.[^cite_note-69] Synthetic rubber substitutes that are not adversely affected by biofuels, such as [Viton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viton), for seals and hoses are available.[^cite_note-70]

As of May 2025 SAF is generally required to be blended with fossil fuel—because jet fuel needs [cycloalkanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycloalkanes) and [aromatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatic_compound), which are generally deficient in SAF; as well as the more prevalent in SAF [n-alkanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkane#Linear_alkanes) and [isoalkanes](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/isoalkane).[^cite_note-NREL-MIT-Comstock-71]

#### Anti-Oxidant

ASTM D7566 mandates addition of anti-oxidant additives for SAF derived via certain pathways such as HEFA.[^cite_note-\:2-44]

#### Low Temperature Viscosity

Due to the higher viscosity of some types of SPK, ASTM D7566 includes an additional kinematic viscosity limit of 12mm²/s at -40°C for certification of SAF[^cite_note-\:2-44].

#### Microbiological Growth

The [United States Air Force](32090.md) found harmful bacteria and fungi in their biofueled aircraft, and used [pasteurization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteurization) to disinfect them.[^cite_note-72]

## Economics

Economic viability has historically been a major barrier to SAF adoption. Early assessments by Lander and Reif (1986) noted that producing jet fuel from alternative sources would require significant capital investment compared to conventional petroleum refining.[^cite_note-73]

In 2019 the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) forecast SAF production should grow from 18 to 75 billion litres between 2025 and 2040, representing a 5% to 19% share of aviation fuel.[^cite_note-IEA18mar2019-17] By 2019, fossil jet fuel production cost was \$0.3-0.6 per L given a \$50–100 crude [oil barrel](594043.md), while aviation biofuel production cost was \$0.7-1.6, needing a \$110–260 crude oil barrel to [break-even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break-even).[^cite_note-IEA18mar2019-17] As of 2024, SAF represents just 0.3% of global aviation fuel.[^cite_note-IATA25_web-74]

As of 2020 aviation biofuel was more expensive than fossil jet kerosene,[^cite_note-IU4dec2020-1] considering [aviation taxation and subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_taxation_and_subsidies) at that time.[^cite_note-75]

As of a 2021 analysis, VFA-SAF break-even cost was \$2.50/US gal (\$0.66/L).[^cite_note-Huq-2021-76] This number was generated considering credits and incentives at the time, such as [California's LCFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCFS) (Low Carbon Fuel Standard) credits and the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) [Renewable Fuel Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_Fuel_Standard_\(United_States\)) incentives.

In countries with high biomass availability like Brazil, the primary pathways identified for decarbonization are Hydroprocessed Esters and Fatty Acids (HEFA) and Alcohol-to-Jet (AtJ).[^cite_note-Ambrosio2025-15]

From 2020, [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas) planned to use a 50/50 blend of SG Preston's biofuel on its Los Angeles-Australia flights. SG Preston also planned to provide fuel to [JetBlue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue) over 10 years.[^cite_note-AvWeek26apr2019-79] At its sites in [Singapore](27318.md), [Rotterdam](26049.md) and [Porvoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porvoo), Finland's [Neste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neste) expected to improve its renewable fuel production capacity from 2.7 to 3.0 million t (6.0 to 6.6 billion lb) a year by 2020, and to increase its Singapore capacity by 1.3 million t (2.9 billion lb) to reach 4.5 million t (9.9 billion lb) in 2022 by investing €1.4 billion (\$1.6 billion).[^cite_note-AvWeek26apr2019-79]

By 2020, [International Airlines Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Airlines_Group) had invested \$400 million to convert waste into sustainable aviation fuel with [Velocys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer%E2%80%93Tropsch_process#Velocys).[^cite_note-Flight3jan2020-80]

United Airlines has expanded SAF use across multiple airports worldwide, including [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol) in 2022,[^cite_note-81] [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_International_Airport) and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport) in 2023,[^cite_note-82] and [Chicago O'Hare](248449.md) and [Los Angeles](18131.md) in 2024.[^cite_note-83]

In March 2024, regular use of SAF began in the Northeastern United States at [John F. Kennedy International Airport](97218.md), as part of a new effort by JetBlue.[^cite_note-84] [Southwest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airlines) began using sustainable jet fuel at [Chicago Midway International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Midway_International_Airport) in October 2024.[^cite_note-85]

Fuel supplier Avfuel expanded its SAF offerings in Texas in November 2025 through its fixed-base operator (FBO) partner Million Air at [Austin-Bergstrom International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin%E2%80%93Bergstrom_International_Airport) (AUS). Less than 1% of global liquid biofuels are currently used for aviation purposes, with biojet fuel fueling less than 0.5% of all flights; most is used for road transport, but even if the entire biofuel production was allocated to aviation, this would provide, at most, one-third of demand.[^cite_note-86]

As of 2022, some 450,000 flights had used sustainable fuels as part of the fuel mix, although such fuels were \~3x more expensive than the traditional fossil jet fuel or [kerosene](16992.md).[^cite_note-Economist18aug2022-87] In 2023, SAFs account for less than 0.1% of all aviation fuels consumed.[^cite_note-88] Throughout 2024, [Alaska Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines) was the leader among U.S. airlines in SAF implementation, accounting for 0.68% of its fuel usage. Other major airlines including United, Delta and JetBlue used SAF in roughly .3% of fuel.[^cite_note-\:0-50]

## Sustainability Certification



In 2016, [Oslo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Airport) became the first international airport to offer sustainable aviation fuel as part of the fuel mix.

[Sustainable biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_biofuel) do not use [food crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_crop), prime [agricultural land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_land) or fresh water. **Sustainable aviation fuel** (SAF) is certified by a third-party such as the [Roundtable For Sustainable Biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_biofuel#Roundtable_on_Sustainable_Biomaterials).[^cite_note-89]

A SAF sustainability certification ensures that the product satisfies criteria focused on environmental, social, and economic "[triple-bottom-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-bottom-line)" considerations. Under many emission regulation schemes, such as the [European Union Emissions Trading Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Emissions_Trading_Scheme) (EUTS), a certified SAF product may be exempted from carbon compliance liability costs.[^cite_note-90] This marginally improves SAF's economic competitiveness versus fossil-based fuel.[^cite_note-91]

The first reputable body to launch a sustainable biofuel certification system was the European-based Roundtable on Sustainable Biomaterials (RSB) NGO.[^cite_note-92] Leading airlines and other signatories to the Sustainable Aviation Fuel Users Group (SAFUG) pledged to support RSB as their preferred certification provider.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Some SAF pathways procured [RIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_Identification_Number) pathways under the United States's [renewable fuel standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_Fuel_Standard_\(United_States\)) which can serve as an implicit certification if the RIN is a [Q-RIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_Identification_Number).

EU RED II Recast (2018)

Greenhouse gas emissions from sustainable fuels must be lower than those from the fuels they replace: at least 50% for production built before 5 October 2015, 60% after that date and 65% after 2021.

[^cite_note-95]

&#x20;Raw materials cannot be sourced from land with high biodiversity or high carbon stocks (i.e. primary and protected forests, biodiversity-rich grasslands, wetlands and [peatlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peatland)). Other sustainability issues are set out in the Governance Regulation and may be covered voluntarily.

ICAO 'CORSIA'

GHG Reduction - Criterion 1: lifecycle reductions of at least 10% compared to fossil fuel. Carbon Stock - Criterion 1: not produced from biomass obtained from land whose uses changed after 1 January 2008 from primeval forests, wetlands or peatlands, as all these lands have high carbon stocks. Criterion 2: For [land use changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use_change) after 1 January 2008, (using IPCC land categories), if emissions from direct land use change (DLUC) exceed the default value of the induced land use change (ILUC), the value of the DLUC replaces the default (ILUC) value.

### Global impact

As [emissions trading schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_trading_schemes) and other carbon compliance regimes emerge, certain biofuels are likely to be exempted ("zero-rated") by governments from compliance due to their closed-loop nature, if they can demonstrate appropriate credentials. For example, in the EUTS, SAFUG's proposal was accepted[^cite_note-96] that only fuels certified as sustainable by the RSB or similar body would be zero-rated.[^cite_note-97] SAFUG was formed by a group of interested airlines in 2008 under the auspices of [Boeing Commercial Airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Commercial_Airplanes). Member airlines represented more than 15% of the industry, and signed a pledge to work towards SAF.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

In addition to SAF certification, the integrity of aviation biofuel producers and their products could be assessed by means such as [Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson)'s Carbon War Room,[^cite_note-100] or the Renewable Jet Fuels initiative.[^cite_note-101] The latter works with companies such as LanzaTech, SG Biofuels, AltAir, Solazyme, and Sapphire.[^cite_note-102]

Along with her co-authors, [Candelaria Bergero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candelaria_Bergero?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California)'s [Earth System Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_System_Science) Department stated that "main challenges to scaling up such sustainable fuel production include technology costs and process efficiencies", and widespread production would undermine [food security](216361.md) and [land use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use).[^cite_note-103]

## Environmental impact

Further information: [Environmental impact of aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_aviation) and [Biofuel § Greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel#Greenhouse_gas_emissions)

Plants absorb [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_Dioxide) as they grow, therefore plant-based biofuels emit only the same amount of [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) as they had previously absorbed. Biofuel production, processing, and transport, however, emit greenhouse gases, reducing the emissions savings.[^cite_note-Doliente2020-2] Biofuels with the most emission savings are those derived from photosynthetic algae (98% savings) although the technology is not developed, and those from [non-food crops and forest residues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-generation_biofuels) (91–95% savings).[^cite_note-Doliente2020-2]

[Jatropha oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatropha_oil), a non-food oil used as a biofuel, lowers CO<sub>2</sub> emissions by 50–80% compared to Jet-A1, a [kerosene](16992.md)-based fuel.[^cite_note-AImar2009-104] Jatropha, used for [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel), can thrive on [marginal land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_land) where most plants produce low [yields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_yield).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] A [life cycle assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_cycle_assessment) on jatropha estimated that biofuels could reduce greenhouse gas emissions by up to 85% if former agro-pastoral land is used, or increase emissions by up to 60% if natural woodland is converted.[^cite_note-107]

[Palm oil](57561.md) cultivation is constrained by scarce land resources and its expansion to forestland causes [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss), along with direct and indirect emissions due to [land-use change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_change).[^cite_note-Doliente2020-2] [Neste Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neste_Corporation)'s renewable products include a refining [residue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byproduct) of food-grade palm oil, the oily waste [skimmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_recovery) from the palm oil mill's [wastewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wastewater). Other Neste sources are [used cooking oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Used_cooking_oil) from [deep fryers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_fryer#Oil_filtration) and animal fats.[^cite_note-108] [Neste's sustainable aviation fuel](https://www.neste.com/en-us/products-and-innovation/sustainable-aviation/sustainable-aviation-fuel) is used by [Lufthansa](97733.md);[^cite_note-109] [Air France](14998012.md) and [KLM](16882.md) announced 2030 SAF targets in 2022[^cite_note-110] including multi-year purchase contracts totaling over 2.4 million tonnes of SAF from Neste, [TotalEnergies](804161.md), and DG Fuels.[^cite_note-111]

Aviation fuel from wet waste-derived feedstock ("VFA-SAF") provides an additional environmental benefit. Wet waste consists of waste from landfills, sludge from wastewater treatment plants, agricultural waste, greases, and fats. Wet waste can be converted to volatile fatty acids (VFA's), which then can be catalytically upgraded to SAF. Wet waste is a low-cost and plentiful feedstock, with the potential to replace 20% of US fossil jet fuel.[^cite_note-Huq-2021-76] This lessens the need to grow crops specifically for fuel, which in itself is energy intensive and increases CO<sub>2</sub> emissions throughout its life cycle. Wet waste feedstocks for SAF divert waste from landfills. Diversion has the potential to eliminate 17% of US methane emissions across all sectors. VFA-SAF's carbon footprint is 165% lower than fossil aviation fuel.[^cite_note-Huq-2021-76] This technology is in its infancy; although start-ups are working to make this a viable solution. Alder Renewables, BioVeritas, and ChainCraft are a few organizations committed to this.

NASA has determined that 50% aviation biofuel mixture can cut [particulate emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_emissions) caused by air traffic by 50–70%.[^cite_note-112] Biofuels do not contain sulfur compounds and thus do not emit [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide).[^cite_note-113] While, it may be true that the burning of biofuels do not emit sulfur compounds, some forms of production, such as [pyrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrolysis), can in fact produce sulfur compounds and other pollutants. Some potential pollutants that could be released are [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide) and different nitrogen compounds like [hydrogen cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_cyanide), [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia), and [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide).[^cite_note-114] There are other forms of biofuel production that may not have the same emissions.

Because of the scaling required to make aviation biofuel mainstream, the impact of land usage is a current hindrance to the growth of the biofuel industry. Potential solutions to this issue have begun to surface. For example, algae farms can produce a lot more biofuel per unit of area than crops.[^cite_note-115] Trials of using algae as biofuel were carried out by [Lufthansa](97733.md) and [Virgin Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Atlantic) as early as 2008, although there is little evidence that using algae is a reasonable source for jet biofuels.[^cite_note-116] By 2015, cultivation of [fatty acid methyl esters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid_methyl_ester) and [alkenones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkenone) from the algae, *Isochrysis*, was under research as a possible jet biofuel [feedstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedstock).[^cite_note-117]
