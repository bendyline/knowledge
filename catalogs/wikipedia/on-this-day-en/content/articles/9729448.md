In [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport), a **limited express** is a special [express train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_train) or [bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_bus) service that makes fewer stops (hence “limited”) than local, [limited-stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-stop), and regular express services along the same corridor. Limited expresses are typically the most direct service on a line, serving only major destinations such as [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_station) and [interchange stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_station), making them the fastest option for [inter-city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) travelers.

## Japan

See also: [Rail transport in Japan § Types and names of trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Japan#Types_and_names_of_trains)



[JR East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Japan_Railway_Company) limited express trains, including the Saphir Odoriko, [Narita Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_Express) (N'EX), [Odoriko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoriko).

The term "limited express" is a common translation of the Japanese compound noun *tokubetsu kyūkō* (特別急行); lit. 'special express', most often abbreviated *tokkyū* (特急), although some operators translate the word differently.

Limited express trains can be categorized into two types, [intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) and [commuter](6286.md), although railway companies rarely formalize this distinction. The former type of trains generally use long-distance coaches, better equipped than other ordinary express trains, and may include reserved seating, dining cars, food and beverage cars, and "green cars" (first-class cars). The latter type usually incurs no surcharge, but seating tends to be first-come, first-served, as this type of train uses commuter train coaches. Both types of trains travel faster and stop at fewer stations than "express" (急行 *kyūkō*) or "local" (普通 *futsū* or 各駅停車 *kaku-eki teisha*) services.

Until 1972, the *[Hikari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikari_\(train\))* on the [Tōkaidō Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_Shinkansen) was officially a *chōtokkyū* ([超特急](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%B6%85%E7%89%B9%E6%80%A5)) "super express" (lit. 'beyond limited express') and was priced higher than the *[Kodama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodama_\(train\))*, the limited express service on the same line. Presently, all [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen) services are officially limited expresses, but are referred to as "super express" in English.

## Australia

In Australia, particularly in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_rail_network), selective [commuter trains](6286.md) often skip smaller stations during [peak hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_hour), primarily for the purpose of more efficient delivery of passengers to [interchange stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_station), or higher-[patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage_\(transportation\)) stations. However, generally limited express trains only skip a small number of stations while express trains run non-stop on a large section of the line, contrary to Japan's definition; these trains are more properly [limited-stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-stop) services. In [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Rail_City_network), and [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSW_TrainLink), limited stop services are formed by commuter trains that run as limited stops or express services from the city centre to the edge of the suburban area and then as all stops in the interurban area (an example of such an express pattern can be seen on the [Gold Coast line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_railway_line)).

## New Zealand

In the era of [steam-hauled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive) provincial expresses, limited express services were common on high traffic routes. The *[Night Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Limited)* was the premier express train on the [North Island Main Trunk Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Island_Main_Trunk_Railway) between [Auckland](18660332.md), and [Wellington](33804.md) from 1924 until 1971; during peak seasons, it was augmented by the *[Daylight Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_Limited)*. Following the *Night Limited* was a slower unnamed express that stopped at more stations and provided a lower level of comfort.

A different pattern was employed on the [Main South Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_South_Line). The *[South Island Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Island_Limited)* express ran three days a week from [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch) through [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) to [Invercargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invercargill), with a slower regular express operating on the other four days. Both regular and limited expresses were augmented by additional services between intermediate destinations, such as an evening [railcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railcar) between Christchurch and Dunedin operated by NZR's [88 seater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZR_RM_class_\(88_seater\)), or [Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZR_RM_class_\(Vulcan\)) Class Railmotors.

Limited expresses were rare beyond the main trunk routes and the regular provincial expresses were typically augmented with even slower [mixed trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_train). However, when the *[Rotorua Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotorua_Express)* schedule was accelerated in 1930 and its carriages upgraded, it was re-branded as the *Rotorua Limited*. Due to the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) and rising car ownership levels, it did not achieve the level of success intended, thus in 1937 it reverted to the status and service pattern of a regular express.

After the demise of the *Night Limited* and the *South Island Limited* in 1971, the term "limited express" fell into disuse in New Zealand and has not been applied to any subsequent trains. However, from 1971 to 1979, the *[Silver Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Star_\(NZR_train\))* performed the role of a limited express as it operated to a faster schedule than a supplementary slower service that was known from 1975 as the *[Northerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northerner_\(train\))*.

As of 13 March 2017, [Auckland Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Transport) introduced limited express services on the [Onehunga Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onehunga_Line). These services operate until 7\:30 pm on weekdays, bypassing Parnell, Remuera and Greenlane stations.[^cite_note-2]

## Philippines

There were trains under the [Manila Railroad Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Railroad_Company) and the [Philippine National Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways) that were called "Limited Express" or simply "Limited", as well as *Special* and *Express* trains that nonetheless stopped at fewer stations.[^cite_note-3] The first of such services were introduced in the 1950s and peaked in the 1970s. They only stop at major stations along the two intercity main lines in [Luzon](69375.md) unlike regular expresses. They were given the highest priority, dedicated rolling stock, and the highest level of comfort and amenities.[^cite_note-Nikko-4]

The North Main Line had the *Ilocos Special* and the *Amianan Night Express* that ran between 1973 and 1984, then the fastest services in the PNR and stopped at fewer stations compared to the *Dagupan Express*. Meanwhile, the second [*Bicol Express*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNR_South_Main_Line#Bicol_Express), opened in 1954, only stopped at 8 out of 66 stations of the line between [Tutuban station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutuban_station) in downtown [Manila](184334.md) and [Legazpi station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi_station) in [Albay](253167.md).[^cite_note-5] Other examples on the South Main Line include the *Peñafrancia Express*,[^cite_note-Nikko-4] the *Isarog/Manila Limited*,[^cite_note-6] and the *Mayon Limited*, all of these were defunct by 2013 when all intercity rail was discontinued in favour of building a new line.

There are plans to revive the "limited express" trains. The [North–South Commuter Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Commuter_Railway) and the new South Main Line under the South Long Haul project will both feature "limited express" services. Both limited expresses will run on [standard-gauge track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) and will use dedicated rolling stock capable of running at speeds of up to 160 kilometers per hour (99 mph) or [higher-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-speed_rail).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-tg-8] For the North–South Commuter Railway (NSCR), an [airport express train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_rail_link) will connect [Clark International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport) with [Alabang station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabang_station) in [Muntinlupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntinlupa), skipping all the other stations in [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) and only stopping at four other stations in [Metro Manila](72340.md). In comparison, the "Commuter Express" services on the NSCR will serve more stations but will use the same [electric multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_multiple_unit) as the regular commuter service.

To the south of the NSCR, the *Bicol Express* flagship service of the [PNR South Long Haul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNR_South_Long_Haul) was originally proposed as a limited express service in 2018.[^cite_note-9] However, a new basic design report released in June 2021 reclassified the services into two groups: Local and Express. The new *Bicol Express* service has since been relegated to a regular [express train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_train) service.[^cite_note-10]

## United States

Some of the most elite trains in the United States in the twentieth century were called "limited", a name that typically graced overnight trains that made very few stops. (However, the fastest train between [New York](645042.md) and [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC), a day train, in the Pennsylvania Railroad era was called the *Congressional Limited Express*, and it had few stops, like the longer distance "Limiteds".)

Some limiteds of America have included:&#x20;

- the [Chicago, Milwaukee & St Paul Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Milwaukee_&_St._Paul_Railway)'s [Pioneer Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Limited_\(train\)) - one of the first and longest running named trains in the country

- the [New York Central Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Central_Railroad)'s *[20th Century Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Limited)* and its [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) successor, the *[Lake Shore Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Shore_Limited)*

- the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad)'s (and later, Amtrak) *[Broadway Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Limited)*

- the [New Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_New_Haven_and_Hartford_Railroad)'s (and later, Amtrak) *[Merchants Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchants_Limited)*

- the [Baltimore and Ohio Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_and_Ohio_Railroad)'s *[National Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Limited)*

- the [Rock Island Rail Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Island_Rail_Road)'s and the [Southern Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Railroad)'s *[Golden State Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Limited)*

- the [Santa Fe Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Railroad)'s *[Super Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Chief)*

- the [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad)'s *[City of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Los_Angeles_\(train\))*

- the Southern Pacific Railroad's [Sunset Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Limited)

- the B\&O's [Capitol Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Limited_\(B\&O_train\))

From September 23, 1978, to April 15, 1990, the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) operated a limited express premium-fare subway service from Manhattan to [JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFK_Airport), which was called the [JFK Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFK_Express). The subway service made express stops at subway stations in Manhattan and [one subway station in Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Street_%E2%80%93_MetroTech_\(New_York_City_Subway\)), before running nonstop to [Howard Beach-JFK Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Beach-JFK_Airport_\(IND_Rockaway_Line\)), where transfers to free airport shuttle buses were provided. The JFK Express proved to be unsuccessful, seeing low ridership in part because the service did not actually serve any airline terminals.

Some commuter railroads operate express trains making limited stops. The [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) operates some [rush hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_hour) trains that run 50 miles between the central city station and the first stop of its express route itinerary, for instance, on the [Ronkonkoma Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronkonkoma_Branch).[^cite_note-11] The [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad) runs some rush hour trains that run 29 miles between the central city and the first stop of their route itinerary on the [Hudson Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Line_\(Metro-North\)) and the [New Haven Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven_Line).

Due to the wide availability of service on the *[Northeast Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional)* and *[Acela Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express),* most of Amtrak's medium- and long-distance trains operating along the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) only stop to discharge passengers from [Washington Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Union_Station) (or in some cases, [Alexandria Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Union_Station)) northward, and to receive passengers from [Newark Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Penn_Station) southward.
