**Winnipeg** ([/ˈwɪnɪpɛɡ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c8/Winnipeg.ogg/Winnipeg.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c8/Winnipeg.ogg/Winnipeg.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the capital and largest city of the [Canadian province](75763.md) of [Manitoba](18926.md). It is centred on the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the [Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North) and [Assiniboine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine_River) rivers. As of 2021, Winnipeg had a city population of 749,607 and a metropolitan population of 834,678, making it Canada's [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_municipalities_in_Canada_by_population) city and [eighth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_census_metropolitan_areas_and_agglomerations_in_Canada) metropolitan area.[^cite_note-2021Metrocensus-7] In 2025, the city's estimated population reached 850,260.[^cite_note-11]

The city is named after the nearby [Lake Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnipeg); the name "Winnipeg" comes from the [Western Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language) words for "muddy water" – *winipīhk*. The region was a trading centre for [Indigenous peoples](196075.md) long before the [arrival of Europeans;](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) it is the traditional territory of the [Anishinaabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anishinaabe) (Ojibway), Ininew ([Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree)), [Oji-Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oji-Cree), [Dene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dene), and [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people), and is the birthplace of the [Métis Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis_people_in_Canada).[^cite_note-12] French traders built the first fort, [Fort Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rouge_\(fortification\)), on the site in 1738. A settlement was later founded by the [Selkirk settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Colony) of the Red River Colony in 1812, the nucleus of which was incorporated as the "City of Winnipeg" in 1873. Being far inland, [the city's climate](#Climate) is extremely seasonal (continental) even by Canadian standards, with average January highs of around −11 °C (12 °F) and average July highs of 26 °C (79 °F).

Known as the "Gateway to the West", Winnipeg is a railway and transportation hub with a diversified economy. It hosts numerous annual festivals, including the *[Festival du Voyageur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_du_Voyageur)*, the [Winnipeg Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Folk_Festival), the [Jazz Winnipeg Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_Winnipeg_Festival), the [Winnipeg Fringe Theatre Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Fringe_Theatre_Festival), and [Folklorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklorama). Winnipeg was the first Canadian host of the [Pan American Games in 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Pan_American_Games). It is home to several professional sports franchises, including the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers) (Canadian football), [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) (ice hockey), [Manitoba Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Moose) (ice hockey), [Winnipeg Sea Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Sea_Bears) (basketball), and the [Winnipeg Goldeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Goldeyes) (baseball).

## Etymology

Winnipeg is named after nearby [Lake Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnipeg), 65 kilometres (40 mi) north of the city. English explorer [Henry Kelsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kelsey) may have been the first European to see the lake in 1690. He adopted the [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language) and [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) name, *win-nipi* (also transcribed *win-nipiy* or *ouenpig*), meaning "murky water" or "muddy water"[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-boyens-15] (modern [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language): *wīnipēk*, ᐑᓂᐯᐠ). French-Canadian fur trader [La Vérendrye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gaultier_de_Varennes,_sieur_de_La_V%C3%A9rendrye) referred to the lake as *Lac Gouinipique* or *Ouinipigon* when he built the first forts in the area in the 1730s.[^cite_note-16] Local newspaper *The Nor'-Wester* included the name on its masthead on 24 February 1866, and the city was incorporated by that name by the [Manitoba Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Legislature) in 1873.[^cite_note-boyens-15]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Winnipeg history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Winnipeg_history).

### Early history

Winnipeg lies at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the [Assiniboine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine_River) and the [Red River of the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley), a location now known as "[The Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forks,_Winnipeg)". This point was at the crossroads of [canoe](99960.md) routes travelled by [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) before European contact.[^cite_note-forkshistory-17] Evidence provided by archaeology, [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph), [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art), and oral history indicates that native peoples used the area in prehistoric times for camping, harvesting, hunting, tool making, fishing, trading and, farther north, for agriculture.[^cite_note-harris-18]

Estimates of the date of first settlement in the area range from 11,500 years ago for a site southwest of the present city to 6,000 years ago at the Forks.[^cite_note-ce-19][^cite_note-20] In 1805, Canadian colonists observed First Nations peoples engaged in farming activity along the Red River. The practice quickly expanded, driven by the demand by traders for provisions.[^cite_note-21] The rivers provided an extensive transportation network linking northern First Peoples with those to the south along the [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) and [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) rivers. The [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe) made some of the first maps on [birch bark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch_bark), which helped [fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) navigate the waterways of the area.[^cite_note-22]

[Sieur de La Vérendrye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gaultier_de_Varennes,_sieur_de_La_V%C3%A9rendrye) built the first [fur-trading post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_post) on the site in 1738, called [Fort Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rouge_\(fortification\)).[^cite_note-23] French trading continued at the site for several decades. The British [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) took over when France ceded the territory following its defeat in the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War).[^cite_note-24] Many French men who were trappers [married First Nations women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_'%C3%A0_la_fa%C3%A7on_du_pays'); their mixed-race children hunted, traded, and lived in the area. Their descendants are known as the [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis_people_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-25]



An 1821 painting of winter fishing on the ice of the [Assiniboine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine_River) and [Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North) rivers. [Fort Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Gibraltar) was erected in 1809.

[Lord Selkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Douglas,_5th_Earl_of_Selkirk) was involved with the first permanent settlement (known as the [Red River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Colony)), the purchase of land from the Hudson's Bay Company, and a survey of river lots in the early 19th century.[^cite_note-26] The [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company) built [Fort Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Gibraltar) in 1809, and the Hudson's Bay Company built [Fort Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Douglas_\(Canada\)) in 1812, both in the area of present-day Winnipeg.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-27] The two companies competed fiercely over trade.[^cite_note-28] The Métis and Lord Selkirk's settlers fought at the [Battle of Seven Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Seven_Oaks) in 1816. In 1821, the Hudson's Bay and North West Companies merged, ending their long rivalry.[^cite_note-29] Fort Gibraltar was renamed [Fort Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Garry) in 1822 and became the leading post in the region for the Hudson's Bay Company.[^cite_note-timeline-30] A flood destroyed the fort in 1826 and it was not rebuilt until 1835.[^cite_note-timeline-30] A rebuilt section of the fort, consisting of the front gate and a section of the wall, is near the modern-day corner of Main Street and Broadway in downtown Winnipeg.[^cite_note-31]

In 1869–70, present-day Winnipeg was the site of the [Red River Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Rebellion), a conflict between the local provisional government of Métis, led by [Louis Riel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Riel), and newcomers from eastern Canada. General [Garnet Wolseley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet_Wolseley,_1st_Viscount_Wolseley) was sent to suppress the uprising. The [Manitoba Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Act) of 1870 made Manitoba the [fifth province](75763.md) of the three-year-old [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] [Treaty 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_1), which encompassed the city and much of the surrounding area, was signed on 3 August 1871 by representatives of the Crown and local Indigenous groups, comprising the Brokenhead Ojibway, Sagkeeng, Long Plain, Peguis, Roseau River Anishinabe, Sandy Bay and Swan Lake communities.[^cite_note-35] On 8 November 1873, Winnipeg was incorporated as a city, with the Selkirk settlement as its nucleus.[^cite_note-Winnipeg's_History-36] Métis legislator and interpreter [James McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_McKay_\(fur_trader\)) named the city.[^cite_note-mckaysidea-37] Winnipeg's mandate was to govern and provide municipal services to citizens attracted to trade expansion between [Upper Fort Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Garry) / [Lower Fort Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Fort_Garry) and [Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-\:0-38]

Winnipeg developed rapidly after the coming of the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway) in 1881.[^cite_note-39] The railway divided the North End, which housed mainly Eastern Europeans, from the richer Anglo-Saxon southern part of the city.[^cite_note-ce-19] It also contributed to a demographic shift beginning shortly after Confederation that saw the francophone population decrease from a majority to a small minority group. This shift resulted in Premier [Thomas Greenway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Greenway) controversially ending legislative bilingualism and [removing funding for French Catholic Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Schools_Question) in 1890.[^cite_note-40]

### Modern history (1900–present)



Crowd gathered outside old City Hall during the [Winnipeg General Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_General_Strike) in 1919

By 1911, Winnipeg was Canada's third-largest city.[^cite_note-ce-19] However, the city faced financial difficulty when the [Panama Canal](24849.md) opened in 1914.[^cite_note-41] The canal reduced reliance on Canada's rail system for international trade; the increase in shipping traffic helped [Vancouver](32706.md) to surpass Winnipeg in both prosperity and population by the end of [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-42]

More than 30,000 workers walked off their jobs in May 1919 in what came to be known as the [Winnipeg general strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_general_strike).[^cite_note-43] The strike was a product of postwar recession, labour conditions, the activity of union organizers and a large influx of returning World War I soldiers seeking work.[^cite_note-44] After many arrests, deportations, and incidents of violence, the strike ended on 21 June 1919 when the [Riot Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Act) was read. A group of [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) officers charged a group of strikers.[^cite_note-bloody-45] Two strikers were killed and at least thirty others were injured on the day that became known as *Bloody Saturday*; the event polarized the population.[^cite_note-bloody-45] One of the leaders of the strike, [J. S. Woodsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._S._Woodsworth), went on to found Canada's first major socialist party, the [Co-operative Commonwealth Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-operative_Commonwealth_Federation), which later became the [New Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democratic_Party_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-46]

The [Manitoba Legislative Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Legislative_Building), constructed mainly of [Tyndall stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyndall_stone), opened in 1920; its dome supports a bronze statue finished in gold leaf, titled "Eternal Youth and the Spirit of Enterprise" (commonly known as the "[Golden Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Boy_\(Manitoba\))").[^cite_note-History-47] [Proportional voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_voting) was also introduced in 1920, and used provincially until 1957.[^cite_note-48] The [stock market crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_crash_of_1929) and the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) resulted in widespread unemployment, worsened by drought and low agricultural prices.[^cite_note-49] The Depression ended after the start of [World War II](32927.md) in 1939.[^cite_note-ce-19]



In 1942, the Canadian Victory Loan campaign [simulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_Day) a [Nazi](21212.md) occupation of the city to raise [war bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_bonds).

In the [Battle of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hong_Kong), [The Winnipeg Grenadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Winnipeg_Grenadiers) were among the first Canadians to engage in combat against Japanese military forces. Battalion members who survived combat were taken prisoner and endured brutal treatment in [prisoner of war](25008.md) camps.[^cite_note-Vance1994-50] In 1942, the Victory Loan Campaign staged a [mock Nazi invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_Day) of Winnipeg to promote awareness of the stakes of the war in Europe.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] When the war ended, pent-up demand generated a boom in housing development, although building activity was checked by the [1950 Red River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Red_River_flood).[^cite_note-flood-53] The federal government estimated damage at over \$26 million, although the province indicated that it was at least double that.[^cite_note-54] The damage caused by the flood led then-Premier [Duff Roblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dufferin_Roblin) to advocate for the construction of the [Red River Floodway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Floodway).[^cite_note-55]

Before 1972, Winnipeg was the largest of thirteen cities and towns in a metropolitan area around the Red and Assiniboine Rivers. In 1960, the Metropolitan Corporation of Greater Winnipeg was established to co-ordinate service delivery in the metropolitan region.[^cite_note-\:0-38] A consolidated metropolitan "[unicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamation_of_Winnipeg)" government incorporating Winnipeg and its surrounding municipalities was established on 27 July 1971, taking effect in 1972.[^cite_note-unicity-56] The [City of Winnipeg Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Winnipeg_Act) incorporated the current city.[^cite_note-ce-19] In 2003, the City of Winnipeg Act was repealed and replaced with the City of Winnipeg Charter.[^cite_note-\:0-38]

Winnipeg experienced a severe economic downturn in advance of the [early 1980s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1980s_recession), during which the city incurred closures of prominent businesses, including the *[Winnipeg Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Tribune)*, as well as the [Swift's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_&_Company) and [Canada Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Packers) meat packing plants.[^cite_note-57] In 1981, Winnipeg was one of the first cities in Canada to sign a tripartite agreement with the provincial and federal governments to redevelop its downtown area,[^cite_note-SanctonYoung2009-58] and the three levels of government contributed over \$271 million to its development.[^cite_note-59] In 1989, the reclamation and redevelopment of the [CNR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Railway) [rail yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_yard) turned the Forks into Winnipeg's most popular tourist attraction.[^cite_note-forkshistory-17][^cite_note-ce-19] The city was threatened by the [1997 Red River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Red_River_flood) as well as further floods in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Red_River_flood) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Red_River_flood).[^cite_note-60]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography and climate of Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_and_climate_of_Winnipeg)



Docks at [the Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forks,_Winnipeg) following heavy rainfall. The city lies at the bottom of the [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley), a flood plain with a flat topography.

Winnipeg lies at the bottom of the [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley), a flood plain with an extremely flat topography.[^cite_note-61] It is on the eastern edge of the [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies) in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) and is known as the "Gateway to the West".[^cite_note-ce-19] Winnipeg is bordered by [tallgrass prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallgrass_prairie) to the west and south and the [aspen parkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen_parkland) to the northeast, although most of the native prairie grasses have been removed for agriculture and urbanization.[^cite_note-62] It is relatively close to many large [Canadian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield) lakes and parks, as well as [Lake Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnipeg) ([the Earth's 11th largest freshwater lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_by_area)).[^cite_note-63] Winnipeg has North America's largest extant mature urban elm forest.[^cite_note-64] The city has an area of 464.08 square kilometres (179.18 sq mi).[^cite_note-area-3]

Winnipeg has four major rivers: the [Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North), [Assiniboine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine_River), [La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Salle_River) and [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_River_\(Manitoba\)).[^cite_note-65] The city was subject to severe flooding in the past. The Red River reached its greatest flood height in [1826](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1826_Red_River_flood). Another large flood in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Red_River_flood) caused millions of dollars in damage and mass evacuations.[^cite_note-66] This flood prompted [Duff Roblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dufferin_Roblin)'s provincial government to build the [Red River Floodway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Floodway) to protect the city.[^cite_note-ce-19] In the [1997 flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Red_River_flood), flood control dikes were reinforced and raised using sandbags; Winnipeg suffered limited damage compared to the flood's impact on cities without such structures, such as [Grand Forks, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks,_North_Dakota).[^cite_note-67] The generally flat terrain and the poor drainage of the Red River Valley's clay-based soil also results in many [mosquitoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito) during wetter years.[^cite_note-68]

### Climate



Winters in Winnipeg are cold with little precipitation.

Winnipeg's location in the Canadian Prairies gives it a warm-summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate)[^cite_note-69] ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfb*),[^cite_note-70] with warm, humid summers, and long, severely cold winters. Summers have a July mean average of 19.7 °C (67.5 °F).[^cite_note-normals-5] Winters are the coldest time of year, with the January mean average around −16.4 °C (2.5 °F) and total winter precipitation (December through February) averaging 55.2 millimetres (2.17 in).[^cite_note-normals-5] Temperatures occasionally drop below −40 °C (−40 °F).[^cite_note-normals-5]

On average, there are 317.8 days per year with measurable sunshine, with July seeing the most.[^cite_note-Canadian_Climate_Normals_1971-2000-71] With 2,353 hours of sunshine per year, Winnipeg is the second-sunniest city in Canada.[^cite_note-72] Total annual precipitation (both rain and snow) is just over 521 millimetres (20.5 in).[^cite_note-normals-5] Thunderstorms are very common during summer and sometimes severe enough to produce tornadoes.[^cite_note-storms-73] Low [wind chill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_chill) values are a common occurrence in the local climate. The wind chill has gone down as low as −57.1 °C (−70.8 °F), and on average twelve days of the year reach a wind chill below −40 °C (−40 °F).[^cite_note-normals-5] Deaths have been recorded relating to the extreme cold.[^cite_note-74]

The highest temperature ever recorded in Winnipeg was during the [1936 North American heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_North_American_heat_wave). The temperature reached 42.2 °C (108.0 °F) on 11 July 1936 while the highest minimum temperature, recorded on the following day, 12 July 1936, was 28.3 °C (82.9 °F).[^cite_note-July_1936-75] The [apparent heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_temperature) can be even more extreme due to bursts of humidity, and on 12 July 2026 a [humidex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidex) reading of 48.0 °C (118.4 °F) was measured.[^cite_note-76]

The frost-free season is comparatively long for a location with such severe winters. The last spring frost is on average around 23 May, while the first fall frost is on 22 September.[^cite_note-normals-5]

### Cityscape

See also: [List of Winnipeg neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Winnipeg_neighbourhoods), [List of tallest buildings in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Winnipeg), and [Subdivisions of Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Winnipeg)



Centred on the intersection of [Portage and Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage_and_Main), [Downtown Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Winnipeg) is the city's [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district).



The [Canadian Museum for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Museum_for_Human_Rights) and [downtown Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Winnipeg) skyline at night, viewed from [the Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forks,_Winnipeg).

There are officially 236 neighbourhoods in Winnipeg.[^cite_note-83] [Downtown Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Winnipeg), the city's financial heart and economic core, is centred on the intersection of [Portage Avenue and Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage_and_Main) and covers about 2.6 square kilometres (1.0 sq mi). More than 72,000 people work downtown, and over 40,000 students attend classes at its universities and colleges.[^cite_note-BIZ-84]

Downtown Winnipeg's [Exchange District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_District) is named after the area's original grain exchange, which operated from 1880 to 1913.[^cite_note-BIZ-84] The 30-block district received [National Historic Site of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Sites_of_Canada) status in 1997; it includes North America's most extensive collection of early 20th-century [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta) and cut stone architecture, [Stephen Juba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Juba) Park, and Old Market Square.[^cite_note-BIZ-84] Other major downtown areas are [the Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forks,_Winnipeg), [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park_\(Winnipeg\)), Broadway-Assiniboine and [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Winnipeg). Many of Downtown Winnipeg's major buildings are linked with the [Winnipeg Walkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Walkway).[^cite_note-85] Residential neighbourhoods surround the downtown in all directions; expansion is greatest to the south and west, although several areas remain underdeveloped.[^cite_note-86] The city's largest park, [Assiniboine Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine_Park), houses the [Assiniboine Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine_Park_Zoo) and the [Leo Mol Sculpture Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Mol).[^cite_note-87] Other large city parks include [Kildonan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildonan_Park) and [St. Vital Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vital_Park). The city's major commercial areas are [Polo Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo_Park), Kildonan Crossing, South St. Vital, Garden City (West Kildonan), Pembina Strip, Kenaston Smart Centre, and [Osborne Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborne_Village).[^cite_note-88] The main cultural and nightlife areas are the Exchange District, the Forks, Osborne Village and Corydon Village (both in Fort Rouge), Sargent and Ellice Avenues (West End) and Old St. Boniface.[^cite_note-89] [Osborne Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborne_Village) is Winnipeg's most densely populated neighbourhood[^cite_note-90] and one of the most densely populated neighbourhoods in Western Canada.[^cite_note-91]

In the [2021 Census of Population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census) conducted by [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada), Winnipeg had a population of 749,607 living in 300,431 of its 315,465 total private dwellings, a change of 6.3% from its 2016 population of 705,244. With a land area of 461.78 km<sup>2</sup> (178.29 sq mi), it had a population density of 1,623.3/km<sup>2</sup> (4,204.3/sq mi) in 2021.[^cite_note-2021census-100] As of the 2021 census, 16.6 percent of residents were 14 years old or younger, 66.4 percent were between 15 and 64 years old, and 17.0 percent were 65 or over. The average age of a Winnipegger was 40.3.[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6]

At the [census metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_metropolitan_area) (CMA) level in the 2021 census, the Winnipeg CMA had a population of 834,678 living in 330,326 of its 347,144 total private dwellings, a change of 6.6% from its 2016 population of 783,099. With a land area of 5,285.46 km<sup>2</sup> (2,040.73 sq mi), it had a population density of 157.9/km<sup>2</sup> (409.0/sq mi) in 2021.[^cite_note-2021censusCMA-101]

In 2021 Winnipeg represented 54.9% of the population of the province of Manitoba, the highest population concentration in one city of any province in Canada.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] Apart from the city of Winnipeg, the Winnipeg CMA includes the [rural municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rural_municipalities_in_Manitoba) of [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_Springfield), [St. Clements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_St._Clements), [Taché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_Tach%C3%A9), [East St. Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_East_St._Paul), [Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_Macdonald), [Ritchot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_Ritchot), [West St. Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_West_St._Paul), [Headingley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_Headingley), the [Brokenhead 4 reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokenhead_4,_Manitoba), [Rosser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_Rosser) and [St. François Xavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Municipality_of_St._Fran%C3%A7ois_Xavier).[^cite_note-104] [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada)'s estimate of the Winnipeg CMA population as of 1 July 2020 is 850,056, making it the 7th largest CMA in Canada.[^cite_note-105]

Winnipeg has a significant and increasing [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_peoples_in_Canada) population, with both the highest percentage of Indigenous peoples (12.4%) for any major Canadian city, and the highest total number of Indigenous peoples (90,995) for any single non-reserve municipality.[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6] The Indigenous population grew by 22% between 2001 and 2006, compared to an increase of 3% for the city as a whole; this population tends to be younger and less wealthy than non-Indigenous residents.[^cite_note-uaps-106] Winnipeg also has the highest Métis population in both percentage (6.5%) and numbers (47,915);[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6] the growth rate for this population between 2001 and 2006 was 30%.[^cite_note-uaps-106]

The 2021 census reported that [immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada) comprised 201,040 persons or 27.3% of the total population of Winnipeg. Of the total immigrant population, the top countries of origin were the Philippines (62,100 persons or 30.9%), India (27,605 persons or 13.7%), and China (8,900 persons or 4.4%).[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6] The city receives over 10,000 net international immigrants per year.[^cite_note-ctr-107] Winnipeg has the greatest percentage of Filipino residents (11.3%) of any major Canadian city, although [Toronto](64646.md) has more Filipinos by total population.[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6] As of 2021, 34% of residents were of a [visible minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_minority).[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6]

More than a hundred languages are spoken in Winnipeg, of which the most common is English: 65 percent of Winnipeggers speak English as their native language (of Canada's official languages, English and French, 95 percent learned English first), and 2.8 percent have a first language of French.[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6] The [St. Boniface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Boniface,_Winnipeg) neighbourhood of Winnipeg is home to the largest Francophone community in Western Canada, where 25 percent of local residents can speak French.[^cite_note-108] Other languages spoken as a [mother tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_tongue) in Winnipeg include [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) (6.0%), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (4.1%), and [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_language) (1.5%). Several Indigenous languages are also spoken, such as [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) (0.2%) and [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language) (0.1%).[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6]

The [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_census_\(Canada\)) reported the religious make-up of Winnipeg as: 50.4% [Christian](18337522.md), including 24.0% [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic), 4.0% [United Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Canada), and 2.7% [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican); 4.4% [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh); 3.3% [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim); 2.0% [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu); 1.5% [Jewish](25955086.md); 0.9% [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist); 0.4% [traditional (aboriginal) spirituality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_religion); 0.7% other; and 36.4% no religious affiliation.[^cite_note-2021CityCensus-6]

## Economy

See also: [List of corporations based in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_corporations_based_in_Winnipeg)



Manitoba Hydro operates out of Manitoba Hydro Place in Winnipeg.

Winnipeg is an economic base and regional centre. It has a diversified economy, with major employment in the health care and social assistance (14%), retail (11%), manufacturing (8%), and public administration (8%) sectors.[^cite_note-ecprofile-109] There were approximately 450,500 jobs in the city as of 2019.[^cite_note-ecprofile-109] Some of Winnipeg's largest employers are government and government-funded institutions, including the Province of Manitoba, the University of Manitoba, the City of Winnipeg, [Manitoba Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Hydro), and [Manitoba Liquor & Lotteries Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Liquor_&_Lotteries_Corporation). Major private-sector employers include Canad Corporation of Manitoba, [Canada Life Assurance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great-West_Life_Assurance_Company), [StandardAero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StandardAero), and [SkipTheDishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkipTheDishes).[^cite_note-110] The [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) for the census metropolitan area was [CA\$](101846.md)45.0 billion as of 2020.[^cite_note-gdp2020-111] The city had an unemployment rate of 5.3% in 2019, compared to a national rate of 5.7%. Household income per capita was \$47,824, compared to \$49,744 nationally.[^cite_note-indicators-112] The [Royal Canadian Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Mint), established in 1976, produces all circulating coinage in Canada.[^cite_note-113] The facility, located in southeastern Winnipeg, also produces coins for many other countries.[^cite_note-114]

In 2012, Winnipeg was ranked by [KPMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPMG) as the least expensive location to do business in western Canada.[^cite_note-115] Like many prairie cities, Winnipeg has a relatively low cost of living.[^cite_note-116] The average house price in Winnipeg was \$301,518 as of 2018.[^cite_note-indicators-112] As of May 2014, the [Consumer Price Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Price_Index) was 125.8 relative to 2002 prices, reflecting consumer costs at the Canadian average.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

## Culture

Main article: [Winnipeg arts and culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_arts_and_culture)

See also: [List of people from Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Winnipeg), [Category\:Museums in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Museums_in_Winnipeg), [Category\:Theatre companies in Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Theatre_companies_in_Manitoba), [List of TV and films shot in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_TV_and_films_shot_in_Winnipeg), and [List of Winnipeg musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Winnipeg_musicians)



The [Esplanade Riel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esplanade_Riel) is a landmark and pedestrian bridge in the city. It connects downtown Winnipeg with the [St. Boniface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Boniface,_Winnipeg) neighbourhood.

Winnipeg was named the Cultural Capital of Canada in 2010 by Canadian Heritage.[^cite_note-119] As of 2021, there are 26 [National Historic Sites of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Historic_Sites_of_Canada_in_Manitoba) in Winnipeg.[^cite_note-120] One of these, [The Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forks,_Winnipeg), attracts four million visitors a year.[^cite_note-forks-121] It is home to the [City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_\(TV_network\)) television studio, the [Nestaweya River Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestaweya_River_Trail), [Manitoba Theatre for Young People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Theatre_for_Young_People), the [Winnipeg International Children's Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_International_Children's_Festival), and the [Manitoba Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Children's_Museum). It also features a 2,800-square-metre (30,000 sq ft) skate plaza, a 790-square-metre (8,500 sq ft) bowl complex, which features a mural of Winnipeg skateboarding pioneer Jai Pereira, the [Esplanade Riel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esplanade_Riel) bridge,[^cite_note-122] a river walkway, [Shaw Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Park), and the [Canadian Museum for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Museum_for_Human_Rights).[^cite_note-forks-121] The [Winnipeg Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Public_Library) is a public library network with 20 branches throughout the city, including the main [Millennium Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Library_\(Winnipeg\)).[^cite_note-123]

[Winnipeg the Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_the_Bear), which would become the inspiration for part of the name of [Winnie-the-Pooh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnie-the-Pooh), was purchased in Ontario by Lieutenant [Harry Colebourn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Colebourn) of [the Fort Garry Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fort_Garry_Horse). He named the bear after the regiment's hometown of Winnipeg.[^cite_note-124] [A. A. Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._A._Milne) later wrote a series of books featuring the fictional Winnie-the-Pooh. The series' illustrator, [Ernest H. Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Shepard) created the only known oil painting of Winnipeg's adopted fictional bear, which is displayed in Assiniboine Park.[^cite_note-shepard-125]

The city has developed many distinct dishes and cooking styles, notably in the areas of [confectionery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confectionery) and [hot-smoked fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-smoking). Both the First Nations and more recent Eastern Canadian, European, and Asian immigrants have helped shape Winnipeg's dining scene, giving birth to dishes such as the desserts [schmoo torte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmoo_torte) and [wafer pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flapper_pie).[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

The [Winnipeg Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Art_Gallery) is Western Canada's oldest public art gallery, founded in 1912. It is the sixth-largest in the country[^cite_note-128] and includes the world's largest public collection of contemporary Inuit art.[^cite_note-ce-19][^cite_note-129] Since the late 1970s Winnipeg has also had an active [artist run centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_artist-run_centres) culture.[^cite_note-130]



[Centennial Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Concert_Hall) is a [performing arts centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts_centre) that is home to the [Manitoba Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Opera), [Royal Winnipeg Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Winnipeg_Ballet), and the [Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Symphony_Orchestra).

Winnipeg's three largest performing arts venues, the [Centennial Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Concert_Hall), [Royal Manitoba Theatre Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Manitoba_Theatre_Centre) and the [Pantages Playhouse Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantages_Playhouse_Theatre), are downtown. The Royal Manitoba is Canada's oldest English-language regional theatre, with over 250 performances yearly.[^cite_note-131] The Pantages Playhouse Theatre opened as a [vaudeville house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville#Architecture) in 1913.[^cite_note-132] Other city theatres include the [Burton Cummings Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Cummings_Theatre) (a National Historic Site of Canada built in 1906[^cite_note-133]) and [Prairie Theatre Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Theatre_Exchange). [Le Cercle Molière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Cercle_Moli%C3%A8re), based in St Boniface, is Canada's oldest theatre company, founded in 1925.[^cite_note-134] [Rainbow Stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Stage) is a musical theatre production company based in [Kildonan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildonan_Park) that produces professional, live Broadway musical shows and is Canada's longest-surviving outdoor theatre.[^cite_note-ce-19][^cite_note-135] The [Manitoba Theatre for Young People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Theatre_for_Young_People) at the Forks is one of only two Theatres for Young Audiences in Canada with a permanent residence and the only Theatre for Young Audiences that offers a full season of plays for teenagers.[^cite_note-136] The [Winnipeg Jewish Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jewish_Theatre) is the only professional theatre in Canada dedicated to Jewish themes.[^cite_note-137] [Shakespeare in the Ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare_in_the_Ruins) (SIR) presents adaptations of [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare) plays.[^cite_note-138]

Winnipeg has hosted numerous [Hollywood](418334.md) productions: *[Shall We Dance?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shall_We_Dance%3F_\(2004_film\))* (2004), *[Capote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capote_\(film\))* (2005), *[The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Assassination_of_Jesse_James_by_the_Coward_Robert_Ford)* (2007), and *[A Dog's Purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dog's_Purpose_\(film\))* (2017), among others were filmed in the city.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] The [Winnipeg Film Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Film_Group) has produced numerous award-winning films.[^cite_note-141] There are several TV and film production companies in Winnipeg: the most prominent are [Farpoint Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farpoint_Films), [Frantic Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frantic_Films), [Buffalo Gal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Gal_Pictures), and [Les Productions Rivard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Productions_Rivard).[^cite_note-142] [Guy Maddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Maddin)'s *[My Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Winnipeg)*, an independent film released in 2008, is a comedic rumination on the city's history.[^cite_note-143]



Located in Winnipeg, the [Canadian Museum for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Museum_for_Human_Rights) is a [national museum of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_museums_of_Canada).

The [Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Symphony_Orchestra) is the largest and oldest professional musical ensemble in Winnipeg.[^cite_note-144] The [Manitoba Chamber Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Chamber_Orchestra) runs a series of chamber orchestral concerts each year.[^cite_note-145] [Manitoba Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Opera) is Manitoba's only full-time professional opera company.[^cite_note-146] Among the most notable musical acts associated with Winnipeg are [Bachman–Turner Overdrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachman%E2%80%93Turner_Overdrive),[^cite_note-juno-147] [The Guess Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guess_Who),[^cite_note-148] [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young),[^cite_note-149] [The Weakerthans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weakerthans),[^cite_note-150] the [Crash Test Dummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_Test_Dummies),[^cite_note-151] [Propagandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propagandhi),[^cite_note-152] [Bif Naked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bif_Naked),[^cite_note-153] and [The Watchmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Watchmen_\(band\))[^cite_note-154] among many others.[^cite_note-juno-147] Winnipeg also has a significant place in Canadian jazz history, being the location of Canada's first jazz concert in 1914 at the [Pantages Playhouse Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantages_Playhouse_Theatre).[^cite_note-155]

The [Royal Winnipeg Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Winnipeg_Ballet) (RWB) is Canada's oldest [ballet company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_company) and the longest continuously operating ballet company in North America. It was the first organization to be granted a [royal title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter) by [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) and has included notable dancers such as [Evelyn Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Hart) and [Mikhail Baryshnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Baryshnikov). The RWB also runs a full-time classical dance school.[^cite_note-rwb-156]

The [Manitoba Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Museum), the city's largest museum, depicts the history of the city and province. The full-size replica of the ship [*Nonsuch*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsuch_\(1650_ship\)) is the museum's showcase piece.[^cite_note-157] The [Manitoba Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Children's_Museum) is a nonprofit [children's museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_museum) at the Forks that features twelve permanent galleries.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] The [Canadian Museum for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Museum_for_Human_Rights) is the only Canadian [national museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_museum) for human rights and the only national museum west of Ottawa.[^cite_note-160] The federal government contributed \$100 million towards the estimated \$311 million project.[^cite_note-161] Construction of the museum began on 1 April 2008,[^cite_note-162] and the museum opened to the public 27 September 2014.[^cite_note-163]

The [Royal Aviation Museum of Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aviation_Museum_of_Western_Canada), near Winnipeg's [James Richardson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_James_Armstrong_Richardson_International_Airport), features military jets, commercial aircraft, Canada's first helicopter, the "flying saucer" [Avrocar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avrocar), flight simulators, and a [Black Brant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Brant_\(rocket\)) rocket built in Manitoba by [Bristol Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Aerospace).[^cite_note-McCaffery2000-164] The [Winnipeg Railway Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Railway_Museum) at Via Rail Station has a variety of locomotives, notably the *[Countess of Dufferin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countess_of_Dufferin)*, the first [steam locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive) in Western Canada.[^cite_note-The_Winnipeg_Railway_Museum-165]

### Sports

Main article: [Sport in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Winnipeg)



[Canada Life Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Life_Centre) is an indoor arena in downtown Winnipeg. It is the home arena of the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) ([NHL](21809.md)) and the [Manitoba Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Moose) ([AHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League)).



Marchers at the [2019 Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Grey_Cup) parade for the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers)

Winnipeg has been home to several professional [hockey](14790.md) teams. The [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) have called the city home since 2011.[^cite_note-172] The original [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets_\(1972%E2%80%931996\)), the city's former NHL team, left for [Phoenix, Arizona](49121.md), after the 1995–96 season due to mounting financial troubles, despite a campaign effort to "Save the Jets."[^cite_note-173] The Jets play at [Canada Life Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Life_Centre), which is ranked the world's 19th-busiest arena among non-sporting touring events, 13th-busiest among facilities in North America, and 3rd-busiest in Canada as of 2009.[^cite_note-174]

Past hockey teams based in Winnipeg include the [Winnipeg Maroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Maroons_\(ice_hockey\)), [Winnipeg Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Warriors), three-time Stanley Cup Champion [Winnipeg Victorias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Victorias) and the [Winnipeg Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Falcons), who were the gold medalists representing Canada at the [1920 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) in Antwerp, Belgium. Another professional ice hockey team in Winnipeg is the [Manitoba Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Moose), the American Hockey League primary affiliate of the Winnipeg Jets that the same group owns.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] On the international stage, Winnipeg has hosted national and world hockey championships on a number of occasions, most notably the [1999 World Junior Hockey Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Junior_Ice_Hockey_Championships) and [2007 Women's World Hockey Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship).[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] The city is also home to the [Manitoba Herd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Herd) team in the [National Ringette League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ringette_League).[^cite_note-179]

The [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers) play in the [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League). They are twelve-time [Grey Cup](230107.md) champions, most recently in 2021.[^cite_note-gc-180] From 1953 to 2012, the Blue Bombers called [Canad Inns Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canad_Inns_Stadium) home; they have since moved to [Princess Auto Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Auto_Stadium), which opened in 2013. The facility is also the home of the [Winnipeg Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Rifles) of the [Canadian Junior Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Junior_Football_League).[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] Winnipeg is the only city with two women's football teams in the [Western Women's Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Women's_Canadian_Football_League): the [Manitoba Fearless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Fearless) and the [Winnipeg Wolfpack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Wolfpack).[^cite_note-183] The University of Manitoba [Bisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Bisons) and the University of Winnipeg [Wesmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Wesmen) represent the city in [university-level sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Sports).[^cite_note-184] In soccer, it is represented by [FC Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Manitoba) in the [USL League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_Two).[^cite_note-185] Winnipeg has been home to several professional baseball teams, most recently the [Winnipeg Goldeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Goldeyes), who play at [Shaw Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Park).[^cite_note-186]

Winnipeg was the first Canadian city to host the [Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Games), and the second city to host the event twice, in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Pan_American_Games) and again in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pan_American_Games).[^cite_note-187] The [Pan Am Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Pool), built for the 1967 Pan Am Games, hosts aquatic events, including [diving](8402.md), [speed swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)), [synchronized swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronized_swimming) and [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo).[^cite_note-indoor_pools-188] Other notable sporting events hosted by Winnipeg include the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) (co-hosted with Edmonton, Montreal, Vancouver, Ottawa, and Moncton)[^cite_note-189] the [2017 Canada Summer Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Canada_Summer_Games)[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191] the [2023 World Police and Fire Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Police_and_Fire_Games),[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] and several [Grey Cup](230107.md) finals.[^cite_note-194]

## Local media

Main article: [Media in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Winnipeg)



CBC Radio-Canada studio

Winnipeg has two daily newspapers: the *[Winnipeg Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Free_Press)* and the *[Winnipeg Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Sun)*.[^cite_note-196] There are also several ethnic weekly newspapers.[^cite_note-197]

Radio broadcasting in Winnipeg began in 1922;[^cite_note-MOIR15-198] by 1923, government-owned [CKY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBW_\(AM\)#History) held a monopoly position that lasted until after the Second World War. Winnipeg is home to 33 AM and FM radio stations, two of which are [French-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) stations.[^cite_note-199] [CBC Radio One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Radio_One) and [CBC Radio 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Radio_2) broadcast local and national programming in the city.[^cite_note-200] [NCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Communications_Inc) is devoted to Indigenous programming.[^cite_note-201]

Television broadcasting in Winnipeg started in 1954. The federal government refused to license any private broadcaster until the [Canadian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) had created a national network. In May 1954, [CBWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBWT) went on the air broadcasting four hours daily.[^cite_note-A_History_v_1995-202] There are now five English-language stations and one French-language station based in Winnipeg. Additionally, some American network affiliates are available over-the-air.[^cite_note-203]

## Law and government

Main article: [Law, government, and crime in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law,_government,_and_crime_in_Winnipeg)



Winnipeg City Hall is the [seat of municipal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_City_Council).

Since 1992, the city of Winnipeg has been represented by 15 city councillors and a mayor, both elected every four years.[^cite_note-munic-204] The present mayor, [Scott Gillingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Gillingham), was first elected to office in 2022.[^cite_note-205] The city is a single-tier municipality, governed by a [mayor-council system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council_government).[^cite_note-ce-19] The structure of the municipal government is set by the provincial legislature in the City of Winnipeg Charter Act, which replaced the old [City of Winnipeg Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Winnipeg_Act) in 2003.[^cite_note-charter-206] The mayor is elected by direct popular vote to serve as the [chief executive](37570.md) of the city.[^cite_note-207] At Council meetings, the mayor has one of 16 votes. The city governance functions off the "strong-mayor" model, which allows for a "two-tiered system" or voting block between the councillors who are on or not on the Executive Policy Committee.[^cite_note-208] The City Council is a [unicameral](204304.md) legislative body, representing geographical [wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_\(division\)) throughout the city.[^cite_note-charter-206] In provincial politics, Winnipeg is represented by 32 of the 57 provincial [Members of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly) (MLAs) in the [43rd Manitoba Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_Manitoba_Legislature). As of 2023, Winnipeg districts are represented by 28 members of the [New Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democratic_Party_of_Manitoba) (NDP), three by the [Progressive Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Manitoba), and one by the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Liberal_Party).[^cite_note-209]

In federal politics, as of 2025, Winnipeg is represented by eight [Members of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada): five [Liberals](149536.md), two [Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada) and one [New Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democratic_Party_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-210] There are six [Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada) representing Manitoba in [Ottawa](22219.md).[^cite_note-senate-211]

### Crime

Main article: [Law, government, and crime in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law,_government,_and_crime_in_Winnipeg)



Winnipeg is home to the [Manitoba Legislative Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Legislative_Building), which houses the [Legislative Assembly of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Manitoba).

From 2007 to 2011, Winnipeg was the "murder capital" of Canada, with the highest per-capita rate of [homicides](123451.md); as of 2022, with a homicide rate of 7.2 per 100,000, it is in second place, behind [Thunder Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay) (13.7 per 100,000).[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] In 2019, Winnipeg had the 13th-highest [violent crime index](705876.md#Violent_crime_severity_index_by_CMA) in Canada, and the highest robbery rate.[^cite_note-215] Winnipeg was the "violent crime capital" of Canada in 2020 according to the Statistics Canada police-reported violent crime severity index.[^cite_note-216] Despite high overall violent crime rates, crime in Winnipeg is mostly concentrated in the inner city, which makes up only 19% of the population[^cite_note-217] but was the site of 86.4% of the city's shootings, 66.5% of the robberies, 63.3% of the homicides and 59.5% of the sexual assaults in 2012.[^cite_note-CrimeStat-218]

From the early 1990s to the mid-2000s, Winnipeg had a significant auto-theft problem, with the rate peaking at 2,165.0 per 100,000 residents in 2006[^cite_note-Every_Annual_Crime_Report-219] compared to 487 auto-thefts per 100,000 residents for Canada as a whole.[^cite_note-220] To combat auto theft, [Manitoba Public Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Public_Insurance) established financial incentives for motor vehicle owners to install ignition [immobilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immobilizer) in their vehicles, and now requires owners of high-risk vehicles to install immobilizers.[^cite_note-221] These initiatives resulted in an 80% decrease in auto thefts between 2006 and 2011.[^cite_note-222]

As of 2018, the [Winnipeg Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Police_Service) had 1,914 police officers, which is one officer per 551 city residents, and cost taxpayers \$290,564,015.[^cite_note-2018Report-223] In November 2013, the national police union reviewed the Winnipeg Police Force and found high average response times for several categories of calls.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] In 2017, the city started to deal with an increasingly large [methamphetamine](11038318.md) problem, fuelling violent crime.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227]

## Education

See also: [List of schools of Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_of_Winnipeg)



Located in Winnipeg, the [University of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manitoba) is the largest post-secondary institution in the province.

Winnipeg has seven [school divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_division): [Winnipeg School Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_School_Division), [St. James-Assiniboia School Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._James-Assiniboia_School_Division), [Pembina Trails School Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembina_Trails_School_Division), [Seven Oaks School Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Oaks_School_Division), [Division Scolaire Franco-Manitobaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-manitoban_School_Division), [River East Transcona School Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_East_Transcona_School_Division), and [Louis Riel School Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Riel_School_Division).[^cite_note-228] Winnipeg also has several religious and secular private schools.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]

The [University of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manitoba) is the largest university in Manitoba.[^cite_note-um-231] It was founded in 1877, making it Western Canada's first university.[^cite_note-um-231] [Université de Saint-Boniface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_de_Saint-Boniface) is the city's [French-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-language) university.[^cite_note-232] The [University of Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Winnipeg) received its charter in 1967.[^cite_note-uw-233] The [Canadian Mennonite University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Mennonite_University) is a private [Mennonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mennonite) undergraduate university established in 1999.[^cite_note-234]

Winnipeg also has two independent colleges: [Red River College Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_College_Polytechnic) and [Booth University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booth_University_College). Red River College offers diploma, certificate, and apprenticeship programs and, starting in 2009, began offering some degree programs.[^cite_note-235] Booth University College is a private Christian [Salvation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_Army) university college established in 1982. It offers mostly arts and [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) training.[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Winnipeg)

Winnipeg has had public transit since 1882, starting with [horse-drawn streetcars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsecar).[^cite_note-horse-238] They were replaced by [electric trolley cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_tram). The trolley cars ran from 1892 to 1955, supplemented by motor [buses](4146.md) after 1918, and electric [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) from 1938 to 1970.[^cite_note-horse-238] [Winnipeg Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Transit) now runs diesel buses on [its routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Winnipeg_bus_routes).[^cite_note-239] In August 2025, Winnipeg Transit announced that a 60-foot zero-emission bus (ZEB) would go into service. Winnipeg became the first Canadian city with 60-foot battery-electric buses in its fleet.[^cite_note-240]



[Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Winnipeg\)) is the [inter-city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_rail) [railway station](26161590.md) for the city.

Winnipeg is a railway hub and is served by [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail) at [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Winnipeg\)) for passenger rail, and [Canadian National Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Railway), [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway), [Burlington Northern Santa Fe Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Northern_Santa_Fe_Manitoba), and the [Central Manitoba Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Manitoba_Railway) for [freight rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_freight_transport). It is the only major city between Vancouver and [Thunder Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay) with direct US connections by rail (freight).[^cite_note-241]

Winnipeg is the largest and best-connected city in Manitoba and has highways leading in all directions from the city. To the south, Winnipeg is connected to the United States via [Provincial Trunk Highway 75 (PTH 75)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Highway_75) (a continuation of [I-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_29) and [US 75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_75), known as Pembina Highway or [Route 42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Route_42) within Winnipeg). The highway runs 107 kilometres (66 mi) to [Emerson, Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson,_Manitoba), and is the busiest [Canada–United States border](630260.md) crossing on the Prairies.[^cite_note-242] The four-lane [Perimeter Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perimeter_Highway), built in 1969, serves as a [Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltway), with [at-grade intersections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-grade_intersection) and a few [interchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_\(road\)). It allows travellers on the [Trans-Canada Highway](70775.md) to bypass the city.[^cite_note-243] The [Trans-Canada Highway](70775.md) runs east to west through the city (city route), or circles around the city on the Perimeter Highway (beltway). Some of the city's major arterial roads include [Route 80 (Waverley St.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Route_80), [Route 155 (McGillivray Blvd)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Route_155), [Route 165 (Bishop Grandin Blvd.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Route_165), [Route 17 (Chief Peguis Trail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Route_17), and [Route 90 (Brookside Blvd., Oak Point Hwy., King Edward St., Century St., Kenaston Blvd.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Route_90).[^cite_note-244]



[Winnipeg James Armstrong Richardson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_James_Armstrong_Richardson_International_Airport) is the only commercial [international airport](1519705.md) in the province.

The [Winnipeg James Armstrong Richardson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_James_Armstrong_Richardson_International_Airport) completed a \$585 million redevelopment in October 2011. The development brought a new terminal, a four-level parking facility, and other infrastructure improvements.[^cite_note-245] [Winnipeg Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Bus_Terminal), at Winnipeg International Airport, was previously served by [Greyhound Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Canada).[^cite_note-246]

Approximately 8,100 hectares (20,000 acres) of land to the north and west of the airport has been designated as an [inland port](1341936.md), [CentrePort Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CentrePort_Canada), and is Canada's first [Foreign Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Trade_Zone). It is a private sector initiative to develop the infrastructure for Manitoba's trucking, air, rail and sea industries.[^cite_note-247] In 2009, construction began on a \$212 million four-lane freeway to connect CentrePort with the Perimeter Highway.[^cite_note-248] Named CentrePort Canada Way, it opened in November 2013.[^cite_note-249]

Several taxi companies serve Winnipeg, the largest being Unicity, Duffy's Taxi and Spring Taxi. Ride-sharing was legalized in March 2018 to where services including [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) now operate in Winnipeg.[^cite_note-250] Cycling is popular in Winnipeg, and there are many bicycle trails and lanes around the city. Winnipeg holds an annual [Bike-to-Work Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike-to-Work_Day)[^cite_note-251] and [Cyclovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclovia),[^cite_note-252] and bicycle commuters may be seen year-round. Active living infrastructure in Winnipeg includes [bike lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_lanes)[^cite_note-253] and [sharrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_lane_marking).[^cite_note-254]

### Medical centres and hospitals

See also: [List of hospitals in Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Manitoba)

Winnipeg has multiple major hospitals: [Health Sciences Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Sciences_Centre_\(Winnipeg\)) (including [HSC Winnipeg Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSC_Winnipeg_Children's_Hospital)), [Concordia Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_Hospital), [Deer Lodge Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_Lodge_Centre), [Grace Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Hospital_\(Winnipeg\)), [Saint Boniface General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Boniface_General_Hospital_\(Winnipeg\)), [Seven Oaks General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Oaks_General_Hospital), and [Victoria General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_General_Hospital_\(Winnipeg\)).[^cite_note-255]

The [National Microbiology Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Microbiology_Laboratory) in Winnipeg is one of only a handful of [biosafety level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosafety_level) 4 microbiology laboratories in the world.[^cite_note-256] The NML houses laboratories of the Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) and the Canadian Food Inspection Agency, in the National Centre for Foreign Animal Disease collocated in the same facility. Research facilities are also operated through hospitals and private biotechnology companies in the city.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258]

### Utilities

Water and sewage services are provided by the city.[^cite_note-259] The city draws its water via an [aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Winnipeg_Water_District_Aqueduct) from [Shoal Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Lake_\(Kenora_District,_Ontario\)), treating and [fluoridating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fluoridation) it at the Deacon Reservoir just outside the city prior to pumping it into the Winnipeg system.[^cite_note-260] The city's system has over 2,500 kilometres (1,600 mi) of underground water mains, which are subject to breakage due to corrosion and pressure from extreme dry, wet, or cold soil conditions.[^cite_note-261]

[Electricity](9550.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) are provided by [Manitoba Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba_Hydro), a provincial [crown corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_corporation) headquartered in the city; it uses primarily [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) power.[^cite_note-262] The primary telecommunications carrier is [Bell MTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_MTS), although other corporations offer telephone, cellular, television and internet services.[^cite_note-263]

Winnipeg contracts out several services to private companies, including garbage and recycling collection, street plowing and snow removal. This practice represents a significant budget expenditure. The services have faced numerous complaints from residents about missed service.[^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267]

## Military

See also: [CFB Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFB_Winnipeg)



Entrance to [CFB Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFB_Winnipeg). CFB Winnipeg is the home garrison for a number of [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force) units.

[Canadian Forces Base Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFB_Winnipeg), co-located at the [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_James_Armstrong_Richardson_International_Airport), is home to many flight operations support divisions and several training schools. It is also the headquarters of [1 Canadian Air Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Canadian_Air_Division) and the Canadian [North American Aerospace Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command) (NORAD) Region,[^cite_note-268] as well as the home base of 17 Wing of the [Canadian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces). It supports 113 units, stretching from [Thunder Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay) to the [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan)–[Alberta](717.md) border, and from the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north) to the high [Arctic](36971.md), as well as the Central Flying School.[^cite_note-17_Wing-269] 17 Wing also acts as a deployed operating base for [CF-18 Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF-18_Hornet) [fighter-bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-attack_aircraft) assigned to the Canadian NORAD Region.[^cite_note-17_Wing-269]

There are two squadrons based in the city. The [402 "City of Winnipeg" Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._402_Squadron_RCAF) flies the Canadian-designed and produced de Havilland [CT-142 Dash 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_Dash_8) navigation trainer.[^cite_note-270] The [435 "Chinthe" Transport and Rescue Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/435_Transport_and_Rescue_Squadron) flies the Lockheed [CC-130 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules) in airlift [search and rescue](172599.md) roles.[^cite_note-chinte-271] In addition, 435 Squadron is the only [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force) squadron equipped and trained to conduct tactical [air-to-air refuelling](237949.md) of [fighter aircraft](10929.md).[^cite_note-chinte-271]

There are several units of the Canadian Army Primary Reserve based in Winnipeg. These include [The Royal Winnipeg Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Winnipeg_Rifles), [The Queen's Own Cameron Highlanders of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_Own_Cameron_Highlanders_of_Canada), 38 Service Battalion, 38 Combat Engineer Regiment, 38 Signal Regiment, and [The Fort Garry Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fort_Garry_Horse).[^cite_note-272] [HMCS Chippawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMCS_Chippawa) is a Royal Canadian Navy reserve division in Winnipeg.[^cite_note-273]

For many years, Winnipeg was the home of the Second Battalion of [Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Patricia's_Canadian_Light_Infantry). Initially, the battalion was based at the Fort Osborne Barracks, now the location of the Rady Jewish Community Centre.[^cite_note-274] They eventually moved to the Kapyong Barracks between [River Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Heights,_Winnipeg) and [Tuxedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuxedo,_Winnipeg). Since 2004, the battalion has operated out of [CFB Shilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFB_Shilo) near [Brandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon,_Manitoba).[^cite_note-275]
