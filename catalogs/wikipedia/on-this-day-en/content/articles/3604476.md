**Hadhramaut Governorate**[^cite_note-3] ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): محافظة حضرموت, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Muḥāfaẓat Ḥaḍramawt*) is a [governorate](690277.md) of [Yemen](350939.md). Lying within the large historical region of [Hadhramaut](325752.md), it is the country's largest governorate. The capital of Hadhramaut is the city of [Mukalla](2414800.md). Other cities in Hadhramaut include the historical towns of [Shibam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibam), [Sena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena,_Yemen), [Seiyun](438897.md), [Tarim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarim,_Yemen), and [Ash Shihr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Shihr).

The [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra) Archipelago was transferred from the [Aden Governorate](3604348.md) to the Hadhramaut Governorate in 2004. It was subsequently separated to create [Socotra Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate) in December 2013.[^cite_note-4]

## Etymology

This section is an excerpt from [Hadhramaut § Toponymy](325752.md#Toponymy).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hadhramaut\&action=edit)]

The origins of the name *Ḥaḍramawt* are disputed, and numerous debated hypotheses regarding its meaning exist. The most popular [folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology) is that the word comes from a nickname of *Amer bin Qahtan*, who is thought to be the first person to dwell in the region, meaning "death has come", from the words in Arabic: حَضَر, romanized: *ḥaḍara*, lit. 'he came' and Arabic: مَوْت, romanized: *mawt*, lit. 'death'.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Another theory is that it is a variant of the name of Islamic prophet [Hud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hud_\(prophet\))[^cite_note-7] who was sent to this region or his last words "Ludara al-mawt," meaning "death has come."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewcock198621-8]

The name Ḥaḍramawt has also been found within Biblical [Hazarmaveth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazarmaveth).[^cite_note-11] The name means "court of death" and is composed of two parts: [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): חֲצַרְ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *ḥaṣar*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'dwelling; court' and Hebrew: מָוֶת *māweṯ* "death".[^cite_note-12] There, Hud was the descendant of [ʿĀd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BF%C4%80d), son of [Joktan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joktan),[^cite_note-13] the purported ancestor and progenitor of the [South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia) kingdoms. According to tradition, the family of ʿĀd was the first to settle in the region, and when the sons of ʿĀd had died, Amer bin Qahtan, nicknamed "Hadhramaut", came to power.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewcock198621-8]

Though the origins of the name are unknown, there are several scholarly proposals. [Kamal Salibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamal_Salibi) says that the [diphthong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diphthong) "-aw" is an incorrect vocalisation, noting that "-ūt" is a frequent ending for place names in the Ḥaḍramawt; given that "Ḥaḍramūt" is the colloquial pronunciation of the name, and also its ancient pronunciation, the correct reading of the name would thus be "place of *ḥḍrm*". Salibi proposes, then, that the name means "the green place", which is appropriate given its [well](15105978.md)-irrigated [wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi), giving a lushness that contrasts with the surrounding high desert [plateau](538005.md).[^cite_note-14]

Variations of the name are attested to as early as the middle of the [1st millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_millennium_BC). The names *ḥḍrmt* (𐩢𐩳𐩧𐩣𐩩) and *ḥḍrmwt* (𐩢𐩳𐩧𐩣𐩥𐩩) are found in texts of the Old South Arabian languages (Ḥaḍramitic, [Minaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minaean_language), [Qatabanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatabanian_language) and [Sabaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaic)), though the second form is not found in any known Ḥaḍramitic inscriptions.[^cite_note-15] In either form, the word itself can be a [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy), a tribal name, or the name of the kingdom of Ḥaḍramawt. In the late [fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_century_BC) or early [3rd century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_century_BC), [Theophrastus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophrastus) gives the name Άδρραμύτα,[^cite_note-16] a direct transcription of the Semitic name into Greek.
