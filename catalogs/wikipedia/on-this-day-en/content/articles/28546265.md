[Air pollution](10934212.md) has been an ongoing problem in many countries in the [Southeast Asia](28741.md) region, and Malaysia is one of the worst affected. [Haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze) has been a major issue for the country, driven by [slash and burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_and_burn) practices by farmers and [peat fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat#Fires) blown by the wind from [Indonesia](14579.md).

A [state of emergency](298608.md#Malaysia) was announced in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Malaysian_haze) at [Port Klang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Klang) as the Air Pollution Index (API) went above 500.[^cite_note-1] Malaysia has worked with the Indonesian authorities to help curb peat fires.[^cite_note-2] Malaysia and Indonesia, together with other members of the [ASEAN](106539.md) community, signed the [ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Agreement_on_Transboundary_Haze_Pollution) in 2002 as a result of a [1997 haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Southeast_Asian_haze).[^cite_note-3]

## Air Pollution Index

Main article: [Air Pollution Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Pollution_Index)

The air quality in Malaysia is reported as the Air Pollution Index (API). Four of the index's pollutant components (i.e., carbon monoxide, ozone, nitrogen dioxide and [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide)) are reported in ppmv but PM<sub>10</sub> particulate matter is reported in μg/m<sup>3</sup>.

This scale below shows the health classifications used by the Malaysian government.

- &#x20; 0-50  Good

- &#x20;51-100 Moderate

- 101-200 Unhealthy

- 201-300 Very unhealthy

- 301- Hazardous

If the API exceeds 500, a state of emergency is declared in the reporting area. Usually, this means that non-essential government services are suspended, and all ports in the affected area are closed. There may also be a prohibition on private sector commercial and industrial activities in the reporting area excluding the food sector.

## 2005 Malaysian haze

Main article: [2005 Malaysian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Malaysian_haze)

The [2005 Malaysian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Malaysian_haze) was a week-long choking smog-like haze over Malaysia that almost brought the central part of Peninsular Malaysia to a standstill, prompted crisis talks with Indonesia and caused widespread inconvenience. The haze was at its worst on August 11, 2005. This was a comeback of the haze crisis which last hit Malaysia in September 1997.

## 2015 Southeast Asian haze

Main article: [2015 Southeast Asian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Southeast_Asian_haze)

The 2015 Southeast Asian haze was caused by mass illegal burning in Indonesia and resulted in over a month of haze in Malaysia, Singapore and parts of Thailand - triggering school closures and disrupting air travel.[^cite_note-4] The haze was considered among the worst in history according to a NASA scientist.[^cite_note-5] Researchers from Harvard and Columbia universities in the US estimated 6,500 people in Malaysia died prematurely due to the incident.[^cite_note-6] But later, the claim was refuted by Indonesia, Singapore and Malaysian health authorities.[^cite_note-7]
