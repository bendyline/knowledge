**Afula** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): עפולה) is a city in the [Northern District](324281.md) of [Israel](9282173.md), often known as the "Capital of the Valley" due to its strategic location in the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley). In 2024 the city had a population of 64,452.[^cite_note-Israelpopulations-1]

Afula's ancient [tell (settlement mound)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_\(archaeology\)) suggests habitation from the [Late Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Chalcolithic) to the [ Ayyubid period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Ayyubid_period). A fortress was built at the site during the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) or [ Mamluk period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Mamluk_period).

A small Arab Palestinian village during the Ottoman period, it was sold in 1872 along with the entire Jezreel Valley to the [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) [Sursock family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sursock_family). In 1925, the same area was acquired by the [American Zionist Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Zionist_Commonwealth) as part of the [Sursock Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sursock_Purchase). The majority Muslim and Christian population were removed by the family, and [Jewish immigrants](351513.md), marking the foundation of modern Afula. After the establishment of the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel) in 1948, Afula was settled by [Jewish](25955086.md) immigrants from [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Iraq), [ Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemenite_Jews) and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Romania). In 1972, it gained the status of a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_\(Israel\)). The 1990s saw Jewish immigration from [ Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Israel) and the [ former Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) contribute to the growth of the city. Since 1995, the city has almost doubled its population.

## Etymology

The name follows that of the small Arab village which occupied the site until the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), possibly originating in the Canaanite-Hebrew root *ʿofel* "fortress tower",[^cite_note-EJ-2] or the Arab word for "ruptured".[^cite_note-3]

## History

The ancient mound of ʿAfula, known as Tel ʿAfula, is close to the city center, west of Route 60 and south of Ussishkin Street. Very little of the initial six-acre tell remains due to construction work done in this area since the [British Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) period. The southern peak of the mound is the better preserved part. It was once widely considered to be the biblical site of [Ophrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophrah), the hometown of the [judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_judges) [Gideon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon),[^cite_note-4] but contemporary scholars generally disagree with this supposition. Archaeological finds date from the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) through the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) period, followed by remains from the Crusader and Mamluk periods.[^cite_note-Pringle-5][^cite_note-Shalev-6]

### Bronze Age to Byzantine period

ʿAfula is possibly the place of [ Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Bronze_Age) *ʿOphlah*, mentioned in the lists of Pharaoh [Thutmose III](155730.md).[^cite_note-SWP40-7] [Zev Vilnay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zev_Vilnay) suggested to identify Afula with biblical ([Iron Age II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Iron_Age_II)) *Ophel*, mentioned in [2 Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books_of_Kings).[^cite_note-8] After the destruction of the [Kingdom of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Israel_\(Samaria\)), the area continued to be inhabited and excavations have revealed artifacts from the periods of [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Persian_period) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Roman_period) rule.[^cite_note-Tsafrir-9]

The first excavations at Tel ʿAfula, carried out in 1948, found Late Chalcolithic– [Early Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Early_Bronze_Age) remains. Tombs from the Early Bronze Age, [ Middle Bronze Age II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Middle_Bronze_Age), [ Late Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Late_Bronze_Age)– [Iron Age I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Iron_Age_I) and Roman period were discovered near the municipal water tower. Archaeologists discovered the Crusader-Mamluk fortress on the southern peak of the tell, a Byzantine olive oil press and evidence of an Early Bronze Age settlement near the northern peak.[^cite_note-Feig-10]

In 1950–1951, excavations on the northwestern slope of the peak revealed a pottery workshop for [Tell el-Yahudiyeh Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_el-Yahudiyeh_Ware) from Middle Bronze Age II and another pottery workshop from Middle Bronze Age I.[^cite_note-Feig-10]

From the 1990s, several small excavations unearthed an uninterrupted sequence of settlement remains from the Chalcolithic until the Late Byzantine periods as well as remains from the Mamluk period.[^cite_note-11]

In 2012, excavations were conducted by the [Israel Antiquities Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Antiquities_Authority) on the southern peak of Tel ʿAfula where the Crusader-Mamluk fortress is located. Due to construction activity from the 1950s, settlement layers on the tell may have been destroyed. Only meager remnants were found, indicative of a settlement from Early Bronze Age I and the Roman period. Pottery from Early Bronze Age III, Iron Age I and a single [ Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Hellenistic_period) [Attic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attica) fragment indicate settlement on the tell in these periods. Fragments of glazed bowls from the 13th century (Mamluk period) were found along the southern edge of the excavation.&#x20;

### Crusader/Ayyubid and Mamluk periods



Remains of [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) fortress in Afula. Note the [spolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolia): [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) [sarcophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus) as the top layer.

At the centre of Tel ʿAfula stand the remains of a 19-metre square fortress from the [ Mamluk period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Mamluk_period), possibly first built during the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) period.[^cite_note-Shalev-6][^cite_note-Pringle-5] The lower four [courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_\(architecture\)) are made of rough boulders, while the top remaining layer is made of reused [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) [sarcophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus). The wall is a total of 5.5 meters tall. Pottery remains indicate that it was occupied in the 12th and 13th century.[^cite_note-Pringle-5] The gate is dated based on pottery findings to the Mamluk period (13th–14th centuries CE), but as of the end of the 2017 excavation campaign it could not be determined when fortress itself was built, since it is perfectly possible that just the gate was renovated in the Mamluk period; the square shape and the use of Roman sarcophagi as building stones is closely resembling the [ Crusader tower at Sepphoris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepphoris#Crusader_tower).[^cite_note-Shalev-6]

In 1321, ʿAfula was mentioned under the name of *Afel* by [Marino Sanuto the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marino_Sanuto_the_Elder).[^cite_note-SWP41-12]

### Late Ottoman period

A map by [Pierre Jacotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Jacotin) from [ Napoleon's invasion of 1799](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_campaign_in_Egypt_and_Syria) showed this place, named as *Afouleh* in a French transliteration of the Arabic.[^cite_note-13]

In 1816, [James Silk Buckingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Silk_Buckingham) passed by and described *Affouli* as being built on rising ground and containing only a few dwellings. He noted several other nearby settlements in sight, all populated by Muslims.[^cite_note-14]

In 1838, [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) described both ʿAfula and the adjacent [El Fuleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Fuleh) as "deserted".[^cite_note-15] [^cite_note-16] [William McClure Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McClure_Thomson), in a book published in 1859, noted that ʿAfula and the adjacent [El Fuleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Fuleh) were "both now deserted, though both were inhabited twenty-five years ago when I first passed this way." Thomson blamed their desertion on the [Bedouin](61770.md).[^cite_note-17]

In 1875, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) described ʿAfula as a village on a small hill overlooking a little plain. The houses were built of [adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe) and various other materials. Around the [well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_well), which Guérin thought was probably ancient, he noticed several broken sarcophagi serving as [ troughs](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/trough#English).[^cite_note-18] In 1882, the [Palestine Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *Survey of Western Palestine* described El ʿAfula as a small adobe village in the plain, supplied by two wells.[^cite_note-SWP40-7]

A population list from about 1887 showed that el ʿAfula had about 630 inhabitants, all Muslim.[^cite_note-19] [Gottlieb Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottlieb_Schumacher), as part of surveying for the construction of the [Jezreel Valley railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley_railway), noted in 1900 that it consisted of 50-55 huts and had 200 inhabitants. North of the village was a grain stop, belonging to the [Sursocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sursock_family).[^cite_note-20]



Old water tower at Afula station

In 1904, the Ottoman authorities inaugurated the Jezreel Valley railway, at first operating between [Haifa](55606.md) and [ Beysan](323286.md) via ʿAfula and soon extended to [Dera'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera'a). Work eventually continued with an extension towards Jerusalem, the connection to [Jenin](50997.md) being completed in 1913.

#### First World War

Main article: [Capture of Afulah and Beisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Afulah_and_Beisan)

During the [Great War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_War), ʿAfulah was a major communications hub. In 1917, when Colonel [Richard Meinertzhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Meinertzhagen) from the British intelligence established contact with the [Nili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nili) Jewish spy network in Palestine, a German Jewish doctor stationed at al ʿAfulah railway junction provided the British with valuable reconnaissance reports on Ottoman and German troop movements southwards.

With the advance of General [Edmund Allenby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Allenby)'s British forces into Ottoman Palestine, al ʿAfulah was captured by the [4th Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Cavalry_Division_\(British_Indian_Army\)) of the [Desert Mounted Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Mounted_Corps), during the cavalry phase of the [Battle of Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sharon) in September 1918.

### British Mandate

According to the [British Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine)'s [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine), *Affuleh* had 563 inhabitants; 471 Muslims, 62 Christians, 28 Jews and 2 followers of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith);[^cite_note-21] 61 of the Christians were Orthodox, while one was [Melkite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite).[^cite_note-22]

#### Jewish Afula (est. 1925)

In 1925, the area was acquired with money from the [American Zion Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Zion_Commonwealth) as part of the [Sursock Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sursock_Purchase).[^cite_note-JBG-23][^cite_note-Segev242-24] The Arab tenant farmers were given four years during which they could either buy the land or leave, in the meantime having the right to cultivate it.[^cite_note-JBG-23] A quarter of the one hundred Arab families who had lived in the area accepted compensation for their land and left voluntarily; the remainder were evicted by the new owners.[^cite_note-Segev242-24][^cite_note-25] Jews began settling in Afula shortly after as the town developed, with many American and [Polish Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) purchasing the parcels. Many of the Polish Jews who bought land in the town perished in the [Holocoaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), and were therefore unable to develop their plots.[^cite_note-26] Nearby land had been purchased in a similar manner in 1909 or 1910, when [Yehoshua Hankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehoshua_Hankin), in his first major purchase in the Jezreel Valley, bought 10,000 dunams (10 km<sup>2</sup>) of land on which [ Merhavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merhavia_\(moshav\)) and [Tel Adashim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Adashim) were to be built.[^cite_note-Segev242-24]

In 1924 former leaders of [Hashomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashomer) established an arms factory in Afula. Disguised as a farm equipment repair workshop, it produced bullets and weapon parts.[^cite_note-27]

By the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine), the population had increased to 874; with 786 Jews, 86 Muslims, nine Christians, and three classified as "no religion", in a total of 236 houses.[^cite_note-28]



1940s [Survey of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_of_Palestine) map of ʿAfula and Merhavya

In a [1945 survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945), the population of ʿAfula was estimated at 2300 Jews and ten Muslims.[^cite_note-DoS1945-29] The town had a total of 18,277 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land, according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-30] Of this, 145 dunams of land was used to cultivate citrus and bananas, 347 dunams were for plantations and irrigable land, 15,103 for cereals,[^cite_note-31] while 992 dunams were built-up land.[^cite_note-32]

During this time, the community was served by the [Jezreel Valley Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley_Railway), a side branch of the larger [Hejaz Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Railway). Since 1913 it had also been the [terminus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminus_station) of the branch connecting it to [Jenin](50997.md) and later also to [Nablus](233369.md). Sabotage actions of Jewish underground militias in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_the_Trains), [1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_the_Bridges) and shortly before the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) rendered first the connection to Jenin, then progressively the entire Valley Railway, [inoperable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley_railway#Closure).

#### Railroad (1948-49; 2010s)

Repairs to the Jezreel Valley Railway after 1948 restored service to [Haifa](55606.md), but only until 1949 when it was abandoned. In 2011, construction began on a large-scale project to build a new [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) railway from Haifa to [Beit She'an](323286.md) with stations in [Afula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afula_railway_station) and other towns, along roughly the same route as the historical valley railway. [Israel Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Railways) began passenger service on the [new line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley_railway#Modern_railway) on October 16, 2016.[^cite_note-33]

#### Terror attacks (1990s-2000s)

Due to Afula's proximity to the [West Bank](33209.md), it has been a target for [Palestinian political violence](2646043.md).[^cite_note-34] On 6 April 1994, the [Afula Bus suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afula_Bus_suicide_bombing) killed five people in the center of Afula. In the [Afula axe attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afula_axe_attack) in November 1994, a 19-year-old female soldier was attacked and murdered by an axe-wielding Arab [Hamas](13913.md) member.[^cite_note-35]

During the [Second Intifada](71717.md), Afula was the target of a [suicide attack](18835454.md) on a bus on 5 March 2002, in which one person died and several others were injured at Afula's central bus station. In the [Afula mall bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afula_mall_bombing) on 19 May 2003, a female suicide bomber [blew herself up at the Amakim mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afula_mall_bombing), killing three and wounding 70.[^cite_note-36] This attack was claimed by the [Islamic Jihad Movement in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Movement_in_Palestine) and the [Fatah](11104.md) movement's [Al-Aqsa Martyrs Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Martyrs_Brigades).

#### 2006 Lebanon War

On 17 July 2006, during the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War), [Hezbollah](13919.md) fired [Katyusha rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rocket_launcher) at Afula, one of the southernmost rocket attacks on Israel from Lebanon. Six people were treated for shock as a result of the attack. On 28 July, a rocket landed causing a fire. The rocket carried 100 kilograms (220 pounds) of explosives.[^cite_note-37]

#### Recent development plans

In September 2016, it was announced that seven new neighborhoods would be built, doubling the city's population.[^cite_note-38]

##### "Afula as a Jewish-only city" grassroots campaigns

In 2015, a tender of the [Israel Land Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Land_Authority) for 27 lots in Afula was won by 45 Arab [Palestinian](23267.md) citizens. Following the results, a group of [Jewish](25955086.md) residents petitioned to cancel the tender, formally citing price coordination but also admitting that the arrival of "a large group of [Arab citizens](492331.md) coming to live here together" raised sensitivities in the neighborhood.[^cite_note-39]

In another case in 2018, a Jewish homeowner in Afula refused to rent an apartment to an Arab woman. The Nazareth District Court ruled that the refusal constituted unlawful [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) on national grounds and ordered him to pay 30,000 NIS in damages, noting that despite the landlord’s claim that he held no racist views, his actions demonstrated anti-Arab bias.[^cite_note-40] Reports in the [Israeli press](10480502.md) have highlighted the persistence of [anti-Arab sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Arab_racism) in the city.[^cite_note-41] Former Afula Mayor Avi Elkabetz joined the protest and said, "the residents of Afula [don't want a mixed city, but rather a Jewish city, and it's their right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafrada). This is not racism."[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The land tender sparked protests by local Jewish residents who declared their opposition to Arabs settling in the city. Demonstrators warned that the influx of Arab families would turn the neighborhood into "a village… soon there will be a mosque and a school… where have we come to?" and claimed this would drive Jews to leave Afula.[^cite_note-44] Civil society groups condemned the protests as discriminatory and urged the municipality to respect equal rights for all citizens. On 13 June 2018, a demonstration was held in Afula against the sale of an apartment to Arabs, joined not only by residents but also by the city’s deputy mayor. In response, the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) (ADL) sent a letter to the mayor of Afula expressing strong opposition to institutional discrimination, calling on him to take a firm public stance against anti-Arab bias and to address stereotypes and prejudice through educational initiatives for youth in the city.[^cite_note-45] Additional coverage of these campaigns and the city council’s involvement appeared in the Israeli media.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

In June 2019, a demonstration happened in protest against a house being sold to an Arab family, joined by Afula's mayor, Avi Elkabetz, who had run for office on a platform of "preserving the Jewish character of Afula."[^cite_note-48]

## Climate

Afula has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*). The average annual temperature is 20.4 °C (68.7 °F), and around 468 mm (18.43 in) of precipitation falls annually.

- **Tadiran**, an air conditioner factory.

- **Keter Plastic**, an Israeli plastics manufacturer.

## Education and culture



Great [synagogue](48559.md) of Afula



City Auditorium and Art Gallery

According to the [Israeli Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (CBS), as of 2001 there were 24 schools in Afula, serving a total of 8,688 students: 16 elementary schools with 3,814 students and 12 high schools with 4,874 students. In that year, 52.3% of 12th-grade students were eligible for a [bagrut (matriculation certificate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagrut_certificate).[^cite_note-57]

Cultural life in Afula includes community events, parks, and leisure programming that contribute to quality of life. The municipality emphasizes the importance of both formal and [informal education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_education), aiming to foster academic aspiration, communal values, integrity, and excellence. [Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport) also play a role as an educational tool to promote healthy lifestyles, teamwork, and civic responsibility.[^cite_note-58]

## Health care

Afula’s primary health institution, [HaEmek Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaEmek_Medical_Center), was established on 29 April 1930 following initial planning in 1924.[^cite_note-59] It is a major regional hospital, serving an estimated 700,000 residents from Afula and surrounding areas, with a workforce of around 1,900 employees.[^cite_note-60]

&#x20;HaEmek is a district general and teaching hospital, affiliated with the [Rappaport Faculty of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ruth_and_Bruce_Rappaport_Faculty_of_Medicine) at the Technion, and is operated by [Clalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clalit_Health_Services) Health Services.[^cite_note-61] It is also noted as a place of Arab–Israeli coexistence, where personnel from diverse backgrounds work together.[^cite_note-62]

## Voting Patterns

In the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election), 69 percent of voters in Afula voted for the parties of the right-wing coalition led by [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), while 28 percent voted for the parties of the center-left coalition led by [Yair Lapid](2448072.md) and 0.3 percent voted for the Arab parties.[^cite_note-63]
