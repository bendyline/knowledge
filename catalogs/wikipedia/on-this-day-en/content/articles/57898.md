**Norfolk** (locally [/ˈnɔːrfək/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/aa/Pronounce_Norfolk_Virginia.ogg/Pronounce_Norfolk_Virginia.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/aa/Pronounce_Norfolk_Virginia.ogg/Pronounce_Norfolk_Virginia.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*NOR-fək*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is an [independent city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)) in the [U.S. commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_\(U.S._state\)) of [Virginia](32432.md). It had a population of 238,005 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), making it the [third-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Virginia) in both the [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads) metropolitan area and in Virginia, after neighboring cities [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia) and [Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Virginia), as well as the [101st-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the United States.[^cite_note-QF2020-4] The city holds a strategic position as the historical, urban, financial, and cultural center of the Hampton Roads area (sometimes called "[Tidewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidewater_\(region\))"), which has more than 1.8 million inhabitants and is the [37th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the U.S.[^cite_note-5]

Norfolk was established in 1682 as a colonial seaport. Strategically located at the confluence of the [Elizabeth River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_River_\(Virginia\)) and the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay), it quickly developed into a major center for trade and shipbuilding. During the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) and [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), its port and naval facilities made it a critical military target. Norfolk's prominence grew in the 20th century with the expansion of the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md), particularly through the establishment of [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md) in 1917, which remains the world's largest naval base.

Norfolk is an important contributor to the [Port of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Virginia) and houses one of [NATO](21133.md)'s two [Strategic Command headquarters](2789125.md). It is home to [Maersk Line, Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._P._Moller-Maersk_Group#Maersk_Line,_Limited), which manages the world's largest fleet of [US-flag vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_State). The city has numerous cultural institutions including the [Nauticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauticus) maritime museum, [Chrysler Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Museum_of_Art), and [Virginia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Zoo). Norfolk has many miles of riverfront and bayfront areas, including beaches on the Chesapeake Bay.

### Colonial era

When the [South Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hampton_Roads) portion of the shire was separated, Thoroughgood suggested the name of his birthplace for the newly formed [New Norfolk County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Norfolk_County,_Virginia). One year later, it was divided into two counties, [Upper Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Norfolk_County) and [Lower Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Norfolk_County,_Virginia) (the latter now incorporated into the City of Norfolk), chiefly on Thoroughgood's recommendation.[^cite_note-Norfolk_History_Chapter_4-6] This area of Virginia became known as the place of entrepreneurs, including men of the [Virginia Company of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Company).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Norfolk developed in the late-seventeenth century as a "Half Moone" fort was constructed and 50 acres (200,000 m<sup>2</sup>) were acquired from local natives of the [Powhatan Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powhatan_Confederacy) in exchange for 10,000 pounds of tobacco. The House of Burgesses established the "Towne of Lower Norfolk County" in 1680.[^cite_note-Norfolk_History_Chapter_6-9][^cite_note-Norfolk_History_Chapter_7-10]

Norfolk was incorporated in 1705. In 1730, a tobacco inspection site was located here. According to the [Tobacco Inspection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_Inspection_Act), the inspection was *"At Norfolk Town, upon the fort land, in the County of Norfolk; and [Kemp's Landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempsville,_Virginia), in Princess Anne, under one inspection."* In 1736 [George II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain) granted it a royal charter as a borough.[^cite_note-Norfolk_History_Chapter_9-11] It was an important port for exporting goods to the British Isles and beyond.[^cite_note-12]



A cannonball lodged in the wall of [St. Paul's Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul's_Episcopal_Church_\(Norfolk,_Virginia\)), fired by [Lord Dunmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Murray,_4th_Earl_of_Dunmore)'s fleet during the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War)

After the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) forced the [Royal Governor of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colonial_governors_of_Virginia), [John Murray, 4th Earl of Dunmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Murray,_4th_Earl_of_Dunmore), to flee from Williamsburg, he made Norfolk the new capital of Virginia in 1775. Norfolk's 6,250 residents were primarily Loyalist because the British government had granted trade monopolies to many of their businesses. After losing at the [Battle of Great Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Great_Bridge) that December, Lord Dunmore partially destroyed the city with naval cannonading. On January 16, 1776, the Patriots' [Fourth Virginia Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Virginia_Convention) agreed to destroy most remaining homes to prevent Dunmore from using the city to amass British troops and runaway slaves.[^cite_note-13]

Ultimately, [Colonel Woodford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Woodford) drove Dunmore into exile, ending more than 168 years of British rule in Virginia.[^cite_note-History_from_Colonial_Williamsburg-14][^cite_note-15] Only the walls of [Saint Paul's Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul's_Episcopal_Church_\(Norfolk,_Virginia\)) survived the bombardment and subsequent fires. A cannonball from the bombardment (fired by the *[Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Liverpool_\(1758\))*) remains within the wall of Saint Paul's.[^cite_note-HMS_Otter-16]

### Nineteenth century



*Norfolk, from Gosport, Virginia*, New York Public Library

When the British occupied Norfolk, [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) ordered its destruction by fire.[^cite_note-w009-17] Following recovery from the Revolutionary War's burning, Norfolk and its citizens struggled to rebuild. In 1804, another serious fire along the city's waterfront destroyed some 300 buildings and the city suffered a serious economic setback. During the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), British forces launched an unsuccessful raid against Norfolk.[^cite_note-Durant-18]



[Joseph Jenkins Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Jenkins_Roberts), born and raised in Norfolk, became the first [president of Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Liberia)

The [American Colonization Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Colonization_Society) proposed to "[repatriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-to-Africa_movement)" free blacks and freed slaves to Africa by establishing the new colony of Liberia and paying for transportation. But most African Americans wanted to stay in their birthplace of the United States and achieve freedom and rights there. For a period, many emigrants to Liberia from Virginia and [North Carolina](21650.md) embarked from the port of Norfolk. [Joseph Jenkins Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Jenkins_Roberts), a [free person of color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_person_of_color) native to Norfolk, emigrated via the American Colonization Society and later was elected as the first president of [Liberia](17791.md), establishing a powerful family.[^cite_note-Roberts-19]

In 1845, Norfolk was incorporated as a city.[^cite_note-Norfolk_History_1845-1887-20] On June 7, 1855, the 183-foot vessel *Benjamin Franklin* put into Hampton Roads for repairs. The ship had just sailed from the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies), where there had been an outbreak of [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever). The port health officer ordered the ship quarantined. After eleven days, a second inspection found no issues, so it was allowed to dock. A few days later, the first cases of yellow fever were discovered in Norfolk, and a machinist died from the disease on July 8. By August, several people were dying per day, and a third of the city's population had fled in the hopes of escaping the epidemic. No one understood how the disease was transmitted. With both Norfolk and Portsmouth being infected, New York banned all traffic from those sites. Neighboring cities also banned residents from Norfolk. The epidemic spread through the city via mosquitoes and poor sanitation, affecting every family and causing widespread panic. The number of infected reached 5,000 in September, and by the second week, 1,500 had died in Norfolk and Portsmouth.[^cite_note-21] As the weather cooled, the outbreak began to wane, leaving a final tally of about 3,200 dead.[^cite_note-22] It took the city some time to recover.



[Battle of Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hampton_Roads) between the [USS *Monitor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Monitor) and the [CSS *Virginia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSS_Virginia)

On April 4, 1861, Norfolk city delegate to the [Virginia Secession Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Secession_Convention_of_1861), George Blow, voted against secession. Following the [Battle of Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Sumter), another vote occurred on April 17, where Blow voted for secession, the vote passed and Virginia seceded from the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)). In the spring of 1862, the [Battle of Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hampton_Roads) took place off the northwest shore of the city's [Sewell's Point Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewell's_Point), marking the first fight between two [ironclads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship), the [USS *Monitor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Monitor) and the [CSS *Virginia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSS_Virginia). The battle ended in a stalemate but changed the course of naval warfare; from then on, [warships](263960.md) were fortified with metal.[^cite_note-Monitor-Merrimac-23]

In May 1862, Norfolk Mayor [William Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lamb_\(Confederate_States_Army_officer\)) surrendered the city to Union [General](2965165.md) [John E. Wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_E._Wool) and his forces. They held the city under [martial law](50477.md) for the duration of the Civil War. Thousands of slaves from the region escaped to Union lines to gain freedom; they quickly set up schools in Norfolk to start learning how to read and write, years before the end of the war.[^cite_note-Norfolk_History_Chapter_46-24]

### 20th century to present



Granby Street from corner of City Hall Avenue, 1915

1907 brought both the [Virginian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginian_Railway) and the [Jamestown Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Exposition) to [Sewell's Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewell's_Point). The large [Naval Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Review) at the Exposition demonstrated the peninsula's favorable location and laid the groundwork for the world's largest naval base. Southern Democrats in Congress gained its location here. Commemorating the tricentennial anniversary of the founding of Jamestown, the exposition featured many prominent officials, including President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt), [members of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Congress), and [diplomats](28165911.md) from twenty-one countries. By 1917, as the US prepared to enter [World War I](4764461.md), the Naval Air Station Hampton Roads had been constructed on the former exposition grounds. This facility was established as an air training station and a patrol base to conduct experimental work in seaplane operations at the Naval Operation Base.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Jamestown_Exposition-26]

In the first half of the twentieth century, the city of Norfolk expanded its borders through annexation. In 1906, the city annexed the [incorporated town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporated_town) of [Berkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkley,_Virginia), making the city cross the [Elizabeth River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_River_\(Virginia\)).[^cite_note-Norfolk:_1906_Annexation-27] In 1923, the city expanded to include Sewell's Point, [Willoughby Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willoughby_Spit), the town of Campostella, and the [Ocean View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_View,_Virginia) area. The city included the Navy Base and miles of beach property fronting on [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads) and the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay).[^cite_note-Norfolk:_1923_Annexation-28] After additional annexations in 1955 and 1959, and a 1988 land swap with [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia), the city assumed its current boundaries.[^cite_note-Norfolk:_1955_Annexation-29]

The establishment of the [Interstate Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) following World War II brought new highways to the region. A series of bridges and tunnels, constructed during fifteen years, linked Norfolk with [the Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Peninsula), [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia), and [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia). In 1952, the [Downtown Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Tunnel) opened to connect Norfolk with the city of Portsmouth. The highways also stimulated the development of new housing suburbs, leading to the population spreading out. Additional bridges and tunnels included the [Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads_Bridge-Tunnel) in 1957,[^cite_note-Hampton_Roads_Bridge_Tunnel-30] the [Midtown Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Tunnel_\(Virginia\)) in 1962,[^cite_note-Midtown_Tunnel-31] and the [Virginia Beach-Norfolk Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach-Norfolk_Expressway) ([Interstate 264](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_264_\(Virginia\)) and [State Route 44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Route_44_\(Virginia_1980s\))) in 1967.[^cite_note-Interstate_264-32] In 1991, the new Downtown Tunnel/[Berkley Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkley_Bridge_\(Virginia\)) complex opened a new system of multiple lanes of highway and interchanges connecting Downtown Norfolk and [Interstate 464](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_464) with the Downtown Tunnel tubes.[^cite_note-Downtown_Tunnel-33]

In 1954, the [Supreme Court](31737.md) ruled in *[Brown v. Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education)* that [segregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) public schools were [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconstitutional), as the public system was supported by all taxpayers. It ordered [integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_integration), but Virginia pursued a policy of "[massive resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massive_resistance)". (At this time, most black citizens were still [disfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfranchisement_after_Reconstruction_era) under the state's turn-of-the-century constitution and discriminatory practices related to voter registration and elections.) The [Virginia General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_General_Assembly) prohibited state funding for integrated public schools.[^cite_note-34]

In 1958, [United States district courts](70235.md) in Virginia ordered schools to open for the first time on a racially integrated basis. In response, [Governor](253093.md) [J. Lindsay Almond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Lindsay_Almond) ordered the schools closed. The [Virginia Supreme Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Supreme_Court_of_Appeals) declared the [state law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_law_\(United_States\)) to be in conflict with the [state constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Virginia) and ordered all public schools to be funded, whether integrated or not. About ten days later, Almond capitulated and asked the General Assembly to rescind several "massive resistance" laws.[^cite_note-Massive_Resistance-35] In February 1959, seventeen black children entered six previously segregated Norfolk public schools.[^cite_note-36] *[Virginian-Pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginian-Pilot)* editor [Lenoir Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenoir_Chambers) editorialized against massive resistance and earned the [Pulitzer Prize for Editorial Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Editorial_Writing).[^cite_note-Lenoir_Chambers-37]

With new [suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) developments beckoning, many white middle-class residents [moved out of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_flight) along new highway routes, and Norfolk's population declined, a pattern repeated in numerous cities during the postwar era independently of segregation issues. In the late-1960s and early-1970s, the advent of newer suburban shopping destinations along with freeways spelled demise for the fortunes of downtown's [Granby Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granby_Street) commercial corridor, located just a few blocks inland from the waterfront. The opening of malls and large shopping centers drew off retail business from Granby Street.[^cite_note-JANAF_History-38]

[People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_for_the_Ethical_Treatment_of_Animals) (PETA) has been based in Norfolk since 1996.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Norfolk's city leaders began a long push to [revive its urban core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal). While Granby Street underwent decline, Norfolk city leaders focused on the waterfront and its collection of decaying piers and warehouses. Many obsolete shipping and warehousing facilities were demolished. In their place, planners created a new boulevard, Waterside Drive, along which many of the high-rise buildings in Norfolk's [skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyline) have been erected. In 1983, the city, [The Rouse Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rouse_Company) and the [Enterprise Development Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_Development_Company) developed the [Waterside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterside_\(Norfolk,_Virginia\)) festival marketplace to attract people back to the waterfront and catalyze further downtown redevelopment.[^cite_note-Waterside-41] Waterside was redeveloped in 2017. Additionally, the waterfront area hosts the [Nauticus maritime museum and science center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauticus), [Hampton Roads Naval Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads_Naval_Museum), [Half-Moone Cruise and Celebration Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauticus#Half_Moone_Cruise_and_Celebration_Center), and the [USS *Wisconsin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wisconsin_\(BB-64\)). Other facilities opened in the ensuing years, including the [Harbor Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Park) baseball stadium, home of the [Norfolk Tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Tides) Triple-A [minor league baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league_baseball) team. In 1995, the park was named the finest facility in minor league baseball by *[Baseball America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_America)*.[^cite_note-Harbor_Park-42] Norfolk's efforts to revitalize its downtown have attracted acclaim from economic development and urban planning circles throughout the country. Downtown's rising fortunes helped to expand the city's revenues and allowed the city to direct attention to other neighborhoods.[^cite_note-About_Norfolk-43]

## Geography



[Newport News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_Virginia), [Hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton,_Virginia), [Isle of Wight County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight_County,_Virginia), [Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk,_Virginia), [Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Virginia), [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia) and Norfolk, from space, July 1996. Norfolk is located in the upper-right quadrant; east is at the top.

The city is located at the southeastern corner of [Virginia](32432.md) at the junction of the Elizabeth River and the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay). The Hampton Roads [Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_metropolitan_area) (officially known as the [Virginia Beach-Norfolk-Newport News, VA-NC MSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach-Norfolk-Newport_News,_VA-NC_MSA)) is the [37th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_United_States_Metropolitan_Statistical_Areas) in the United States, with an estimated population of 1,716,624 in 2014. The area includes the Virginia cities of Norfolk, [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia), [Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Virginia), [Hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton,_Virginia), [Newport News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_Virginia), [Poquoson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poquoson,_Virginia), [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia), [Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk,_Virginia), [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia), and the counties of [Gloucester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester_County,_Virginia), [Isle of Wight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight_County,_Virginia), [James City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_City_County,_Virginia), [Mathews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathews_County,_Virginia), and [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_County,_Virginia), as well as the [North Carolina](21650.md) counties of [Currituck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currituck_County,_North_Carolina) and [Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_County,_North_Carolina). The city of Norfolk is recognized as the central business district, while the Virginia Beach oceanside resort district and Williamsburg are primarily centers of tourism. Virginia Beach is the most populated city within the MSA though it functions more as a suburb. Additionally, Norfolk is part of the Virginia Beach-Norfolk, VA-NC [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area), which includes the Virginia Beach-Norfolk-Newport News, VA-NC MSA, the [Elizabeth City, North Carolina Micropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_City,_North_Carolina_Micropolitan_Statistical_Area), and the [Kill Devil Hills, NC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Devil_Hills,_NC) Micropolitan Statistical Area.&#x20;

### Sea level rise and subsidence



Population density and elevation above sea level around the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay). Norfolk is especially vulnerable to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).

Being low-lying and largely surrounded by water, Norfolk is particularly vulnerable to [rising sea levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) caused by [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-44] In addition, the land on which it is built is [slowly subsiding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidence). Some areas already flood regularly at high tide, and the city commissioned a study in 2012 to investigate how to address the issue in the future: it reported the cost of dealing with a sea-level rise of one foot would be around \$1,000,000,000. Since then, scientists at the [Virginia Institute of Marine Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Institute_of_Marine_Science) in 2013 have estimated that if current trends hold, the sea in Norfolk will rise by 5 and 1/2 feet or more by the end of this century.[^cite_note-\:0-45][^cite_note-\:1-46]

### Climate

Narrative below is based on climate data from the 1991–2020 period. Norfolk has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate), typical of the [Southern United States](179553.md), and its USDA Hardiness Zone is 8a. Spring arrives in March with mild days and cool nights, and by late May, the temperature has warmed up considerably to herald warm summer days. Summers are consistently warm and humid, but the nearby Atlantic Ocean often exercises a slight cooling effect on daytime high temperatures, but a slight warming effect on nighttime low temperatures (compared to areas farther inland). As such, temperatures reach 90 °F (32 °C) or higher on an average 35 days annually,[^cite_note-NWS_Wakefield,_VA_(AKQ)-49] and 100 °F (37.8 °C) are uncommon, occurring in fewer than one-third of all years.[^cite_note-50] On average, July is the warmest month, with a normal mean temperature of 81.1 °F (27.3 °C).[^cite_note-NWS_Wakefield,_VA_(AKQ)-49] On average, July and August are the wettest months, due to frequent summer thunderstorm activity. In August and September, rainfall remains high, due to rising frequency of tropical activity (hurricanes and tropical storms), which can bring high winds and heavy rains. These usually brush Norfolk and only occasionally make landfalls in the area; the highest-risk period is mid-August to the end of September. [Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn) is marked by mild to warm days and cooler nights. Winter is usually mild in Norfolk, with average winter days featuring lows near or slightly above freezing and highs in the upper-40s to mid-50s (8 to 13 °C). On average, the coldest month of the year is January, with a normal mean temperature of 42.2 °F (5.7 °C),[^cite_note-NWS_Wakefield,_VA_(AKQ)-49] Snow occurs sporadically, with an average winter accumulation of 6.2 in (15.7 cm).[^cite_note-NWS_Wakefield,_VA_(AKQ)-49] Norfolk's record high was 105 °F (41 °C) on August 7, 1918, and July 24 and 25, 2010, and the record low was −3 °F (−19 °C) recorded on [January 21, 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_1985_Arctic_outbreak).[^cite_note-NWS_Wakefield,_VA_(AKQ)-49]

Of all households, 32.9% were married-couple households, 25.0% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 34.7% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. There were 101,386 housing units, of which 7.8% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 2.0% and the rental vacancy rate was 6.2%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-72]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Norfolk, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Norfolk,_Virginia)

The waterways which almost completely surround the Hampton Roads region play an important part in the local economy. As a strategic location at the mouth of the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay), its protected deep-water channels serve as a major trade artery for the [import](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import) and [export](307592.md) of goods from across the [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_States), [Mid-West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States), and internationally.



Nauticus and [USS *Wisconsin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wisconsin_\(BB-64\))

In addition to commercial activities, Hampton Roads is a major military center, particularly for the [United States Navy](20518076.md), and Norfolk serves as the home for [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md), the world's largest naval installation. Located on [Sewell's Point Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewell's_Point), in the northwest corner of the city, the station is the headquarters of the [United States Fleet Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fleet_Forces_Command) (formerly known as the Atlantic Fleet), which compromises over 62,000 active-duty personnel, 75 ships, and 132 aircraft. The base also serves as the headquarters to [NATO](21133.md)'s [Allied Command Transformation](2789125.md).[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

The region also plays an important role in defense contracting, with particular emphasis in the shipbuilding and ship repair businesses for the city of Norfolk. Major private shipyards located in Norfolk or the [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads) area include: [Huntington Ingalls Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Ingalls_Industries) (formerly Northrop Grumman Newport News) in Newport News, [BAE Systems Norfolk Ship Repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Ship_Repair), [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics) [NASSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Steel_and_Shipbuilding_Company) Norfolk, and Colonna's Shipyard Inc., while the US Navy's [Norfolk Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Naval_Shipyard) is just across the [Downtown Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Tunnel) in [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia). Most contracts fulfilled by these shipyards are issued by the Navy, though some private commercial repair also takes place. Over 35% of Gross Regional Product (which includes the entire Norfolk-Newport News-Virginia Beach MSA), is attributable to defense spending, and that 75% of all regional growth since 2001 is attributable to increases in defense spending.[^cite_note-Hampton_Roads_Statistics-84]

After the military, the second largest and most important industry for Hampton Roads and Norfolk based on economic impact are the region's cargo ports. Headquartered in Norfolk, the [Virginia Port Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Port_Authority) (VPA) is a [Commonwealth of Virginia](32432.md) owned entity that, in turn, owns and operates three major port facilities in Hampton Roads for break-bulk and container type cargo. In Norfolk, [Norfolk International Terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_International_Terminals) (NIT) represents one of those three facilities and is home to the world's largest and fastest container cranes.[^cite_note-NIT_Facilities-85] Together, the three terminals of the VPA handled a total of over 2 million [TEUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization) and 475,000 tons of breakbulk cargo in 2006, making it the second busiest port on the east coast of North America by total cargo volume after the Port of New York and New Jersey.[^cite_note-Hampton_Roads_Ports-86]

In addition to NIT, Norfolk is home to the Lambert's Point docks, the largest coal trans-shipment point in the Northern Hemisphere, with an annual capacity of approximately 48,000,000 tons.[^cite_note-v335-87] [Bituminous coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituminous_coal) is primarily sourced from the Appalachian mountains in western Virginia, [West Virginia](32905.md), and [Kentucky](16846.md). The coal is loaded onto trains and sent to the port where it is unloaded onto large breakbulk cargo ships and destined for New England, Europe, and Asia.

Between 1925 and 2007, [Ford Motor Company](30433662.md) operated [Norfolk Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Assembly), a manufacturing plant located on the [Elizabeth River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_River_\(Virginia\)) that had produced the Model-T, sedans and station wagons before building F-150 pick-up trucks.[^cite_note-vp1-88] Before it closed, the plant employed more than 2,600 people at the 2,800,000 sq ft (260,000 m<sup>2</sup>) facility.[^cite_note-vp1-88]



The [Norfolk World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_World_Trade_Center)&#x20;

Unlike most cruise ship terminals which are located in industrial areas, the downtown location of Norfolk's terminal has received favorable reviews from both tourists and the cruise lines who enjoy its proximity to the city's hotels, restaurants, shopping, and cultural amenities.[^cite_note-Cruise_Norfolk-99]

## Arts and culture



The [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) Statue

Due to its urban density, Norfolk acts as the cultural hub of the Hampton Roads region. In addition to its museums, Norfolk is the principal home for several major performing arts organizations. The city hosts numerous annual festivals and parades, many in [Town Point Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Point_Park) or elsewhere in downtown.

### Museums and galleries

The nationally acclaimed [Chrysler Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Museum_of_Art), the area's most comprehensive art museum, has its campus at the intersection of the [Ghent district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent_district), the Freemason neighborhood, and the NEON district.[^cite_note-102] Since opening in 1933, the museum's main building has been expanded six times to allow for larger glass galleries, generous space for [Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Art) works, and more. Major improvements were completed in 2014, and today the museum features more than 50 galleries, a restaurant, and catering facilities, as well as galleries for traveling exhibits.[^cite_note-Chrysler_Museum_Renovation-103][^cite_note-Chrysler_Museum_Road_Show-104] Of particular note are the American [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) marble sculptures, the extensive glass collection, and the Glass Studio, which has live demonstrations daily.[^cite_note-Chrysler_Museum_Glass_Studio-105]

The Chrysler Museum of Art also administers the 1792 Moses Myers House Museum in the Freemason District, next to MacArthur Mall. This museum interprets Norfolk's history and the lives and legacy of Norfolk's first Jewish family. Seventy percent of the objects in the home are original to the Myers in the early nineteenth century.[^cite_note-106] The museum offers weekend tours and special monthly programming. In October 2022, the council of City of Norfolk, which owns the property, voted to proceed with the possibility of selling it, conjecturing the entirety of the property—the main house, the attached dwelling of the Myers’ enslaved servants, and the historic garden—could be sold as part of a package to developers, perhaps to operate as a bed and breakfast.[^cite_note-107] Multiple entities, including the Norfolk Historical Society, have expressed distress and outrage.

The [Hermitage Foundation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Foundation_Museum_\(Norfolk,_Virginia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), located in an early 20th-century [Tudor-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Revival_architecture) home on a 12-acre (49,000 m<sup>2</sup>) estate fronting the [Lafayette River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_River), is found in the Lochaven neighborhood near the northern terminus of the Elizabeth River Trail that connects many of the city's sites of cultural interest.[^cite_note-108] The Hermitage features an eclectic collection of Asian and Western art, including [Chinese bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_bronze) and ceramics, [Persian rugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_rugs), and [ivory carvings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_carvings), as well as changing exhibitions, arts classes, and special events.[^cite_note-109] The Hermitage Foundation Museum is the only [Smithsonian Affiliate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Affiliate) in the Hampton Roads region.[^cite_note-110]

Downtown Norfolk has several other museums of national significance.



[Nauticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauticus) (National Maritime Center)

[Nauticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauticus), the National Maritime Center, opened on the downtown waterfront in 1994. It features hands-on exhibits, interactive theaters, [aquaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium), digital high-definition films and an extensive variety of educational programs. Since 2000, Nauticus has been home to the battleship [USS *Wisconsin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wisconsin_\(BB-64\)), the last battleship to be built in the United States. It served briefly in [World War II](32927.md) and later in the [Korean](16772.md) and [Gulf Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War).[^cite_note-Nauticus-111] [Wisconsin Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Square) is nearby.

The [MacArthur Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_City_Hall), located in the nineteenth-century Norfolk courthouse and city hall in downtown, contains the tombs of General [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) and his wife, a museum and a vast research library, personal belongings (including his famous [corncob pipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corncob_pipe)) and a short film that chronicles his life.[^cite_note-MacArthur_Memorial-112]

Speciality museums include the [Hunter House Victorian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_House_Victorian_Museum) in the Freemason neighborhood and the [Norfolk Southern Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Museum) in downtown.

### Public art



[JANAF Shopping Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JANAF_Shopping_Center) mermaid - "Jana"

The city is known for its "Mermaids on Parade," a public art program launched in 2002 to place mermaid statues all over the city. Tourists can take a walking tour of downtown and locate 17 mermaids while others can be found further afield.[^cite_note-Mermaids_on_Parade-113]

The NEON district has dozens of murals, many of which are supported through the City of Norfolk's Public Arts Commission.[^cite_note-114]

### Performing arts



[Harrison Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Opera_House)

Norfolk has a variety of performing groups with regular seasons and which also make appearances in the city's annual festivals. The [Virginia Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Symphony_Orchestra), founded in 1920, has been leader in the regional arts scene. Directed by [JoAnn Falletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JoAnn_Falletta) from 1991 until 2020, the orchestra's music director is now [Erik Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Jacobsen_\(conductor\)).[^cite_note-115] Most Norfolk performances take place at [Chrysler Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Hall) in the [Scope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Scope) complex downtown. The orchestra provides musicians for many other performing arts organizations in the area.[^cite_note-Virginia_Symphony-116]

The Virginia Stage Company, founded in 1968, is one of the country's leading regional theaters and produces a full season of plays in the [Wells Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Theatre) downtown. The company shares facilities with the [Governor's School for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor's_School_for_the_Arts).[^cite_note-Virginia_Stage_Company-117]'

The [Virginia Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Opera) was founded in Norfolk in 1974. Its artistic director since its inception has been Peter Mark,[^cite_note-118] who conducted his 100th opera production for the VOA in 2008. Though performances are staged statewide, the company's principal venue is the [Harrison Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Opera_House) in the [Ghent district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent_district).[^cite_note-Virginia_Opera-119]

Large-scale concerts are held at either the [Norfolk Scope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Scope) arena or the [Ted Constant Convocation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Constant_Convocation_Center) at [ODU](308123.md), while [The Norva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norva_Theatre) provides a more intimate atmosphere for smaller groups. Other Norfolk cultural venues include the [Attucks Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attucks_Theatre), the Jeanne and George Roper Performing Arts Center (formerly the Loew's State Theater) and the Naro Expanded Cinema.

### Festivals

A range of arts and cultural festivals take place annually in Norfolk. The [Virginia Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Arts_Festival), founded in 1997, is based in Norfolk and has events throughout the region, drawing in arts from around the world and featuring local talent. One of the key events of the festival is the [Virginia International Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_International_Tattoo).[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] The [Norfolk NATO Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_NATO_Festival), formerly the International Azela Festival, has taken place each spring since 1951 and is the longest continually running festival in the Hampton Roads Region. The Norfolk NATO Festival highlights Norfolk's role as the North American Headquarters of [NATO](21133.md) and fosters cultural exchange and appreciation of NATO allies.[^cite_note-122] The Stockley Gardens Art Festival, which takes place in parks the historic Ghent neighborhood, occurs twice yearly, in May and October. The festival draws vendors from well beyond the region and attracts upwards of 20,000 visitors.[^cite_note-123] The [St. Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_Day) annual parade in the city's [Ocean View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_View,_Virginia) neighborhood, on the northern edge of the city, celebrates Ocean View's rich Irish heritage.[^cite_note-Norfolk_St._Patrick's_Day_Parade-124]

Harborfest, the region's largest annual festival, celebrated its thirtieth anniversary in 2006. It is held during the first weekend of June in Town Point Park and celebrates the region's proximity and attachment to the water. The Parade of Sails (numerous tall sailing ships from around the world form in line and sail past downtown before docking at the marina), music concerts, regional food, and a large fireworks display highlight this three-day festival.[^cite_note-Festevents-125] Bayou Boogaloo and Cajun Food Festival, a celebration of the [Cajun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajun) people and culture, had small beginnings. This three-day festival during the third week of June has become one of the largest in the region and, in addition to serving up [Cajun cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajun_cuisine), also features [Cajun music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajun_music).[^cite_note-Festevents-125] Norfolk's [Fourth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)) celebration of American independence contains a spectacular fireworks display and a special Navy reenlistment ceremony.[^cite_note-Festevents-125] The [Norfolk Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Jazz_Festival?action=edit\&redlink=1), though smaller by comparison to some of the big city jazz festivals, still manages to attract the country's top jazz performers. It is held in August.[^cite_note-Festevents-125] The Town Point Virginia Wine Festival has become a showcase for Virginia-produced wines and has enjoyed increasing success over the years. Virginia's burgeoning wine industry has become noted both within the United States and on an international level. The festival has grown with the industry. Wines can be sampled and then purchased by the bottle and/or case directly from the winery kiosks. This event takes place during the third weekend of October. There is also a Spring Wine Festival held during the second weekend of May.[^cite_note-Festevents-125]

### Role in revitalization

The revitalization of downtown Norfolk has helped to improve the Hampton Roads cultural scene. Many of Norfolk's attractions are now connected by the 10.5-mile (16.9 km) long Elizabeth River Trail, a pedestrian and bike trail that winds along the city's waterfront. The trail's first segment of opened in 2003 on land donated by Norfolk Southern.[^cite_note-126] A large number of clubs, representing a wide range of music interests and sophistication now line the lower Granby Street area.[^cite_note-Nightlife_and_Entertainment_in_Downtown_Norfolk-127] The nearby [Waterside Festival Marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterside_Festival_Marketplace) has also continued to be successful as a nightclub and bar venue.[^cite_note-128]

## Parks and recreation



Canal at the [Norfolk Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Botanical_Garden)

Norfolk has a variety of parks and open spaces in its city parks system.[^cite_note-129] The city maintains three beaches on its north shore in the [Ocean View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_View,_Virginia) area. Five additional parks contain picnic facilities and playgrounds for children. The city also has some community pools open to city residents.[^cite_note-Norfolk_Parks-130] [Town Point Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Point_Park) in downtown plays host to a wide variety of annual events from early spring through late fall.

The [Norfolk Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Botanical_Garden), opened in 1939, is a 155-acre (0.6 km<sup>2</sup>) [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) and [arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum) located near the Norfolk International Airport. It is open year-round.[^cite_note-Norfolk_Botanical_Gardens-131]



[Virginia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Zoo)

The [Virginia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Zoo), opened in 1900, is a 65-acre (260,000 m<sup>2</sup>) [zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo) with hundreds of animals on display, including the [critically endangered](7451605.md) [Siberian tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_tiger) and [threatened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened_species) [white rhino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_rhino).[^cite_note-Virginia_Zoo-132]

## Sports



[Harbor Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Park)

Main article: [Sports in Norfolk, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Norfolk,_Virginia)

Norfolk serves as home to the two highest level professional franchises in the state of Virginia — the [Norfolk Tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Tides) play baseball in the [International League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_League), and the [Norfolk Admirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Admirals_\(ECHL\)) play ice hockey in the [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL).[^cite_note-133]

Norfolk has two universities with Division I sports teams — the [Old Dominion Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dominion_Monarchs) and the [Norfolk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_State_University) Spartans — which provide many sports including football, basketball, and baseball.[^cite_note-Admirals-134][^cite_note-Monarchs-135][^cite_note-Spartans-136]

From 1970 to 1976, Norfolk served as the home court (along with [Hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton,_Virginia), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia), and [Roanoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke,_Virginia)) for the [Virginia Squires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Squires) regional professional basketball franchise of the now-defunct [American Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association) (ABA). From 1970 to 1971, the Squires played their Norfolk home games at the [Old Dominion University Fieldhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dominion_University_Fieldhouse). In November 1971, the Squires played their Norfolk home games at the new [Norfolk Scope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Scope) arena, until the team and the ABA league folded in May 1976.[^cite_note-137]

In 1971, Norfolk built an entertainment and sports complex, featuring [Chrysler Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Hall) and the 13,800-seat [Norfolk Scope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Scope) indoor arena, located in the northern section of downtown. Norfolk Scope has served as a venue for major events including the American Basketball Association All-Star Game in 1974,[^cite_note-138] and the first and second [NCAA Women's Division I Basketball Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Women's_Division_I_Basketball_Championship) (also known as the *Women's Final Four*) in 1982 and 1983.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-1983tourney-140]

Norfolk is also home to the [Norfolk Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Blues) Rugby Football Club. Their home playing fields are Lafayette Park in Norfolk and the [Virginia Beach Sportsplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach_Sportsplex) in Virginia Beach.[^cite_note-141]

[Virginia Beach City FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach_City_FC) is an American professional soccer club based in Norfolk playing in the [National Premier Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Premier_Soccer_League) (NPSL) in the Mid-Atlantic Conference of the Northeast Region. Home matches are played at Powhatan Field.[^cite_note-142]

[National Wrestling Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wrestling_Alliance), [Jim Crockett Promotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crockett_Promotions), [World Championship Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championship_Wrestling), and [World Wrestling Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wrestling_Entertainment) have all presented wrestling shows at Norfolk Arena and the Scope from the 1960s to today, with many of these being [Pay Per View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_Per_View) events. Six-time World Heavyweight Wrestling Champion [Lou Thesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Thesz) lived in Norfolk and opened a wrestling school, Virginia Wrestling Academy, downtown in 1988.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

Norfolk is an [independent city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)#Virginia) with services that both counties and cities in Virginia provide, such as a [sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriffs_in_the_United_States), social services, and a court system. Norfolk operates under a council-manager form of government.

Norfolk city government consists of a [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) with representatives from seven districts serving in a [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) and oversight capacity, as well as a popularly elected, at-large [mayor](101146.md). The [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager) serves as head of the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) and supervises all city departments and executing policies adopted by the council. Citizens in each of the five wards elect one council representative each to serve a four-year term. There are two additional council members elected from two citywide "superwards." The city council meets at City Hall weekly[^cite_note-Norfolk_City_Council-147] and, as of May 2023, consists of: Mayor Kenneth Cooper Alexander; Mamie Johnson, Ward 3; Danica Royster, Superward 7; John E. Paige, Ward 4; Courtney Doyle, Ward 2; Vice-Mayor Martin Thomas, Ward 1; Andria McClellan, Superward 6; Thomas R. Smigiel Jr. Ward 5.[^cite_note-Norfolk_City_Council-147]

Further information: [List of mayors of Norfolk, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Norfolk,_Virginia)

The city government has an infrastructure to create close working relationships with its citizens. Norfolk's city government provides services for neighborhoods, including service centers and civic leagues that interact directly with members of city council. Such services include preserving area histories, home rehabilitation centers, outreach programs, and a university that trains citizens in neighborhood clean-up, event planning, neighborhood leadership, and financial planning.[^cite_note-Neighborhoods-148] [Norfolk's police department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Police_Department) also provides support for neighborhood watch programs including a citizens' training academy, security design, a police athletic program for youth, and business watch programs.[^cite_note-Neighborhood_Police-149]

Norfolk also has a federal courthouse for the [United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia](751799.md). The [Walter E. Hoffman U.S. Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_E._Hoffman_U.S._Courthouse) in Norfolk has four judges, four magistrate judges, and two bankruptcy judges.[^cite_note-150] Additionally, Norfolk has its own [general district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_court) and [circuit courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_court), which convene downtown.[^cite_note-151] It is considered a Democratic stronghold.

Since redistricting Norfolk is located in [Virginia's 3rd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia's_3rd_congressional_district), served by U.S. representative [Robert C. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_C._Scott) (Democrat).

## Education

Main article: [Education in Norfolk, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Norfolk,_Virginia)

[Norfolk City Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_City_Public_Schools), the public school system, comprises five high schools, eight middle schools, 34 elementary schools, and nine special-purpose/preschools. In 2005, Norfolk Public Schools won the \$1 million [Broad Prize for Urban Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Prize_for_Urban_Education) for having demonstrated, "the greatest overall performance and improvement in student achievement while reducing achievement gaps for poor and minority students".[^cite_note-Broad_Prize_for_Urban_Education_Award-152] The city had previously been nominated in 2003 and 2004. There are also a number of [private schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_schools) located in the city, the oldest of which, [Norfolk Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Academy), was founded in 1728.[^cite_note-153] Religious schools located in the city include St. Pius X Catholic School, Alliance Christian School, Christ the King School, [Norfolk Christian Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Christian_Schools) and Trinity Lutheran School.[^cite_note-Private_Schools-154] The city also hosts the [Governor's School for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor's_School_for_the_Arts) which holds performances and classes at the [Wells Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Theatre).



The Jones Institute for Reproductive Medicine at [Eastern Virginia Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Virginia_Medical_School)

Norfolk is home to three public universities and one private. It also hosts a [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college) campus in downtown. [Old Dominion University](308123.md), founded as the Norfolk Division of the [College of William & Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_William_&_Mary) in 1930, became an independent institution in 1962 and now offers degrees in 68 undergraduate and 95 (60 masters/35 doctoral) graduate degree programs.[^cite_note-155] [Eastern Virginia Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Virginia_Medical_School), founded as a community medical school by the surrounding jurisdictions in 1973, is noted for its research into [reproductive medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_medicine)[^cite_note-About_Jones_Institute-156] and is located in the region's major medical complex in the [Ghent district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent_district). [Norfolk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_State_University), founded in 1935 is the second largest HBCU, in Virginia. Norfolk State offers degrees in a wide variety of [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts), Social Work, Nursing, and Engineering. [Virginia State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_University) being the first largest HBCU in Virginia, which was founded in 1882.[^cite_note-AboutNSU-157] [Virginia Wesleyan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Wesleyan_College) is a small private liberal arts college and shares its eastern border with the neighboring city of Virginia Beach.[^cite_note-About_VWU-158] [Tidewater Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidewater_Community_College) offers two-year degrees and specialized training programs and is located in downtown. Additionally, several for-profit schools operate in the city.

### Norfolk Public Library

Norfolk Public Library, Virginia's first public library, consists of one main library, two anchor libraries, nine branch libraries and a [bookmobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookmobile). The library also has a local history and genealogy room and contains government documents dating back to the 19th century. The libraries offer services such as computer classes, book reviews, tax forms, and online book clubs.[^cite_note-Libraries-159]

The Slover Library, centrally located in the heart of downtown Norfolk, holds over 133,000 books and resources available for borrowing, hosts numerous classes and community events, houses the history Sargeant Memorial Collection, and offers studio spaces. Technology areas include a Sound Studio, Design Studio, Production Studio, YOUmedia lab, Maker Studio (Selden Market), and Computer Room and Training Lab.[^cite_note-Slover_Library-160]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Further information: [Transportation in Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Norfolk) and [Hampton Roads Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads_Transit)



[Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads_Bridge%E2%80%93Tunnel)

The city has a long history as a strategic military and transportation point, where many railroad lines started. Norfolk was the terminus of the [Atlantic and Danville Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_and_Danville_Railway) in 1890.[^cite_note-AmRails2020-167] It is linked to its neighbors by an extensive network of [interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System), [bridges](3397.md), [tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel), and three [bridge-tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge-tunnel) complexes, which are the only bridge-tunnels in the United States.



Hampton Roads Transit bus at [Sentara Norfolk General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentara_Norfolk_General_Hospital)

Norfolk is linked with its neighbors through an extensive network of arterial and [Interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System), [bridges](3397.md), [tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel), and [bridge-tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge-tunnel) complexes. The major east–west routes are [Interstate 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_64_in_Virginia), [U.S. Route 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_58_in_Virginia) ([Virginia Beach Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach_Boulevard)) and [U.S. Route 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_60_in_Virginia) (Ocean View Avenue). The major north–south routes are [U.S. Route 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_13_in_Virginia) and [U.S. Route 460](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_460_in_Virginia), also known as [Granby Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granby_Street). Other main roadways in Norfolk include [Newtown Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Route_403), [Waterside Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterside_Drive), [Tidewater Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidewater_Drive), and [Military Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Highway). The Hampton Roads Beltway (I-64 and [I-664](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-664)) makes a loop around Norfolk.

Norfolk is primarily served by the [Norfolk International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_International_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **ORF**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **KORF**, [FAA](11186.md) [LID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_identifier): **ORF**), now the region's major commercial airport. The airport is located near the Chesapeake Bay, along with the city limits straddling neighboring [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia).[^cite_note-ORF_History-168] Seven airlines provide nonstop services to twenty five destinations. ORF had 3,703,664 passengers take off or land at its facility and 68,778,934 pounds of cargo were processed through its facilities.[^cite_note-ORF_Statistics-169] [Newport News/Williamsburg International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News/Williamsburg_International_Airport) also provides commercial air service for the [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads) area.[^cite_note-Newport_News/Williamsburg_International_Airport-170] NNWIA is also the only airport in the region with direct international flights, as of February 2013. The [Chesapeake Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Regional_Airport) provides general aviation services and is located 5 mi (8.0 km) outside the city limits.

Norfolk is served by [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)'s [Northeast Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional) service through the [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_\(Amtrak_station\)) station, located in downtown Norfolk adjacent to [Harbor Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Park) stadium. The line runs west along [Norfolk Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Railway) trackage, paralleling the [US Route 460](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_460_in_Virginia) corridor to [Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersburg,_Virginia), thence on to [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia) and beyond. A high-speed rail connection at Richmond to both the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) and the [Southeast High-Speed Rail Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_High_Speed_Rail_Corridor) are also under study.[^cite_note-SEHSR-171]

[Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) provides service from a central bus terminal in downtown Norfolk.[^cite_note-Today's_Bus-172] The [Virginia Breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Breeze) intercity bus line added service to Virginia Beach on April 20, 2026, stopping at an HRT park and ride on [Military Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Highway). The added service, known as the Tidewater Current, connects to [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach), [Newport News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News), [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia) and [Richmond International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_International_Airport), [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia), and [Staunton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staunton,_Virginia).[^cite_note-173]

In April 2007, construction of the new \$36 million Half Moone Cruise Terminal was completed downtown adjacent to the Nauticus Museum, providing a [state-of-the-art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-of-the-art) permanent structure for various cruise lines and passengers wishing to embark from Norfolk. Previously, makeshift structures were used to embark/disembark passengers, supplies, and crew.[^cite_note-Half_Moone_Terminal-98]

The [Intracoastal Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracoastal_Waterway) passes through Norfolk. Norfolk also has extensive frontage and port facilities on the navigable portions of the [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Branch_Elizabeth_River) and [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Branch_Elizabeth_River) Branches of the [Elizabeth River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_River_\(Virginia\)).



Ferry to Portsmouth

[Light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail), [bus](4146.md), [ferry](50771.md) and [paratransit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratransit) services are provided by [Hampton Roads Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads_Transit) (HRT), the regional [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) system headquartered in Hampton. HRT buses operate throughout Norfolk and South Hampton Roads and onto the Peninsula all the way up to Williamsburg. Other routes travel to [Smithfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithfield,_Virginia). HRT's ferry service connects downtown Norfolk to Old Town Portsmouth.[^cite_note-HRT_Service-174] Additional services include an HOV express bus to the [Norfolk Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Naval_Base), paratransit services, park-and-ride lots, and the Norfolk Electric Trolley, which provides service in the downtown area.[^cite_note-About_HRT-175] The [Tide light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide_Light_Rail) service began operations in August 2011.[^cite_note-HRT_Pressroom-176] The light rail is a starter route running along the southern portion of Norfolk, commencing at Newtown Road and passing through stations serving areas such as [Norfolk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_State_University) and [Harbor Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Park) before going through the heart of downtown Norfolk and terminating at [Sentara Norfolk General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentara_Norfolk_General_Hospital).[^cite_note-Norfolk_Light_Rail_Project-177] Hampton Roads Transportation, Inc. dispatches Black and White Cabs of Norfolk, Yellow Cab of Norfolk and Norfolk Checker Cab.

### Utilities

Water and sewer services are provided by the city's Department of Utilities. Norfolk receives its electricity from [Dominion Virginia Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Virginia_Power) which has local sources including the Chesapeake Energy Center (a gas power plant), coal-fired plants in [Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Virginia) and [Southampton County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_County,_Virginia), and the Surry Nuclear Power Plant. Norfolk-headquartered [Virginia Natural Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGL_Resources#Virginia_Natural_Gas), a subsidiary of [AGL Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGL_Resources), distributes [natural gas](22131.md) to the city from storage plants in [James City County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_City_County,_Virginia) and Chesapeake.

Norfolk's water quality has been recognized one of the cleanest water systems in the United States and ranked as the fourth best in the United States by *[Men's Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_Health_\(magazine\))*.[^cite_note-Norfolk_Utilities-178] The city of Norfolk has a tremendous capacity for clean fresh water. The city owns nine reservoirs: Lake Whitehurst, Little Creek Reservoir, Lake Lawson, Lake Smith, Lake Wright, Lake Burnt Mills, Western Branch Reservoir, Lake Prince and Lake Taylor.[^cite_note-179] The Virginia tidewater area has grown faster than the local freshwater supply. The river water has always been salty, and the fresh groundwater is no longer available in most areas. Currently, water for the cities of Chesapeake and Virginia Beach is pumped from [Lake Gaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Gaston) (which straddles the Virginia-North Carolina border) into the City of Norfolk's reservoir system and then diverted to the City of Chesapeake for treatment by the City of Chesapeake. Virginia Beach's portion of water is treated by the City of Norfolk at Moores Bridges water treatment plant and then piped into Virginia Beach. The pipeline is 76 mi (122 km) long and 60 in (1,500 mm) in diameter. Much of its follows the former [right-of-way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-of-way_\(railroad\)) of an abandoned portion of the [Virginian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginian_Railway).[^cite_note-180] It is capable of pumping 60 million gallons of water per day; Virginia Beach and Chesapeake are partners in the project.[^cite_note-181]

The city provides wastewater services for residents and transports wastewater to the regional Hampton Roads Sanitation District treatment plants.[^cite_note-Norfolk_Utilities-178]

### Healthcare



[Sentara Norfolk General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentara_Norfolk_General_Hospital)

Because of the prominence of the [Naval Medical Center Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Medical_Center_Portsmouth) and the Hampton [VA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Health_Administration) Medical Center in Hampton, Norfolk has had a strong role in medicine. Norfolk is served by [Sentara Norfolk General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentara_Norfolk_General_Hospital), [Sentara Leigh Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentara_Leigh_Hospital), and [Bon Secours DePaul Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Secours_DePaul_Medical_Center). The city is also home to the [Children's Hospital of The King's Daughters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Hospital_of_The_King's_Daughters) and [Lake Taylor Transitional Care Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Taylor_Transitional_Care_Hospital).[^cite_note-Hospital_Listings-182]

Norfolk is home to [Eastern Virginia Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Virginia_Medical_School) (EVMS), which is known for its specialists in [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus), [dermatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dermatology), and [obstetrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetrics). It achieved international fame on March 1, 1980, when Drs. Georgianna and Howard Jones opened the first [in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization)[^cite_note-183] clinic in the U.S. at EVMS. The country's first in-vitro [test-tube baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test-tube_baby) was born there in December 1981.[^cite_note-Jones_Institute_-_About_Us-184]

The international headquarters of [Operation Smile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Smile), a nonprofit organization that specializes in repairing facial deformities in underprivileged children from around the globe, is located in the city.[^cite_note-Operation_Smile-185]

[Physicians for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicians_for_Peace?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-186] a non-profit that focuses on providing training and education to medical professionals in the developing world, is based in Norfolk.
