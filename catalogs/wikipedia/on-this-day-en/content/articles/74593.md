**Bydgoszcz**[^cite_note-16] is a city in northern [Poland](22936.md) and the largest city in the historical region of [Kuyavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavia).[^cite_note-TERYT-17] Straddling the confluence of the [Vistula River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula_River) and its [left-bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_\(geography\)) tributary, the [Brda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brda_\(river\)), the strategic location of Bydgoszcz has made it an inland port and a vital centre for trade and transportation. With a city population of 339,053 as of December 2021,[^cite_note-population-8] Bydgoszcz is the eighth-largest city in Poland. Today, it is the seat of [Bydgoszcz County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_County) and one of the two capitals of the [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) as a seat of its centrally appointed governor, a [voivode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivode).

[Bydgoszcz metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_metropolitan_area) comprising the city and several adjacent communities is inhabited by half a million people, and forms a part of an extended polycentric [Bydgoszcz-Toruń metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz-Toru%C5%84_metropolitan_area) with a population of approximately 0.8 million inhabitants. Since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Bydgoszcz served as a [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) until [partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) and experienced the industrialisation period bolstered by the construction of the [Bydgoszcz Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Canal) in the late 18th century. Its academic and cultural landscape is shaped by [Casimir the Great University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Wielki_University_in_Bydgoszcz), [Bydgoszcz University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_University_of_Science_and_Technology), the [Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University_Ludwik_Rydygier_Collegium_Medicum_in_Bydgoszcz) of [Nicolaus Copernicus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University_in_Toru%C5%84), [Feliks Nowowiejski Music Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliks_Nowowiejski_Music_Academy), the [Pomeranian Philharmonic](48480582.md), and the [Opera Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Nova_Bydgoszcz). Bydgoszcz also plays a role of the biggest centre of [NATO headquarters in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_forces_in_Poland).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The city is served by an [international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski_Airport) and is a member of [Eurocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocities).

Bydgoszcz is an architecturally rich city, with [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture), [Neoclassicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture), [Neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival), [Neo-Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Revival), [Modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_architecture) and [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) styles present, for which, combined with extensive green spaces, it has earned the nickname *Little Berlin*.[^cite_note-LitBer1-1] The notable granaries on [Mill Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Island,_Bydgoszcz) and along the riverside belong to one of the most recognized [timber-framed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber_framing) landmarks in Poland.[^cite_note-20] In 2023, the city entered the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) and was named UNESCO [City of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Music).[^cite_note-21]

## Etymology

The name *Bydgoszcz*, originally *Bydgoszcza*, derives from *Bydgost*, a personal name, and the suffix -*ja*, denoting ownership. The German name *Bromberg* is an alteration of *Braheberg*, meaning "hill on the Brahe River" (Polish: *Brda*).[^cite_note-22] The [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) names for the city are *Bidgostia* and *Civitas Bidgostiensis*.

In [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), the city's name has feminine grammatical gender.

## History

### Early history and first settlements



Mockup of the old castle in the Old Town

In [ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history) times, there was a development of settlements related to lively trade contacts with the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), as [Askaukalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askaukalis) a convenient location of today's Bydgoszcz laid on the [Amber Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Road) heading northwest to the [Baltic](3335.md) coastline avoiding crossing the Vistula river.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

During the [early Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs) period a fishing settlement called *Bydgoszcza* ("Bydgostia" in Latin) became a stronghold on the Vistula [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route).

The [gród](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%B3d) of Bydgoszcz was built between 1037 and 1053 during the reign of [Casimir I the Restorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_I_the_Restorer). In the 13th century it was the site of a [castellany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castellany), mentioned in 1238, probably founded in the early 12th century during the reign of [Bolesław III Wrymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_III_Wrymouth). In the 13th century, the church of [Saint Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Giles) was built as the first church of Bydgoszcz. The Germans later demolished it in the late 19th century.[^cite_note-TB-26] The first bridge was constructed at the reign of [Casimir I of Kuyavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_I_of_Kuyavia). In the early 14th century, the [Duchy of Bydgoszcz and Wyszogród](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bydgoszcz_and_Wyszogr%C3%B3d) was created, with Bydgoszcz serving as its capital with Wyszogród, a settlement today within its borders.

During the [Polish–Teutonic War (1326–1332)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Teutonic_War_\(1326%E2%80%931332\)), the city was captured and destroyed by the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights) in 1330.[^cite_note-TB-26] Briefly regained by Poland, it was occupied by the Teutonic Knights from 1331 to 1337 and annexed to their [monastic state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order) as *Bromberg*. In 1337, it was recaptured by Poland and was relinquished by the Knights in 1343 at their signing of the [Treaty of Kalisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kalisz_\(1343\)) along with [Dobrzyń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrzy%C5%84_Land) and the remainder of [Kuyavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavia).

### Royal city of Poland

King [Casimir III of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_III_the_Great) granted Bydgoszcz [city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_privileges) (charter) on 19 April 1346.[^cite_note-27] The king granted a number of privileges, regarding river trade on the [Brda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brda_\(river\)) and [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula) and the right to mint coins, and ordered the construction of the castle, which became the seat of the castellan.[^cite_note-VB-28] Bydgoszcz was an important [royal city of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Poland) located in the [Inowrocław Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inowroc%C5%82aw_Voivodeship).

The city increasingly saw an influx of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) after that date. In 1555, however, due to pressure from the clergy, the Jews were expelled and returned only with their annexation to [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) in 1772. After 1370, Bydgoszcz castle was the favourite residence of the grandson of the king and his would-be successor Duke [Casimir IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV,_Duke_of_Pomerania), who died there in 1377.[^cite_note-VB-28] In 1397 thanks to Queen [Jadwiga of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadwiga_of_Poland), a [Carmelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelites) convent was established in the city, the third in Poland after [Gdańsk](12099.md) and [Kraków](16815.md).[^cite_note-VB-28]



[Brick Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Gothic) [Bydgoszcz Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Cathedral)

During the [Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian%E2%80%93Teutonic_War) in 1409 the city was briefly captured by the Teutonic Knights.[^cite_note-TB-26] In the mid-15th century, during the [Thirteen Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Years'_War_\(1454%E2%80%931466\)), King [Casimir IV of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_of_Poland) often stayed in Bydgoszcz. At that time, the defensive walls were built[^cite_note-TB-26] and the [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) parish church (the present-day [Bydgoszcz Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Cathedral)). The city was developing dynamically thanks to river trade. Bydgoszcz [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery) and beer were popular throughout Poland. In the 15th and 16th centuries, Bydgoszcz was a significant location for [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) trading, one of the largest in Poland.[^cite_note-TB-26] The first mention of a school in Bydgoszcz is from 1466.[^cite_note-TB-26]

In 1480, a Bernardine monastery was established in Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-VB-28] The Bernardines erected a new Gothic church and founded a library, part of which has survived to this day.[^cite_note-VB-28] A [Sejm of the Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) was held in Bydgoszcz in 1520.[^cite_note-29] In 1522, after a decision taken by the Polish king, a [salt](1605200.md) depot was established in Bydgoszcz, the second in the region after [Toruń](31258.md).[^cite_note-TB-26] In 1594, Stanisław Cikowski founded a private [mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_\(facility\)), which in the early 17th century was transformed into a royal mint, one of the [leading mints in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Mint).[^cite_note-VB-28]

In 1621, on the occasion of the Polish victory over the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) at [Chocim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khotyn_\(1621\)), one of the most valuable and largest coins in the history of Europe was minted in Bydgoszcz – 100 ducats of [Sigismund III Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund_III_Vasa).[^cite_note-VB-28] In 1617 the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) came to the city, and subsequently established a Jesuit college.[^cite_note-TB-26]



Panorama of Bydgoszcz in 1657 by [Erik Dahlbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Dahlbergh) at the time of Swedish occupation.

During the year of 1629, shortly before the end of the [Polish-Swedish War of 1626–29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Swedish_War_\(1626%E2%80%9329\)), the town was conquered by Swedish troops led by king [Gustav II Adolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Adolphus_of_Sweden) of [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sweden) personally. During this war, the town suffered destruction.[^cite_note-30] The town was conquered a second and third time by Sweden in 1656 and 1657 during the [Second Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Northern_War). On the latter occasion, the castle was destroyed completely and has since remained a ruin. After the war only 94 houses were inhabited, 103 stood empty and 35 had burned down. The suburbs had also been considerably damaged.[^cite_note-31]

The [Treaty of Bromberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Bromberg), agreed in 1657 by King [John II Casimir Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_Casimir_Vasa) of [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) and Elector [Frederick William II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William_II_of_Prussia) of [Brandenburg-Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia), created a military alliance between Poland and Prussia while marking the withdrawal of Prussia from its alliance with Sweden.

After the [Convocation Sejm of 1764](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convocation_Sejm_\(1764\)), Bydgoszcz became one of three seats of the [Crown Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Tribunal) for the [Greater Poland Province of the Polish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Province_of_the_Polish_Crown) alongside [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84) and [Piotrków Trybunalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotrk%C3%B3w_Trybunalski).[^cite_note-TB-26] In 1766 royal cartographer [Franciszek Florian Czaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciszek_Florian_Czaki), during a meeting of the Committee of the Crown Treasury in [Warsaw](32908.md), proposed a plan of building a canal, which would connect the Vistula via the Brda with the [Noteć](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Note%C4%87) river. [Józef Wybicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Wybicki), Polish jurist and political activist best known as the author of the lyrics of the [national anthem of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_Is_Not_Yet_Lost), worked at the Crown Tribunal in Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-kd-32]

### Late modern period



Battle of Bydgoszcz in 1794 during [Kościuszko Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%9Bciuszko_Uprising).

In 1772, in the [First Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Partition_of_Poland), the town was acquired by the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) as Bromberg and incorporated into the [Netze District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netze_District) in the newly established province of [West Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Prussia). At the time, the town was seriously depressed and semi-derelict.[^cite_note-AEP-33] Under [Frederick the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great) the town revived, notably with the construction of a canal from Bromberg to [Nakel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nak%C5%82o_nad_Noteci%C4%85) (Nakło) which connected the north-flowing Vistula River via the Brda to the west-flowing [Noteć](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Note%C4%87), which in turn flowed to the [Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder_River) via the [Warta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta).[^cite_note-34] From this period until the end of the German Empire, a large majority of the city's inhabitants spoke German as their main language, and the city would later acquire the nickname "little Berlin" from its similar architectural appearance to the prewar image of the German capital and the work of shared architects such as [Friedrich Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Adler_\(architect\)), [Ferdinand Lepcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Lepcke), [Heinrich Seeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Seeling), or [Henry Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Gross_\(architect\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-LitBer2-4] During the [Kościuszko Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%9Bciuszko_Uprising), in 1794 the city was briefly recaptured by Poles, commanded by General [Jan Henryk Dąbrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Henryk_D%C4%85browski),[^cite_note-VB-28] and the local Polish administration was co-organized by [Józef Wybicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Wybicki).[^cite_note-kd-32]



A postcard (ca 1899-1916), on the left the [municipal theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Theatre,_Bydgoszcz), demolished in 1945.



[Bydgoszcz Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Canal) was one of the key contributions to the city's industrialisation.



An architectural ensemble of tenements built along the leat canal of the [Brda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brda_\(river\)) river near [Mill Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Island,_Bydgoszcz) would often become inspiration for local artists.



[Dworcowa Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dworcowa_Street) developed rapidly after the construction of the main railway station in 1851 and became a home for the [Prussian Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Eastern_Railway) [headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Eastern_Railway_Headquarters,_Bydgoszcz).

In 1807, after the defeat of Prussia by [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) and the signing of the [Treaty of Tilsit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tilsit), Bydgoszcz became part of the short-lived Polish [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw), within which it was the seat of the [Bydgoszcz Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Department). With Napoleon's defeat at the [Battle of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nations) in 1813, the town was re-annexed by Prussia as part of the [Grand Duchy of Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Posen) (Poznań), becoming the capital of the [Bromberg Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromberg_\(region\)). During the [November Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising), a [Polish insurgent organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_movements_in_partitioned_Poland_\(1795%E2%80%931918\)) was active in the city and local Poles helped smuggle volunteers, weapons and ammunition to the [Russian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Partition) of Poland.[^cite_note-35] After the fall of the uprising, one of the main escape routes for surviving insurgents and civilian insurgent authorities from partitioned Poland to the [Great Emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Emigration) led through the city.[^cite_note-36]

In 1871 the Province of Posen, along with the rest of the Kingdom of Prussia, became part of the newly formed [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). During German rule, the oldest church of the city (church of Saint Giles), the remains of the castle,[^cite_note-TB-26][^cite_note-VB-28] and the Carmelite church and monastery were demolished. In the mid-19th century, the city saw the arrival of the [Prussian Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Eastern_Railway). The first stretch, from Schneidemühl ([Piła](386801.md)), was opened in July 1851.

At the time of [World War I](4764461.md), Poles in Bydgoszcz formed secret organizations, preparing to regain control of the city in the event of Poland regaining its independence.[^cite_note-TYG-37]

### Interbellum



The construction of [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) [Saint Vincent de Paul Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_de_Paul_Basilica,_Bydgoszcz) was dedicated to the return of the city to the Poles.

[^cite_note-38]

After the war, Bydgoszcz was assigned to the [recreated Polish state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) by the 1919 [Versailles Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_Treaty). Now officially Bydgoszcz again, the city belonged to the [Poznań Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84_Voivodeship_\(1921%E2%80%931939\)). The local populace was required to acquire Polish citizenship or leave the country. This led to a drastic decline in ethnically [German residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_minority_in_Poland), whose number within the town decreased from over 40.000 in 1910 to 11,016 in 1926.[^cite_note-39] A Nazi German youth organization was subsequently founded, which distributed [Nazi propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany) books from Germany among the German minority.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska200923-40]

The city's boundaries were greatly expanded in 1920 to include the surrounding suburbs of Okole, Szwederowo, Bartodzieje, Kapuściska, Wilczak, Jachcice and more, which made Bydgoszcz the third largest city in the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic)[^cite_note-41] in terms of area. In 1938, the city was made part of the Polish [Greater Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_changes_of_Polish_Voivodeships_on_April_1,_1938).

### World War II

Main article: [German repressions against the people of Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_repressions_against_the_people_of_Bydgoszcz)

Public executions of Poles by Nazis on 9 September 1939



Execution of Polish civilians caught by the Germans in a street roundup



Execution of Polish priests and civilians in the [Old Market Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_Square,_Bydgoszcz)

During the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), at the beginning of [World War II](32927.md), on September 1, 1939, Germany carried out air raids on the city. The Polish [15th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Infantry_Division_\(Poland\)), which was stationed in Bydgoszcz, fought off German attacks on September 2, but on September 3 was forced to retreat. During the withdrawal of Poles, as part of the diversion planned by Germany, local Germans opened fire on Polish soldiers and civilians. Polish soldiers and civilians were forced into a defensive battle in which several hundred people were killed on both sides. The event, referred to as the *[Bloody Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloody_Sunday_\(1939\))* by the [propaganda of Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany), which exaggerated the number of victims to 5,000 "defenceless" Germans, was used as an excuse to carry out dozens of [mass executions of Polish residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executions_in_the_Valley_of_Death) in the Old Market Square and in the [Valley of Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Death_\(Bydgoszcz\)).[^cite_note-TB-26][^cite_note-VB-28] Between September 3–10, 1939, the Germans executed 192 Poles in the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009110-42]

On September 5, while the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) entered the city, German-Polish skirmishes still took place in the Szwederowo district, and the [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) of the city began. The German *[Einsatzgruppe IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzgruppen)*, *[Einsatzkommando 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzkommando)* and [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel)-Totenkopf-Standarte "Brandenburg" entered the city to commit [atrocities against the Polish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation), and afterwards some of its members co-formed the local German police.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska200955,_61–62-43] Many of the murders were carried out as part of the *[Intelligenzaktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligenzaktion)*, aimed at exterminating the Polish elites and preventing the establishment of a [Polish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska200971-44] which emerged nevertheless. On September 24, the local German *[Kreisleiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreisleiter)* called local Polish city officials to a supposed formal meeting in the city hall, from where they were taken to a nearby forest and exterminated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009102-45] The *Kreisleiter* also ordered the execution of their family members to "avoid creating martyrs".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009102-45] By decision from September 5, 1939, one of the first three German special courts in occupied Poland was established in Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-46]

The Germans established several camps and prisons for Poles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009110-42] As of September 30, 1939, over 3,000 individuals were imprisoned there, and in October and November, the Germans carried out further mass arrests of over 7,200 people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009157–158-47] Many of those people were then murdered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009158-48] Poles from Bydgoszcz were massacred at various locations in the city, at the Valley of Death and in the nearby village of [Tryszczyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryszczyn).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009158-48] The victims were both men and women, including activists, school principals, teachers, priests, local officials, merchants, lawyers, and also boy and girl scouts, gymnasium students and children as young as 12.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009158–160-49] The executions were presented as punishment for supposedly "murdering Germans" and "destroying peace", and were used by Nazi propaganda to show the world that it was alleged "Polish terror" that forced [Hitler](2731583.md) to start the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009158-48] On the [Polish National Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Independence_Day_\(Poland\)), November 11, 1939, the Germans symbolically publicly executed Leon Barciszewski, the mayor of Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009160-50] On November 17, 1939, the commander of the local [SD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicherheitsdienst)-[EK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzkommando) unit declared there was no more Polish [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia) capable of resistance in the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009160-50]





The local synagogue was destroyed during the German occupation. The inscription reads in German: "This city is free of Jews".

The city was annexed to the newly formed province of [Reichsgau Danzig-West Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsgau_Danzig-West_Prussia) as the [seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_\(legal_entity\)) of the district or county (*kreis*) of Bromberg. However, the annexation was not recognised in international law. Extermination of the inhabitants continued throughout the war, and in total, around 10,000 inhabitants, mostly [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), but also [Polish Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland), were killed.[^cite_note-TB-26] Some Polish inhabitants were also murdered in the village of [Jastrzębie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jastrz%C4%99bie,_%C5%9Awiecie_County) in January 1940, and local teachers were also among Polish teachers murdered in both [Mauthausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen_concentration_camp) and [Dachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp) concentration camps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWardzyńska2009180–182-51] The history of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) in Bydgoszcz ended with the [German invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Poland) and the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust). The city's Jewish citizens, who constituted a small community in the city (about two percent of the prewar population)[^cite_note-EP-52] and many of whom spoke German, were sent to [extermination camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extermination_camps) or murdered in the town itself. The city renamed *Bromberg* was the site of [Bromberg-Ost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromberg-Ost), a women's subcamp of the [Stutthof concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stutthof_concentration_camp). A deportation camp was situated in Smukała village, now part of Bydgoszcz. On February 4, 1941, the first mass transport of 524 Poles came to the [Potulice concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potulice_concentration_camp) from Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-53] The local train station was one of the locations, where Polish children aged 12 and over were sent from the Potulice concentration camp to slave labor.[^cite_note-ap-54] The children reloaded freight trains.[^cite_note-ap-54]

During the [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)), the Germans destroyed some of the city's historic buildings to erect new structures in the [Nazi style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_architecture).[^cite_note-VB-28] The Germans built a huge secret dynamite factory (*[DAG Fabrik Bromberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromberg_Dynamit_Nobel_AG_Factory)*) hidden in a forest in which they used the [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) of several hundred forced laborers,[^cite_note-VB-28] including [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) [prisoners of war](25008.md) from the [Stalag XX-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_XX-A) POW camp in Toruń.[^cite_note-55] In 1943, local Poles managed to save some [kidnapped Polish children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_of_children_by_Nazi_Germany) from the [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87) region, by buying them from the Germans at the local train station.[^cite_note-56]

The Polish resistance was active in Bydgoszcz. Activities included distribution of underground Polish press, sabotage actions, stealing German ammunition to aid Polish partisans, espionage of German activity[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChrzanowski202230,_40–41,_47–48,_57,_62-57] and providing shelter for British POWs who escaped from the Stalag XX-A POW camp.[^cite_note-58] The [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) cracked down on the Polish resistance several times.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChrzanowski202239-59]

In spring 1945, Bydgoszcz was occupied by the advancing [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army). Those German residents who had survived [fled or were expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_\(1944%E2%80%931950\)) and the city was returned to Poland, although with a Soviet-installed communist regime, which stayed in power until the 1980s. The Polish resistance remained active in Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChrzanowski202274-60]

### Post-war period



Contemporary rear view of the [Old Market Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market_Square,_Bydgoszcz) with [Mill Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Island,_Bydgoszcz), [Opera Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Nova_Bydgoszcz), and [Nordic Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Haven?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the background.

In the same year 1945, the city was made the seat of the [Pomeranian Voivodship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Voivodship), the northern part of which was soon separated to form [Gdańsk Voivodship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk_Voivodeship_\(1945%E2%80%931975\)). The remaining part of the Pomeranian Voivodship was renamed [Bydgoszcz Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Voivodeship) in 1950. In 1951 and 1969, [Bydgoszcz University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_University_of_Science_and_Technology) and [Kazimierz Wielki University in Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Wielki_University_in_Bydgoszcz) were founded respectively.

In 1973, the former town of [Fordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordon_\(Bydgoszcz_district\)), located on the left bank of the Vistula, was included in the city limits[^cite_note-EP-52] and became the easternmost district of Bydgoszcz. In March 1981, [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(Polish_trade_union\))'s activists [were violently suppressed in Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_events).

With the [Polish local government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_local_government_reforms) of 1999, Bydgoszcz became the seat of the governor of a province entitled [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship). In 2005, [Casimir the Great University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Wielki_University_in_Bydgoszcz) was opened in Bydgoszcz.

Currently, Bydgoszcz is the biggest center of [NATO](21133.md) headquarters in Poland, the most known being the [Joint Force Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Force_Training_Centre). In May 2023, debris of a Russian [Kh-55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-55) [air-sol missile](1544116.md) was found in the forest of the near village [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship).[^cite_note-61]

The oldest building in the city is the [Cathedral of St Martin and St Nicolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Cathedral), commonly known as Fara Church. It is a three-aisle late Gothic church, erected between 1466 and 1502, which boasts a late-Gothic painting entitled *Madonna with a Rose* or *the Holy Virgin of Beautiful Love* from the 16th century. The colourful 20th-century polychrome is also especially worthy of note.



19th-century water tower

The [Church of the Assumption of the Holy Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Clares'_Church,_Bydgoszcz), commonly referred to as "The Church of [Poor Clares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Clares)," is a famous landmark of the city. It is a small, Gothic-Renaissance (including [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Renaissance) additions), single-aisle church built between 1582 and 1602. The interior is rather austere since the church has been stripped of most of its furnishings. This is not a surprising fact, considering that in the 19th century the Prussian authorities dissolved the [Order of St Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Poor_Ladies) and turned the church into a warehouse, among other uses. Nonetheless, the church is worth visiting. In particular, the original wooden polychrome ceiling dating from the 17th century draws the attention of every visitor.



The Old Port Granary built in 1835

*Wyspa Młyńska* ([Mill Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Island_in_Bydgoszcz)) is among the most spectacular and atmospheric places in Bydgoszcz. What makes it unique is the location in the very heart of the city centre, just a few steps from the old Market Square. It was the 'industrial' centre of Bydgoszcz in the Middle Ages and for several hundred years thereafter, and it was here that the famous [royal mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Mint) operated in the 17th century. Most of the buildings which can still be seen on the island date from the 19th century, but the so-called *Biały Spichlerz* (the White Granary) recalls the end of the 18th century. However, it is the water, footbridges, historic red-brick tenement houses reflected in the rivers, and the greenery, including old chestnut trees, that create the unique atmosphere of the island.



[Hotel Pod Orlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Pod_Orlem,_Bydgoszcz)

["Hotel pod Orłem"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_%22Pod_Orlem%22_in_Bydgoszcz) (The Eagle Hotel), an icon of the city's 19th-century architecture, was designed by the distinguished Bydgoszcz architect Józef Święcicki, the author of around sixty buildings in the city. Completed in 1896, it served as a hotel from the very beginning and was originally owned by Emil Bernhardt, a hotel manager educated in Switzerland. Its façade displays forms characteristic of the Neo-baroque style in architecture.

[Saint Vincent de Paul's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent_de_Paul_Basilica_Minor_in_Bydgoszcz), erected between 1925 and 1939, is the largest church in Bydgoszcz and one of the biggest in Poland. It can accommodate around 12,000 people. This monumental church, modeled after the [Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome) in Rome, was designed by the Polish architect Adam Ballenstaedt. The most characteristic element of the neo-classical temple is the reinforced concrete dome 40 metres in diameter.

The [three granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodzka_Street_in_Bydgoszcz#Grain_Granaries) in [Grodzka Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodzka_Street_in_Bydgoszcz), picturesquely located on the Brda River near the old Market Square, are the official symbol of the city. Built at the turn of the 19th century, they were originally used to store grain and similar products, but now house exhibitions of the city's Leon Wyczółkowski District Museum.

The building of the former [Prussian Eastern Railway Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Eastern_Railway_Headquarters,_Bydgoszcz) erected between 1886 and 1889 in Dutch Mannierist style is another notable structure in the city. Initially it served as a headquarters of the Prussian Eastern Railway and later it belonged to the Polish State Railways. Since 2022 it is privately owned.

The city is mostly associated with water, sports, [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) buildings, waterfront, music, and urban greenery. Bydgoszcz boasts the largest city park in Poland (830 ha). The city was also once famous for its industry.

Some major landmarks have been destroyed, for example, the church in the Old Market Square and the [Municipal Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Municipal_Theatre_in_Bydgoszcz). Additionally, the Old Town lost a few characteristic tenement houses, including the western frontage of the Market Square. The city also lost its Gothic castle and defensive walls. In Bydgoszcz, there are a great number of villas in the style of typical [garden suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_city_movement).

In the city, there are 38 banks represented through a network of 116 branches (including the headquarters of the [Bank Pocztowy SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Pocztowy)), whilst 37 insurance companies also have offices in the city. [JP Morgan Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP_Morgan_Chase), one of the largest financial institutions in the world, has established a branch in Bydgoszcz. Most industrial complexes are scattered throughout the city. Of note are the ['Zachem' chemical works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachem_Chemical_Plant_in_Bydgoszcz), covering dozens of square kilometers in the south-east of the city. These remnants of the German explosives factory built in World War II occupy an area which has its own rail lines, internal communication and housing, plus a large forested area. ## Culture



The Arts House on Gdańska Street, currently in the hands of [Feliks Nowowiejski Music Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliks_Nowowiejski_Music_Academy).

Bydgoszcz is a major cultural centre in the country, especially for music. Traditions of the municipal theatre date back to the 17th century, when the Jesuit college built a theatre. In 1824, a [permanent theatre building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Municipal_Theatre_in_Bydgoszcz) was erected, and this was rebuilt in 1895 in a monumental form by the Berlin architect Heinrich Seeling. The first music school was established in Bydgoszcz in 1904; it had close links to the very well-known European piano factory of Bruno Sommerfeld. Numerous orchestras and choirs, both German (Gesangverein, Liedertafel) and Polish (St. Wojciech Halka, Moniuszko), have also made the city their home. Since 1974, Bydgoszcz has been home to a very prestigious [Academy of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Music_Academy_-_%22Feliks_Nowowiejski%22). Bydgoszcz is also an important place for contemporary European culture; one of the most important European centers of jazz music, the Brain club, was founded in Bydgoszcz by Jacek Majewski and Slawomir Janicki.

Bydgoszcz was a candidate for the title of [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2016. [^cite_note-MW-67] It joined the list of [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s [Cities of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Music_\(UNESCO\)) in 2023.[^cite_note-68]

### Museums





Postindustrial landscape of Mill Island is today a home to many museum and cultural facilities.



The seat of [Leon Wyczółkowski Regional Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Museum_in_Bydgoszcz), originally a 17th-century nunnery.

Muzeum Okręgowe im. Leona Wyczółkowskiego ([Leon Wyczółkowski District Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Wycz%C3%B3%C5%82kowski_District_Museum)) is a municipally owned museum. Apart from a large collection of [Leon Wyczółkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Wycz%C3%B3%C5%82kowski)'s works, it houses permanent as well as temporary exhibitions of art. It is based in several buildings, including the old granaries on the [Brda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brda_\(river\)) River and [Mill Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Island_in_Bydgoszcz) and the remaining building of the Polish royal mint. [Exploseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploseum), a museum built around the [World War II](32927.md) [Nazi Germany](21212.md) munitions factory, is also part of it.

In Bydgoszcz, the [Pomeranian Military Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84ska_Street,_Bydgoszcz#Main_edifices) specializes in documenting 19th- and 20th-century Polish military history, particularly the history of the Pomeranian Military District and several other units present in the area.

The city has many art galleries, two [symphony orchestras](48480582.md), many chamber orchestras and choirs. Bydgoszcz's cultural facilities also include libraries, including the [Provincial and Municipal Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_and_Municipal_Public_Library_in_Bydgoszcz) with an extensive collection of volumes from the 15th to the 19th centuries. The municipally owned [Palaces and park ensemble in Ostromecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaces_and_park_ensemble_in_Ostromecko) near the city contains the [Andrzej Szwalbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Szwalbe) Collection of Historical Pianos, one of the largest such collections in Poland.

### Classical music

### Theatre



[Polish Theatre in Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Theatre_in_Bydgoszcz)

[Teatr Polski im Hieronima Konieczki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Theatre_in_Bydgoszcz) (Hieronim Konieczka's Polish Theatre): Despite its name, the theatre offers a wide variety of shows both of national and foreign origin. It also regularly plays host to a large number of touring shows. Founded in 1949, since 2002 the theatre has taken part in the "Festiwal Prapremier" where the most renowned Polish theatres stage their latest works. There are also a number of private theatre companies operating in Bydgoszcz.

From 1960 to 1986, there was an outdoor theater, the reactivation of which is currently being pursued by the Theatre Culture Association, "Fides" and the Acting School A. Grzymala-Siedlecki.



*Opera Nova* on the Brda River bank

The [Pomeranian Philharmonic](48480582.md) named after [Ignacy Jan Paderewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski) has existed since 1953. The concert hall, which can hold 920 people is classified, in terms of sound, as one of the best in Europe, which is confirmed by well-known artists and critics (including [Jerzy Waldorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Waldorff)). Due to the phenomenon of acoustics, it attracts the interest of many famous artists. Bydgoszcz's stage has been frequented by many global celebrities, including [Arthur Rubinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rubinstein), [Benjamin Britten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Britten), [Witold Małcużyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Ma%C5%82cu%C5%BCy%C5%84ski), [Luciano Pavarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti), [Shlomo Mintz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Mintz), [Mischa Maisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mischa_Maisky), [Kevin Kenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kenner), [Kurt Masur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Masur), [Kazimierz Kord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Kord), [Jerzy Maksymiuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Maksymiuk) and [Antoni Wit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Wit). In recent years, the city has also hosted an excellent range of bands such as the BBC Radio Symphony Orchestra, the [Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_National_Radio_Symphony_Orchestra), and others.

The [Opera Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Nova_Bydgoszcz), in existence since 1956, started the construction of a new building in 1974 which was to consist of three main halls, situated on the Brda. The Opera Nova has become a cultural showcase of Bydgoszcz in the world. Considering the short history of the Opera, its success has been astounding; a large number of famous opera singers have performed there and theatrical troops from the Wrocław Opera, Theatre of Leningrad, Moscow, Kiev, Minsk, and Gulbenkian Foundation of Lisbon have also made appearances.
