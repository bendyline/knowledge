**Cotabato**, and also still commonly referred to as **North Cotabato**[^cite_note-4] and officially the **Province of Cotabato**, is a landlocked [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) primarily located in the [Soccsksargen](298888.md) [region](216786.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md). Its capital is the city of [Kidapawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidapawan), the most populous in the province. [Some of the barangays in its municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_barangays_in_Cotabato) are under the jurisdiction of the nearby [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region](37260549.md).[^cite_note-5]

## Etymology

The name *Kuta Watu* in [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language) (or *Kota Batu* in [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language)) means "stone fortress".

## History

See also: [Cotabato (historical province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_\(historical_province\))

### Maguindanao Sultanate

Further information: [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao)

According to Maguindanao royal records, Islam was introduced to the Maguindanaos in the late 15th century by Sharif [Muhammad Kabungsuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_Kabungsuwan), a Johorean Malay Muslim noble and missionary of Arab descent. Sharif Kabungsuan invaded [Malabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabang) in 1475, facing armed resistance from the principality, nevertheless successfully vanquishing and subjugating it to his Islamic rule through the employment of [Samal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal_people) forces.[^cite_note-Halili-PhilippineHistory-6]

Cotabato became the capital of the Maguindanao Sultanate. The city became the seat of power of Sultan [Muhammad Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Kudarat) in 1619.

### Spanish invasion

[Christianity](8276776.md) was introduced in 1596, but the Spaniards were unable to penetrate into the region until the second half of the 19th century. The district of Cotabato was formed in 1860,[^cite_note-CotabatoGovPH-History-7] covering the areas of what is now Cotabato, [Maguindanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Norte), [Maguindanao del Sur](67871248.md), and [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat) provinces with its capital at [Tamontaka](298955.md). [Fort Pikit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pikit) was established by the Spaniards in 1893 as they continued their conquest of the remnants of [Maguindanao Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_Sultanate), which would soon be the site of one of the province's oldest towns, [Pikit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikit,_Cotabato).

Further information: [Spanish–Moro conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93Moro_conflict)

### American rule

The coming of the Americans ushered in the creation of the [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) on July 15, 1903, through *Act No. 787* of the [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission). Cotabato, covering what are now the provinces of Cotabato, [Maguindanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Norte), [Maguindanao del Sur](67871248.md), [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat), [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato) and [Sarangani](297388.md), became a district of the huge Moro province. During the American period, large companies were established in Cotabato to exploit the vast timber resources of the region. By the 1930s, settlers from Luzon and Visayas established homesteads in Cotabato.

Further information: [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) and [Department of Mindanao and Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mindanao_and_Sulu)

### Japanese invasion

In December 1941, Japanese planes bombed and invaded Cotabato. In 1942, Cotabato was occupied by the [Japanese Imperial forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_forces). A military headquarters of the [Philippine Commonwealth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) existed in Cotabato from January 3, 1942, to June 30, 1946, and the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) 10th Constabulary Regiment was stationed in Cotobato from October 28, 1944, to June 30, 1946. Moro guerrilla fighters operating in the province of Cotabato helped local forces of the Philippine Commonwealth Army and the Philippine Constabulary 10th Constabulary Regiment fight against the Imperial Japanese Army until 1944. The Moro guerrillas fought the Japanese troops until liberation. In 1945, Cotabato was recaptured from the Japanese Imperial forces by the combined Filipino and American troops together with the recognized Moro guerrilla units. The guerrillas used the traditional Moro [Kampilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampilan), [Barong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barong_\(knife\)) and [Kris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris) swords as their weapons.

Further information: [Battle of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maguindanao) and [Battle of Cotabato (1945)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cotabato_\(1945\))

### Postwar era

The pace of settlement in the region accelerated in the 1950s and 1960s. The [former province of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_Province_\(1914-1973\)) was once the largest in the Philippines. In 1966, [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato) was created as a separate province.[^cite_note-ChanRobles-RA4849-8]

Mindanao, including Cotabato, was relatively peaceful in the 1950s and the mid-1960s. While there were some ethnic tensions, there was essentially no presence of secessionist groups in Mindanao yet.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-9]

### Martial law era

Main articles: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos), [Moro conflict](4602307.md), [Manili massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manili_massacre), and [Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship)

The relative peace of the postwar era began to change in the last years of President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos)' [first term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_term_of_the_presidency_of_Ferdinand_Marcos), when news about the [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre) ignited a furor in the Moro community, and ethnic tensions encouraged with the formation of secessionist movements.[^cite_note-10] None of these groups had enough followers to form a viable opposition until Marcos declared martial law,[^cite_note-RevoltinMindanao:-11] but when political parties were dissolved upon the proclamation of martial law in September 1972, it became easy for the armed secessionist group known as the [Moro National Liberation Front](2821886.md) to consolidate power, and the decades-long [Moro conflict](4602307.md) began in earnest.[^cite_note-BetweenIntegrationandSecession-12]

One major event of martial law which took place in Cotabato was the [Manili massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manili_massacre), which saw the [mass murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murder) of 70[^cite_note-pcij-13] to 79[^cite_note-Arguillas-14] [Moros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people), including women and children, committed in a [mosque](19894.md) in Manili, [Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen,_Cotabato) on June 19, 1971.[^cite_note-lar-15] The Muslim residents of the town had gathered in their mosque to participate in a supposed peace talk with [Christian](18337522.md) groups when a group of armed men suspected to be part of the [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga) militant group[^cite_note-pcij-13] opened fire on them.[^cite_note-lar-15]

On November 22, 1973, through *Presidential Decree No. 341*, what remained of the old Cotabato was further divided into the provinces of *North Cotabato*, [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao), and [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat).[^cite_note-decree341-16] *North Cotabato* was later renamed *Cotabato* through *Batas Pambansa Blg. 660* approved on March 7, 1984.[^cite_note-batas660-17]

### Contemporary history

By the aftermath of the [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bangsamoro_autonomy_plebiscite) to form the autonomous administrative region of [Bangsamoro](37260549.md) in February 2019, 63 barangays from Cotabato's western municipalities, all of which inhabited mostly by Muslim natives, opted to join the newly formed region.[^cite_note-CNN_Philippines_Staff-18] This will result in the reduction of the number of barangays the province currently administers after the national midterm elections on May 13, 2019.[^cite_note-Carolyn_O._Arguillas-19] Cotabato now comprises the capital city of [Kidapawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidapawan) and 25 municipalities.

In September 2017, Representative [Roger Mercado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Mercado) authorized House Bill 6408, proposing to change the name of the provinces to *Cotabato del Norte*.

## Geography

Cotabato covers a total area of 9,008.90 square kilometres (3,478.36 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-20] occupying the northeastern tip of [Region XII](298888.md) and is centrally located in [Mindanao](20546.md). It is bounded on the north by the provinces of [Lanao del Sur](297370.md) and [Bukidnon](261222.md), on the east by [Davao City](489401.md) and [Davao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte), on the west by [Maguindanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Norte) and [Maguindanao del Sur](67871248.md), on the south by [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat), and on the southeast by [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur).

Cotabato is strategically linked to the major "Arterial Road System" that traverses and connects the province to Davao City - Soccsksargen - Cotabato Corridor. The Cotabato via Kabacan - Maramag - Kibawe, Bukidnon [Sayre Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayre_Highway) meanwhile serves as its link to the [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro)-[Iligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan) City Corridor.

Cotabato stretches from the east at [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo), which separates it from Davao, to the [Piapayungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ragang) Range on its boundary with Lanao in the west.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-Cotabato-21] Between these highlands is the [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of the [Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River) or [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao),[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-Cotabato-21] the second longest in the Philippines at 373 kilometres (232 mi), which rises in Bukidnon and flows westward to Maguindanao del Norte and [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay). The province's fertile plains are traversed by [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributaries) of this great river.

Typhoons do not pass through Cotabato and rainfall is evenly distributed throughout the year.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-Cotabato-21]

### Reduction due to the establishment of the Bangsamoro

During the 2001 Referendum for inclusion to the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao, 2 out of 28 barangays of [Carmen, Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen,_Cotabato) chose to be part of ARMM, but were excluded because they were not connected to the main region of ARMM. During 2010-2016 Administration, the Bangsamoro ideal sprouted and a newly proposed region was in the making. According to the agreements signed by the Moro Islamic Liberation Front and the Administration of Noynoy Aquino, Carmen will be included in Bangsamoro because of the two out of twenty-eight barangays that voted in favor of joining the Muslim region back in 2001. Unfortunately, Bangsamoro was not implemented before the term of Aquino ended. Despite this, the current administration aims to establish a Federal Government, where Bangamoro will be realized into a State of the Philippines wherein Carmen will be included in Bangsamoro.

Plans to establish a new municipality in the south of Carmen so that the indigenous and Christian central and northern part of Carmen will be retained in Cotabato have yet to be confirmed. The idea arose because once the entire municipality of Carmen is included in [Bangsamoro](37260549.md), the province of Cotabato will be cut into half, leaving three towns ([Banisilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banisilan), [Alamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamada), and [Libungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libungan)) at its west without connection to the center of Cotabato, isolating these towns in the process.

The issue was partially resolved when the second part of the plebiscite was conducted on February 6, 2019, when 63 barangay from the towns of [Pigkawayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigcawayan,_Cotabato), Libungan, [Midsayap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsayap), [Aleosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleosan,_Cotabato), [Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen,_Cotabato), [Banisilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banisilan,_Cotabato) and [Pikit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikit,_Cotabato) opted to join the new administrative autonomous region of [Bangsamoro](37260549.md) after the results of the [region's formation plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bangsamoro_autonomy_plebiscite) were confirmed three days later on February 9.[^cite_note-CNN_Philippines_Staff-18] These barangays are to join either the adjacent province of [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao) as parts of the said province's nearby and respective municipalities or to cluster on their own as new municipalities within the said province after the [national midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_general_election) on May 13, 2019.[^cite_note-Carolyn_O._Arguillas-19] The size and population of Cotabato province had officially diminished on November 20, 2019, as the barangays which voted to join the Bangsamoro made their exit from the province and their respective mother municipalities and were officially turned over to the Bangsamoro government as [an interim province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Special_Geographic_Area) within the autonomous region divided into several clusters.[^cite_note-psaboardresono13-27][^cite_note-28]

Based on the 2024 census, Cotabato has an overall population of 1,286,142.[^cite_note-PSA20–12-33] The average population growth rate was 2.27% in the period 2010–2015, which is higher than the national average of 1.72%. The province had a density of 140 inhabitants per square kilometre or 360 inhabitants per square mile.

Cotabato is an ethnolinguistically diverse province. The first [Visayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_people) settlers reached the town of [Pikit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikit,_Cotabato) in 1913, and since then, Christian migrants have moved and lived in Cotabato, cohabitating in the province with the local indigenous groups. 71% of Cotabato's population descended from settlers who migrated from the Visayas and Luzon, while the remaining 18% belong to the communities of Magindanaons, Iranuns, Ilianens, Dulangan Manobo, Obo, Tagabawa, and Tboli. The major languages spoken are Hiligaynon (46%), Maguindanao (38%), Cebuano (8%), and Ilocano (4%).[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-Cotabato-21]

#### Catholicism

Cotabato's population is predominantly Catholic. According to the 2020 census,[^cite_note-36] 52.77% of the population are [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-37]

#### Islam

As of 2020, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) represent close to 20% of the population.

#### Others

&#x20;The other significant minorities are [Evangelical Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Christians) at 8.4% of the population, Southern Baptist Church with 3.7% of the population, and Iglesia Ni Cristo which forms 2.2% of the province population.[^cite_note-38]

Cotabato is considered a major food basket in Mindanao. It is a top producer of cereals, tropical fruits, vegetables, sugarcane, coconut, coffee, freshwater fish and livestock.

It is also one of the country's leading producers of raw and semi-processed rubber and industrial trees,[^cite_note-MindaNews-Rubber-48] with markets in Asia and Europe.

Among its major natural assets are [Mt. Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Apo), the country's highest peak at 9,692 feet (2,954 m) above sea level, the [Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River) which is a major contributor to Mindanao's irrigation system and hydro-electric energy, and the vast [Liguasan Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liguasan_Marsh) which not only supplies a bounty of [freshwater fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_fish) and organic fertilizer but considered as a possible source as well of [natural gas](22131.md).

Power utility in the province comes from two energy sources - the *[NAPOCOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Power_Corporation) Agus Grid* in [Iligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan) transmitted through its [Tacurong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacurong) Substations and the [Mount Apo Geothermal Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo_Geothermal_Power_Plant) at the foot of Mt. Apo in Ilomavis, [Kidapawan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidapawan_City) which produces 97 megawatts of electricity. Power distribution is handled by [Cotabato Electric Cooperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_Electric_Cooperative), Inc. (COTELCO).

The province has a 4,131.32-kilometre (2,567.08 mi) road network connecting the major centers to each other and the outlying barangays, and communication linkage through NDD-IDD, fax, cellular phone and the internet is available.
