The **African Union** (**AU**) is a [continental union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_union) of 55 member states located on the continent of [Africa](5334607.md).[^cite_note-UIA-9][^cite_note-10] The AU was announced in the [Sirte Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte_Declaration) in [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte), Libya, on 9 September 1999, calling for the establishment of the African Union. The bloc was launched on 9 July 2002 in [Durban](313650.md), South Africa.[^cite_note-African_Union_2002-11] The intention of the AU was to replace the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) (OAU), established on 25 May 1963 in [Addis Ababa](52593.md) by 32 signatory governments; the OAU was disbanded on 9 July 2002.[^cite_note-12] The most important decisions of the AU are made by the [Assembly of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_African_Union), a semi-annual meeting of the heads of state and [government](12229.md) of its member states. The [African economy](1055517.md) is regionally integrated through this union.

The AU's secretariat, the [African Union Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Commission), is based in Addis Ababa. The largest city in the AU is [Lagos](85232.md), Nigeria, while the [largest urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Africa_by_population) is [Cairo](6293.md), in Egypt. The African Union has more than 1.5 billion people and an area of around 30 million km<sup>2</sup> (12 million sq mi) and includes world landmarks such as the [Sahara](325363.md) and the [Nile](21244.md).[^cite_note-13] The primary working languages are [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), [Spanish](26825.md), and [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language).[^cite_note-au.int-3] Within the African Union, there are official bodies, such as the [Peace and Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Security_Council) and the [Pan-African Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Parliament). The AU is a member of the [G20](2206608.md).[^cite_note-14]

## Overview

The objectives of the African Union are:[^cite_note-15]

1. To achieve greater unity, cohesion and solidarity among the African countries and [African](5334607.md) nations.

2. To defend the sovereignty, [territorial integrity](31335.md) and independence of its Member States.

3. To accelerate the political and social-[economic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_integration) of the continent.

   [^cite_note-16]

   [^cite_note-17]

4. To promote and defend [African](5334607.md) common positions on issues of interest to the continent and its peoples.

5. To encourage international cooperation, taking due account of the [Charter of the United Nations](88805.md) and the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights).

6. To promote peace, security, and stability on the continent.

7. To promote democratic principles and institutions, popular participation and [good governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_governance).

8. To promote and protect human and peoples' rights in accordance with the [African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Charter_on_Human_and_Peoples'_Rights) and other relevant [human rights instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_human_rights_instruments).

9. To establish the necessary conditions which enable the continent to play its rightful role in the global economy and in international negotiations.

10. To promote [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development) at the [economic, social and cultural levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic,_social_and_cultural_rights) as well as the integration of African economies.

11. To promote co-operation in all fields of human activity to raise the [living standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) of African people.

12. To coordinate and harmonise the policies between the existing and future [Regional Economic Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Economic_Communities) for the gradual attainment of the objectives of the Union.

13. To advance the development of the continent by promoting research in all fields, in particular in science and technology.

14. To work with relevant international partners in the eradication of preventable diseases and the promotion of good health on the continent.

The African Union is made up of both political and administrative bodies. The highest decision-making organ is the [Assembly of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_African_Union), made up of all the heads of state or government of member states of the AU. The Assembly is chaired by [Évariste Ndayishimiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89variste_Ndayishimiye), President of [Burundi](21490998.md). The AU also has a representative body, the [Pan-African Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Parliament), which consists of 265 members elected by the national legislatures of the AU member states. Its president is [Fortune Z. Charumbira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Z._Charumbira).

Other political institutions of the AU include:

- the [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_the_African_Union), made up of [foreign ministers](317153.md), which prepares decisions for the Assembly;

- the [Permanent Representatives Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representatives'_Committee_of_the_African_Union), made up of the ambassadors to [Addis Ababa](52593.md) of AU member states; and

- the [Economic, Social, and Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic,_Social,_and_Cultural_Council) (ECOSOCC), a civil society consultative body.

The [AU Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Commission), the secretariat to the political structures, is chaired by [Mahamoud Ali Youssouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamoud_Ali_Youssouf) of Djibouti.

Other AU structures are hosted by different member states:

- the [African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights](1620260.md) is based in [Banjul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjul), [the Gambia](65656.md); and

- the [New Partnership for Africa's Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Partnership_for_Africa's_Development) (NEPAD) and [APRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APRM) [Secretariats](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/secretariat) and the [Pan-African Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Parliament) are in [Midrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midrand), South Africa.

The AU's first military intervention in a member state was the May 2003 deployment of a peacekeeping force of soldiers from South Africa, [Ethiopia](187749.md), and [Mozambique](19301.md) to oversee the implementation of the various agreements in [Burundi](21490998.md). AU troops were also deployed in [Sudan](27421.md) for peacekeeping during the [Darfur Conflict](5043637.md), before the mission was handed over to the [United Nations](31769.md) on 1 January 2008 via [UNAMID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations%E2%80%93African_Union_Mission_in_Darfur). The AU has a peacekeeping mission in [Somalia](27358.md), consisting of troops from [Uganda](31816.md) and Burundi.[^cite_note-18]

The AU has adopted a number of important new documents establishing norms at the continental level, to supplement those already in force when it was created. These include the [African Union Convention on Preventing and Combating Corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Convention_on_Preventing_and_Combating_Corruption) (2003), the African Charter on Democracy, Elections and Governance (2007), the [New Partnership for Africa's Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Partnership_for_Africa's_Development) (NEPAD) and its associated Declaration on Democracy, Political, Economic and Corporate Governance.[^cite_note-19]

## History

Main articles: [History of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_African_Union) and [History of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Africa)



African leaders who founded the [OAU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) the predecessor of the African Union, Emperor [Haile Selassie I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie) (center), President [Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Kenyatta) (right) and other African leaders including President [Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Obote) (behind, left side)

The historical foundations of the African Union originated in the **First Congress of Independent African States**, held in [Accra](56824.md), [Ghana](12067.md) from 15 to 22 April 1958. The conference was aimed at establishing [Africa Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Day) to annually mark the liberation movement concerning the willingness of the African people to free themselves from colonial rule, as well as subsequent attempts to unite Africa, including the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) (OAU), which was established on 25 May 1963, and the [African Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Economic_Community) in 1991.[^cite_note-20] Critics argued that the OAU in particular did little to protect the rights and liberties of African citizens from their own political leaders, often dubbing it the "Dictators' Club".[^cite_note-bbc2115736-21]

The idea of creating the AU was revived in the mid-1990s under the leadership of Libyan head of state [Muammar al-Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muammar_al-Gaddafi);[^cite_note-22] the heads of state and governments of the OAU issued the [Sirte Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte_Declaration) (named after Sirte, Libya) on 9 September 1999, calling for the establishment of an African Union.[^cite_note-23] The Declaration was followed by summits at [Lomé](57067.md) in 2000, when the [Constitutive Act of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutive_Act_of_the_African_Union) was adopted, and at [Lusaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusaka) in 2001, when the plan for the implementation of the African Union was adopted. During the same period, the initiative for the establishment of the [New Partnership for Africa's Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Partnership_for_Africa's_Development) (NEPAD) was also established.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The African Union was launched in [Durban](313650.md) on 9 July 2002 by its first chairperson, former South African head of state [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki), at the first session of the Assembly of the African Union.[^cite_note-26] The second session of the Assembly was held in [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo) in 2003 and the third session in [Addis Ababa](52593.md) was held on 6 July 2004.

Since 2010, the African Union eyes the establishment of a joint [African space agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Space_Agency).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

[Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) was the first-ever sitting United States president to speak in front of the African Union in Addis Ababa on 29 July 2015. During his speech, he encouraged the world to increase economic ties via investments and trade with the [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_Africa) and lauded the signs of progress made in [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Africa), infrastructure and [economy](1055517.md). However, he also criticized a lack of democracy and leaders who refuse to step down, discrimination against minorities (including [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) people, religious groups and ethnicities) and corruption. He suggested an intensified [democratization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization) and free trade to significantly increase living quality for Africans.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

The African Union has a number of official bodies:

[Pan-African Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Parliament) (PAP)

To become the highest legislative body of the African Union. The seat of the PAP is at [Midrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midrand), [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa. The Parliament is composed of 265 elected representatives from all 55 AU states, and intended to provide popular and civil-society participation in the processes of democratic governance. Its president is [Fateh Boutbig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fateh_Boutbig), of [Algeria](358.md).

[Assembly of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_African_Union)

Composed of heads of state and heads of government of AU states, the Assembly is currently the supreme governing body of the African Union. It is gradually devolving some of its decision-making powers to the Pan-African Parliament. It meets once a year and makes its decisions by consensus or by a two-thirds majority. The current [chair of the AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson_of_the_African_Union) since February 2026 is President [Évariste Ndayishimiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89variste_Ndayishimiye), President of [Burundi](21490998.md).

[African Union Commission (or Authority)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Commission)

The secretariat of the African Union, composed of ten commissioners and supporting staff and headquartered in [Addis Ababa](52593.md), Ethiopia. In a similar fashion to its [European](9317.md) counterpart, the [European Commission](9974.md), it is responsible for the administration and coordination of the AU's activities and meetings.

[Court of Justice of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_African_Union)

The Constitutive Act provides for a Court of Justice to rule on disputes over interpretation of AU treaties. A protocol to set up this Court of Justice was adopted in 2003 and entered into force in 2009. It was, however, superseded by a protocol creating an [African Court of Justice and Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Court_of_Justice_and_Human_Rights), which will incorporate the already established [African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Court_on_Human_and_Peoples'_Rights) (see below) and have two chambers: one for general legal matters and one for rulings on the human rights treaties.

[Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_the_African_Union)

Composed of ministers designated by the governments of member states. It decides on matters such as foreign trade, social security, food, agriculture and communications, is accountable to the Assembly, and prepares material for the Assembly to discuss and approve. It is chaired by Shawn Makuyana of [Zimbabwe](34399.md) (2015–).

[Permanent Representatives' Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representatives'_Committee_of_the_African_Union)

Consisting of nominated permanent representatives of member states, the Committee prepares the work for the Executive Council, similar to the role of the [Committee of Permanent Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Permanent_Representatives) in the [European Union](9317.md).

[Peace and Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Security_Council) (PSC)

Proposed at the 2001 Lusaka Summit and established in 2004 under a protocol to the Constitutive Act adopted by the AU Assembly in July 2002. The protocol defines the PSC as a collective security and early-warning arrangement to facilitate timely and effective response to conflict and crisis situations in Africa. Other responsibilities conferred to the PSC by the protocol include prevention, management and resolution of conflicts, post-conflict peace building and developing common defence policies. The PSC has fifteen members elected on a regional basis by the Assembly. Similar in intent and operation to the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md).

[Economic, Social and Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic,_Social_and_Cultural_Council)

An advisory organ composed of professional and civic representatives, similar to the European [Economic and Social Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Social_Committee). The chair of ECOSOCC, elected in 2008, is Cameroonian lawyer [Akere Muna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akere_Muna) of the Pan-African Lawyers Union (PALU).

[Specialised Technical Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialised_Technical_Committees_of_the_African_Union)

Both the Abuja Treaty and the Constitutive Act provide for Specialised Technical Committees to be established made up of African ministers to advise the Assembly. In practice, they have never been set up. The ten proposed themes are: Rural Economy and Agricultural Matters; Monetary and Financial Affairs; Trade, Customs, and Immigration; Industry, Science and Technology; Energy, Natural Resources, and Environment; Transport, Communications, and Tourism; Health; Labour, and Social Affairs; Education, Culture, and Human Resources.

Financial institutions

- [African Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Central_Bank) – [Abuja](56823.md), Nigeria

- [African Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Investment_Bank) – [Tripoli, Libya](31232.md)

- [African Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Monetary_Fund) – [Yaoundé](57671.md), Cameroon

These institutions have not yet been established; however, the Steering Committees working on their founding have been constituted. Eventually, the AU aims to have a single currency (the [Afro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro_\(currency\))).

Health

The [Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention](63909811.md) (Africa CDC) was founded in 2016 and launched in 2017. Its headquarters are in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.

Human rights

The [African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights](1620260.md), in existence since 1986, is established under the [African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Charter_on_Human_and_Peoples'_Rights) (the African Charter) rather than the Constitutive Act of the African Union. It is the premier African human rights body, with responsibility for monitoring and promoting compliance with the African Charter. The [African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Court_on_Human_and_Peoples'_Rights) was established in 2006 to supplement the work of the commission, following the entry into force of a protocol to the African Charter providing for its creation. It is planned that the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights will be merged with the Court of Justice of the African Union (see above).

Space

The [African Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Space_Agency) was officially formed in 2023. It is headquartered in Cairo.

Energy

The [African Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Energy_Commission) was founded in 2008, and is based in Algiers.

### Member states

Main article: [Member states of the African Union](30486666.md)

All [UN member states](31969.md) in the African continent are members of the African Union. Additionally, the [partially recognized state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) of the [Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](38762388.md) (SADR), which is not a member of the UN, is also a member of the AU. [Morocco](19291.md) withdrew from the Organisation of African Unity in 1984 due to the admission as a member state of the SADR, whose territory of [Western Sahara](33220.md) it claims sovereignty over. It was readmitted by the AU as a member state on 30 January 2017.[^cite_note-worldbulletin-37] [Somaliland](51425.md), which claims independence from [Somalia](27358.md), applied to join the AU in 2005, but has not been accepted.[^cite_note-Nelson-38]

### Suspended members

As of 2026, six members are suspended from the AU due to having undergone [coups d'état](25914154.md): Mali (since 2021), Sudan (since 2021), Burkina Faso (since 2022), Niger (since 2023), Madagascar (since 2025), and Guinea-Bissau (since 2025).

Mali was suspended from the African Union on 19 August 2020 following a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-39] On 9 October of the same year, the [Peace and Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Security_Council) of the African Union lifted the suspension imposed on Mali, citing progress made to return to democracy.[^cite_note-40] The country was again suspended on 1 June 2021, following its [second military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) within nine months.[^cite_note-41]

Sudan's membership was suspended by the African Union on 27 October 2021, after a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2021_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) deposed the civilian government led by Prime Minister [Abdalla Hamdok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok).[^cite_note-42]

Burkina Faso's membership was suspended by the African Union in the aftermath of a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2022_Burkina_Faso_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) on 31 January 2022.[^cite_note-43]

Niger's membership was suspended by the African Union on 22 August 2023 following a [military coup in late July](74454838.md) that deposed democratically elected president [Mohamed Bazoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Bazoum); this has since also led to the [2023–2024 Nigerien crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerien_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%932024\)).[^cite_note-44]

Following the [2025 Malagasy coup d'état](81321149.md), Madagascar's membership was suspended.[^cite_note-45]

Following [2025 Guinea-Bissau coup d'état](81695397.md), Guinea-Bissau's membership was suspended.[^cite_note-46]

### Formerly suspended members

Guinea's membership was also suspended by the African Union on 10 September 2021, after a [military coup](68651369.md) deposed the country's President [Alpha Condé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Cond%C3%A9).[^cite_note-47] After almost five years, Guinea was readmitted into the AU in January 2026.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Gabon's membership was suspended by the African Union on 31 August 2023 following a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Gabonese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) that deposed president [Ali Bongo Ondimba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Bongo_Ondimba).[^cite_note-50] In April 2025, the African Union decided to readmit Gabon.[^cite_note-51]

### Governance

The principal topic for debate at the July 2007 AU summit held in [Accra](56824.md), Ghana, was the creation of a Union Government,[^cite_note-63] with the aim of moving towards a [United States of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Africa). A study on the Union Government was adopted in late 2006,[^cite_note-64] and proposes various options for "completing" the African Union project. There are divisions among African states on the proposals, with some (notably [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Socialist_People's_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya)) following a maximalist view leading to a common government with an AU army; and others (especially the southern African states) supporting rather a strengthening of the existing structures, with some reforms to deal with administrative and political challenges in making the AU Commission and other bodies truly effective.[^cite_note-65]

Following a heated debate in Accra, the [Assembly of Heads of State and Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_African_Union) agreed in the form of a declaration to review the state of affairs of the AU with a view to determining its readiness towards a Union Government.[^cite_note-66] In particular, the Assembly agreed to:

- Accelerate the economic and political integration of the African continent, including the formation of a Union Government of Africa;

- Conduct an audit of the institutions and organs of the AU; review the relationship between the AU and the RECs; find ways to strengthen the AU and elaborate a timeframe to establish a Union Government of Africa.

The declaration lastly noted the "importance of involving the African peoples, including [Africans in the Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_African_Union#African_diaspora), in the processes leading to the formation of the Union Government".

Following this decision, a panel of eminent persons was set up to conduct the "audit review". The review team began its work on 1 September 2007. The review was presented to the Assembly of Heads of State and Government at the January 2008 summit in Addis Ababa. No final decision was taken on the recommendations, however, and a committee of ten heads of state was appointed to consider the review and report back to the July 2008 summit to be held in Egypt.[^cite_note-67] At the July 2008 summit, a decision was once again deferred, for a "final" debate at the January 2009 summit to be held in Addis Ababa.&#x20;

In February 2025, Djiboutian diplomat Mahamoud Ali Youssouf was elected Chairperson of the African Union Commission during the African Union summit held in Addis Ababa [^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

One of the key debates in relation to the achievement of greater continental integration is the relative priority that should be given to integration of the continent as a unit in itself or to integration of the sub-regions. The 1980 [Lagos Plan of Action for the Development of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Plan_of_Action_for_the_Development_of_Africa) and the 1991 treaty to establish the African Economic Community (also referred to as the Abuja Treaty), proposed the creation of [Regional Economic Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Economic_Communities) (RECs) as the basis for African integration, with a timetable for regional and then continental integration to follow.[^cite_note-70]

Currently, there are eight RECs recognised by the AU, each established under a separate regional treaty. They are:

- [Arab Maghreb Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Maghreb_Union) (AMU)

- [Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Market_for_Eastern_and_Southern_Africa) (COMESA)

- [Community of Sahel-Saharan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Sahel-Saharan_States) (CEN-SAD)

- [East African Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Community) (EAC)

- [Economic Community of Central African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_Central_African_States) (ECCAS)

- [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS)

- [Intergovernmental Authority on Development](1921406.md) (IGAD)

- [Southern Africa Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa_Development_Community) (SADC)

The membership of many of the communities overlaps, and their rationalisation has been under discussion for several years—and formed the theme of the 2006 Banjul summit. At the July 2007 Accra summit the Assembly finally decided to adopt a Protocol on Relations between the African Union and the Regional Economic Communities.[^cite_note-71] This protocol is intended to facilitate the harmonisation of policies and ensure compliance with the Abuja Treaty and Lagos Plan of Action time frames.

### Selection of the chairperson

In 2006, the AU decided to create a Committee "to consider the implementation of a rotation system between the regions" in relation to the presidency. Controversy arose at the 2006 summit when Sudan announced its candidacy for the AU's chairmanship, as a representative of the East African region. Several member states refused to support Sudan because of tensions over [Darfur](605837.md) (see also below). Sudan ultimately withdrew its candidacy and President [Denis Sassou-Nguesso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Sassou-Nguesso) of the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md) was elected to a one-year term. At the January 2007 summit, Sassou-Nguesso was replaced by President [John Agyekum Kufuor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Agyekum_Kufuor) of Ghana, despite another attempt by Sudan to gain the chair. The year 2007 was the 50th anniversary of Ghana's independence, a symbolic moment for the country to hold the chair of the AU—and to host the mid-year summit at which the proposed Union Government was also discussed. In January 2008, President [Jakaya Kikwete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakaya_Kikwete) of Tanzania took over as chair, representing the East African region and thus apparently ending Sudan's attempt to become chair—at least till the rotation returned to East Africa.[^cite_note-72] The current chair is [Évariste Ndayishimiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89variste_Ndayishimiye), President of [Burundi](21490998.md), whose term began on February 14, 2026.[^cite_note-73]

### Headquarters

Main article: [African Union Conference Center and Office Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Conference_Center_and_Office_Complex)

The main administrative capital of the African Union is in [Addis Ababa](52593.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), where the African Union Commission is headquartered. A new headquarters complex, the [AU Conference Center and Office Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Conference_Center_and_Office_Complex) (AUCC), was inaugurated on 28 January 2012, during the 18th AU summit.[^cite_note-AU_NewHQ-79] The complex was built by [China State Construction Engineering Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_State_Construction_Engineering_Corporation) as a gift from the [Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), and accommodates, among other facilities, a 2,500-seat plenary hall and a 20-story office tower. The tower is 99.9 meters high to signify the date 9 September 1999, when the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) voted to become the African Union.[^cite_note-Chinadaily_NewHQ-80] The building cost US \$200 million to construct.[^cite_note-\:1-81]

#### Espionage accusations

Main article: [2018 China–African Union espionage allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_China%E2%80%93African_Union_espionage_allegations)

On 26 January 2018, five years after the completion of the building of the AU Headquarters, the French newspaper *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde)*[^cite_note-82] published an article stating that the Chinese government had heavily bugged the building, installing listening devices in the walls and furniture and setting up the computer system to copy data to servers in Shanghai daily.[^cite_note-\:1-81] The Chinese government denied that they bugged the building, stating that the accusations were "utterly groundless and ridiculous".[^cite_note-\:1-81] Ethiopian Prime Minister [Hailemariam Desalegn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailemariam_Desalegn) rejected the French media report.[^cite_note-83] [Moussa Faki Mahamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Faki), head of the African Union Commission, said the allegations in the *Le Monde*'s report were false. "These are totally false allegations and I believe that we are completely disregarding them."[^cite_note-84] The African Union replaced its Chinese-supplied servers and started encrypting its communications following the event.[^cite_note-85]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_African_Union)

The individual member states of the African Union coordinate foreign policy through this agency, in addition to conducting their own [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) on a state-by-state basis. The AU represents the interests of African peoples at large in [intergovernmental organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_organisation) (IGOs); for instance, it is a [permanent observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_observers) at the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md). Both the African Union and the United Nations work in tandem to address issues of common concerns in various areas. The African Union Mission to the United Nations aspires to serve as a bridge between the two organisations.

Membership of the AU overlaps with other IGOs, and occasionally, these third-party organisations and the AU will coordinate on matters of public policy. The African Union maintains special diplomatic representation with the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassadors_from_the_United_States) and the [European Union](2899127.md).

#### Africa–Caribbean relations

See also: [Group of 77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_77) and [Organisation of African, Caribbean and Pacific States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African,_Caribbean_and_Pacific_States)

Many [Caribbean](18956035.md) nations have sought to deepen ties with the continent of [Africa](5334607.md). The African Union has referred to the Caribbean as the potential "Sixth Region" of the bloc.[^cite_note-136] Some Caribbean nations moved to join African institutions, including [Barbados](3455.md),[^cite_note-137] [the Bahamas](3451.md) and [Guyana](17238691.md), which all became members of the [African Export–Import Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Export%E2%80%93Import_Bank).[^cite_note-138] Also, the [Caribbean Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Development_Bank) signed a cooperation strategic partnership agreement with the [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank) (AfDB).[^cite_note-139] [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md) has also shown interest in a direct transport link between Africa and the Caribbean.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] However, it has resulted in some challenges regarding migration.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] Africa and the Caribbean have formed a collaborative alliance known as the A3 Plus to formulate matters of common interest in global fora.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

#### Africa–China relations

Main article: [Africa–China relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa%E2%80%93China_relations)

One of the leading economic partners of the continent has been the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC). The [Forum on China–Africa Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_on_China%E2%80%93Africa_Cooperation) is the main multi-lateral coordination mechanism between the African countries and China.[^cite_note-152] Since joining FOCAC in 2012, the African Union has increasingly played a coordinating role, although each African country in FOCAC continues to represent itself individually.[^cite_note-153]

#### Africa–EU relations



Group photo at the [2024 Italy–Africa Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italy%E2%80%93Africa_Summit) in [Rome](25458.md)

For the European Commission, the European Union's relationship with Africa is a key priority. The future Africa-EU partnership vision of the European Commission and the European External Action Service is outlined in the Joint Communication "Towards a Comprehensive Strategy with Africa". It proposes partnering on:[^cite_note-auto-154][^cite_note-155]

- Green Transition and Energy Access

- Digital Transformation

- Sustainable Growth and Jobs

  [^cite_note-156]

  [^cite_note-africa-eu-partnership.org-157]

- Peace, Security and Governance

  [^cite_note-africa-eu-partnership.org-157]

- Migration and Mobility

  [^cite_note-158]

On 2 December 2020, five Africa-Europe Foundation Strategy Groups were established in the areas of Health, Digital, Agriculture and Sustainable Food Systems, Sustainable energy and Transport and Connectivity. It was done together with a consortium comprising Friends of Europe, Mo Ibrahim Foundation and IPEMED.[^cite_note-auto-154]

In regards to strengthening resilience, peace, security and governance, the African Union and the European Union recognize that resilience, peace, security and governance are all closely linked. Peace and security are important elements for sustainable development, prosperity and resilience of societies.[^cite_note-africa-eu-partnership.org-157] For ensuring peace and security, it had worked out the African Peace Facility.[^cite_note-159] This has been itself replaced in 2021 by the European Peace Facility.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] The AU and EU are also partnering on promoting sustainable resources management, [environmental resilience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation), and [climate change mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Africa)[^cite_note-africa-eu-partnership.org-157] The [Africa Adaptation Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Adaptation_Initiative) is also being supported by the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-162]

#### Africa–India relations

Main article: [Africa-India relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa-India_relations)



Inaugural [India–Africa Forum Summit](16850418.md) held in New Delhi, 2008

The [India–Africa Forum Summit](16850418.md) (IAFS) is the official platform for [Africa-India relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa-India_relations). IAFS is held once in every three years. It was first held from April 4 to April 8, 2008 in [New Delhi](51585.md), [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-163] In 2015, the [Third India-Africa Forum Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_India-Africa_Forum_Summit) was held in [New Delhi](51585.md) and summit was attended by 51 heads of states of the African Union.[^cite_note-164] In 2023, the African Union was admitted to [G20](2206608.md) under the presidency of [India](14533.md), chaired by Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) in [New Delhi](51585.md).

#### Africa–Palestine relations

The African Union periodically invites the [President of the State of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_State_of_Palestine) to make an opening speech during its annual Summit in Addis Ababa. According to Article 10 of the Rules of Procedure of the Union, during the opening of the meeting sessions, the President of the State of Palestine in person is entitled to make an opening speech. After the [Hamas attack on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Al-Aqsa_Flood) in October 2023, African Union Commission Chairperson [Moussa Faki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Faki) expressed his "utmost concern" at the situation and called for an immediate cessation of hostilities; additionally, he recalled that denial of the fundamental rights of the [Palestinian people](23267.md), particularly that of an independent and sovereign [State of Palestine](241405.md), was the "main cause of the permanent [Israeli-Palestinian tension](46216.md)".[^cite_note-165]

#### Africa–Russia relations

Russia hosted the first [Africa-Russia heads-of-state summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Russia%E2%80%93Africa_Summit) on October 23–24, 2019, Representatives from all 54 African states, including 43 heads-of-state or government, attended the summit.[^cite_note-166]

The [second Russia-Africa Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Russia-Africa_Summit) was scheduled for October 2022 in [Addis Ababa](52593.md), but was then rescheduled to 26–29 July 2023 in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

The first summit was the [South Korea–Africa Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_Korea%E2%80%93Africa_Summit), Seoul in June 2024.[^cite_note-169]

#### Africa–Turkey relations

Turkey–Africa relations have gained substantial momentum since the declaration of Turkey as a strategic partner of the continent by the African Union in January 2008. Since 2008, various major summits and meetings have been taking place between Turkey and AU. The first summit was The Turkey–Africa Cooperation summit, Istanbul in August 2008.[^cite_note-170]

As of 2020, Turkey has embassies in 42 countries and commercial counselors in 26 countries on the Africa continent. Turkey's national flag carrier [Turkish Airlines](167925.md) also flies to 35 destinations on the continent.

#### Africa–United Kingdom relations

The sovereignty of the [Chagos Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago) in the Indian Ocean is [disputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago_sovereignty_dispute) between the United Kingdom and [Mauritius](19201.md).[^cite_note-171] In February 2019, the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) in [The Hague](30269.md) issued [an advisory opinion stating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_Consequences_of_the_Separation_of_the_Chagos_Archipelago_from_Mauritius_in_1965) that the UK must transfer the Chagos Archipelago to Mauritius.[^cite_note-172] The African Union has urged the United Kingdom to comply with a [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) resolution calling for it to withdraw from the Chagos Islands.[^cite_note-173]

#### Africa–United States relations



[AUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Commission) Chairperson [Moussa Faki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Faki), US President [Joe Biden](145422.md) and African leaders at the [United States–Africa Leaders Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Africa_Leaders_Summit_2022) in Washington, D.C., 15 December 2022

In 2017, [Donald Trump](4848272.md), [President of the United States](24113.md), issued an executive order to ban citizens from seven countries with suspected links to terrorism from entering the United States. Three of these are African countries, and members of the AU. During the 28th African Union Summit in Ethiopia, African leaders criticised the ban as they expressed their growing concerns for the future of the African economy under President Trump's leadership and subsequent policies.[^cite_note-174]

### Military

See also: [List of African Union military interventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_Union_military_interventions)

The African Union has the power to militarily intervene on behalf of its member states as laid out in Article 4(h) of the [Constitutive Act of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutive_Act_of_the_African_Union), "in respect of grave circumstances, namely: war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity".[^cite_note-175]

#### Togo

In response to the death of [Gnassingbé Eyadéma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnassingb%C3%A9_Eyad%C3%A9ma), President of [Togo](30138.md), on 5 February 2005, AU leaders described the appointment of his son, [Faure Gnassingbé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faure_Gnassingb%C3%A9), to the presidency to have been a [military coup](25914154.md).[^cite_note-176] Togo's constitution calls for the speaker of parliament to succeed the president in the event of his death. By law, the parliament speaker must call national elections to choose a new president within sixty days. The AU's protest forced Gnassingbé to hold elections. Under heavy allegations of election fraud, he was officially elected president on 4 May 2005.

#### Mauritania

On 3 August 2005, a coup in [Mauritania](18778516.md) led the African Union to suspend the country from all organisational activities. The military council that took control of Mauritania promised to hold elections within two years.[^cite_note-177] These were held in early 2007, the first time that the country had held elections that were generally agreed to be of an acceptable standard. Following the elections, Mauritania's membership of the AU was restored. However, on 6 August 2008, a fresh coup overthrew the government elected in 2007. The AU once again suspended Mauritania from the continental body.[^cite_note-178] The suspension was once again lifted in 2009 after the military junta agreed with the opposition to organise elections.[^cite_note-179]

#### Mali



[Al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb) (formerly [GSPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafist_Group_for_Preaching_and_Combat)) area of operations

In March 2012, a military coup was staged in Mali, when an alliance of Touareg and Islamist forces conquered the north, resulting in a coming to power of the Islamists. This resulted in the deaths of hundreds of Malian soldiers and the loss of control over their camps and positions.[^cite_note-180] After a military intervention with help from French troops, the region was in control of the Malian army. To reinstall local authorities, the AU helped to form a caretaker government, supporting it and holding presidential elections in Mali in July 2013.[^cite_note-dandc.eu-181]

In 2013, a summit for the African Union was held and it was decided that the African Union was going to enlarge their military presence in Mali. The AU decided to do this because of increasing tensions between al-Qaeda forces and the Mali army. There have been several rebel groups that are vying for control of parts of Mali. These rebel groups include the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad (MNLA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad), the National Front for the Liberation of Azawad (FLNA), Ganda Koy, Ganda Izo, [Ansar ad-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Din_Front), and [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) in the [Islamic Maghreb (AQIM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb). AU forces have been tasked with counterinsurgency missions in Mali as well as governing presidential elections to ensure as smooth a transition of power as possible.[^cite_note-182]

#### 2021 hotspots

A disputed election in December 2020 has led to intensification of the [Central African Republic Civil War](38024441.md), displacing 200,000 people. United Nations peacekeepers, including soldiers from Russia and Rwanda, have kept the rebels out of [Bangui](56617.md), but rebels control much of the rest of the country. The AU has not sent peacekeepers to the areas because of a lack of agreement on how to handle the situation: [Chad](5488.md) and the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md) support the rebels while [Rwanda](25645.md) and [Angola](701.md) support the government.[^cite_note-2021_priorities-183]

The [Tigray War](65760352.md) in Ethiopia has left millions in need of humanitarian aid. Eritrean troops are said to be supporting the Ethiopian government, and there have been border conflicts with Sudan. The relationship between Sudan and Ethiopia is further complicated by the [Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ethiopian_Renaissance_Dam) project, which also directly affects Egypt.[^cite_note-2021_priorities-183]

##### Coup Belt



Map of successful coups in Africa since 2020

The term [Coup Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_Belt) originated from coups that were staged beginning in the early 2020s, including in [Mali](19127.md) in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), [Guinea](68651369.md), [Chad](39073394.md#President_of_the_Transitional_Military_Council), and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Sudan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) in 2021, two in [Burkina Faso](3470.md) in [January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2022_Burkina_Faso_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [September](71882074.md) in 2022, and [Niger](21373.md) and [Gabon](12027.md) in 2023. The region also saw attempted coups in [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nigerien_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2021_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) in 2021, [Guinea–Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Guinea-Bissau_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) and [The Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Gambian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) in 2022, and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sudan_conflict) and [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sierra_Leone_coup_plot) in 2023. After the 2023 Nigerien coup, these countries formed a continuous chain stretching between the east and west coasts of Africa.

### Regional conflicts and peacekeeping

Main article: [African Standby Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Standby_Force)

One of the objectives of the AU is to "promote peace, security, and stability on the continent".[^cite_note-184] Among its principles is "Peaceful resolution of conflicts among Member States of the Union through such appropriate means as may be decided upon by the Assembly".[^cite_note-185] The primary body charged with implementing these objectives and principles is the Peace and Security Council. The PSC has the power, among other things, to authorise peace support missions, to impose sanctions in case of unconstitutional change of government, and to "take initiatives and action it deems appropriate" in response to potential or actual conflicts. The PSC is a decision-making body in its own right, and its decisions are binding on member states.

Article 4(h) of the Constitutive Act, repeated in Article 4 of the Protocol to the Constitutive Act on the PSC, also recognises the right of the Union to intervene in a member state in circumstances of war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity. Any decision to intervene in a member state under Article 4 of the Constitutive Act will be made by the Assembly on the recommendation of the PSC.

Since it first met in 2004, the PSC has been active in relation to the crises in [Darfur](605837.md), [Comoros](5403.md), [Somalia](27358.md), [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), [Burundi](21490998.md), [Ivory Coast](57744.md) and other countries. It has adopted resolutions creating the AU peacekeeping operations in Somalia and Darfur, and imposing sanctions against persons undermining peace and security (such as travel bans and asset freezes against the leaders of the rebellion in Comoros). The council is in the process of overseeing the establishment of a "standby force" to serve as a permanent African peacekeeping force.[^cite_note-\:4-4]

The founding treaty of the AU also called for the establishment of the [African Peace and Security Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Peace_and_Security_Architecture) (APSA), including the [African Standby Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Standby_Force) (ASF), which is to be deployed in emergencies. That means, in cases of genocide or other serious human-rights violations, an ASF mission can be launched even against the wishes of the government of the country concerned, as long as it is approved by the AU. In past AU peacekeeping missions, the concept was not yet applied and forces had to be mobilised from member states. Although the ASF was declared fully operational in 2016 and used from 2017 onwards in ECOWAS, ECCAS and SADC interventions to the [Gambia](65656.md), [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho), [Mozambique](19301.md) and the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), it has not been deployed in the same way as originally conceived. Instead of the PSC acting as the primary initiator of ASF action as previously planned, Regional Economic Communities have taken charge of mobilizing ASF capabilities. In light of continued criticism, ASF reform remains a prominent topic of discussion at the AU.[^cite_note-dandc.eu-181][^cite_note-186]

#### Darfur, Sudan

Main article: [African Union Mission in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_in_Sudan)



[South Sudanese independence referendum, 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudanese_independence_referendum,_2011)

In response to the 2003–2020 [Darfur conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfur_conflict) in Sudan, the AU deployed 7,000 peacekeepers, many from Rwanda and Nigeria, to [Darfur](605837.md). While a donor's conference in Addis Ababa in 2005 helped raise funds to sustain the peacekeepers through that year and into 2006, in July 2006 the AU said it would pull out at the end of September when its mandate expires.[^cite_note-187] Critics of the AU peacekeepers, including [Eric Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Reeves), said these forces were largely ineffective due to lack of funds, personnel, and expertise. In June 2006, the [United States Congress](31756.md) appropriated US \$173 million for the AU force. Some, such as the [Genocide Intervention Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_Intervention_Network), called for UN or [NATO](21133.md) intervention to augment and/or replace the AU peacekeepers. The UN considered deploying a force, though it would not likely enter the country until at least October 2007.[^cite_note-188] The under-funded and badly equipped AU mission was set to expire on 31 December 2006 but was extended to 30 June 2007 and merged with the [United Nations African Union Mission in Darfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_African_Union_Mission_in_Darfur) in October 2007. In July 2009 the African Union ceased cooperation with the [International Criminal Court](14880.md), refusing to recognise the international arrest warrant it had issued against Sudan's leader, [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md), who was indicted in 2008 for [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-189]

The AU struggled to have a strategic role in the independence talks and the reconciliation process of [South Sudan](32350676.md), due to overwhelming interests of African and non-African powers, its influence was limited and not consistent.[^cite_note-190]

#### Somalia

Main article: [African Union Mission to Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia)



Kenyan soldiers and fighters of the [Ras Kamboni Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Kamboni_Brigades), a Somali government-allied militia, near [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo), Somalia, 2012

From the early 1990s up until 2000, [Somalia](27358.md) was without a functioning central government. A peace agreement aimed at ending the [civil war](3070332.md) that broke out following the collapse of the [Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre) regime was signed in 2006 after many years of peace talks. However, the new government was almost immediately threatened by further violence. In February 2007, the African Union (AU) and European Union (EU) worked together to establish the [African Union Mission in Somalia (AMISOM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia_\(2007-present\)). The purpose of AMISOM was to create a foundation that would hopefully provide aid to some of Somalia's most vulnerable and keep the peace in the region. They are tasked with everything from protecting federal institutions to facilitating humanitarian relief operations. Much of the AU's opposition comes from an Islamic extremist group named [al-Shabaab](8726463.md).[^cite_note-\:3-191] To temporarily shore up the government's military base, starting in March 2007, AU soldiers began arriving in [Mogadishu](60154.md) as part of a peacekeeping force that was intended by the AU to eventually be 8,000 strong.[^cite_note-bbc2115736-21] Eritrea recalled its ambassadors to the African Union on 20 November 2009[^cite_note-192] after the African Union called on the United Nations Security Council to impose sanctions on them due to their alleged support of Somali Islamists attempting to topple the [Transitional Federal Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Federal_Government) of Somalia, the internationally recognised government of Somalia which holds Somalia's seat on the African Union.[^cite_note-193] On 22 December 2009, the United Nations Security Council passed [UNSCR 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1907), which imposed an arms embargo on Eritrea, travel bans on Eritrean leaders, and asset freezes on Eritrean officials. Eritrea strongly criticised the resolution. In January 2011, Eritrea reestablished their mission to the AU in Addis Ababa.[^cite_note-194]

In the fall of 2011, AMISOM forces, along with Kenyan and Ethiopian forces, launched a set of offensive attacks on the [al-Shabaab](8726463.md). In these attacks, [AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia_\(2007-present\)) forces were able to reclaim key cities including the Somali capital of [Mogadishu](60154.md). In September 2013, political scientist Ethan Bueno de Mesquita argued that with the help of [AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia_\(2007-present\)) forces, they had made it "nearly impossible for [al-Shabaab](8726463.md) to hold territory even in its former strongholds in southern Somalia". Although much progress has been made towards peace in the region, it should still be noted that African Union forces' still get attacked regularly. Despite [AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia_\(2007-present\)) being effective, it is vastly underfunded and many forces lack the resources required. Funding for humanitarian relief and the formation of armies tends to be vastly undercut.[^cite_note-\:3-191]

#### Anjouan, Comoros

Main article: [2008 invasion of Anjouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_invasion_of_Anjouan)

A successful 2008 invasion of [Anjouan](184799.md) by AU and [Comoros](5403.md) forces to stop self-declared president [Mohamed Bacar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Bacar), whose 2007 re-election was declared illegal.[^cite_note-web.archive.org-195] Prior to the invasion, France helped transport Tanzanian troops but their position in the disagreement was questioned when a French police helicopter was suspected of attempting to sneak Bacar into French exile.[^cite_note-196] The first wave of troops landed on Anjouan Bay on 25 March and soon took over the airfield in [Ouani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouani), ultimately aiming to locate and remove Bacar from office.[^cite_note-web.archive.org-195] On the same day, the airport, capital, and second city were overrun and the presidential palace was deserted.[^cite_note-197] Bacar escaped and sought asylum in France. The Comoros government demanded his return, in order to determine his fate.[^cite_note-198] Many of Bacar's main supporters were arrested by the end of March, including [Caabi El-Yachroutu Mohamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caabi_El-Yachroutu_Mohamed) and [Ibrahim Halidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Halidi). Bacar's asylum request was rejected on 15 May, as France agreed to cooperate with the Comoran government's demand.[^cite_note-199] At the 29 June elections, [Moussa Toybou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Toybou) won the presidency.[^cite_note-200]

### Current issues

The AU faces many challenges, including health issues such as combating [malaria](20423.md) and the [AIDS/HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS/HIV) epidemic; political issues such as confronting undemocratic regimes and mediating in the many civil wars; economic issues such as improving the [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of millions of impoverished, uneducated Africans; ecological issues such as dealing with recurring famines, [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification), and lack of ecological [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability); as well as the [legal](8195726.md) [issues regarding Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_Western_Sahara).

#### Corruption

Daniel Batidam, an anti-corruption advisory board member of the African Union, resigned after stating that the organisation had "multiple irregularities" and that "issues have come up over and over again" regarding corruption. The African Union quickly accepted his resignation, with Batidam saying that it was a sign that mismanagement towards corruption will "continue with business as usual".[^cite_note-201]

In a story published on 12 March 2020, AU staff alleged that Commission chairman Moussa Faki Mahamat was guilty of corruption and cronyism, as well as running of a "mafia-style cartel" that operates with impunity.[^cite_note-202] The allegations were contained in a memo leaked to the South African *[Mail & Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail_&_Guardian)*. This followed [sexual harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_harassment) allegations raised in late 2018.

#### Libyan crisis

In 2011, when the conflict in Libya began, the African Union was initially criticised for doing little to prevent the conflict's escalation. Additionally, the AU hesitated to take a side. It was unclear whether the AU supported the Libyan regime or the rebels. This occurred as several human rights violations were perpetrated against members of the Libyan regime. It was later realised that the AU's hesitation was due to its lack of capacity and its inability to engage in democratic reform.[^cite_note-\:2-203]

The AU attempted to mediate in the early stages of the [2011 Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_civil_war), forming an *ad hoc* committee of five presidents ([Congolese](19599929.md) President [Denis Sassou Nguesso](158608.md), [Malian](19127.md) President [Amadou Toumani Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Toumani_Tour%C3%A9), [Mauritanian](18778516.md) President [Mohamed Ould Abdel Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Ould_Abdel_Aziz), [South African](17416221.md) President [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma), and [Ugandan](31816.md) President [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md)) to broker a truce.[^cite_note-204] However, the beginning of the [NATO](21133.md)-led [military intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya) in March 2011 prevented the committee from traveling to [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Socialist_People's_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) to meet with Libyan leader [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md).[^cite_note-205] As a body, the AU sharply dissented from the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md)'s decision to create a [no-fly zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-fly_zone) over Libya,[^cite_note-206] though a few member states, such as [Botswana](3464.md),[^cite_note-207] [Gabon](12027.md),[^cite_note-208] [Zambia](34415.md),[^cite_note-209] and others expressed support for [the resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1973).

As a result of Gaddafi's defeat at the [Battle of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Battle_of_Tripoli) (the war's deciding battle), in August 2011, the [Arab League](52625.md) voted to recognise the anti-Gaddafi [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) as the legitimate government of the country, pending elections.[^cite_note-210] The council has been recognised by several AU member states, including two [Arab League](52625.md) members.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212] However, the AU [Peace and Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Security_Council) voted on 26 August 2011 not to recognise it, insisting on a ceasefire and on the formation of a national [unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_government) by both sides.[^cite_note-213] A number of AU member states led by [Ethiopia](187749.md), [Nigeria](21383.md), and [Rwanda](25645.md) requested that the AU recognise the [NTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) as Libya's interim governing authority,[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215] and several other AU member states have recognised the [NTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) regardless of the Peace and Security Council's decision.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] However, AU member states [Algeria](358.md)[^cite_note-218] and [Zimbabwe](34399.md)[^cite_note-219] have indicated they will not recognise the [NTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council), and [South Africa](17416221.md) has expressed reservations as well.[^cite_note-220]

On 20 September 2011, the African Union officially recognised the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) as the legitimate representative of [Libya](17633.md).[^cite_note-221]

In post-Gaddafi Libya, the African Union believes it still has an important responsibility to the country despite its failure to contribute to the conflict when it originated. Although the African Union is there to keep peace, it is not a long-term solution. The goal, as stated by the AU, is to establish a Libyan government that is sustainable to ensuring peace in Libya. To achieve some level of peace in Libya, the AU has to moderate peace talks which are aimed at achieving compromises and power sharing accommodations as well.[^cite_note-\:2-203]

Member [states](23604120.md) of the African Union cover almost the entirety of continental Africa, except for several territories held by Spain ([Ceuta](6443.md), [Melilla](20226.md), and [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera)). In addition, European countries have dependencies among the offshore islands of Africa: Spain (the [Canary Islands](5717.md) and the islands of the [plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa)); France ([Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte), [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union), and the [Scattered Islands in the Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_Islands_in_the_Indian_Ocean)); Portugal (the [Azores](3226.md), [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira), and the [Savage Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savage_Islands)); and the United Kingdom ([Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena,_Ascension_and_Tristan_da_Cunha)). The geography of the African Union is wildly diverse, including the world's largest hot desert (the [Sahara](325363.md)), huge jungles and [savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna), and the world's longest river (the [Nile](21244.md)).

The AU has an area of 29,922,059 square kilometres (11,552,972 sq mi), with 24,165 kilometres (15,015 mi) of coastline. The vast majority of this area is on continental Africa, while the only significant territories off the mainland are the island of [Madagascar](18964.md) (the world's largest [microcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_fragment) and [fourth-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area)) and the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) (geographically a part of Asia), accounting for slightly less than 2% of the total area.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_African_Union)

The [African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Free_Trade_Area) was created among 54 of the 55 AU nations, with trade commencing as of 1 January 2021.[^cite_note-222] The [African Export–Import Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Export%E2%80%93Import_Bank), to support this initiative, created the [Pan-African Payment and Settlement System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Payment_and_Settlement_System).

The AU's future goals include a [customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_union), a [single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_market), a [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank), and a common currency (see [African Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Monetary_Union)), thereby establishing [economic and monetary union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_monetary_union).[^cite_note-223]

## Demographics

Main articles: [Demographics of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Africa) and [Member states of the African Union](30486666.md)

### Population

The total population of the African Union, as of 2017, is estimated at more than 1.25 billion, with a growth rate of more than 2.5% p.a.[^cite_note-239]

### Migration

In 2018,[^cite_note-240] the African Union adopted the Free Movement Protocol.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242] This protocol allows for free movement of people between countries that are part of the African Union.

Article 14 of the Protocol to the treaty establishing the African economic community relating to the free movement of persons, right of residence, and the right of establishment discusses the free movement of workers.[^cite_note-243]

The African Union also has a Migration Policy Framework for Africa (MPFA).[^cite_note-244]

Forced displacement of people and groups has also been an area of focus for the AU—over thirty states have ratified the [Kampala Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala_Convention), the only continental treaty focusing on internally displaced persons in the world.[^cite_note-245]

Beginning in 2016, the African Union introduced continent-wide passports.[^cite_note-passport-246]

As of 2025 there has been an increased push for an implementation of the Free Movement Protocol. The UN has urged more states to ratify the protocol, and has praised Ghana for allowing visa free travel for all individuals with a passport of an African country.[^cite_note-247]

### Languages

See also: [List of official languages by country and territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_official_languages_by_country_and_territory)



Map showing the traditional language families represented in Africa (1996)

The official languages of the African Union are Arabic, English, French, Portuguese, Spanish, [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language), and "any other African language".[^cite_note-au.int-3][^cite_note-\:4-4] The primary working languages of the African Union are English and French. To a lesser extent Portuguese and Arabic are used. The [Constitutive Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutive_Act_of_the_African_Union), for example, is written in English, French and Arabic, while the protocol amending the Constitutive Act is written in English, French and Portuguese. As of 2020, the AU website is available in its entirety in English, partially in French and minimally in Arabic.[^cite_note-248] Portuguese and Swahili versions were added as "coming soon" (*em breve*) in April 2019.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

According to the Constitutive Act of the African Union:[^cite_note-251]

> The working languages of the Union and all its institutions shall be, if possible, African languages, Arabic, English, French and Portuguese.

A protocol amending the Constitutive Act was adopted in 2003 and as of April 2020 has been ratified by 30 of the 37 member states needed for a two-thirds majority. It would change the above wording to:[^cite_note-252]

> 1\. The official languages of the Union and all its institutions shall be Arabic, English, French, Portuguese, Spanish, Kiswahili and any other African language.\
> &#x20;2\. The Executive Council shall determine the process and practical modalities for the use of official languages as working languages.

Founded in 2001 under the auspices of the AU, the [African Academy of Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Academy_of_Languages) promotes the usage and perpetuation of [African languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Africa) among African people. In 2004 [Joaquim Chissano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquim_Chissano) of Mozambique addressed the assembly in Swahili, but had to translate his words himself.[^cite_note-253] The AU declared 2006 the Year of African Languages.[^cite_note-Year_of_African_Languages-254][^cite_note-Year_of_African_Languages_2-255] 2006 also marked Ghana's 55th anniversary since it founded the [Bureau of Ghana Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Ghana_Languages), originally known as the Gold Coast Vernacular Literature Bureau.

### Health

#### AIDS in Africa

The AU has been active in addressing the AIDS pandemic in Africa. In 2001, the AU established AIDS Watch Africa to coordinate and mobilise a continent-wide response.[^cite_note-256] [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md), especially southern and eastern Africa, is the most affected area in the world. Though this region is home to only 6.2% of the world's population, it is also home to half of the world's population infected with HIV.[^cite_note-257] While the measurement of HIV prevalence rates has proved methodologically challenging, more than 20% of the sexually active population of many countries of southern Africa may be infected, with South Africa, Botswana, [Kenya](188171.md), Namibia, and [Zimbabwe](34399.md) all expected to have a decrease in [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) by an average of 6.5 years. The pandemic has had massive implications for the economy of the continent, reducing economic growth rates by 2–4% across Africa.[^cite_note-258]

In July 2007, the AU endorsed two new initiatives to combat the AIDS crisis, including a push to recruit, train and integrate two million community health workers into the continent's healthcare systems.[^cite_note-259]

In January 2012, the African Union Assembly requested that the African Union Commission would work out "a roadmap of shared responsibility to draw on African efforts for a viable health funding with support of traditional and emerging partners to address AIDS dependency response". Once created, the roadmap (as it is officially known) provided a group of solutions that would enhance the shared responsibility and global solidarity for [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS), [TB](30653.md), and [Malaria](20423.md) responses in Africa by 2015. The roadmap was organised into three pillars: diversified financing, access to medicines, and enhanced health governance. The roadmap held stakeholders accountable for the realisation of these solutions between 2012 and 2015.

The first pillar, diversified financing, ensures that countries begin to develop a country specific financial sustainability plans with clear targets, and identify and maximise opportunities to diversify funding sources in order to increase the domestic resource allocation to AIDS and other diseases.

The second pillar, access to affordable and quality-assured medicines, tries to promote and facilitate investing in leading medicine hub manufacturers in Africa, accelerate and strengthen medicine regulatory harmonisation, and create legislation that would help to protect the knowledge of the researchers who develop these life-saving medicines.

The third pillar, enhanced leadership and governance, tries to invest in programs that support people and communities to prevent HIV and ensure that leadership at all levels is mobilised to implement the roadmap. There are several organisations that will ensure the smooth implementation of the roadmap, including [NEPAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Partnership_for_Africa's_Development), [UNAIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_United_Nations_Programme_on_HIV/AIDS), [WHO](33583.md), and several other UN partners.[^cite_note-260]

#### COVID-19 pandemic

By February 2021, the [COVID-19 pandemic in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Africa) had resulted in 3.6 million confirmed cases and 89,000 related deaths, and only 25% of African countries had adequate plans for vaccination, according to the [Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention](63909811.md) (Africa CDC).[^cite_note-2021_priorities-183] The pandemic has also devastated economies around the world, including in Africa.

## Culture

### Symbols



Emblem of the African Union

The [emblem of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_the_African_Union) consists of a gold ribbon bearing small interlocking red rings, from which palm leaves shoot up around an outer gold circle and an inner green circle, within which is a gold representation of Africa. The red interlinked rings stand for African solidarity and the blood shed for the liberation of Africa; the palm leaves, for peace; the gold, for Africa's wealth and bright future; the green, for African hopes and aspirations. To symbolise African unity, the silhouette of Africa is drawn without internal borders.[^cite_note-261]

The African Union adopted its new flag at its 14th Ordinary Session of the Assembly of Heads of State and Government taking place in Addis Ababa 2010. During the 8th African Union Summit which took place in Addis Ababa on 29 and 30 January 2007, the Heads of State and Government decided to launch a competition for the selection of a new flag for the Union. They prescribed a green background for the flag symbolising hope of Africa and stars to represent Member States.

Pursuant to this decision, the African Union Commission (AUC) organised a competition for the selection of a new flag for the African Union. The AUC received a total of 106 entries proposed by citizens of 19 African countries and 2 from the Diaspora. The proposals were then examined by a panel of experts put in place by the African Union Commission and selected from the five African regions for short listing according to the main directions given by the Heads of State and Government.[^cite_note-bojia-262]

At the 13th Ordinary Session of the Assembly, the Heads of State and Government examined the report of the Panel and selected one among all the proposals. The flag is now part of the paraphernalia of the African Union and replaces the old one.

The old [flag of the African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_African_Union) bears a broad green horizontal stripe, a narrow band of gold, the emblem of the African Union at the centre of a broad white stripe, another narrow gold band and a final broad green stripe. Again, the green and gold symbolise Africa's hopes and aspirations as well as its wealth and bright future, and the white represents the purity of Africa's desire for friends throughout the world. The flag has led to the creation of the "[national colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_colours)" of Africa of gold and green (sometimes together with white). These colours are visible in one way or another in the flags of many African nations. Together the colours green, gold, and red constitute the [Pan-African colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_colours).

The African Union has adopted the anthem "[Let Us All Unite and Celebrate Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_Us_All_Unite_and_Celebrate_Together)".

### Celebration

[Africa Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Day) (formerly African Freedom Day and African Liberation Day) is an annual commemoration regarding the founding of the Organisation of African Unity (OAU), on 25 May 1963, and occurring on the same date of the month each year. Other celebrations include the following:

- The Fez Festival of World Sacred Music: a week-long celebration for harmony between cultures with dancing, Moroccan music, art exhibitions and films.

  [^cite_note-263]

- The Knysna [Oyster festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_festival): held in [Knysna](1166026.md) and focused around sport, food and their oyster heritage.

  [^cite_note-264]

- [Lake of Stars Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_Stars_Festival): three-day celebration that takes place in [Lake Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Malawi), showcasing African music and welcoming people from around the world.

  [^cite_note-265]

- [Fête du Vodoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%AAte_du_Vodoun): also known as the Ouidah Voodoo Festival. It is centered around their rituals on voodoo temples, with entertainment that includes horse races and traditional drum performances.

  [^cite_note-266]

- [Umhlanga (ceremony)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umhlanga_\(ceremony\)): is mainly a private event for young women but on the sixth and seventh days the traditions are done publicly.

  [^cite_note-267]

- [African Border Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Border_Day) is celebrated annually on 7 June to promote peace, [cross-border cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-border_cooperation) and regional and continental integration in Africa

- Marsabit [Lake Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Turkana) Cultural Festival: held in Kenya and celebrates harmony amongst tribes with their culture, singing, dancing and traditional costumes.

  [^cite_note-268]

- [Enkutatash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enkutatash) is the word for the Ethiopian New Year in Amharic, the official language of Ethiopia. It occurs on 11 September in the Gregorian Calendar; except for the year preceding a leap year, when it occurs on 12 September.
