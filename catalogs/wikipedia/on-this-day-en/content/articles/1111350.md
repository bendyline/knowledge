The **Qatar national football team** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): منتخب قطر لكرة القدم), nicknamed "The Maroons", represents [Qatar](8238258.md) in international [football](10568.md), and is controlled by the Qatar Football Association, which is affiliated with the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC) and comes under the global jurisdiction of world football’s governing body [FIFA](11049.md). They play their home games at [Jassim bin Hamad Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jassim_bin_Hamad_Stadium).[^cite_note-4]

The team won the Arabian Gulf Cup in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup), [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Arabian_Gulf_Cup). They have appeared in ten [Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) tournaments and won it twice in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup), beating [Japan](993546.md), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), and [South Korea](1018627.md) in the process during 2019, conceding just one goal.

Qatar hosted the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) and therefore qualified automatically for what was their first appearance in the event. Amidst the controversy, it was the first time an [Arab nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) hosted the competition.[^cite_note-5] On 25 November 2022, Qatar were the first team to be eliminated from the World Cup; subsequently, they became the worst performing host nation in the history of the FIFA World Cup, losing every game. On 14 October 2025, Qatar qualified directly for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after a 2–1 victory over the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-6]

Qatar has footballing rivalries with [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team),[^cite_note-7] [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team),[^cite_note-8] and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-9]

## History

### Early years (1940s–1950s)

[Football](10568.md) was brought to [Qatar](8238258.md) during a time which coincided with the initial discovery of oil reserves in [Dukhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukhan) in 1940.[^cite_note-10] By 1948, teams were created by expatriate oil workers from countries such as [India](14533.md), and various Arabian nations. Among the first documented teams in Qatar were a team formed for the Northern Arab Region, and a team formed near the Al-Khatia area of Dukhan.[^cite_note-11] The first tournament in the country was the "Ezz Eddin Tournament", held by the Qatar Oil Company in 1951. In the late 1950s, the name was changed to the "Pukett Cup".[^cite_note-12]

With the growing interest in football, The [Qatar Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Football_Association) (QFA) was established in 1960, and the QFA joined [FIFA](11049.md) in 1963.[^cite_note-13] Simultaneously during this period, the [Bahrain Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_Football_Association) were drawing up plans for the establishment of a regional football competition within the [GCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperation_Council_for_the_Arab_States_of_the_Gulf) and Qatari officials were involved with the collaboration of this proposal.[^cite_note-14] The plans came to fruition and in March 1970, when the [Arabian Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Gulf_Cup) was inaugurated.[^cite_note-15]

### Rise of Qatari football: 1970s–1980s

The Qatar national team played its first official match on 27 March 1970 against hosts [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team) in the [1970 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Arabian_Gulf_Cup), losing 1–2 with Mubarak Faraj scoring the sole goal for Qatar.[^cite_note-16] The newly formed Qatar side gave underwhelming performances in the tournament, coming in last place with a single point, with the highlight of their tournament being a 1–1 draw with rivals [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) in their final match, and player [Khaled Ballan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Ballan) winning the Best Player of the Tournament.[^cite_note-17]

In 1972, the next edition of the [1972 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Arabian_Gulf_Cup), Qatar again finished last place after suffering 3 straight defeats.[^cite_note-18] The next tournament in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Arabian_Gulf_Cup) proved to be somewhat of a breakthrough for the Qataris as they achieved their first triumph in international football with a 4–0 victory over [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team). The team lost to [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) in the semi-finals, where they achieved a 3rd place finish in the tournament, after emerging victorious in a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) against the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), Qatari player [Mohammed Ghanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Ghanim_\(footballer\)) won the Best Player of the Tournament award.[^cite_note-19]

Qatar entered the [1976 AFC Asian Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification) for the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) which was held in the next year. They were not successful in qualifying, with [Iraq](1039652.md) and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) qualifying. Despite this setback, Qatar went on to finish 3rd place in the [1976 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup) as the host nation the next year, solidifying themselves as one of the best teams in the [Arab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), with emerging players such as [Mansour Muftah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Muftah), [Khaled Ballan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Ballan), [Mohammed Ghanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Ghanim_\(footballer\)).[^cite_note-20]

The national team participated in the [FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) match for the [1978 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup). Qatar was set to play the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) on 11 March 1977, but a last-minute with-drawal of the [Emiratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiratis) team from the competition merely postponed Qatar's debut until two days later when [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team) were defeated 2–0 in [Doha](26214389.md).[^cite_note-21]

### 1980s–1990s

Qatar debuted for their first AFC Asian Cup in the [1980 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_AFC_Asian_Cup) under Brazilian head coach [Evaristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaristo_de_Macedo). They had qualified for the tournament after topping a relatively easy group composing of [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_football_team) and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_national_football_team). In the main tournament, Qatar was unsuccessful in advancing to the knockout stage, making an early exit from the competition with two defeats, one draw and one win.[^cite_note-22]

In 1984, Qatar narrowly lost to [Iraq](1039652.md) in the finals of the [1984 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup), nonetheless they were named runners-up, one of their most impressive achievements until their [1992 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup).[^cite_note-23]

They failed to make it out of the preliminary stages of the [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md) qualifying rounds. However, the team qualified for both the [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_AFC_Asian_Cup) and [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_AFC_Asian_Cup) editions of the Asian Cup. They fell short of qualifying for the semi-finals of the [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_AFC_Asian_Cup) tournament, with [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team)'s [Mohaisen Al-Jam'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohaisen_Al-Jam'an)'s 88th-minute goal against [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team), ensuring a semi-final position for both teams. They also missed out on a semi-final place in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_AFC_Asian_Cup), however, they notably defeated [Japan](993546.md) by a score of 3–0.[^cite_note-24]

### Golden era: 1990s–2000s

Qatar arguably reached its peak in the 1990s, attaining a [FIFA](11049.md) rating of (53) in August 1993.[^cite_note-25] Qatar started off with an emphatic qualifying campaign for the [1990 FIFA World Cup](157232.md), finishing at the top of their group. However they couldn't advance in the tournament, due to finishing below the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) and [South Korea](1018627.md) in the final round of the qualifiers.

In the [1990 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup), the national team once again finished runners-up as [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team) won the final two matches of the tournament.[^cite_note-26]

In 1992, they won the [1992 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup) on home soil for the first time under the leadership of Sebastião Lapola, despite a 1–0 loss against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) in their final game.[^cite_note-27] [Mubarak Mustafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Mustafa), won both the top scorer and man of the tournament awards.[^cite_note-28] They were also named runners-up in the [1996 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup).

Qatar reached the Asian Zone's final qualifying round for the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). After wins against China and Iran, they played their last match against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), where a victory would have earned qualification. However, they lost out as Saudi Arabia won 1–0 to reach the finals.

As [1998 Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Arab_Cup) hosts, they finished runners-up to Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-29]

### 2000–2010

They made it to the quarter-finals of the [2000 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup) despite finishing 3rd in their group, but lost to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team) in their quarter-final confrontation.[^cite_note-30]

They reached the final qualifying round again in 2001 but were defeated by [Bora Milutinovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Milutinovic)'s China team, who topped the section to progress to their first FIFA World Cup. Frenchman [Philippe Troussier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Troussier) took the manager's job after the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea) and [Japan](15573.md), but was unsuccessful in both the [2004 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup) and the [qualifying campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Germany](11867.md).

Troussier was sacked after the World Cup qualifying campaign, and under Bosnian [Džemaludin Mušović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%BEemaludin_Mu%C5%A1ovi%C4%87), the team won the [Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Gulf_Cup) in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Arabian_Gulf_Cup) and the [under 23 teams'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_national_under-23_football_team) [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2006_Asian_Games) gold in 2006. Mušović stepped down after Qatar only earned two points from three matches in the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup).

The job of coaching the team in qualifying for the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) fell to Uruguayan head coach [Jorge Fossati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Fossati), who led the team throughout the first and second AFC rounds up to the third round. After leaving them at the top of their group with only two played matches, Fossati had to undergo stomach surgery. Subsequently, the Qatar Football Association ended their cooperation with him in September 2008, as the QFA claimed he needed too long to recover from surgery.[^cite_note-31] [Bruno Metsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Metsu) was called up for the job, but Qatar failed to qualify after finishing fourth in their qualifying group.

### 2010–2020



Qatar national team in 2011 during the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) qualifying rounds.

Qatar was announced as hosts of the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in December 2010.

In 2011, as hosts of the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup), they advanced to the quarter-finals. They succumbed to a late 2–3 defeat to eventual champions [Japan](993546.md) after a goal was scored by [Masahiko Inoha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahiko_Inoha) in the 89th minute.

Also, as hosts, they went on to win the [2013 WAFF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_WAFF_Championship) after defeating [Jordan](1090065.md) 2–0 in the final. The competition was made up primarily of youth and reserve teams, of which Qatar's was the latter.[^cite_note-32] [Djamel Belmadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamel_Belmadi), the head coach of the B team, replaced [Fahad Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahad_Thani) as the head coach of the senior team as a result of the team's positive performances. 10 months later, Djamel Belmadi led Qatar to gold in the [2014 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Arabian_Gulf_Cup). They advanced from the group stages after three draws, going on to defeat [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) 3–1 in the semi-final, and were victorious in the final against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), who was playing in front of a home crowd, by a margin of 2–1.[^cite_note-33]

Despite winning the Gulf Cup and finishing the year 2014 with only one defeat, Qatar showed poor form in the [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup). Qatar was defeated 1–4 by the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) in their opener. This was continued with a 0–1 loss to [Iran](272865.md) and 1–2 to [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team). Qatar was eliminated in the group stages with no points and placed 4th in Group C.

Qatar's campaign in [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in [Russia](25391.md) was a surprise. Their start in the [second round of FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round) in the [AFC](715249.md) was nearly perfect, with seven wins and only one loss. However, their success in the second round didn't follow them to the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round). Qatar finished bottom of their group, ensuring they would play their first World Cup match on home soil in 2022, the first team to do so since [Italy](362466.md) in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup).

Qatar continued its poor form in the [2017 Gulf Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Arabian_Gulf_Cup), which was hosted by [Kuwait](7515890.md). Qatar opened the tournament with a 4–0 win against [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_national_football_team), but that was followed by a 1–2 loss to [Iraq](1039652.md) and an unconvincing 1–1 draw to Bahrain. Qatar took third place in Group B with four points and was eliminated in the group stage of the competition, which was considered an upsetting of the tournament, especially after winning the 2014 edition.

Qatar starting line-up against [Japan](993546.md) at the [2019 AFC Asian Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final), a match they won 3–1.

However, Qatar had an excellent campaign at the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup). Their opener saw them defeat [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) 2–0. This was followed by a 6–0 thrashing of [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) and a 2–0 win against three-time champions Saudi Arabia, which sealed the team getting first place in the [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_E). They had a 1–0 win against Iraq in the [Round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Qatar_vs_Iraq) and a late win against defending runners-up [South Korea](1018627.md) in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#South_Korea_vs_Qatar), seeing them through to the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Qatar_vs_United_Arab_Emirates) for the first time ever, where they defeated the hosts United Arab Emirates 4–0 to set up a final against 4-time winners Japan. Qatar ended up winning the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_Final) 3–1 over Japan, marking their first-ever major tournament title in their history, and capping off one of the most improbable Asian Cup runs in the tournament's history, especially since they conceded only one goal in all their games.[^cite_note-34]

Qatar was invited to the [2019 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). They were placed in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Group_B) with [Colombia](680175.md), [Argentina](454699.md) and [Paraguay](804025.md). Their first game was against Paraguay where they came back from a 2–0 deficit to tie it 2–2, but this marked the first time Qatar suffered more than one goal in any major competition since winning the Asian Cup in the UAE.[^cite_note-35] It was followed by a 0–1 loss to Colombia, ending the team's undefeated streak in any major competition to eight.[^cite_note-36] A 0–2 loss to Argentina meant Qatar took the last place in Group B with a single point and was eliminated in the group stage of the competition.[^cite_note-37]

### Preparation for the FIFA World Cup as host: 2020–present

Despite Qatar having automatic qualification for the 2022 World Cup as hosts, they had to play the AFC qualifiers in the second round as part of it also acting as qualification for the [2023 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup) and won 7 of 8 games, conceding only one goal. Qatar was invited for the first time to the [2021 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). They played in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) with [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team), [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team) and [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada_national_football_team).[^cite_note-38] In December 2020, [UEFA](32332.md) invited Qatar to play [friendlies](977090.md) against the teams in [Group A of the 2022 World Cup qualifying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_A) – [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_national_football_team), [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team), [Portugal](576328.md), the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) and [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team) – as five teams in one group means one team will not be playing on any given match day. These friendlies did not count in the qualifying group standings. Qatar played their "home" matches in Europe in order to allow short travel times for their opponents.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]



Qatar opening match starting line-up against Ecuador at the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md).&#x20;

In the [2021 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), Qatar claimed 7 points in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). Their debut was against [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team) with a 3–3 draw, ensuring them their first point. This was followed by a 4–0 victory over [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada_national_football_team) and a 2–0 win over [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) ensuring a quarter-final place where they would face [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team), ultimately securing a semi-final place against the [United States](582488.md) with a 3–2 win. However, against the hosts with its squad made up of the majority of [MLS](71802.md) players, Qatar failed to find the way to the net, in spite of having a penalty in the 60th minute, ultimately conceding a late goal from [Gyasi Zardes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyasi_Zardes) to end Qatar's campaign with a 1–0 loss. In the [2021 FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Arab_Cup), Qatar won all of its group-stage matches and faced [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), winning 5–0. They lost 2–1 in the semi-final against [Algeria](1029528.md),[^cite_note-41] eventually placing 3rd.[^cite_note-42]

Despite the very successful performance of Qatar in various major tournaments, the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) held at home proved to be a nightmare for the Qataris. Being automatically drawn into the [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_A) as hosts, Qatar started with a 0–2 loss to [Ecuador](1016797.md) in the opening match, conceding two goals in the first half from [Enner Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enner_Valencia), making Qatar the first host country to lose their opening game.[^cite_note-43] The situation did not improve for the Qataris in their second meeting with Senegal, when the Africans beat the Qataris 3–1 to condemn the hosts to an early World Cup exit, becoming the second host country after [South Africa](1018598.md), the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) hosts, to have such an unwanted record (although they were eliminated only on inferior goal differences to [Mexico](808402.md) after three matches); they became the first host team to be eliminated after two group games and guaranteed to be the worst-host ever, although Ghanaian-born [Mohammed Muntari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Muntari) became Qatar's first World Cup scorer in this game.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] With Qatar's elimination confirmed, the hosts then fell to the Netherlands 0–2, making an unwanted record as the first hosts to score the fewest goals (1), the first to lose all three group games (all previous hosts had won a match), and the lowest-ranked team in the tournament (ranked 32nd).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

14 months later, they would redeem themselves at the [2023 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup), also held at home. The Maroons defeated [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) 3–0 to start off their campaign, and they entered the round of 16 as group winners following 2 other successes, all without conceding a goal. They had to overcome [Palestine](1302455.md) (2–1) and then [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) in the next round (1–1 a.e.t., 3 p.s.o. to 2) to reach the last four, but without convincing. After defeating [Iran](14653.md) narrowly in a 3–2 dramatic semifinal match, they would face off against [Jordan](7515964.md) in the final, where a penalty hat-trick by [Akram Afif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_Afif) helped Qatar defeat [Jordan](1090065.md) 3–1 to clinch their second successive [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup), becoming the first team since [Japan](993546.md) to successfully defend their title.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

On 14 October 2025, Qatar secured qualification for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after defeating the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) 2–1 at the [Jassim bin Hamad Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jassim_bin_Hamad_Stadium) in [Doha](26214389.md), [Qatar](8238258.md). The result marked their first-ever qualification for a World Cup through the standard AFC qualification process, having previously participated in [2022](17742072.md) as hosts.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Naturalised players

While it is reasonably common for footballers to represent national teams other than their birth nations,[^cite_note-60] the nature and extent of the practice for the Qatari team have been the subject of scrutiny and criticism at various points during the 21st century. In the early 1970s, shortly after the formation of the national team, the [QFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Football_Association) revealed that it had naturalized its first players in a February 1976 telegram sent to FIFA: Ezzuldin Osman of Sudan, Hassan Mukhtar of Egypt and Ryad Murad of Lebanon. This practice of naturalizing Arabic-speaking foreigners to compete for the national team continued throughout the 20th century, albeit at a relatively slow rate.[^cite_note-61] In 2004, FIFA cited the intention of three Brazilian players – [Aílton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%ADlton_\(footballer,_born_1973\)), [Dedé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ded%C3%A9_\(footballer,_born_1978\)) and [Leandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_de_Deus_Santos) – to play for the Qatar national team as the immediate trigger to their decision to tighten eligibility rules to ensure players have ties to the country they represent.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

While Qatari authorities described it as a humanitarian effort and a way to provide competition for native Qatari players, critics claimed that it was merely another exploitative way of acquiring naturalized players,[^cite_note-64] with *[Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_\(magazine\))* linking it to human rights abuses and the [kafala system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafala_system).[^cite_note-65] The [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization) (ILO) and Qatar announced the removal of the Kafala on 12 December 2016; the law came into effect in 2018.[^cite_note-66] The reform took place between UN's International labor organization and the state of Qatar and was proven to be the part of many said assurances that nation has claimed for the 2022 World Cup. Though claimed by independent bodies of Qatar foundering to achieve the same, both sides had agreed to revise previous acts that had been taken. To make this all possible, the hosting country declared to pay compensation for the deaths of its migrant workers on 12 August 2022.[^cite_note-67]

Job changes between September 2020 and March 2022, the establishment of a nondiscriminatory wage system for all workers in March 2021, and workers' funds and insurance policies in the workplace are all data that showcased the functioning of the state for its workers.[^cite_note-68]

In the 2015 friendly against [Algeria](1029528.md), six of the eleven players in the starting team were born outside of Qatar.[^cite_note-69] Then-president of FIFA, [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter), warned Qatar that FIFA would monitor their player selection to ensure that they were not relying too heavily on naturalised players. He made comparisons to the [Qatar men's national handball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_men's_national_handball_team), referring to the team's selection for the [2015 World Men's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_World_Men's_Handball_Championship) as an "absurdity".[^cite_note-70] The following year, naturalized players formed the backbone of the team and were sufficiently integral that head coach [Jorge Fossati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Fossati) threatened to resign if they were removed.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

The reliance on naturalised players has subsequently reduced, with only two members of the squad that beat [Switzerland](681862.md) in a 2018 friendly being born outside Qatar.[^cite_note-73] However, at the [2019 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup), amidst [diplomatic tensions between the two countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar%E2%80%93United_Arab_Emirates_relations), the [United Arab Emirates Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Football_Association) lodged a formal complaint against Qatar, alleging that [Almoez Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoez_Ali) and [Bassam Al-Rawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassam_Al-Rawi) were not eligible to play for them.[^cite_note-74] These complaints were dismissed by the AFC.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

Of the 26 players called up to the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), 10 players were born outside of Qatar.[^cite_note-\:0-77]
