Since March 2025, [protests](266137.md) against [immigration](1247786.md) have taken place in parts of the [United Kingdom](31717.md), some of which have led to [violent disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_disorder). At least 41 police officers have been injured and over 180 people have been arrested. Some have been focused around hotels being used by the [government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md) to provide accommodation for [asylum seekers](276005.md), while others are against immigration more broadly. Many were organised or attended by [far-right](221220.md) groups including [Reform UK](59757202.md), [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First), [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP), [Homeland Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Party_\(United_Kingdom\)), and [Patriotic Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alternative), who have also been accused of spreading misinformation online.

With some protests also occurring in the first half of the year, the volume of hotel protests began to increase in the aftermath of a series starting in [Epping](26609107.md) on 13 July, which repeatedly ended in disorder. Protests occurred across the country, with disorder in cities including [Bristol](36741.md), [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), [Liverpool](18081.md), [Manchester](20206.md) and [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne); the largest protest came on 13 September when up to 150,000 people took part in a [London](17867.md) march organised by far-right activist [Tommy Robinson](32537879.md), where 26 police officers were injured.

These protests are part of a series which have previously led to riots, most notably [in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_riots) when over 1,800 people were arrested and over 300 police officers injured. Earlier in the year, [a series of riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Northern_Ireland_riots) took place in Northern Ireland which saw over 100 police officers being injured.&#x20;

## Background

See also: [Modern immigration to the United Kingdom](1247786.md)

### Use of hotels to house asylum seekers

The [government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md) is legally obliged to provide accommodation for [asylum seekers](276005.md) who would otherwise be homeless until their applications have been considered. Prior to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), hotels were only for temporary "contingency" use, with the government instead paying private contractors to rent apartments or houses. During the [lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdown_in_the_United_Kingdom), the eviction of asylum seekers whose cases had been decided on was paused to prevent them from becoming homeless and they were moved into empty hotels in order to comply with [social distancing measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_distancing_measures_related_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic); this decision was described as a "temporary measure" by the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) government under [Boris Johnson](19065069.md).[^cite_note-7]

However, what began as a temporary measure has become a long-term arrangement, with many of these facilities effectively ceasing to operate as commercial hotels. Hotels were often closed to other guests at short notice, with reports of weddings and other events being cancelled.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The buildings are no longer open to paying guests and instead function as institutional accommodation centres, typically housing hundreds of asylum seekers on long-term contracts with the Home Office. At the height of hotel use under the Conservative government, in June 2023, 51,000 asylum seekers were housed in more than 400 hotels across the UK, costing the Home Office £8 million a day.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-auto1-14] Recent actions to add bedspaces include maximising capacity by requiring asylum seekers to share hotel rooms, with limited access to cooking facilities.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-auto1-14] Many asylum seekers remain in these facilities for months or years whilst their claims are processed, far exceeding the original "temporary" designation.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] In the year to June 2025, a record 111,084 people applied for asylum in the UK, with fewer than a third of applicants being housed in temporary hotel accommodation.[^cite_note-19]

Living conditions in these facilities have been subject to extensive criticism from [refugee](45547.md) advocacy groups and parliamentary committees, with reports describing substandard and overcrowded accommodation, malnutrition due to inadequate provisions, and rats all commonplace.[^cite_note-20] Inspection reports have documented hotels with poor record keeping, inadequate security, and facilities lacking basic amenities such as cooking areas and outside space for children.[^cite_note-21] Residents typically receive £49.18 per week to cover all living costs outside of accommodation, significantly below the standard [Universal Credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Credit) allowance, and are prohibited from working whilst their claims are pending.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The concentration of large numbers of asylum seekers in these former hotels has led to tensions in some local communities, with local residents rarely being informed of the use of a hotel in advance, creating what has been described as a "damaging sense of community powerlessness".[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Earlier anti-immigration protests

In recent years, [anti-immigration sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-immigration_sentiment) has grown in the United Kingdom. A report published by [Runnymede Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runnymede_Trust) in 2025 proposed that this was encouraged by negative framing in the media and political discourse, with migrants consistently being portrayed in terms of crime and illegality during 2010 to 2014. The word 'illegal' was found to be one of the top five words associated with migrants in news reports and parliamentary debates in that time period, with the association becoming even stronger between 2019 to 2024.[^cite_note-29] Experts and campaigners have suggested [far-right](221220.md) groups have been using this to stoke tensions.[^cite_note-Big_Issue-30] This reached a peak most notably when in 2024 [widespread riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_riots) led to over 1,800 arrests, with over 350 police officers being injured.[^cite_note-31]

The spate of riots in 2024 followed [a mass stabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Southport_stabbing) in which three children were killed and ten others injured. Soon after the attack, [misinformation](2203174.md) was spread online which incorrectly claimed that the attacker was an [asylum seeker](276005.md) and a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). These claims were spread by number of high-profile accounts on social media including [Tommy Robinson](32537879.md), whilst a number of [far-right](221220.md) activists called for nationwide protests.[^cite_note-32] Along with pre-existing [Islamophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamophobic) and [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racist) attitudes, disorder spread across the country and led to attacks against Asian, Black and Muslim people as well as [mosques](19894.md), shops and community buildings such as libraries.[^cite_note-33]

More recently, [a series of riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Northern_Ireland_riots) in [Northern Ireland](21265.md) in June 2025 led to over 50 arrests, with more than 100 police officers being injured.[^cite_note-34] They were triggered by the alleged sexual assault of a girl in [Ballymena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballymena) by two teenagers who appeared in court with a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language) interpreter; a month later, it was estimated that two-thirds of the [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_people) residents in the town had left.[^cite_note-35]

Other more localised protests have occurred outside asylum hotels since 2021; however, they never spread nationwide.[^cite_note-36] A few of these escalated into riots, including one in [Knowsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowsley,_Merseyside) on 10 February 2023 when a police van was set on fire and missiles were thrown at officers, leaving three of them injured.[^cite_note-37]

The 2025 protests marked a significant escalation in [far-right](3884621.md) mobilisation around asylum accommodation, with established extremist groups seizing upon local concerns to advance broader anti-immigration agendas. While initial protests in locations such as Epping began with local residents, they were rapidly co-opted by organised far-right activists.[^cite_note-Big_Issue-30] The "Epping Says No" Facebook group, which organised demonstrations at the Bell Hotel, was administered by three members of the [Homeland Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Party_\(United_Kingdom\)), a splinter group from the [neo-Nazi](54361.md) organisation [Patriotic Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alternative).[^cite_note-38]

Multiple far-right groups were documented as being present at the protests, including members of [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First), the Homeland Party, [Patriotic Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alternative), and former [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP) activists.[^cite_note-39] These groups were accused of spreading [misinformation](2203174.md) online to inflame tensions and recruit new supporters.[^cite_note-Nov130925-40] The involvement of [Reform UK](59757202.md) provided a more mainstream political vehicle for anti-immigration sentiment, with the party's MPs such as [Lee Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Anderson) posting videos outside asylum hotels saying he was "absolutely furious" about their use for asylum seekers.[^cite_note-Nov130925-40]

Some of the protests have been specifically organised by [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP) under their *"Mass Deportations Tour"*, which has seen them march or rally in cities such as Glasgow, Nottingham, Liverpool, Newcastle, and London.[^cite_note-41]

The protests have seen an increase in far-right efforts to use fears of violence against women to lend their views respectability.[^cite_note-independent-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Multiple experts consulted by [The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent) - including Andrea Simon, director of the [End Violence Against Women Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_Violence_Against_Women_Coalition); Lois Shearing, author of *Pink Pilled*; and a researcher for [Hope not Hate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_not_Hate) - said that the far-right was are exploiting fears to make the far-right seem more "palatable", and that this is to appeal to a broader audience and gain more of a foothold in mainstream opinion. According to Simon, "these narratives are promoted by those who exploit genuine public concerns about sexual violence to intentionally fuel racism in our communities."[^cite_note-independent-42] Another development is the formation of organised women's groups attending the protests, such as The Pink Ladies and Women's Safety Initiative, who both reject the label far-right. The Hope not Hate researcher said of them; “with mobilisation comes recruitment”. On this Shearing said “a broader contingency of women” is being appealed to.[^cite_note-independent-42]

### Role of misinformation

Social media platforms played a central role in coordinating and amplifying the 2025 asylum hotel protests.[^cite_note-45] The protests were organised through Facebook groups with names following the pattern "X Community Says No to Y Local Hotel", alongside coordination via [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp) and [Telegram](41501305.md).[^cite_note-46] Telegram served as a key coordination hub for far-right networks, with the [Institute for Strategic Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Strategic_Dialogue) documenting the platform's role as a "safe space for extremists to coordinate activity and instigate violence".[^cite_note-47]

The role of [Elon Musk](909036.md) and [X](9988187.md) in amplifying misinformation drew significant attention.[^cite_note-48] Since Musk's takeover in 2022, X reinstated previously banned accounts including that of [Tommy Robinson](32537879.md), who had been removed for hate speech violations.[^cite_note-49] During the 2024 riots, Musk posted 46 times generating 808 million impressions, including a tweet claiming "civil war is inevitable" in the UK.[^cite_note-50] Robinson's posts on X received over 580 million views in the two weeks following the Southport attack, an unprecedented reach for a previously banned figure.[^cite_note-51]

A police inspectorate review published in May 2025 found that "misinformation and disinformation that had been posted online had been left up for too long and that helped fuel the disorder."[^cite_note-52] The contemporaneous flag-raising campaign [Operation Raise the Colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Raise_the_Colours), coordinated by far-right activist Andrew Currien through social media platforms including Telegram and Facebook, was identified as connected to the broader network of anti-immigration activity.[^cite_note-53]

## Timeline of demonstrations

### Sporadic protests earlier in the year



UKIP rally in Portsmouth on 15 March with a "Mass Deportations Now" banner.

On 15 March, a rally was held in [Portsmouth](24207.md) by [British nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nationalism) political party [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP) calling for mass-deportations of migrants. Party leader [Nick Tenconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Tenconi) was in attendance. A counter protest was led by Stand Up To Racism Portsmouth, at which the Leader of [Portsmouth City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_City_Council), Steve Pitt, spoke.[^cite_note-54]

On 13 April, a group called 'Fighting Justice Against Predators' held a protest about a hotel used to house asylum seekers in [Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisley,_Renfrewshire). They were opposed by a counter-protest from [Stand Up To Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_To_Racism).[^cite_note-55] According to [*The Herald*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Herald_\(Glasgow\)), the organisation focuses on immigration, has ties to the far-right [Patriotic Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alternative), and have a history of spreading unfounded allegations on Facebook. [Police Scotland](34423036.md) stated that criminality had not been established upon investigation of related reports.[^cite_note-56]

On 27 April, a protest against illegal immigration organised by motorcycle group 'Kent Motor Heads' was held in [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover). The bikers gathered in the town centre, where they were joined by more people on foot. The protesters carried [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack) flags and signs with the phrase "Stop the boats". The group passed through the town and into the Market Square, where they were met by a group of counter-protesters including [Stand Up To Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_To_Racism) and members of Medway Trades Union Council. [Paul Golding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Golding), leader of [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascist) party [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First), was in attendance. Kent Police said the event passed without any reported incidents of disorder or arrests.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

On 17 May 2025 supporters of [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascist) party [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First), including leader [Paul Golding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Golding), gathered from about midday in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) city centre. They marched to [Victoria Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Square,_Birmingham) where a stage and video screen had been set up, despite [Birmingham City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_Council) saying the protestors would not be welcome and would not be allowed on the square. City council leader, Cllr John Cotton said their access of the area would be investigated and called it "a serious breach of security". Police kept them apart from counter protesters. The *[Birmingham Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Mail)* reported Britain First's demands as "A ban on mosque construction", "The removal of 'non-indigenous' people – including those born here", and "The criminalisation of Islam in public life".[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

On the same day, 100 members of [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP) were in [Bristol](36741.md) city centre calling for "mass-deportations". They were opposed by around 250 counter-protesters who carried Antifascism Action banners. The police gave a statement saying "Overall, both groups were calm and engaged with officers, however we did see a handful of clashes between the two sides." UKIP gathered initially by the [Colston Statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Edward_Colston) plinth, whilst the counter-protesters gathered at [Bristol Cenotaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Cenotaph).[^cite_note-61] UKIP leader [Nick Tenconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Tenconi) was present. The protests came to an end at around 3.30pm, with police transporting the remaining UKIP supporters away from the area in their riot vans as they were surrounded on both sides by counter-protesters.[^cite_note-62]

### Epping protests (July–September)



The Bell Hotel in 2019

On 7 and 8 July, a 41-year-old [Ethiopian](187749.md) asylum seeker was accused of three sexual assaults in [Epping](26609107.md), only eight days after arriving in the UK by boat on 29 June. He was arrested and [remanded](14632360.md) in custody after appearing at [magistrates' court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrates'_court_\(England_and_Wales\)) in [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester) on 10 July, where he denied three charges of sexual assault and charges of inciting a girl to engage in sexual activity and harassment without violence.[^cite_note-63] He appeared in court again on 17 July for a case management hearing, where he was told he would face a two-day trial from 26 August.[^cite_note-64] On 4 September 2025, he was found guilty of two counts of sexual assault, attempted sexual assault, attempt to cause/incite a girl 13 to 15 to engage in sexual activity, and harassment.[^cite_note-65] The man, whose name is Hadush Kebatu, was sentenced to 12 months in prison on 23 September; as such, a deportation order must be made under the [UK Borders Act 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Borders_Act_2007).[^cite_note-66] Kebatu was erroneously released on 24 October instead of being sent to an immigration detention centre as part of the deportation process.[^cite_note-67] Knowing he was supposed to be deported, he returned to the prison reception "four or five times", waiting outside the prison for roughly "an hour and a half" before leaving. He was re-arrested two days later and deported to Ethiopia the following week.[^cite_note-68]

The first protest about asylum seekers took place outside the Bell Hotel in Epping on 13 July; the hotel was being used to house asylum seekers by the [Home Office](225695.md), who had faced calls from [Neil Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hudson_\(politician\)), MP for [Epping Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Alex Burghart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Burghart), MP for [Brentwood and Ongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood_and_Ongar_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and Christopher Whitbread, leader of [Epping Forest District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_District_Council), to close the hotel. During the protest, two security personnel at the hotel were left with serious injuries after being attacked by a group of men.[^cite_note-69]

A second protest took place against the hotel for asylum seekers took place in Epping on 17 July, attracting hundreds of demonstrators who were mostly local residents, although they were joined by some far-right activists, some of whom donned [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First) T-shirts. A separate counter-protest which included demonstrators from [Stand Up To Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_To_Racism) (SUTR) took place outside [Epping tube station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_tube_station); however, they later converged with the initial protest at the hotel which had been peaceful until that point. Disorder then ensued as clashes occurred with the counter-protesters, with riot police surrounding the small group of them as bottles, eggs and flour were thrown at them; the town's [High Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Street) was blocked off for many hours as police were directly attacked, with many of their vehicles being vandalised.[^cite_note-70] The hotel itself was also damaged and the [assistant chief constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_chief_constable) of [Essex Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_Police) said eight officers had suffered minor injuries, whilst two people had been arrested following the protest.[^cite_note-71] A third person was arrested in connection to the disorder on 19 July and was charged with violent disorder and criminal damage.[^cite_note-72] [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) called for the chief constable to resign after the police force was accused of "bussing" counter-protesters to the hotel; a police spokesman said the claims were "categorically wrong" but said officers had provided a foot cordon around protesters on their way to the protest.[^cite_note-73]

The next anti-immigration protest took place at the same location on 20 July and was attended by over 1,000 people. Disorder also took place during this protest, with six people being arrested after what was described by the [chief superintendent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_superintendent) of Essex Police as "mindless thuggery" . Bottles and smoke flares were thrown at police vehicles and officers from [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Constabulary) and [Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_Constabulary) were drafted in; one officer from the former was hospitalised after being struck in the face by a bottle.[^cite_note-74] Essex Police later said the cost of policing the protests on 17 and 20 July had exceeded £100,000.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

On 24 July, a fourth protest took place at the Bell Hotel in Epping, with another taking place simultaneously at [Epping Civic Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Civic_Offices), where a meeting regarding the hotel was taking place. Unlike the two previous protests, there was no disorder and only one person was arrested for refusing to remove a face covering, which police said was the 17th arrest in total. The council meeting concluded with a unanimous vote to urge the government to close the hotel.[^cite_note-77]

On 27 July, another asylum hotel protest took place with a large number of counter-protesters attending. There were between 300 and 400 anti-migrant protesters, however they were outnumbered by an estimated 2,000 counter-protesters who made their way from the train station to the hotel. Essex Police described the protest as peaceful but added that three people were arrested; officers from the [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire_Constabulary), [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Constabulary), [Dyfed-Powys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyfed-Powys_Police), [Hampshire](3509504.md), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Police), [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire_Police), [Metropolitan](192450.md), [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire_Police), [Surrey](3006917.md), [Sussex](3509799.md) and [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Police) forces were at the scene, with the [British Transport Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Transport_Police) also assisting.[^cite_note-78] In [Altrincham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altrincham), protesters and counter-protesters faced off on either side of the [A56 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A56_road) outside a hotel with a small line of police between them.[^cite_note-79]

On 31 July, another protest took place outside the Bell Hotel and passed peacefully with a march facilitated by police. Essex Police said that a total of 23 people had been arrested in connection with the earlier protests, 14 of whom had been charged.[^cite_note-80]

On 3 August, a further protest passed peacefully, with police arresting and charging two more people in connection to the disorder on 17 July, bringing the total to 25 arrested and 16 charged.[^cite_note-81] On 8 August, police arrested two people at another protest, one of whom was arrested after an officer was struck by an object; despite this, police said the protest passed peacefully. These arrests brought the total in Epping to 27.[^cite_note-82] On 10 August, another protest attracted over 150 protesters.[^cite_note-83]

On 17 August shadow Justice Secretary [Robert Jenrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jenrick) posted photos on [X](9988187.md) of himself attending that day's protest outside the hotel. Far-right activist [Eddy Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddy_Butler), who was a key strategist in the rise of the [British National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_National_Party), can be seen wearing sunglasses in the background of one of the photos, which he then shared to his own social media. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* referred to this posting as being "seized on by far-right figures eager to exploit the protest".[^cite_note-84]

On 29 August, following the overturning of a temporary injunction which would have blocked asylum seekers being housed there, disorder broke out at the Bell Hotel after several weeks of peaceful protests. Two police officers were injured and three men were arrested: one for assaulting a police officer, one for drink-driving, and the other for violent disorder.[^cite_note-85]

On 31 August, around 200 demonstrators gathered outside the council offices in Epping, where three people were arrested after protesters clashed with police.[^cite_note-86]

On 5 September, further disorder broke out at a protest as flares were lit and one person was arrested, bringing the total number of arrests in the town to 32.[^cite_note-87] Police later said the antisocial behaviour was not carried out by legitimate protesters and added that a resident of the hotel had been assaulted.[^cite_note-88]

On 21 September, police put in place a dispersal order during a protest. This was intended to prevent the public from being harassed, alarmed or distressed, as well as to prevent disorder or crime in the area, while still allowing peaceful protest.[^cite_note-89]

On 23 September, two further men were arrested at a protest outside the hotel. A 48-year-old was arrested on suspicion of breaching his bail conditions, and a 17-year-old was arrested on suspicion of failing to abide by the restrictions placed upon the protest.[^cite_note-90]

### Protests elsewhere (July–November)

#### July

On 5 July, an anti-migrant protest held on South Parade in [Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea), Portsmouth was met by counter-protesters. Videos showed the groups fighting and facing off with each other. The police issued a dispersal order, with three arrests made and one officer was assaulted.[^cite_note-91]

On 19 July, a right-wing protest took place in [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover), with participants chanting "stop the boats" as they made their way to the seafront. Some [Reform UK](59757202.md) councillors were in attendance. A small number of people supporting the far-right group [Patriotic Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alternative) were also at the protest. One man, who self-identified to [KentOnline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KentOnline) as far-right, had a flag bearing the group's logo, and wore a hat with the word "[Remigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remigration)" on.[^cite_note-92] An anti-immigration protest took place in [Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_upon_Hull) alongside a small counter-protest; three number-shaped balloons were released representing the ages of the three girls killed in the Southport stabbing.[^cite_note-93]

On 21 July, protesters gathered outside a hotel housing asylum-seeking families in [Diss, Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diss,_Norfolk), which the Home Office planned to change to house males. Initially, there were 60 protesters and 30 counter-protesters on opposing sides of a road, however after a confrontation the counter-protesters moved elsewhere.[^cite_note-94] The number of protesters grew to around 150 and two people were arrested in connection to the protest later in the week.[^cite_note-95]

On 22 July, a protest took place outside a four-star hotel in [Canary Wharf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Wharf), which [Tower Hamlets Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_Hamlets_Council) said was going to be used as temporary accommodation for asylum seekers. [Apsana Begum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsana_Begum), MP for [Poplar and Limehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poplar_and_Limehouse_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), said "refugees are welcome here", whilst [Lee Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Anderson), the [Chief Whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Whip) of [Reform UK](59757202.md), expressed his disapproval online and said he was "absolutely furious" in a video taken outside the hotel.[^cite_note-96] A second protest took place the following day, where anti-migrant protesters and counter-protesters shouted at each other from across the street as police guarded the hotel.[^cite_note-97]

On 25 July, one person was arrested and charged in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) for obstructing a police officer during a peaceful protest at a hotel in [Seacroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seacroft).[^cite_note-98] An estimated 300 anti-immigration protesters gathered in [Sutton-in-Ashfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton-in-Ashfield) following the arrest of a man for the rape of a woman. Lee Anderson, whose constituency, [Ashfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashfield_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), was where the rape took place, claimed online that the suspect was an asylum seeker. The protesters gathered in the town centre, with a much smaller number of counter-protesters gathering at a nearby church.[^cite_note-99] Protests also took place in [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth) and [Southampton](7920751.md), with one in [Portsmouth](24207.md) seeing anti-migrant protesters face off with counter-protesters outside a hotel.[^cite_note-100]

On 26 July, around 400 anti-immigration protesters gathered outside a hotel housing asylum seekers in [Bowthorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowthorpe), with around 150 counter-protesters also in attendance. Around 60 police officers were also at the scene, however the protest passed without incident and the force said no arrests were made.[^cite_note-101] A "mass [deportation](412990.md)" protest in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) led by [Nick Tenconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Tenconi), the leader of [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP), saw around 150 supporters march through the city as hundreds of anti-racists staged a counter-demonstration organised by SUTR.[^cite_note-102]

On 29 July, a peaceful protest took place in [Hoylake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoylake) after it was reported that the home officed planned to change the residents of a hotel from asylum-seeking families to single males. [Wirral Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirral_Council) said they "strongly opposed" the plans and residents described their frustration over the lack of communication with the local community.[^cite_note-103]

On 30 July, over 1,000 anti-immigration protesters gathered in [Waterlooville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterlooville) in opposition to plans to house asylum seekers in flats above shops. The protesters were supported by [Suella Braverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suella_Braverman), MP for [Fareham and Waterlooville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareham_and_Waterlooville_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and former [Home Secretary](149104.md), who said the plans were "utterly inappropriate" and that she was "very proud" of protesters.[^cite_note-104] The plans were dropped on 8 August following the protest and a petition by Braverman which received over 10,000 signatures.[^cite_note-105]

A man was arrested during a third day of protests in Hoylake for supporting a proscribed terrorist organisation, although police said the offence took place outside the area.[^cite_note-106] A protest also took place at a hotel in [Stanwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanwell) after the Home Office revealed plans to exclusively house male asylum seekers there rather than the families which were currently being housed there. Over 100 protesters were in attendance and there were reports of anti-social behaviour and criminal damage; the following day it was announced that the plans had been paused.[^cite_note-107]

#### August



Protest in the city of Aberdeen in Scotland on 9 August

On 1 August, a 64-year-old counter-protester was hospitalised after being hit by a placard during a protest at [The Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meadows,_Edinburgh) in [Edinburgh](9602.md).[^cite_note-108] A protest took place at a hotel housing asylum seekers in Portsmouth following an alleged incident with one of its residents; it was attended by both anti-migrant and anti-racism demonstrators.[^cite_note-109] Further protests also took place in Bournemouth and Southampton.[^cite_note-110]

On 2 August, a total of 15 people were arrested in [London](17867.md), [Manchester](20206.md) and [Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne) following clashes amid protests at hotels housing asylum seekers. In London, protesters gathered at the Thistle City Barbican Hotel in [Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islington), with counter-protesters from SUTR also in attendance; police said nine people were arrested. In Newcastle, protesters and counter-protesters gathered at a hotel, with [Northumbria Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria_Police) saying four people were arrested there. In Manchester, around 1,500 people took place in a march organised by [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First), with around 250 counter-protesters from SUTR; two people were arrested at the start of the march, which tracked from [Manchester Piccadilly station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Piccadilly_station) to [Manchester Central Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Central_Library).[^cite_note-111] A demonstration outside accommodation for asylum seekers in [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen) led to two men being arrested and charged under the [Hate Crime Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_Crime_and_Public_Order_\(Scotland\)_Act_2021), one of whom was also charged for an assault.[^cite_note-112] A further five men in attendance were later also charged under the Hate Crime Act.[^cite_note-113] A group of anti-migrant protesters gathered outside a hotel in [Cannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock), with counter-protesters also in attendance.[^cite_note-114] Around 200 people gathered for a protest in [Newtownabbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtownabbey).[^cite_note-115]

On 3 August another protest took place at the Britannia International Hotel in Canary Wharf and flares were set off, with protesters being accused of trying to break into the hotel as they attempted to breach the fencing; one man was arrested on suspicion of assaulting an emergency worker.[^cite_note-116] [Merseyside Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside_Police) said they had arrested a further three people for public order offences following protests in Hoylake earlier that week.[^cite_note-117]

On 4 August, around 250 people gathered at the Civic Centre in [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield) before marching to the police station following claims that asylum seekers were being housed there, with organisers accusing local police of failing to protect the public.[^cite_note-118] A small group of around 20 protesters blocked the entrance to a hotel in [Watton, Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watton,_Norfolk) after its renovation sparked rumours that it was to be repurposed by the Home Office to house asylum seekers.[^cite_note-119]

On 7 August, protestors gathered outside the offices of [Spelthorne Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelthorne_Borough_Council) as they discussed the Home Office's plans for the hotel in Stanwell; the discussion resulted in the council urging the government to reconsider its plans.[^cite_note-120]

'British Unity' describe themselves as: "a movement dedicated to the preservation and advancement of the United Kingdom's values, culture, and heritage."[^cite_note-242] In Canterbury a second anti-immigration demonstration outside Connors House in as many month was held. A counter-protest was also present, organised by groups including SUTR. Cllr Paul Prentice, who represents Barton ward, attended the counter.[^cite_note-243]



Flares set off at a protest in Southampton on 26 October.

On 26 October two marches took place in [Southampton](7920751.md), with police blocking off part of Portswood Road to keep them apart. Several hundred anti-immigration protesters, led by [Tommy Robinson](32537879.md) and waving union and St George's flags, marched through the city. Police used powers under the Public Order Act to restrict their route to being from Highfield Lane to [Hoglands Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_Parks#Hoglands_Park) in the city centre.[^cite_note-BBC261025-244][^cite_note-245] An estimated 600 anti-racism counter-protesters assembled to oppose them. A second flashpoint at Thomas Lewis Way and Lodge Road saw police holding back the anti-immigration protesters, with Thomas Lewis Way also being closed for a time while protesters passed. One arrest was made on suspicion of throwing a bottle.[^cite_note-BBC261025-244] In [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee) another anti-immigration protest occurred at Alloway Place, again met with counter-protesters. One arrest made.[^cite_note-246]

In [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport), an estimated 100 to 200 took part in a protest dubbed the 'Southport Freedom March' took place, beginning at Marine Drive. A scuffle broke out between counter-protestors and the police, as the march made its way past counter demonstrators positioned outside Southport Funland. The protesters had lots of union flags, with one flag bearing the slogan Unite the Kingdom and another sign showed support for the fascist political party [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First). Counter-protesters chanted "Nazi scum, off our streets".[^cite_note-247] Both groups were surrounded by the police outside the Scarisbrick Hotel.[^cite_note-248] One arrest was made.[^cite_note-249]

On 30 October anti-immigration protesters met at the clock tower in [Herne Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herne_Bay) to "back British fisherman", "stop the boats" and "say no to [digital ID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Digital_ID)". Their presence attracted a large group of counter-protesters, chanting "asylum is a human right" and "refugees are welcome here". A line of about 50 police officers separated the two groups.[^cite_note-250]

#### November

On 8 November a [UKIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKIP) anti-immigration march in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield) city centre was countered by a much larger group of anti-fascist organisations, with both groups kept apart by police. UKIP started in [Tudor Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Square) while the anti-fascists set off from [Sheffield Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Cathedral). Seven arrests were made.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252]

On 14 November around 50 anti-immigration protesters met outside Highfield House Hotel in [Portswood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portswood), with Southampton SUTR holding a counter-protest of around 100 people.[^cite_note-253]

On 15 November an anti-immigration demonstration of about 75 people, of which UKIP leader Nick Tenconi was a member, was held outside the [Mercure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercure_\(hotel\)) hotel in [Redcliffe, Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcliffe,_Bristol), by a group called 'Bristol Patriots'. This was met by a larger pro-immigration counter-protest of about 400 people, containing organisations such as the Bristol chapters of [Palestine Solidarity Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Solidarity_Campaign) and SUTR. Police separated the two sides, with clashes in the early afternoon between an [anti-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascist) counter-protester group and the police who were pushing the group back up Redcliffe Hill when trying to meet the anti-migrant protesters. Five people were arrested, and an officer received treatment at hospital after sustaining injuries.[^cite_note-254]

On 21 November a further anti-immigration protest was held outside Highfield House Hotel in [Southampton](7920751.md), where a man was arrested after displaying a homophobic sign. Police said the arrest was made "on suspicion of several offences, including possessing a Class B controlled drug, failing to give a name or address, and using threatening or abusive words or behaviour likely to cause harassment, alarm, or distress."[^cite_note-255]

On 22 November an anti-immigration protest called 'British Unity Walk' of about 150 people marched in [Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter). About 800 people gathered for a march called 'Exeter is for Everyone' in response. The two groups took different routes to around the city centre, Police said the protests were peaceful overall and did not confirm if any arrests were made.[^cite_note-256]

### Unite the Kingdom rally (13 September)

On 13 September, between 110,000 and 150,000 people gathered in central London for a march organised by [Tommy Robinson](32537879.md) called the Unite the Kingdom rally; simultaneous counter-protests by [Stand Up To Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_To_Racism) and local [anti-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascist) groups attracted around 5,000 demonstrators.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259] Around 1,000 officers were deployed in the city, with a further 500 officers drafted from other forces, including [Devon and Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_and_Cornwall_Police), [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire_Police) and [Nottinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_Police). Police said 24 people were arrested following "wholly unacceptable" violence which left 26 police officers injured[^cite_note-260] (the Metropolitan Police later appealed for help in identifying a further 28 people suspected of public order offences and assaulting emergency workers at the protests).[^cite_note-261] The two groups clashed on [Whitehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehall) and [Trafalgar Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafalgar_Square), where lines of police and horses separated them. Police reported that the crowd was too large to fit into Whitehall, leading to confrontations with officers after some protesters tried to access the area from other routes; many officers were assaulted and had objects such as bottles and flares thrown at them, with one bottle striking a horse. The event was livestreamed by Robinson on [X](9988187.md) and at its peak had 2.9 million viewers.[^cite_note-262]

The recent [assassination of Charlie Kirk](81040139.md) was used to mobilise support for the protest after hundreds of people gathered for a vigil in the city the previous day.[^cite_note-263] During the march, Robinson spoke to crowds and stated the court had decided undocumented migrants' rights superseded those of locals.[^cite_note-264] [Elon Musk](909036.md) spoke to the crowd through a video call, where he said a "dissolution of parliament" is needed and said that "massive uncontrolled migration" was contributing to the "destruction of Britain"; he continued to tell the crowd "violence is coming" and that "you either fight back or you die".[^cite_note-265]

[Ben Habib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Habib), leader of [Advance UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advance_UK) (one of the event sponsors), led a speech.[^cite_note-266] European far-right politicians, including French [Reconquête](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconqu%C3%AAte) leader [Éric Zemmour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Zemmour), [Danish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_People's_Party) leader [Morten Messerschmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morten_Messerschmidt), [Alliance for the Union of Romanians](64666536.md) leader [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion), Polish [Law and Justice](520309.md) MEP [Dominik Tarczynski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Tarczynski), Belgian [Vlaams Belang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlaams_Belang) MP [Filip Dewinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Dewinter) and [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) MEP [Petr Bystron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Bystron) also spoke at the event.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] The New Zealander and Christian fundamentalist [Brian Tamaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Tamaki) also spoke at the rally, calling for all public expressions of non-Christian faiths to be banned.[^cite_note-270] Politicians including [Diane Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Abbott) and [John McDonnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McDonnell) attended the anti-racism march and also made speeches.[^cite_note-271] In response to the rally, described by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* as "the largest nationalist protest in decades", the Prime Minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) made a statement to the newspaper:

> People have a right to peaceful protest. It is core to our country's values ... But we will not stand for assaults on police officers doing their job or for people feeling intimidated on our streets because of their background or the colour of their skin ... Britain is a nation proudly built on tolerance, diversity and respect. Our flag represents our diverse country and we will never surrender it to those that use it as a symbol of violence, fear and division.[^cite_note-272]

The statement was interpreted by *The Guardian* as a welcome change in tone on equality and anti-racism. That it would ease the sense of "crisis" among many Labour MPs at the government's response to the "rapid rise in far-right rhetoric and violence" and the "perma-presence in the media" of Nigel Farage. And it would signal an intent to "take on Farage and the hard right in the language of Labour values".[^cite_note-273]

## Concurrent events

### Flags raised on streets

See also: [Operation Raise the Colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Raise_the_Colours)



Flags hung on a block of flats in [Seacroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seacroft), Leeds



A Saint George's cross painted over a mini-roundabout in [Market Deeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Deeping)

In mid-July, [Union Jacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack) and [England flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_England) began to be put up in [Weoley Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weoley_Castle), a suburb of Birmingham, apparently by a group dubbed the "Weoley Warriors" who did so in response to a 12-year-old girl in nearby [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_Warwickshire) being stopped from making a speech at school about being British because she was wearing a Union Jack-themed dress. Initially the appearance of flags was confined to areas of Birmingham, but by mid-August this phenomenon had spread across the country, with thousands of flags being attached to lampposts; there have also been some cases of England flags being painted on [mini-roundabouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini-roundabout).[^cite_note-Flags-274]

Much of this has been assumed to be the work of "[Operation Raise the Colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Raise_the_Colours)", a group that has encouraged people to put up flags and is alleged to have accepted a donation from the [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) organisation [Britain First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain_First). According to the research group [Hope Not Hate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Not_Hate), it was co-founded by Andrew Currien, otherwise known as Andy Saxon, who has alleged links to the [far-right](221220.md) groups [English Defence League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Defence_League) and Britain First.[^cite_note-Flags-274] Lewis Nielsen of the research group has said it "was never about flags, it's about giving confidence to racists and fascists to target refugees and migrants."[^cite_note-GuardDanger-275]

### Threats to lawyers and refugee NGOs

More than 150 lawyers, human rights, refugee and environmental organisations have signed a statement saying they are being "pressured into silence" after some received rape and death threats from far-right and anti-migrant protesters. At least two refugee [non-governmental organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organisation) (NGOs) that have been supporting asylum seekers have closed their offices after credible threats to their safety. Organisations including [Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_\(advocacy_group\)), [Greenpeace](12233.md), Care4Calais, [Bail for Immigration Detainees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bail_for_Immigration_Detainees?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Choose Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choose_Love_\(organisation\)), the [Runnymede Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runnymede_Trust) and [Freedom from Torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_from_Torture), as well as lawyers, including [KCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Counsel), have signed the statement.[^cite_note-276]

## Response

### Political support

On 24 July, [Epping Forest District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_District_Council) unanimously voted in favour of urging the government to close the Bell Hotel in Epping, which was where the protests started.[^cite_note-277] On 30 July, a letter to the home secretary calling for an urgent meeting discussing the use of hotels to house asylum seekers was signed by five Conservative politicians (two MPs, two County councillors, and the police, fire and crime commissioner for Essex; [Kevin Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Bentley_\(politician\)), [Alex Burghart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Burghart), [Roger Hirst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Hirst), [Neil Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hudson_\(politician\)) and Christopher Whitbread).[^cite_note-278]



The home secretary [Yvette Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Cooper) said she agreed with "local councils and communities who want the asylum hotels in their communities closed".

Home Secretary [Yvette Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Cooper) expressed understanding for local concerns, stating she agreed with "local councils and communities who want the asylum hotels in their communities closed".[^cite_note-auto-279] Starmer said he "completely" understood residents' concerns, adding that "local people by and large do not want these hotels in their towns."[^cite_note-auto-279]



[Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage), leader of [Reform UK](59757202.md), called for protests outside asylum hotels

[^cite_note-280]

&#x20;and later announced plans for [mass deportations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_deportations) of asylum seekers.

[^cite_note-281]

[Reform UK](59757202.md) leadership supported the Epping protests. Party leader [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) hoped they would inspire similar protests outside migrant hotels across the country to "put pressure on local councils to go to court" to try to block other hotels from housing asylum seekers.[^cite_note-282]



[Rupert Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Lowe), former [Reform UK](59757202.md) MP who became an independent, advocated for mass deportations.

Former Reform UK MP [Rupert Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Lowe), who later became independent, stated his support for "one million plus deportations"[^cite_note-283] and posted on [X](9988187.md) "Detain. Deport. All of them."[^cite_note-284] He argued that "legal immigration is a bigger problem than illegal immigration".[^cite_note-285]

Several [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) MPs also expressed support for local concerns about asylum hotels. [Neil Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hudson_\(politician\)) MP and [Alex Burghart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Burghart) MP issued a joint statement calling for the immediate closure of the Bell Hotel and Phoenix Hotel, citing public safety concerns.[^cite_note-286]

### Political opposition

Some Labour MPs criticised their own government's approach to asylum policy, with concerns raised about echoing far-right rhetoric. Critics within the party objected to aspects of Starmer's immigration rhetoric, including accusations that he echoed [Enoch Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enoch_Powell)'s [Rivers of Blood speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivers_of_Blood_speech).[^cite_note-auto-279]

[Plaid Cymru](53343.md) Westminster leader [Liz Saville Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Saville_Roberts) challenged Starmer in Parliament, saying "This Prime Minister once spoke of compassion and dignity for migrants, and defending free movement. Now he talks of 'islands of strangers' and 'taking back control'."[^cite_note-287] Current Plaid Cymru leader [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth) stated that some areas in Wales need more immigration, saying asylum seekers need to be "processed properly".[^cite_note-288]

The [Green Party](79787.md), under new leader [Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski) elected in 2025, maintained opposition to anti-immigration rhetoric. Green MP [Siân Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Si%C3%A2n_Berry) joined counter-protests against far-right demonstrations in Brighton.[^cite_note-289]

### Counter-protests

[Stand Up To Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_To_Racism) organised counter-protests across multiple locations where anti-asylum hotel demonstrations took place. Counter-demonstrations were held in cities including [Liverpool](18081.md), [Bristol](36741.md), [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne), [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds), [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth), and [Cardiff](5882.md), with protesters displaying banners reading "Refugees Welcome, Celebrate Diversity".[^cite_note-290] The counter-protest organisers stated their opposition to what they called "far-right and fascist thugs" bringing "their message of hate" to local communities.[^cite_note-291]

### Legal actions

On 12 August, [Epping Forest District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_District_Council) applied for an [interim injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_injunction) from the [High Court](26569402.md) to stop migrants being housed at the hotel.[^cite_note-292]

On 15 August, [Mr Justice Eyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_Justice_Eyre) ordered the hotel to refuse any new applications from asylum seekers until he had given his ruling following a hearing at the [Royal Courts of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Courts_of_Justice) where he heard from both the council and the hotel's owners.[^cite_note-293] Despite an effort from home secretary [Yvette Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Cooper) to get the case dismissed, the council was granted the injunction on 19 August, with all asylum seekers housed there to be moved out by 16\:00 [BST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_the_United_Kingdom) on 12 September.[^cite_note-294]

Following the High Court ruling, the Home Office said the decision could "substantially impact" their ability to house asylum seekers, with fears that it may set a precedent and create pressure to find alternative accommodation sooner than expected.[^cite_note-295] A number of councils subsequently said they would work to stop the use of hotels for housing asylum seekers, including all 12 councils controlled by Reform UK and the Conservative-run [Broxbourne Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broxbourne_Borough_Council).[^cite_note-296]

On 29 August, [Lord Justice Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Justice_Bean) said the High Court ruling was "seriously flawed in principle" after overturning the injunction at the [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_\(England_and_Wales\)).[^cite_note-297]

On 11 November, Mr Justice Mould of the High Court ruled against a permanent [injunction](39343.md) sought by the local district council against the use of the Bell hotel.[^cite_note-298]

### Asylum seekers

Speaking to the *BBC*, asylum seekers living in hotels in the South East have said that due to the demonstrations they are afraid to go outside, and in a "state of constant anxiety".[^cite_note-299]

## Policing costs

Policing repeat protests has caused significant increase in costs for local police forces. In late September 2025, [ITV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_News) reported that in [Epping](26609107.md) protesters had gathered "twice a week, almost every week, since mid-July".[^cite_note-300] [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex) police said their response had required a "substantial level" of resources, telling a crime panel meeting that cost of policing protests in Epping could reach £1.7m by October.[^cite_note-301]

More than £100,000 was spent in August to manage the several protests that happened across [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire) and the [Isle of Wight](15102.md).[^cite_note-302]

In September, the Northumbria Police and Crime Commissioner, Susan Dungworth, said that weekly demonstrations at an asylum hotel had strained police resources.[^cite_note-303]
