The **South China Sea** is a marginal [sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea) of the Western [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). It is bounded in the north by [South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China), in the west by the [Indochinese Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochinese_Peninsula), in the east by the islands of [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_island) and northwestern [Philippines](23440.md) (mainly [Luzon](69375.md), [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro), and [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_\(island\))), and in the south by [Borneo](4518.md), eastern [Sumatra](28679.md), and the [Bangka Belitung Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Belitung_Islands), encompassing an area of around 3,500,000 km<sup>2</sup> (1,400,000 sq mi). It communicates with the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea) via the [Taiwan Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Strait), the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea) via the [Luzon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_Strait), the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea) via the straits around [Palawan](240395.md), the [Java Sea](467143.md) via the [Karimata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimata_Strait) and [Bangka Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Strait), and directly with the [Gulf of Thailand](198107.md). Additionally, the [Gulf of Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin) is part of the South China Sea.

\$3.4 trillion of the world's \$16 trillion [maritime shipping](28769.md) passed through the South China Sea in 2016. Oil and natural gas reserves have been found in the area. The Western Central Pacific accounted for 14% of the world's commercial fishing in 2010.

The [South China Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea_Islands), collectively comprising several [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) clusters of mostly small uninhabited islands, islets ([cays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cay) and [shoals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal)), [reefs](240844.md)/[atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll), and [seamounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamount) numbering in the hundreds, are subject to [competing claims of sovereignty](32005536.md) by several countries. These claims are also reflected in the variety of names used for the islands and the sea.

*South China Sea* is the dominant term used in English for the sea, and the name is equivalent in almost all European languages. This name is a result of early European interest in the sea as a route from Europe and South Asia to the trading opportunities of China. In the 16th century, Portuguese sailors referred to it as the China Sea (*Mare da China*); later, the need to differentiate it from nearby bodies of water led to its renaming to the South China Sea.[^cite_note-3] The [International Hydrographic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hydrographic_Organization) refers to the sea as "South China Sea (Nan Hai)".[^cite_note-IHO-4]

The *[Yi Zhou Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Zhou_Shu)*, which was a chronicle of the [Western Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Zhou) dynasty (1046–771 BCE), gives the first Chinese name for the South China Sea as *Nanfang Hai* (Chinese: 南方海; pinyin: *Nánfāng Hǎi*; lit. 'Southern Sea'), claiming that barbarians from that sea gave tributes of [hawksbill sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_sea_turtle) to the Zhou rulers.[^cite_note-Shen-5] The *[Classic of Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_of_Poetry)*, *[Zuo Zhuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuo_Zhuan)*, and *[Guoyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guoyu_\(book\))* classics of the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) (771–476 BCE) also referred to the sea, but by the name *Nan Hai* (Chinese: 南海; pinyin: *Nán Hǎi*; lit. 'South Sea') in reference to the [State of Chu's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_\(state\)) expeditions there.[^cite_note-Shen-5] Nan Hai, the South Sea, was one of the [Four Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Seas) of Chinese literature. There are three other seas, one for each of the four cardinal directions.[^cite_note-6] During the [Eastern Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty#Eastern_Han) (23–220 CE), China's rulers called the sea *Zhang Hai* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 漲海; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Zhǎng Hǎi*; lit. 'distended sea').[^cite_note-Shen-5] *Fei Hai* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 沸海; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Fèi Hǎi*; lit. 'boiling sea') became popular during the [Southern and Northern dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_dynasties). Usage of the current Chinese name, *Nan Hai* (South Sea), gradually became widespread during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty).[^cite_note-7]

In Southeast Asia, it was once called the **Champa Sea** or *Sea of Cham*, after the maritime kingdom of [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa) (nowadays [Central Vietnam](14624160.md)), which flourished there before the 16th century.[^cite_note-NG-The_Cham-8] The majority of the sea came under Japanese naval control during World War II, following the military acquisition of many surrounding South East Asian territories in 1941. Japan calls the sea *Minami [Shina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shina_\(word\)) Kai* "South China Sea". This was written 南支那海 until 2004, when the Japanese Foreign Ministry and other departments switched the spelling to 南シナ海, which has become the standard usage in Japan.

In China, it is referred to as the *South Sea*, (南海; *Nánhǎi*), and in [Vietnam](202354.md) the *East Sea*, *Biển Đông*.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] In [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md), it was long known as the *South China Sea* ([Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Dagat Timog Tsina*, [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Laut China Selatan*), with the part within Philippine territorial waters often called the "Luzon Sea", *Dagat Luzon*, by the Philippines.[^cite_note-12]

However, following an escalation of the [Spratly Islands dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands_dispute) in 2011, various Philippine government agencies started using the name [West Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philippine_Sea). A [Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA) spokesperson stated that the sea to the east of the Philippines will continue to be called the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea).[^cite_note-13] In September 2012, Philippine President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) signed Administrative Order No. 29, mandating that all government agencies use the name *West Philippine Sea* to refer to the parts of South China Sea within the [Philippines exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_the_Philippines), including the Luzon Sea as well as the waters around, within and adjacent to the [Kalayaan Island Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalayaan_Island_Group) and [Bajo de Masinloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajo_de_Masinloc), and tasked the [National Mapping and Resource Information Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Mapping_and_Resource_Information_Authority) to use the name in official maps.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

In July 2017, to assert its sovereignty, Indonesia renamed the northern reaches of [its exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Indonesia) in the South China Sea as the *North Natuna Sea*, which is located north of the Indonesian [Natuna Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Regency), and borders the southern [Vietnam exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Vietnam), corresponding to the southern end of the South China Sea.[^cite_note-16] The [Natuna Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Sea) is located south of Natuna Island within Indonesian territorial waters.[^cite_note-17] Therefore, Indonesia has named two seas that are portions of the South China Sea: the Natuna Sea, located between the Natuna Islands and the [Lingga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Regency) and [Tambelan Archipelagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambelan_Archipelago?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the North Natuna Sea, located between the Natuna Islands and [Cape Cà Mau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_C%C3%A0_Mau) on the southern tip of the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta) in Vietnam. There has been no agreement between China and Indonesia regarding the so-called the Natuna waters dispute, with China remaining ambiguous about the southern limit of its area of interest.[^cite_note-18]

## Hydrography

States and territories with borders on the sea (clockwise from north) include: the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), the [Republic of China (Taiwan)](25734.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Vietnam](202354.md). Major rivers that flow into the South China Sea include the [Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_\(China\)), [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Fujian\)), [Jiulong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiulong_River), [Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_\(Vietnam\)), [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong), [Menam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Phraya_River), [Rajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajang_River), [Baram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baram_River), [Kapuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapuas_River), [Batang Hari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batang_Hari_River), [Musi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musi_River_\(Indonesia\)), [Kampar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampar_River), [Indragiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indragiri_River), [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang_River), [Agno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River), [Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga_River), and [Pasig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River) Rivers.

The South China Sea has an average depth of 1,212 m (3,976 ft), and the deepest recorded point is either 5,559 m (18,238 ft) or 5,567 m (18,264 ft).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Geology

See also: [Tectonics of the South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics_of_the_South_China_Sea)



Sunset on the South China Sea off [Mũi Né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C5%A9i_N%C3%A9) village on the south-east coast of [Vietnam](202354.md)

The sea lies above a drowned [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf); during [recent ice ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_glaciation), global sea level was hundreds of metres lower, and [Borneo](4518.md) was part of the Asian mainland.

The South China Sea opened around [45 million years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) when the "[Dangerous Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Ground_\(South_China_Sea\))" rifted away from southern China. Extension culminated in [seafloor spreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafloor_spreading) around 30 million years ago, a process that propagated to the southwest, resulting in the V-shaped basin we see today. Extension ceased around 17 million years ago.[^cite_note-22]

Arguments have continued about the role of tectonic extrusion in forming the basin. [Paul Tapponnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tapponnier) and colleagues have argued that as India collides with Asia, it pushes [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina) to the southeast. The relative shear between Indochina and China caused the South China Sea to open.[^cite_note-23] This view is disputed by geologists who do not consider Indochina to have moved far relative to mainland Asia. Marine geophysical studies in the Gulf of Tonkin by [Peter Clift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Clift) have shown that the [Red River Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Fault) was active and causing basin formation at least by 37 million years ago in the northwest South China Sea, consistent with extrusion playing a part in the formation of the sea. Since opening, the South China Sea has been the repository of large sediment volumes delivered by the [Mekong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong), [Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_\(Asia\)), and [Pearl River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River). Several of these deltas are rich in oil and gas deposits.

South China Sea

The South China Sea contains over 250 small [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island), [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll), [cays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cays), [shoals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoals), [reefs](240844.md), and [sandbars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbars), most of which have no indigenous people, many of which are naturally underwater at high tide, and some of which are permanently submerged. The features are:

- The [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands)

- The [Paracel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracel_Islands)

- [Pratas Island](410487.md) and the Vereker Banks

- The [Macclesfield Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macclesfield_Bank)

- The [Scarborough Shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_Shoal)

The Spratly Islands span an area of approximately 810 by 900 km, covering around 175 identified insular features, the largest being [Taiping Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Island) (Itu Aba) at just over 1.3 kilometres (0.81 mi) long and with its highest elevation at 3.8 metres (12 ft).

The largest singular feature in the area of the Spratly Islands is a 100 kilometres (62 mi) wide [seamount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamount) called [Reed Tablemount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Tablemount), also known as Reed Bank, in the northeast of the group, separated from Palawan Island of the Philippines by the Palawan Trench. Now completely submerged, with a depth of 20 metres (66 ft), it was an island until it was covered approximately 7,000 years ago by increasing sea levels after the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). With an area of 8,866 square kilometres (3,423 sq mi), it is one of the largest submerged atoll structures in the world.

## Trade route

The South China Sea has historically been an important trade route between northeast Asia, China, southeast Asia, and going to India and the West.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The number of shipwrecks of trading ships that lie on the ocean's floor attests to a thriving trade going back centuries. Nine historic trade ships carrying ceramics dating back to the 10th century until the 19th century were excavated under Swedish engineer [Sten Sjöstrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sten_Sj%C3%B6strand?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-28]

Of the world's \$16 trillion [maritime shipping](28769.md), \$3.4 trillion passed through the South China Sea in 2016.[^cite_note-29] The 2019 data showed that the sea carries trade equivalent to 5 percent of global GDP.[^cite_note-30]

In 2023, the South China Sea remained a major energy trade route, with 10 billion barrels of petroleum and petroleum products and 6.7 trillion cubic feet of liquefied natural gas passing through it. The LNG volume represented 34% of global LNG trade.[^cite_note-31]



Millions of barrels of crude oil are traded through the South China Sea each day

## Natural resources

In 2012–2013, the [United States Energy Information Administration](1704673.md) estimated that there is relatively little oil and natural gas in contested areas such as the Paracel and the Spratly Islands. Most of the proved or probable 11 billion barrels of oil and 190 trillion cubic feet of natural gas in the South China Sea exist near undisputed shorelines.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In 2010, the Western Central Pacific (excluding the northernmost reaches of the South China Sea closest to the PRC coast) accounted for 14% of the total world catch from commercial fishing of 11.7 million tonnes. This was up from less than 4 million tonnes in 1970.[^cite_note-34]

China announced in May 2017 a breakthrough for mining [methane clathrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_clathrate), when they extracted methane from hydrates in the South China Sea, but commercial adoption may take a decade or more.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

Several countries have made competing territorial claims over the South China Sea. Such disputes have been regarded as some of Asia's potentially most dangerous points of conflict. Both the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC) and the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China) (ROC, commonly known as Taiwan) claim almost the entire body as their own, demarcating their claims within what is known as the "[nine-dash line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-dash_line)", which claims overlap with virtually every other country in the region. Competing claims include:

- Indonesia, Vietnam,

  [^cite_note-37]

  &#x20;China, and Taiwan over the waters northeast of the [Natuna Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Islands)

- The Philippines, China, and Taiwan over [Scarborough Shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_Shoal).

- Vietnam, China, and Taiwan over the waters west of the [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands). Some or all of the islands themselves are also disputed between Vietnam, China, Taiwan, Brunei, Malaysia, and the Philippines.

- The [Paracel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracel_Islands) are disputed between China, Taiwan, and Vietnam.

- Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, and Vietnam over areas in the [Gulf of Thailand](198107.md).

- Singapore and Malaysia along the [Strait of Johore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Johor) and the [Strait of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Strait).



Map of various countries occupying the [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands)

China and Vietnam have both been vigorous in prosecuting their claims. China (various governments) and South Vietnam [each controlled part of the Paracel Islands before 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracel_Islands#Historical_perspectives). A [brief conflict in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Paracel_Islands) resulted in 18 Chinese and 53 Vietnamese deaths, and China has controlled the whole of the Paracel since then. The Spratly Islands have been the site of a naval clash, in which over 70 Vietnamese sailors were killed just south of [Chigua Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chigua_Reef) in March 1988. Disputing claimants regularly report clashes between naval vessels,[^cite_note-tension-38] and these now also include airspace incidents.[^cite_note-Nguyen2024-39]

[ASEAN](106539.md), in general, and Malaysia, in particular, have been keen to ensure that the territorial disputes within the South China Sea do not escalate into armed conflict. As such, joint development authorities have been established in areas of overlapping claims to jointly develop the area and divide the profits equally without settling the issue of sovereignty over the area. This is true particularly in the Gulf of Thailand. Generally, China has preferred to resolve competing claims bilaterally,[^cite_note-40] while some ASEAN countries prefer multilateral talks,[^cite_note-41] believing that they are disadvantaged in bilateral negotiations with the much larger China and that, because many countries claim the same territory, only multilateral talks could effectively resolve the competing claims.[^cite_note-42]



The overlapping maritime claims in Spratly Islands

The overlapping claims over [Pedra Branca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedra_Branca,_Singapore) or [Pulau Batu Putih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedra_Branca,_Singapore), including the neighbouring [Middle Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Rocks), by both Singapore and Malaysia were settled in 2008 by the [International Court of Justice](14918.md), awarding Pedra Branca/Pulau Batu Puteh to Singapore and the Middle Rocks to Malaysia.[^cite_note-43] In July 2010, US Secretary of State Hillary Clinton called for China to resolve the territorial dispute. China responded by demanding that the US keep out of the issue. This came at a time when both countries had been engaging in naval exercises as a show of force to the opposing side, which increased tensions in the region.[^cite_note-tension-38] The [US Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Defense) released a statement on August 18 where it opposed the use of force to resolve the dispute, and accused China of assertive behaviour.[^cite_note-44] On July 22, 2011, one of India's amphibious assault vessels, the [INS *Airavat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Airavat_\(L24\)) which was on a friendly visit to Vietnam, was reportedly contacted at a distance of 45 nautical miles (83 km) from the Vietnamese coast in the disputed South China Sea on an open radio channel by a vessel identifying itself as the Chinese Navy and stating that the ship was entering Chinese waters.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-45][^cite_note-46] The spokesperson for the Indian Navy clarified that, as no ship or aircraft was visible from INS *Airavat,* it proceeded as scheduled on its onward journey. The Indian Navy further clarified that "\[t]here was no confrontation involving the INS Airavat. India supports freedom of navigation in international waters, including in the South China Sea, and the right of passage in accordance with accepted principles of international law. These principles should be respected by all."[^cite_note-autogenerated3-45]

In September 2011, shortly after China and Vietnam had signed an agreement seeking to contain a dispute over the South China Sea, India's state-run explorer, [Oil and Natural Gas Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_Natural_Gas_Corporation) (ONGC) said that its overseas investment arm ONGC Videsh Limited had signed a three-year deal with [Petrovietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovietnam) for developing long-term cooperation in the oil sector[^cite_note-47] and that it had accepted Vietnam's offer of exploration in certain specified blocks in the South China Sea.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-48] In response, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Jiang Yu issued a protest.[^cite_note-autogenerated2-49][^cite_note-autogenerated4-50] The spokesman of the Ministry of External Affairs of the Government of India responded by saying that "The Chinese had concerns, but we are going by what the Vietnamese authorities have told us and have conveyed this to the Chinese."[^cite_note-autogenerated2-49] The Indo-Vietnamese deal was also denounced by the Chinese state-run newspaper *[Global Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Times)*.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-48][^cite_note-autogenerated4-50]

In 1999, Taiwan claimed the entirety of the South China Sea islands under the administration of [Lee Teng-hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Teng-hui).[^cite_note-51] The entire subsoil, seabed, and waters of the Paracels and Spratlys are claimed by Taiwan.[^cite_note-52]

In 2012 and 2013, Vietnam and Taiwan butted heads against each other over anti-Vietnamese military exercises by Taiwan.[^cite_note-53]



[Subi Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subi_Reef) being built up into an artificial island, 2015

In May 2014, China established an [oil rig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiyang_Shiyou_981_standoff) near the Paracel Islands, leading to multiple incidents between Vietnamese and Chinese ships.[^cite_note-bbc-54][^cite_note-55] Vietnamese analysis identifies this change in strategy, generating ongoing incidents as occurring since 2012.[^cite_note-Nguyen2024-39]

In December 2018, retired Chinese admiral [Luo Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_Yuan_\(admiral\)) proposed that a possible solution to tensions with the United States in the South China Sea would be to sink one or two [United States Navy](20518076.md) aircraft carriers to break US morale.[^cite_note-BILockie-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Also in December 2018, Chinese commentator and Senior Colonel in the [People's Liberation Army Air Force](381445.md), [Dai Xu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Xu), suggested that China's navy should ram [United States Navy](20518076.md) ships sailing in the South China Sea.[^cite_note-BILockie-56][^cite_note-60]

The US, although not a signatory to [UNCLOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_and_the_United_Nations_Convention_on_the_Law_of_the_Sea), has maintained its position that its naval vessels have consistently sailed unhindered through the South China Sea and will continue to do so.[^cite_note-61] At times, US warships have come within the 12 nautical-mile limit of Chinese-controlled islands (such as the Paracel Islands), arousing China's ire.[^cite_note-62] During the US Chief of Naval Operations' visit to China in early 2019, he and his Chinese counterpart worked out rules of engagement, whenever American warships and Chinese warships encounter on the high seas.[^cite_note-63]

On 26 June 2020, the 36th Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit was held virtually. Vietnam, as the Chairman of the Summit, released the Chairman's Statement. The statement said the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea is "the basis for determining maritime entitlements, sovereign rights, jurisdiction and legitimate interests over maritime zones, and the 1982 UNCLOS sets out the legal framework within which all activities in the oceans and seas must be carried out."[^cite_note-64]

In November 2025, Australia raised concerns over China's military expansion in the South and East China Seas, citing increased risks to trade routes. The remarks followed an incident in which a Chinese fighter jet dropped flares near an Australian patrol plane, deemed “unsafe and unprofessional,” and were made during the Indo-Pacific Navy Conference amid Australia's AUKUS nuclear submarine program.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

### 2016 arbitration

Main article: [South China Sea Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea_Arbitration)

In January 2013, the Philippines initiated arbitration proceedings against China (PRC) over issues surrounding the [nine-dash line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-dash_line), characterization of maritime features, and EEZ.[^cite_note-PCA_Award-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] China did not participate in the arbitration.[^cite_note-\:04-72]<sup>: 127 </sup>

On 12 July 2016, an [arbitral tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitral_tribunal) ruled in favor of the Philippines on most of its submissions. It clarified that it would not "rule on any question of sovereignty over land territory and would not delimit any maritime boundary between the Parties" but concluded that China had not historically exercised exclusive control within the nine-dash line, hence has "no legal basis" to claim "historic rights" over the resources.[^cite_note-73] It also concluded that China's historic rights claims over the maritime areas (as opposed to land masses and territorial waters) inside the nine-dash line would have no lawful effect outside of what is entitled to under UNCLOS.[^cite_note-74] It criticized China's land reclamation projects and its construction of artificial islands in the Spratly Islands, stating that it had caused "severe harm to the coral reef environment."[^cite_note-75] Finally, it characterized [Taiping Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Island) and other features of the Spratly Islands as "rocks" under UNCLOS, and therefore are not entitled to a 200 nautical mile [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone).[^cite_note-76] The arbitral tribunal decision was ruled as final and non-appealable by either country.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

China rejected the ruling, calling it "ill-founded".[^cite_note-BBC_2016-79] China's response was to ignore the arbitration result and to continue pursuing bilateral discussions with the Philippines.[^cite_note-\:04-72]<sup>: 128 </sup>

Taiwan, which currently administers Taiping Island, the largest of the Spratly Islands, also rejected the ruling.[^cite_note-80] As of November 2023, 26 governments support the ruling, 17 issued generally positive statements noting the ruling but not calling for compliance, and eight rejected it.[^cite_note-AMTI2023-81] The governments in support are [Australia](4689264.md), [Austria](26964606.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Canada](5042916.md), the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Denmark](76972.md), [Finland](10577.md), [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Greece](12108.md), [India](14533.md), [Ireland](14560.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Japan](15573.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Portugal](23033.md), [Romania](25445.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Sweden](5058739.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the [United States](3434750.md); the governments in opposition are [China](5405.md), [Montenegro](20760.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Sudan](27421.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), and [Vanuatu](32443.md).[^cite_note-AMTI2023-81][^cite_note-82] The [United Nations](31769.md) itself does not have a position on the legal and procedural merits of the case or on the disputed claims, and the Secretary-General expressed his hope that the continued consultations on a Code of Conduct between [ASEAN](106539.md) and China under the framework of the Declaration of the Conduct of Parties in the South China Sea will lead to increased mutual understanding among all the parties.[^cite_note-83]
