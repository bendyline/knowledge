On 29 January 2026, clashes erupted in the [Tigray Region](357350.md) of [Ethiopia](187749.md) between the [Tigray People's Liberation Front](1276759.md) (TPLF) and Ethiopian government forces.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3] On 1 February, the [Tigray Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_Defense_Force) (TDF) withdrew from [Tselemti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tselemti) district as its commander [Tadesse Werede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadesse_Werede) demanded peaceful resolution and dialogue.

## Background

Main articles: [Tigray War](65760352.md) and [Tigray political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_political_crisis)

In November 2020, the TPLF and [Tigray Defense Forces](67562601.md) (TDF) [launched attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Command_attacks_\(Ethiopia\)) on [Ethiopian Army](68039791.md) bases in the Tigray region after tensions regarding the [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Tigray_regional_election) to be held the next year, and over accusations of Ethiopian government corruption. This ultimately culminated into the subsequent [Tigray War](65760352.md).[^cite_note-4] By March 2022, the war had come to a virtual standstill[^cite_note-5] and on 2 November 2022, the [Pretoria Peace Agreement](72171064.md) was signed, ending the Tigray War.[^cite_note-6] Despite the agreement stipulating it, the TPLF/TDF did not demobilise, and still controlled much of the Tigray region. Alongside that, as of February 2026, much of [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Tigray) remains occupied by [Amhara militias](1746709.md) who [claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welkait_question) the territory.

In March 2025, tensions intensified again when a faction of the TPLF, led by [Debretsion Gebremichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debretsion_Gebremichael), took over several offices in [Mekelle](1221559.md) in a coup against the [Interim Regional Administration of Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Regional_Administration_of_Tigray). Armed men belonging to the faction patrolled the streets of the city at night, checking people's identification. *The Guardian* reported that some accounts suggested that Eritrean intelligence had helped Debretsion's faction assume power, while [Getachew Reda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getachew_Reda), the interim leader of Tigray, had fled to [Addis Ababa](52593.md).[^cite_note-7]

These tensions continued in late 2025 when the TPLF accused the Ethiopian government of funding and aiding multiple anti-TPLF groups—particularly the [Tigray Peace Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_Peace_Forces) (TPF), a splinter group of former TPLF/TDF members—active on Tigray's border with the neighboring [Afar Region](2483633.md), which launched attacks on Tigrayan forces, killing several.[^cite_note-8] Furthermore, in November, the Afar Regional government accused elements of the TPLF of crossing over the Tigray-Afar border and attacking civilians, and warned that it would retaliate if the TPLF did not withdraw. The TPLF denied the allegations, accusing Ethiopian-backed militants of orchestrating the attack.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The day after, the TPLF accused Ethiopian forces of launching a drone strike on [Raya Azebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raya_Azebo), killing Tigrayan forces and civilians in violation of the Pretoria ceasefire.**[^cite_note-11]**

## Clashes

On 29 January, these tensions erupted into clashes between [Tigrayan fighters](67562601.md) and [Federal Forces](9405.md) (aided by Amharan militias) in [Mai Degusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai_Degusha?action=edit\&redlink=1), a contested area of [Tselemti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tselemti) district in western Tigray. [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) cancelled its flights from [Addis Ababa](52593.md) to cities in Tigray in response.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-12] [Getachew Reda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getachew_Reda), former Tigrayan interim president deposed in the March 2025 coup, accused "Power-hungry individuals within the TPLF" of "trying to drag Tigray into another round of war."[^cite_note-13]

On 30 January, Tigrayan forces captured the towns of [Alamata](11602398.md) and [Korem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korem) in [Southern Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Zone,_Tigray) after Ethiopian forces withdrew, leading to fears that clashes would spread to the region.[^cite_note-14]

On 31 January, a Tigrayan official reported drone strikes that hit two Isuzu trucks near [Enticho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enticho) and [Gendebta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendebta?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Tigray, which were claimed to have been transporting weapons, killing one person and injuring another. The official accused the Ethiopian Armed Forces of the strikes without providing evidence.[^cite_note-auto-1]

On 1 February, Tigrayan forces withdrew from the [Tselemti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tselemti) district after several days of intense clashes. [Tadesse Werede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadesse_Werede) described the withdrawal as a demonstration of their readiness for dialogue and a peaceful resolution to the current tensions.[^cite_note-15]

On 3 February, Ethiopian Airlines reinstated air services to destinations in northern Tigray.[^cite_note-16]
