**Naha** ([Japanese](15606.md): 那覇市, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Naha-shi*; Japanese: [\[naꜜ.ha, na.haꜜ.ɕi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese);[^cite_note-3] [Okinawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_language): *Naafa*[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] or *Nafa*[^cite_note-6]) is the [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Japan) of [Okinawa Prefecture](22477.md), the southernmost prefecture of Japan. As of 1 June 2019, the city has an estimated population of 317,405 and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 7,939 people per km<sup>2</sup> (20,562 persons per sq. mi.). The total area is 39.98 km<sup>2</sup> (15.44 sq mi).

Naha is located on the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea) coast of the southern part of [Okinawa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island), the largest island of Okinawa Prefecture. The modern city was officially founded on May 20, 1921. Before that, Naha had for centuries been one of the most important and populous sites in Okinawa.

Naha is the political, economic and educational center of Okinawa Prefecture. In the medieval and early modern periods,[^cite_note-7] it was the commercial center of the [Ryukyu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Kingdom).

## History



Naha Harbor during the [Ryukyu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Kingdom)

According to the *Irosetsuden* ([遺老説伝](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%81%BA%E8%80%81%E8%AA%AC%E4%BC%9D)), the name of Naha comes from its original name, Naba, which was the name of a large, [mushroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom)-shaped stone in the city. (*Naba* is a [Western Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_dialects) and [Ryukyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_languages) word for "[mushroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom).") Gradually, the stone wore away and became buried, and the name's pronunciation and its [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji) gradually changed.[^cite_note-8]



"Naha from Bamboo Village" looking toward the seashore. Lithography by [Wilhelm Heine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Heine) (1856).

In Naha, some archeological relics of the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) were found. From a [Jōmon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) *[kaizuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden)* (shell mound), ancient Chinese coins were found. Pottery found by archaeologists indicates that the area was an active site of trade with the Japanese archipelago and Korean peninsula at least as early as the 11th century. Though it is not known just when the area first became organized as a functioning port city, it was active as such by the time of the unification of the Ryūkyū Kingdom in the early 15th century.[^cite_note-9]

Though today Naha has grown to incorporate the former royal capital city of [Shuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri,_Okinawa), center of Chinese learning [Kumemura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumemura), and other towns and villages, in the period of the Ryūkyū Kingdom, it was a smaller city, prominent as a major port, but not as a political center.

Medieval Naha was on a tiny island called Ukishima, connected to the mainland of Okinawa Island by a narrow causeway called Chōkōtei (長虹堤, *lit. "long rainbow embankment"*) which led on to Shuri. The main port area for international trade, Naha proper, was divided into the East (東, *higashi*) and West (西, *nishi*) districts and was on the southwestern portion of Ukishima. A large open-air marketplace was active in front of the royal government trading center, or *oyamise* (親見世). A number of Japanese temples and shrines were located here, along with a residence and embassy, known as the Tenshikan (天使館), for visiting Chinese officials. A pair of forts ([Mie gusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_Castle) and [Yarazamori gusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarazamori_Castle)) built atop embankments extending out across the entrance to the harbor defended the port, and a small island within the harbor held a warehouse, *Omono gusuku* (御物グスク), used for storing trade goods.[^cite_note-Uezato62-10]

Tomari (泊), on the mainland of Okinawa Island to the northeast of Ukishima, served as the chief port for trade within the Ryūkyū Islands. The administrators of Tomari were also responsible for collecting and managing the [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute) paid to the kingdom by the [Amami Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Islands), whose tribute ships made port here.[^cite_note-Uezato62-10]

Kume-Ōdōri (久米大通り; "Kume Great Avenue") ran across Ukishima from southeast to northwest, forming the center of the walled community of [Kumemura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumemura), the center of classical Chinese learning in Ryūkyū for centuries.[^cite_note-Uezato62-10] Kumemura is traditionally believed to have been founded by 36 [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakers_of_Min_Chinese) families sent to Ryūkyū by the Ming Chinese Imperial Court and to be inhabited primarily or solely by descendants of those settlers; historian Uezato Takashi points out, however, that due to Naha's prominence in international maritime trade networks, it is quite likely that many other Chinese, chiefly from [Fujian](88585.md) and other maritime trading areas along the southern Chinese coast, would have settled here as well.[^cite_note-11]

Major sites in the community included the Tensonbyō [Taoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) temple near the northern end of Kume-Ōdōri and two shrines called Upper and Lower Tenpigū, dedicated to the Taoist goddess of the sea Tenpi, also known as [Matsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazu_\(goddess\)).[^cite_note-Uezato62-10] A Confucian temple, the gift of the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor), was built in Kumemura in the 1670s; the [Meirindō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meirind%C5%8D?action=edit\&redlink=1), a school of classic Confucian Chinese learning, was established in 1718.[^cite_note-12] Following their destruction in [World War II](32927.md), the Meirindō, Confucian temple, and Tenpigū shrines were rebuilt on the site of the Tensonbyō in northern Kume, where they stand today as the Confucian temple [Shiseibyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiseiby%C5%8D).

On the northwest side of Ukishima lay Wakasamachi (若狭町; "Wakasa town"), a community traditionally said to have been founded by Japanese settlers. It was organized around Wakasamachi-Ōdōri, an avenue which intersected with Kume-Ōdōri and ran across tidal mudflats to the east of Ukishima, connecting the community to the port of Tomari on the Okinawan mainland. A number of Japanese shrines and temples were located in Wakasamachi, including the [Naminoue Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naminoue_Shrine), the [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) temple Kōganji, and temples devoted to [Ebisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebisu_\(mythology\)) and [Jizō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiz%C5%8D). The community had lodgings specifically set aside for traders and travelers from the [Tokara Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokara_Islands).[^cite_note-Uezato62-10]



Kokusai Dori, International Main Street in Naha, 1950s

Another settlement, known as Izumizaki, lay on the mainland of Okinawa Island, just across the Kumoji River from Ukishima. Izumizaki had no notable or major port facilities and is believed to have been simply an extension of the residential community of Naha proper, which thus spread onto the mainland as the population and according demand for land grew.[^cite_note-Uezato62-10] At some point, the tidal mudflats and Kumoji River separating Ukishima, that is, Naha, from Okinawa Island were filled in. The neighborhoods of Kume, Wakasa, and Tomari can still be found in Naha today.

Commodore [Matthew C. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_C._Perry)'s expeditionary squadron stopped in Naha en route to Tokyo in 1853; and the American ships visited several more times. The lithographs prepared from drawings made by the expedition's official artist would be widely circulated. These images would provide the basis for 19th century impressions of the geography and people of the Ryūkyū islands.

After the replacement of the Ryūkyū Kingdom with the [Ryūkyū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%ABky%C5%AB_Domain) [Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_\(country_subdivision\)) in 1872, Naha became the capital city. The Ryūkyū Domain was abolished in 1879 and the former Ryūkyū Kingdom came to an end, fully annexed by Japan as [Okinawa Prefecture](22477.md), with Naha remaining as the capital city. Shuri and other neighboring municipalities were absorbed into the city.

An Imperial decree in July 1899 established Naha as an open port for trading with the United States and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-13]

During the [battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) in [World War II](32927.md), Naha suffered extensive damage from the fighting. The entire center of the city had to be rebuilt. On 1 September 1954, the village of [Oroku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oroku) was merged into the city.[^cite_note-14]

On April 1, 2013, Naha became a [core city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_cities_of_Japan), a category of [cities of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Japan) under the [Local Autonomy Law of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Autonomy_Law). Naha now carries out many of the functions, notably for public health care, normally delegated to the prefectural government. Naha is the first core city in Okinawa Prefecture.[^cite_note-15]

Naha City Hall in 2013

### City center

Central Naha consists of the Palette Kumoji shopping mall, the [Okinawa Prefecture Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture_Office), Naha City Hall, and many banks and corporations, located at the west end of Kokusai-dōri, the city's main street. Kokusai-dōri (国際通り; "International Avenue") boasts a 1.6 kilometer (1 mile) long stretch of stores, restaurants and bars. Kokusai-dōri ends at the main bus terminal in Okinawa and is served by several stations along the [Okinawa Urban Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Urban_Monorail), the only train system in the prefecture.

Spurring off from Kokusai-dōri is the covered [Heiwa-dōri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiwa-d%C5%8Dri) Shopping Arcade and Makishi Public Market, a massive *[shōtengai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dtengai)* filled with fresh fish, meat, and produce stands, restaurants, tourist goods shops, and liquor shops. Just outside the market area is the neighborhood of Tsuboya (壺屋; "pot/jar shop"), which was once a major center of ceramic production (see [Tsuboya-yaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuboya-yaki)).

Northeast of Kokusai-dōri is a relatively new commercial district called Shintoshin (新都心; "New Metropolitan Center"). The area, formerly [United States military](32212.md) housing, was released to Okinawa in 1987, but major development only began in the mid-1990s. [Omoromachi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omoromachi_Station) is attached directly to an upscale shopping mall; another mall, Naha Main Place, a few hundred meters (yards) down the street, contains many upscale Western-brand fashion boutiques, with restaurants and other shops. Frequented by young people, the area boasts large stores such as [Toys R Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toys_R_Us) and Best Denki (an electronics store), a co-op market, many restaurants and a movie theater.

The [Okinawa Prefectural Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefectural_Museum), containing sections devoted to the art, history, and natural history of the Ryukyus, opened in the area in November 2007 and sits in front of [Shintoshin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shintoshin_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### Climate

Naha has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*)—bordering on [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Af*)—with hot summers and mild winters. Precipitation is abundant throughout the year; September is the wettest month and December is the driest. Naha has hot and humid summers with July and August being the city's warmest months, exceeding an average high of 31 degrees Celsius (88 °F). Naha has warm winters, with average high temperatures in the coolest months of January and February, hovering around 19–20 degrees Celsius (66 to 68 °F) and average lows around 14–15 degrees Celsius (57 to 59 °F). The city sees a substantial amount of rainfall, averaging in excess of 2,000 mm (79 in) of rain per year.&#x20;

## Culture

### Religion

There are numerous shrines throughout the city, ranging from small huts to temples consisting of several houses. The religion is a mix of Shintoism, Buddhism, Taoism, and indigenous traditions. The most visible part of the local beliefs though is by far the shisa, the Okinawan [shiisaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiisaa) ("lion dogs") that are considered protectors of the island and are found everywhere – walls, roofs, windows, street corners and parks.

The Cathedral of the Immaculate Heart of Mary (Kainan Church) is the episcopal see of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Naha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Naha).

The restored and rebuilt [Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle), the former royal palace of the Ryūkyū Kingdom, is one of the finest *[gusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuku)* (Okinawan castle) and among the most important historical sites in Naha. The palace, and a series of tunnels underneath it, were used as a major command post by the Imperial Japanese military during World War II, and the castle was subsequently almost destroyed in 1945 by the US Marines, Army and Navy. After the war, the University of the Ryūkyūs was constructed on the site. Today Shuri Castle has been reconstructed, including the famous [Shureimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shureimon), its main gate, and is registered, along with a number of other *gusuku* and other Okinawan historical and sacred sites, as a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).

[Lake Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Man), covered with [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) woods on the boundary of the city of [Tomigusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomigusuku,_Okinawa), is listed on the [Ramsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention) list of [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland).

### Martial arts

*[Naha-te](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha-te)*, (Naha-hand), called *Nawate* by [Gichin Funakoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gichin_Funakoshi), is a type of [martial art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_art) developed in Naha.[^cite_note-18] The successor styles to Naha-te include [Gōjū-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C5%8Dj%C5%AB-ry%C5%AB), [Uechi-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uechi-ry%C5%AB), [Ryūei-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%ABei-ry%C5%AB), and [Tōon-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Don-ry%C5%AB).

## Education

Four universities are in the Naha area. Two are run by Okinawa Prefecture; two are private. The [University of the Ryukyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Ryukyus), the sole national university in Okinawa Prefecture, was also in Naha, on the site of Shuri Castle. Before the restoration of the castle, the university moved to the town of [Nishihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishihara,_Okinawa) to the northeast of Naha.

Naha's public elementary and junior high schools are operated by the [Naha City Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha_City_Board_of_Education?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-19] Naha's public high schools are operated by the [Okinawa Prefectural Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefectural_Board_of_Education). Private schools include the [Okinawa Actors' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Actors'_School).

## Economy

Naha is an economic center of Okinawa dominated by tourism, retail and service industries. Okinawa's largest banks, [Bank of the Ryukyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_the_Ryukyus), [Bank of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Okinawa?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Okinawa Kaiho Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Kaiho_Bank?action=edit\&redlink=1), are headquartered in Naha. The [Bank of Japan](188155.md), [Mizuho Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizuho_Bank), [Shoko Chukin Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoko_Chukin_Bank) and [Japan Post Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Post_Bank) also have branches in Naha. Major international insurance companies also have call centers based in the city.

[Naha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha_Airport) is a major transportation hub for the region, and [Japan Transocean Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Transocean_Air) and [Ryukyu Air Commuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Air_Commuter), subsidiaries of [Japan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Airlines), are headquartered in Naha.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Transportation



National Route 58 in Naha City

### Air

[Naha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha_Airport) and Naha Port serve the city. Naha Airport is the hub of Okinawa Prefecture.

### Train

The [Okinawa Urban Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Urban_Monorail), also known as the Yui Rail (ゆいレール) carries passengers from Naha Airport Station to the center of Naha, Kokusai-dōri, Shintoshin, Shuri, and to the terminal at [Tedako-Uranishi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedako-Uranishi_Station) in [Urasoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urasoe,_Okinawa).

### Ferry

The Tomari wharf in Naha connects the main Okinawa island to the rest of the islands around it. Notably it connect to the main land via the a daily ferry to [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima), but also many smaller one to get to the [Kerama islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerama_Islands) (such as [Tokashiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokashiki_Island), [Aka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aka_Island) and [Zamami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamami_Island))
