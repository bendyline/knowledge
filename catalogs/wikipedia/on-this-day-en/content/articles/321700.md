The **president of South Africa** is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md) of the [Republic of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Africa). The president directs the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) branch of the government and is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force). Between 1961 and 1994, the office of head of state was the [state presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_President_of_South_Africa).

The president is elected by the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_South_Africa), the lower house of [Parliament](376182.md), and is usually the leader of the largest party, which has been the [African National Congress](2503.md) since the first multiracial election was held on 27 April 1994. The Constitution limits the president's time in office to two five-year terms.[^cite_note-info.gov.za-2] The first president to be elected under the new constitution was [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela). The incumbent is [Cyril Ramaphosa](1089194.md), who was elected by the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_South_Africa) on 15 February 2018 following the resignation of [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma).

Under the interim constitution (valid from 1994 to 1996), there was a [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Nelson_Mandela), in which a member of Parliament (MP) from the largest opposition party was entitled to a position as [deputy president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_President). Along with Thabo Mbeki, the last [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) president, [F. W. de Klerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._W._de_Klerk) also served as deputy president, in his capacity as the leader of the [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(South_Africa\)) which was the second-largest party in the new Parliament. But De Klerk later resigned and went into opposition with his party. A voluntary coalition government continues to exist under the new constitution (adopted in 1996), although there have been no appointments of opposition politicians to the post of deputy president since.

The president is required to be a member of the National Assembly at the time of the election. Upon election, the president immediately resigns their seat for the duration of the presidential term. The president may be removed either by a [motion of no-confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_no-confidence) or an [impeachment trial](15328.md).

## Origins

A number of manifestations of the office of president have existed. Aspects of these offices exist within the presidency today. The executive leadership of the British colonies of [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Natal) and the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony) were vested in their governors. Likewise, it was invested in the presidents of the Boer republics of the [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Republic) and the [Orange Free State](180208.md). Alternating sovereignty as a result of wars culminated in the [Treaty of Vereeniging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Vereeniging) which concluded the [South African War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War).

The [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa), a British Dominion, was established on 31 May 1910[^cite_note-3] with the [British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_the_United_Kingdom) as titular head of state, represented by a viceroy, the [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Union_of_South_Africa).

Following the [1960 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_South_African_republic_referendum) and subsequent declaration of the Republic of South Africa on 31 May 1961, the office of [State President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_President_of_South_Africa) was created.[^cite_note-4] It was originally a ceremonial post, but became an executive post in 1984 when a new [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Constitution_of_1983) abolished the post of [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_South_Africa) and transferred its powers to the state president. The country ended minority rule in 1994. The office of the president, and the roles that come with it, were established by chapter five of the Constitution of South Africa.[^cite_note-info.gov.za-2]

## Electoral system

South Africa has a distinctive system for the election of its president. Unlike other former British colonies and dominions who have adopted a parliamentary republican form of government and those that follow the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system), South Africa's president is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md) and commander-in-chief of the [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force) (SANDF). Contrary to presidential systems around the world, the president of South Africa is elected by the [Parliament of South Africa](376182.md)[^cite_note-5] rather than by the people directly. They are thus answerable to it in theory and able to influence legislation in practice as head of the majority party.

The president is elected at the first sitting of Parliament after an election, and whenever a vacancy arises. The president is elected by the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_South_Africa), the lower house of Parliament, from among its members. The [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_South_Africa) must oversee the election. Once elected, a person is no longer a member of the National Assembly. They must then be sworn in as president within five days of the election. Should a vacancy arise, the date of a new election must be set by the chief justice, but not more than 30 days after the vacancy occurs.[^cite_note-6]

The Constitution has thus prescribed a system combining both parliamentary and presidential systems in a unique manner. Only [Botswana](3464.md) and [a few other countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_system_of_government#Parliamentary_republics_with_an_executive_president) use a similar system. Between 1996 and 2003 [Israel](9282173.md) combined the two systems in an opposite way, with [direct elections for the post of prime minister](24898.md#Direct_election).[^cite_note-7]

Although the presidency is the key institution, it is hedged about with numerous checks and balances that prevent its total dominance over the government, as was the case in many African countries. The presidential term is five years, with a limit of two terms. Thus the electoral system attempts (at least on paper) to prevent the accumulation of power in the president as occurred during Apartheid and as is the case currently in many other African countries.[^cite_note-8]

## Succession

According to chapter five of the [constitution](441610.md), the president can only exercise the powers of the presidential office while within the [Republic of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Africa). The president may appoint an [acting president](691917.md) when travelling outside the country or unable to fulfill the duties of the office.

A presidential vacancy should be filled first by the [deputy president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_President_of_South_Africa), then a Cabinet minister selected by the president, thereafter a Cabinet minister selected by the Cabinet, and finally by the [speaker of the National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_National_Assembly_of_South_Africa).[^cite_note-9]

The president is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), [head of government](37570.md) and [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force). The rights, responsibilities and remuneration of the president are enumerated in Chapter V of the [Constitution of South Africa](441610.md) and subsequent amendments and laws passed by the [Parliament of South Africa](376182.md).

The executive powers of the republic are vested in the president, who appoints various officials to positions listed in the Constitution, the most significant of which are the Cabinet ministers and justices of the [Supreme Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Appeal_of_South_Africa) and the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa). The president's [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_South_Africa) implements and enforces the Constitution and laws and carries out the president's political objectives. Judges are appointed on the advice of the [Judicial Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Service_Commission_\(South_Africa\)).

The president plays a role in the formation of legislation. The president can sign bills into laws or veto legislation (subject to an override), refer bills back to Parliament or to the Constitutional Court, or call for a referendum. The president summons parliament, and often delivers a State of the Nation Address at the beginning of each session.

The president is the commander-in-chief of the [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force) thereby possessing influence or control over foreign and security policy. The president is accorded the constitutional powers to declare war and make peace, negotiate and sign (although not ratify) treaties (and the alliances that may come with them), and receives and appoints diplomatic officials, confers honours and grants pardons.[^cite_note-10]
