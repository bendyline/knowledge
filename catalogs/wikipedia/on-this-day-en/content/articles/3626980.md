**Hashim Thaçi**[^cite_note-5] (born 24 April 1968) is a [Kosovan](17391.md) politician who was the first [prime minister of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Kosovo) (2008–2014) and the [foreign minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Kosovo\)) and [deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Kosovo) (2014–2016) in the cabinet led by [Isa Mustafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isa_Mustafa). He also served as [president of Kosovo](1260820.md) from 2016 until his resignation in 2020.

Thaçi is from the region of [Drenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drenica) in Kosovo, which is where the [Kosovo Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Liberation_Army) (KLA) originated. He studied philosophy in Pristina before moving to [Switzerland](26748.md), where he joined the KLA in 1993. An Albanian nationalist, he rose through the ranks of the KLA to become leader of the most powerful faction by 1999, during the [Rambouillet negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambouillet_conference). He then joined the [interim Kosovo administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Administrative_Council) after the war.

Thaçi became leader of the [Democratic Party of Kosovo](1260606.md) (PDK), which won the largest share of the vote in the [2007 Kosovo elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Kosovan_parliamentary_election). In 2008, Thaçi read the declaration of independence of Kosovo and became its first prime minister. In 2016 he was elected president of Kosovo. Thaçi pursued a [pro-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Americanism) policy while in office.

In September 2026, Thaçi was convicted by the Kosovo Specialist Chambers' first instance Trial Panel of [war crimes](25576082.md) in the [Kosovo War](16760.md).[^cite_note-BBC_Kosovo_former_pres_sentenced_25-6][^cite_note-KSC_Thaci_found_guilty-7] Thaçi's defence lawyers stated that they would appeal the decision.[^cite_note-8] In 2020, the [Kosovo Specialist Chambers and Specialist Prosecutor's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Specialist_Chambers_and_Specialist_Prosecutor's_Office) in The Hague filed a ten-count indictment against Thaçi and others, charging them with [crimes against humanity](61099.md) and [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-9] To face these charges, Thaçi resigned from the presidency, stating that he did so to "protect the integrity of the presidency of Kosovo".[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] He was arrested and pleaded not guilty to all charges.[^cite_note-\:2-12] His trial opened in April 2023. The [Specialist Prosecutor's Office](49113145.md) sought a sentence of 45 years imprisonment for war crimes and [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution) of [Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Serbs) and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Kosovo) minorities.[^cite_note-13] In September 2026, he was convicted and sentenced to 25 years in prison for war crimes[^cite_note-Reuters_Thaci_sentenced_25-14][^cite_note-15] including the murder of 96 people and the torture of 303 people. The Trial Panel found Thaçi not guilty of crimes against humanity.[^cite_note-KSC_Thaci_found_guilty-7]

There have been controversies regarding Thaçi's role in the KLA and allegations about him being involved in organized crime.[^cite_note-PhillipsBurns2012-16][^cite_note-Boyle2014-17][^cite_note-KoehlerZurcher2003-18]

## Early life and education

Hashim Thaçi was born on 24 April 1969 in the village of [Burojë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buroj%C3%AB), [Skenderaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skenderaj), [SFR Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFR_Yugoslavia) (now [Kosovo](17391.md)).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] His family comes from the [Thaçi tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tha%C3%A7i_\(tribe\)).[^cite_note-21] Skenderaj is located in the [Drenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drenica) valley, a region historically resistant to Serbian rule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndrejevich2009107-22] Drenica would become the birthplace of the [Kosovo Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Liberation_Army) (KLA) in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndrejevich2009107-22]

Thaçi studied [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and history at the [University of Pristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pristina_\(1969%E2%80%931999\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndrejevich2009107-22] By 1993, he was living in Switzerland, where he joined the Albanian political émigré group. He registered for postgraduate studies at the [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Z%C3%BCrich) in the departments of history and international relations, achieving a [master's degree](170346.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

As a young man, Thaçi joined the [People's Movement of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Movement_of_Kosovo) (LPK), an underground group committed to overthrowing the [Yugoslav government](34244.md) that had been historically financed and ideologically shaped by the regime of Albania's former dictator, [Enver Hoxha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Hoxha).[^cite_note-25]

## Role in KLA

In 1993, Thaçi became a member of the inner circle of the KLA. The charisma of leaders such as Thaçi helped the KLA gain support from Kosovo Albanians.[^cite_note-Perritt2010-26] On 22 May 1993, together with [Ilaz Kodra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaz_Kodra), Thaçi led an [attack in Glogovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glogovac_attack) against Serbian police. The attack was a victory for the KLA; five officers were killed and two were injured.[^cite_note-27]

Thaçi (*[nom de guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noms_de_guerre)* "Gjarpëri" − *The Snake*)[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] was responsible for securing financial means and armaments, and training recruits in [Albania](738.md) to be dispatched to Kosovo. On 11 July 1997, Thaçi was tried in absentia and convicted by the district court in Pristina for terrorism associated with his activities in the KLA, and sentenced to 10 years in prison.[^cite_note-30]

Thaçi participated in the Battle of Rezalla, the KLA's first major battle, which occurred on 25 November. Many Yugoslav fighters were killed and their artillery and vehicles damaged after they were ambushed by KLA insurgents led by [Adem Jashari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adem_Jashari). As a result, Yugoslav forces retreated to the village of Llausha where they shot two Albanian teachers who worked in the primary school of the village.[^cite_note-31]

In March 1999, Thaçi participated in the [Rambouillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambouillet_Agreement) negotiations as the leader of the Kosovar Albanian team.[^cite_note-query.nytimes.com-32] Thaçi was perceived by western diplomats during the negotiations as the "voice of reason" within the KLA; his attendance demonstrated a willingness to accept autonomy for Kosovo within Serbia at a time when other rebel leaders rejected any solution short of full national independence.[^cite_note-query.nytimes.com-32]

Thaçi emerged from the final diplomatic settlement as the leader of the strongest faction within a KLA rife with factionalism. He moved quickly to consolidate power, unilaterally naming himself prime minister within a provisional government and allegedly ordering the assassination of the leaders of rival armed factions.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Organized crime allegations

Thaçi was alleged of having extensive criminal links.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-PhillipsBurns2012-16]<sup>: 211–212 </sup>[^cite_note-Boyle2014-17][^cite_note-KoehlerZurcher2003-18] During his time as head of the Kosovo Liberation Army, *[The Washington Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Times)* reported that the KLA was financing its activities by trafficking [heroin](14034.md) and [cocaine](7701.md) into western Europe.[^cite_note-36] The KLA received an estimated \$75–100 million from the [Albanian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_diaspora) in Europe and the United States.[^cite_note-Perritt2010-26] There is a possibility that among donors to the KLA were people involved in illegal activities such as drug trafficking; however, insufficient evidence exists to prove direct KLA involvement.[^cite_note-Perritt2010-26]

The [BBC](19344654.md) reported in 2000 that Thaçi was allegedly central to the criminal activities of the [Kosovo Protection Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Protection_Corps) (KPC), who reportedly extorted money from businessmen under the guise of "taxes" for his self-appointed government.[^cite_note-Kosovo-gripped-by-racketeers-37] While the KLA was officially disbanded at the end of armed conflict in Kosovo in 1999, the new Kosovo Protection Corps was composed primarily of former KLA fighters and the [Democratic Party of Kosovo](1260606.md) (PDK). The party was formed largely from the political leadership of the KLA. A near monopoly on the means of force, based on the absorption of the KLA into the KPC, allowed the Democratic Party of Kosovo to seize control of the machinery of government at the municipal level.[^cite_note-Kosovo-gripped-by-racketeers-37] The PDK regularly used violence and intimidation of political rivals to maintain local political control and protect criminal enterprises that depend upon cooperation from friendly local authorities.[^cite_note-38]

In 2001, the Democratic Party of Kosovo suffered electoral defeat in the first free elections in the province in 2001. The BBC said at the time, "The tumbling reputation of the former KLA was to have a disastrous effect on the PDK because of the perceived overlap between its political leadership and post-KLA organised crime."[^cite_note-39]

A 2008 analysis of organised crime in Kosovo prepared by the German intelligence service [BND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesnachrichtendienst) and a confidential report contracted by the [German military](288188.md) accused Thaçi, [Ramush Haradinaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramush_Haradinaj), and [Xhavit Haliti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhavit_Haliti), the majority leader of the Kosovo parliament, of far-reaching involvement in organised crime. The BND wrote: "The key players (including Haliti, Haradinaj, and Thaçi) are intimately involved in inter-linkages between politics, business, and organised crime structures in Kosovo."[^cite_note-Welt-40] The report accused Thaçi of leading a "criminal network operating throughout Kosovo" by the end of the 1990s.[^cite_note-Welt-40] The BND report accused Thaçi of having contacts with the Czech and Albanian mafias. It said that he, together with Haliti, ordered killings by a professional hit man, 'Afrimi', responsible for at least 11 contract murders.[^cite_note-Welt-40]

Thaçi has faced other accusations, such as the involvement in the lucrative [heroin trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroin_trade) in the 1990s when officers of the Kosovo Liberation Army and their backers were moving staggering quantities of narcotics through an underworld network into Central Europe. Police had long suspected that illegal narcotics were fueling the revolt in Kosovo.[^cite_note-41]

A report to the Council of Europe, written by [Dick Marty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Marty), issued on 15 December 2010[^cite_note-Paul-Lewis-14-december-2010-42] states that Hashim Thaçi was the leader of the "Drenica Group" in charge of trafficking organs taken from Serbian prisoners. As reported by several international,[^cite_note-43] Serbian,[^cite_note-44] Kosovan[^cite_note-45] and Albanian[^cite_note-46] news agencies, in an interview for Albanian television on 24 December 2010, Thaçi said he would publish information about Marty and Marty's collaborators' names.

BBC News reported having seen a draft of the Council of Europe document, and asserted that it named Thaçi "27 times in as many pages". They said the report charged the former KLA commanders with involvement in [organized crime](22625.md), including organ and [drug trafficking](218449.md).[^cite_note-Paul-Lewis-14-december-2010-42][^cite_note-47] In 2011, Marty clarified that his report implicated Thaçi's close associates but not Thaçi himself.[^cite_note-48]

## Prime Minister of Kosovo

### Victory in 2007 election and declaration of Kosovar independence



US president [George W. Bush](3414021.md) shakes hands with Kosovo president [Fatmir Sejdiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatmir_Sejdiu) (center) and Kosovo prime minister Hashim Thaçi (left) during a meeting in the [White House](33057.md) on 21 July 2008, after Kosovo declared independence.

[Elections were held in Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Kosovan_parliamentary_election) on 17 November 2007. After early results based on 90 percent of the votes, Hashim Thaçi, who was on course to gain 34 percent, claimed victory for the PDK. He stated his intention to declare independence without delay on 10 December, the date set by the [United Nations](31769.md) for the end of negotiations with [Serbia](29265.md). At 45 percent, [turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) was particularly low, as most Serbs refused to vote.[^cite_note-49]

On 19 November 2007, several [EU](9317.md) foreign ministers warned Thaçi and his allies against proceeding with their [declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence) without consultations. [Luxembourg](17515.md)'s [Jean Asselborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Asselborn) and [Sweden](5058739.md)'s [Carl Bildt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt) urged the PDK not to make any hasty moves, while the EU foreign policy chief [Javier Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Solana) stressed the importance of proper preparations prior to formal independence. After EU talks on Kosovo in [London](17867.md) on 19 November 2007, the UK's Europe minister, [Jim Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Murphy), said independence without foreign support could isolate the breakaway province.[^cite_note-50]



Thaçi and U.S. senator [Joe Biden](145422.md) with [Declaration of Independence of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence)

Thaçi was designated as the next leader of Kosovo's government on 11 December 2007 by the Kosovar president Fatmir Sejdiu and told to form a government "as soon as possible". His Democratic Party of Kosovo (PDK) began coalition talks with the [Democratic League of Kosovo](1256321.md) (LDK) as well as the [Alliance for New Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_New_Kosovo) (AKR). Those parties cumulatively controlled 75 of the assembly's 120 seats.[^cite_note-51]

On 9 January 2008, Thaçi was elected prime minister by parliament, with 85 votes in favor and 22 against. On this occasion, he stated his intention to achieve independence for Kosovo in the first half of 2008.[^cite_note-52]

On 16 February 2008, Thaçi announced that the next day would be key for "implementing the will of the citizens of Kosovo", strongly implying the province would declare independence from Serbia.[^cite_note-53] On 17 February, [Kosovo declared its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence) from Serbia. Thaçi became prime minister of the newly independent state.[^cite_note-54] On 6 June 2008, a gunman broke into Thaçi's home in [Pristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pristina). Thaçi was not in his home when the break-in occurred.[^cite_note-55]

## President of Kosovo

### 2016 presidential election

Thaçi was elected [president of Kosovo](1260820.md) in February 2016, and took office on 7 April 2016.[^cite_note-bbc_08-04-2016-56] In August 2019, Thaçi asserted that his nation would hold parliamentary elections on 6 October. The president declared that the nation was in need of a "functional and accountable government" capable enough to face challenges of the state and society.[^cite_note-57]

### Foreign policy



Thaçi during the [MSC](1464620.md) 2018

In January 2018, Thaçi said that Kosovo would have supported U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s decision to relocate the U.S. embassy from [Tel Aviv](31453.md) to [Jerusalem](16043.md), which would have made it the only Muslim-majority nation to do so. Thaçi told *[Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Express)* that if his country was given full United Nations membership, it would vote "all the time" with the U.S., even on the resolution motioned last month to protest Trump's decision. Thaçi met with Trump on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly in September 2017 in New York, inviting him to visit Pristina, the capital of Kosovo. According to local media, he said their meeting was "exceptionally friendly, warm."[^cite_note-58]

"The Head of State thanked President Trump for his personal support for Kosovo," his office said in a statement, calling the U.S. a "strategic partner" and saying the support of Washington was "crucial to the peace, stability and overall development of Kosovo and the region." Thaçi told Pristina-based broadcaster *[RTV21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTV21)*, "President Trump, like all other U.S. presidents, has a clear vision for Kosovo. It is unique support to our country. He said that Kosovo is a wonderful country and that we are a wonderful people."[^cite_note-59]

On 26 November 2019, [an earthquake struck Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Albania_earthquake). President Thaçi was part of a presidential delegation that visited the earthquake epicentre and expressed his condolences on behalf of Kosovo.[^cite_note-Mortimer-60] In 2019 he met with Trump's special envoy Grenell, and in February 2020 signed an agreement with Serbia's [president Vučić](13349267.md).[^cite_note-61]

## Resignation and arrest

On 24 April 2020, the [Kosovo Specialist Chambers and Specialist Prosecutor's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Specialist_Chambers_and_Specialist_Prosecutor's_Office) (KSC) located in [The Hague](30269.md) filed a ten-count indictment for the court's consideration, charging Hashim Thaçi, [Kadri Veseli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadri_Veseli) and others for [crimes against humanity](61099.md) and [war crimes](48626.md), including murder, enforced disappearance of persons, persecution, and torture.[^cite_note-SPO-62][^cite_note-BIRN-63][^cite_note-\:0-64] The indictment charged the suspects with approximately 100 murders of [Kosovo Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Albanians), [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Serbs), [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Kosovo), and political opponents.[^cite_note-SPO-62] According to the press release, the specialist prosecutor stated that it was necessary to make the issue public due to repeated efforts by Thaçi and Veseli to obstruct and undermine the [Kosovo Specialist Chambers](49113145.md).[^cite_note-BIRN-63] Thaçi was then on a diplomatic visit to the United States, but returned to Kosovo on learning of the indictment.[^cite_note-Trump-65]

On 5 November 2020, Thaçi announced his resignation to reporters "to protect the integrity of the presidency of Kosovo".[^cite_note-66] He was arrested the same day and transferred to The Hague.[^cite_note-Trump-65] Thaçi was replaced by the speaker of the [Assembly of Kosovo](1068656.md), [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md).[^cite_note-67]

## Trial and conviction

Thaçi made his initial court appearance on 9 November 2020; he pleaded not guilty.[^cite_note-68] His three co-defendants also pleaded not guilty.[^cite_note-69] The trial opened in April 2023.[^cite_note-\:2-12] In December 2024, the KSC filed a new case against Thaçi, in which he was charged with three counts of [obstruction](405736.md) of official persons in performing official duties, four counts of violating secrecy of proceedings and four counts of contempt of court over "alleged unlawful efforts to influence witness testimonies" during the trial.[^cite_note-70] In March 2025, Thaçi was temporarily released to visit his ailing father in Pristina.[^cite_note-71] After his father died shortly later, Thaçi was again temporarily released to allow him to visit his father's grave, having been previously barred from attending the funeral.[^cite_note-72]

On 16 September 2026, Thaçi was convicted and sentenced to 25 years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-BBC_Kosovo_former_pres_sentenced_25-6] Based on evidence from 273 witnesses, 5,467 pieces of evidence,[^cite_note-KSC_Thaci_found_guilty-7] the KSC found Thaçi guilty together with Veseli, Selemi, and Krasniqi of the murder of 96 political opponents and alleged collaborators with Serbian forces in the 1990s, the illegal detention of 385 people, the torture of 303 and the cruel treatment of 49 as part of a [joint criminal enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_criminal_enterprise). He and his co-accused were cleared of six charges of crimes against humanity.[^cite_note-Reuters_Thaci_sentenced_25-14] The KSC stated that Thaçi played a key role in "formulating and implementing the common purpose" of the crimes, and that he "personally participated in crimes, including the arrest, arbitrary detention and interrogation of 13 parliamentarians and the arrest, detention, transfer and murder of Behajdin Allaqi." The KSC found that Thaçi had made false statements about victims and detainees and gave "false assurances of compliance with [international humanitarian law](1093604.md) on behalf of the KLA."[^cite_note-KSC_Thaci_found_guilty-7]
