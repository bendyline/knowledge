**Sevastopol** ([/ˌsɛvəˈstoʊpəl, səˈvæstəpoʊl/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*SEV-ə-STOH-pəl, sə-VAS-tə-pohl*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-5] sometimes written **Sebastopol**, is the largest city in [Crimea](163045.md) and a major port on the [Black Sea](3386.md). Due to its strategic location and the navigability of the city's harbours, Sevastopol has been an important [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Sevastopol) and [naval base](59166849.md) throughout its history. Since the city's founding by [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) in 1783, it has been a major base for Russia's [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet). During the [Cold War](325329.md) of the 20th century, it was a [closed city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_city). The total administrative area is 864 square kilometres (334 sq mi) and includes a significant amount of rural land. The urban population, largely concentrated around [Sevastopol Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevastopol_Bay), is 479,394,[^cite_note-stats2021-6] and the total population is 547,820.[^cite_note-2021Census-7]

Sevastopol, along with the rest of Crimea, is internationally recognised as part of [Ukraine](31750.md), and under the Ukrainian legal framework, it is administratively one of two [cities with special status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_with_special_status) (the other being [Kyiv](585629.md)). However, it has been occupied by [Russia](25391.md) since 27 February 2014, before Russia [annexed Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) on 18 March 2014 and gave it the status of a [federal city of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia). Both Ukraine and Russia consider the city administratively separate from the [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea) and the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)), respectively. The city's population has an ethnic [Russian](59510.md) majority and a substantial minority of [Ukrainians](145144.md).

Sevastopol's unique naval and maritime features have been the basis for a robust economy. The city enjoys mild winters and moderately warm summers, characteristics that help make it a popular [seaside resort](293990.md) and tourist destination, mainly for visitors from the [former Soviet republics](36870.md). The city is also an important centre for [marine biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology) research. In particular, the military has studied and trained [dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin) in the city for [military use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_marine_mammal) since the 1960s.[^cite_note-8]

The name of Sevastopolis was originally chosen following the same [etymological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) trend as other cities in the Crimean peninsula; it was intended to express its ancient Greek origins. It is a compound of the Greek adjective, σεβαστός (*[sebastós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastos)*, [Byzantine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greek_language) pronunciation: [\[sevasˈtos\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); 'venerable') and the noun πόλις (*pólis*, 'city'). Σεβαστός is the traditional Greek equivalent (see [Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_\(name\))) of the Roman honorific *[Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_\(honorific\))*, originally given to the first emperor of the Roman Empire, [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) and later awarded as a title to his successors.

The city was probably named after Empress ("[Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_\(honorific\))") [Catherine II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) who founded Sevastopol in 1783. She visited the city in 1787, accompanied by [Joseph II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), the Emperor of Austria, and other foreign dignitaries.

In the west of the city, there are well-preserved ruins of the ancient Greek port city of [Chersonesos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesos_Taurica), founded in the 5th[^cite_note-A1-9] century BC by settlers from [Heraclea Pontica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclea_Pontica). This name means "peninsula", reflecting its immediate location. It is not related to the ancient Greek name for the Crimean Peninsula as a whole: *Chersonēsos [Taurikē](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurica)* ("the [Taurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauri) Peninsula").

### Founding of Russian city



"Soldier and Sailor" Memorial to Heroic Defenders of Sevastopol



The [Monument to the Sunken Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Sunken_Ships), dedicated to ships [scuttled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuttling) during the siege of Sevastopol during the Crimean War, designed by [Amandus Adamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amandus_Adamson)



Sevastopol in 1889, [Department of Image Collections](https://www.nga.gov/research/library/imagecollections/features/watsons_russia.html), National Gallery of Art Library, Washington, D.C.

Sevastopol was founded in June 1783, in the area of the ancient but ruined and abandoned Greek city of [Chersonesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesus_\(Crimea\)), as a base for a naval squadron under the name Akhtiar[^cite_note-17] (*White Cliff*),[^cite_note-bse-18] by [Rear Admiral Thomas MacKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_MacKenzie_\(Russian_admiral\)) ("Foma Fomich Makenzi"), a native [Scot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) in Russian service; soon after, the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) annexed the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate). Five years earlier, [Alexander Suvorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Suvorov) had ordered that earthworks be erected along the harbour and Russian troops be placed there. The Crimean Tatar version of this name is now written *[Aqyar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Aqyar)*.

In February 1784, [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) ordered [Grigory Potyomkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Potyomkin) to build a fortress there and call it Sevastopol. The realisation of the initial building plans fell to Captain [Fyodor Ushakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Ushakov) who in 1788 was named commander of the [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Sevastopol) and of the [Black Sea squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet).[^cite_note-19] The city was established on the western shore of Southern Bay which branches away from the bigger [Sevastopol Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevastopol_Bay). The ruins of ancient Chersonesus were situated to the west. The newly built settlement became an important [naval base](291782.md) and later a commercial [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport). In 1797, under an edict issued by Emperor [Paul I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_I_of_Russia), the military stronghold was again renamed Akhtiar. Finally, on 29 April (10 May), 1826, the Senate returned the city's name to Sevastopol. In 1803 to 1864 along with [Mykolaiv](901495.md) the city was part of Nikolayev–Sevastopol Military Governorate. The town had 3,000 inhabitants by the 1840s.[^cite_note-20]

### Crimean War

Main article: [Siege of Sevastopol (1854–1855)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_\(1854%E2%80%931855\))



British Memorial Complex, Sevastopol, [Department of Image Collections](https://www.nga.gov/research/library/imagecollections/features/watsons_russia.html), National Gallery of Art Library, Washington, D.C.

From 1853 to 1856, the Crimean peninsula's strategic position in controlling the Black Sea caused it to be the site of the principal engagements of the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), where Russia lost to a French-led alliance.[^cite_note-21]

After a minor skirmish at Köstence (now [Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba)), the allied commanders decided to attack Sevastopol as Russia's main naval base in the Black Sea. After extended preparations, allied forces landed on the peninsula in September 1854 and marched to a point south of Sevastopol after winning the [Battle of the Alma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Alma) on 20 September. The Russians counterattacked on 25 October in what became the [Battle of Balaclava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Balaclava) and were repulsed, but the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Victorian_Era)'s forces were seriously depleted as a result. A second Russian counterattack, [at Inkerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Inkerman) in November, ended in a stalemate as well. The front settled into the [siege of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_\(1854%E2%80%931855\)), involving brutal conditions for troops on both sides.

Sevastopol finally fell after eleven months, after the French [had assaulted Fort Malakoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Malakoff). Isolated and facing a bleak prospect of invasion by the West if the war continued, Russia [sued for peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suing_for_peace) in March 1856. France and Britain welcomed the development, owing to the conflict's domestic unpopularity. The [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1856\)), signed on 30 March 1856, ended the war and forbade Russia from basing warships in the Black Sea.[^cite_note-22] This hampered the Russians during the [Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)) and in the aftermath of that conflict, Russia moved to reconstitute its naval strength and fortifications in the Black Sea.

### World War II

Main article: [Siege of Sevastopol (1941–1942)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_\(1941%E2%80%931942\))

During [World War II](32927.md), Sevastopol withstood intensive bombardment by the Germans in 1941–42, supported by their Italian and Romanian allies during the [Battle of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sevastopol). German forces used [railway artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_artillery)—including history's largest-ever calibre railway artillery piece in battle, the 80-cm calibre *[Schwerer Gustav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerer_Gustav)*—and [specialised mobile heavy mortars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ger%C3%A4t) to destroy Sevastopol's extremely heavy fortifications, such as the [Maxim Gorky Fortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Gorky_Fortresses). After fierce fighting, which lasted for 250 days,[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] the fortress city finally fell to Axis forces in July 1942.[^cite_note-26] It was intended to be renamed to "*Theodorichshafen*"[^cite_note-27] (in reference to [Theodoric the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great) and the fact that Crimea had been home to Germanic [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) until the 18th or 19th century) in the event of a German victory against the Soviet Union, and like the rest of Crimea was designated for future colonisation by the Third Reich. It was liberated by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) on 9 May 1944 and was awarded the [Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)) title a year later.

### Part of the Ukrainian SSR

During the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) era, Sevastopol became a so-called "[closed city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_city)". This meant that any non-residents had to apply to the authorities for a temporary permit to visit the city.

On 29 October 1948, the Presidium of the Supreme Council of the Russian SFSR issued an *ukaz* (order) which confirmed the special status of the city.[^cite_note-myths-28] Soviet academic publications since 1954, including the *[Great Soviet Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Soviet_Encyclopedia)*, indicated that Sevastopol, [Crimean Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Oblast) was part of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-bse-18]

In 1954, under [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), both Sevastopol and the remainder of the Crimean peninsula were administratively [transferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_transfer_of_Crimea) from being territories within the Russian SFSR to being territories administered by the Ukrainian SSR. Administratively, Sevastopol was a municipality excluded from the adjacent [Crimean Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Oblast). The territory of the municipality was 863.5 km<sup>2</sup> and it was further subdivided into four raions (districts). Besides the City of Sevastopol proper, it also included two towns—Balaklava (having had no status until 1957), Inkerman, urban-type settlement Kacha, and 29 villages.[^cite_note-YavlinskyUkr-30]

For the 1955 Ukrainian parliamentary elections on 27 February, Sevastopol was split into two electoral districts, Stalinsky and Korabelny (initially requested three Stalinsky, Korabelny, and Nakhimovsky).[^cite_note-myths-28] Eventually, Sevastopol received two [people's deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Deputy_of_Ukraine) of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) elected to the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md), A. Korovchenko and M. Kulakov.[^cite_note-myths-28][^cite_note-31]

In 1957, the town of [Balaklava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaklava) was incorporated into Sevastopol.

### Part of Ukraine



The Black Sea Fleet Museum

Following [Ukraine](31750.md)'s declaration of [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ukraine#Independence) from the USSR in 1991, Sevastopol became the principal base of the [Ukrainian navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_navy). As the key naval base of the former Soviet [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet), it was a source of tensions for [Russia–Ukraine relations](17446303.md) until a [set-term lease agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_Treaty_on_the_Status_and_Conditions_of_the_Black_Sea_Fleet) was signed in 1997.

On 10 July 1993, the [Russian parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_parliament) passed a resolution declaring Sevastopol to be "a federal Russian city".[^cite_note-doyle-32] At the time, many supporters of President [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin) had ceased taking part in the parliament's work.[^cite_note-33] On 20 July 1993, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) denounced the decision of the Russian parliament. According to [Anatoliy Zlenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Zlenko), it was the first time that the council had to review and qualify actions of a legislative body.[^cite_note-myths-28]

On 14 April 1993, the Presidium of the Crimean Parliament called for the creation of the presidential post of the Crimean Republic. A week later, the Russian deputy, Valentin Agafonov, said that [Russia](25391.md) was ready to supervise a referendum on Crimean independence and include the republic as a separate entity in the [CIS](36870.md). On 28 July 1993, one of the leaders of the Russian Society of Crimea, Viktor Prusakov, said that his organisation was ready for an armed mutiny and the establishment of Russian administration of Sevastopol.

In September, the commander of the joint Russian-Ukrainian Black Sea Fleet, [Eduard Baltin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Baltin?action=edit\&redlink=1), accused Ukraine of converting some of his fleet and conducting an armed assault on his personnel and threatened to take countermeasures placing the fleet on alert. (In June 1992, the Russian president Yeltsin and the Ukrainian president [Leonid Kravchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kravchuk) had agreed to divide the former Soviet Black Sea Fleet between Russia and Ukraine. Eduard Baltin had been appointed commander of the Black Sea Fleet by Yeltsin and Kravchuk on 15 January 1993.)

The Moscow mayor [Yury Luzhkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Luzhkov) claimed the city, and in December 1996, the Russian [Federation Council](1268578.md) officially endorsed the claim, threatening negotiations. In response, Ukraine proposed a "special partnership" with NATO in January 1997.[^cite_note-34]

In May 1997, Russia and Ukraine signed the [Russian–Ukrainian Friendship Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian%E2%80%93Ukrainian_Friendship_Treaty), ruling out Moscow's territorial claims to Ukraine.[^cite_note-35] This was followed by the [Partition Treaty on the Status and Conditions of the Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_Treaty_on_the_Status_and_Conditions_of_the_Black_Sea_Fleet) on 28 May 1997. A separate agreement established the terms of a long-term lease of land, facilities, and resources in Sevastopol and the Crimea by Russia. Russia kept its naval base, with around 15,000 troops stationed in Sevastopol.[^cite_note-36]



Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) with Ukrainian president [Leonid Kuchma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kuchma) on board the Black Sea Fleet's flagship in July 2001

The ex-Soviet Black Sea Fleet and its facilities were divided between Russia's Black Sea Fleet and the [Ukrainian Naval Forces](1590554.md). The two navies co-used some of the city's harbours and piers, while others were demilitarised or used by either country. Sevastopol remained the location of the Russian Black Sea Fleet headquarters, and the Ukrainian Naval Forces Headquarters were also located in the city. A judicial row periodically continued over the naval [hydrographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography) infrastructure both in Sevastopol and on the Crimean coast (especially [lighthouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouses) historically maintained by the Soviet and Russian Navy and also used for civil navigation support).

As in the rest of Crimea, Russian remained the predominant language of the city, although following the independence of Ukraine there were some attempts at [Ukrainisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainisation), with very little success. Russian society in general and even some outspoken government representatives never accepted the loss of Sevastopol and tended to regard it as temporarily separated from Russia.[^cite_note-37]

In July 2009, the chairman of the [Sevastopol City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevastopol_City_Council), Valeriy Saratov ([Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions)),[^cite_note-38] said that Ukraine should increase the amount of compensation it is paying to the city of Sevastopol for hosting the foreign Russian Black Sea Fleet, instead of requesting such compensation from the Russian government and the Russian Ministry of Defence in particular.[^cite_note-39]

On 27 April 2010, Russia and Ukraine ratified the [Russian Ukrainian Naval Base for Gas treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ukrainian_Naval_Base_for_Gas_treaty), which extended the Russian Navy's lease of the Crimean facilities for 25 years after 2017 (through 2042) with the option to prolong the lease in five-year extensions. The ratification process in the [Ukrainian parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_parliament) encountered stiff opposition and even resulted in a brawl in the parliament chamber. Eventually, the treaty was ratified by a 52% majority vote—236 of 450. The [Russian Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Duma) ratified the treaty by a 98% majority.[^cite_note-40]

### Occupation and annexation by Russia

Further information: [Russian occupation of Crimea](71944314.md)

On 23 February 2014, a pro-Russian rally took place in Nakhimov Square declaring allegiance to Russia and protesting against the new government in [Kyiv](585629.md) following the [overthrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) of the president, [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych).[^cite_note-41] On 27 February, pro-Russian militia, including Russian troops, seized control of government buildings in Crimea, and by 28 February, controlled other strategic locations such as the military airport in Sevastopol.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

On 16 March 2014, an internationally unrecognised [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_status_referendum) was held in Sevastopol with official results claiming an 89.51% turnout and 95.6% of voters choosing to join Russia. Ukraine and almost all other countries of the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) consider the referendum illegal and illegitimate.[^cite_note-sevsovet-44][^cite_note-45]

On 18 March, [Russia annexed Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), incorporating the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)) and [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of Sevastopol as [federal subjects of Russia](701438.md).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] However, the annexation remains internationally unrecognised, with most countries recognising Sevastopol as a [city with special status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_with_special_status) within Ukraine.[^cite_note-48] While Russia has taken *de facto* control of Sevastopol and Crimea, the international community considers the area as part of Ukraine.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Geography



Satellite image of the Sevastopol area.



A view of the Bay of Sevastopol.



[Cape Fiolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fiolent), on the southwestern coast of Sevastopol.

The city of Sevastopol is located at the southwestern tip of the Crimean Peninsula in a headland known as [Heracles Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles_Peninsula) on a coast of the [Black Sea](3386.md). The city is designated a special city-region of Ukraine which besides the city itself includes several of its outlying settlements. The city itself is concentrated mostly in the western portion of the region and around the long [Bay of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Sevastopol). This bay is a [ria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ria), a river canyon drowned by [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) sea-level rise, and the outlet of [Chorna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorna_River_\(Crimea\)). Away in a remote location southeast of Sevastopol is located the former city of [Balaklava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaklava) (since 1957 incorporated within Sevastopol), the bay of which in the Soviet era served as a main port for the Soviet diesel-powered submarines.

The coastline of the region is mostly rocky, in a series of smaller bays, a great number of which are located within the Bay of Sevastopol. The biggest of them are Southern Bay (within the Bay of Sevastopol), Archer Bay, a gulf complex that consists of Deergrass Bay, the Bay of Cossack, Salty Bay, and many others. There are over thirty bays in the immediate region.

Three rivers flow through the region: the Belbek, Chorna, and Kacha. All three mountain chains of the [Crimean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Mountains) are represented in Sevastopol, the southern chain by the Balaklava Highlands, the inner chain by the Mekenziev Mountains, and the outer chain by the Kara-Tau Upland (Black Mountain).

### Climate

Sevastopol has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*). Due to the summer mean straddling 22 °C (72 °F) it borders on a four-season [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), with cold winters and warm summers.

The average yearly temperature is 15–16 °C (59–61 °F) during the day and around 9 °C (48 °F) at night. In the coldest months, January and February, the average temperature is 5–6 °C (41–43 °F) during the day and around 1 °C (34 °F) at night. In the warmest months, July and August, the average temperature is around 26 °C (79 °F) during the day and around 19 °C (66 °F) at night. Generally, the summer/holiday season lasts 5 months, from around mid-May and into September, with the temperature often reaching 20 °C (68 °F) or more in the first half of October.

The average annual temperature of the sea is 14.2 °C (58 °F), ranging from 7 °C (45 °F) in February to 24 °C (75 °F) in August. From June to September, the average sea temperature is greater than 20 °C (68 °F). In the second half of May and the first half of October; the average sea temperature is about 17 °C (63 °F). The average rainfall is about 400 millimetres (16 in) per year. There are about 2,345 hours of sunshine duration per year.[^cite_note-meteoweb-52]

## Government and politics

Since 18 March 2014, when Russia [annexed Crimea, including Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), the *[de facto](43594.md)* government and politics of Sevastopol has been that of a [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of Russia.

### Government

Executive

The head of the executive branch in the city is the [Governor of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Sevastopol_\(Russia\)). According to the city charter, amended on 29 November 2016, the governor is elected in a direct election for a term of five years and no more than two consecutive terms.[^cite_note-54] The current governor is [Mikhail Razvozhayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Razvozhayev).

Legislature

During the [annexation of Ukrainian Crimea by Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), the pro-Russian [Sevastopol City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevastopol_City_Council) threw its support behind Russian citizen [Alexei Chaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Chaly) as a "people's mayor" and said it would not recognise orders from [Kyiv](585629.md).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] After Russia annexed Crimea, the [Legislative Assembly of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Sevastopol) replaced the City Council.

Administrative and municipal divisions

Main article: [Administrative and municipal divisions of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_and_municipal_divisions_of_Sevastopol)

Within the Russian [municipal framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the territory of the federal city of Sevastopol is divided into nine [municipal okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_okrug) and the town of [Inkerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkerman). While individual municipal divisions are contained within the borders of the administrative districts, they are not otherwise related to the administrative districts.

### Politics

Sevastopol [elects one Deputy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevastopol_constituency) to the Russian [State Duma](1010187.md). The current Deputy, [Tatyana Lobach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Lobach) of [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia), a former Deputy Chairwoman of the [Legislative Assembly of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Sevastopol), was elected at the [2021 Russian legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_legislative_election).

### Life expectancy

In 2015, Sevastopol had the largest decrease in [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) at birth among all regions of Russia.\
In 2020, after beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic, Sevastopol became the only region of Russia where there was increase of life expectancy.\
In 2021, average life expectancy at birth in Sevastopol was 72.25 years (67.87 for males and 76.43 for females).[^cite_note-rosstat-62][^cite_note-uiiss-63]

There are many historical buildings in the central and eastern parts of the city and Balaklava, some of which are architectural monuments. The Western districts have modern architecture. More recently, numerous skyscrapers have been built. Balaklava Bayfront Plaza (on hold), currently under construction, will be one of the tallest buildings in Ukraine, at 173 m (568 ft) with 43 floors.[^cite_note-64]

After the [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md) the city's monument to [Petro Konashevych-Sahaidachny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Konashevych-Sahaidachny) was removed and handed over to [Kharkiv](2788558.md).[^cite_note-65]
