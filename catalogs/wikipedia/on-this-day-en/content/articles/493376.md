**Shan State** ([Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_language): မိူင်းတႆး, *Möng Tai*; [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language): ရှမ်းပြည်နယ်, pronounced [\[ʃáɰ̃ pjìnɛ̀\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Burmese)) is a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Myanmar) of [Myanmar](19457.md). Shan State borders China (Yunnan) to the north, Laos ([Louang Namtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louang_Namtha_Province) and [Bokeo Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokeo_Province)) to the east, and Thailand ([Chiang Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai_province), [Chiang Mai](211630.md) and [Mae Hong Son provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Hong_Son_province)) to the south, and five administrative divisions of Myanmar in the west ([Kachin State](493369.md), [Mandalay Region](2357817.md), [Kayin State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayin_State), [Kayah State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayah_State), and [Sagaing Region](493361.md)). The largest of the 14 administrative divisions by land area, Shan State covers 155,800 km<sup>2</sup>, almost a quarter of the total area of Myanmar. The state gets its name from the Burmese name for the Tai peoples: "[Shan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people)". The Tai (Shan) constitute the majority among several ethnic groups that inhabit the area. Shan State is largely rural, with only three cities of significant size: [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio), [Kengtung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengtung), and the capital, [Taunggyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunggyi).[^cite_note-5] Taunggyi is 150.7 kilometres (93.6 mi) northeast of the nation's capital [Naypyitaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyitaw).

The Shan state, with many ethnic groups, is home to several armed ethnic groups. While the [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council) has signed ceasefire agreements with most groups, vast areas of the state, especially those east of the [Salween River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salween_River), remain outside the central government's control, and in recent years have come under heavy ethnic-[Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Chinese) economic and political influence. Other areas are under the control of military groups such as the [Shan State Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_Army-South).

According to data from the [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime) (UNODC), Shan State is the region that produces the most [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) in Myanmar, though production declined through the mid-to-late 2010s.[^cite_note-6]

## Names and etymology

**Muang Dai** ([Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_language): မိူင်းတႆး) is the native name for the region as well as a term used for the Tai-inhabited parts of Myanmar outside of Shan State. *Muang* (မိူင်း) means country in Tai languages and is used before the names of other countries, e.g. *Muang Maan* (Myanmar).[^cite_note-7]

**Shan Pyi** ([Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language): ရှမ်းပြည်) derives from a Burmese corruption of the name *Siam* which is an old name for [Lower Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Thailand). *Pyi* is a Burmese word meaning country and thus *Shan Pyi* can be translated as Shan State. Officially, the region is called *Shan State* in English and *Shan Pyine* in Burmese but the *ne* in often dropped in colloquial speech.

**Shanland**[^cite_note-8] alongside *Muang Dai* are often used in political and nationalist dialogue when referring to the region.[^cite_note-9]

## History

Shan State is the unitary successor state to the Burmese Shan States, the [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states) that were under some degree of control of the [Irrawaddy valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_river)-based Burmese kingdoms.

Historical [Tai-Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Nua_people) states extended well beyond the Burmese Shan States, ranging from full-fledged kingdoms of [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) in the northwest to [Lan Xang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Xang) in the east, to [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na) and [Ayutthaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom) in the southeast, as well as several petty princely states in between, covering present-day northern [Chin State](493366.md), northern [Sagaing Region](493361.md), [Kachin State](493369.md), [Kayah State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayah_State) in Myanmar as well as [Laos](17752.md), [Thailand](30128.md) and the southwestern part of [Yunnan](166410.md), China. The definition of Burmese Shan States does not include the [Ava Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ava_Kingdom) and the [Hanthawaddy kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanthawaddy_kingdom) of the 13th to 16th centuries, although the founders of these kingdoms were Burmanized Shans and Monized Shans, respectively.

### Early history

The founding of Shan States inside the present-day boundaries of Burma began during the [Pagan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_Kingdom) in the [Shan Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_Hills) and accelerated after 1287, when the Pagan Kingdom fell to the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols). The Tai people, who came south with the Mongols, stayed, and quickly came to dominate much of northern to the eastern arc of Burma—from northwestern [Sagaing Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagaing_Division) to [Kachin Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Hills) to the present-day Shan Hills. The most powerful Shan states were [Mong Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongyang_State) (Mohnyin) and [Mong Kawng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongkawng) (Mogaung) in present-day Kachin State, followed by [Hsenwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsenwi_State) (Theinni), [Hsipaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsipaw) (Thibaw) and [Mong Mit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongmit) (Momeik) in present-day northern Shan State.[^cite_note-jf-10] Smaller Shan states, such as [Kale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalay) in northwestern [Sagaing Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagaing_Division), [Bhamo](2150645.md) in Kachin State, [Yawnghwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yawnghwe) (Nyaungshwe) and [Kengtung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengtung_State) (Kyaingtong) in Shan State, and [Mong Pai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Pai) (Mobye) in [Kayah State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayah_State), played a precarious game of paying allegiance to more powerful states, sometimes simultaneously.

The newly founded Shan States were multi-ethnic, and included other ethnic minorities such as the [Chin](1828130.md), the [Kachin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_people), the [Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_people), the [Ta'ang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaung_people), the Lisu, the Lahu, the Pa O, and the [Kayah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayah_people). Although Burmanised Shans founded the [Ava Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ava_Kingdom) that ruled central Burma, other Shan states, Mohnyin in particular, constantly raided Ava territories throughout the years. A Mohnyin-led [Confederation of Shan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_Shan_States) finally conquered Ava in 1527.[^cite_note-Htin-11]<sup>: 95 </sup>

### Toungoo and Konbaung periods (1555–1885)



Shan States after 1557, then inside Bayinnaung's empire

In 1555, King [Bayinnaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayinnaung) dislodged Shan king [Sithu Kyawhtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sithu_Kyawhtin) from Ava. By 1557 he went on to conquer all of what would become known as the Burmese Shan states under his rule, from the Assamese border in the northwest to those in [Kachin Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Hills) and [Shan Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_Hills), including the two most powerful Shan states, Mohnyin and Mogaung.[^cite_note-Phayre-12]<sup>: 108–109 </sup> The Shan states were reduced to the status of governorships, but the [Saophas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saopha) were permitted to retain their royal regalia and their feudal rights over their own subjects. Bayinnaung introduced Burmese customary law and prohibited all human and animal sacrifices. He also required the sons of Saophas to reside in the Burmese king's palace, essentially hostages, in order to ensure the good conduct of their fathers and to receive training in Burmese court life. Burmese kings continued this policy until 1885 when the kingdom fell to the British.[^cite_note-Htin-11]<sup>: 117–118 </sup> (The northernmost Shan states, in Yunnan, had already fallen to the Chinese [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) by the middle of the 15th century.[^cite_note-13])

The reach of the Burmese sovereign waxed and waned with the ability of each Burmese monarch. Shan states became briefly independent following the collapse of the first Toungoo dynasty, in 1599. The Restored Toungoo dynasty under King [Nyaungyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaungyan_Min) and King [Anaukpetlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaukpetlun) recovered the Shan states, including the two strongest—Monhyin and Mogaung by 1605 and Lan Na by 1615.[^cite_note-Phayre-12]<sup>: 108 </sup> In the early-18th century, the rule of Burmese monarchs declined rapidly and by the 1730s, the northernmost Shan states, many of which had paid dual tribute to China and Burma, had been annexed by the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) of China. The annexed border states ranged from Mogaung and Bhamo in present-day Kachin State to Hsenwi and Kengtung in present-day Shan State to [Sipsongpanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Hung) (Kyaingyun) in present-day [Xishuangbanna Dai Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xishuangbanna_Dai_Autonomous_Prefecture), Yunnan.[^cite_note-Phayre-12]<sup>: 191–192, 201 </sup>

In the middle of the 18th century, the Burmese [Konbaung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_dynasty)'s reassertion of the easternmost boundaries of Burmese Shan states led to war with China. It made [four separate invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Burmese_War_\(1765%E2%80%931769\)) of Burma from 1765 to 1769, during the Sino-Burmese War. The Burmese success in repelling Chinese forces laid the foundation for the present-day boundary between Burma and China.

The present-day boundary of southern Shan State vis-à-vis Thailand was formed shortly thereafter. Burma lost southern Lan Na (Chiang Mai) in 1776 and northern Lan Na ([Chiang Saen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Saen)) in 1786 to a resurgent Bangkok-based Siam,[^cite_note-dkw-125-14] ending more than two centuries of Burmese suzerainty over the region. It retained only Kengtung on the Burmese side. The southern border of Shan State remained contested in the following years. Siam invaded Kengtung in [1803–1804](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese%E2%80%93Siamese_War_\(1803%E2%80%9308\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [1852–1854](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese%E2%80%93Siamese_War_\(1849%E2%80%9355\)), and Burma invaded Lan Na in [1797](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese%E2%80%93Siamese_War_\(1797\)) and 1804. Siam occupied Kengtung during [World War II](32927.md) (1942–1945).

Throughout the Burmese feudal era, Shan states supplied much manpower in the service of Burmese kings. Without Shan manpower, the [Burmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamar_people) alone would not have been able to achieve their victories in Lower Burma, Siam, and elsewhere. Shans were a major part of Burmese forces in the [First Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Burmese_War) of 1824–1826, and fought valiantly—a fact that the British commanders acknowledged.[^cite_note-Myint-U-15]<sup>: 123–124 </sup>

After the [Second Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Burmese_War) of 1852, the Burmese kingdom was reduced to Upper Burma alone. The Shan states—especially that east of the [Salween River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salween_River), were essentially autonomous entities, paying token tribute to the king. In 1875, King [Mindon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindon_Min), to avoid certain defeat, ceded [Karenni states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_States), long part of Shan states, to the British.[^cite_note-Htin-11]<sup>: 177 </sup> When the last king of Burma, [Thibaw Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thibaw_Min), ascended the throne in 1878, the rule of central government was so weak that Thibaw had to send thousands of troops to tame a rebellion in the Shan state of Mongnai and other eastern Shan states for the remainder of his six-year reign.[^cite_note-Myint-U-15]<sup>: 161 </sup>

### Colonial period (1886–1948)



Shan trader, circa 1900

On 28 November 1885, the British captured [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay), officially ending the [Third Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Burmese_War) in 11 days. But it took until 1890 for the British to subdue all of the various Shan states. Under the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) administration, established in 1887, the Shan states were ruled by their [saophas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saopha) as [feudatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudatory) [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states) of the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Crown). The British placed Kachin Hills inside Mandalay Division and northwestern Shan areas under Sagaing Division. In October 1922, the Shan and the [Karenni states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_States) were merged to create the [Federated Shan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_Shan_States),[^cite_note-md-16] under a commissioner who also administered the [Wa States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_States). This arrangement survived the constitutional changes of 1923 and 1937.

During World War II, most of the Shan states were occupied by the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan). Chinese [Kuomintang](16903.md) forces who entered Burmese territory came down to northeastern Shan states to face the Japanese. Thai forces, allied with the Japanese, occupied [Kengtung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengtung) and [surrounding areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharat_Thai_Doem) in 1942, [annexing](18950961.md) the territory to the Thai state.[^cite_note-17]

After the war, the British returned, while many Chinese KMT forces stayed inside Burmese Shan states. Negotiations leading to independence at the [Panglong Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panglong_Conference) in February 1947 secured a unitary Shan State, including former Wa states, but without the Karenni states.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] More importantly, Shan State gained the right of secession in 10 years from independence.

### Independence (1948–2010)

Soon after gaining independence in January 1948, the central government led by [U Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Nu) faced several armed rebellions. The most serious was the Chinese Nationalist KMT invasion of Shan State in 1950. Driven out by the Chinese Communist forces, Nationalist KMT armies planned to use the region east of the Salween River as a base from which to regain their homeland. In March 1953, the KMT forces, with US assistance, were on the verge of taking the entire Shan State and within a day's march of the state capital [Taunggyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunggyi).[^cite_note-Myint-U-15]<sup>: 274 </sup> The Burmese army drove the invaders east across the Salween, but much of the KMT army and their [progeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offspring) have remained in the eastern Shan State under various guises to the present day. The Burmese army's heavy-handedness fueled resentment.[^cite_note-Myint-U-15]<sup>: 274 </sup>

In 1961, Shan saophas led by [Sao Shwe Thaik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_Shwe_Thaik), the first president of Burma and saopha of [Yawnghwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaung_Shwe), proposed a new federal system of government for greater autonomy, although the Shans had the constitutional right to secede. Though Shan leaders promised not to exercise the right, the Burmese army led by Gen. [Ne Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne_Win) thought the proposal was secessionist.[^cite_note-Myint-U-15]<sup>: 274 </sup> Gen. [Ne Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne_Win)'s [coup d'état](25914154.md) in 1962 brought an end to the Burmese experiment with democracy and with it, the call for greater autonomy for ethnic minorities. The coup fueled the Shan rebellion, started in 1958 by a small group called *Num Hsük Han* ('young warriors'), now joined by the [Shan State Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_Army) (SSA).

By the early-1960s, eastern Shan State festered with several insurgencies and warlords, and it emerged as a major opium-growing area, part of the so-called [Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_\(Southeast_Asia\)). Narcotics trafficking became a vital source of revenue for all insurgencies. Major forces consisted of the SSA and the [Communist Party of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Burma) (CPB), as well as those of the drug lords [Khun Sa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khun_Sa), and [Lo Hsing Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Hsing_Han). By the mid-1960s, CPB had begun receiving open support from China. Thailand also began a decades-long policy of support for non-communist Burmese rebels. Families of insurgent leaders were allowed to live in Thailand, where insurgent armies were free to buy arms, ammunition, and other supplies.[^cite_note-Myint-U-15]<sup>: 299 </sup>

In the late-1980s and 1990s, the [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council) signed ceasefire agreements with 17 groups, including all major players in Shan State. An uneasy truce has ensued, but all forces remain heavily armed. Today, the 20,000-strong [United Wa State Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Wa_State_Army) (UWSA) is the largest armed group, and is heavily involved in the narcotics trade. Under the 2008 Constitution, endorsed by the Burmese [junta](216815.md), certain UWSA-controlled areas were given the status of an autonomous region.[^cite_note-irrawaddy-20]

In recent decades, Chinese state and ethnic Chinese involvement in Shan State has deepened. Hundreds of thousands of immigrants from China have come to work in Upper Burma since the 1990s.[^cite_note-pks-21][^cite_note-22] Chinese investment in the state has funded everything from hydro power and mining projects to rubber plantations, logging, and wildlife trade.[^cite_note-23]  Wa and Kokang regions, led by local leaders, use the [Chinese yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_yuan) and operate on [Chinese Standard Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Standard_Time).

### New constitution (2010–present)



Kyaukme Train Station in 2017

In the general election of November 2010, 117 seats were open for Shan State Parliament (or Shan [State Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Hluttaw)): two each for 55 townships and seven seats for different ethnic constituencies. But elections for [Mongmao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongmao), Pangwaun, Pangkham, Namphan, and Mong La Township Constituencies were canceled. Fifty-four candidates from [Union Solidarity and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party) (USDP), 31 from [Shan Nationalities Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_Nationalities_Democratic_Party) (SNDP), six from PaO National Organization, four from Ta'ang (Palaung) National Party, three each from Inn National Development Party and Wa Democratic Party, four from three other parties, and two independent candidates were elected. Only one candidate from [National Unity Party (Burma)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_Party_\(Burma\)) was elected for Shan State Hluttaw (2011), although it was the second largest party in term of numbers of candidates.[^cite_note-24]

In 2011, Aung Myat (aka Sao Aung Myat), a former military officer of the [Myanmar Army](9503548.md) and a USDP candidate of Pindaya constituencies, was named as Chief Minister of Shan State Government.[^cite_note-25] Two candidates from SNDP were named for the first Shan State Government. Sai Ai Pao (aka Sai Aik Paung) was named for Industry and Mining Minister and Sai Naw Kham (aka Tun Tun Aung) was named for Construction Minister.[^cite_note-26] In the Shan State cabinets (2011), one was from the Myanmar Army and six were from the Union Soldiery and Development Party (USDP).[^cite_note-27]

[Sai Mauk Kham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Mauk_Kham) (aka Maung Ohn), one of the two vice presidents of Myanmar (2011–2015), was elected from Shan State No. 3 Constituency as a National Assembly candidate in the November 2010 election.[^cite_note-28]

According to data from the [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime) (UNODC), the region produces the most [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) in Myanmar, accounting for 82% (331 metric tons) of the country's total output (405 metric tons) in 2020.[^cite_note-\:0-29] However, opium poppy cultivation has declined year-on-year since 2015. In 2020, cultivation in Shan State declined a further 12%, with reductions taking place in East, North and South Shan with respective decreases of 17%, 10% and 9% from previous levels in 2019.[^cite_note-\:0-29]

Following the [2021 coup](66567997.md) and the [ensuing civil war](69807451.md), Shan State saw fierce fighting between the military junta and the alliance of ethnic armed rebel groups. In November 2023, the rebels launched [an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_1027) which overran much of Shan State, taking multiple towns, military outposts, and border crossings with China.[^cite_note-30]

## Geography

Most of the Shan State is a hilly [plateau](538005.md), the Shan Plateau, which together with the higher mountains in the north and south forms the [Shan Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_Hills) system. The [gorge](78478.md) of the [Thanlwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanlwin) (Salween/Namhkong) River cuts across the state. [Inle Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inle_Lake) where the leg-rowing [Intha people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intha_people) live in floating villages, in the great [Nyaung Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaung_Shwe) "plain", is the second largest natural expanse of water in Burma, shallow but 14 miles (23 km) long and 7 miles (11 km) wide. [Pindaya Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pindaya_Caves) near [Aungpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aungban) are vast [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) caverns which contain 6,226 [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha) images.[^cite_note-31]

The road to Taunggyi via [Kalaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaw) and [Aungpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aungban) branches off at [Thazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thazi,_Meiktila,_Mandalay_Division) from the main [Yangon](57404.md)–[Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay) Road; another road via [Ywangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ywangan) and [Pindaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pindaya) branches off from [Kyaukse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaukse) south of [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay). The [railhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railhead) stops short of [Taunggyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunggyi) at [Shwenyaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwenyaung), again from [Thazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thazi_Township) junction, while nearby [Heho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heho) has an airport.

A [severe magnitude 6.8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2011_Myanmar_earthquake) earthquake struck in Tarlay, [Tachileik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachileik) Township, the eastern part of Shan State, on 24 March 2011. It killed more than 70 and injured more than 100 people. 390 houses, 14 Buddhist monasteries, and nine government buildings were damaged.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Government



Shan State Government Office

### Executive

Main article: [Shan State Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_Government)

The [Shan State Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_Government) is the government of Shan State in [Myanmar](19457.md). It is responsible for overseeing local government institutions, entities and for the overall administration of Shan State. The Shan State Government is led by the **Chief Minister of Shan State**. However, before 2026, the state was under the administration of the [State Administration Council (SAC)](66589972.md).

### Legislature



Shan State Hluttaw

Main article: [Shan State Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_Hluttaw)

The [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of Shan State in [Myanmar](19457.md), called the [Shan State Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_Hluttaw), was established on 8 February 2016. It is a [unicameral](204304.md) body, consisting of 137 members—103 elected members and 34 military representatives.[^cite_note-uec3/16-37][^cite_note-38] As of February 2016,[^cite_note-39] [Sai Long Hseng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Long_Hseng?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Union Solidarity and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party) (USDP) leads the Hluttaw as its **Speaker**.

### Judiciary

The [Shan State High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_State_High_Court?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the highest court within Shan State, responsible for judicial matters.

### Ethnic makeup

The [Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people) (Tai) make up approximately one-third of Shan State's population, while the [Pa-O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa'O_people), [Bamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamar_people), [Palaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaung_people), and other ethnicities make up significant minorities. Other visible minorities in Shan State include the [Intha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intha_people), [Lahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahu_people), [Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people), [Taungyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taungyo), [Danu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danu_people), [Ahka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahka), and [Kachin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_peoples) ([Jingpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_people)).[^cite_note-handbook-41]

The valleys and tableland are inhabited by the Shan people, who resemble the [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_\(ethnic_group\)), [Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siam_people?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_people) people in language and customs. They are largely [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada_Buddhism) and are mainly engaged in agriculture. Among the Shans live the [Bamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamar), [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Chinese), and [Karens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people). The hills are inhabited by various peoples, notably the [Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Va_people), who are numerous in the north and along the Chinese border. The Palaung people are numerous in the Northern Shan State, in the townships of [Namkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namkham_Township?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_Se_Township), [Namhpaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namhpaka?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kutkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutkai_Township), and [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio_Township) along the [China-Myanmar border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China-Myanmar_border), and in the middle of Shan State, in the townships of [Namhsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namhsan), [Kyaukme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaukme_Township), and [Hsipaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsipaw_Township). Some of the Palaung people can also be found in [Kalaw Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaw_Township) in the Southern Shan State.

The [Lisu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people) are numerous in the Northern Shan State, in the townships of [Mongmit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongmit_Township), [Hsipaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsipaw_Township), [Kyaukme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaukme_Township), [Namhsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namhsan), [Namhpaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namhpaka?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kutkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutkai_Township), [Namtu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namtu_Township), [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio_Township), [Hopang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopang_Township), and [Tangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangyan_Township), as well as the [Kokang](1635498.md) region. There is also a numerous Lisu population in the Southern Shan State, in the townships of [Taunggyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunggyi_Township), [Pekon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekon_Township), [Hopon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopong_Township), [Mongpon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongpon?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Loilem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loilem_Township), [Lai-Hka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai-Hka_Township), [Nansang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nansang_Township), [Mong Nai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Nai_Township), [Mong Pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Pan_Township), and [Mong Ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Tong_Township). The [Jingpo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_people) are numerous along the Chinese border in the Northern Shan State, in the townships of [Namkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namkham_Township?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_Se_Township), [Namhpaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namhpaka?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kutkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutkai_Township), [Mungmyit Kodawng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mungmyit_Kodawng?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kengtung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengtung_Township), and [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio_Township).

There is a dwindling population of [Anglo-Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Burmese) in major hill stations, such as [Kalaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaw) and in [Taunggyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunggyi), a hold-over from the colonial period.

After the 2014 [Census in Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_Myanmar), the Burmese government indefinitely withheld release of detailed ethnicity data, citing concerns around political and social concerns surrounding the issue of ethnicity in Myanmar.[^cite_note-\:7-42] In 2022, researchers published an analysis of the [General Administration Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Administration_Department)'s nationwide 2018-2019 township reports to tabulate the ethnic makeup of Shan State.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-\:7-42]

According to the [2014 Myanmar Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Myanmar_Census), [Buddhists](3267529.md) make up 81.7% of Shan State's population, forming the largest religious community there.[^cite_note-TUR2-45] Minority religious communities include [Christians](5211.md) (9.8%), [Muslims](6037917.md) (1%), [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (0.1%), and [animists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) (6.6%) who collectively comprise the remainder of Shan State's population.[^cite_note-TUR2-45] 0.9% of the population listed no religion, other religions, or were otherwise not enumerated.[^cite_note-TUR2-45]

According to the [State Sangha Maha Nayaka Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Sangha_Maha_Nayaka_Committee)'s 2016 statistics, 77,513 Buddhist monks were registered in Shan State, comprising 14.5% of Myanmar's total [Sangha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangha) membership, which includes both novice [samanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanera) and fully-ordained bhikkhu.[^cite_note-mahana-46] Shan State is home to Myanmar's largest samanera community.[^cite_note-mahana-46]

The majority of monks belong to the [Thudhamma Nikaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thudhamma_Nikaya) (97%), followed by [Shwegyin Nikaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwegyin_Nikaya) (2.9%), with the remainder of monks belonging to other small [monastic orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik%C4%81ya);[^cite_note-mahana-46] 3,814 [thilashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thilashin) were registered in Shan State, comprising 6.3% of Myanmar's total thilashin community.[^cite_note-mahana-46]

## Economy

[Silver](27119.md), [lead](17747.md), and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) are mined, notably at the [Bawdwin mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawdwin_Mine), and there are smelters at [Namtu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namtu). Rubies are extracted in large quantity in [Mong Hsu Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mong_Hsu_Township) with output peaking in the late-1990s and early 2000s.[^cite_note-47]

[Teak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teak) is cut, and [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and other crops are grown. Shan State is known for its garden [produce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Produce) of all sorts of fresh fruit and vegetables thanks to its temperate and sunny climate. Itinerant markets that travel from place to place, setting up on every fifth day in each small town or village, are typical, although large towns have permanent markets. It is part of the [Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_\(Southeast_Asia\)), an area in which some of the world's [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) and [heroin](14034.md) are still illegally produced, and it has become a leading global production area for methamphetamine. [Drug trafficking](218449.md) is controlled by local [warlords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord) in partnership with foreign organized crime groups, some of whom have private armies amounting to thousands of soldiers.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Much of the [methamphetamine](11038318.md) (*[ya ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya_ba)*) that ends up across the Mekong and wider Asia Pacific region is produced in Shan as well.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

There are some border trading centers along the Shan State border and neighboring countries.[^cite_note-52] [Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse,_Burma), the biggest border trading center along the Myanmar–China border and [Tachileik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachileik), another important trading center between Myanmar and Thailand are in Shan State.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

The construction project of [Sino-Burma pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Burma_pipelines) of oil and gas that passes through northern part of Shan State was started in September 2010 and was finished in June 2013.[^cite_note-55]
