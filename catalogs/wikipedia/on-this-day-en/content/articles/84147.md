**Ticino** ([/tɪˈtʃiːnoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*tih-CHEE-noh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), sometimes **Tessin** ([/tɛˈsiːn, tɛˈsæ̃/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*tess-EEN*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), officially[^cite_note-5] the **Republic and Canton of Ticino**,[^cite_note-6] is one of the [26 cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Switzerland) forming the [Swiss Confederation](26748.md). It is composed of eight districts and its capital city is [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona). It is also traditionally divided into the [Sopraceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopraceneri) and the [Sottoceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sottoceneri), respectively north and south of [Monte Ceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Ceneri).

Ticino is the southernmost canton of Switzerland. It is one of the three large southern [Alpine](981.md) cantons, along with [Valais](198789.md) and the [Grisons](169850.md). However, unlike all other cantons, it lies almost entirely south of the Alps and has no natural access to the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau). Through the main crest of the [Gotthard Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Gotthard_Massif) and adjacent mountain ranges, it borders the canton of Valais to the northwest, the canton of [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) to the north and the canton of Grisons to the northeast; the latter canton being also the only one to share some borders with Ticino at the level of the plains. The canton shares international borders with [Italy](14532.md) as well, including a [small Italian enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campione_d'Italia).

Named after the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)), its longest river, it is the only canton where [Italian](14708.md) is the sole official language and represents the bulk of the Italian-speaking area of Switzerland along with the [southern parts of the Grisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Grisons). In 2020, Ticino had a population of 350,986.[^cite_note-Stat2020_TI-2] The largest city is [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano), and the two other notable centres are Bellinzona and [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno). While the geography of the Sopraceneri region is marked by the High Alps and [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore), that of the Sottoceneri is marked by the Alpine foothills and [Lake Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano). The canton, which has become one of the major tourist destinations of Switzerland, distinguishes itself from the rest of the country by its warm climate, and its culture and gastronomy.

The land now occupied by the canton was annexed from [Italian cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_city-states) in the 15th century by various Swiss forces in the last [transalpine campaigns of the Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transalpine_campaigns_of_the_Old_Swiss_Confederacy). In the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic), established in 1798, it was divided between the two new cantons of [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bellinzona) and [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lugano). The [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Mediation) in 1803 saw these two cantons combine to form the modern canton of Ticino. Because of its unusual position, the canton relies on important infrastructure for connection with the rest of the country. The first major north–south railway link across the Alps, the [Gotthard Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Railway), opened in 1882. In 2016, the [Gotthard Base Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Base_Tunnel) was inaugurated, which finally provided a fully flat route through the Alps.

The GDP per capita of Ticino was 83,450 Swiss francs in 2020, above the Swiss average of 80,418 Swiss francs in the same period.[^cite_note-7] It is one of the wealthiest areas in Europe. Ticino had the second highest [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) (85.2 years) in Europe in 2018.[^cite_note-8] The [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of 0.961 in 2021 was one of the [highest found anywhere in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_subnational_entities_with_the_highest_and_lowest_Human_Development_Index).

## Etymology

The name *Ticino* was chosen for the newly established canton in 1803, after the river [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) which flows through it from the [Novena Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nufenen_Pass) to [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore).[^cite_note-aet-9]

Known as *Ticinus* in Roman times, the river appears on the *[Tabula Peutingeriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabula_Peutingeriana)* as *Ticenum*. [Johann Kaspar Zeuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Kaspar_Zeuss) attributed Celtic origins to the name, tracing it to the Celtic *tek*, itself from an Indo-European root *tak*, meaning "melting, flowing".[^cite_note-rria-10]

The official name of the canton is *Republic and Canton of Ticino* ([Italian](14708.md): *Repubblica e Cantone Ticino*), and the two-letter code is TI. It is one of the four cantons of Switzerland officially referred to as "republics", along with [Geneva](292834.md), [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuch%C3%A2tel) and [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura).

## History

Further information: [Transalpine campaigns of the Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transalpine_campaigns_of_the_Old_Swiss_Confederacy)



The [Castles of Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castles_of_Bellinzona), guarding the access to the [Gotthard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass) and other Alpine passes since the Roman Era

During the [Bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and [Iron Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), the area of what is today Ticino was settled by the [Lepontii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepontii), a [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celt) tribe. Later, probably around the rule of [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus), it became part of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). After the fall of the Western Empire, it was ruled by the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) and the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks). Around 1100 it was the centre of a struggle between the free communes of [Milan](36511.md) and [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como): in the 14th century, it was acquired by the [Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_of_Milan), Dukes of [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan). In the fifteenth century, the [Swiss](26748.md) Confederates conquered the valleys south of the Alps in three separate conquests.

Between 1403 and 1422 some of these lands were already annexed by forces from the [canton of Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri), but subsequently lost. Uri conquered the [Leventina Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_Leventina) in 1440.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoolidge1911934-11] In a second conquest Uri, [Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz) and [Nidwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Nidwalden) gained the town of [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona) and the Riviera in 1500.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoolidge1911934-11] Some of the land and Bellinzona itself were previously annexed by [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) in 1419 but lost again in 1422. The third conquest was fought by troops from the entire Confederation (at that time constituted by 12 cantons). In 1512 [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno), the [Maggia Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggia_Valley), [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano) and [Mendrisio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendrisio) were annexed. Subsequently, the upper valley of the river [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)), from the [St. Gotthard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Gotthard_Massif) to the town of Biasca (Leventina Valley) was part of Uri. The remaining territory (*Baliaggi Ultramontani*, *Ennetbergische Vogteien*, the Bailiwicks Beyond the Mountains) was administered by the Twelve Cantons. These districts were governed by bailiffs holding office for two years and purchasing it from the members of the League.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoolidge1911934-11]



[Ticinese franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_franco), currency of Ticino until the introduction of the [Swiss franc](361362.md) in 1850.

The lands of the canton of Ticino are the last lands to be conquered by the [Swiss Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederation). The Confederation gave up any further conquests after their defeat at the [battle of Marignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marignano) in 1515 by [Francis I of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_I_of_France). The Valle Leventina revolted unsuccessfully against [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) in 1755.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoolidge1911934-11] In February 1798 an attempt of annexation by the [Cisalpine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic) was repelled by a volunteer militia in Lugano. Between 1798 and 1803, during the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic), two cantons were created ([Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bellinzona) and [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lugano)) but in 1803 the two were unified to form the canton of Ticino that joined the Swiss Confederation as a full member in the same year under the [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Mediation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoolidge1911933-12] During the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_War), many Ticinesi (as was the case for other [Swiss](7351032.md)) served in Swiss military units allied with the [French](5843419.md). The canton minted its own [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency), the [Ticinese franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticinese_franco), between 1813 and 1850, when it began the use of the [Swiss franc](361362.md).

As a particularly poor region, Ticino was a land of emigration. Notable examples include the chocolatiers (*cioccolatieri*) of the [Val Blenio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Blenio), who migrated throughout Europe (see [History of Swiss Chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_chocolate#History)).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]



Men playing [Bocce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocce) in [Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre,_Switzerland) (early 20th century)

Until 1878 the three largest cities, Bellinzona, Lugano and Locarno, alternated as capital of the canton. In 1878, however, Bellinzona became the only and permanent capital. The 1870–1891 period saw a surge of political turbulence in Ticino, and the authorities needed the assistance of the federal government to restore order in several instances, in 1870, 1876, 1889 and 1890–1891.[^cite_note-15]

The current cantonal constitution dates from 1997. The previous constitution, heavily modified, was codified in 1830, nearly 20 years before the constitution of the [Swiss Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Confederation).[^cite_note-16]

## Geography

See also: [Geography of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Switzerland)



The [Verzasca Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verzasca_Valley) (here near [Lavertezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavertezzo)) is the most central valley of Ticino

[^cite_note-17]

Ticino is the southernmost canton of Switzerland. With a few exceptions in the extreme north and south of the canton, it lies entirely in the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) basin, a tributary of the [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)). Along with [Valais](198789.md) and the [Grisons](169850.md), it is one of the three cantons whose territory extends into the Po basin (lands to the south of the [Alps](981.md)). However, unlike the other Po basin cantons (and all other cantons), all settlements of Ticino are on the south side of the Alps, therefore separated from the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau) (and most of the country) by the great Alpine barrier. The canton also comprehends some small areas in the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) basin in the north, at the [Gotthard Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass) and around [lake of Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_da_Sontga_Maria). The extreme south of the canton is drained by the Po as well, but through the [Breggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breggia_\(river\)), [Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_\(river\)),[^cite_note-Swisstopo-18] Gaggiolo, [Olona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olona), and [Lambro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambro).

The canton is traditionally (but not administratively) split into two regions. The northern region, the [Sopraceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopraceneri), is formed by the valleys around [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) and includes the highest mountains of the canton and the main [Alpine watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_chain_of_the_Alps). The southern region, the [Sottoceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sottoceneri), is the region around [Lake Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano), and marks the beginning of the southern Alpine foothills. Between the two regions is [Monte Ceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Ceneri), a moderately elevated mountain pass and important north–south axis.[^cite_note-Swisstopo-18] The Sopraceneri is constituted by the districts of [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona_\(district\)), [Blenio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blenio_\(district\)), [Leventina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leventina), [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_\(district\)), [Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riviera_\(district\)) and [Vallemaggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallemaggia_\(district\)), and makes up about 85% of the territory and 43% of the population.[^cite_note-19] The Sottoceneri is constituted by the districts of [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano_\(district\)) and [Mendrisio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendrisio_\(district\)), and makes up about 15% of the territory and 57% of the population.[^cite_note-20] While [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano), the largest city, is in the densely populated Sottoceneri, the two other main cities, [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona) and [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno), are in the Sopraceneri.

The Ticino, which gives its name to the canton, is the largest river of Ticino. It flows from the northwest through the [Bedretto Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedretto_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Leventina Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leventina_Valley) to enter Lake Maggiore near [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno). Its main tributaries are the [Brenno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenno_\(river\)) in the [Blenio Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blenio_Valley) and the [Moesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moesa_\(river\)) in the [Mesolcina Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolcina_Valley) in the [Grisons](169850.md). The lands of most of the canton are shaped by the river, which in its mid portion forms a wide valley, commonly known as the *Riviera*. The western lands of the canton, however, are drained by the [Maggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggia_\(river\)). The [Verzasca Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verzasca_Valley) is between the Leventina Valley and the Maggia Valley. There is also a smaller area that drains directly into the [Lake Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano). Most of the land is considered within the Alps, but a small area is part of the plain of the Po which drains the north of [Italy](14532.md).



High Alpine landscape on [Pizzo Campo Tencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzo_Campo_Tencia)

Although it includes the lowest point of Switzerland (Lake Maggiore) as well as its lowest town ([Ascona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascona)), the topography of Ticino is extremely rugged, as it is the canton with the [fourth largest elevation difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swiss_cantons_by_elevation). It lies essentially within the Alps, in particular the [Lepontine Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepontine_Alps), the [Saint-Gotthard Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Gotthard_Massif) and the [Lugano Prealps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano_Prealps). The longest and deepest valleys are those of the Ticino, Verzasca and Maggia. The two highest mountains are the [Rheinwaldhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinwaldhorn) and the [Basòdino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas%C3%B2dino). Other notable mountains are [Pizzo Rotondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzo_Rotondo) (highest of the Gotthard Massif), [Pizzo Campo Tencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzo_Campo_Tencia) (highest fully within the canton), [Monte Generoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Generoso) (highest south of Lake Lugano) and [Monte Tamaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Tamaro) (most prominent of the canton). For an exhaustive list, see [list of mountains of Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_of_Ticino).

The area of the canton is 2,812 square kilometres (1,086 sq mi), of which about three-quarters are considered productive to trees or crops.[^cite_note-BFS_Tessin-21] Forests cover about a third of the area, but also the lakes [Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) (or *Verbano*) and [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano) (or *Ceresio*) make up a considerable minority. The canton shares borders with three other cantons across the main ridge of the Alps: [Valais](198789.md) to the northwest, to which it is connected by the [Nufenen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nufenen_Pass), [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) to the north, to which it is connected by the [Gotthard Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass) and the [Grisons](169850.md) to the northeast, to which it is connected by the [Lukmanier Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukmanier_Pass) and the [Mesolcina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolcina) Valley; the latter valley, a few kilometres north of Bellinzona, being the only (natural) low elevation access to another canton. Ticino shares international borders with [Italy](14532.md) as well. To the southwest is the region of [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) and to the southeast is the region of [Lombardy](43807.md). The main border crossing between Italy and Switzerland is that of [Chiasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiasso), in the extreme south of the canton.[^cite_note-Swisstopo-18]

### Climate

Gandria

Brissago Islands

Olive trees at [Gandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandria) (Lake Lugano) and palm trees on the [Brissago Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brissago_Islands) (Lake Maggiore)

The climate of Ticino is mostly influenced by the Mediterranean Sea, the Alps protecting it from north European weather.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] As a consequence, the plains experience warm and moist summers, and mild winters. This climate is noticeably warmer and wetter than [the rest of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Switzerland)'s. In German-speaking Switzerland, Ticino is nicknamed *Sonnenstube* (sun porch), owing to the more than 2,300 [sunshine hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_hours) the canton receives every year, compared to 1,700 for Zurich.[^cite_note-SteinerKarnusian2014-24] The canton can experience particularly heavy storms and rainfalls in summer. It is the region of Switzerland with the highest level of lightning discharge.[^cite_note-25] Conversely, the canton can experience severe droughts in both summer and winter, making it the region most affected by forest fires in the country.[^cite_note-26]

The climate of Ticino is highly diverse as elevations range from Lake Maggiore, affected by [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate), to the high Alps, affected by [subarctic and tundra climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Therefore, similarly to the rest of Switzerland, many different types of ecosystems are found in the region. In the lower areas, deciduous forests are omnipresent, while at high elevations they tend to be replaced by coniferous forests, except in the Sottoceneri ([Lugano Prealps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano_Prealps)), where they are almost absent. The treeline is located at around 2,000 metres in the Sopraceneri and 1,600 metres in the Sottoceneri.[^cite_note-29] The [Basòdino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas%C3%B2dino), Ticino's second-highest mountain, is covered by the [largest glacier of the canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas%C3%B2dino_Glacier). In winter, skiing is popular in the highest locations, notably in [Airolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airolo) and [Bosco/Gurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosco/Gurin). In the lower regions, especially around Lake Maggiore and Lake Lugano, vineyards, olive trees[^cite_note-30] and other fruits common to southern Europe are grown.[^cite_note-31] Several types of cold hardy [palm trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_trees) and other subtropical species may be grown here, and although none are native, their presence in the ecosystem is increasing.[^cite_note-32] Numerous gardens, especially near the lakes, such as the [Brissago Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brissago_Islands) and the [Scherrer Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scherrer_Park), are renowned for their exotic plants.

### Diocese

The [Diocese of Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Lugano) is co-extensive to the canton.

## Government





Palazzo delle Orsoline and nearby Piazza Indipendenza with commemorative obelisk in [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona)

The current Constitution of the Republic and Canton of Ticino, originating from a draft approved on 18 August 1801 during the Helvetic Republic,[^cite_note-33] was approved on 14 December 1997.[^cite_note-const-34] In its [preamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preamble), it states that it was created by the Ticinese people ([popolo](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/popolo)) *"in order to guaranty peaceful life together with respect for the dignity of man, fundamental liberties and social justice (...) faithful to its historic task to interpret Italian culture within the Helvetic Confederation"*.[^cite_note-const-34]

The [Grand Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Council_of_Ticino) (*Gran Consiglio*) is the legislative authority of the canton, exercising sovereignty over any matter not explicitly delegated by the constitution to another authority.[^cite_note-const-34] The Gran Consiglio has 90 members called *deputati* (deputies), elected in a single [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) using the [proportional representation](23298.md) system.[^cite_note-const-34] Deputies serve four-year terms, and annually nominate a President and two vice-presidents.

The five-member [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_of_Ticino?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Italian](14708.md): *Consiglio di Stato*), not to be confused with the federal [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)), is the executive authority of the canton, and it directs cantonal affairs according to law and the constitution. It is elected in a single [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) using the [proportional representation](23298.md) system. Currently, the five members of the Government are Piero Marchesi, Raffaele De Rosa, Marina Carobbio Guscetti, Norman Gobbi and Christian Vitta.[^cite_note-35]

Each year, the Council of State nominates its president.[^cite_note-const-34] The current president of the Council of State is Marina Carobbio Guscetti.[^cite_note-36]

The most recent elections were held in April 2023; the next elections will be on 11 April 2027.[^cite_note-elections-37]

The cantonal capital is [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona). The Palazzo delle Orsoline on Piazza Governo is the meeting place for both the Grand Council and the Council of State.[^cite_note-const-34] Nearby Piazza Governo is Piazza Indipendenza, which commemorates the independence of the canton.

### Referendum decisions

Since a referendum in September 2013, Ticino is the only Swiss canton where wearing full-face veils is illegal.[^cite_note-40] Supporters of the ban cited the case of a 20-year-old Pakistani woman from [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona), who was killed by her husband for refusing to wear a headscarf.[^cite_note-dt-41][^cite_note-42] The Burqa ban was later approved by the [Grand Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Council_of_Ticino) in November 2015.[^cite_note-43]

In September 2016, Ticino voters approved a [Swiss People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party)-sponsored referendum that gives precedence to Swiss workers, as opposed to foreign workers, defying [freedom of movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_movement_for_workers_in_the_European_Union) agreements [between Switzerland and the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland%E2%80%93European_Union_relations).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

### History of the districts

Leventina was a [subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogt_\(Switzerland\)) of the [canton of Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) until 1798, the year the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic) was founded, when it became part of the new [canton of Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bellinzona) along with the [Swiss condominiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogt_\(Switzerland\)) of Bellinzona, Riviera and Blenio. The condominiums of Locarno, Lugano, Mendrisio and Vallemaggia became part of the new [canton of Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lugano) in 1798. These two cantons formed into one canton, Ticino, in 1803 when it joined the (restored) Swiss Confederation as a member canton. The former condominiums and Leventina became the eight districts of the canton of Ticino, which exist to the present day and are provided for by the cantonal constitution.

### Municipalities and circles

Main article: [Municipalities of the canton of Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_canton_of_Ticino)

There are 100 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Switzerland) in the canton (as of April 2025). These municipalities (*comuni*) are grouped in 38 *circoli* (circles or sub-districts) which are in turn grouped into the eight districts (*distretti*).[^cite_note-47]

The mayor (*sindaco*) is the president of the municipal government (*municipio*) which comprises at least three members; a council also exists. The members of the council and the *municipio* are elected every four years by the [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_citizen) resident in the *comune* – the next elections are scheduled for April 2024.[^cite_note-elections-37]

Since the late 1990s, there has been an ongoing [project to aggregate some municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_canton_of_Ticino#Changes), with the constitution of the canton allowing for the Grand Council of Ticino to promote and lead in deciding on mergers.[^cite_note-constitution-46] This has resulted in changes to some of the circles, with many circles now consisting of just one or two municipalities. The most populous municipality – Lugano (having merged with numerous other municipalities) – is subdivided into *[quartieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartieri)* (quarters) which are grouped into three (cantonal) circles. In the modern day, the circle serves only as a territorial unit with limited public functions, most notably the local judiciary.

Ticino has a population (as of 31 December 2020) of 350,986.[^cite_note-Stat2020_TI-2] As of 2013, the population included 94,366 foreigners, or about 27.2% of the total population. The largest groups of foreign population were Italians (46.2%), followed by Croats (6.5%) and Portuguese (5.9%).[^cite_note-Annuario_Statistico_Ticinese_2015-48] The population density (in 2005) is 114.6 persons per km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-BFS_Tessin-21] As of 2000, 83.1% of the population spoke Italian, 8.3% spoke German and 1.7% spoke [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian_language).[^cite_note-BFS_Tessin-21]

As of 2019, 70.0% of the total population was [Catholic](606848.md).[^cite_note-49] According to a 2012 survey, the population aged 15 years and older was mostly Catholic (70%); further Christian denominations accounted for 10% of the population (including [Swiss Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformed_Church) 4%), 2% were [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) and 1% of the population adhered to another religion (including [Jews](25955086.md) 0.1%).[^cite_note-Annuario_Statistico_Ticinese_2015-48]

The official language, and the one used for most written communication, is [Swiss Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Italian). Despite being very similar to [standard Italian](14708.md), Swiss Italian presents some differences to the Italian spoken in [Italy](14532.md) due to the influence of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and German from which it assimilates words. Dialects of the [Lombard language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) such as [Ticinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticinese_dialect) are still spoken, especially in the valleys, but they are not used for official purposes.

Despite the dominance of Italian speakers, fluency in [Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German) or [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German) is sometimes taken to be an important prerequisite for employment, regardless of sector or sphere of work.[^cite_note-50]

In 2016, Ticino was the European region with the second highest life expectancy at 85.0 years, and the highest male life expectancy at 82.7 years.[^cite_note-51]

## Economy



[Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano), the largest Italian-speaking city of Switzerland

[Tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector) workers make up 76.5% of the Ticinese workforce, compared to the Swiss average of 67.1%. Commerce (23.1%), tourism (10.1%) and financial activities (3.9%) are all important for the local economy, while the contribution from agriculture and fishing is marginal, employing 6.5% of the workforce on a Swiss average of 15.4%.[^cite_note-53] The median gross private sector monthly salary in 2012 was 5,091 francs (US\$5,580), below the national average of 6,118 francs (US\$6,703). [^cite_note-54] However, due to lesser cost of living and lower taxation compared to most other cantons, the overall disposable mean income is high.[^cite_note-55] The GDP per capita at 82,438 francs in 2014, was seventh highest in Switzerland.[^cite_note-56] Ticino is counted among the most prosperous regions of Switzerland and of Europe.[^cite_note-57]

Lugano is Switzerland's third largest financial centre after Zurich and Geneva.[^cite_note-58] The banking industry alone has 8,400 employees and generates 17% of the gross cantonal product.[^cite_note-DoC-59] Because of Ticino's shared language and culture, its financial industry has very close ties to Italy.[^cite_note-DoC-59] In 2017, Ticino had an unemployment rate of 4%, higher than the Switzerland average which was estimated at 3.7%.[^cite_note-60]

*Frontalieri*, [commuter workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_worker) living in Italy (mostly in the provinces of [Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varese) and [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como)) but working regularly in Ticino, form a large part (over 20%) of the workforce, far larger than in the rest of Switzerland, where the rate is below 5%. Foreigners in general hold 44.3% of all the jobs, again a much higher rate than elsewhere in the Confederation (27%).[^cite_note-61] Frontalieri are usually paid less than Swiss workers for their jobs, and tend to serve as low-cost labour.[^cite_note-62]



Hikers above Lake Maggiore. Ticino is a popular tourist destination for its climate and scenery

Italy is by far Ticino's most important foreign trading partner, but there's a huge [trade deficit](4810.md) between imports (5 billion [CHF](361362.md)) and exports (1.9 billion).[^cite_note-63] By 2013, Germany had become the canton's main export market, receiving 23.1% of the total, compared to 15.8% for Italy and 9.9% for the United States.[^cite_note-64] Many Italian companies relocate to Ticino, either temporarily or permanently, seeking lower taxes and an efficient [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy):[^cite_note-65] just as many Ticinese entrepreneurs doing business in Italy complain of [red tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_tape) and widespread [protectionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism).[^cite_note-66] The region has been attracting multinational companies particularly from the [fashion industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_industry) due to its closeness to [Milan](36511.md). [Hugo Boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Boss), [Gucci](844136.md), [VF Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VF_Corporation) and other popular brands are located there. Because the international fashion business has become a significant employer for Swiss and Italians alike, the region has also been termed the "Fashion Valley".[^cite_note-67]

Three of the world's largest gold refineries are based in Ticino,[^cite_note-68] including the Pamp refinery in [Castel San Pietro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_San_Pietro), the leading manufacturer of minted gold bars.[^cite_note-69] Large companies based in the canton include: [Bally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally_Shoe), [Hupac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hupac).

The opening of the [Gotthard Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Railway) in 1882 led to the establishment of a sizeable tourist industry mostly catering to German speakers,[^cite_note-70] although since the early 2000s the industry has suffered from the competition of more distant destinations. In 2011, 1,728,888 overnight stays were recorded.[^cite_note-71] The mild climate throughout the year makes the canton a popular destination for hikers.[^cite_note-yroun-72] The high Alps of Ticino include numerous tourist facilities such as the [Monte Generoso Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Generoso_Railway), the [Ritom Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritom_funicular) and the [Cardada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardada) Cableway. Among other tourist attractions are the [Verzasca Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verzasca_Dam), popular with bungee jumpers,[^cite_note-yroun-72] and Swissminiatur in [Melide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melide,_Switzerland), a [miniature park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_park) featuring scale models of over 120 Swiss monuments.[^cite_note-WilliamsSimonis2009-73] The [Brissago Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brissago_Islands) on [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) are the only Swiss islands south of the Alps, and house botanical gardens with 1,600 different plant species from five continents.[^cite_note-74]

## Transport



[Leventina Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leventina_Valley). Leading to Central Switzerland, the Gotthard axis consists of several railways and highways, here the [A2 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_motorway_\(Switzerland\)) and south portal of the [Gotthard Base Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Base_Tunnel).

The [Gotthard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass) is a strategic mountain pass of Central Switzerland and Ticino since the 13th century. Several tunnels underneath the Gotthard connect the canton to northern Switzerland: the first to open was the 15 kilometres (9.3 mi) long [Gotthard Rail Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Rail_Tunnel) in 1882, replacing the pass road, connecting [Airolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airolo) with [Göschenen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6schenen) in the [canton of Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri).[^cite_note-75] A 17 km (11 mi) motorway tunnel, the [Gotthard Road Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Road_Tunnel), opened in 1980.[^cite_note-76] A second rail tunnel through the pass, the [Gotthard Base Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Base_Tunnel), was opened on 1 June 2016. The new tunnel is [the longest tunnel in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest_tunnels),[^cite_note-alpt-77] reducing travel time between [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich) and [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano) to 1 hour 40 minutes.[^cite_note-alpt-77] It is the first flat route through the Alps and provides for the first time a low-level route to the cities of the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau).

The [Ceneri Base Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceneri_Base_Tunnel), inaugurated in 2020, constitutes another revolution in the canton, by providing fast links to both Locarno and Bellinzona from Lugano, and making the latter city an important railway node. The base tunnel bypasses the old [Monte Ceneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Ceneri) axis.



Treno Gottardo at Bellinzona

[Treni Regionali Ticino Lombardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treni_Regionali_Ticino_Lombardia) (TiLo), a [joint venture](538782.md) between the Italian [Ferrovie dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato) and the [Swiss Federal Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Railways) launched in 2004, manages the traffic between the regional railways of [Lombardy](43807.md) and the Ticino railway network via a [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn) system.[^cite_note-78] The canton is also served by the Treno Gottardo from northern Switzerland, operated by the [Südostbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCdostbahn) (SOB).

The [Regional Bus and Rail Company of Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Bus_and_Rail_Company_of_Ticino) provides the urban and suburban bus network of Locarno, operates the cable cars between Verdasio and Rasa, and between Intragna – Pila – Costa on behalf of the owning companies, and, together with an Italian company, the [Centovalli and Vigezzina Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodossola%E2%80%93Locarno_railway_line) which connects the Gotthard trans-Alpine rail route at Locarno with the [Simplon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_Pass) trans-Alpine route at [Domodossola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodossola), with further connections with [Brig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brig,_Switzerland) in [Valais](198789.md).

The canton has a higher than average incidence of traffic accidents, recording 16 deaths or serious injuries per 100 million km in the 2004–2006 period, compared to a Swiss average of 6.[^cite_note-79]

Since March 2025, there are no regular flights scheduled to or from [Lugano Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano_Airport). Currently, only [Silver Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Air_\(Czech_Republic\)) operates to Lugano Airport. While there is limited service to Lugano's airport, the nearest airport to the area is [Milan Malpensa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Malpensa_Airport) which is connected to Lugano by a direct hourly train with a travel time of 1\:45h from/to Lugano,[^cite_note-Times-80][^cite_note-TILO-81] or about one hour, 80 km by road.

## Education and science

There are two major centres of education and research located in the canton of Ticino. [University of Italian Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A0_della_Svizzera_italiana) (USI, Università della Svizzera Italiana) in Lugano is the only Swiss university teaching primarily in Italian. The [University of Applied Sciences and Arts of Southern Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUPSI) (SUPSI, Scuola Universitaria Professionale della Svizzera Italiana), in [Manno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manno), is a professional training college focused on a practical method of teaching in the areas of applied art, economy, social work, technology and production science.[^cite_note-DoC-59]

There is also a small American and Swiss accredited private college, [Franklin University Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_University_Switzerland), located above [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano),[^cite_note-82] as well as [The American School in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_School_in_Switzerland) in [Collina d'Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collina_d'Oro), a K-13 international school accepting day and boarding students.

## Culture

See also: [List of cultural property of national significance in Switzerland: Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cultural_property_of_national_significance_in_Switzerland:_Ticino) and [List of museums in Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Ticino)



People gathering on Piazza Grande during the [Locarno Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_Festival)

### Cultural identity

As the only predominantly Italian-speaking canton, Ticino notably distinguishes itself from the rest of the country by its meridional, or Mediterranean, culture.[^cite_note-83] Cultural identity of Ticino is complex and is marked by its long history as a bailiwick of the Swiss Confederacy, until its independence of 1803.[^cite_note-84] Ticinese identity was gradually forged in the 19th century, partly thanks to the efforts of major intellectual figures such as [Stefano Franscini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Franscini) and [Carlo Cattaneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Cattaneo).[^cite_note-85] Cantonal patriotism is particularly strong in Ticino; this is reflected by the use of the term *repubblica* in official documents.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

### Architecture

Ticino is particularly known for its rich architectural heritage, ranging from the anonymous rock architecture of [grottos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grotto) and splüi, over [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) and [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) to contemporary styles. The birthplace of [Francesco Borromini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Borromini), the canton is home to internationally recognized architects, such as [Mario Botta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Botta), [Aurelio Galfetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelio_Galfetti), [Luigi Snozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Snozzi), and [Livio Vacchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livio_Vacchini).[^cite_note-snws-88] As early as the 18th century, aristocrats from Russia and Italy employed numerous architects from Ticino.[^cite_note-btuc-89] More recently, the region became a centre of the [Neo-Rationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Rationalist) *Tendenza* movement.[^cite_note-Hays2000-90]

### World heritage sites

Ticino hosts four [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites): the [Three Castles of Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Castles_of_Bellinzona), [Monte San Giorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_San_Giorgio) and the living traditions of the holy week processions in Mendrisio and the Valle di Lodano Forest Reserve[^cite_note-91].



Rustic stone houses in Foroglio (Val Bavona - Maggia Valley)

### Film

During the second week of August, Locarno is host to the [Locarno International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_International_Film_Festival), Switzerland's most prestigious film festival.[^cite_note-Oettli2011-92]

### Music

[Estival Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estival_Jazz), a free open-air jazz festival, is held in Lugano and [Mendrisio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendrisio) in late June and July.[^cite_note-WilliamsSimonis2009b-93][^cite_note-Lane2007-94] Another jazz festival is [held in Ascona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascona_Jazz_Festival). [Rabadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabadan) is the major carnival festival of the canton. It has been ongoing now for more than 150 years.[^cite_note-95]

Traditional folk music of Ticino also distinguishes itself from that of northern Switzerland.[^cite_note-96] Among traditional instruments are the accordion, the guitar and, since the 19th century, the mandolin. Duos and trios with mandolin and guitar typically accompany regional folk songs.[^cite_note-97] However, like most of Switzerland, Ticino has a long brass-band tradition. A regional, reduced version, is the *bandella*, an ensemble consisting of brass instruments and clarinets.[^cite_note-98]

## Sport

## Cuisine



A family harvesting chestnuts in the early 1900s.

[Polenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polenta), along with [chestnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut) and potatoes, was for centuries one of the staple foods in Ticino, and it remains a mainstay of local cuisine.[^cite_note-102] Nowadays, the most typical dishes are polenta, often served with meat (such as rabbit) and gravy sauce, and [risotto, sometimes with saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risotto_alla_ticinese).[^cite_note-103] Local products of Ticino, called *Nostrani*, include a large variety of cheeses, meat specialities such as [salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salame_ticinese) and [prosciutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosciutto),[^cite_note-104] and wines, especially red merlot. [Olive oil is produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_production_in_Switzerland) in small quantities but olive cultivation is growing in the canton.[^cite_note-105]









Specialities from Ticino: salami, polenta, rice and olive oil

Sweet products of Ticino notably include the *torta di pane*, a cake made with stale bread softened in milk and containing dried and candied fruits,[^cite_note-106] and Panettone, a yeast-leavened bread containing candied fruits.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] *Gazzosa ticinese*, a [soft drink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_drink) available in lemon and a number of other flavours, is one of the most popular beverages from Ticino, and is also common in other regions of Switzerland. It usually comes in [flip-top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip-top) bottles.[^cite_note-109] The estimate for the production of *gazzosa* in Ticino is 7–8 million bottles a year.[^cite_note-110] Food and wine were historically conserved in grottos, which were ubiquitous stone structures built in shadowy and fresh areas. They have become rustic, family-run open-air restaurants in the latter part of the 20th century. They serve traditional food and local wine (usually [Merlot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlot) or similar), often in a little ceramic jug known as *boccalino*, which is also a popular souvenir for tourists.[^cite_note-bocgr-111]



A group of people drinking [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(wine_region\)) from *tazzini* in [Valle di Blenio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_di_Blenio) (early 20th century)

*[Bruscitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruscitti)*, originating from [Alto Milanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto_Milanese), are common in the whole [Insubria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubria) area. This is a braised meat dish cut very thinly and cooked in wine and [fennel seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennel), historically obtained by stripping leftover meat. It is particularly popular in the [province of Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Varese) (Lombardy),[^cite_note-112] in the Alto Milanese area (Lombardy; particularly in the area of the city of [Busto Arsizio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busto_Arsizio), where it originates), in the [province of Verbano-Cusio-Ossola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Verbano-Cusio-Ossola) (Piedmont)[^cite_note-113] and in lower Ticino.[^cite_note-rsi-114]
