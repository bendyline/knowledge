The **Federal Police** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Bundespolizei*, [\[ˈbʊndəspoliˌtsaɪ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fe/De-Bundespolizei.ogg/De-Bundespolizei.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fe/De-Bundespolizei.ogg/De-Bundespolizei.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, **BPOL**) is the national and principal federal [law enforcement agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Germany) of the [Federal Government of Germany](1327803.md). It is subordinate to the [Federal Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Germany\)) and is responsible for land and maritime [border control](247927.md), law enforcement at [airports](471997.md) and on the [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Germany) system, and the protection of federal institutions. Before 1 July 2005, the agency was known as the *_[Bundesgrenzschutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesgrenzschutz)_* (Federal Border Guard). It operates separately from the [state police](964240.md) forces (*[Landespolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landespolizei)*), which are responsible for general law enforcement within each of Germany's 16 [federal states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany).

## Missions

The Bundespolizei is responsible for:

- Border security, including passport control at Germany's external [Schengen borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_border) (air, sea, and any land borders on the Schengen Area's external frontier), as well as at internal borders during periods when temporary border controls are reinstated under Schengen rules.

- Transport security at [international airports](471997.md) and on the [German railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Germany) system.

- Operating the federal [air marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_marshal) service.

- Operating the [GSG 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSG_9) [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) unit.

- Providing mobile federal support forces for major internal security incidents.

- Protecting certain federal buildings, including [Schloss Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schloss_Bellevue) and the [Federal Constitutional Court](34781059.md) and [Federal Court of Justice](561684.md) in [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe).

- Supporting international police missions for the [United Nations](31769.md) and the [European Union](9317.md) in locations such as [Kosovo](17391.md), [Sudan](27421.md), [Liberia](17791.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), [Moldova](19260.md), and [Georgia](48768.md).

- Providing security personnel for selected [German embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Germany).

- Operating federal rescue helicopters for [medical emergencies](146346.md), [search‑and‑rescue](172599.md) missions, and [disaster response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_response).

The *Bundespolizei* can also be used to reinforce [state police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landespolizei) if requested by a state *(Land)* government. The BPOL maintains these reserve forces to deal with major demonstrations, disturbances or emergencies, supplementing the capabilities of the [State Operational Support Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereitschaftspolizei). Several highly trained detachments are available for crisis situations requiring [armored cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_car_\(military\)), [water cannon](431462.md) or other special equipment.

BPOL investigators conduct criminal investigations only within its jurisdiction; otherwise the cases are referred to the appropriate state police force or to the federal criminal investigative agency, the [Federal Criminal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Criminal_Police_Office_\(Germany\)) (*Bundeskriminalamt*, BKA).

In addition, the *Bundespolizei* cooperates closely with German state executive authorities, such as prosecutor's offices (*[Staatsanwaltschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsanwaltschaft)*) in pursuing criminal investigations.

### Restoration of border control tasking on all borders (2015)



German Federal Police inspect a [Ukrainian](31750.md) coach and its passengers at the Lipperts [rest area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_area#Europe) on [Autobahn 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_9), northbound

&#x20;On the night of 13 September 2015 Germany unilaterally reintroduced [border controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_controls), under emergency provisions of the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md), due to the [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md) overwhelming Germany's available resources, law enforcement and otherwise.&#x20;

The nominally temporary border controls were initially put in place just on the border with Austria, but by the following day (Monday 14 September 2015) they were being put in place at all borders with fellow EU members.&#x20;

The same day, Austria and other EU members who were part of the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) began to put in place their own border controls (again meant to be temporary) in response to Germany's actions.

The new German border controls are to be primarily enforced both by the various *[Landespolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landespolizei)* of those German states that adjoin external borders, and in particular by the *Bundespolizei*.

## Organization



*Bundespolizei* districts of Germany



Uniform sleeve patch of the German Federal Police

The BPOL national headquarters (BPOL-*Präsidium*) in [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam) performs all central control functions. Eight regional headquarters (BPOL-*Direktion*) control the BPOL stations that conduct rail police and border protection missions. These areas of responsibility conform to the federal state boundaries which they did not do prior to 1 March 2008.

The regional headquarters are as follows:

- [Bad Bramstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Bramstedt) covering [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md) and [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) as well as the North Sea and Baltic Sea as part of the [German Federal Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Federal_Coast_Guard).

- [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover) covering [Bremen](9420388.md), [Hamburg](13467.md) and [Lower Saxony](18435.md).

- [Sankt Augustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Augustin) covering [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md).

- [Koblenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koblenz) covering [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland), [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md) and [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse).

- [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) covering [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md).

- [Munich](19058.md) covering [Bavaria](3764.md).

- [Pirna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirna) covering [Saxony](28395.md), [Saxony-Anhalt](38138.md) and [Thuringia](31130.md).

- [Berlin](3354.md) covering [Berlin](3354.md) and [Brandenburg](3765.md).

These regional headquarters each have an investigation department and a mobile inspection and observation unit. Moreover, they control the 67 BPOL stations (BPOL-*Inspektion*) which in turn control the *Bundespolizeireviere* or precincts located in places that require a 24-hour presence by BPOL officers.

A special *Direktion* is responsible for [Frankfurt International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_International_Airport).

The central school for advanced and vocational training is in Lübeck and controls the six basic training schools in [Swisttal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swisttal), [Neustrelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neustrelitz), [Oerlenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oerlenbach), [Walsrode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsrode), [Eschwege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschwege) and [Bamberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamberg). It is also in charge of the Federal Police Sport School in [Bad Endorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Endorf) and a competitive sport project in Kienbaum near Berlin. The sport school specialises in winter sport events and has trained many of Germany's top skiers and skaters such as [Claudia Pechstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Pechstein).

The *Zentrale Direktion Bundesbereitschaftspolizei* controls the mobile support and rapid reaction battalions located in [Bayreuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayreuth), [Deggendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deggendorf), Blumberg (near Berlin), [Hünfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%BCnfeld), [Uelzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uelzen), [Duderstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duderstadt), [Sankt Augustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Augustin), [Bad Bergzabern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Bergzabern), [Bad Düben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_D%C3%BCben) and [Ratzeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratzeburg). The number of *[Bereitschaftspolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereitschaftspolizei)* companies increased in March 2008 from 28 to 29 comprising approx. 25 percent of Germany's police support units.[^cite_note-3]

### BPOL Special Units

The following special units also exist:

- The BPOL Aviation Group is subordinate to the Bundespolizei Direktion 11 (BPOLD 11) in Berlin. It controls the five aviation squadrons around the country that operate the force's helicopters. These are located in [Fuhlendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuhlendorf,_Schleswig-Holstein) (north, with satellite airfield in [Gifhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifhorn)), Blumberg (east), Fuldatal (centre), [Oberschleißheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberschlei%C3%9Fheim) (south) and Sankt Augustin (west). Its duties include; [border surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_surveillance), monitoring installations belonging to [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn), helping in serious accidents and disasters in Germany and abroad, searching for missing persons, searching for criminals on the run, supporting the [police forces of the federal states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landespolizei), providing transportation for persons whose security is endangered, providing transportation for guests of the Federal government, supporting federal and state authorities, and providing air search and rescue services in coordination with the 12 air rescue centers throughout Germany.

- The [BFE+ units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_unit#BFE+) (abbreviated from *Securing of Evidence and Arrest of Suspects*) are a specialized division of regular BPOL [arresting units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_unit). These units were organized after the [2015 Charlie Hebdo attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hebdo_shooting) in France with the aim of responding faster and with higher firepower to massive terrorist attacks. BFE+ units are decentralized and work as a first response force until the more specialized and centralized GSG9 arrive at the scene, and are equipped similarly to [SEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spezialeinsatzkommando) units.

- The [GSG 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSG_9) [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) group is directly subordinate to the BPOL HQ.

- The BPOL Information and Communications Center is now a department of the BPOL HQ in Potsdam.

- Most special units of the Federal Police are subordinate to the unified command of [Federal Police Directorate 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundespolizeidirektion_11).

- The [water police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_police) stations with 16 patrol craft and helicopters are part of the [German Federal Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Federal_Coast_Guard) and assigned to coastal BPOL stations. The watercraft include six offshore patrol vessels, e.g. those of the [*Bad Bramstedt* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Bramstedt_class_patrol_vessel), and the large *Potsdam class* as well as a number of fast inshore vessels and one [tugboat](216434.md).

## History



Early green sleeve patch of the *Bundesgrenzschutz* (Federal Border Guard), used 1952–1976

In 1951, the West German government established the Federal Border Guard (*[Bundesgrenzschutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesgrenzschutz)* or BGS) composed of 10,000 men under the Federal Interior Ministry's jurisdiction. The force replaced allied military organisations such as the [U.S. Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Constabulary) then patrolling West Germany's borders. The BGS was described as a mobile, lightly armed police force for border and internal security despite fears that it would be the nucleus of a new German army. When West Germany did establish an army, the *[Bundeswehr](288188.md)*, BGS personnel were given the choice of staying in the BGS or joining the army. Most decided to join the army.

In 1953, the BGS took control of the German Passport Control Service. In 1972 the [Compulsory Border Guard Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_Border_Guard_Service) was enacted by the parliament, which – in theory – is still in force, but suspended, similar to the conscription for the *[Bundeswehr](288188.md)*.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] In 1976, the state police grades replaced the military rank structure and BGS training was modified to closely match that of the state police forces (*[Landespolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landespolizei)*). The West German Railway Police (*[Bahnpolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnpolizei)*), formerly an independent force, and the East German *[Transportpolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportpolizei)* were restructured under the BGS in 1990.



*Bundesgrenzschutz* officers in 1987

In July 2005, the BGS was renamed the *Bundespolizei* or BPOL (Federal Police) to reflect its transition to a multi-faceted federal police agency. The change also involved a shift to blue uniforms and livery for vehicles and helicopters. The German Interior Ministry reviewed the structure of the BPOL in 2007 and in March 2008 made the structure leaner to get more officers out of offices and onto patrol.

### Dogs

Approximately 500 working dogs are used in the Federal Police at present. Most of the dogs are [German Shepherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Shepherd). Other dog breeds are also used such as [Malinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Shepherd_Dog_\(Malinois\)), [German Wirehaired Pointer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Wirehaired_Pointer), [Giant Schnauzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Schnauzer), and [Rottweiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rottweiler). They accompany their handlers on daily missions in railway facilities, at airports, at the border or in physical security. Most working dogs live with the families of their handlers. Basic and advanced training is performed under the supervision of the Federal Police Academy at the Federal Police canine schools in [Bleckede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleckede) (Lower Saxony) and [Neuendettelsau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuendettelsau) (Bavaria) where dogs and handlers go through patrol dog and explosive detection courses.

## Ranks

In 2003, the federal German police ranks and insignia were unified with those used by police units of federal lands.
