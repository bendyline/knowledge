The **XIX International Chopin Piano Competition** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *XIX Międzynarodowy Konkurs Pianistyczny im. Fryderyka Chopina*) was held from 2 to 23 October 2025 in [Warsaw](32908.md), Poland.

The 2025 competition marked the beginning of the [International Chopin Piano Competition](1598653.md)'s centenary celebrations and drew a record 642 applicants. Following a preliminary round, in which 162 candidates were selected to perform, 85 pianists were admitted to the main stage, including 19 prize-winners of other major piano competitions who qualified directly. The competition proceeded through three solo stages, after which eleven pianists advanced to the final. For the first time, finalists were required to perform the [Polonaise-Fantaisie, Op. 61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaise-Fantaisie_\(Chopin\)), in addition to one of Chopin's [two piano concertos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopin's_compositions_for_piano_and_orchestra) with the [Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Philharmonic_Orchestra).

The competition was won by [Eric Lu](48574279.md) of the United States, with [Kevin Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Chen_\(pianist\)) of Canada taking second place and [Zitong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitong_Wang) of China third. The jury's verdict was met with some surprise by commentators, as no clear frontrunner had emerged during the competition, in contrast to previous editions. Jury chairman [Garrick Ohlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrick_Ohlsson) noted that the deliberations had been long and difficult.

## Background

The [Chopin Competition](1598653.md) is considered Poland's most important musical event and one of the most prestigious piano competitions in the world. Commentators have called the event the "[Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympics) of the piano world";[^cite_note-NYTBarone-1][^cite_note-HewettTelegraph-2][^cite_note-MinderFT-3] Joshua Barone of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* wrote that victory can be "just as glorious as a [gold medal](526488.md)".[^cite_note-NYTBarone-1][^cite_note-GFPA-4] The competition generates a nationwide cultural phenomenon described as "Chopin-mania";[^cite_note-MinderFT-3] according to the Chopin Institute, tickets released online in October 2024 sold out within 30 minutes, with those for the finals selling out in two minutes.[^cite_note-F24-5] This demand extended to the box office, with hundreds of people queuing for hours, often from before 5 a.m., for a limited number of standby tickets to the daily auditions.[^cite_note-PolskieRadioQueue-6] Jury member [Nelson Goerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Goerner) called the public's engagement a "kind of national passion", asking, "where else would people queue for hours to hear a dozen pianists perform the same waltz?"[^cite_note-MinderFT-3]



The [National Philharmonic in Warsaw](53768962.md) hosted the competition.

Pianists born between 1995 and 2009 were eligible to participate.[^cite_note-GFPA-4] A record 642 pianists applied, which [Fryderyk Chopin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin_Institute) Director [Artur Szklener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Szklener) characterized as a sign of both the expanding pool of excellent pianists and the increasing difficulty talented musicians face in forging careers in a commercialized market.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier1-7] [Wojciech Grzędziński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Grz%C4%99dzi%C5%84ski?action=edit\&redlink=1), who has served as the competition's official photographer since 2005, noted a significant shift in the profile of the participants. He observed that while competitors in 2005 were often at the very beginning of their careers, the pianists in recent editions are "young professionals" who arrive with established careers and use the competition to "consolidate" their standing in the classical music world.[^cite_note-PolskieRadioGrzedzinski-8] The competitor pool also reflected a notable geographical shift, with Asian pianists particularly prominent.[^cite_note-HewettTelegraph-2]

### Centenary celebrations

The 2025 edition held special significance as it marked the beginning of the competition's centenary celebrations. The official 100th anniversary falls in 2027, but the Fryderyk Chopin Institute initiated a five-year series of events and projects that began on 2 October 2025 and will culminate with the 20th edition in 2030.[^cite_note-Accompanying-9][^cite_note-10] Director Artur Szklener said the competition represented "a century of musical heritage, a galaxy of the most outstanding pianists of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, but also a completely new social phenomenon: a sort of nationwide celebration of Chopin's music".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier1-7]

The centenary prompted a number of accompanying events that transformed Warsaw into a city-wide celebration of the composer. Initiatives included "music lover zones" (*strefy melomana*) where audiences could watch live broadcasts; over 30 establishments creating special Chopin-themed menus, desserts, and cocktails; a multimedia laser show set to Chopin's music at the [Polish Army Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Army_Stadium); jazz concerts at [Chopin's birthplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthplace_of_Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin) in [Żelazowa Wola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBelazowa_Wola); and a new film, *[Chopin, a Sonata in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopin,_a_Sonata_in_Paris)*, which premiered at the [Gdynia Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdynia_Film_Festival). Numerous exhibitions were held across the city, including an immersive experience titled "Romantic Chopin", outdoor displays of archival photographs, and a temporary exhibition at the [Fryderyk Chopin Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin_Museum) organized in collaboration with the [Musée de la Vie romantique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_de_la_Vie_romantique) in Paris.[^cite_note-Accompanying-9] Further plans for the 2027 anniversary include a festival in Warsaw featuring past prize-winners, concerts around the world, and a Global Chopin Gala of interconnected concerts spanning time zones from Tokyo to Vancouver.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier1-7]

### Russian participants

The 2025 competition was the first held since the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). Organizers announced that Russian pianists would be admitted only as "individual neutral pianists", a policy the Chopin Institute compared to that for [athletes at the Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Neutral_Athletes_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics). Participants from Russia were also required to sign a statement condemning the violation of international law. Two pianists, [Philipp Lynov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Lynov?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Andrey Zenin, were admitted under these conditions.[^cite_note-F24-5]

### Poster competition

An international competition organized by the [Fryderyk Chopin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin_Institute) and the [Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts,_Warsaw) selected the poster design for promoting the competition. Fifteen artists submitted a total of 30 entries. The jury, consisting of [Błażej Ostoja Lniski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C5%82a%C5%BCej_Ostoja_Lniski?action=edit\&redlink=1), Prot Jarnuszkiewicz, [Mieczysław Wasilewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieczys%C5%82aw_Wasilewski), and Artur Szklener, awarded the 40,000 złoty (US\$10,000) prize to Marcin Władyka. All submitted poster designs were exhibited from 1 to 31 October 2025 at the Academy's [Czapski Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czapski_Palace).[^cite_note-11]

## Main stage

The main competition from 3 to 20 October consisted of three stages and a final. An inaugural concert was held on 2 October, and the prize-winners' concerts took place from 21 to 23 October.[^cite_note-Rules-12]

The 2025 edition introduced a new system for determining the order of performances. In previous competitions, a single letter drawn by lot would set the alphabetical order for all stages. Citing scientific research on judging in international music competitions, organizers modified the procedure to address a potential disadvantage for those performing earliest. According to Szklener, jurors require time to establish an internal scale for the competition's artistic level, which can result in the first performances serving as a baseline and potentially receiving less precise or favorable evaluations. To ensure greater fairness, the starting letter for the performance order was advanced by six letters of the Latin alphabet for each successive stage, distributing the early performance slots more evenly among the participants.[^cite_note-SzklenerInterview-15]

### Program



The 2025 edition introduced the *[Polonaise-Fantaisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaise-Fantaisie_\(Chopin\))*, Op. 61, as a required work in the final alongside the piano concerto.

Participants had to select a different program for each stage of the competition. The competition repertoire had to be played from memory and could be performed in any order. Contestants could not play the same piece twice in different stages of the competition, though they could perform pieces they performed in the preliminary round (except the first two etudes) in the main stage. Participants could use any available edition of Chopin's works, though the [Chopin National Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopin_National_Edition) is recommended.[^cite_note-Rules-12]

The 2025 edition introduced the [Polonaise-Fantaisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaise-Fantaisie_\(Chopin\)), Op. 61, as a required work in the final alongside the piano concerto. According to Szklener, while the piano concertos provide a virtuosic and grand conclusion, they were written early in Chopin's career and offer a limited view of his compositional style at a crucial decision-making stage of the competition. He described the Polonaise-Fantaisie as standing at the opposite pole from the brilliant idiom of the concertos, characterizing it as one of Chopin's last works with quasi-improvisational, dreamlike, and ethereal qualities, featuring harmonically ambiguous passages with almost impressionistic coloring, blurred polonaise rhythm, and an inverted formal structure in which the principal theme serves as the composition's goal rather than its starting point. Szklener said the juxtaposition allows finalists to present a fuller palette of pianistic means and enables jurors to assess their maturity, while the form of the Polonaise-Fantaisie allows for a cohesive artistic narrative in which it paves the way for the concerto, referencing the tradition of the improvised prelude.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier1-7]

Other program changes for 2025 included the addition of a waltz in the first round. Szklener noted that Chopin's waltzes require not just understanding but a profound sense of the gestures of couples on a dance floor. The change also enabled competitors to perform the complete set of 24 [Preludes, Op. 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preludes_\(Chopin\)), during the second round. While the complete set could have been played during the previous two competitions in the third round instead of a sonata, the 2025 rules restored the obligatory status of the sonata in the semifinal while extending the maximum duration of the second-round recital to 50 minutes. Szklener expressed satisfaction that 20 participants opted to perform the complete set of preludes, calling the preludes a genre in which Chopin combined references to [Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach) with a modern compositional approach, investing miniature forms with diverse pianistic and emotional content while transferring architectural relations to the level of the set as a whole.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier1-7]

## Timeline

### Inaugural concert



The 2021 winner, [Bruce Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Liu), performed Saint-Saëns's [Piano Concerto No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Concerto_No._5_\(Saint-Sa%C3%ABns\)) at the inaugural concert.

The competition opened on 2 October with an inaugural concert by the [Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Philharmonic_Orchestra) under the direction of [Andrey Boreyko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Boreyko). The program framed Chopin's music with works by composers connected to him: [Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach), [Camille Saint-Saëns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Saint-Sa%C3%ABns), and [Francis Poulenc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Poulenc). The concert began with [Grzegorz Fitelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grzegorz_Fitelberg)'s arrangement of Chopin's [Polonaise in A major, Op. 40 No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaises_Op._40_\(Chopin\)) for orchestra. The 2021 winner, [Bruce Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Liu), performed Saint-Saëns's [Piano Concerto No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Concerto_No._5_\(Saint-Sa%C3%ABns\)). Jurors [Garrick Ohlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrick_Ohlsson) (1970 winner) and [Yulianna Avdeeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulianna_Avdeeva) (2010 winner) performed Poulenc's [Concerto for Two Pianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerto_for_Two_Pianos_\(Poulenc\)). The evening concluded with Liu, Ohlsson, and Avdeeva being joined by fellow juror and 1980 winner [Đặng Thái Sơn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%B7ng_Th%C3%A1i_S%C6%A1n) for Bach's [Concerto for Four Pianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWV_1065), a performance music critic John Allison hailed as a "moment of pianistic history".[^cite_note-23]

### Stage I

Stage I of the competition took place from 3 to 7 October. Eighty-four pianists each performed a recital of approximately 30 minutes, which included a required étude, nocturne, waltz, and a large-scale work (a ballade, the [Barcarolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcarolle_\(Chopin\)), or the [Fantaisie in F minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantaisie_in_F_minor_\(Chopin\))).[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D1-24] Reviewers noted the challenge for the first participants in setting the standard for the competition, with Jakub Puchalski of the *Chopin Courier* observing that listeners' expectations are often shaped by recordings of past masters.[^cite_note-ChopinCourierPuchalski3-25]

#### 3 October

On the first day, 3 October, [Ryan Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Wang) drew significant praise, with Jed Distler of *[Gramophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramophone_\(magazine\))* calling his performance the "most striking impression" of the day and a "masterclass in how to contour Chopin's polyphony".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D1-24] Puchalski also commended Wang for "convincingly gauging the drama" of his nocturne.[^cite_note-ChopinCourierPuchalski3-25] [Jan Widlarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Widlarz) was noted by multiple outlets; reviewers for *[Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio)* found his performance reliably and professionally prepared, though perhaps staying "within a safe zone",[^cite_note-PolskieRadioWidlarz-26] while Mateusz Ciupka of the Polish music magazine *[Ruch Muzyczny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruch_Muzyczny?action=edit\&redlink=1)* praised his "retro" style as a continuation of the "old, good Polish school of playing Chopin".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeEtap1-27] Tomoharu Ushida was highlighted by Ciupka as a "pianist of perfect balance" in every musical aspect.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeEtap1-27] Other notable performers included Miki Yamagata, praised by Distler for her "communicative energy",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D1-24] and Victoria Wong, who reviewers from both *Gramophone* and *Polskie Radio* noted gave a solid and well-prepared recital on her return to the competition.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D1-24][^cite_note-PolskieRadioWong-28]

#### 4 October

On the second day, 4 October, [Kevin Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Chen_\(pianist\)) received exceptional reviews, with Marcin Gmys of the *Chopin Courier* calling him "the greatest among them", highlighting his [Fantaisie in F minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantaisie_in_F_minor_\(Chopin\)),[^cite_note-ChopinCourierGmys4-29] and Distler describing his playing as "unambiguously transcendental virtuosity on every level".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D2-30] Gmys also identified him as one of the top contenders for the gold medal.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Aleatoryzm-31] At 17, Jacky Zhang impressed reviewers with his maturity; Distler praised his inventive voicings,[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D2-30] Łucja Siedlik of the *Chopin Courier* noted he "breathed life into the music",[^cite_note-ChopinCourierSiedlik4-32] and *Polskie Radio* commended his sense of timing.[^cite_note-PolskieRadioZhang-33] Yanyan Bao was described by Distler as a "rare breed" for her ability to project strong ideas,[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D2-30] while *Polskie Radio* called her a "master of subtlety and poetic mood".[^cite_note-PolskieRadioBasista-34] Piotr Alexewicz also received positive notices for being in "stellar form",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D2-30][^cite_note-ChopinCourierSiedlik4-32] with Gmys later highlighting his "superbly constructed" Fantaisie and calling him a potential finalist.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Aleatoryzm-31]

#### 5 October

On 5 October, 16-year-old Zihan Jin was called "the real deal" and a "remarkable talent" by Distler for his "ravishingly contoured" playing.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D3-35] Eric Guo's interpretation was noted for its unique qualities; Distler found his playing "stimulating and refreshing", attributing his lean textures to experience with period instruments,[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D3-35] while Anna Chęćka of *Ruch Muzyczny* praised his masterful use of piano resonance and polyphony.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Pianoludzie-36] Gmys considered Guo a frontrunner aiming for a "Chopin Grand Slam" after his 2023 victory at the Chopin Competition on Period Instruments.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Aleatoryzm-31] Shiori Kuwahara's recital was hailed by Monika Pasiecznik of *Ruch Muzyczny* as the "culmination of the entire day," praising her monumental sound and a Ballade in F minor that synthesized her many strengths.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeEtap1-27] Yang (Jack) Gao delivered what Distler called a "strong and memorable set" and Siedlik an "excellent, evenly balanced performance".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D3-35][^cite_note-ChopinCourierSiedlik5-37] Adam Kałduński was described as an individualist,[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D3-35][^cite_note-PolskieRadioKhrikuli-38] with Pasiecznik noting his deep, personal tone and Chęćka praising his artistic maturity.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeEtap1-27][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Pianoludzie-36]

#### 6 October

On 6 October, established artist and returning prize-winner [Eric Lu](48574279.md) demonstrated what Distler called "formidable mastery".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D4-39] Both Gmys and Chęćka questioned his decision to return, calling it a risky move that could harm his established career.[^cite_note-ChopinCourierGmys6-40][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Pianoludzie-36] Sixteen-year-old Tianyao Lyu's performance was a highlight, with Gmys calling it a "near revelatory moment" and praising her "dazzling" ['Double Thirds' Etude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tude_Op._25,_No._6_\(Chopin\)).[^cite_note-ChopinCourierGmys6-40] Chęćka described her as a "serious talent",[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Pianoludzie-36] while Distler found her [Barcarolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcarolle_\(Chopin\)) "heartfelt, elegant and proportioned".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D4-39] [Nathalia Milstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathalia_Milstein) delivered one of the "most consistently satisfying presentations" of the first stage, according to Distler, and Ruben Micieli was praised for communicating the influence of *[bel canto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel_canto)* on Chopin's music.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D4-39]

#### 7 October

On the final day of auditions, 7 October, the competition's youngest participant, 15-year-old Yulia Nakashima, delivered a performance where Klaudia Baranowska of the *Chopin Courier* noted her "impressive technical prowess",[^cite_note-ChopinCourierBaranowska7-41] and Distler pointed to her "remarkable command" and natural musicality.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D5-42] Yehuda Prokopowicz was praised by Baranowska for his "refined, poetical sound" and "huge artistic potential",[^cite_note-ChopinCourierBaranowska7-41] while Distler found his Fantasy reminiscent of "[Van Cliburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cliburn) in his prime".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D5-42] [Anthony Ratinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ratinov?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s performance was noted by Distler for its "sharpened focus",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D5-42] and by Dorota Szwarcman of the *Chopin Courier* for excelling in technique and structure.[^cite_note-ChopinCourierSzwarcman7-43] Gabriele Strata was described as a strong personality with a narrative style,[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D5-42][^cite_note-ChopinCourierSzwarcman7-43][^cite_note-PolskieRadioStrata-44] with Ciupka comparing his powerful bass sound to that of an "organist in the guise of a pianist".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeEtap1-27] Returning finalist Hao Rao was seen to have matured since the 2021 competition.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S1D5-42][^cite_note-ChopinCourierSzwarcman7-43]

#### Results

Following the conclusion of Stage I, the jury announced that 40 participants would advance to the second stage.[^cite_note-PolskieRadioResults-45] The results were met with some surprise, with Gmys noting the "very strong" impression that the competition was "ruled by chance" due to the elimination of strong contenders such as Mateusz Dubiel, Wei-Ting Hsieh, and Yulia Nakashima.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Aleatoryzm-31] The Polish representation was reduced from 13 to four,[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeEtap1-27] while pianists from China formed a significant contingent, with 14 of the 29 Chinese participants advancing.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Aleatoryzm-31]

### Stage II

Stage II of the competition took place from 9 to 12 October. Participants were required to perform a recital of 40 to 50 minutes, which had to include one of several specified polonaises and a group of six consecutive preludes from [Op. 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preludes_\(Chopin\)), with the option to perform the entire cycle.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D1-46]

#### 9 October

On the first day, 9 October, [Kevin Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Chen_\(pianist\))'s performance of the complete [Études, Op. 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tudes_\(Chopin\)), a first for the competition, drew significant attention. Gmys wrote that the audience was "watching history unfold live",[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S2D1-47] and commentators for *[Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio)* compared his technical and expressive command favorably to that of [Bruce Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Liu) and [Yunchan Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunchan_Lim).[^cite_note-PolskieRadioChen_S2D1-48] Mateusz Ciupka of *Ruch Muzyczny* noted his performance, "devoid of technical limitations," allowed the works to shine as musically rich miniatures.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIEtap-49] Distler noted Chen's "superhuman virtuoso display" but questioned if the music was sometimes lost "underneath the stunt".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D1-46] Yanyan Bao received universal acclaim; Distler called her "humanly super" and praised her for conveying "maturity beyond her 18 years", while Klaudia Baranowska of the *Chopin Courier* found her a "pianist of great artistic maturity, and deep sensitivity".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S2D1-50] *Polskie Radio* commentators were "delighted" by her playing, noting her "sparks of imagination".[^cite_note-PolskieRadioBao_S2D1-51] Yang Gao's performance of the complete Op. 28 Preludes was praised by Distler as "consistently satisfying", while Gmys noted that "ideas danced off every page".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D1-46][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S2D1-47] Piotr Alexewicz's interpretation of the same cycle drew divided responses: Distler was "largely disappointed",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D1-46] whereas Baranowska praised his "more personal and thoughtful vision" and Ciupka highlighted its "cohesive and dark" quality.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S2D1-50][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIEtap-49] *Polskie Radio* also deemed his performance successful.[^cite_note-PolskieRadioBao_S2D1-51] [Jonas Aumiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Aumiller?action=edit\&redlink=1) was noted for his "thoughtful, intellectual approach" to programming and what Distler called his "reserved Classicism", though reviewers for *Polskie Radio* found his [Polonaise in F-sharp minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaise_in_F-sharp_minor,_Op._44_\(Chopin\)) lacked an "oneiric" quality.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D1-46][^cite_note-PolskieRadioBao_S2D1-51] Zixi Chen was lauded by Distler for his "dazzling fireworks display" in the [Rondo in C minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondo_in_C_minor_\(Chopin\)), which Gmys also found "refreshing".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D1-46][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S2D1-47]

#### 10 October

On the second day, 10 October, the morning session was met with reservations from reviewers; commentators for *Polskie Radio* described it as a "great series of doubts", and Dorota Szwarcman of the *Chopin Courier* felt the Preludes performances did not reach the level of the previous day.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Szwarcman_S2D2-52][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Morning_S2D2-53] Despite this, individual pianists drew praise. Eric Guo was commended for his mazurkas, with Distler writing that they "truly 'mazurked'".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D2-54] Distler also noted David Khrikuli's improvisatory style and Adam Kałduński's "thoughtful, unhurried interpretations".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D2-54] The evening session featured a standout performance by [Shiori Kuwahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiori_Kuwahara?action=edit\&redlink=1), whom Distler described as demonstrating "big, Romantic pianism" and whose [A-flat Polonaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaise_in_A-flat_major,_Op._53) he felt Arthur Rubinstein "would have been proud" of.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D2-54] Puchalski also credited her with one of the competition's finest interpretations of the [Barcarolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcarolle_\(Chopin\)).[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S2D2-55] Tianyou Li was called the "pleasant surprise of the day" by *Polskie Radio* for his performance of the [Sonata No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._1_\(Chopin\)).[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Evening_S2D2-56] Returning competitor [Hyuk Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyuk_Lee?action=edit\&redlink=1) was seen by Puchalski to have matured since 2021,[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S2D2-55] and Krzysztof Stefański of *Ruch Muzyczny* praised his lyrical and dramatic performance, positioning him as a podium contender.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIEtap-49] Distler, however, maintained his earlier opinion that Lee's playing, while technically proficient, "doesn't ever surprise you".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D2-54]

#### 11 October

On 11 October, returning prize-winner [Eric Lu](48574279.md) received mixed reviews for his performance of the ["Funeral March" Sonata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._2_\(Chopin\)). Distler praised his "utterly mesmerising" third movement but found other parts "sectionalised",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D3-57] commentators for *Polskie Radio* questioned his program choices and felt he had not recaptured the "subtle poet" quality of his 2015 performance,[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Lu_S2D3-58] and Gmys described his sonata as "heavy and indigestible".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_StatekPlynie-59] In contrast, [Nathalia Milstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathalia_Milstein) received strong praise for her complete Preludes cycle, with Puchalski noting her traditional approach and clear pedaling, and Distler admiring the "transparency and sinew" of her playing.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S2D3-60][^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D3-57] Ciupka highlighted her ironic and unconventional interpretations.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIEtap-49] Distler also called Ruben Micieli's performance a "glorious concert",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D3-57] while Ciupka noted his construction of a "highly personal, funereal requiem" from Chopin's works.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIEtap-49] Zhexiang Li delivered what Distler described as "one virtuoso knockout after another", including a "demonic" [Scherzo No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scherzo_No._3_\(Chopin\)).[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D3-57] Tianyao Lyu impressed Szwarcman with her "impeccable technique, humour and charisma", while Xiaoxuan Li's Preludes were described by Distler as "jewels".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D3-57][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Szwarcman_S2D3-61] Other notable performances included that of [Piotr Pawlak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Pawlak), whose *[Allegro de concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegro_de_concert_\(Chopin\))* was described as "symphonic" by *Polskie Radio*, and Yumeka Nakagawa and Vincent Ong, who both received positive notices for their renditions of the complete Preludes cycle.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D3-57][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S2D3-60][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Pawlak_S2D3-62]

#### 12 October

On the final day of Stage II, 12 October, William Yang gave a performance that Distler felt "obliterated whatever reservations" he had from the first stage, praising his interpretation as one that "avoids clichés, while embracing the modern piano's sonic and expressive potential".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D4-63] Pasiecznik hailed his unique and consistent vision, stating that he revealed a "new, separate Chopin" to the listener.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIEtap-49] Chęćka later categorized his style as "Apollonian", describing him as a "humble virtuoso" whose restrained approach created a "profound beauty".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Piekno-64] Gabriele Strata's recital was highlighted by Baranowska as "one of the most moving moments in the morning session", with a performance of the rarely-heard *[Boléro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%A9ro_\(Chopin\))* that combined "rhythmic precision with engaging freedom".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S2D4-65] Distler also praised Strata's "fusion of meticulous workmanship, recreative fantasy and obsession with beautiful sound", likening it to that of [Ivan Moravec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Moravec).[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2D4-63] Yehuda Prokopowicz was lauded for his "poetic" playing and "original" interpretations by *Polskie Radio*, and Baranowska was particularly impressed by his "well-conceived" performance of the [Op. 17 Mazurkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazurkas,_Op._17_\(Chopin\)).[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S2D4-65][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Prokopowicz_S2D4-66] [Zitong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitong_Wang) garnered attention for her eclectic program and what Gmys called a "courageous" and sensitive interpretation, while [Tomoharu Ushida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoharu_Ushida) was noted for his "splendid playing, with lovely tone".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S2D4-67]

#### Results

Following the conclusion of Stage II, the jury announced that 20 participants would advance to the third stage.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Results_S2-68] Distler expressed regret over the elimination of Yanyan Bao and Gabriele Strata, but noted that David Khrikuli, William Yang, and Vincent Ong had showcased themselves to "far better advantage" in the second stage, likely factoring into their advancement.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S2_Eval-69] Chęćka suggested that Khrikuli's advancement signaled the jury's openness to more "Dionysian", or controversial, performers,[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Piekno-64] while Gmys highlighted Piotr Pawlak's success with the rarely played *Allegro de concert* and Tianyao Lyu's "gigantic talent".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_StatekPlynie-59] Gmys concluded by observing that no modern competitor attempted to emulate the style of "golden era" masters like [Rachmaninoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Rachmaninoff) or [Rubinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rubinstein), a trend he attributed to the pragmatic expectations of modern juries.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_StatekPlynie-59]

### Stage III

Stage III of the competition took place from 14 to 16 October. The 20 remaining participants were required to perform a recital of 45 to 55 minutes, which had to include one of Chopin's two mature piano sonatas ([Op. 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._2_\(Chopin\)) or [Op. 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._3_\(Chopin\))) and a complete opus of mazurkas.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D1-70] Reviewers noted that the sonatas, in particular, proved to be a significant hurdle for many participants, with Mateusz Ciupka of *Ruch Muzyczny* observing that several acclaimed pianists struggled with the technical and structural demands of the large-scale works.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIIEtap-71]

#### 14 October

On the first day, 14 October, Yang Gao opened the stage with a performance Distler described as "world-class perfection", while commentators for *[Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio)* noted that he "operated more with color than tempo".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D1-70][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Gao_S3D1-72] Eric Guo was called the "most intriguing and complete interpretation of the session" by Puchalski, with Distler praising his "eloquent" and "poetic" playing, particularly in the mazurkas.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D1-70][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S3D1-73] David Khrikuli's performance was hailed by *Polskie Radio* as a historic moment: "We were witnesses to something great. Something like this happens... once every few Competitions".[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Gao_S3D1-72] Distler also praised his sonata, likening its "mastery of timescale" to that of [Emil Gilels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Gilels),[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D1-70] while Stefański noted he excelled in capturing both the "ludic and salon" qualities of the mazurkas.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIIEtap-71] [Shiori Kuwahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiori_Kuwahara?action=edit\&redlink=1) drew conflicting reviews; Dorota Szwarcman of the *Chopin Courier* called her "a name to reckon with", and Distler praised her "healthy and generous" interpretations,[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D1-70][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Szwarcman_S3D1-74] but *Polskie Radio* found her playing "forceful" and her mazurkas "one-dimensional".[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Kuwahara_S3D1-75] Chęćka later identified her as an "ideal candidate for gold", an artist whose playing "transcends stereotypes".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Marsjasz-76] Tianyou Li was a "surprise" for Szwarcman, who called his sonata the "most expressive of the evening" and his [Variations, Op. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variations_on_%22L%C3%A0_ci_darem_la_mano%22), "no less captivating than [Bruce Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Liu)'s".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Szwarcman_S3D1-74] Hyo Lee and [Hyuk Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyuk_Lee?action=edit\&redlink=1) both delivered what Szwarcman deemed "excellent performances", though Distler gave a more critical assessment, calling Hyo's recital "uneven" and Hyuk's sonata a "blunt and blustering non-event".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D1-70][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Szwarcman_S3D1-74]

#### 15 October

On 15 October, the day's schedule was altered after [Eric Lu](48574279.md) postponed his performance due to illness.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S3D2-77] Xiaoxuan Li was praised by Baranowska for the "lyricism and sound sensitivity" of his mazurkas, while Distler noted his gifts as a "tone painter", though he found Li's expressive gestures "somewhat deflated the music's continuity" in the sonata.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D2-78][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S3D2-77] *Polskie Radio* commentators also described him as a "cultured, refined pianist" of "great restraint".[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_LiLyu_S3D2-79] The 16-year-old Tianyao Lyu's performance elicited differing responses; Baranowska praised her "astonishing" maturity and a "consistently developed, suspenseful narrative" in the sonata,[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S3D2-77] while Chęćka found the performance a moving experience, describing Lyu as a "messenger of musical mystery" and her choice to end with the [Berceuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berceuse_\(Chopin\)) after the sonata's darkness as a profound "gesture of comfort".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Marsjasz-76] Distler and commentators for *Polskie Radio*, however, felt Lyu's interpretation was not yet fully personalized, with the latter noting she had trouble "getting out of a certain corset that she was constrained by".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D2-78][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_LiLyu_S3D2-79] Vincent Ong's recital sharply divided critics. Puchalski called his sonata a "revelation" that "unfolded through stark contrasts",[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S3D2-80] whereas commentators for *Polskie Radio* were highly critical, calling his playing "mechanical" and questioning the jury's decision to advance him.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_PawlakProkopowicz_S3D2-81] Distler also found his performance of the *Variations, Op. 2* "proficient yet characterless".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D2-78] Yehuda Prokopowicz was praised by *Polskie Radio* for his "excellent feel for the mazurka idiom" and "great intelligence",[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_PawlakProkopowicz_S3D2-81] though Distler noted that "myriad mishaps" in the sonata threw him "off his game".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D2-78] Miyu Shindo's performance also drew varied commentary; Puchalski wrote that she gave a "deeply emotional account" of her sonata[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S3D2-80] and Ciupka praised her self-control,[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIIEtap-71] while Distler found her interpretations "conventional to the point of anonymity", though he acknowledged that "a true personality emerges" when she prioritizes musicality.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D2-78] The day concluded with a widely praised performance by [Piotr Pawlak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Pawlak). Distler awarded him a "'third time's the charm' award", highlighting his "dazzling virtuosity" in the rarely-heard *[Rondo à la Krakowiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondo_%C3%A0_la_Krakowiak)* and his "altogether more idiomatic" mazurkas.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D2-78] Puchalski noted his "literal" approach created "structural cohesion and order",[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_S3D2-80] while *Polskie Radio* also lauded his "electrifying" performance of the Rondo.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_PawlakProkopowicz_S3D2-81]

#### 16 October

On the final day of Stage III, 16 October, [Tomoharu Ushida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoharu_Ushida) opened the day with a performance described by commentators for *Polskie Radio* as technically "almost flawless", though Distler found it "loud and undifferentiated".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_UshidaWangWu_S3D3-83] [Zitong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitong_Wang)'s recital drew polarized reviews; Baranowska called her "one of the most captivating personalities" of the competition with an "exquisitely considered vision", and both Stefański and Chęćka praised her excellent mazurkas,[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Baranowska_S3D3-84][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIIEtap-71][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Marsjasz-76] while Distler was critical of her tone, finding it "hollow and aggressive" despite a "shimmering and ghostly" finale to her sonata.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82] Yifan Wu, one of the youngest participants, was noted by Distler for delivering one of the competition's top performances of the [Ballade No. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballade_No._2_\(Chopin\)), though *Polskie Radio* felt his sonata performance revealed his "emotional immaturity".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_UshidaWangWu_S3D3-83] William Yang received universal acclaim for what Distler called a display of "astonishing brilliance and arresting originality". Gmys described him as one of the "most striking eccentrics" with "dazzling virtuosity",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S3D3-85] with Chęćka noting his playing was an "embodiment of Chopin's principle of simplicity" and marking him as a potential winner.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Marsjasz-76] *Polskie Radio* commentators hailed it as "one of the best performances of Stage III".[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_YangChenLu_S3D3-86] Piotr Alexewicz delivered what Distler considered his "best playing... so far", with mazurkas played as if he was "inventing the music on the spot",[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82] though Gmys felt the recital had "occasional blemishes" and Stefański pointed to issues with "inaccuracy in the realization of the text".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S3D3-85][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIIEtap-71] [Kevin Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Chen_\(pianist\))'s technically accomplished performance was viewed differently by critics. Stefański declared that Chen "keeps the competition at a distance", and Gmys saw him as a contender for multiple special prizes,[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeIIIEtap-71][^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_S3D3-85] while Distler praised his "restraint and mindful music-making".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82] Commentators for *Polskie Radio*, however, felt a "sense of slight disappointment", finding the interpretation lacked a personal connection to the music.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_YangChenLu_S3D3-86] Returning after his illness, [Eric Lu](48574279.md) gave a performance that divided reviewers. Distler lauded his "technical mastery, rainbow of colours and poetic ardour", calling his rendition of the [Op. 56 Mazurkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazurkas,_Op._56_\(Chopin\)) among the competition's finest. *Polskie Radio* commentators were split, with one hailing "the return of the poet" while another found it "mannered" and "calculated".[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3D3-82][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_YangChenLu_S3D3-86]

#### Results

Following the conclusion of Stage III, the jury announced that 11 participants would advance to the final. Distler expressed gladness at the selection of Shiori Kuwahara, David Khrikuli, and William Yang, but was "greatly disappointed" by the elimination of Yang Gao, whom he considered to have delivered the "most consistently satisfying, probing and committed" interpretations in every round.[^cite_note-Gramophone_S3_Eval-87] Commentators for *Polskie Radio* considered the eliminations of Eric Guo and Hyuk Lee to be the biggest surprises, and noted that unlike in previous competitions, no clear frontrunner had emerged for the title.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_GuoLee_S3-88] Chęćka speculated that the jury's selections promoted pianists who blended "aristocratic nobility and the purest musicality".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_Marsjasz-76]

### Final

The final round took place from 18 to 20 October. Each finalist was required to perform the [Polonaise-Fantaisie, Op. 61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonaise-Fantaisie_\(Chopin\)), followed by one of Chopin's two piano concertos with the [Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Philharmonic_Orchestra) conducted by [Andrey Boreyko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Boreyko). Seven finalists opted to perform the [Piano Concerto No. 1 in E minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Concerto_No._1_\(Chopin\)) and four chose the [Piano Concerto No. 2 in F minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Concerto_No._2_\(Chopin\)).[^cite_note-ChopinCompetition_FinalSchedule-89] Monika Pasiecznik of *Ruch Muzyczny* noted that the inclusion of the Polonaise-Fantaisie, a work of significant interpretative difficulty, raised the threshold for the finalists.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90]

#### 18 October

On the first day of the final, 18 October, Tianyou Li's performance received a reserved assessment from Distler, who found his Polonaise-Fantaisie played "carefully and shapelessly" and his Concerto in E minor dogged by a "lack of character and contrast".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD1-91] Pasiecznik characterized Li's playing as technically impeccable yet interpretively safe, resulting in a performance of the concerto that she found exceptionally [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_\(music\)) and even [Mozartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart) in character.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90]

Returning prize-winner [Eric Lu](48574279.md) offered a Concerto in F minor that Puchalski called a "firmly articulated and meticulously shaped vision", and a Polonaise-Fantaisie he deemed the "most coherently" prepared of the evening.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_FinalD1-92] Pasiecznik also noted Lu played "more personally and gives himself over to emotions", finding a "touch of madness" in the Polonaise-Fantaisie's finale and praising his concerto for combining melancholy with an attention to detail.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Distler noted Lu's "tonal magic" in the Polonaise-Fantaisie, but felt it "lacked rhythmic backbone" and that the concerto's rondo "never managed to dance".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD1-91]

Tianyao Lyu's performance of the Concerto in E minor was deemed the highlight. Distler wrote that only Lyu "brought this concerto to life tonight, with consistent sparkle and rhythmic point in every phrase", praising her interaction with the orchestra.[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD1-91] Commentators for *[Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio)* agreed, with Róża Światczyńska stating that "it was precisely the concerto part in which her talent blossomed in all its glory" and that the piece was "as if written by Chopin especially for her".[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_FinalD1-93] Pasiecznik praised Lyu's understanding of the "Chopin idiom", highlighting the "clear reminiscences of dance" in her Polonaise-Fantaisie and a concerto that impressed with its "youthful energy", in which "delicacy was juxtaposed with an eruption of temperament".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Puchalski added that Lyu reminded the audience of what Chopin's concertos truly are: "youthful works of spontaneous inspiration, brilliant in the most natural way, songful and radiant".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_FinalD1-92]

The day concluded with Vincent Ong, whose recital drew varied responses. Puchalski praised his Concerto in E minor for its "maturity" and "palette of mysterious colours", writing that in terms of emotional depth, the concerto's Romanza "surely belonged to the Malaysian pianist".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Puchalski_FinalD1-92] Distler, however, found Ong's concerto lacking in contrast, though he credited the pianist as an "idea man" who held his attention in a "dynamically contrasted" Polonaise-Fantaisie.[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD1-91] Pasiecznik found Ong's Polonaise-Fantaisie "rather peculiar" and "essentially devoid of dance character", but was intrigued by his "undoubted musicality" in the concerto; she noted that while his use of large contrasts and sudden crescendos harmed the music's fluidity, it imbued the performance with a "particular energy" and a "distinct style".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90]

#### 19 October

On the second day, 19 October, Miyu Shindo was praised by Jessica Duchen of the *Chopin Courier* for her Polonaise-Fantaisie, which had a "powerful sense of atmosphere", and by Distler as the "finest and most authentic playing" he had heard from her in the competition.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Duchen_FinalD2-94][^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD2-95] Her Concerto in E minor, however, was seen by Distler as showcasing "the competitor over the artist",[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD2-95] while commentators for *[Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio)* found her cantilena lacked the "fluidity and free-flowing nature" required for the *style brillant*.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_FinalD2-96] Ciupka also found her performance disappointing, calling the Polonaise-Fantaisie a "cycle of unrelated episodes" divided by "mannered rubatos", and felt her concerto wavered between [Tchaikovskian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky) pathos and impressionistic explorations, culminating in a frustrating finale.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90]

[Zitong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitong_Wang)'s recital received a mixed reception. Her Polonaise-Fantaisie began with what Distler called "a few technical stumbles and highly exposed wrong chords", and commentators for *Polskie Radio* felt it was a "bad day" for her.[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD2-95][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_FinalD2-96] Despite this, Duchen praised her for showing "much mature musicianship" and creating a "rapt intensity" that helped the complex work feel "almost like an improvisation", and Ciupka noted that despite memory slips in the Polonaise-Fantaisie, her concerto had "a lot of light and warmth" in place of *brillante* displays, particularly praising the final rondo as a "celebration of a jaunty [krakowiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakowiak)" with beautifully differentiated articulation.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Duchen_FinalD2-94][^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Distler found her most convincing playing came in the concerto's slow movement, where her phrasing "reclaimed the fluidity and eloquence of her finest work", while Andrzej Sułek of *Polskie Radio* admired her "true, sincere personality".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD2-95][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_FinalD2-96]

William Yang's performance drew exceptional praise, with Distler declaring him "a genius" and "in a class by himself" for an interpretation that "eschews received opinion".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD2-95] Ciupka considered him the "greatest phenomenon of this Competition", praising his "flawless" execution and "most coherent interpretation" of the Polonaise-Fantaisie, which he achieved through a style of "feigned improvisation" that rejected sentimentality in favour of creating larger rhetorical wholes.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Duchen lauded his Polonaise-Fantaisie, which she considered the "finest" of the competition so far, noting that he "shaped it unerringly", and described his Concerto in F minor as a "blend of filigree delicacy" and "great clarity" with "a completely assured personal concept".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Duchen_FinalD2-94] In a contrasting assessment, commentators for *Polskie Radio* felt his performance was "withdrawn" and that in the concerto he was "hidden somewhere behind the orchestra".[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_FinalD2-96]

The final performer, [Piotr Alexewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Alexewicz?action=edit\&redlink=1), was described by Duchen as an "extrovert player" who "took ownership of the Polonaise-Fantasy in the very first notes".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Duchen_FinalD2-94] Ciupka contrasted his "extroverted" style, characterized by broad, narrative, and at times [Brahmsian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Brahms) phrasing with a "meaty, dark bass", with Yang's introversion, comparing their respective sounds to those of [Emil Gilels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Gilels) and [Keith Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Jarrett), though he also noted Alexewicz had "significant stumbles" at the end of the concerto's rondo.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Commentators for *Polskie Radio* hailed him as a "thoroughbred pianist and virtuoso who is not afraid of the orchestra", while Distler noted his "vibrant dynamic contrasts" and "hearty sense of narrative", though he felt the outer movements of the Concerto in F minor "lacked variety in articulation".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD2-95][^cite_note-PolskieRadio_FinalD2-96]

#### 20 October

On the final day of the competition, 20 October, [Shiori Kuwahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiori_Kuwahara?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s Polonaise-Fantaisie was described by Distler as "full-bodied and amply inflected", while Gmys found it "restrained" but with an "excellent command of the keyboard".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_FinalD3-97][^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98] Her Concerto in E minor was highly praised by Distler, who wrote that it "sang its heart out in every bar" and was played by "a young pianist with an old soul and a generous sound".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98] Stefański also noted her strength in building dramaturgy, describing a "turbulent" Polonaise-Fantaisie with a deep, sonorous tone, but felt this powerful, earthbound approach deprived the concerto's final krakowiak of lightness, resulting in a few stumbles.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90]

David Khrikuli's performance drew polarized reviews. Gmys called his Polonaise-Fantaisie "exuberant" and his Concerto in F minor a "serious rival" to William Yang's, noting that he "intoned the dramatic recitativo of the Larghetto superbly".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_FinalD3-97] Stefański praised him as one of the competition's "most original participants", who successfully combined brillant playing with "strong emotion" and delivered a "jaunty" and "spirited" finale, though he also cautioned that the pianist's "nonchalant" approach sometimes proved "treacherous".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Distler found he "drove through the Polonaise-Fantaisie with detached impatience" and that his concerto's "ultra-polished finger work" produced "two-dimensional, undifferentiated results".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98]

The final performance by [Kevin Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Chen_\(pianist\)) was met with surprise. Gmys praised his Polonaise-Fantaisie as "philosophically attuned", with Chen "sculpting silence with exquisite finesse".[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_FinalD3-97] His interpretation of the Concerto in E minor, however, was described by Gmys as a potential "disappointment" for many, with "moderate, even conservative, tempi" that foregrounded the work's "lyrical qualities" but lacked "youthful verve". Gmys suggested it was a deliberate artistic choice, an "unaffected abdication" from a purely virtuosic display.[^cite_note-ChopinCourier_Gmys_FinalD3-97] Stefański wrote that Chen's performance countered perceptions of him as an "emotionless technician", praising the "natural" Larghetto where the pianist "almost breathed with the orchestra" and noting that his technical skill allowed him to shade even the most virtuosic passages, but speculated that minor slips and a "slightly weaker disposition" on the night may have cost him the gold medal.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Distler gave a harsher assessment, calling the concerto a "heavy, four-square and charmless" performance that "seemed to go on forever".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98]

#### Results

The jury announced the results after a lengthy period of deliberation. American pianist [Eric Lu](48574279.md) was awarded the first prize, a notable achievement as he had placed fourth in the [2015 competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition). [Kevin Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Chen_\(pianist\)) of Canada was awarded the second prize, and [Zitong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitong_Wang) of China the third. Fourth prize was shared by Tianyao Lyu and Shiori Kuwahara, while fifth prize was shared by [Piotr Alexewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Alexewicz?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Vincent Ong. William Yang was awarded sixth prize.[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98]

The jury's verdict was met with surprise by several commentators. Marcin Majchrowski of *Polskie Radio* called the result a "considerable surprise for observers and commentators", noting that unlike in the previous two competitions, no clear frontrunner had emerged.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Results-99] Sułek commented on the field being "so even and at the same time so incomplete in terms of a certain ideal... expected from the first prize winner" that he would have found it "fair and appropriate" if no first prize had been awarded.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Results2-100] Światczyńska also expressed doubts, noting the difficulty she would have in selecting highlights from the winner's performances for a traditional laureate album.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Results2-100] Distler wrote that he "would not have predicted Eric Lu's first prize, nor Kevin Chen placing second", though he acknowledged their "virtuosic prowess and sheer finesse".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98] Distler also expressed surprise at Wang's third prize, felt that Yang "deserved better", and questioned why David Khrikuli did not place given his "overall strength in the solo rounds".[^cite_note-Gramophone_FinalD3-98] Majchrowski voiced similar regret for Khrikuli, who "ignited my imagination in Stage III", and felt that Shiori Kuwahara "shone the most" in the final. He also pointed to comments from jury chairman [Garrick Ohlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrick_Ohlsson) about "difficult discussions" and "long deliberations", suggesting a challenging decision-making process.[^cite_note-PolskieRadio_Results-99]

Stefański noted Lu's victory was like a "film script" after his illness in Stage III, and pointed to the dispersal of special prizes (including to non-finalists Adam Kałduński and Yehuda Prokopowicz) as evidence of the jury's difficult decisions.[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_RelacjeFinal-90] Gmys categorized the finalists into two interpretive camps based on [M. H. Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._H._Abrams)'s literary theory: "mirrors", who faithfully reflect the score (Chen, Kuwahara, Lu), and "lamps", who illuminate it with their own personality (Alexewicz, Khrikuli, Wang, Yang). Gmys also addressed public accusations that the competition was biased towards pianists from the Far East, refuting them as a "conspiracy" and attributing the success of these musicians to the constantly rising level of pianism in the region, a trend visible at international competitions worldwide, concluding that the final results were not a "scandal", as "in the face of such a balanced field, someone had to be eliminated".[^cite_note-RuchMuzyczny_BezKontrowersji-101]

### Voting procedure

Jury members gave each performance 1 to 25 points, with the following rating categories: Perfect (25), Exceptional (23–24), Very good (18–22), Good (16–17), Average (12–15), Below average (6–11), and Poor (1–5). Jury members did not evaluate their own students, defined as participants who had studied with them regularly for more than six months since the [2021 competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVIII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition) or with whom they had a relationship that could affect impartial judgment.[^cite_note-JuryRules-103] To ensure fairness, scores were adjusted using the Correction to the [Mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) (CMEAN) method: if any juror's score deviated from a contestant's mean by more than 3 points in round one or 2 points in subsequent rounds, it was adjusted to the nearest boundary value.[^cite_note-JuryRules-103] It has been argued that this approach can introduce ranking inconsistencies, reverse jurors' original preferences, and is susceptible to manipulation; the Jurors' Scores Transposition (JST) method, used in several international competitions including the 2025 National Chopin Competition in Miami, has been proposed as an alternative, which [normalizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalization_\(statistics\)) scores by ensuring each juror's set of scores has the same mean and [spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_dispersion), thereby preserving their original rankings and preferences.[^cite_note-104]

Cumulative scores were calculated using different weightings for each stage. After round two, scores were weighted 30% for round one and 70% for round two. After round three, the weighting shifted to 10% for round one, 20% for round two, and 70% for round three. Final scores combined all four stages: 10% round one, 20% round two, 35% round three, and 35% for the final. In principle, no more than 40 participants would advance to round two, 20 to round three, and 10 to the final. After the final performances, the jury chairman proposed a verdict on prize awards, which required approval by at least two-thirds of the jury. The jury could adjust the final ranking by up to one position from the cumulative scores (or two positions with three-quarters approval), and could also modify the distribution of prizes.[^cite_note-JuryRules-103]

For special prizes, each jury member submitted a list of three participants (worth 3, 2, and 1 points respectively), and the prize was awarded to the participant with the highest total, provided they received at least twice as many points as there were voting jury members. If no participant reached this threshold or there was a tie, the chairman would conduct a vote requiring an absolute majority; if this failed, the special prize would not be awarded.[^cite_note-JuryRules-103]
