**East Sussex** is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) in [South East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England). It is bordered by [Kent](16766.md) to the north-east, [West Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sussex) to the west, [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey) to the north-west, and the [English Channel](9230.md) to the south. The largest settlement is the city of [Brighton and Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_and_Hove).&#x20;

The county has an area of 1,792 km<sup>2</sup> (692 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 844,752 in 2024. The largest settlements are on the coast, and include [Brighton](49796.md) and [Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hove) in the south-west, [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne) in the south, and [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings) in the south-east. Away from the coast the county is largely rural, and its settlements include [Crowborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowborough) in the north, [Hailsham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailsham) in the south-centre, and [Lewes](67930.md) in the west. For [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes East Sussex comprises a [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county), with five districts, and the [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) of Brighton and Hove. East Sussex and West Sussex [historically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) formed a single county, [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex).&#x20;

The north east of East Sussex is part of the [Weald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weald), a [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) [anticline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticline) that was once an extensive woodland. The highest point in this area is Crowborough Hill (242 m (794 ft)), part of the High Weald uplands. The south west of the county is part of the [South Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs), a rolling [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) that stretches west into West Sussex and Hampshire. [Ditchling Beacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditchling_Beacon) (248 m (814 ft)) is the highest point. Where the downs reach the sea, they form high cliffs such as the [Seven Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sisters,_East_Sussex), where eroded [dry valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_valley) create an undulating skyline.[^cite_note-5] The county does not contain large rivers, but its largest are the [Rother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Rother,_East_Sussex), which forms part of the boundary with Kent, the [Cuckmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Cuckmere), and the [Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ouse,_Sussex), which rises in West Sussex and flows through [Lewes](67930.md) before reaching the English Channel at [Newhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhaven,_East_Sussex).[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

## History

Main article: [History of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sussex)

East Sussex is part of the [historic county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) of [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex), which has its roots in the ancient [kingdom of the South Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sussex), who established themselves there in the 5th century AD, after the departure of the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome). Archaeological remains are plentiful, especially in the upland areas. The area's position on the coast has also meant that there were many invaders, including the Romans and later the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans), following the defeat of the English army by [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) at the [Battle of Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hastings) in 1066.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Earlier industries included fishing, iron-making, and the wool trade, all of which have declined or been lost completely.&#x20;

## Governance

Main article: [East Sussex County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sussex_County_Council)

Further information: [History of local government in Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_local_government_in_Sussex)



County Hall, [Lewes](67930.md)

[Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex) was historically sub-divided into six [rapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape_\(county_subdivision\)). From the 12th century the three eastern rapes together and the three western rapes together had separate [quarter sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_sessions), with the [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of the three eastern rapes being [Lewes](67930.md).[^cite_note-9] This situation was formalised by Parliament in 1865, and the two parts were made into [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties), each with its own elected [county council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_council) in 1889 under the [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888). In East Sussex there were also three self-administered [county boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough): [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Borough_of_Brighton), [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Borough_of_Eastbourne) and [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Borough_of_Hastings).&#x20;



Arms of East Sussex County Council

Upon its foundation, East Sussex included the south of [Tunbridge Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Tunbridge_Wells) and the south of [Lamberhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamberhurst); by the [Local Government Act 1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1894) these areas were transferred to [Kent](16766.md).&#x20;

In 1974 East Sussex was made a [non-metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) and [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England), and the three county boroughs became districts within the county. At the same time the western boundary was altered, so that the [Mid Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_Sussex_District) region (including [Burgess Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_Hill) and [Haywards Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haywards_Heath)) was transferred to the non metropolitan county of [West Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sussex). In 1997, [Brighton and Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_and_Hove) became a self-administered [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority); it was granted [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) in 2000, whilst remaining part of the ceremonial county of East Sussex.&#x20;

The area of East Sussex County Council's jurisdiction is divided into five [local government districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district). Three are large rural districts (from west to east): [Lewes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_\(district\)), [Wealden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealden_District), and [Rother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rother_District). [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne) and [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings) are mainly urban areas. The rural districts are further subdivided into [civil parishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parishes_in_England).[^cite_note-10]&#x20;

For parliamentary purposes, East Sussex (including Brighton and Hove) is divided into [nine Westminster constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_constituencies_in_East_Sussex), returning nine [Members of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament). The current boundaries were established by the [Boundary Commission for England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Commission_for_England) following its [2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Periodic_Review_of_Westminster_constituencies) and came into effect at the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]&#x20;

### Future

In July 2026, the UK Government announced its intention to reorganise East Sussex into two [unitary authority areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority_areas) by April 2028. One will cover Brighton and Hove, with larger boundaries than the current district, and the other will cover the remainder of the county. Elections to the new authorities will take place in May 2027.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In September 2026, this process was paused for review and local elections for West Sussex were reinstated for May 2027.[^cite_note-15]&#x20;

## Geography

### Geology

Main article: [Geology of East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_East_Sussex)



[Beachy Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachy_Head) and [Belle Tout Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_Tout_Lighthouse), [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne), East Sussex

Geologically, East Sussex is part of southern [anticline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticline) of the [Weald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weald): the [South Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs), a range of moderate [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) hills which run across the southern part of the county from west to east and mirrored in [Kent](16766.md) by the [North Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Downs). To the north lie parallel valleys and ridges, the highest of which is the Weald itself (the Hastings beds and Wealden Clay). The sandstones and clays meet the sea at Hastings; the Downs, at [Beachy Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachy_Head). The area contains significant reserves of shale oil, totalling 4.4 billion [barrels of oil](594043.md) in the Wealden basin according to a 2014 study, which then Business and Energy Minister [Michael Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fallon) said "will bring jobs and business opportunities" and significantly help with UK energy self-sufficiency. [Fracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the area is required to achieve these objectives, which has been opposed by environmental groups.[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

See also: [List of hills of East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hills_of_East_Sussex)

### Climate

East Sussex, like most counties by the south coast, has an annual average total of around 1,750[^cite_note-Englandclimate-17] hours of sunshine per year. This is much higher than the UK's average of about 1,340 hours of sunshine a year.&#x20;

### Relief and drainage

The relief of the county reflects the geology.&#x20;

The [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) uplands of the [South Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs) occupies the coastal strip between Brighton and Eastbourne. There are two river gaps: the [Rivers Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ouse,_Sussex) and [Cuckmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Cuckmere). The [Seven Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sisters,_Sussex), where the Downs meet the sea, are the remnants of dry valleys cut into the chalk; they end at [Beachy Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachy_Head), 530 feet (162 m) above sea level. To the east of Beachy Head lie the marshlands of the [Pevensey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pevensey) Levels, formerly flooded by the sea but now enclosed within a deposited beach. At [Bexhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bexhill-on-Sea) the land begins to rise again where the sands and clays of the Weald meet the sea; these culminate in the sandstone cliffs east of Hastings. Further east are the [Pett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pett) Levels, more marshland, beyond which is the estuary of the [River Rother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Rother_\(Eastern\)). On the far side of the estuary are the [dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune) of [Camber Sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camber_Sands). The highest point in the county is [Ditchling Beacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditchling_Beacon) on the South Downs, which has a maximum elevation of 248 m (814 ft)[^cite_note-18] and is classed as a [Marilyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_\(hill\)).&#x20;

The [Weald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weald) occupies the northern borderlands of the county. Between the Downs and Weald is a narrow stretch of lower lying land; many of the rivers and streams occupying this area originate in the Weald. The High Weald is heavily wooded in contrast to the South Downs; the Low Weald less so. Part of the Weald is the [Ashdown Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdown_Forest).&#x20;

### Settlements

See also: [List of places in East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_places_in_East_Sussex) and [List of settlements in East Sussex by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_settlements_in_East_Sussex_by_population)

The location of settlements in East Sussex has been determined both by its history and its geography. The original towns and villages tended to be where its economy lay: fishing along the coast and agriculture and iron mining on the Weald. Industry today tends to be geared towards tourism, particularly along the coastal strip where towns such as [Bexhill-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bexhill-on-Sea), [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne), and [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings) are located. [Newhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhaven,_East_Sussex) and [Rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye,_East_Sussex) are ports, although the latter is also of historical importance. [Peacehaven](452037.md) and [Seaford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaford,_East_Sussex) serve as [dormitory towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormitory_town). Away from the coast are former market towns such as [Hailsham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailsham), [Heathfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathfield,_East_Sussex) and [Uckfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uckfield); [Crowborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowborough) is a centre for the Ashdown Forest. [Lewes](67930.md), the [County town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of East Sussex; [Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle,_East_Sussex), with its [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) beginnings, and [Wadhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadhurst) are the other three towns of significance.&#x20;

### Economic activities

Measured by the number of registered companies, East Sussex is not a major manufacturing or industrial area of the United Kingdom. Of the companies registered, the leading category in 2023 was "construction", followed by "real estate activities".[^cite_note-21] An important contribution to the county's economy comes from the fact that many residents of Brighton and Hove, Eastbourne and Hastings commute to London or [work remotely](158555.md) for companies outside the county.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

There were 1,720 agricultural holdings in East Sussex (excluding Brighton and Hove) in 2022, with a total farmed area of 108,825 hectares. They employed just over 4000 people and, in 2020, produced a gross value added of £66 million. Livestock grazing accounts for almost 40% of the farmed area, with there being approximately six times as many sheep as cattle.[^cite_note-23] Fishing is limited but there are still fishing fleets, notably at Rye and Hastings, although the number of boats is much reduced. Historically, the fisheries were of great importance.&#x20;

## Landmarks



Mermaid Street in [Rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye,_East_Sussex) showing typically steep slope and cobbled surface

The [Seven Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sisters,_East_Sussex) area forms part of the [South Downs National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs_National_Park). Other notable natural landmarks include [Beachy Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachy_Head) and the low-lying coast around [Normans Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans_Bay).&#x20;

East Sussex also contains numerous historic landmarks. There are castles at [Bodiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodiam_Castle), [Herstmonceux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herstmonceux), [Lewes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_Castle) and [Pevensey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pevensey_Castle); and defence works such as the [Martello towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martello_towers) and [Eastbourne Redoubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_Redoubt).[^cite_note-24] Other notable sites include [Battle Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Abbey), [Bateman's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bateman%27s), [Hammerwood Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammerwood_Park) and the [University of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex) campus at [Falmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falmer). [Bexhill-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bexhill-on-Sea) is also home to the [De La Warr Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Warr_Pavilion). East Sussex also includes two chalk [hill figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_figure), being the [Litlington White Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litlington_White_Horse) and the [Long Man of Wilmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Man_of_Wilmington), which are both situated in the [Sussex Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Downs).&#x20;

## Transport

### Roads



The A23, one of the major north–south routes.

East Sussex has no motorways, and even dual carriageways are sparse in the county. The main roads through the county are those part of the radial pattern from London: the [A21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A21_road_\(England\)) from [Kent](16766.md) to Hastings; the [A22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A22_road) from Surrey to Eastbourne; and the [A23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A23_road) from Gatwick to Brighton. Cross-country routes include the [A26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A26_road) which carries traffic from Newhaven and Lewes north into Kent; and the south coast trunk route, which starts in [Folkestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkestone) (Kent) as the [A259 trunk road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A259_road), and traverses the south coast to [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne), where it becomes the [A27 trunk road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A27_road) and heads westwards towards [Chichester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester) in West Sussex and ultimately to [Honiton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honiton) in Devon. The county's main roads suffer from congestion and traffic problems, particularly the [A27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A27_road), which connects Eastbourne with [Portsmouth](24207.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]&#x20;

Bus routes serve all the main areas of settlement and many of the villages in the county.&#x20;

### Railways

The railways serve the main towns in a similar fashion to the roads. Until the closures of many branch railways in the 20th century, rural East Sussex was well-served by rail: few such branch lines escaped the [Beeching cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeching_cuts) so that today only main-line services remain. They include the [East Coastway Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coastway_Line) (including the [Marshlink Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshlink_Line)); the [Hastings line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_line); and the Uckfield branch of the [Oxted Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxted_Line). There are three [heritage railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_railway): the [Kent and East Sussex Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_and_East_Sussex_Railway) operates from [Tenterden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenterden) in Kent to [Bodiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodiam); the [Bluebell Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluebell_Railway) from [Sheffield Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Park_railway_station) to [East Grinstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Grinstead_railway_station); and the [Lavender Line Steam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Line) is a one-mile line at [Isfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfield_railway_station), near [Uckfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uckfield).[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

Trains in the county are operated by [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_\(train_operating_company\)), [Southeastern](68837941.md), [Thameslink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Thameslink_Railway) and [Great Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Railway_\(train_operating_company\)). Southern is the key operator for the county, operating services along the West Coastway and East Coastway routes, as well as trains from [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_railway_station), [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_railway_station), [Seaford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaford_railway_station_\(England\)) and [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_railway_station) to [London Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Victoria_railway_station), and to a lesser extent [London Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Bridge_railway_station), which is also where trains to/from [Uckfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uckfield_railway_station) go. Southeastern operate trains from [London Charing Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charing_Cross_railway_station) to Hastings. Thameslink operate trains from Brighton to [Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_railway_station).&#x20;

### Airports

There are no airports offering scheduled flights in East Sussex. London's second airport, [Gatwick Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport), is a short distance from East Sussex and easily accessible by road and rail from most of the county. Despite its name, [Brighton City Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_City_Airport), the former Shoreham Airport, is in West Sussex. Three airfields service [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation), helicopters and flying schools: Spilsted Farm near Hastings;[^cite_note-28] Kittyhawk Aerodrome near Lewes;,[^cite_note-29] and [Deanland Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Deanland), also near Lewes.[^cite_note-30] Sussex Gliding Club operate from Kitsons field at The Broyle on the outskirts of Ringmer.[^cite_note-31]&#x20;

### Ferries

A ferry operates from [Newhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhaven,_East_Sussex) to [Dieppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieppe) in France twice daily in the off-season and three times daily in the summer. Both vehicles and foot passengers are accepted.[^cite_note-32]&#x20;

### Footpaths

See also: [Recreational walks in East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_walks_in_East_Sussex)

Among the [long-distance footpaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_footpaths) in East Sussex are the [South Downs Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs_Way); [1066 Country Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1066_Country_Walk), [High Weald Landscape Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Weald_Landscape_Trail), [Saxon Shore Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Shore_Way), [Sussex Border Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Border_Path), [Sussex Ouse Valley Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Ouse_Valley_Way), [Vanguard Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_Way), [Wealdway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealdway) and [The Monarch's Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Monarch%27s_Way).&#x20;

## Education

Main article: [List of schools in East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_East_Sussex)

The Universities of [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex) and [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Brighton) are based in East Sussex, with facilities in [Brighton](49796.md), [Falmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falmer) and [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Centre_Hastings).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]&#x20;

Over 150 primary schools serve the area; many of them small and local in scope.&#x20;

State and independent secondary schools include [Lewes Old Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_Old_Grammar_School), which celebrated its 500th anniversary in 2012. Overall, more than 50 serve East Sussex, including further education colleges in larger towns.&#x20;

A number of independent boarding schools that also cater to day students are located in the county, including [Brighton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_College) and [Roedean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roedean_School). The [Pestalozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Heinrich_Pestalozzi) Children's Village, an international foundation, is located at [Sedlescombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedlescombe).&#x20;

## Culture



Theatre at Glyndebourne

East Sussex has many theatres and performance venues, including the [Theatre Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Royal,_Brighton) in Brighton and the [Brighton Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Dome), the [Attenborough Centre for the Creative Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attenborough_Centre_for_the_Creative_Arts) at the University of Sussex, the [Royal Hippodrome Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Hippodrome_Theatre) and [Devonshire Park Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonshire_Park_Theatre) in Eastbourne, and the [White Rock Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rock_Theatre) in Hastings. The annual [Glyndebourne Festival Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyndebourne_Festival_Opera) takes place at [Glyndebourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyndebourne) near Lewes. The [Brighton Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Festival) is a large multi-arts festival held every May.&#x20;

There is a wide range of museums and art galleries in East Sussex. Notable amongst these are the [Brighton Museum & Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Museum_%26_Art_Gallery); the [Hove Museum of Creativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hove_Museum_of_Creativity); the [Hastings Museum and Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Museum_and_Art_Gallery); [Hastings Contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Contemporary); [Charleston Farmhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston_Farmhouse) near Lewes, with an exhibition devoted to the artistic work of the [Bloomsbury Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomsbury_Group); the De La Warr Pavilion in Bexhill-on-Sea, which houses a contemporary art gallery; and the [Towner Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towner_Eastbourne) gallery, which celebrated its centenary in 2023 and hosted the award ceremony for the [Turner Prize](149045.md) on 5 December of that year.[^cite_note-35]&#x20;

## Tourism



The Royal Pavilion

The county's coastal towns, including [Brighton](49796.md) and [Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hove), [Bexhill-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bexhill-on-Sea), [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings) and [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne), are popular visitor destinations, particularly in summer. Brighton and Hove have a range of attractions including the [Royal Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Pavilion). Beginning in 1787, it was built in three stages as a seaside retreat for [George, Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_IV_of_the_United_Kingdom), who became King George IV in 1820. A more recent attraction is the [Brighton i360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_i360), a 162 m observation tower on the seafront.[^cite_note-36]&#x20;

[Brighton Palace Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Palace_Pier), [Eastbourne Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_Pier) and [Hastings Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Pier) serve as entertainment centres that attract many visitors. Several other piers built in the heyday of day tripper visits by train no longer exist, including [St Leonards Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Leonards_Pier), in [St Leonards-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Leonards-on-Sea), and the [West Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pier) in Brighton, parts of which can still be seen in the sea opposite the Brighton i360. Originally, the profits from the i360 were seen as a potential source of funds to rebuild the West Pier, but that now seems unlikely.[^cite_note-37] Other important tourist attractions within East Sussex include [Ashdown Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdown_Forest), [Drusillas Zoo Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drusillas_Zoo_Park) near [Alfriston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfriston) and [Michelham Priory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelham_Priory). The county has two narrow-gauge railways, the [Hastings Miniature Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Miniature_Railway), which is powered by a steam locomotive, and [Volk's Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volk%27s_Electric_Railway), which runs from Brighton's Palace Pier to [Brighton Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Marina) along the seafront. Volk's is the oldest operational electric railway in the world. There are also several castles that attract many visitors, as indicated in the Landmarks section above.&#x20;



A 1903 De Dion-Bouton arrives in Brighton in the 2012 Veteran Car Run

Annual events also promote tourism to the county. [Brighton Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Pride) is one of the UK's largest and oldest [pride parades](58168.md) and other pride events take place at Eastbourne and Hastings.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] The London to Brighton Veteran Car Run is the world's longest-running motoring event. To qualify, cars must have been built before 1905. It is also the world's largest gathering of veteran cars. The race finishes on [Madeira Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira_Terrace) in Brighton.[^cite_note-40]&#x20;

Other popular destinations in the county include [Alfriston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfriston); [Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle,_East_Sussex), site of the Battle of Hastings; [Ditchling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditchling); [Herstmonceux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herstmonceux), with the castle of the same name; Lewes; [Rottingdean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rottingdean); and [Rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye,_East_Sussex).&#x20;

## Traditions

The [Lewes Bonfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_Bonfire) is a set of bonfire celebrations held on [Guy Fawkes Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Fawkes_Night), which both celebrates the uncovering of the [Gunpowder Plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_Plot) in 1605 and commemorates the memory of the [seventeen Protestant martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_Martyrs) from [Mayfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfield_and_Five_Ashes) burned at the stake between 1555 and 1557. It has a history of religious antagonism and anti-Catholic imagery, including the burning of an effigy of the [Pope](23056.md). The event attracts tens of thousands of spectators every year. There are also many other [bonfire societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Bonfire_Societies) in East Sussex.[^cite_note-41]&#x20;

## Sport

### Football



The Dripping Pan, home of Lewes FC men's and women's teams

[Brighton & Hove Albion F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_%26_Hove_Albion_F.C.), based in [Brighton and Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_and_Hove), is the county's highest-ranked football club and plays in the [Premier League](11250.md). It reached the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_FA_Cup_final) in 1983. Other clubs in the county include [Eastbourne Town F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_Town_F.C.) and [Lewes F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_F.C.), the latter of which also fields a [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes_F.C._Women).[^cite_note-42]&#x20;

### Cricket

Cricket had its [origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_to_1725) in the counties of [Kent](16766.md), [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey) and Sussex. There is no team representing East Sussex. It combines with West Sussex as the [Sussex County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_County_Cricket_Club), playing first-team matches at the [County Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cricket_Ground,_Hove) in Hove and reserve-team matches in both East and West Sussex. There are around 25 local clubs playing the game in East Sussex.[^cite_note-43]&#x20;

### Rugby

There are around 15 rugby clubs in East Sussex, with many of them fielding several teams including women's and girls' teams. [Seaford Rugby Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaford_RFC) claims the distinction of playing its home games below sea level.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;

### Cycling

The fourth stage of the [1994 Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Tour_de_France) ended in Brighton after departing from [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover). The route included a climb over Ditchling Beacon.[^cite_note-45] The hilly nature of the Downs presents a good challenge for cyclists and there are at least eight cycling clubs in East Sussex.[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

### Golf

There are 42 golf courses in East Sussex, many on downland. Four of these (two in Brighton and Hove, one in Eastbourne and one in Crowborough) are public courses; the remainder private, semi-private or resorts.[^cite_note-47]&#x20;

### Other sports

Other popular sports include sailing, [bowls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowls), [stoolball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoolball), and tennis.&#x20;
