The **DR Congo national football team** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *équipe nationale de football de la République démocratique du Congo*; [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala_language): *lisanga ya ndémbô ya Republíki ya Kongó Demokratíki*), recognised by [FIFA](11049.md) as **Congo DR** and by [CAF](715265.md) as **DR Congo**, represents the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). It is controlled by the [Congolese Association Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_Association_Football_Federation). They are nicknamed *Les Léopards*, meaning **The Leopards**.[^cite_note-2] The team is a member of FIFA and the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF). DR Congo have previously competed variously as **Belgian Congo**, **Congo-Kinshasa** and **Zaïre**.

DR Congo have been ranked as high as 28th in the [FIFA Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Rankings). They won the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) twice (in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_African_Cup_of_Nations) as Congo-Kinshasa and in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations) as Zaire), and were the first [Sub-Saharan African](27067.md) team to qualify for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), as Zaire in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup). They are also one of the most successful teams in the [African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Nations_Championship) with two titles ([2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_African_Nations_Championship) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_African_Nations_Championship)), second only to [Morocco](1024865.md) with three. They qualified for the World Cup in [2026](36581929.md) as DR Congo, and are currently ranked 41st in the [FIFA Rankings](719095.md) (July 2026).[^cite_note-3]

## History

### Early history

The [Congolese Association Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_Association_Football_Federation) was founded in 1919 when the country was not yet independent. The team played their first game in 1948 as [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo) against [Northern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rhodesia), now Zambia. The team recorded a 3–2 victory at home. DR Congo has been [FIFA](11049.md) affiliated since 1962 and has been a member of [CAF](715265.md) since 1963. The team's first official match was on 11 April 1963, against [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania_national_football_team) in the [L'Amitié Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Amiti%C3%A9_Tournament?action=edit\&redlink=1) played in [Dakar](44251.md), Senegal. DR Congo won the match 6–0.[^cite_note-4] The national team appeared in the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) for the first time in [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_African_Cup_of_Nations).

### First World Cup appearance

The Democratic Republic of the Congo had its first international success at the [1968 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_African_Cup_of_Nations) held in [Ethiopia](187749.md), beating [Ghana](1029620.md) 1–0 in the final. The team's biggest ever win came on 22 November 1969 when they recorded a 10–1 home victory against [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team). Although a handful of Congolese players were playing in Europe (particularly Belgium) during these years, foreign-based players were seldom recalled for international duty; a rare exception was [Julien Kialunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Kialunda) who represented Zaire (as the country was by then known) at the [1972 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_African_Cup_of_Nations_squads) while playing for [Anderlecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSC_Anderlecht).

See also: [1968 African Cup of Nations final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_African_Cup_of_Nations_final)



Zaire versus Brazil in the 1974 World Cup



Zaire versus Scotland in 1974 World Cup

The second continental title came at the [1974 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations) in Egypt. The Leopards recorded a 2–1 victory against [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team), another 2–1 victory against rivals [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team) and a 4–1 victory against [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_national_football_team). These results carried Zaire through to the semi-finals where they beat hosts [Egypt](1022953.md) 3–2. In the final, Zaire drew with [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) 2–2. Therefore, the match was replayed two days later, where Zaire won the game 2–0. Zaire player [Ndaye Mulamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndaye_Mulamba) was top scorer with nine goals, which remains a record for the tournament. After this, the team returned to Zaire on the Presidential plane, lent to them by [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko).

See also: [1974 African Cup of Nations final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations_final)

Zaire were the first [Sub-Saharan African](27067.md) team to participate in a [World Cup](11370.md), qualifying for the [1974 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) in place of the 1970 participant [Morocco](1024865.md), whom they defeated in the decisive qualifier 3–0 in [Kinshasa](45773.md).[^cite_note-5] Such was the desire to foster an identity of Zaire as a global player that Mobutu paid for advertising hoardings at the World Cup to display messages such as ‘Zaire-Peace’ and ‘Go to Zaire’.[^cite_note-6] After their qualification for the World Cup, Mobutu invited the Zaire squad to his palace in [Gbadolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gbadolite), where he promised them cash and other gifts if they did well.[^cite_note-NYT-Zaire-7]

At the tournament itself, Zaire did not manage to score any goals and lost all of its games, but gave credible performances against [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and [Brazil](149286.md). However, the team's morale was low because they did not receive the resources they were promised, and had problems involving their Yugoslav coach, [Blagoje Vidinić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blagoje_Vidini%C4%87). Their match against [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) became a 9–0 loss, and remains one of the worst World Cup defeats. The Zaire team was threatened by their government that they would face consequences if they were to have a loss against Brazil by more than 3–0. A bizarre moment came in the match versus Brazil; facing a free-kick 25 yards out, defender [Mwepu Ilunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwepu_Ilunga), upon hearing the referee blow his whistle, ran out of the Zaire wall and kicked the ball upfield, for which he received a yellow card. This was voted the 17th greatest World Cup moment in a Channel 4 poll.[^cite_note-NYT-Zaire-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Ilunga has stated that he was quite aware of the rules and was hoping to convince the referee to send him off. The intended red card would have been a protest against his country's authorities, who were alleged to be depriving the players of their earnings.[^cite_note-10] Many contemporary commentators instead held it to be an example of African football's "naïvety and indiscipline".[^cite_note-11]

### Crisis period

After winning the [1974 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations) and participating in the [1974 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), the team was eliminated in the first round of the [1976 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_African_Cup_of_Nations) after recording a draw and two losses in the group stage. [Morocco](1024865.md) went on to win the tournament. From 1978 to 1986, the country did not qualify for the [African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Cup_of_Nations), while not participating in qualification for the [1978 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1986 World Cup](157231.md). In the [1988 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_African_Cup_of_Nations), Zaire finished last in their group despite having two draws.

### Return to success

From 1992 to 1996, Zaire reached three consecutive [African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Cup_of_Nations) quarter-finals. In 1992 and 1994, they were beaten by [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), and in 1996 they were beaten by [Ghana](1029620.md). In 1997, the country returned to its former name of Democratic Republic of the Congo, and the national team was re-branded as the Simbas, a nickname that stuck for the next nine years.[^cite_note-12] DR Congo played their first game on 8 June 1997 in [Pointe-Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe-Noire) which ended in a 1–0 loss to the Republic of the Congo. At the [1998 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_African_Cup_of_Nations), DR Congo, led by Louis Watunda, surprisingly took third place, beating [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals and hosts [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team) 4–1 on penalties in their last match after scoring three late goals to tie the encounter 4–4.

At the [2000 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_African_Cup_of_Nations), the team finished third in their group, and in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations) were eliminated in the quarter-finals by [Senegal](1017436.md). Then, in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations), DR Congo were eliminated after three straight defeats in the group stages. In [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_African_Cup_of_Nations), led by [Claude Le Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Le_Roy), having finished second in the group behind [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team), the Congolese were eliminated in the quarter-finals by [Egypt](1022953.md) 4–1.

### Struggles

DR Congo were drawn in group 10 for qualifications for the [2008 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_African_Cup_of_Nations), along with [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team), [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) and [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia_national_football_team). Before the last match day, the Congolese led the group, but they drew 1–1 with Libya in their final match while Namibia beat Ethiopia 3–2. This sent Namibia through to the Finals, while the Leopards were eliminated. DR Congo also failed to qualify for the [2010 World Cup](168079.md). In 2009, DR Congo won the [2009 African Championship of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_African_Championship_of_Nations), a competition reserved to players in domestic leagues, a tournament they would again win in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_African_Nations_Championship). DR Congo reached the [2013 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) finals in South Africa but were knocked out in the group stages after drawing all three matches.

### Rise and near World Cup miss



DR Congo versus Guinea in 2023

In the [2015 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), DR Congo again drew all three group matches but this time finished second in the group behind Tunisia, and therefore advanced to the quarter-finals to play their rivals [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team) (Republic), a match in which the Leopards came from two goals down to win 4–2. However, they were knocked out by the Ivory Coast 3–1 in the semi-finals. They ended up finishing third, beating Equatorial Guinea on penalties, after the third place match finished 0–0 in regulation time.

DR Congo under Ibengé improved radically and had an outstanding performance for many decades in a World Cup qualification. During the [2018 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), DR Congo was grouped with [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team), [Tunisia](1029953.md) and [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team). DR Congo managed an outstanding performance, beating Libya and Guinea home and away, but missed the chance after losing 1–2 to eventual World Cup qualifier Tunisia in [Tunis](57659.md) and drew 2–2 at home to the same opponent.

### Second World Cup appearance

DR Congo finished second behind Senegal in the [2026 World Cup qualification Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_Group_B), before defeating both Cameroon and Nigeria in the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_second_round).[^cite_note-13] In the [inter-confederation play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(inter-confederation_play-offs\)), they secured a 1–0 extra-time victory over [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team) to qualify for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), marking their second World Cup appearance after 1974.[^cite_note-14] The day after, the authorities announced a public holiday in the country for people to celebrate the achievement.[^cite_note-15] On 17 June 2026, during the [group-stage](81660204.md), [Yoane Wissa](52748696.md) scored the country's first ever World Cup goal against [Portugal](576328.md) in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRG_Stadium) in an unexpected 1–1 draw, also claiming the country's first ever point in the tournament.[^cite_note-16] On 27 June, DR Congo qualified for the [knockout stage](81667005.md) for the first time after achieving their first World Cup victory, a 3–1 win over [Uzbekistan](1032413.md), in [Atlanta](3138.md). Yoane Wissa and [Fiston Mayele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiston_Mayele) scored goals against Uzbekistan.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

On 1 July 2026, during the [round of 32](81667041.md), DR Congo lost to [England](9904.md) in 2–1 match in Atlanta, after shockingly leading during the first half of the game thanks to a goal by [Brian Cipenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Cipenga) seven minutes into the match. Yoane Wissa took another shot at 42 minutes, which would have doubled the lead for DR Congo, but the ball hit the right side goalpost. Goalkeeper [Lionel Mpasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Mpasi) made multiple saves during the match, including two against England's [Jude Bellingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Bellingham), but at 75 minutes [Harry Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Kane) scored a goal, and followed it up with another one at 86 minutes, which, ultimately, gave England the 2-1 victory.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Home stadium

[Stade des Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_des_Martyrs) has been the home of the national team since its establishment and they occasionally play their games at [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi).

## Gestures and celebrations of the national team

**Fimbu**

The term means "whip" in Lingala, this gesture came from the song Fimbu by artist [Felix Wazekwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Wazekwa). It is a goal celebrations and an iconic cultural dance from the Country. The gesture simulates the act of whippings or lashing. Players line up, hold an imaginary whip, and make rapid arm movements, simulating "punishing" the opponent with lashes in a coreographed manner. How it began: it gained prominence during the [2017 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) popularized by players such as [Firmin Mubele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmin_Mubele) and [Junior Kabananga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Kabananga). It became the trademark celebrations for the Leopards victories most recently seen following [Yoane Wissa](52748696.md)'s equalizer against [Portugal](576328.md).
