**Darnytskyi District**[^cite_note-3] is an [urban district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Ukraine) of [Kyiv](585629.md), the capital of [Ukraine](31750.md). It is located in the south-eastern part of the city on the Left Bank[^cite_note-4] of the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River) and is the third largest district, with the total area of 134 km². The estimated population of the district is 347,512 inhabitants.[^cite_note-5]

The area was included into the city limits in 1927. In 1935, it was detached into a separate district. The district has assumed its current borders in 2001, merging with Kharkivskyi District as part of the administrative reform in Kyiv. Darnytskyi District borders [Holosiivskyi District](7136989.md) to its west, across the river, [Dniprovskyi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniprovskyi_District,_Kyiv) to its north, across the railway, [Brovary Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brovary_Raion) and [Boryspil Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boryspil_Raion) of [Kyiv Oblast](494884.md) to its east and south.

## Historical background

Although the exact date of establishment of the area is not known, there is evidence that during the 4th and 3rd centuries BC, a Neolithic settlement existed near lake *Sviatyshche* ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Святище). During the 9th century, Darnytsia was an important centre of the [Kyivan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyivan_Rus'), where ambassadors and delegations from other powers were housed while waiting to meet the *[kniaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knyaz)* and offer gifts. This gave the area its name *Darnytsia* (derived from [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages)/[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) *dar*, meaning gift).

While throughout the 19th century the area was progressively becoming more densely populated, the dwellers were technically residing outside of Kyiv in village-like (but non-village by status) [settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) called [slobidkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobidka) or [slobodkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodkas). Certain parts of Darnytsia still bear the names of the slobidkas they displaced, notably *[Mykilska Sloboda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykilska_Sloboda)* (St. Nicholas settlement).

Until the late 19th century, the name Darnytsia was commonly applied to the plain on the left bank of Dnipro river across from the Kyiv city center. Up to the [Bolshevik Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik_Revolution) in 1917, the area was legally part of the [Chernihiv Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Governorate) ([Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv)), despite being adjacent to Kyiv, also a center of its Guberniya, with the Dnipro river being the official division line. The village of Nova-Darnytsia and the rest of the area of the today's raion were incorporated into the city of Kyiv in 1927. In 1935 Darnytsia became a separate raion of the city. With establishment of the Kyiv city Darnytsia Raion, villages Pozniaky and Osokorky were merged and liquidated as separate populated localities.

The urbanized area started industrializing between [World War I](4764461.md) and [World War II](32927.md), after becoming a part of Kyiv. Following World War II, the area underwent a major reconstruction and modernization and became heavily industrialized, with the installation of [chemical](58721.md), [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry), [pharmaceutical](560876.md), and food processing industries. Major infrastructure projects, including heat-and-power co-generation units, railroad stations, highways, and [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Metro) lines were accomplished. Several research institutes found their new home here, including three major chemical institutes of the [National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](2281470.md).

## Overview

Today, the term *Darnytsia* is sometimes broadly (but incorrectly) applied to the entire Left Bank area of Kyiv, except the distant areas of [Vyhurivshchyna-Troieshchyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troieshchyna) to the North and [Pozniaky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozniaky) to the South. Formally, the district underwent many border changes, and today encompasses approximately one-third of the Left Bank. A planned district change could make Darnytsia district either expand to encompass half of the Left Bank area or cease to exist being redistributed between [Desnianskyi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desnianskyi_District,_Kyiv) and [Dniprovskyi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniprovskyi_District,_Kyiv).

Neighborhood

The Darnytsia neighborhood borders are generally understood to be:

- Mykilska Slobidka, [Rusanivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusanivka) Island and [Berezniaky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezniaky) to the West

- [Voskresenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskresenka_\(Kyiv\)), [Troieshchyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troieshchyna) and [Lisovyi masyv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisovyi_masyv) to the North

- Bykivnia neighborhood and the town of [Bortnychi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bortnychi) to the East

- [Pozniaky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozniaky), [Osokorky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osokorky) and [Kharkivskyi masyv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkivskyi_Masyv) to the South
