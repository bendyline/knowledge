# Landslide victory

&#x20;A **landslide victory** is an [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election) result in which the winning [candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate#Candidates_in_elections) or [party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) achieves a decisive victory by an overwhelming margin, securing a very large majority of votes or seats far beyond the typical competitive outcome.[^cite_note-Murse-1][^cite_note-2] The term became popular in the 19th century to describe a victory in which the opposition is "buried",[^cite_note-Murse-1] similar to the way in which a geological [landslide](37208.md) buries whatever is in its path. A landslide victory for one party is often accompanied by an [electoral wipeout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_wipeout) for the opposition, as the overwhelming support for the winning side inflicts a decisive loss on its rivals. What qualifies as a landslide victory can vary depending on the type of electoral system, as the term does not entail a precise, technical, or universally agreed-upon measurement. Instead, it is used informally in everyday language, making it subject to interpretation. Even within a single [electoral system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_system), there is no consensus on the exact margin that constitutes a landslide victory.[^cite_note-Murse-1]

A landslide victory implies a powerful expression of [popular will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_will) and a ringing endorsement by the [electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter) for the winner's [political platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_platform). A landslide can be viewed by a winning candidate or party as a [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_\(politics\)) or a tacit authorization from the public to implement their proposed [policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy) and pursue their [agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_agenda) with confidence. Emboldened by the result, the winner may undertake ambitious [reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform) or significant policy shifts to reflect the electorate's desire for meaningful change.[^cite_note-3] However, it can also indicate deep [political polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarization) in an electorate[^cite_note-4] or an [unfair election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfair_election).[^cite_note-5]

A combination of factors, including [charismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charisma) leadership, a favorable shift in public sentiment driven by dissatisfaction with or support for the [status quo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo), strategic [electoral campaigning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_campaign) and a positive [media portrayal](18932.md), can create the conditions necessary for a landslide victory. A landslide may fundamentally reshape the [political landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_structure) of a country; one example of this phenomenon is [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt)'s election as US president in 1932.[^cite_note-6] In a post-landslide scenario, a winning party can sometimes implement its policies with little resistance, while its severely weakened opposition may struggle to perform essential [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checks_and_balances).

### Canada

In a Canadian federal election, a landslide victory occurs when a political party gains a significant majority of the [House of Commons of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada).[^cite_note-13]

Landslide victories may also occur during provincial elections, and territorial elections in [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon). Landslide victories are not possible for territorial elections in the [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories), and [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut), as its members are elected without reference to political parties, operating as a [consensus government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_government).

#### National landslide victories

The following Canadian federal elections resulted in landslide victories:[^cite_note-14]

- [1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Canadian_federal_election) – The Liberals, led by [William Lyon Mackenzie King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lyon_Mackenzie_King), won 171 seats (an increase of 34) and gained a majority of 50, while the Conservatives, led by [R. B. Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._B._Bennett), won just 39 (a decrease of 98).

- [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Canadian_federal_election) – The Liberals, led by William Lyon Mackenzie King, won 179 seats (an increase of 6) and gained a majority of 56 seats while [the Progressive Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Canada), led by [Robert James Manion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_James_Manion), won just 39 (unchanged from their previous share).

- [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Canadian_federal_election) – The Liberals, led by [Louis St. Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_St._Laurent), won 191 seats (an increase of 73) and gained a majority of 59, while the Progressive Conservatives, led by [George A. Drew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_A._Drew), won just 41 (a decrease of 25).



The vote by province in 1958 shows the scale of the Progressive Conservatives' landslide victory.

- [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Canadian_federal_election) – The Progressive Conservatives, led by [John Diefenbaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Diefenbaker), won 208 seats (an increase of 96) and gained a majority of 75, while the Liberals, led by [Lester B. Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson), won just 48 (a decrease of 57).



The vote by province in 1984 shows the scale of the Progressive Conservatives' landslide victory.

- [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Canadian_federal_election) – The Progressive Conservatives, led by [Brian Mulroney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Mulroney), won 211 seats (an increase of 111) and gained a majority of 69, while the Liberals, led by [John Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Turner), won just 40 (a decrease of 95). Mulroney is the only prime minister to have won a majority of seats in every single province.

- [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Canadian_federal_election) – The Liberals, led by [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chr%C3%A9tien), won 177 seats (an increase of 96) and gained a majority of 29, while the [Bloc Québecois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloc_Qu%C3%A9becois), led by [Lucien Bouchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Bouchard), which ran only in Quebec, won 54 (an increase of 44). The ruling Progressive Conservatives, led by [Kim Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Campbell), won just 2 (a decrease of 154).

#### Senate elections

- [1949 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Philippine_Senate_election) – The [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Philippines\)) won all the 8 seats contested in the election, wiping out the [Nacionalistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalista_Party) and pushing their total number of seats in the [Senate](299521.md) up to 17.

  [^cite_note-\:0-29]

- [1951 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Philippine_Senate_election) – The Nacionalista Party won all 9 contested seats, with [Senate President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_president_of_the_Philippines) [Mariano Jesús Cuenco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Jes%C3%BAs_Cuenco) losing his Senate seat.   [^cite_note-\:0-29]

- [1955 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Philippine_Senate_election) – The Nacionalista Party won all 8 contested seats in the election, while the Liberal Party lost all 4 of their remaining seats in the Senate. This was the first time in the history of the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Republic_of_the_Philippines) that a single party had control of all seats in the Senate.

- [1957 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Philippine_Senate_election) – The Nacionalista Party won 6 of the 8 seats contested in the election, bringing down their total number of seats to 22, the Liberal Party meanwhile only managed to win 2 seats in the Senate.

- [1967 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Philippine_Senate_election) – The Nacionalista Party won 6 of the 8 seats contested in this election, bringing their total number of seats from 11 to 15. The Liberal Party only managed to win 1 seat contested in this election.

- [1969 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_Senate_election) – The Nacionalista Party won 6 of the 8 seats contested in this election, bringing their total number of seats from 15 to 17. The Liberal Party only managed to take 2 seats.

#### House of Representatives elections

Before 1972, the [House of Representatives](293605.md) was under a [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system) dominated by either [Nacionalistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalista_Party) or the [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Philippines\)). By the time the [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Philippine_House_of_Representatives_elections) election took place however, new parties have garnered enough support to win multiple seats in [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines), effectively switching into a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system) and thus made landslides from a single party very unlikely.

  [^cite_note-\:0-29]

- [1978 Philippine parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Philippine_parliamentary_election) – In the inaugural elections of the [Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa_\(legislature\)) under the parliamentary system, [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) led the [Kilusang Bagong Lipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilusang_Bagong_Lipunan) to victory with 150 seats, thus making him the first [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_Philippines) of the country since 1899. ### United Kingdom

In UK general elections, a landslide victory involves winning a large [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority) in parliament and often goes with a large [swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_\(United_Kingdom\)) from one party to another as well. Landslide victories have usually occurred after a long period of government from one particular party and a change in the popular mood.[^cite_note-56]

Large majorities, however, are not always the advantage they appear to be. Anthony Seldon gives a number of examples of the infighting that can arise from large majorities. He claims that a "sweet spot" parliamentary majority of 35–50 seats is enough to protect from by-elections and still comfortably pass legislation.[^cite_note-57]

**Notable landslide election results**

- [1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_United_Kingdom_general_election) – [Henry Campbell-Bannerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Campbell-Bannerman) led his [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(UK\)) to victory over [Arthur Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Balfour)'s Conservative Party who lost more than half their seats, including his own seat in [Manchester East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_East_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), as a result of the large national swing to the Liberal Party (The 5.4% swing from the Conservatives to Liberals was at the time the highest ever achieved). The Liberal Party won 397 seats (an increase of 214) and a majority of 124 seats, while the Conservative Party were left with 156 seats (a decrease of 246).

A landslide victory in presidential elections occurs when a candidate has an overwhelming majority in the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College). In federal and state congressional elections, a landslide victory occurs when a party wins 60% or more of the seats in a legislative chamber.[^cite_note-67]

#### Presidential elections

- [1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_United_States_presidential_election) – [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) ([R](32070.md)) received 336 (70.6%) of the electoral votes while [Alton B. Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alton_B._Parker) ([D](5043544.md)) received only 140 (29.4%)

- [1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election) – [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) (D) received 435 (81.9%) of the electoral votes while Theodore Roosevelt ([P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Moose_Party)) received only 88 (16.6%) and [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft) (R) received only 8 (1.5%)

- [1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_United_States_presidential_election) – [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding) (R) received 404 (76.1%) of the electoral votes while [James M. Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_M._Cox) (D) received only 127 (23.9%). Additionally, Harding won 60.4% of the popular vote.

- [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_States_presidential_election) – [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge) (R) received 382 (71.9%) of the electoral votes while [John W. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Davis) (D) received only 136 (25.6%) and [Robert M. La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette) ([P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_\(United_States,_1924%E2%80%931927\))) received 13 (2.4%).

- [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_United_States_presidential_election) – [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover) (R) received 444 (83.6%) of the electoral votes while [Al Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Smith) (D) received only 87 (16.4%).

- [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_presidential_election) – [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) (D) received 472 (88.9%) of the electoral votes while Herbert Hoover (R) received only 59 (11.1%).



The map of the Electoral College in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election) shows the scale of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt)'s landslide victory.

- [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election) – Franklin D. Roosevelt (D) received 523 (98.5%) of the electoral votes—the largest share since 1820 and the largest in a non-unanimous election—while [Alf Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Landon) (R) received only 8 (1.5%). Additionally, Roosevelt received 60.8% of the popular vote.

- [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_United_States_presidential_election) – Franklin D. Roosevelt (D) received 449 (84.6%) of the electoral votes while [Wendell Willkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Willkie) (R) received only 82 (15.4%).

- [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_United_States_presidential_election) – Franklin D. Roosevelt (D) received 432 (81.4%) of the electoral votes while [Thomas E. Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Dewey) (R) received only 99 (18.6%).

- [1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_United_States_presidential_election) – [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) (R) received 442 (83.2%) of the electoral votes while [Adlai Stevenson II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlai_Stevenson_II) (D) received only 89 (16.8%).

- [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_United_States_presidential_election) – Dwight D. Eisenhower (R) received 457 (86.1%) of the electoral votes while Adlai Stevenson II (D) received only 73 (13.7%).

- [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election) – [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) (D) received 486 (90.3%) of the electoral votes while [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater) (R) received only 52 (9.7%). Additionally, Johnson received 61.1% of the popular vote.



The map of the Electoral College in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_United_States_presidential_election) shows the scale of [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon)'s landslide victory.

- [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_United_States_presidential_election) – [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) (R) received 520 (96.7%) of the electoral votes while [George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern) (D) received only 17 (3.2%). One Republican elector voted for [John Hospers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hospers) of the [Libertarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_\(United_States\)). Additionally, Nixon received 60.7% of the popular vote.

- [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_election) – [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) (R) received 489 (90.9%) of the electoral votes while [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) (D) received only 49 (9.1%).



The map of the Electoral College in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election) shows the scale of [Ronald Reagan](25433.md)'s landslide victory.

- [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election) – Ronald Reagan (R) received 525 (97.6%) of the electoral votes while [Walter Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale) (D) received only 13 (2.4%). Additionally, Reagan received 58.8% of the popular vote.

- [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_United_States_presidential_election) – [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) (R) received 426 (79.2%) of the electoral votes while [Michael Dukakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dukakis) (D) received only 111 (20.8%).

- [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_presidential_election) – [Bill Clinton](3356.md) (D) received 379 (70.4%) of the electoral votes while [Bob Dole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dole) (R) received 159 (29.6%) and [Ross Perot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Perot) of the [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_of_the_United_States_of_America) received none.

#### Congressional elections

House of Representatives

- [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections) – Coinciding with Franklin D. Roosevelt's win in the presidential election, the Democratic Party won 313 seats with 54.5% of the popular vote, which caused the Republican Party to lose its two-seat majority. This election marked a beginning of political dominance for the Democratic Party that would last until the 1994 election.

- [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections) – The Democratic Party gained nine seats from the previous election.

- [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections) – Coinciding with Franklin D. Roosevelt's election to a second term as president, the Democratic Party gained an additional twelve seats from the previous election with 55.9% of the popular vote, bringing the total amount of seats held by the party to 334. This would be the most recent election where one party won more than 300 seats in the House of Representatives, and was the last of four straight elections where Republicans lost seats due to the effects of the Great Depression.
