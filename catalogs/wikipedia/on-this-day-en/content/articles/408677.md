**Nagaland** ([/ˈnɑːɡəlænd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a state in the [north-eastern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India) of India. It is bordered by the Indian states of [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) to the north, [Assam](186162.md) to the west, [Manipur](422642.md) to the south, and the [Naga Self-Administered Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Self-Administered_Zone) of the [Sagaing Region](493361.md) of [Myanmar (Burma)](19457.md) to the east. Its capital city is [Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohima) and its largest city is the twin [Chümoukedima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%BCmoukedima)–[Dimapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur). The state has an area of 16,579 square kilometres (6,401 sq mi) with a population of 1,980,602 as per the [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India), making it one of the least populated states in India.[^cite_note-coiprov-8]

Nagaland consists of 17 administrative districts, inhabited by 17 major tribes along with other sub-tribes. Each tribe is distinct in character from the other in terms of customs, language and dress. It is a land of [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) passed down the generations through word of mouth. The earliest recorded history of the Nagas of the present-day Nagaland dates back to the 13th century.[^cite_note-9]

In the 19th century, the [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India) forces began expanding their influence in [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India), including the [Naga Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Hills). After India's independence in 1947, the question of the Naga Hills' political status emerged. Nagaland was a district in the [State of Assam](186162.md) until 1957, known to others as "The Naga Hills". The [Naga National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_National_Council), led by [Zapu Phizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapu_Phizo), demanded an independent Naga state and launched an armed insurgency. The [Indian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Government), however, maintained that Nagaland was an integral part of the Indian Union. The conflict between the Naga National Council and the Indian Government resulted in a protracted [insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_nationalism). The State of Nagaland was formally inaugurated on 1 December 1963, as the 16th state of the Indian Union, and a [democratically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) elected government took office in 1964.

Nagaland is home to a rich variety of natural, cultural, and environmental resources. It is a mountainous state and lies between the parallels of 95° and 94° eastern longitude and 25.2° and 27.0° latitude north. The high-profile [Dzukou Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzukou_Valley) is at [Viswema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswema), in the southern region of the state. The state has significant resources of natural minerals, petroleum, and hydropower, with the [primary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector) which is mostly agriculture still accounting for 24.6% of its economy.[^cite_note-N1-10] Other significant activities include [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), tourism, insurance, real estate, horticulture, and miscellaneous [cottage industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_industry).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-cc1-12][^cite_note-cc2-13]

## Etymology

The origin of the word 'Naga' is unclear.[^cite_note-undp-14] The present day Naga people have historically been referred to by many names, like "Noga" or "Naka" by the inhabitants of the [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) in what is now considered as [Assam](186162.md) which means "naked",[^cite_note-15] "Hao" by [Meitei people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people) of [Imphal Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_Valley)[^cite_note-16] and "Nakas" or 'Naga' by [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamar_people) of what is now considered as [Myanmar](19457.md)[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Burmese_sounds-18][^cite_note-19] meaning "people with earrings", while others suggest it means pierced noses.[^cite_note-Inato_cultural-20] Eventually, Nakanchi or Naganchi came to be an endonym for the region.[^cite_note-21] In recent years, some cultural activists have called for the state to be renamed Naganchi.[^cite_note-22]

Before the arrival of [European colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonialism) in South Asia, there had been many wars, [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution) and raids from [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) on the Nagas, [Meiteis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people) and others in India's northeast. The invaders came for "[head hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headhunting)" and to seek [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth) and [captives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) from these [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe) and [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group). When the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) inquired with Burmese guides about the people living in the northern Himalayas, they were told 'Naka'. This was recorded as 'Naga' and has been in use thereafter.[^cite_note-cc2-13][^cite_note-undp-14]

## History

Main article: [History of the Nagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Nagas)

See also: [Naga people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_people)

The ancient history of the Nagas is unclear. Ethnic groups migrated at different times, each settling in the northeastern part of present India and establishing their respective sovereign mountain terrains and village states. There are no records of whether they came from the northern Mongolian region, southeast Asia, or southwest China, except that their origins are from the east of India, and historical records show the present-day [Naga people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_people) settled before the arrival of the [Ahoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) in 1228 CE.[^cite_note-cc1-12][^cite_note-undp-14]

### Kingdom of Ava

In [Yan-aung-myin Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Aung_Myin_Shwe_Lett_Hla_Pagoda) inscription found in [Pinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinya) of [Myanmar](19457.md) mentions that the [Kingdom of Ava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ava) under [Minkhaung I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkhaung_I) (1400–1421) in the early 1400s extended till the territories of the Nagas.[^cite_note-23]



Ava kingdom in 1450

### British administration

With the arrival of the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in the early 19th century, followed by the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), Britain expanded its domain over the whole of [South Asia](21566765.md), including the [Naga Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Hills_District,_British_India). The first [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans) to enter the hills were Captain [Francis Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Jenkins_\(East_India_Company_officer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Lieutenant [Robert Pemberton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pemberton?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1832. The early contact with the Naga ethnic groups was characterised by suspicion and [conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_\(process\)). The [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonialism) interests in Assam, such as managers of [tea estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_estates) and other trading posts led defensive action against raids from the ethnic groups who were known for their bravery and "[head hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headhunting)" practices. To put an end to these raids, the [British troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_troops) recorded 10 [military expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare) between 1839 and 1850.[^cite_note-undp-14] In February 1851, at the bloody [Battle of Kikrüma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kikr%C3%BCma), people died on both the British side and the Kikrüma (Naga) side; in the days after the battle, inter-ethnic warfare followed that led to more bloodshed. After that war, the British adopted a policy of caution and non-interference with Naga ethnic groups.[^cite_note-TOI-24][^cite_note-Reddy-25]



A sketch of Angami Naga tribesman from 1875

&#x20;Despite this, [colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonialism) continued to move into Naga peoples' territory. Between 1851 and 1865, Naga ethnic groups continued to raid the British in Assam. The British India Government took over the holdings of the East Indian Company following the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). The failings and [atrocities](26684335.md) of the [East Indian Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) led the British Crown to review its governance structure throughout [South Asia](21566765.md) including its northeastern region. In 1866, the British India administration established a post at Samaguting with the explicit goal of ending intertribal warfare and tribal raids on property and personnel.[^cite_note-cc1-12][^cite_note-cc2-13]



A British India 1940 map showing Nagaland and Kohima City as part of Assam

In 1869, Captain Butler was appointed to lead and consolidate the British presence in the Nagaland Hills. In 1878, the headquarters were transferred to Kohima – creating a city that remains an important centre of administration, commerce, and culture for Nagaland.[^cite_note-undp-14]

On 4 October 1879, British political agent G. H. Damant went to [Khonoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khonoma) with troops, where he was shot dead with 35 of his team.[^cite_note-26] Kohima was subsequently attacked and the stockade looted. This violence led to a determined effort by the British Raj to return and respond. The subsequent defeat of Khonoma marked the end of serious and persistent ultimatums in the Naga Hills.[^cite_note-undp-14]

Between 1880 and 1922, the British administration consolidated their position over a large area of the Naga Hills and integrated it into its Assam operations. The British administration enforced the [rupee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupee) as the currency for economic activity and a system of structured ethnic government that was very different from historic social governance practices.[^cite_note-cc1-12]

In parallel, since the mid-19th century, [Christian missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) from the United States and Europe, stationed in India,[^cite_note-\:1-27] reached into Nagaland and neighbouring states, converting Nagaland's Naga ethnic groups from [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) to Christianity.[^cite_note-cc1-12][^cite_note-28]

### World War II



[Kohima War Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohima_War_Cemetery), Kohima, Nagaland



Kohima War Cemetery

Main article: [Battle of Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kohima)

See also: [Battle of the Tennis Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Tennis_Court)

In 1944, during [World War II](32927.md), the Japanese Army, with the help of the [Indian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Army) led by [Netaji Subhashchandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhashchandra_Bose), invaded through Burma and attempted to take India through Kohima. The population was evacuated. [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) soldiers [defended the area of Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kohima) and having lost many of their original force were relieved by British in June 1944. Together the British and Indian troops successfully repelled the Japanese troops.[^cite_note-29] The battle was fought from 4 April to 22 June 1944 from the town of Kohima, coordinated with [action at Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal), Manipur.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The Indian National Army lost half their numbers, many through starvation, and were forced to withdraw through Burma.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

There is the [World War II](32927.md) Cemetery, and the War Museum, in honour of those who died during World War II during the fighting between the British Empire and Japanese troops. Nearly 4,000 British Empire troops died, along with 3,000 Japanese. Many of those who died were Naga people, particularly the Angami Nagas. Near the memorial is the [Kohima Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Help_of_Christians_Cathedral,_Kohima), on [Aradura Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aradura_Hill?action=edit\&redlink=1), built with funds from the families and friends of deceased Japanese soldiers. Prayers are held in Kohima for peace and in memory of the fallen of both sides of the battle.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Naga national awakening

In 1929, a memorandum was submitted to the [Simon Statutory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Commission), requesting that the Nagas be exempt from reforms and new taxes proposed in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India), and should be left alone to determine their own future.[^cite_note-36]

The [Naga Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Memorandum?action=edit\&redlink=1) submitted by the [Naga Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Club) (which later became the [Naga National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_National_Council)) to the Simon Commission explicitly stated, 'to leave us alone to determine ourselves as in ancient times.'[^cite_note-sks-37]

### Post-independence history

After the [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) in 1947, the area remained a part of the province of [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Province). Nationalist activities arose among a section of the Nagas. [Phizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angami_Zapu_Phizo)-led [Naga National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_National_Council) demanded a political union of their ancestral and native groups. The movement led to [a series of violent incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_conflict_in_Nagaland), that damaged government and civil infrastructure, attacked government officials and civilians. The central government sent the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) in 1955, to restore order. In 1957, an agreement was reached between Naga leaders and the Indian government, creating a single separate region of the [Naga Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Hills). The Tuensang frontier was united with this single political region, Naga Hills Tuensang Area (NHTA),[^cite_note-Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly-38] and it became an autonomous area under [Sixth Schedule to the Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Schedule_to_the_Constitution_of_India). It was to be "administered by the Governor as the agent of the President but will be distinct from the North East Frontier Administration".[^cite_note-Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly-38]

This was not satisfactory to the Nagas, however, and agitation with violence increased across the state – including attacks on army and government institutions, banks, as well as non-payment of taxes. In July 1960, following discussion between [Prime Minister](24452.md) Nehru and the leaders of the Naga People Convention (NPC), a [16-point agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_16-Point_Agreement) was arrived at whereby the Government of India recognised the formation of Nagaland as a full-fledged state within the Union of India.[^cite_note-39]

### Nagaland statehood and late 20th century

Accordingly, the territory was placed under the Nagaland Transitional Provisions Regulation, 1961[^cite_note-40] which provided for an Interim body consisting of 45 members to be elected by tribes according to the customs, traditions and usage of the respective tribes. Subsequently, Nagaland attained statehood with the enactment of the state of Nagaland Act in 1962[^cite_note-41] by the [Parliament](410238.md). The interim body was dissolved on 30 November 1963 and the state of Nagaland was formally inaugurated on 1 December 1963 and [Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohima) was declared as the state capital. After elections in January 1964, the first democratically elected [Nagaland Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly) was constituted on 11 February 1964.[^cite_note-Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly-38][^cite_note-42]

The rebel activity continued in many Naga inhabited areas both in India and Burma. Ceasefires were announced and negotiations continued, but this did little to stop the violence. In March 1975, a direct presidential rule was imposed by the then Prime Minister [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi) on the state. In November 1975, some leaders of the largest rebel groups agreed to lay down their arms and accept the [Indian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_constitution), a small group did not agree and continued their insurgent activity.[^cite_note-43] The [Nagaland Baptist Church Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaland_Baptist_Church_Council) played an important role by initiating peace efforts in the 1960s.[^cite_note-undp-14]

### 21st century

In 2004, [two powerful bombs were set off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Dimapur_bombings) on the same day and struck the Dimapur Railway Station and the Hong Kong Market, resulting in 30 deaths and wounding over 100 others in the deadliest terrorist attack in Nagaland to date.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

Over the 5-year period of 2009 to 2013, between 0 and 11 civilians died per year in Nagaland from rebellion related activity (or less than 1 death per 100,000 people), and between 3 and 55 militants died per year in inter-factional killings (or between 0 and 3 deaths per 100,000 people).[^cite_note-46]

In early 2017, [Nagaland went into a state of civil unrest and protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Nagaland_protests) in response to the announcement to implement 33% women's reservation in the Civic Elections.[^cite_note-47]

On 4 December 2021, a unit of the [21st Para Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_\(Special_Forces\)) of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) [killed six civilian labourers near Oting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nagaland_killings) Village in the [Mon district](3390836.md) of Nagaland. Eight more civilians and a soldier were killed in subsequent violence. The incident was widely condemned, with many calling out to repeal and revoke the [Armed Forces Special Powers Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Special_Powers_Act).[^cite_note-48]

The most recent Nagaland Legislative Assembly election took place on 27 February 2023 to elect the Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLA) in the 60 Assembly Constituencies in the state. A voter turnout of 87% was observed in the election.[^cite_note-49] The election created history by electing two women candidates for the first time in Nagaland – [Hekani Jakhalu Kense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hekani_Jakhalu_Kense) and [Salhoutuonuo Kruse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salhoutuonuo_Kruse). Both candidates were from the ruling [Nationalist Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Democratic_Progressive_Party) (NDPP).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Salhoutuonuo Kruse later became the first woman minister of the Nagaland Legislative Assembly.[^cite_note-52]

## Geography



Sunset from the hills of the central part of Nagaland

Twenty per cent of the total land area of the state is covered with wooded forest, a haven for flora and fauna. The evergreen tropical and subtropical forests are found in strategic pockets in the state.[^cite_note-53]

### Climate

Nagaland has a largely monsoon climate with high humidity levels. Annual rainfall averages around 1,800–2,500 millimetres (70–100 in), concentrated in the months of May to September. Temperatures range from 21 to 40 °C (70 to 104 °F). In winter, temperatures do not generally drop below 4 °C (39 °F), but frost is common at high elevations. Summer is the shortest season in the state, lasting only a few months. The temperature during the summer season remains between 16 and 31 °C (61 and 88 °F). Winter often arrives early, with bitter cold and dry weather striking certain regions of the state. The maximum average temperature recorded in the winter season is 24 °C (75 °F). Strong northwest winds blow across the state during the months of February and March.[^cite_note-54]

About one-sixth of Nagaland is covered by tropical and sub-tropical evergreen forests—including palms, bamboo, rattan as well as timber and [mahogany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahogany) forests. Nagaland has a rich birdlife with more than 490 species.[^cite_note-Choudhury2003-57] The [great Indian hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Indian_hornbill) has a place in Naga culture. [Blyth's tragopan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragopan_blythii), a [vulnerable species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_species) of galliform, is the state bird of Nagaland. It is sighted in [Mount Japfü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Japf%C3%BC) and [Dzüko Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dz%C3%BCko_Valley) of Kohima District, Satoi range in [Zünheboto district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCnheboto_district) and [Pfütsero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pf%C3%BCtsero) in [Phek district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phek_district).[^cite_note-58] The state is also known as the "falcon capital of the world"[^cite_note-amur2-55] thanks to the hundreds of thousands of [Amur falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_falcon) that stop at Doyang Reservoir to feast on flying termites on their way from China and Siberia to Africa each year.

### Geology

Several preliminary studies indicate significant recoverable reserves of petroleum and natural gas. Limestone, marble and other decorative stone reserves are plentiful, and other as yet unexploited minerals include iron, nickel, chromium, and cobalt.[^cite_note-pn-61]

### Urbanisation

The Nagaland population is largely rural with 71.14% living in rural regions in 2011.[^cite_note-62] Census reports up to 1951 listed just one settlement in Nagaland as a town, the capital Kohima. The next two settlements, [Dimapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur) and [Mokokchung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokokchung) were listed as towns from 1961. Four more towns appeared in 1981: [Tuensang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuensang), [Wokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wokha), [Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon,_India) and [Zünheboto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCnheboto).[^cite_note-Yadav-63]

The relatively slow rate of urbanisation in Nagaland was described in the 1980s as being an effect of (a) the largely administrative roles of the towns, except for Dimapur which had a more diversified economy, and (b) a low level of mobility among the tribes of Nagaland, [scheduled tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_tribes) constituting nearly 90% of the population.[^cite_note-Yadav-63]

The population of Nagaland consists of almost 2.2 million people, consisting of 1.04 million males and 0.95 million females.[^cite_note-undp-14] Among its districts, Kohima has the largest population (270,063) followed by Dimapur (170,000). The least populated district is Longleng (50,593). 75% of the population lives in the rural areas. As of 2013, about 10% of rural population is below the poverty line; among the people living in urban areas 4.3% of them are below the poverty line.[^cite_note-66]

The state showed a population drop between the 2001 census and the 2011 census, the only state to show a population drop in the census. The [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) of Nagaland stands at 75.4 years, 79.9 years for females and 71.5 years for males (2019–21)[^cite_note-YadavLE-74] while the [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality_rate) amounts to 3 per 1,000 births (2019),[^cite_note-\:0-75] on par with the most developed countries. #### Christianity

Main article: [Christianity in Nagaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Nagaland)

See also: [Nagaland Baptist Church Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaland_Baptist_Church_Council)



The Catholic Cathedral in Kohima City. About 80% of Nagaland's citizens are [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists).

The state's population is 1.978 million, out of which 88% are Christians.[^cite_note-Indian_Census-88][^cite_note-vj-89] Nagaland is known as "the only predominantly [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) State in the World" and "the most Baptist State in the World."[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

Christianity arrived in Nagaland in the early 19th century. The American Baptist Naga mission grew out of the Assam mission in 1836. [Miles Bronson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Bronson), [Nathan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Brown_\(missionary\)) and other [Christian Missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Missionaries) working out of [Jaipur](166537.md) to bring Christianity to the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), saw the opportunity for gaining converts since many parts of [India's northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India) was principally [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animist) and [folk religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_religion)-driven. Along with other tribal regions of the northeast, the people of Nagaland converted to Christianity.[^cite_note-\:1-27]

#### Hinduism

Hinduism form around 8% of Nagaland. Hindus are concentrated mainly in the erstwhile Dimapur District (as per the [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India), comprising the present districts of Dimapur, Niuland, and Chümoukedima)[^cite_note-93] (28.75%) and Kohima District (9.51%).[^cite_note-census2011-86] [Dimapur Kalibari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur_Kalibari) is a famous temple in Nagaland.[^cite_note-94] A small number of [Nagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_peoples) (5,176) follow Hinduism according to the 2011 census.[^cite_note-95]

#### Islam

Islam is the third practised religion with approximately 2.5 per cent of the Nagaland population.[^cite_note-96] The highest concentration of Muslims in [Dimapur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur_district), while other districts have less than 2%.[^cite_note-97]

Historical accounts indicate that Islamic influence reached parts of the [Naga Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Hills) during the seventeenth century through the activities of the Assamese Sufi figure [Azan Fakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azan_Fakir).[^cite_note-Ali_Irshad_2012-98] According to one account, he delegated an associate named Saleh Saheb to remain in the hills and continue religious work among local communities. The descendants of Saleh Saheb later became known as the *Parbatia Dewan* ("Chief of the Hill").[^cite_note-ANM_Irshad_2007-99] Despite these efforts, Islam did not lead to the formation of a distinct indigenous tribal Naga-Muslim community in Nagaland.[^cite_note-ANM_Irshad_2007-99]

### Other religions

There are also [folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_religion) practised by some Nagas, specially among the [Zeliangrongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeliangrong) ([Zemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeme_people), [Liangmais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangmai_people) and [Rongmeis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongmei_Naga_people)) but few among other Naga ethnic groups.[^cite_note-100]

## Economy

The Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) of Nagaland was about ₹12,065 crore (US\$1.3 billion) in 2011–12.[^cite_note-106] Nagaland's GSDP grew at 9.9% compounded annually for a decade, thus more than doubling the per capita income.[^cite_note-ibef-107]

Nagaland has a literacy rate of 80.1 per cent. The majority of the population in the state speaks English, which is the official language of the state. The state offers technical and medical education.[^cite_note-ibef-107] Nevertheless, agriculture and forestry contribute a majority of Nagaland's Gross Domestic Product. The state is rich in mineral resources such as coal, limestone, iron, nickel, cobalt, chromium, and marble.[^cite_note-108]



Terrace farming at [Pfütsero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pf%C3%BCtsero)

Plantation crops such as premium coffee, cardamom, and tea are grown in hilly areas in small quantities with large growth potential. Most people cultivate rice as it is the main staple diet of the people. About 80% of the cropped area is dedicated to rice. Oilseeds is another, higher income crop gaining ground in Nagaland.

The farm productivity for all crops is low, compared to other Indian states, suggesting a significant opportunity for farmer income increase. Currently, the Jhum to Terraced cultivation ratio is 4\:3; where Jhum is the local name for cut-and-burn shift farming. Jhum farming is ancient, causes a lot of pollution and soil damage, yet accounts for the majority of the farmed area. The state does not produce enough food and depends on the trade of food from other states of India.[^cite_note-pn-61]

Tourism has a lot of potentials but was largely limited due to insurgency and concern of [terrorist](30636.md) violence over the last five decades. More recently, a number of Small Medium Enterprises and private sector companies have actively promoted Nagaland tourism, helping initiate a growing tourism market. Tourism experts contend that the state's uniqueness and strategic location in northeast India give Nagaland an advantage in tapping into the tourism sector for economic growth.[^cite_note-'_A_window_to_the_northeast'-109]

The state generates 87.98 MU compared to a demand for 242.88 MU. This deficit requires Nagaland to buy power. The state has significant hydroelectric potential, which if realised could make the state a power surplus state. In terms of power distribution, every village and town, and almost every household has an electricity connection; but, this infrastructure is not effective given the power shortage in the state.[^cite_note-pn-61]

### Natural resources

After a gap of almost 20 years, Nagaland state Chief Minister, T. R. Zeliang launched the resumption of oil exploration in Changpang and Tsori areas, under Wokha District in July 2014. The exploration will be carried out by the Metropolitan Oil & Gas Pvt. Ltd. Zeliang has alleged failures and disputed payments made to the statement made by the previous explorer, the state-owned [Oil and Natural Gas Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_Natural_Gas_Corporation) (ONGC).[^cite_note-110]

## Transportation

&#x20;Nagaland's rugged and mountainous landscape presents a major challenge to the infrastructural development of transport. Roads are the backbone of Nagaland's transportation network. The state has over 15,000 km of surfaced roads, but these are not satisfactorily maintained given the weather damage. Yet, in terms of population served for each kilometre of surfaced road, Nagaland is the second best state in the region after Arunachal Pradesh.[^cite_note-pn-61]

#### Hornbill Festival

Main article: [Hornbill Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbill_Festival)

The Hornbill Festival[^cite_note-114] was launched by the [Government of Nagaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nagaland) in December 2000 to encourage inter-ethnic interaction and to promote cultural heritage of the state.

It is held at the [Kisama Heritage Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisama_Heritage_Village) which is about 12 km south of Kohima. All the ethnic groups of Nagaland take part in this festival. The aim of the festival is to revive and protect the rich culture of Nagaland and display its history, culture and traditions.[^cite_note-115]



Hornbill Festival, Nagaland

The festival is named after the [hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Indian_hornbill) bird, which is displayed in folklores in most of the state's ethnic groups. The week-long festival unites Nagaland and people enjoy the colourful performances, crafts, sports, food fairs, games, and ceremonies. Traditional arts which include paintings, wood carvings, and sculptures are on display. Festival highlights include traditional Naga Morungs exhibition and sale of arts and crafts, food stalls, herbal medicine stalls, shows and sales, cultural medley – songs and dances, fashion shows, beauty contest, traditional archery, naga wrestling, indigenous games, and musical concerts. Additional attractions include the Konyak fire eating demonstration, pork-fat eating competitions, the Hornbill Literature Festival (including the Hutton Lectures), Hornbill Global Film Fest, Hornbill Ball, Choral Panorama, North East India Drum Ensemble, Naga King Chilli eating competition, Hornbill National Rock Contest,[^cite_note-116] Hornbill International Motor Rally and WW-II Vintage Car Rally.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

#### Kene

Further information: [Kene (Naga wrestling)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kene_\(Naga_wrestling\))

*Kene* or *Naga wrestling* is a [folk wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_wrestling) style and traditional [sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport) of the [Nagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_people).[^cite_note-119]

#### Aki Kiti

Further information: [Aki Kiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_Kiti)

*Aki Kiti* or *Sümi kick fighting* is a traditional [combat sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sport) originating from and was practised by the [Sümi Nagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCmi_Naga). It is characterised by [kicking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kicking) and [blocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocking_\(martial_arts\)) solely using the [soles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sole_\(foot\)) of the feet. The sporting event served the purpose of righting wrongs, restoring honour, or "settling scores" between tribes and tribesmen without resorting to violence. It was practised during tribal ceremonies.[^cite_note-ChrisCrudelli-120]

### Historical rituals

#### Feasts of Merit

Main article: [Feast of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_Merit)

In Naga society, individuals were expected to find their place in the social hierarchy, and prestige was the key to maintaining or increasing social status. To achieve these goals a man, whatever his ascendancy, had to be a [headhunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headhunting) or great warrior, have many sexual conquests among women, or complete a series of merit feasts.[^cite_note-121]

The [Feasts of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_Merit) reflected the splendor and celebration of Naga life.[^cite_note-cc1-12] Only married men could give such Feasts, and his wife took a prominent and honoured place during the ritual which emphasised male-female co-operation and interdependence. His wife brewed the [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer) which he offered to the guests. The event displayed ceremonies and festivities organised by the sponsor. The Feast given by a wealthier community person would be more extravagant.[^cite_note-122] He would typically invite everyone from the ethnic group. This event bestowed honour to the couple from the community. After the Feast, the tribe would give the couple rights to ornaments equally.[^cite_note-cc1-12][^cite_note-123]

Tourism experts contend that the state's uniqueness and strategic location in northeast India give Nagaland an advantage in tapping into the tourism sector for economic growth.[^cite_note-'_A_window_to_the_northeast'-109] The state has been extremely successful in promoting the great [Hornbill Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbill_Festival), which attracts Indian and foreign tourists alike.The key thrusts of Nagaland's tourism are its rich culture, showcasing of history and wildlife.[^cite_note-128] Tourism infrastructure is rapidly improving.[^cite_note-129] Local initiatives and tourism pioneers are now beginning to promote a socially [responsible tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_tourism) model involving the participation of the councils, village elders, the church and the youth.[^cite_note-130]

## Notability

The [Naga Mircha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Mircha) is a variety of [chilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper) mainly grown in this state. They are one of the world's hottest chillies and an indigenous treasure of Nagaland, deeply rooted in the region's lifestyle since ancient times. For generations, farmers in Nagaland's chilli-growing areas have cultivated it relying on it as a primary source of income and livelihood. This iconic chilli is an integral part of Nagaland's culinary identity, inseparably linked to the local community as a staple ingredient.[^cite_note-131]

### Geographical indication

It was awarded the [Geographical Indication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geographical_indications_in_India) (GI) status tag from the [Geographical Indications Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Indication_Registry_\(India\)) under the [Union Government of India](553883.md) on 2 December 2008 (valid until 21 August 2027).[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

The Secretary, Department of Horticulture & Agriculture, Government of Nagaland, from [Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohima), proposed the GI registration of Naga Mircha. After filing the application in August 2007, the chilli was granted the GI tag in 2008 by the Geographical Indication Registry in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), making the name "Naga Mircha" exclusive to the chilies grown in the region. It thus became the first chilli variety from Nagaland and the first type of goods from Nagaland to earn the GI tag.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]
