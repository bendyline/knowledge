The all-time medal table for all [Olympic Games](22576.md) from 1896 to 2026, including [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md), [Winter Olympic Games](34069.md), and a combined total of both, is tabulated below. These [Olympic medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal) counts do not include the [1906 Intercalated Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_Intercalated_Games) which are no longer recognized by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC) as official Games. The IOC itself does not publish all-time tables, and publishes unofficial tables only per single Games. This table was thus compiled by adding up single entries from the IOC database.[^cite_note-IOC-1]

The results are attributed to the [IOC country code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_IOC_country_codes) as currently displayed by the IOC database. Usually, a single code corresponds to a single [National Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) (NOC). When different codes are displayed for different years, medal counts are combined in the case of a simple change of IOC code (such as from HOL to NED for the [Netherlands](21148.md)) or simple change of country name (such as from Ceylon to [Sri Lanka](26750.md)). As the medals are attributed to each NOC, [not all totals include medals](#Notes) won by athletes from that country for another NOC, such as before independence of that country. Names in italic are national entities that no longer exist. The totals of NOCs are not combined with those of their predecessors and successors.

## List of NOCs with medals (sortable & unranked)

The table is pre-sorted by the name of each Olympic Committee, but can be displayed as sorted by any other column, such as the total number of [gold medals](526488.md) or total number of overall medals. To sort by gold, silver, and then bronze, sort first by the bronze column, then the silver, and then the gold. The table does not count revoked medals (e.g., due to [doping](860860.md)).

A total of 162 current and historical NOCs have earned at least one medal. Medal totals in this table are current through the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md), and all [changes in medal standings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stripped_Olympic_medals) and medal redistribution up to 11 August 2024 for the Summer Games and 19 September 2025 for the Winter Games are taken into account.

As of completion of the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md), 12 [National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committees) (NOC) have participated on a standalone basis in all 24 Winter Olympic Games. As of completion of the 2024 Summer Olympics, two NOCs ([Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_at_the_Olympics) and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_at_the_Olympics)) have participated on a standalone basis in all 30 Summer Olympic Games. Four other NOCs have missed a perfect participation record due to the [1980 Summer Olympics boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics_boycott), while two additional NOCs ([France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_at_the_Olympics) and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_at_the_Olympics)) missed the [1904 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics), although athletes of their nationalities competed for other countries (see [§ Early Olympics](#Early_Olympics), below).[^cite_note-olympedia_nationality-2] Of the combined 55 Olympic Games, only Great Britain has a 100% participation rate.

The sum total of gold, silver, and bronze medals are not equal for the following reasons:

- Some sports (such as [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo_at_the_Summer_Olympics), and [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_Summer_Olympics)) award or have previously awarded two bronze medals per competition.

- Team sports medals, such as in football or basketball count as one, even if there are multiple players on each team, who get a medal.

- Some tied performances have resulted in multiple medals of the same colour being awarded for an event. If this tie is for gold or silver, there will be a consequent absence of a silver or bronze medal for that event.

- Some medals that have been revoked have not been re-awarded.

- Some early events, such as [cricket at the 1900 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_at_the_1900_Summer_Olympics), had only two entrants.

- Retroactively awarding gold, silver, and bronze medals for the 1896 and 1900 games results in some anomalies, such as the [100 metre freestyle swimming event in 1896](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_1896_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_100_metre_freestyle) where no surviving records distinguish the places of those who finished between 3rd and 10th position.

## Variations

### Early Olympics

See also: [1900 Summer Olympics medal table § Changes in medal standings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics_medal_table#Changes_in_medal_standings), and [1904 Summer Olympics medal table § Changes in medal standings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics_medal_table#Changes_in_medal_standings)

For the [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics) and [1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics) Summer Olympics, several countries attribute appearances and medals to athletes of their nationality that the IOC attributes to other countries. In 2024, the IOC reinstated its original and current attribution policy for athletes at the early Olympic Games (1896—1904). From 2021 to 2024, the IOC briefly attributed medals in individual events according to the athlete's nationality, even when the athlete represented a foreign club or team. Since 2024, participation and medals have instead been attributed according to the national affiliation of the club or team represented by the athlete.[^cite_note-olympedia_nationality-2]

Other differences from the official table are based on disagreements about which events were Olympic. This affects several of the events in the 1900 and 1904 Olympics. In addition, some sources include the [1906 Intercalated Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_Intercalated_Games) when compiling their medal tables.[^cite_note-SR5-14]

### Art competitions

-  [Art competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_competitions_at_the_Summer_Olympics) (1912–1948)

Art competitions were held between the 1912 and 1948 Games, with medals awarded.[^cite_note-Stromberg_2012-15] The IOC classifies art competitions as non-sport competitions, separate from the Olympic sports programme.[^cite_note-IOC_Olympic_Programme-3][^cite_note-IOC_Art_competitions-16] In 1952, art competition medals were removed from the official national medal counts.[^cite_note-e048-17] Only since 2021 have they been officially listed again by the IOC in the medal tables and respective [National Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) (NOC) profiles on its website.[^cite_note-SUI_profile-18]

### Alpinism and aeronautics

-  [Alpinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpinism_at_the_Olympic_Games) (1924 w, 1932, 1936)

-  [Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) (1936)

From 1924 through 1936, the IOC on several occasions [awarded gold medals for feats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_sports#Feats_and_artistic_events) of alpinism and aeronautics that occurred in the preceding four-year Olympiad. Three gold medals in alpinism were awarded (for the [1922 British Mount Everest expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_British_Mount_Everest_expedition), the first ascent of the northern wall of the [Matterhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matterhorn) in 1931, and for the [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) expeditions of 1930 and 1934 combined), respectively in the 1924 Winter, 1932 Summer, and 1936 Summer Games.[^cite_note-Alpinism-19] In addition to the alpinism awards, in 1936, a gold medal was awarded in aeronautics to [Hermann Schreiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Schreiber_\(Olympic_aeronaut\)) of Switzerland for the first crossing of the Alps in a glider in 1935.[^cite_note-20]

Some sources include these IOC awards of gold medals in the overall count. However, the IOC classifies these awards, like the medals awarded in artistic competitions, as separate from the official Olympic sports programme.[^cite_note-IOC_Olympic_Programme-3] The contemporary *Olympia-Zeitung*, the official journal from the 1936 Berlin Games, shows that these awards in 1936—final time such awards were offered—like art competition medals, were not included in the national medal count,[^cite_note-Olympia-Zeitung_1936-21] unlike in the current IOC database for the medal tables of the Games in question.[^cite_note-olympics_1936-22]

### British West Indies

The West Indies Federation,[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] also known as the West Indies,[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] the Federation of the West Indies[^cite_note-27] or the West Indian Federation,[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] was a short-lived [political union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) that existed from 3 January 1958 to 31 May 1962. Various islands in the [Caribbean](18956035.md) that were [part of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indies), including [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), [Barbados](3455.md), [Jamaica](15660.md), and those on the [Leeward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Leeward_Islands) and [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Windward_Islands), came together to form the Federation.The expressed intention of the Federation was to create a political unit that would [become independent](302084.md) from Britain as a single state[^cite_note-31] Before that could happen, the Federation collapsed due to internal political conflicts over how it would be governed or function viably.

Athletes from the [West Indies Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_Federation) competed under the name [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indies_at_the_1960_Summer_Olympics) (ANT),[^cite_note-32] renamed to [British West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indies_at_the_1960_Summer_Olympics) (BWI) by the IOC, at the [1960 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics) in [Rome](25458.md), [Italy](14532.md). Thirteen competitors—two from Barbados, four from Trinidad, and seven from Jamaica—all men, took part in thirteen events in five sports.[^cite_note-SportsRef-33] The short-lived nation only participated at these single Games, as [Jamaica](15660.md) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) competed independently again in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics), and [Barbados](3455.md) started competing at the [1968 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics).

### Netherlands Antilles

The [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles) participated at the [Olympic Games](22576.md) from 1952 until 2008 as a constituent country of the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands). The [National Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) for the Netherlands Antilles was created in 1931 and recognized by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) from 1950 until 2011 upon the [dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Netherlands_Antilles).[^cite_note-curtain-37]

[Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) left the Netherlands Antilles in 1986 to become a constituent country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Between 1952 until 1984, Aruban athletes competed as part of the Netherlands Antilles. Since the Olympic Games in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics), athletes from Aruba have competed separately under [their own Olympic banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba_at_the_Olympics) and have participated in each [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md) since then.

After the dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles in 2010, Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba became part of the Netherlands as [special municipalities of the Netherlands](27203221.md#Administration). Curaçao and Sint Maarten became separate constituent countries of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. At the 2012 Summer Olympics, participants from the five islands competed as [independent athletes under the Olympic flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Olympic_Athletes_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics). However, athletes from the former Netherlands Antilles who qualified for the 2012 Summer Olympics were allowed to participate independently under the Olympic flag. In addition since then, athletes from the territories that constituted the Netherlands Antilles have the possibility of competing for the [Netherlands](21148.md) (as for example [Churandy Martina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churandy_Martina) did) or [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) (as for example [Philip Elhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Elhage) did).[^cite_note-AHO-38] Ultimately, three athletes from the Netherlands Antilles participated as Independent Olympic Athletes.

### China

The [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) (ROC) participated in its first [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Summer_Olympic_Games) in 1932 under the name of *China*. After the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), the ROC [retreated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_Government_of_the_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan) to the [island of Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_Taiwan) in 1949, and only Taiwan-based athletes have competed on its behalf since then. In 1971, the ROC was [expelled from the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Taiwan#1971_expulsion_from_the_UN), but was permitted to compete under its official name, flag, and anthem in the [1972 Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Winter_Olympics), [1972 Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics), and [1976 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics). It was denied official representation in the [1976 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Summer_Olympics) and [boycotted](18591996.md) it as a result. The 1979 Nagoya Resolution allowed the ROC to compete under the [deliberately-ambiguous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policy_of_deliberate_ambiguity) name "[Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei)"; it protested against this decision and boycotted the [1980 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics) as well, but has competed under this name since the [1984 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics).

The Republic of China took part in the Opening Ceremony of the [1924 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics), but its four athletes, all of whom were tennis players, withdrew from competition.[^cite_note-39]

[Hong Kong](13404.md) first competed at the [Olympic Games](22576.md) in 1952, then as a [British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colony) ([British Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong)). After the [sovereignty of Hong Kong was transferred back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong) to the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC) in 1997, the NOC for the new [special administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China) (SAR) of China has now been known as **Hong Kong, China**.[^cite_note-AP1-40]

### Yugoslavia

Teams from Yugoslavia first participated at the Olympic Games in 1920. *[Yugoslavia](34244.md)* has been the designation for Olympic teams from three distinct national entities:

- [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) (officially called the *Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes* until 1929) from 1920 to 1936

- [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) from 1948 to the [1992 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Winter_Olympics)

- [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), formed as a joint state by only Montenegro and Serbia after the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia), from 1996 to 2002

The [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_777)[^cite_note-47] affirmed that the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia had ceased to exist, and the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (FRY) was a new state. All former republics were entitled to state succession, while none of them continued SFR Yugoslavia's international legal personality. As a result of the U.N. resolution, individual FRY athletes were allowed to compete as [Independent Olympic Participants at the 1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Olympic_Participants_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics), and FRY was not allowed to compete at the [1994 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Winter_Olympics).

The *[Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia)*, consisting of the [Republic of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Serbia_\(federal\)) and the [Republic of Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Montenegro_\(federal\)), participated at the Games since 1996. At the 1996[^cite_note-OR1996-48] and 2000[^cite_note-OR2000-49] Games, the nation was designated with the same code, *Yugoslavia* (YUG), as the defunct [SFRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). It was rechartered as the *[State Union of Serbia and Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro)* in 2003 with there being no territorial changes. The *Serbia and Montenegro* (SCG) designation and code were used at the 2004 Games.[^cite_note-OR2004-50]

Two of the successor nations ([Croatia](5573.md) and [Slovenia](27338.md)) began to compete as independent teams at the Olympics starting at the 1992 Winter Games and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) at the 1992 Summer Games and as of the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics), all six successor nations, former socialist republics, have participated independently. [Kosovo](17391.md), a former autonomous province, made its Olympic debut as an independent national team at the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics).

In addition to the teams of [Independent Olympians at the Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Olympians_at_the_Olympic_Games) mentioned above—Kuwait, Russia, the former Netherlands Antilles, and the former Yugoslavia—several other NOCs were represented by independent Olympic athletes. East Timor's team in 2000 consisted of four athletes competing as [Individual Olympic Athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Olympic_Athletes_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics). In the 2014 Winter Olympics, the team from India included one [independent Olympic participant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Olympic_Participants_at_the_2014_Winter_Olympics).

[**<sup>S</sup>**](#ref_SS) In 2009, historians from the International Society of Olympic Historians discovered that cyclist [Frank Bizzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Bizzoni) was an Italian citizen when he competed in 1904 being granted US citizenship in 1917.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-SR10-65] However, the IOC does not officially recognize Italy's participation in the 1904 Olympics. [**<sup>Fm</sup>**](#ref_FFm) The IOC counts one gold, one silver, and two bronze medals won by American fencer [Albertson Van Zo Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertson_Van_Zo_Post) for Cuba instead of the United States. The one silver and one bronze medal won by American [Charles Tatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Tatham_\(fencer\)) are also counted for Cuba instead of the United States. |
