**Sarangani**, officially the **Province of Sarangani** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Lalawigan sa Sarangani*; [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language): *Kapuoran sang Sarangani*; [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language): *Dairat nu Sarangani*, [Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_Alphabet): دايرت نو سرڠان; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lalawigan ng Sarangani*), is a [province](165100.md) in the Philippines located in the [Soccsksargen](298888.md) [region](216786.md). Its capital is [Alabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabel,_Sarangani) while [Glan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glan,_Sarangani) is the most populous municipality in the province. With a 230-kilometer (140 mi) coastline along the [Sarangani Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Bay) and [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea), the province is at the southernmost tip of [Mindanao](20546.md) island, and borders [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato) and [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur) to the north, [Davao Occidental](38297169.md) to the east, and the [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea) to the south.

Sarangani is part of the South Cotabato-Cotabato-Sultan Kudarat-Sarangani-General Santos (Soccsksargen) development cluster, and is linked by paved roads to the international airport and harbor of [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos).

The province is divided into two sections, separated by the [Sarangani Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Bay) and the city of General Santos, and it used to be part of South Cotabato until it was made an independent province in 1992.[^cite_note-CongressGovPH-RA07228-4]

## History

### Early history

The earliest civilization in the province can be found in [Maitum, Sarangani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitum,_Sarangani), where the [Maitum Anthropomorphic Pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitum_Anthropomorphic_Pottery) or Maitum Jars were found. The jars have been dated to approximately 5 BC to 370 AD, one of the oldest in the entire Southeast Asian region and the Philippines. The discovery testified to the long history of cultural exchanges in Sarangani and its people.

The Sarangani was once part of [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao). The establishment of the Sultanate in the area caused more Maguindanaon settlers arrival. After the fall of the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao) as a great power in Mindanao, Datu Uto of Buayan expanded his domain towards Sarangani Bay. Sarangani would eventually be under the [Sultanate of Buayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Buayan) until the American era.

### Spanish colonial era

[Sarangani Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Island) (now part of [Davao Occidental](38297169.md)) was named *Antonia* by the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) [explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_explorer) [Ruy López de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_L%C3%B3pez_de_Villalobos) in 1543, in honor of [Antonio de Mendoza y Pacheco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_Mendoza), the [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_New_Spain) of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain) who had appointed López de Villalobos to lead [an expedition to the Western Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villalobos_Expedition) (now the Philippines) because of their [relation by marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-laws). The early inhabitants who first inhabited Sarangani were the [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) natives, called *MunaTo*, a native term for "first people."[^cite_note-5]



Map showing the present territories of Sarangani as part of [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao) in 1800s.

### Japanese occupation

In 1942, the [Japanese troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_Army) occupied Southern Cotabato. In 1945, Filipino troops of the 6th, 10th, 101st and 102nd Infantry Division of the [Philippine Commonwealth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) and 10th Constabulary Regiment of the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) entered in and liberated Southern Cotabato and fought against the Japanese Imperial Army forces during the [Battle of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maguindanao) at the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) under the [Japanese Occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Occupation_of_the_Philippines).

### Philippine independence

Before its inception in 1992, Sarangani was part of [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato) under its [third district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato's_3rd_congressional_district). The province was created by [Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Acts_of_the_Philippines) No. 7228 on March 16, 1992,[^cite_note-CongressGovPH-RA07228-4] penned by Congressman James Chiongbian, who would later become the province's first representative to the [House of Representatives](293605.md), ratified in a plebiscite on May 19, 1992 and installed its first set of officials on November 28, 1992. His wife, Priscilla Chiongbian, became the first Governor of Sarangani.

The [Sangil people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangil_people) are active in the struggle for self-determination as part of their strategy to preserve and develop their culture and social institutions. The creation of Sarangani province itself was an effort to ease tensions between the Sangil people and the Philippine government.[^cite_note-ncca-6]

## Geography



Western coast of the province at [Maitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitum,_Sarangani)

Sarangani covers a total area of 3,601.25 square kilometers (1,390.45 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-7] occupying the southern tip of the [Soccsksargen](298888.md) in central [Mindanao](20546.md). The province is bordered on the central-north by [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato), northeast by [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur), east by [Davao Occidental](38297169.md), south by the [Sarangani Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Bay) and [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea), and northwest by [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat).

Sarangani is divided into two (eastern and western) sections, separated by the [Sarangani Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Bay) and [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos) in the middle. The western portion comprises the towns of [Maitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitum,_Sarangani), [Kiamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiamba,_Sarangani), and [Maasim](842903.md), and is bounded on the north by [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato) and on the northwest by [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat). The eastern section consists of [Alabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabel,_Sarangani), [Glan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glan,_Sarangani), [Malapatan](854734.md), and [Malungon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malungon,_Sarangani).

The *General Santos Metropolitan Area* or *Metro General Santos* is a metropolitan area encompassing the highly urbanized city of [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos), The Regional Agro-Industrial Center of Alabel, the towns of Glan, Kiamba, Maasin, Maitum, Malapatan and Malungon and the neighboring provinces of [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato) adding Metro General Santos adding [Lake Sebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sebu,_South_Cotabato), [Polomolok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polomolok), [T'Boli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T'Boli,_South_Cotabato) and [Tupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi,_South_Cotabato).

### Administrative divisions

Sarangani comprises seven [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_Philippines). A [single legislative district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_district_of_Sarangani) encompasses all towns.[^cite_note-nscb-7]

[Christianity](8276776.md) is the majority religion in the province with a total of 79% (48% [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_the_Philippines)[^cite_note-rappler_map_catholicism-13] and 31% [evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism_in_the_Philippines)).[^cite_note-Philippine_Church_National_Summary-14] Other religious minorities are [Islam](6037917.md) (9%)[^cite_note-rappler_map_islam-15] and [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) (2%).[^cite_note-rappler_map_iglesia-16] The remainder is usually divided among other Christian churches. The majority of Muslims are from the [Sangil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangil_people), [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people), and [Kalagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_people) ethnic groups. Furthermore, there are quite a few individuals who have converted to Islam, also known as *[Balik Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balik_Islam?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (reverting to Islam).[^cite_note-ncca-6]

### Ethnic groups

Sarangani has a huge ethnic diversity, such as [Lumads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad_people), consisting of [Blaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaan_people), [T'boli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T'boli_people), [Tagakaulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagakaulo_people), [Kalagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_people), [Manobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo_people), [Ubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubo_people), [Moro Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people), and Christian settlers, mainly from [Cebu](160278.md), [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol), [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor), [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros) and [Panay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panay) in [Visayas](72418.md) and [Ilocandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region) and [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley) in [Luzon](69375.md) Muslims comprise 7 ethnic groups, Lumads 17 ethnic groups, and Christian migrant settlers, at least 20 ethnic groups.[^cite_note-gov-17] [Sarangani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_people) and [Sangil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangil_people) peoples, although no longer dominant, still have a presence here.[^cite_note-ncca-6] Being one of the most ethnically and linguistically diverse regions in Mindanao.[^cite_note-gov-17]

The Blaan people are the largest minority and are spread across the municipalities of [Malapatan](854734.md), [Glan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glan,_Sarangani), [Alabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabel,_Sarangani), [Maasim](842903.md), and [Malungon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malungon). Most of Blaan people lives in Malapatan, constituting 37% of the municipality's population. Other ethnic groups, such as the [Maguindanao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people) reside in the municipalities of Malapatan, [Maitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitum), and Maasim, the T'boli people mostly reside in Maitum, [Kiamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiamba,_Sarangani), and Maasim, while the Tagakaulo people is almost entirely in [Malungon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malungon). [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people) settlers are found in Glan and Alabel, [Ilonggo (Hiligaynon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilonggo_people) are located in Malungon, while [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) mostly live in Kiamba and Maitum.[^cite_note-gov-17]

Thus, the mixed population of Sarangani is quite diverse, with the language of the Christian settlers being used commonly by both Moro Muslims and natives, consisting of the Blaan and [Cebuano-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) Muslims on the east coast, the Tboli, Manobo, and [Ilocano-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language) Muslims on the west coast, and the [Ilonggo-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilonggo_language) Blaan and Tagakaulo peoples of the northern highlands.[^cite_note-gov-17]

Coconut, corn, rice, banana, mango, durian, rubber, and sugarcane are major crops now being planted by the inhabitants. The province has plantations (mango, banana, pineapple, asparagus), cattle ranches, and commercial fishponds that have been operating in the area, some of which having existed as far back as 40 years.

Electricity comes from the [National Power Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Power_Corporation), and augmented by a 50 MW power plant in Alabel, the province's capital. Water is provided for by sustainable spring development projects.

## Tourism

Sarangani celebrates its foundation anniversary every November, named as *MunaTo Festival*.[^cite_note-MB-MunaToFest-27]

Sarangani has ancient [burial jars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_jars), discovered by archaeologists from the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum) in *Ayub Cave* in [Maitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitum), in 1991 and in 2008, and at *Sagel Cave* in Maitum (now declared by [National Historical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Institute) as a national historical site). Amid [Mindanao](20546.md)'s armed conflicts, [artifacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artifact_\(archaeology\)) found thereat prove settlements of pre-historic civilization in Maitum.[^cite_note-MB-MunaToFest-27][^cite_note-PIA-IslandResort-28]
