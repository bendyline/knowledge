**Santa Marta** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[ˌsanta ˈmaɾta\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)), officially the **Distrito Turístico, Cultural e Histórico de Santa Marta** (English: Historic, Cultural & Tourist District of Santa Marta), is a port [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Colombia) on the coast of the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) in northern [Colombia](5222.md). It is renowned in [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American) as the place of death of [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar).

Santa Marta is the capital of [Magdalena Department](372981.md) and the fourth-largest urban city of the [Caribbean Region of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Region_of_Colombia), after [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla), [Cartagena](63927.md), and [Soledad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad,_Atlantico). Founded on July 29, 1525, by the Spanish conqueror [Rodrigo de Bastidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_de_Bastidas), it was one of the first Spanish settlements in Colombia, its oldest surviving city, and second-oldest in [South America](26769.md).[^cite_note-3] This city is situated on a bay by the same name and as such, it is a prime tourist destination in the Caribbean region.[^cite_note-4]

The [Universidad del Magdalena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Magdalena) is located in Santa Marta. Santa Marta's flag consists of two colors: white and blue. White symbolises peace, in that all are united without restriction. Blue symbolises the sky, the sea, the magic found in the horizon, and the snow-capped Sierra Nevada mountains. The **Cathedral Basilica of Santa Marta** is one of the oldest structures in the city. Built in the eighteenth century, it held the remains of Bastidas and Bolívar after their deaths.[^cite_note-5]

## History

### Pre-Columbian times

Further information: [Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era)

Before the arrival of Europeans, the South American continent was inhabited by a number of [indigenous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas). Due to a combination of tropical weather, significant rainfall, and the destruction and misrepresentation of many records by Spanish conquistadors, our understanding of the peoples of this region is limited.

The [Tairona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tairona) formed mid- to large-size population centers, consisting of stone pathways, terraces, protected waterways, and spaces dedicated to agricultural produce. Their economy was primarily agricultural, cultivating corn, pineapple, yucca, and other local foodstuffs. The Tayrona are considered quite advanced for their time period. Surviving archaeological sites consisted of formed terraces and small scale underground stone channels. They were known to actively collect and process salt, which was a significant trading commodity.&#x20;

They traded with other indigenous groups along the coast and interior. Archaeological excavations have recovered significant works in pottery, stonework and gold.

## Simón Bolívar

[Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar), the liberator of much of South America, spent his last weeks in Santa Marta, and died of tuberculosis in 1830, at the age of 47 at [Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinta_de_San_Pedro_Alejandrino), Santa Marta. He was buried in the cathedral of Santa Marta, but subsequently his remains were moved to [Caracas](55289.md).[^cite_note-6]

## Geography and Climate



Satellite view of the [Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_de_Santa_Marta).

Santa Marta is located on Santa Marta Bay of the Caribbean Sea in the province of Magdalena. It is 992 km from Bogotá and 93 km from Barranquilla. It is bordered to the north and west by the Caribbean and to the south by the municipalities of Aracataca and Ciénaga.

Santa Marta experiences a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*), bordering on a hot [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) climate, with largely uniform temperatures year round. The dry season lasts from December to April, while the wet season lasts from May to November.

## Economy

Santa Marta's economy is based primarily on tourism, followed by trade, port activities, fishing and, agriculture. The main agricultural products are: [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_%C3%97_paradisiaca), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangifera_indica), [citrus cultivars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus), [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean) and [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manihot_esculenta).

### Infrastructure

Santa Marta is a major port. [Simón Bolívar International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar_International_Airport_\(Colombia\)) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **SMR**) is 16 km (10 mi) from the city centre. Historic figure [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) died here, a significant event for South America as a whole. His villa known as [La Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Quinta_de_San_Pedro_Alejandrino) is located just outside the city centre. As the main city centre is located close to the coast, the city itself has had difficulty controlling expansion. Although, technically a separate locality, Rodadero is part of Santa Marta itself.
