The community of **Indians in Kuwait** includes Indian expatriates in [Kuwait](7515890.md), as well as Kuwaiti citizens of Indian origin or descent. According to the Indian ministry of external affairs, there are around 1,000,726 Indians as of 2023.[^cite_note-population-1] The majority of these originate from the south Indian states of [Kerala](4349459.md), [Karnataka](16880.md), [Panjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md), [Telangana](990267.md), [Tamil Nadu](29918.md), with the former constituting for 66% of the population of Indian nationals.[^cite_note-mae-2][^cite_note-ABS_Ancestry-3]

## Overview

See also: [1990 airlift of Indians from Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_airlift_of_Indians_from_Kuwait)

Kuwaitis view India as a fast-growing economy and a source of highly qualified professional and technical personnel.

Professionals like Engineers, doctors, Lawyers, [Chartered accountants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_accountant), Scientists, software experts, management professionals and consultants, Architects, retail traders and businessmen mainly constitute the Indian community. Of late, there has been an increase in the number of highly qualified Indian experts in hi-tech areas, especially in the software and financial sector in Kuwait. In the field of health, India not only supplies top specialists but also para-medical staff who enjoy a high reputation. Inward remittances from Kuwait to India are substantial.

There are 18 Indian schools in Kuwait affiliated to the [Central Board of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) (CBSE).[^cite_note-4] There were 164 Indian community Associations earlier registered with the Indian Embassy of Kuwait. Following introduction of a re-registration requirement, 106 of these Indian community Associations have once again registered with the Embassy and the number of registered Associations is growing at a steady pace.

## Religion

Indian are the most numerous expat group in Kuwait.[^cite_note-5] consisting of a number of [Muslims](19541.md), [Christians](18337522.md), [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus), [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) and [Buddhists](3267529.md). Members of religious groups not mentioned in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), such as Hindus, Sikhs and Buddhists, are not allowed to build official places of worship. However, these groups have religious freedom and can freely engage in religious activities, including public marriage and other celebrations.[^cite_note-6]
