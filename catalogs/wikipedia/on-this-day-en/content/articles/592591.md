# Ceasefire

"Truce" redirects here. For other uses, see [Truce (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truce_\(disambiguation\)).

For other uses, see [Ceasefire (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceasefire_\(disambiguation\)).



*A truce—not a compromise, but a chance for high-toned gentlemen to retire gracefully from their very civil declarations of war*\
By [Thomas Nast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Nast) in *[Harper's Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper's_Weekly)*, February 17, 1877, p. 132.

A **ceasefire** (also known as a **truce**),**[^cite_note-\:2-1]** also spelled **cease-fire** (the [antonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonym) of 'open fire'),[^cite_note-\:0-2] is a stoppage of a [war](33158.md) in which each side agrees with the other to suspend aggressive actions, often due to mediation by a third party.[^cite_note-\:3-3][^cite_note-4] Ceasefires may be between state actors or involve non-state actors.**[^cite_note-\:2-1]** Unlike declarations of a cessation of hostilities or a truce, a cease-fire is generally meant to be binding. Like a cessation of hostilities or truce, it is only temporary in nature but is generally expected to last for a longer period of time. Unlike a truce, a humanitarian pause, or a cessation of hostilities, the declaration of a cease-fire often applies to the entire geographical area of a conflict, according to the UN.[^cite_note-5]

Ceasefires may be declared as part of a formal [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) but also as part of an informal understanding between opposing forces.[^cite_note-\:0-2] They may occur via mediation or otherwise as part of a [peace process](24903.md) or be imposed by [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) resolutions via Chapter VII of the [United Nations Charter](88805.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2] A ceasefire can be temporary with an intended end date or may be intended to last indefinitely. A ceasefire is distinct from an [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice) in that the armistice is a formal end to a war whereas a ceasefire may be a temporary stoppage.[^cite_note-6]

The immediate goal of a ceasefire is to stop violence but the underlying purposes of ceasefires vary. Ceasefires may be intended to meet short-term limited needs (such as providing humanitarian aid), manage a conflict to make it less devastating, or advance efforts to peacefully resolve a dispute.[^cite_note-\:2-1] An actor may not always intend for a ceasefire to advance the peaceful resolution of a conflict but instead give the actor an upper hand in the conflict (for example, by re-arming and repositioning forces or attacking an unsuspecting adversary), which creates [bargaining problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargaining_model_of_war) that may make ceasefires less likely to be implemented and less likely to be durable if implemented.[^cite_note-\:3-3][^cite_note-\:2-1][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

The durability of ceasefire agreements is affected by several factors, such as [demilitarized zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarized_zone), withdrawal of troops and third-party guarantees such as [monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceasefire_monitoring_and_verification) or [peacekeeping](84617.md). Ceasefire agreements are more likely to be durable when they reduce incentives to attack, reduce uncertainty about the adversary's intentions, and when mechanisms are put in place to prevent accidents from spiraling into conflict.[^cite_note-\:3-3]

## Overview

Ceasefire agreements are more likely to be reached when the costs of conflict are high and when the actors in a conflict have lower [audience costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audience_cost).[^cite_note-9] Scholars emphasize that war termination is more likely to occur when actors have more information about each other, when actors can make credible commitments, and when the domestic political situation makes it possible for leaders to make war termination agreements without incurring domestic punishment.[^cite_note-10]

By one estimate, there were at least 2202 ceasefires across 66 countries in 109 civil conflicts over the period 1989–2020.[^cite_note-\:2-1]

## Ceasefire types

Researchers Govinda Clayton and Valerie Sticher classified [civil-war](7085.md) ceasefire agreements into three types, with successively broader peace agreements and higher trust levels:

- a "cessation of hostilities" agreement that can be implemented quickly, while detailed peace negotiations are still unrealistic and trust levels are low;

- a "preliminary ceasefire" agreement, in which the motivations for the parties to stop the conflict are stronger and negotiations are ongoing, but trust remains low;

- a "definitive ceasefire" agreement, in which the ceasefire is part of a broad peace agreement, typically including [disarmament, demobilization and reintegration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament,_demobilization_and_reintegration) procedures.

  [^cite_note-IntlStudQuart_ClaytonSticher-11]

Clayton and Sticher found, based on ceasefire data for civil wars from 1990 to 2019, that the periods without violence following a ceasefire were successively longer for cessations of hostilities, preliminary ceasefires, and definitive ceasefires. Within three months of the ceasefire initiation, 30 percent of cessations of hostility, 50 percent of preliminary ceasefires, and 70 percent of definitive ceasefires still had no conflict-related deaths.[^cite_note-IntlStudQuart_ClaytonSticher-11]

## Historical examples

Historically, the concept of a ceasefire existed at least by the time of the Middle Ages, when it was known as a ['truce of God'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Truce_of_God).[^cite_note-12]

### World War I

Main article: [Christmas truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_truce)

During [World War I](4764461.md), on December 24, 1914, there was an [unofficial ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_truce) on the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)) as [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland), and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) observed [Christmas](6237.md). There are accounts that claimed the unofficial ceasefire took place throughout the week leading to Christmas, and that British and German troops exchanged seasonal greetings and songs between their trenches.[^cite_note-13] The ceasefire was brief but spontaneous. Beginning when German soldiers lit [Christmas trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree), it quickly spread up and down the Western Front.[^cite_note-14] One account described the development in the following words:

> It was good to see the human spirit prevailed amongst all sides at the front, the sharing and fraternity. All was well until the higher echelons of command got to hear about the effect of the ceasefire, whereby their wrath ensured a return to hostilities.[^cite_note-15]

There was no peace treaty signed during the [Christmas truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_truce), and the war resumed after a few days.

### Karachi Agreement

Main article: [Karachi Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Agreement)

The **Karachi Agreement** of 1949 was signed by the military representatives of India and Pakistan, supervised by the [United Nations Commission for India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_for_India_and_Pakistan), establishing a cease-fire line in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) following the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947).[^cite_note-16]

### Korean War

On November 29, 1952, the US [president-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States), [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower), went to Korea to see how to end the [Korean War](16772.md). With the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN)'s acceptance of [India](14533.md)'s proposed armistice, the ceasefire between the [UN Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Command) on the one side and the [Korean People's Army](21263.md) (KPA) and the [People's Volunteer Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Volunteer_Army) (PVA) on the other took hold at approximately the [38th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_parallel_north). These parties signed the [Korean Armistice Agreement](30076555.md) on July 27, 1953,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-findlaw-19530727-18] but South Korean President [Syngman Rhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngman_Rhee), who attacked the ceasefire proceedings, did not.[^cite_note-19] Upon agreeing to the ceasefire which called upon the governments of South Korea, the [United States](3434750.md), North Korea and [China](5405.md) to participate in continued peace talks, the principal [belligerents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belligerents) of the war established the [Korean Demilitarized Zone](328874.md) (DMZ) and it has since been patrolled by the joint [Republic of Korea Army](1515574.md), US, and UN Command on the one side and the KPA on the other. The war is considered to have ended at that point even though there still is no peace treaty.

### Vietnam War

On New Years Day, 1968, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) convinced [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam) and the United States to declare a 24-hour-truce. However, the [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong) and [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam) did not adhere to the truce, and ambushed the 2nd Battalion, [Republic of Vietnam Marine Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vietnam_Marine_Division), 10 minutes after midnight in [Mỹ Tho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BB%B9_Tho_\(provincial_city\)). The Viet Cong would also attack a [U.S. Army](32087.md) fire support base near [Saigon](66956.md), causing more casualties.[^cite_note-20]

On January 15, 1973, US President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) ordered a ceasefire of the aerial bombings in [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam). The decision came after [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger), the [National Security Advisor](44028.md) to the President, returned to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), from [Paris, France](22989.md), with a draft peace proposal. Combat missions continued in [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam). By January 27, 1973, all parties of the [Vietnam War](32611.md) signed a ceasefire as a prelude to the [Paris Peace Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Accord).

### Persian Gulf War

After [Iraq](7515928.md) was driven out of [Kuwait](7515890.md) by US-led coalition forces during [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm), Iraq and the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) signed a ceasefire agreement on March 3, 1991.[^cite_note-21] Subsequently, throughout the 1990s, the [U.N. Security Council](31956.md) passed numerous resolutions calling for Iraq to disarm its [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction) unconditionally and immediately. Because no peace treaty was signed after the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), the war still remained in effect, including an alleged assassination attempt of former US President [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) by Iraqi agents while on a visit to Kuwait; [Iraq being bombed in June 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile_strikes_on_Iraq_\(June_1993\)) as a response, Iraqi forces firing on coalition aircraft patrolling the [Iraqi no-fly zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_no-fly_zones), US President [Bill Clinton](3356.md)'s bombing of Baghdad in 1998 during [Operation Desert Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Fox), and an earlier 1996 bombing of Iraq by the US during [Operation Desert Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile_strikes_on_Iraq_\(1996\)). The war remained in effect until 2003, when US and UK forces [invaded Iraq](201936.md) and toppled [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein)'s regime from power.

### Kashmir conflict

Main articles: [Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947%E2%80%931948) and [UN mediation of the Kashmir dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_mediation_of_the_Kashmir_dispute)

A UN-mediated ceasefire was agreed between [India](14533.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md), on 1 January 1949, ending the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947) (also called the 1947 Kashmir War). Fighting broke out between the two newly independent countries in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) in October 1947, with India intervening on behalf of the princely ruler of Kashmir, who had joined India, and Pakistan supporting the rebels. The fighting was limited to Kashmir, but, apprehensive that it might develop into a full-scale international war, India referred the matter to the UN Security Council under Article 35 of the UN Charter, which addresses situations "likely to endanger the maintenance of international peace". The Security Council set up the dedicated [United Nations Commission for India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_for_India_and_Pakistan), which mediated for an entire year as the fighting continued. After several UN resolutions outlining a procedure for resolving the dispute via a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite), a ceasefire agreement was reached between the countries towards the end of December 1948, which came into effect in the New Year. The Security Council set up the [United Nations Military Observer Group for India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Military_Observer_Group_for_India_and_Pakistan) (UNMOGIP) to monitor the ceasefire line.[^cite_note-22] India declared a ceasefire in Kashmir Valley during Ramadan in 2018.

### Northern Ireland

The [Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army) held several Christmas ceasefires (usually referred to as truces) during the Northern Ireland conflict.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Israeli–Palestinian conflict

Main article: [2023 Gaza war ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Gaza_war_ceasefire)



Christmas Eve Ceasefire Vigil at the [White House](33057.md) in Washington, D.C. on 24 December 2023

An example of a ceasefire in the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md) was announced between [Israel](9282173.md) and the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) on February 8, 2005. When announced, chief [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) [negotiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation) [Saeb Erekat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeb_Erekat) publicly defined the ceasefire as follows: "We have agreed that today President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) will declare a full cessation of violence against Israelis anywhere and Prime Minister [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) will declare a full cessation of violence and military activities against Palestinians anywhere."[^cite_note-25] On November 21, 2023, Qatar announced that they had negotiated a truce between Israel and Hamas would pause [Gaza war](74998519.md), allow for the release of some hostages and bring more aid to Palestinian civilians. As part of the deal, 50 hostages held by Hamas were released while Israel released 150 Palestinian prisoners.[^cite_note-26]

### Russo-Ukrainian War

Main article: [Peace negotiations in the Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_negotiations_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)

Several attempts have been made to broker ceasefires during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In May 2023, [Donald Trump](4848272.md) told the UK's GB news that as US president he would end the war within 24 hours, given that he had good relationships with the leaders of Ukraine and Russia. He added that it would be easy to conclude a ceasefire agreement to end the war. [^cite_note-33]

[2026 Russo-Ukrainian truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2026_Russo-Ukrainian_truce) - On 11 April 2026 at 13\:00 UTC a 32-hour truce began for Orthodox Easter between the Russian Federation and the Republic of Ukraine.

### 2020 global ceasefire

Main article: [Global ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_ceasefire)

The 2020 global ceasefire was a response to a formal appeal by United Nations Secretary-General [António Manuel de Oliveira Guterres](471806.md) on March 23 for a global ceasefire as part of the United Nations' response to the COVID-19 [coronavirus pandemic](62750956.md). On 24 June 2020, 170 UN Member States and Observers signed a non-binding statement in support of the appeal, rising to 172 on 25 June 2020, and on 1 July 2020, the UN Security Council passed a resolution demanding a general and immediate global cessation of hostilities for at least 90 days.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### 2024 Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire

Main article: [Israel–Hezbollah conflict (2023–present)](75005425.md)

The 2024 Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire was announced by United States President [Joe Biden](145422.md) on November 26, 2024.[^cite_note-36]
