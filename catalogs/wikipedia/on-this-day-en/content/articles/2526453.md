The **High Court** of [Fiji](10707.md) is one of three courts that was established by [Chapter 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Constitution_of_Fiji:_Chapter_9) of the [1997 Constitution of Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Constitution_of_Fiji) — the others being the [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_of_Fiji) and the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Fiji). The Constitution empowered [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Fiji) to create other courts; these were to be subordinate to the High Court, which was authorized to oversee all proceedings of such courts. The High Court had unlimited [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) to hear and determine any civil or criminal proceedings under any law and such other original jurisdiction as is conferred on it under the Constitution.

The High Court consists of the [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Fiji) and at least ten (and no more than eighteen[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]) [puisne judges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puisne_judge_of_Fiji). Parliament may also allow for junior judges, called [Masters of the High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_the_High_Court_of_Fiji), to sit on the High Court. Section 129 of the Constitution declares that *"A judge who has sat in a trial of a matter that is the subject of appeal to a higher court must not sit in the appeal."* As the membership of the High Court overlaps to a large extent with that of the Appeal Court and the Supreme Court, this clause is inserted to prevent a conflict of interest.

In 2007, following the military coup, six [Australian](4689264.md) and [New Zealand](4913064.md) judges resigned from the court. They were replaced by two [Malaysian](3607937.md) judges, "with more appointments from [Asia](689.md) expected to follow soon".[^cite_note-RNZI_34901-3]

Until July 2007, under the *High Court Act*, foreign judges appointed to the High Court had to be nationals of one of the following countries: [Australia](4689264.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Papua New Guinea](22943.md), [Samoa](27238.md), [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Tonga](30158.md), or the [United Kingdom](31717.md). In practice, appointees were almost exclusively nationals of Australia and New Zealand. In July 2007, [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Fiji) [Ratu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratu) [Josefa Iloilovatu Uluivuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josefa_Iloilo) issued the *High Court Act (Amendment) Promulgation*, which extended the list to "any country which is at the time of the appointment a member of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations)". The Promulgation particularly highlighted [Singapore](27318.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [India](14533.md) as potential sources for the appointment of new judges. Hong Kong was specifically cited as an authorised source for judges despite not being a member of the Commonwealth. The Promulgation was approved by the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Fiji) led by interim [Prime Minister](471842.md) [Voreqe Bainimarama](525868.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]
