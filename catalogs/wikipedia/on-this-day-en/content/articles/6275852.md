**Ochopee** is an [unincorporated community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) in [Collier County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier_County,_Florida), [Florida](18933066.md), United States.[^cite_note-GR1-2] It is located to the east of the intersection of [US 41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_41_in_Florida) and [State Road 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_29), near [Carnestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnestown,_Florida). The community is part of the [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples,_Florida)–[Marco Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Island,_Florida) [Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples-Marco_Island,_Florida_Metropolitan_Statistical_Area).

The community got its name when a visitor to the general store asked the owner what the place was called. A native man was trading in the store that day so the owner asked him the Seminole word for farm. The farmer replied, "O-Chopp-ee".[^cite_note-3]

Ochopee began as a one-family tomato-farming community in the early 1920s. James Gaunt bought 240 acres (97 ha) of land along U.S. Highway 41 for \$100 per acre, and started with only army tents.[^cite_note-4] A community called Ochopee grew up around Gaunt's tomato farm.

After the original post office burned down, residents used an old storage shed to house mail. Now the [Ochopee Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochopee_Post_Office), it is the smallest in the nation.[^cite_note-5] It continues as an active post office and sometime tourist attraction.[^cite_note-Gray-6]

The original farm and settlement were gradually absorbed by the federal government as part of a movement to conserve the [Everglades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everglades). A few small businesses remain, along with the headquarters of the [Big Cypress National Preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Cypress_National_Preserve). Jeff Whichello, a native of the area, has written a book about his childhood called *What Happened to Ochopee?*[^cite_note-Gray-6]

## Detention center

In July 2025, [South Florida Detention Facility](80275802.md), a detention, processing, and deportation facility for undocumented migrants was installed at the [little-used airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dade-Collier_Training_and_Transition_Airport) in Ochopee.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] In June 2026, however, [Florida Governor](233840.md) [Ron DeSantis](36729527.md) confirmed that the detention center would close operations.[^cite_note-9]
