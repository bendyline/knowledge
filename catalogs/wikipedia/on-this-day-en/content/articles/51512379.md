**Tigst Assefa Tessema** ([Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic_language): ትእግስት አሰፋ; [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_language): Tigist Asaffaa Tasammaa; born 3 December 1996)[^cite_note-WAprofile-4] is an Ethiopian [long-distance runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_running) and the current [world record holder](310762.md#Women) in the women's [marathon](49132.md) (women only race). She has won four [World Marathon Majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Marathon_Majors), twice in [Berlin](1663911.md) and twice in London. A former [800 metres](585165.md) specialist, Tigst switched to [road races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_running) in 2018 and ran her first marathon in 2022.[^cite_note-RW_20220928-5]

At the age of 16, she won the bronze medal in the 800 metres at the [2013 African Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_African_Junior_Athletics_Championships). She finished fourth at the senior [African Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_African_Championships_in_Athletics) the following year, and then represented Ethiopia in the event at the [2016 Rio Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics) aged 19. Tigst ran the then third-fastest female marathon in history at the [2022 Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Berlin_Marathon)[^cite_note-RW_20220928-5] and defeated the world record by 2 minutes and 11 seconds at the [2023 Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Berlin_Marathon), on 24 September, with a time of 2 hours 11 minutes and 53 seconds,[^cite_note-Guardian_20230924-6][^cite_note-BBC_20230929-7] becoming the first woman to break the 2\:14, 2\:13, and 2\:12 barriers in a marathon. Tigst subsequently set the world record for a women's-only marathon at the [2025 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_London_Marathon), a record she went on to break herself [one year later](83030339.md).&#x20;

## Career



Tigst (in yellow shirt) at a meet in [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims), France in 2013

At the age of 12, Tigst won the bronze medal in the [400 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/400_metres) at the 2009 senior [Ethiopian Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Athletics_Championships), stopping the clock at 56.70 seconds.[^cite_note-WAprofile-4] She gained her first international experience in January 2012, in France, where she competed for the first time in the [800 metres](585165.md), although failing to finish. The 15-year-old represented Ethiopia in the 400 m at the senior [African Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_African_Championships_in_Athletics) in [Porto-Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto-Novo), Benin, setting a new personal best in the heats with a time of [54.05 s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_African_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Women's_400_metres).[^cite_note-WAprofile-4]

In 2013, aged 16, Tigst became Ethiopian [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under-20_athletics) champion, clocking 2\:01.25 in [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A0_dei_Castelli), Switzerland, and earned bronze at the [African Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_African_Junior_Athletics_Championships) held in [Réduit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moka), Mauritius, where she also added silver for the 4 × 400 m relay. The following year, she ran 1\:59.24 at the [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletissima) [Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Diamond_League), finished fourth at the [African Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_African_Championships_in_Athletics) in [Marrakesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh), won the [ISTAF Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISTAF_Berlin) meet, and placed fourth at the [IAAF Continental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_IAAF_Continental_Cup) in Marrakesh.[^cite_note-WAprofile-4]

In 2016, Tigst represented Ethiopia in the 800 metres at the [World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_IAAF_World_Indoor_Championships) held in [Portland](23503.md), United States and at the [Rio Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics) in Brazil. She failed to advance past preliminary rounds both in the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_IAAF_World_Indoor_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) and [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) event.[^cite_note-WAprofile-4]

After 2016, Tigst moved to road racing as an ongoing [Achilles tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon) injury prevented her from training in [spikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_spikes). After a two-year break, in November 2018, she raced the [10 km](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10K_run) in [Dubai](211583.md) for a time of 34\:35.[^cite_note-RW_20220928-5] Tigst improved by almost three minutes (31\:45) in Germany the following year, when she debuted in the [half marathon](1019031.md) at the [Valencia Half Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Half_Marathon) in Spain, finishing fifth in 68\:24.[^cite_note-WAprofile-4]

### 2022: #3 female marathon all time

In March, after a two-year hiatus due to a career-threatening injury sustained in 2019 in Valencia and also the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Tigst debuted in the [marathon distance](49132.md) at the inaugural [Riyadh Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Marathon), finishing seventh with a time of 2\:34\:01.[^cite_note-RW_20220928-5][^cite_note-BBC_20230929-7] She competed in five additional races from March through June, competing in three 10 km events and two half marathons, winning all of them and setting new personal bests (30\:52 in [Langueux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langueux), France and 67\:28 at the adizero Road to Records event in [Herzogenaurach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzogenaurach), Germany, respectively.[^cite_note-WAprofile-4]

Tigst set the third-fastest female performance in history at the [Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Berlin_Marathon) in September. She stopped the clock at two hours 15 minutes 37 seconds, her winning time slower only than 2\:14\:04 of world record-holder [Brigid Kosgei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigid_Kosgei) and 2\:15\:25 of former record-holder [Paula Radcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Radcliffe), an over 18 minutes improvement of her personal best. Running with a [negative split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_split), after the 68\:13 first half of the race, her second half of 67\:24 was faster by 4 s than her then half marathon lifetime best.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] In December, she won the [Bahrain](18933277.md) Royal Night Half Marathon in [Manama](20481.md).[^cite_note-WAprofile-4]

### 2023: World marathon record – first woman under 2\:14, 2\:13 and 2\:12

On 24 September, wearing [super shoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_shoes), Tigst competed at the [2023 Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Berlin_Marathon) and set a new female world record with a time of 2\:11\:53, chopping two minutes and eleven seconds from [Brigid Kosgei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigid_Kosgei)'s record of 2\:14\:04 from the [2019 Chicago Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Chicago_Marathon). Tigst became the first woman to break the 2\:14, 2\:13 and 2\:12 barriers in a marathon. She completed the first half of the race almost two minutes faster than the previous year with a time of 66\:20 and the second half in 65\:33. Tigst beat the runner-up by almost six minutes, with the assistance of [pacemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker_\(running\)) Girmay Birhanu Gebru for almost the entire race.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-BBC_20230929-7][^cite_note-Guardian_20230924-6][^cite_note-14]

This was the first time that an Ethiopian woman broke the marathon world record. Tigst's time was faster than the old men's world best mark of her compatriot [Abebe Bikila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abebe_Bikila), who in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1960_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_marathon). Her time would have been the men's world record until December 1967.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] She received an [World Athlete of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Awards) award in the Out of Stadium category.[^cite_note-17]

Tigst's record was later initially broken by [Ruth Chepng'etich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Chepng'etich), who ran 2\:09\:56 at the [2024 Chicago Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Chicago_Marathon); however, while Chepng'etich was banned for doping in 2025, her record remains. [^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### 2024: Olympic games 2024 – silver medal on the marathon

On August 11, 2024, Tigst finished second in the Olympic marathon with a time of 2\:22\:58, three seconds behind winner [Sifan Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sifan_Hassan). In the final sprint, Tigst drifted towards the barriers, resulting in a collision with Hassan and causing both athletes to stumble. Hassan then passed Tigst and won the race by the narrowest margin in the history of [women's olympic marathons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_at_the_Summer_Olympics). Afterwards, the Ethiopian team filed a protest to disqualify Hassan due to obstruction, which was rejected by the Jury of Appeal. At the post-race news conference, Tigst stated through a translator "I didn't expect at that moment it would happen. Maybe at that moment, if she didn't push me I would have the gold."[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### 2025: London Marathon – gold medal and breaking women's-only world record

On 27 April 2025, Tigst won the 45th [London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Marathon) and set a world record for a women-only marathon.[^cite_note-22]

### 2026: Berlin Marathon

On 27 September 2026 Assefa finished the [2026 Berlin Marathon](84342841.md) in 2\:11\:04. Despite suffering an [Achilles tendon rupture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon_rupture) on the last kilometers and limping across the finish line, she won the race.[^cite_note-23]
