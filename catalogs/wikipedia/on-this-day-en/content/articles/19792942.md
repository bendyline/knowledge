**Americans** are the [citizens](25151566.md) and [nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law) of the [United States of America](3434750.md).[^cite_note-nationals_and_citizens-18][^cite_note-American_Somoans-19] [U.S. federal law](27552742.md) does not equate [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality) with [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\)) or [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity), but rather with citizenship.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Citizen-22][^cite_note-23] The U.S. has 37 [ancestry groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ancestries) with more than one million individuals.[^cite_note-An2000-24] [White Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans) form the largest [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_classification\)) and [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) at 61.6% of the U.S. population, with [non-Hispanic whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites) making up 57.8% of the population.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] [Hispanic and Latino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) form the second-largest group and are 18.7% of the American population. [Black Americans](2154.md) constitute the country's third-largest ancestry group and are 12.4% of the total U.S. population.[^cite_note-An2000-24] [Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans) are the country's fourth-largest group, composing 6% of the American population. The country's 3.7 million [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) account for about 1.1%,[^cite_note-An2000-24] and some 574 native tribes are recognized by the federal government.[^cite_note-27] People of American descent can be found internationally. As many as seven million Americans are estimated to be living abroad, and make up the [American diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_diaspora).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The majority of Americans trace their roots to [immigrants](15051.md) who arrived in what is now the United States, starting with [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) in the 16th century. This includes [European American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) groups such as the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans), [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans), [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans), and others, as well as [Africans](2154.md) forcibly brought as [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) during the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md). However, the [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) population, whose ancestors inhabited the continent for thousands of years before European contact, are a key exception.

Despite its multi-ethnic composition,[^cite_note-DD-31][^cite_note-Society_in_Focus-32] the [culture of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States) held in common by most Americans can also be referred to as mainstream American culture—a [Western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) largely derived from the traditions of [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe) and [Western European](33800.md) colonists, settlers, and immigrants.[^cite_note-DD-31] It also includes significant influences of [African-American culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_culture).[^cite_note-33] [Westward expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifest_destiny) integrated the French-speaking [Creoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Creole_people) and [Cajuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajuns) of Louisiana and the [Hispanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanos_of_New_Mexico) of the American Southwest, who brought close contact with the [culture of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Mexico). Large-scale immigration in the late 19th and early 20th centuries from [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) and [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) introduced a variety of new customs. Immigration from [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_immigration_to_the_United_States), [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_immigration_to_the_United_States), and [Latin America](18524.md) has also had impact. A cultural [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot), or pluralistic [salad bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salad_bowl_\(cultural_idea\)), describes the way in which generations of Americans have celebrated and exchanged distinctive cultural characteristics.[^cite_note-DD-31]

## History

The original [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) population came to what is now the United States from [Siberia](29639.md) via the [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait).[^cite_note-34] For thousands of years prior to [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) tribes had been established in what is now the United States. Estimates for their [pre-contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era) population, range from 3 million to 12 million, living in over 600 distinct societies. Indigenous languages and cultures differed by region. European colonization of what is now the United States began in the 16th century after the [Spanish conquest of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Aztec_Empire), with the failed Spanish [Narváez expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narv%C3%A1ez_expedition) in the southwestern territories in 1527. The next to arrive in North America were the French, followed by the Dutch and finally the British who each settled in different parts of North America. Early interactions between European settlers and Indigenous peoples were often amicable; however by the 1630s, as the number of settlers grew as well as increased self-sufficiency, colonists encroached further upon Indigenous lands and imposed Christianity and their culture upon Native peoples. The impact of the European colonization was catastrophic on the Native population, as they introduced diseases, such as [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis), and [influenza](19572217.md), that decimated many Native American populations.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

The United States is a [diverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism) country, both [racially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\)) and [ethnically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity).[^cite_note-38] [Six races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States_Census) are officially recognized by the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) for statistical purposes: Alaska Native and American Indian, Asian, Black or African American, Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander, White, and people of two or more races. ### White and European Americans

Main articles: [European Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) and [White Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans)

See also: [Old Stock Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Stock_Americans), [White Southerners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Southerners), and [Poor White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_White)



Most common European ancestry in the United States by county (self-reported) in 2020

White Americans constitute the majority of the 331 million people living in the United States, with 204,277,273 people or 61.6% of the population in the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-c2010-44][^cite_note-TWP2010-45] The US census defines "white" as "\[a] person having origins in any of the original peoples of Europe, the Middle East, or North Africa".[^cite_note-46] [Non-Hispanic whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites), which only account for 57.8% of the population, or 191,697,647 people, are the majority in 44 states. There are six [minority-majority states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority-majority_state): [California](5407.md), [Texas](29810.md), [Maryland](18858.md),[^cite_note-47] [New Mexico](21649.md), [Nevada](21216.md), and [Hawaii](13270.md).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] In addition, the [District of Columbia](108956.md) and the five inhabited [U.S. territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) have a non-white majority.[^cite_note-c2010-44] The state with the highest percentage of non-Hispanic white Americans is [Maine](19977.md), while the state with the lowest percentage is Hawaii.[^cite_note-50]

Europe is the largest continent that Americans trace their ancestry to, and many claim descent from various [European ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe).[^cite_note-51]

The [Spaniards](963152.md) were among the first Europeans to establish a continuous presence in what is now the continental United States in 1565 in [San Agustín, La Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) then a part of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain).[^cite_note-loc-52][^cite_note-53] [Virginia Dare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Dare) (b. 1587) in [Roanoke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Island) in present-day [North Carolina](21650.md), was the first child born in the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) to English parents. The Spaniards also established a continuous presence in what over three centuries later would become a possession of the United States with the founding of the city of [San Juan, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico), in 1521.

[Jewish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Americans) trace their ancestry primarily to [Central](5188.md) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), with smaller numbers from the [Middle East](19323.md) and [North Africa](21714.md). Large numbers of Jewish immigrants arrived in the United States during the late 19th and early 20th centuries, fleeing persecution in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and other parts of Eastern Europe. They arrived in cities such as [New York City](645042.md), [Chicago](6886.md), and [Philadelphia](50585.md).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

In the 2020 United States census, [English Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans) 46.5 million (19.8%), [German Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) 45m (19.1%), [Irish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) 38.6m (16.4%), and [Italian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans) 16.8m (7.1%) were the four largest self-reported European ancestry groups in the United States constituting 62.4% of the white American population.[^cite_note-auto2-56] However, the English Americans and [British Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Americans) demography is considered a serious under-count as they tend to self-report and identify as simply "[Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ancestry)" (since the introduction of a new "American" category in the [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_United_States_census) census) due to the length of time they have inhabited America. This is highly over-represented in the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South), a region that was settled historically by the British.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

Overall, as the largest group, [European Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) have the lowest [poverty rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_United_States)[^cite_note-Poverty_rate-63] and the second highest [educational attainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_attainment_in_the_United_States#Ethnicity_and_race) levels, median [household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income_in_the_United_States),[^cite_note-Median_household_income_newsbrief,_US_Census_Bureau_2005-64] and median [personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_income_in_the_United_States)[^cite_note-US_Census_Bureau,_Personal_income_for_Asian_Americans,_age_25+,_2006-65] of any racial demographic in the nation, second only to [Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans) in the latter three categories.

### Hispanic and Latino Americans

Main article: [Hispanic and Latino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans)

Hispanic and Latino Americans constitute the largest [ethnic minority in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_and_ethnic_demographics_of_the_United_States). They form the second-largest group in the United States, comprising 62,080,044 people or 18.7% of the population according to the 2020 United States census.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-tthqvu-67][^cite_note-c2010def-68]

Hispanic and Latino Americans are not considered a race in the United States census, instead forming an ethnic category.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

People of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Americans) or [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic) and [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Americans) descent have lived in what is now United States territory since the founding of [San Juan, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico) (the oldest continuously inhabited settlement on American soil) in 1521 by [Juan Ponce de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n), and the founding of [St. Augustine, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) (the oldest continuously inhabited settlement in the continental United States) in 1565 by [Pedro Menéndez de Avilés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Men%C3%A9ndez_de_Avil%C3%A9s). In the [State of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Texas), Spaniards first [settled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Texas#Establishment_of_Spanish_colony) the region in the late 1600s and formed a unique [cultural group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_identity) known as [Tejanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejanos).

### Black and African Americans

Main article: [African Americans](2154.md)

See also: [Black Southerners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Southerners), [West Indian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_Americans), and [Slavery in the colonial history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_colonial_history_of_the_United_States)

Black and African Americans are citizens and residents of the United States with origins in [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md).[^cite_note-IOM-76] According to the [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget), the grouping includes individuals who self-identify as African American, as well as persons who emigrated from nations in the [Caribbean](18956035.md) and sub-Saharan Africa.[^cite_note-2010USCBAA-77] The grouping is thus based on geography, and may contradict or misrepresent an individual's self-identification since not all immigrants from sub-Saharan Africa are "Black". Among these racial outliers are persons from [Cape Verde](18962637.md), [Madagascar](18964.md), various Arab states, and [Hamito-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) populations in [East Africa](260987.md) and the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel), and the [Afrikaners](18947965.md) of [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa).[^cite_note-IOM-76] [African Americans](2154.md) (also referred to as Black Americans or Afro-Americans, and formerly as American [Negroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negro)) are citizens or residents of the United States who have origins in any of the [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) populations of Africa.[^cite_note-censusblack-78] According to the 2020 United States census, there were 39,940,338 black and African Americans in the United States, representing 12.4% of the population.[^cite_note-factfinder.census.gov-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-NRC201029SEP11-81] Black and African Americans make up the third-largest group in the United States, after white and European Americans, and Hispanic and Latino Americans.[^cite_note-tthqvu-67] The majority of the population (55%) lives in the [South](179553.md); compared to the 2000 United States census, there has also been a decrease of African Americans in the [Northeast](431669.md) and [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_United_States).[^cite_note-NRC201029SEP11-81]

Most African Americans are the direct descendants of captives from [Central](539606.md) and [West Africa](67393.md), from ancestral populations in countries like [Nigeria](21383.md), [Benin](3459.md), [Sierra Leone](27308.md), [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md), [Senegal](27278.md), and [Angola](701.md),[^cite_note-Bryc_2015-82] who survived the [slavery era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) within the boundaries of the present United States.[^cite_note-83] As an adjective, the term is usually spelled *[African-American](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/African-American)*.[^cite_note-84] Montinaro et al. (2014) observed that around 50% of the overall ancestry of African Americans traces back to the [Niger-Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Congo_languages)-speaking [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) of southwestern Nigeria and southern Benin (before the European colonization of Africa this people created the [Oyo Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire)), reflecting the centrality of this West African region in the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md).[^cite_note-Montinaro2014-85] Zakharaia et al. (2009) found a similar proportion of Yoruba associated ancestry in their African American samples, with a minority also drawn from [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people) populations (founders of the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire)), and [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples) populations (who had a varying level of social organization during the colonial era, while some Bantu peoples were still tribal, other Bantu peoples had founded kingdoms such as the [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo)).[^cite_note-Zakharia2009-86]

The first West African [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) were brought to [Jamestown, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown,_Virginia) in 1619. The English settlers treated these captives as [indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servitude) and released them after a number of years. This practice was gradually replaced by the system of race-based slavery used in the [Caribbean](18956035.md).[^cite_note-87] All the American colonies had slavery, but it was usually the form of personal servants in the North (where 2% of the people were slaves), and field hands in plantations in the South (where 25% were slaves);[^cite_note-88] by the beginning of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) 1/5th of the total population was enslaved.[^cite_note-89] During the revolution, some would serve in the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) or [Continental Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Navy),[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] while [others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Loyalist) would serve the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) in the [Ethiopian Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Regiment), and other units.[^cite_note-92] By 1804, the [northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_United_States) states (north of the [Mason–Dixon line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason%E2%80%93Dixon_line)) had [abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) slavery.[^cite_note-93] However, slavery would persist in the [southern states](179553.md) until the end of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) and the passage of the [Thirteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution).[^cite_note-94] Following the end of the [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era), which saw the first [African American representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African-American_officeholders_during_the_Reconstruction) in [Congress](31756.md),[^cite_note-95] African Americans became [disenfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage) and subject to [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws),[^cite_note-96] legislation that would persist until the passage of the [Civil Rights Act of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964) and [Voting Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act) due to the [civil rights movement](49001.md).[^cite_note-97]

According to United States Census Bureau data, very few [African immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_immigration_to_the_United_States) self-identify as African American. On average, less than 5% of African residents self-reported as "African American" or "Afro-American" on the 2000 U.S. census. The overwhelming majority of African immigrants (\~95%) identified instead with their own respective ethnicities. Self-designation as "African American" or "Afro-American" was highest among individuals from West Africa (4%–9%), and lowest among individuals from Cape Verde, East Africa and Southern Africa (0%–4%).[^cite_note-98] African immigrants may also experience conflict with African Americans.[^cite_note-99]

### Asian Americans

Main articles: [Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), [Central Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_Americans), [East Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Americans), [South Asian Americans](11353572.md), and [Southeast Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Americans)

See also: [South Asians in Colonial America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asians_in_Colonial_America)

Another significant population is the Asian American population, comprising 19,618,719 people in 2020, or 5.9% of the United States population.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-2010_Census_AA-102][^cite_note-b02001-103] California is home to 5.6 million Asian Americans, the greatest number in any state.[^cite_note-FFFCENSUS2011-104] In Hawaii, Asian Americans make up the highest proportion of the population (57 percent).[^cite_note-FFFCENSUS2011-104] Asian Americans live across the country, yet are heavily urbanized, with significant populations in the [Greater Los Angeles Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Area), [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), and the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area).[^cite_note-105]

The United States census defines Asian Americans as those with origins to the countries of [Central Asia](6742.md), [East Asia](19605700.md), [South Asia](21566765.md), and [Southeast Asia](28741.md). Although Americans with roots in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) were once classified as "Asian", they are now excluded from the term in modern census classifications.[^cite_note-106] The largest sub-groups are immigrants or descendants of immigrants from Cambodia, mainland China, India, Japan, Korea, Laos, Pakistan, the Philippines, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam. Asians overall have [higher income levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_in_the_United_States) than all other racial groups in the United States, including whites, and the trend appears to be increasing in relation to those groups.[^cite_note-107] Additionally, Asians have a [higher education attainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_attainment_in_the_United_States) level than all other racial groups in the United States.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] For better or for worse, the group has been called a [model minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_minority).[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

While Asian Americans have been in what is now the United States since before the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War),[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-AAWA2009-115] relatively large waves of Chinese, Filipino, and Japanese immigration did not begin until the mid-to-late 19th century.[^cite_note-AAWA2009-115] Immigration and significant population growth continue to this day.[^cite_note-116] Due to a number of factors, Asian Americans have been [stereotyped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_stereotype) as "[perpetual foreigners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_foreigner)".[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

### Middle Eastern and North African Americans

Main articles: [Middle Eastern Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_Americans) and [North Africans in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africans_in_the_United_States)

Middle Eastern Americans and North African Americans are Americans with ancestry from the Middle East and North Africa (MENA). According to the [American Jewish Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jacob_Rader_Marcus_Center_of_the_American_Jewish_Archives) and the [Arab American National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_American_National_Museum), the first [Middle Easterners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_Americans) and [North Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africans_in_the_United_States) (especially [Jews](25955086.md)) to arrive in the Americas landed in the late 15th to mid-16th centuries.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-arabamericanmuseum.org-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] Many fled ethnic or [ethnoreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnoreligious_group) persecution during the [Spanish Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Inquisition).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-arabamericanmuseum.org-120]

In 2014, the United States Census Bureau began finalizing the ethnic classification of people of Middle Eastern and North African ("MENA") origins.[^cite_note-usatoday.com-125] According to the [Arab American Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_American_Institute) (AAI), [Arab Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Americans) have family origins in each of the 22 [Arab League member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Arab_League).[^cite_note-126] Following consultations with MENA organizations, the Census Bureau announced in 2014 that it would establish a new MENA ethnic category for populations from the [Middle East](19323.md), [North Africa](21714.md), and the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), separate from the "white" classification that these populations had previously sought in 1909. The groups felt that the earlier "white" designation no longer accurately represents MENA identity, so they successfully lobbied for a distinct categorization.[^cite_note-127] This new category would also include [Israeli Americans](19995589.md).[^cite_note-128] The Census Bureau does not currently ask about whether one is [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs), because it views them as followers of a religion rather than members of an ethnic group, and it does not combine questions concerning religion with race or ethnicity.[^cite_note-MENA1-129] As of December 2015, the sampling strata for the new MENA category includes the Census Bureau's working classification of 19 MENA groups, as well as [Iranian](2234641.md), [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Americans), [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Americans), [Afghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Americans), [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Americans), and [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Americans) groups.[^cite_note-MENA2-130] In January 2018, it was announced that the Census Bureau would not include the grouping in the 2020 census.[^cite_note-131]

### Native Americans and Alaska Natives

Main articles: [Native Americans in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) and [Alaska Natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Natives)

See also: [Blood quantum laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_quantum_laws) and [Bureau of Indian Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Indian_Affairs)

According to the 2020 United States census, there are 2,251,699 people who are Native Americans or [Alaska Natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Natives) alone; they make up 0.7% of the total population.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-2010CensusAIAN-136] According to the [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget) (OMB), an "American Indian or Alaska Native" is a person whose ancestry have origins in any of the [original peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) of North, Central, or South America.[^cite_note-2010CensusAIAN-136] 2.3 million individuals who are American Indian or Alaskan Native are multiracial;[^cite_note-2010CensusAIAN-136] additionally the plurality of American Indians reside in the [Western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States) (40.7%).[^cite_note-2010CensusAIAN-136] Collectively and historically this race has been known by [several names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_name_controversy#United_States);[^cite_note-137] as of 1995, 50% of those who fall within the OMB definition prefer the term "American Indian", 37% prefer "Native American" and the remainder have no preference or prefer a different term altogether.[^cite_note-138]

Among Americans today, levels of Native American ancestry (distinct from [Native American identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_identity_in_the_United_States)) differ. Based on a sample of users of the [23andMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23andMe) commercial genetic test, genomes of self-reported African Americans averaged to 0.8% Native American ancestry, those of European Americans averaged to 0.18%, and those of Latinos averaged to 18.0%.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] Another genetic study focusing on Native American ancestry in the general population found an average of 38% in Latinos, 1% in African Americans, and 0.1% for European American populations, respectively.[^cite_note-141]

Native Americans, whose ancestry is indigenous to the [Americas](29833.md), [originally migrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_migration_and_settlement_of_the_Americas_from_Asia) to the two continents between 10,000 and 45,000 years ago.[^cite_note-142] These [Paleoamericans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoamericans) spread throughout the two continents and evolved into hundreds of distinct cultures during the [pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era).[^cite_note-143] Following the [first voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus) of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus),[^cite_note-144] the [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) began, with [St. Augustine, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) becoming the first permanent European settlement in the [continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_United_States).[^cite_note-145] From the 16th through the 19th centuries, the [population of Native Americans declined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) in the following ways: [epidemic diseases brought from Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_disease_and_epidemics);[^cite_note-146] [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_genocide_in_the_United_States) and [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_Wars) at the hands of European explorers, settlers and colonists,[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] as well as between tribes;[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] [displacement from their lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_removal);[^cite_note-151] internal warfare,[^cite_note-152] [enslavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_among_Native_Americans_in_the_United_States);[^cite_note-153] and [intermarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interracial_marriage).[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

### Native Hawaiians and other Pacific Islanders

Main articles: [Native Hawaiians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_Americans)

As defined by the United States Census Bureau and the Office of Management and Budget, [Native Hawaiians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and other [Pacific Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander) are "persons having origins in any of the original peoples of [Hawaii](13270.md), [Guam](11974.md), [Samoa](27238.md), or other [Pacific Islands](341111.md)".[^cite_note-2010CensusNHOPI-157] Previously called [Asian Pacific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Pacific_American), along with Asian Americans beginning in 1976, this was changed in 1997.[^cite_note-158] As of the 2020 United States census, there are 622,018 who reside in the United States, and make up 0.2% of the nation's total population.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-FFAPI2011-160] 14% of the population have at least a [bachelor's degree](188874.md),[^cite_note-FFAPI2011-160] and 15.1% live in [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_United_States), below the [poverty threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_thresholds_\(United_States_Census_Bureau\)).[^cite_note-FFAPI2011-160] As compared to the 2000 United States census, this population grew by 40%;[^cite_note-2010CensusNHOPI-157] and 71% live in the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States); of those over half (52%) live in either Hawaii or [California](5407.md), with no other states having populations greater than 100,000. The United States territories in the Pacific also have large Pacific Islander populations such as Guam and the [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md) (Chammoro), and [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa) (Samoan).[^cite_note-2010CensusNHOPI-157] The largest concentration of Native Hawaiians and other Pacific Islanders, is [Honolulu County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_County) in Hawaii,[^cite_note-FFAPI2011-160] and [Los Angeles County](79734.md) in the [continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_United_States).[^cite_note-2010CensusNHOPI-157]

### Two or more races

Main article: [Multiracial Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans)

The United States has a growing multiracial identity movement, and this group is one of the fastest growing demographics in the country.[^cite_note-161] [Multiracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial) Americans numbered 7.0 million in 2008, or 2.3% of the population;[^cite_note-b02001-103] by the 2020 census the multiracial increased to 13,548,983, or 4.1% of the total population.[^cite_note-kHnJrR2011-162] They can be any combination of races (white, black, Asian, native American or Alaska native, native Hawaiian or other Pacific Islander, "some other race") and ethnicities.[^cite_note-163] The largest population of multiracial Americans were those of [white and black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulatto) descent, with a total of 1,834,212 self-identifying individuals.[^cite_note-kHnJrR2011-162] [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama), the 44th President of the United States, is multiracial—his mother is white (of English and Irish descent) and his father is black (of [Kenyan](188171.md) descent)[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]—though he identifies only as African American.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

### Some other race

According to the 2020 United States census, 8.4% or 27,915,715 Americans chose to self-identify with the "some other race" category, the third most popular option. The vast majority of this group was Hispanic or Latino. "Some other race" formed the single largest racial group of Hispanics, with 42.2% of Hispanic/Latino Americans, or 26,225,882 people, choosing to identify as [some other race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States#Members_of_other_races), as these Hispanic/Latinos may feel the United States census does not describe their mixed European and American Indian ancestry as they understand it to be.[^cite_note-169] A significant portion of the Hispanic and Latino population self-identifies as [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), particularly the Mexican and Central American community.[^cite_note-170] Mestizo is not a racial category in the United States census, but signifies someone who has both European and American Indian ancestry.

## National personification





"[Uncle Sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Sam)" is a [national personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_personification) of the United States. The image bears a resemblance to the real [Samuel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wilson) and the pose used here is based on [Lord Kitchener Wants You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kitchener_Wants_You). The female personification, primarily popular during the 18th and 19th centuries, is "[Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_\(name\))".

[Uncle Sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Sam) is a [national personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_personification) of the United States and sometimes more specifically of the [American government](195149.md), with the first usage of the term dating from the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812). He is depicted as a stern elderly white man with white hair and a [goatee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goatee) beard, and dressed in clothing that recalls the design elements of the [flag of the United States](11447.md) – for example, typically a [top hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_hat) with red and white stripes and white stars on a blue band, and red and white striped trousers.

[Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_\(personification\)) is a poetic name for the Americas and the feminine personification of the United States of America, made famous by African American poet [Phillis Wheatley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillis_Wheatley) during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in 1776. It has inspired the names of many persons, places, objects, institutions, and companies in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) and beyond, including the [District of Columbia](108956.md), the seat of government of the United States.

[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) is the [national language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language) and [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of the United States at the federal level.[^cite_note-173] Additionally, some laws—such as [U.S. naturalization requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law#Naturalization)—standardize English. In 2020, about 245 million, or 78% of the population aged five years and older, spoke only English at home. [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_in_the_United_States), spoken by 13.4% of the population at home, is the second most common language and the most widely taught second language.[^cite_note-USCB_Lang-174][^cite_note-175] Prior to the signing of [Executive Order 14224](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14224) in March 2025, which declared English the official language of the U.S., some Americans [advocated making English the country's official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-only_movement), as it is in at least 30 out of the 50 states.[^cite_note-ILW-176] Both English and [Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_language) are official languages in Hawaii by state law.[^cite_note-177] Alaska has declared its 20 [Native American languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Native_languages) to be official, along with English.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] In South Dakota, both dialects of the [Sioux language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_language) have been declared official, along with English.[^cite_note-eu.argusleader.com-180][^cite_note-sdlegislature-181]

While neither has an official language, [New Mexico](21649.md) has laws providing for the use of both English and Spanish, as [Louisiana](18130.md) does for English and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language).[^cite_note-182] Other states, such as California, mandate the publication of Spanish versions of certain government documents. The latter include court forms.[^cite_note-183] Several insular territories grant official recognition to their native languages, along with English: [Samoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_language) and [Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamorro_language) are recognized by [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa) and [Guam](11974.md), respectively; [Carolinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinian_language) and Chamorro are recognized by the Northern Mariana Islands; Spanish is an official language of Puerto Rico.

Religion in the United States has a high adherence level compared to other developed countries and a diversity in beliefs. The [First Amendment](31653.md) to the country's [Constitution](31644.md) prevents the Federal government from making any "law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof". The [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) has interpreted this as preventing the government from having any authority in religion. A majority of Americans report that religion plays a "very important" role in their lives, a proportion unusual among [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country). However, similar to the other nations of the Americas.[^cite_note-185] Many faiths have flourished in the United States, including both later imports spanning the country's multicultural immigrant heritage, as well as those founded within the country; these have led the United States to become the most religiously diverse country in the world.[^cite_note-186]

The United States has the [world's largest Christian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_by_country).[^cite_note-Global_Christianity-187] The majority of Americans (76%) are [Christians](18337522.md), mostly within [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) and [Catholic](606848.md) denominations; these adherents constitute 48% and 23% of the population, respectively.[^cite_note-Gallup2016religion-188] Other religions include [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Islam](6037917.md), and [Judaism](15624.md), which collectively make up about 4% to 5% of the adult population.[^cite_note-ARIS2008-189][^cite_note-ciafact-190][^cite_note-Pew-191] Another 15% of the adult population identifies as having [no religious belief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) or no religious affiliation.[^cite_note-ARIS2008-189] According to the [American Religious Identification Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Religious_Identification_Survey), religious belief varies considerably across the country: 59% of Americans living in Western states (the "[Unchurched Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unchurched_Belt)") report a belief in [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God), yet in the South (the "[Bible Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Belt)") the figure is as high as 86%.[^cite_note-ARIS2008-189][^cite_note-192]

Several of the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) were established by settlers who wished to practice their religion without discrimination: the [Massachusetts Bay Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) was established by English [Puritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritans), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) by Irish and English [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Maryland) by English and [Irish Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Catholics), and [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia) by English [Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism). Although some individual states retained established religious confessions well into the 19th century, the United States was the first nation to have no official state-endorsed religion.[^cite_note-193] Modeling the provisions concerning religion within the [Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Statute_for_Religious_Freedom), the framers of the Constitution rejected any religious test for office. The [First Amendment](31653.md) specifically denied the federal government any power to enact any law respecting either an establishment of religion or prohibiting its free exercise, thus protecting any religious organization, institution, or denomination from government interference. European Rationalist and Protestant ideals mainly influenced the decision. Still, it was also a consequence of the pragmatic concerns of minority religious groups and small states that did not want to be under the power or influence of a national religion that did not represent them.[^cite_note-194]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States)



[Apple pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_pie) and [baseball](3850.md) are icons of American culture.

American culture is primarily a [Western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture), but is influenced by Native American, West African, Latin American, East Asian, and [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia) cultures.

The United States of America has its own unique social and cultural characteristics, such as [dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English), [music](171080.md), [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_art_of_the_United_States), [social habits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_United_States), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_the_United_States), and [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-Society_in_Focus-32]

Its chief early European influences came from [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Americans), [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Americans), and [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) settlers of [colonial America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_history_of_the_United_States) during British rule. [British culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom), due to colonial ties with Britain that spread the English language, [legal system](85441.md) and other cultural inheritances, had a formative influence.[^cite_note-195] Other important influences came from other parts of Europe, especially [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Germany),[^cite_note-kirschbaum-196] [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_France),[^cite_note-197] and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Italy).[^cite_note-198]

Original elements also play a strong role, such as [Jeffersonian democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffersonian_democracy).[^cite_note-MJ-199] Thomas Jefferson's *[Notes on the State of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notes_on_the_State_of_Virginia)* was perhaps the first influential domestic cultural critique by an American and a reaction to the prevailing European consensus that America's domestic originality was [degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_degeneration).[^cite_note-MJ-199] Prevalent ideas and ideals that evolved domestically, such as [national holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_United_States), uniquely [American sports](2257795.md), military tradition,[^cite_note-200] and innovations in the arts and entertainment give a strong sense of [national pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) among the population as a whole.[^cite_note-201]

American culture includes both [conservative](6675.md) and [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) elements, scientific and religious competitiveness, political structures, risk taking and free expression, materialist and moral elements. Despite certain consistent ideological principles (e.g. [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism), [egalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism), faith in freedom and [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy)), the American culture has a variety of expressions due to its geographical scale and demographic diversity.
