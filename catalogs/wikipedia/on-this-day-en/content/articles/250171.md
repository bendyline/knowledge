**Qingdao**,[^cite_note-6] [previously transliterated as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_postal_romanization) **Tsingtao**, is a [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture)-level city in [China](5405.md)'s [Shandong](105032.md) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China). Lying across the [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) and looking out to the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea), it borders the [prefecture-level cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_cities) of [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai) to the northeast, Weifang[^cite_note-7] to the west, and [Rizhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizhao) to the southwest.[^cite_note-8] The [Jiaozhou Bay Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_Bay_Bridge) links Qingdao's main urban area with [Huangdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangdao) district, straddling [Jiaozhou Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_Bay).

Qingdao was an important fortress,[^cite_note-\:1-9] and is a major [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) and [naval base](291782.md), as well as a commercial and [financial center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_center). It is a major nodal city of the [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative) (BRI) that connects Continental and East Asia with Europe.[^cite_note-10]

Administered at the [sub-provincial level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-provincial_city),[^cite_note-11] Qingdao has jurisdiction over seven districts and three county-level cities ([Jiaozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_City), [Pingdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingdu), [Laixi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laixi)). As of the 2020 census, Qingdao built-up (or metro) area made of the seven urban districts ([Shinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinan_District), [Shibei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibei_District), [Huangdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangdao_District), [Laoshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laoshan_District), [Licang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licang_District), [Chengyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengyang_District) and [Jimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimo_District)) was home to 7,172,451 inhabitants, making it the 15th largest city in China by population.[^cite_note-12] It has the highest GDP of any city in the province.

In 1897, the city was ceded to [Germany](11867.md). For the Germans, Qingdao was a strategic trade center, port and base for its [East Asia Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Squadron), allowing the German navy to project dominance in the Pacific.[^cite_note-13] In 1914, following the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md), Japan occupied the city and the surrounding province during the [Siege of Tsingtao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tsingtao). In 1915, China agreed to recognize Japan's special position in the territory through what became known as the [Twenty-One Demands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-One_Demands).[^cite_note-14] In 1918, the Chinese government, under the control of the warlord [Duan Qirui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duan_Qirui), secretly agreed to Japanese terms in exchange for a loan. Following the First World War, during the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)), Japan secured agreements with the Allied powers to recognize its claim to the areas in Shandong, which included Qingdao, previously occupied by Germany. The recognition directly triggered the [May Fourth Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Fourth_Movement) among the Chinese public in a strong response. In 1922, Shandong reverted to Chinese control following the United States' mediation during the [Washington Naval Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Conference).[^cite_note-\:1-9]

Qingdao is home to electronics multinationals such as [Haier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haier) and [Hisense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisense). Its historic German-style architecture and [Tsingtao Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtao_Brewery), the second largest brewery in China,[^cite_note-15] are legacies of the [German occupation (1898–1914)](#German_occupation). Qingdao is classified as a Large-Port Metropolis.[^cite_note-16]

In 2007, Qingdao was named as one of China's top ten cities by the Chinese Cities Brand Value Report.[^cite_note-17] In 2009, Qingdao was named China's most livable city by the Chinese Institute of City Competitiveness.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In 2018, Qingdao held the [Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Qingdao_SCO_summit).[^cite_note-20] In the 2024 [Global Financial Centers Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index), Qingdao ranked 31st.[^cite_note-21] In 2024, Qingdao was rated as a Beta- level [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-22]

Qingdao is also one of the world's top 35 cities for global scientific research as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-23] The city was also ranked 20th globally in the "Global Top 100 Science & Technology Cluster Cities" as of 2024.[^cite_note-\:25-24] It is home to [several notable universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Shandong), including the [Ocean University of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_University_of_China), [China University of Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_University_of_Petroleum), [Shandong University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_University_of_Science_and_Technology), [Qingdao University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_University), [Qingdao University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_University_of_Science_and_Technology), [Qingdao University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_University_of_Technology), and [Qingdao Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Agricultural_University).[^cite_note-\:2-25]

Human settlement in the area dates back 6,000 years. The [Dongyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongyi) lived here and created the [Dawenkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawenkou), [Longshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longshan_culture) and Dongyeshi cultures.[^cite_note-26] In the [Eastern Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Zhou) dynasty (770–256 BC), the town of [Jimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimo,_Shandong) was established, which was then the second-largest one in the Shandong region.

### German occupation

Further information: [Kiautschou Bay Leased Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiautschou_Bay_Leased_Territory)



Sketch map of Qingdao, circa 1906



Main gate of former Chinese munitions depot, taken over by the [Imperial German Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Navy), Kiautschou Bay, Shandong peninsula, 1898

Under the [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_Empire), the area in which Qingdao is now located was known as *Jiao'ao* ([胶澳](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%83%B6%E6%BE%B3)).[^cite_note-27] In 1891, the Qing decided to fortify Jiao'ao as a defense against naval attacks. [Imperial German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) naval officials observed and reported on this activity during a formal survey of [Jiaozhou Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_Bay) in May 1897. In November of that year, the [Juye Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juye_Incident) would take place, in which three German missionaries were attacked, and two were killed. Subsequently, German troops seized and occupied the fortification.[^cite_note-28]

The preindustrial, waning [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) was forced to concede the area to Germany the following year, and the [Kiautschou Bay concession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiautschou_Bay_concession), as it became known, existed from 1898 to 1914.[^cite_note-29]

With an area of 552 km<sup>2</sup> (136,000 acres; 213 sq mi), it was located in the imperial province of [Shandong](105032.md) ([alternately romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Chinese) as [Shantung or Shan-tung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade-Giles)) on the southern coast of the [Shandong](105032.md) Peninsula in northern China. Jiaozhou was alternatively romanized as Kiaochow, Kiauchau, or Kiao-Chau in English, and Kiautschou in German; Qingdao was its administrative center. "The so-called *Marktstrasse* (Market Street) was nothing more than the old main street of the Chinese village of Qingdao, and the buildings lining it were the former homes of [fishermen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishermen) and [farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer). Having sold their property, they resettled their homes and fields in the villages further east."[^cite_note-Matzat1-30]

Upon gaining control of the area, the Germans outfitted the impoverished fishing village of Qingdao with wide streets, solid housing areas, government buildings, electrification throughout, a sewer system and a safe drinking water supply, a rarity in large parts of [Asia](689.md) at that time and later.[^cite_note-Flagge-31]

The area had the highest school density and the highest *per capita* student enrollment in all of China, with primary, secondary and vocational schools funded by the [Imperial German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) treasury and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) and [Roman Catholic](606848.md) missions.[^cite_note-Flagge-31] Commercial interests established the Germania Brewery in 1903, which later became the world-famous [Tsingtao Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtao_Brewery).[^cite_note-Matzat2-32] German cultural and commercial influences extended to other areas of Shandong Province, including the establishment of diverse commercial enterprises.

Identified by the German authorities as a strategically important port, Qingdao was administered by the [Imperial Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsmarineamt) (*Reichsmarineamt*) rather than the [Imperial Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Colonial_Office) (*Reichskolonialamt*). The growing Imperial German Navy based their [East Asia Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Squadron) there, allowing the warships to conduct operations throughout the western [Pacific](23070.md). Beginning in January 1898, the marines of *[III. Seebataillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seebataillon)* were based at Qingdao. Construction of the [Jiaoji Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaoji_Railway) began on 23 September 1899, and was completed in 1904.[^cite_note-33]

Before the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md) (1914–1918), ships of the German naval forces under [Admiral Count von Spee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_von_Spee) were located at central Pacific colonies on routine missions. The fleet then rendezvoused in the [Marianas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianas_Islands) to plan a transit back to Germany rather than be trapped in the Pacific by more powerful and numerous Allied fleets (British and Japanese).[^cite_note-34]

### Japanese occupation

After a minor British naval attack on the German concession in Shandong in 1914, [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) troops occupied the city and the surrounding province during the [Siege of Tsingtao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tsingtao) (Qingdao) after Japan's declaration of war on Germany in accordance with the [Anglo-Japanese Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Japanese_Alliance).[^cite_note-Source_Records_of_the_Great_War,_Vol._III,_ed._Charles_F._Horne,_National_Alumni_1923-35] China protested against Japan's violation of her neutrality but was not able to interfere in the military operations.[^cite_note-36] The decision of the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919) and the [Versailles Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_Treaty) negotiations not to restore Chinese rule over the previous foreign concessions in Qingdao after the Great War triggered the [May Fourth Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Fourth_Movement) (4 May 1919) of anti-imperialism, nationalism and cultural identity in China.[^cite_note-37]



Map of Qingdao in 1912

The city came under Chinese rule in December 1922, under control of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) (ROC) established in 1912 after the [1911 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Revolution) the year before. However, Japan maintained its economic dominance of the railway and the province as a whole.[^cite_note-38] The city became a [direct-controlled municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-controlled_municipality) of the ROC Government in July 1929.

Japan re-occupied Qingdao in 1938, a year after it expanded the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) (1937–1945), (a precursor to [World War II](32927.md), 1939–1945) with its plans of territorial expansion into China's coast. [Nationalist (Kuomintang)](16903.md) ROC forces returned after the [Japanese surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) in September 1945. On 2 June 1949, during the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) and shortly before the founding of the communist [People's Republic of China](5405.md) on 1 October 1949 the city was taken by Chairman [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong) and his troops.

### Qingdao city planning and development

#### 1898–1914

The development of the Qingdao urban space during the German occupation (1898–1914) originated from the port. Mass urban construction began in 1898 with the relocation of Chinese dwellers along the coast.[^cite_note-39] With the completion of such series of mass construction projects such as wharves, the Tsingtao-Jinan Railway Line, Tsingtao Railway Station and locomotive works, a city was starting to take shape.[^cite_note-40] The area had the highest school density and highest per capita student enrollment in all of China, with primary, secondary and vocational schools funded by the Berlin treasury as well as Protestant and Roman Catholic missions.[^cite_note-41]

In 1910, the Germans drew up for the second time the city planning of Tsingtao (Warner 2001, p. 33). The former urban area was extended four times highlighted by the emphasis on the development of commerce and trade. [Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen) (1866–1925), leader of the 1911 Revolution and subsequently first president of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), visited the Tsingtao area and stated in 1912, "I am impressed. The city is a true model for China's future".[^cite_note-42]

#### 1914–1922

The development of Tsingtao urban space continued during the first Japan-occupation period (1914–1922). In 1914, Tsingtao was taken over by the Japanese and served as a base for the exploitation of natural resources of Shandong and northern China. With the development of industry and commerce, a "New City District" was established to furnish the Japanese colonists with commercial sections and living quarters, which suggested a striking contrast to the shabby houses in the local Chinese zones (Li 2007, p. 133). In the meantime, several schools, hospitals, and public buildings were constructed, followed by urban streets and intercity highways as well. The urban spatial layout continued to expand northward along the east bay area.

#### 1922–1938

The development of Tsingtao urban space during the ROC-ruled period (1922–1938). This period saw the substantial progress of the urban development of Tsingtao. The government engaged itself in mass construction that gave birth to villa districts at the beach and bank groups in CBD. Plenty of public buildings and facilities for entertainment and sports were completed. By the year 1937, the urban population numbered 385,000 (Lu 2001, p. 327). Tsingtao consequently distinguished itself as a prominent holiday resort and summer retreat.

#### 1938–1945

The development of Tsingtao urban space continued during the second Japan-occupied period (1938–1945). Japanese armed forces returned to Tsingtao in 1938 and started to strive for the construction of the Greater Tsingtao in the following June. Accordingly, they worked out the city planning of the Greater Tsingtao and the City Planning of the Mother Town (Tsingtao City Proper), even though they had not had the opportunity to actualize either, respectively. The period in question did not witness much urban progress except for the logical construction of No. 6 Wharf, some Japanese residences, and a small number of roads and streets (Lu 2001, p. 339).

### Postwar

After World War II, the [KMT](16903.md) allowed Qingdao to serve as the headquarters of the [Western Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Seventh_Fleet) of the [US Navy](20518076.md) in 1945; however, its headquarters were transferred to the [Philippines](23440.md) sometime in late 1948. On 2 June 1949, the [CPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_China)-led [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Red_Army) entered Qingdao and the city and province have been under [PRC](5405.md) control since that time.

Since the 1984 inauguration of China's open-door policy to foreign trade and investment, Qingdao has rapidly developed into an ultramodern port city. It is now the headquarters of the [Chinese navy's northern fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_Fleet). An early example of the open-door policy occurred on 5 November 1984, when three United States Naval vessels visited Qingdao. This was the first US port call to China in more than 37 years. [USS *Rentz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Rentz), [USS *Reeves*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Reeves_\(DLG-24\)), and [USS *Oldendorf*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Oldendorf) and their crews were officially hosted by the Chinese [People's Liberation Army Navy](24918.md) (PLAN).

Northern Qingdao, particularly [Shibei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibei_District), [Licang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licang_District), and [Chengyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengyang_District) districts, are now major manufacturing centers. The city has recently experienced a strong growth period, with a new central business district created to the east of the older business district. Outside of the center of the city, there is a large industrial zone, which includes chemical processing, rubber, and heavy manufacturing, in addition to a growing high-tech area. Numerous local and national service companies, rather than manufacturers, are based in the city's southern district.

## Geography



Qingdao (labeled CH'ING-TAO (TSINGTAO) 青島) (1954)



Map including Qingdao (labeled as CH'ING-TAO (TSINGTAO))

Qingdao is located on the south-facing coast of the [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Schantung-Halbinsel*). It borders three prefecture-level cities, namely [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai) to the northeast, [Weifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang) to the west, and [Rizhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizhao) to the southwest. The city occupies an area totaling 10,654 km<sup>2</sup> (4,114 sq mi), and stretches in latitude from 35° 35' to 37° 09' N and in longitude from 119° 30' to 121° 00' E. The populated sections of the city are relatively flat while mountains spur up within city limits and nearby. The highest elevation in the city is located 1,133 meters (3,717 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). Of the total area of Qingdao, 15.5% is highland, while the foothill, plain, and lowland areas constitute 25.1%, 37.8%, and 21.7%, respectively. The city has a 730.64-kilometer (454.00 mi)-long coastline. Five significant rivers exceeding 50 kilometers (31 mi) in length can be found in the region.

### Climate

Qingdao has a temperate, four-season, [monsoon](57630.md)-influenced[^cite_note-SDIO-44] climate that lies in the transition between the [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cwa*) and [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Köppen: *Dwa*) regimes. Due to the direct regulation of the marine environment, the city is influenced by the southeast monsoon and the currents and water masses from the ocean, so it also has significant maritime climate characteristics.[^cite_note-45] Winters range from cool to cold and windy, but are generally dry, with a January average of 0.2 °C (32.4 °F). Summer is generally hot and humid, but very hot days are rare, with an August average of 25.6 °C (78.1 °F). Due to its proximity to the coast and location on a peninsula, compared to most inland areas of China, its spring is delayed by one month,[^cite_note-SDIO-44] and the annual [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) is only 6.3 °C (11.3 °F); conversely, its fall is milder than inland areas in Shandong. The water temperature peaks at about 25 °C (77 °F) in late August. Thus, swimming is possible for two months on either side. The annual mean temperature is 13.3 °C (55.9 °F). Extremes since 1951 have ranged from −15.9 °C (3 °F) on 7 and 8 January 2021[^cite_note-46] to 38.9 °C (102 °F) on 15 July 2002;[^cite_note-47] unofficial readings have reached a record low of −16.9 °C (1.6 °F) or for the official weather station there, −16.4 °C (2.5 °F) on 10 January 1931.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] With monthly percent possible sunshine ranging from 38 percent in July to 59 percent in October, the city receives 2,261 hours of bright sunshine annually.[^cite_note-cma_new-50]

During the summer months, the beaches of Qingdao are afflicted by massive [algal blooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_bloom). The decomposing algae release large amounts of [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide) gas, which produces an offensive "rotten egg" odor. [Sea lettuce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_lettuce) blooms, which are partially caused by [seaweed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed_farming) in Jiangsu Province, led local officials to declare a "large-scale algae disaster" in 2013.[^cite_note-51]

Of the 10 million residents of Qingdao, 6.2 million reside in the Qingdao urban area. Another estimated 5 million live in other cities under Qingdao's jurisdiction.[^cite_note-\:0-58] The annual birth rate is calculated around 76,507, with a [birth rate](515898.md) of 10.15 per year per thousand, and a [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) of 6.32, both calculated on an annual basis. Living standards are among the highest of leading Chinese cities due to the strong export economy and relatively high family wages.

There is a large Korean community in Qingdao. By 2009, there were approximately 100,000 Koreans working, studying and living in Qingdao, which makes Qingdao the second in terms of Korean population in China, following Beijing which has about 200,000 Koreans.[^cite_note-59]

According to the 2021 Qingdao Statistical Yearbook, the permanent resident population of Qingdao in 2020 was 10.1057 million, an increase of 182,700 people compared to the previous year.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

## Economy



Qingdao-based [Tsingtao beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtao_beer), China's second-largest domestic brand and its largest export brand.



Qingdao TV Tower

In recent years, an important region in Eastern China, Shandong Province has seen substantial change in its economic landscape. Much of this development has been concentrated in Qingdao.[^cite_note-KPMG-62] Qingdao has seen rapid development. With an annual growth rate of 18.9 percent in 2006, the city's GDP reached 42.3 billion, ranking first in Shandong Province and tenth out of China's top 20 cities.[^cite_note-KPMG-62] [GDP](12594.md) per capita comprised [CN¥](66958.md)52,895 (US\$7,616) in 2008. In 2024, Qingdao's GDP per capita comprised CN¥161,200 (US\$23,000).[^cite_note-63]

In 2006, Qingdao was ranked one of six "golden cities" by the World Bank, out of 120 Chinese cities assessed on factors including investment climate and government effectiveness.[^cite_note-KPMG-62] In 2018, Qingdao's GDP reached CN¥1200.15 billion, though it shrank a little in 2019.[^cite_note-64]



Tsingtao Brewery Company Limited

Internationally, Qingdao is perhaps best known for its [Tsingtao Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtao_Brewery), founded by a German-British joint venture in 1903 that produces [Tsingtao beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtao_beer), the best-known Chinese export beer. It is also home to [Haier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haier), a large [white goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_goods) manufacturer, and [Hisense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisense), a major electronics company. In 2002 guitar manufacturers [Epiphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphone) opened a factory in Qingdao.[^cite_note-65]

In 1984 the Chinese government named a district of Qingdao a [Special Economic and Technology Development Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zones_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) (SETDZ). Along with this district, the entire city had gone through fast-tracked development of [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_Industry) and [tertiary industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy). Qingdao has been the recipient of large foreign investment and its port is a hub for local and international trade. [South Korea](27019.md) and [Japan](15573.md) in particular made extensive investments in the city. In terms of [primary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector) [industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(economics\)), Qingdao has an estimated 50,000 acres (200 km<sup>2</sup>) of [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land). Qingdao has a zigzagging pattern coastline, and thus possesses an invaluable stock of fish, [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp), and other sea resources.

Qingdao's [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) electricity generation performs at among the highest levels in the region.[^cite_note-66]

#### Haiwan Bridge

On 30 June 2011, the longest bridge over water opened in Qingdao. The bridge, [Haiwan Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_Bay_Bridge), is 26.4 miles (42.5 km) long and connects Qingdao to [Huangdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangdao) and Hongdao. The bridge is almost three miles (4.8 km) longer than the previous [record-holder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest_bridges), the [Lake Pontchartrain Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Pontchartrain_Causeway) in the [American](3434750.md) state of [Louisiana](18130.md). Haiwan Bridge is supported by more than 5,000 pillars and costs about 10 billion yuan which is about 1.5 billion dollars. The bridge was designed by the [Shandong Gausu Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Gaosu_Group) and the construction lasted for four years. Haiwan Bridge cut the commute between the city of Qingdao and the sprawling suburb of Huangdao by 30 minutes. At least 10,000 workers toiled in two teams around the clock to build the bridge, which was constructed from opposite ends.[^cite_note-68]

On the same day, the [Jiaozhou Bay Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Jiaozhou_Bay_tunnel) opened. The tunnel brought much convenience to people by supporting public buses and making transport between the two areas more convenient.

### Marine

See also: [Port of Qingdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Qingdao)

Qingdao (official name: Qingdao port international co. ltd.) hosts one of the world's busiest [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Qingdao). Cooperative relations have been established with 450 ports in 130 countries worldwide. The port of Qingdao is part of the [21st Century Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Maritime_Silk_Road).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] In 2003, the annual cargo handling capacity exceeded 100 million [tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton) for the first time. The number of containers reached 3.41 million [twenty-foot equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) (TEU) of cargoes.[^cite_note-71]

By 2011, the port had become the world's sixth-busiest by Total Cargo Volume, having handled 372,000,000 metric tons (366,000,000 long tons; 410,000,000 short tons) of cargo in that year. As of 2016, it was the 8th in the world in terms of TEUs (Twenty Foot Equivalent Units).[^cite_note-72]

The *Orient Ferry* connects Qingdao with [Shimonoseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimonoseki), Japan. There are two ferry lines connecting Qingdao with [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea). The *New Golden Bridge II* operates between Qingdao and [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon), and the *Blue Sea Ferry* operates between Qingdao and [Gunsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunsan).[^cite_note-73]

Qingdao port also includes a number of large adjacent ports including Dongjiakou.[^cite_note-74]

### Aviation

[Qingdao Jiaodong International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Jiaodong_International_Airport), located 39 km (24 mi) away from the [city center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_center), is served by 13 domestic and international [airlines](1942.md) that operate 94 routes, 12 of which are international and regional. ### Intercity rail



Qingdao railway station

Qingdao's railway development picked up during the late 1990s. It is at the start of the [Jinan–Qingdao high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan%E2%80%93Qingdao_high-speed_railway), the [Qingdao–Jinan passenger railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao%E2%80%93Jinan_passenger_railway) and the original [Qingdao–Jinan railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao%E2%80%93Jinan_railway). D and G series high-speed trains travel on the Jinan–Qingdao high-speed railway and reach speeds of 300 km/h (190 mph) on the Jinan-Qingdao Section.[^cite_note-78] Services go to [Beijing](18603746.md), [Shanghai](27643.md), [Hefei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hefei), [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) and [Tianjin](50821.md).[^cite_note-79] The fastest train between Qingdao and Beijing is G206 / G205, which takes 2 hours and 58 minutes.[^cite_note-80]

Domestic rail lines connect Qingdao with many cities in China, including [Beijing](18603746.md), [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou), [Chengdu](170375.md), [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an), [Zhengzhou](288264.md), [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) and [Jining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jining,_Shandong).

#### Metro

Main article: [Qingdao Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Metro)



Platform of Qingdao Metro

After getting the approval from the State Council, the government announced on 18 August 2009 that Qingdao is ready to spend more than 29 billion yuan (\$4.2 billion) before 2016 on its subway construction. Metro Line 3 is the first line in function and opened on 16 December 2015. In the long term, the city plans to build eight subway lines in downtown and some suburban districts, which account for 231.5 km (143.8 mi) in future.[^cite_note-82] The system has an operating length of 243 km (151 mi), lines in operation including [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Qingdao_Metro\)), [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Qingdao_Metro\)), [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Qingdao_Metro\)), [Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_4_\(Qingdao_Metro\)), [Line 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_6_\(Qingdao_Metro\)), [Line 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_8_\(Qingdao_Metro\)), the [Oceantec Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceantec_Valley_Line), and the [West Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Line_\(Qingdao_Metro\)).[^cite_note-QINGDAO_METRO-83]

As of February 24, 2023, according to the approved network plan of Qingdao City, there are 19 long-term plans for Qingdao Metro, with a total length of 872 kilometers; The third phase construction plan has been approved, with a total of 13 lines and a total length of 503 kilometers, ranking ninth in the national approved mileage level; Seven lines have been opened and operated, including Lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 11, and 13, with an operating mileage of 315 kilometers. As of March 2023, there are 146 operating stations, ranking tenth in China and second in northern cities; There are 10 lines under construction (including extension lines), including the West Extension and Phase II of Line 2, Line 5, Phase I and Phase II of Line 6, Phase II of Line 7, South Section and Branch of Line 8, Phase I of Line 9, and Phase I of Line 15. The total length under construction is 188 kilometers, and it is expected to be fully completed by 2028.

## Culture

### Architecture



Qingdao retains many buildings with a German architectural style

There are a large number of German-style buildings in Qingdao's [city center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_center), a remarkable fact considering the German leased-territory period only lasted 16 years (1898–1914). The unique combination of German and Chinese architecture therein, combined with German demographic roots and a large Korean expatriate population, gives Qingdao a rather distinct atmosphere. An old saying described Qingdao as a city of "red tiles green trees, blue sky, and blue sea." A larger number of areas in former foreign styles are well preserved. Although the new city area is under large-scale reconstruction, the old city area (especially the western part of [Shinan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinan_District)) still retains many traditional buildings.

### Language

During the city's leased-territory days, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), the official language, was rigorously taught and promoted. Since the demise of Germany's colonial empire after World War I, the German language is all but gone, leaving little impact on the local languages. A local accent known as [Qingdao dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_dialect) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 青岛话; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Qīngdǎo huà*) distinguishes the residents of the city from those of the surrounding [Shandong](105032.md) province. Due to the efforts by the city government to promote standard [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), most educated people can speak standard Mandarin in addition to their native dialect. With reform policies and English teaching, some young citizens have been taught English and many can converse with English-speaking foreigners. Business and traffic signs in English are becoming more and more common.

Qingdao previously had a large German community and a German garrison, so three German newspapers operated in Qingdao.[^cite_note-Walravens90-84] German papers included *[Deutsch-Asiatische Warte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsch-Asiatische_Warte?action=edit\&redlink=1)* ([traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 泰東古今鑑; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 泰东古今鉴; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Tàidōng Gǔjīn Jiàn*;[^cite_note-Walravens91-85] weekly newspaper published until 1906, included *Die Welt des Ostens, Altes und Neues aus Asiens drei Kaiserreichen*, a cultural supplement),[^cite_note-Walravens90-84] the *[Tsingtauer Neueste Nachrichten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtauer_Neueste_Nachrichten)* and the *[Kiautschou Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiautschou_Post?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (a daily paper published from 1908 to 1912, referring to the [Kiautschou (Jiaozhou) Bay concession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiautschou_Bay_concession)). German publishing in Qingdao ended after [World War I](4764461.md) and the beginning of the Japanese administration.[^cite_note-Walravens91-85]

A 1912 publication of the [United States Department of Commerce](70243.md), [Bureau of Foreign and Domestic Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Foreign_and_Domestic_Commerce) said that the *[Tageblatt für Nordchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tageblatt_f%C3%BCr_Nordchina?action=edit\&redlink=1)* of [Tianjin](50821.md) was read in Qingdao and that major newspapers from [Shanghai](27643.md) were also read in Qingdao.[^cite_note-86]
