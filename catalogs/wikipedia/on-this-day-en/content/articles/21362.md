**Nicaragua**,[^cite_note-19] officially the **Republic of Nicaragua**,[^cite_note-20] is a [country](23604120.md) in [Central America](6121.md), comprising 130,370 square kilometers (50,340 sq mi). With a population of 7,097,329 as of 2026,[^cite_note-21] it is the third-most populous country in Central America after [Guatemala](17238567.md) and [Honduras](13394.md), and the [largest by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_American_countries_by_area) in all of Central America.

Nicaragua is bordered by [Honduras](13394.md) to the north, the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) of the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the east, [Costa Rica](5551.md) to the south, and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) to the west. The country also shares maritime borders with [El Salvador](9356.md) to the west and [Colombia](5222.md) to the east. Nicaragua's largest city and national capital is [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), which is also the [fourth-largest city in Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_in_Central_America#Largest_cities_proper), with a population of 1,055,247 as of December 2020. Nicaragua is known as "the breadbasket of Central America" due to having the most fertile soil and arable land in all of Central America.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] The official language is [Spanish](26825.md), though different groups on the Caribbean coast ([Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast)) speak their own languages and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language). The mixture of cultural traditions has generated substantial diversity in folklore, cuisine, music, and literature, including contributions by Nicaraguan poets and writers such as [Rubén Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Dar%C3%ADo).

Originally inhabited by various indigenous cultures since ancient times, the region was conquered by the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in the 16th century. Nicaragua gained independence from Spain in 1821. The Caribbean region ([Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast)) followed a different historical path, being an independent kingdom under British protection from the 17th century until 1860 with the signing of the [Treaty of Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Managua), which brought it into a [union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_union) with Nicaragua, while its northernmost part was placed under Honduras by the Wyke-Cruz Treaty of 1859.[^cite_note-24] Since its independence, Nicaragua has undergone periods of political unrest, dictatorship, [American occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_occupation_of_Nicaragua) and fiscal crisis, as well as the [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution) of the 1960s and 1970s and the [Contra War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras) of the 1980s.

Though nominally a unitary presidential republic, Nicaragua has experienced significant [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding) since 2007 under the presidency of [Daniel Ortega](8778.md), resulting in [large protests in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Nicaraguan_protests) and a subsequent crackdown. Following the [2021 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nicaraguan_general_election), it has been widely described as an [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship),[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] and the [next national elections](78581270.md) were effectively canceled in 2026.[^cite_note-28] It is a developing country and has the [second lowest GDP per capita (nominal)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latin_American_and_Caribbean_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) and [fourth lowest GDP per capita (PPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latin_American_and_Caribbean_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) among Latin American and Caribbean countries. In 2025, Nicaragua was ranked as the second-most corrupt country in Latin America, after [Venezuela](32374.md), by the [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index#2020%E2%80%932024). Nicaragua's multiethnic population includes people of [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), indigenous, European, and African heritage.

Known as the "land of lakes and volcanoes",[^cite_note-Brierley-29][^cite_note-Wallace-30] Nicaragua is also home to the [Bosawás Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaw%C3%A1s_Biosphere_Reserve), the second-largest tropical rainforest of the Americas.[^cite_note-31] The biological diversity, warm tropical climate and active volcanoes have made Nicaragua an increasingly popular [tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_destination).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Nicaragua is a founding member of the [United Nations](31769.md)[^cite_note-34] and is also a member of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement),[^cite_note-35] [Bolivarian Alliance for the Peoples of Our America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_Alliance_for_the_Peoples_of_Our_America),[^cite_note-36] and [Community of Latin American and Caribbean States](26313243.md).[^cite_note-37]

## Etymology



Location of *Nicanahuac*, the Indigenous name for *[Gran Nicoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Nicoya?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, an area in western Nicaragua and northwestern Costa Rica inhabited by the Nahuas

The etymology of Nicaragua is *Nicānāhuac*, which was the name the [Nicaraos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_people), a [Nawat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawat)-speaking people, gave to the [Greater Nicoya](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran%20Nicoya) region of Nicaragua and [Costa Rica](5551.md).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-cclec.org-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] It means "here lies Anahuac" in Nahuatl and is a combination of the words "nican" (here),[^cite_note-45] and "[Ānāhuac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahuac_\(Aztec\))", which is a combination of the words "atl" (water) and "nahuac", a locative meaning "surrounded". Therefore, the literal translation of *Nicanahuac* is "here surrounded by water", which corraborates the theory that the etymology refers to the large bodies of water in and around the country, the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), lakes [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua) and [Xolotlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Xolotlan), and the rivers and lagoons. The name evolved through phonetic changes such as the "n" becoming an *r* and the *ahuac* in "anahuac" becoming *agua* as both "anahuac" and "agua" relate to the concept of water, which would have made it a convenient circumstance for the Spanish in renaming *Nicanahuac* to *Nicaragua*.

Previous theories about the country's name came from the following: *nican-nahua*, which means "here are the Nahuas"; and [nicarao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_\(cacique\))-agua. However, in 2002, it was discovered that the real name of the cacique was Macuilmiquiztli and not Nicarao, ultimately debunking this theory.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-cclec.org-40][^cite_note-Ideal-50][^cite_note-Pueblo-51][^cite_note-Choque-52][^cite_note-53]

## History

Main article: [History of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nicaragua)

### Pre-Columbian history



An ancient [petroglyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) on [Ometepe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ometepe_Island)

[Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians) first inhabited what is now known as Nicaragua as far back as 12,000 BCE.[^cite_note-Dall2005-54] In later [pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era) times, Nicaragua's [indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) were part of the [Intermediate Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_Area),[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 33 </sup> between the [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) and [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) cultural regions, and within the influence of the [Isthmo-Colombian Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmo-Colombian_Area). Nicaragua's central region and its Caribbean coast were inhabited by [Macro-Chibchan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macro-Chibchan_languages) ethnic groups such as the [Miskito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people), [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_people), [Mayangna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayagna), and [Matagalpas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_people).[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 20 </sup> They had coalesced in Central America and migrated both to and from present-day northern Colombia and nearby areas.[^cite_note-56] Their food came primarily from hunting and gathering, but also fishing and [slash-and-burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn) agriculture.[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 33 </sup>[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 65 </sup>

At the end of the 15th century, western Nicaragua was inhabited by several indigenous peoples related by culture to the Mesoamerican civilizations of the [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs) and [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples), and by language to the [Mesoamerican language area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_language_area).[^cite_note-LOC1-59] The [Mangue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangue_people), or Chorotega, were an ethnic group who had arrived in Nicaragua from what is now the Mexican state of [Chiapas](6787.md) sometime around 800 CE.[^cite_note-Choque-52][^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 26–33 </sup> The [Nicarao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_people) were a branch of [Nahuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuas) who spoke the [Nawat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawat_language) dialect and also came from Chiapas, around 1200 CE.[^cite_note-Campbell1985-60] Prior to that, the Nicaraos had been associated with the [Toltec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toltec) civilization.[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 26–33 </sup>[^cite_note-Campbell1985-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Both Chorotegas and Nicaraos originated in Mexico's [Cholula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholula_\(Mesoamerican_site\)) valley,[^cite_note-Campbell1985-60] and migrated south.[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 26–33 </sup> A third group, the [Subtiabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtiaba_language), were an [Oto-Manguean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oto-Manguean_languages) people who migrated from the Mexican state of [Guerrero](195816.md) around 1200 CE.[^cite_note-Campbell1997-64]<sup>: 159 </sup> Additionally, there were trade-related colonies in Nicaragua set up by the Aztecs starting in the 14th century.[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 26–33 </sup>

### Spanish colonial era (1523–1821)

Further information: [Spanish colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) and [Spanish conquest of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Nicaragua)



The Colonial city of [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua)



The colonial city of [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua) near [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua), one of the most visited sites in [Central America](6121.md)

In 1502, on his fourth voyage, [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) became the first European known to have reached what is now Nicaragua as he sailed southeast toward the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama).[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 193 </sup>[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 92 </sup> Columbus explored the [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) on the Atlantic side of Nicaragua[^cite_note-65] but did not encounter any indigenous people. 20 years later, the Spaniards returned to Nicaragua, this time to its southwestern part. The first attempt to conquer Nicaragua was by the conquistador [Gil González Dávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Gonz%C3%A1lez_D%C3%A1vila),[^cite_note-EBH-66] who had arrived in Panama in January 1520.

In 1522, González Dávila ventured to the area that later became the [Rivas Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivas_Department) of Nicaragua.[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 35 </sup>[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 92 </sup> There he encountered an indigenous Nahua tribe led by chief Macuilmiquiztli, whose name has sometimes been erroneously referred to as "[Nicarao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_\(cacique\))" or "Nicaragua". The tribe's capital was Quauhcapolca.[^cite_note-Encuentro-67][^cite_note-HealyPohl1980-68][^cite_note-Dyck2015-69] González Dávila conversed with Macuilmiquiztli thanks to two indigenous interpreters who had learned Spanish, whom he had brought along.[^cite_note-Sanchez-70]

After exploring and gathering gold[^cite_note-Encuentro-67][^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 35 </sup>[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 55 </sup> in the fertile western valleys, González Dávila and his men were attacked and driven off by the Chorotega, led by chief [Diriangén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diriangen).[^cite_note-Encuentro-67][^cite_note-71] The Spanish tried to convert the tribes to Christianity; Macuilmiquiztli's tribe was baptized,[^cite_note-Encuentro-67][^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 86 </sup> but Diriangén was openly hostile to the Spaniards. Western Nicaragua, at the Pacific Coast, became a port and shipbuilding facility for the [Galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Galleon) plying the waters between [Manila](184334.md) and [Acapulco](1446.md).[^cite_note-72]

In 1524, the first Spanish permanent settlements were founded.[^cite_note-EBH-66] That year, the conquistador [Francisco Hernández de Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Hern%C3%A1ndez_de_C%C3%B3rdoba_\(founder_of_Nicaragua\)) founded two of Nicaragua's main cities: [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua) on [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua), and then [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua), west of [Lake Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Managua).[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 35, 193 </sup>[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 92 </sup> Córdoba soon built defenses for the cities and fought against incursions by other conquistadors.[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 92 </sup> Córdoba was later publicly beheaded for having defied his superior, [Pedro Arias Dávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Arias_D%C3%A1vila).[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 35 </sup> Córdoba's tomb and remains were discovered in 2000 in the [ruins of León Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n_Viejo).[^cite_note-ET-73]

The clashes among Spanish forces did not impede their destruction of the indigenous people and their culture. The series of battles came to be known as the "War of the Captains".[^cite_note-74] Pedro Arias Dávila was a winner;[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 35 </sup> although he lost control of Panama, he moved to Nicaragua and established his base in León.[^cite_note-Whisnant2000-75] In 1527, León became the capital of the colony.[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 93 </sup>[^cite_note-Whisnant2000-75] Through diplomacy, Arias Dávila became the colony's first governor.[^cite_note-ET-73]

Without women in their parties,[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 123 </sup> the Spanish conquerors took Nahua and Chorotega wives and partners, beginning the multiethnic mix of indigenous and European stock now known as "*[mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo)*", which constitutes the great majority of the population in western Nicaragua.[^cite_note-LOC1-59] Many indigenous people were killed by European [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease), compounded by neglect by the Spaniards, who controlled their subsistence.[^cite_note-EBH-66] Many other indigenous peoples were captured and transported as slaves to Panama and Peru between 1526 and 1540.[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 193 </sup>[^cite_note-Newson-58]<sup>: 104–105 </sup>

In 1610, the [Momotombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momotombo) volcano erupted, destroying the city of León.[^cite_note-Geomorph-76] The city was rebuilt northwest of the original,[^cite_note-Whisnant2000-75][^cite_note-Geomorph-76] which is now known as the [ruins of León Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n_Viejo). During the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), Central America was subject to conflict between Britain and Spain. British navy admiral [Horatio Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson) led expeditions in the [Battle of San Fernando de Omoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Fernando_de_Omoa) in 1779 and on the [San Juan River in 1780](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Expedition_\(1780\)), the latter of which had temporary success before being abandoned due to disease.

### Independent Nicaragua (1821–1909)

See also: [History of Nicaragua § Fight for independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nicaragua#Fight_for_independence)



The [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) in 1830



A portrait of the [Battle of San Jacinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Jacinto_\(1856\)) during the [Filibuster War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster_War)

The [Act of Independence of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Independence_of_Central_America) dissolved the [Captaincy General of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala) in September 1821, and Nicaragua soon [became part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America_under_Mexican_rule) of the [First Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire). In July 1823, after the overthrow of the Mexican monarchy in March of the same year, Nicaragua joined the newly formed [United Provinces of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America), a country later known as the Federal Republic of Central America. Nicaragua definitively became an independent republic in 1838.[^cite_note-77]

The early years of independence were characterized by rivalry between the [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalist_Liberal_Party) elite of León and the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Nicaragua\)) elite of Granada, which often degenerated into civil war, particularly during the 1840s and 1850s. [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua) rose to undisputed preeminence as the nation's capital in 1852 to allay the rivalry between the two feuding cities.[^cite_note-Capital-78][^cite_note-79] Following the start of the [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) in 1848, Nicaragua provided a route for travelers from the eastern United States to journey to [California](5407.md) by sea, via the [San Juan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_\(Nicaragua\)) and Lake Nicaragua.[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 81 </sup>

Invited by the Liberals in 1855 to join their struggle against the Conservatives, the American adventurer and [filibuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster_\(military\)) [William Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Walker_\(filibuster\)) set himself up as [President of Nicaragua](302862.md) after conducting a farcical election in 1856; his presidency lasted less than a year.[^cite_note-mined-80] Military forces from Costa Rica, Honduras, El Salvador, Guatemala, and Nicaragua itself united to drive Walker out of Nicaragua in 1857,[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] bringing three decades of Conservative rule.[^cite_note-84]

Great Britain, which had a formal [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) over the [Kingdom of Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) since 1655, delegated its northern area to Honduras in 1859, and its middle region to Nicaragua in 1860. Mosquitia remained semi-independent until the [Nicaragua Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_Crisis_of_1894%E2%80%931895) in 1894, when it was militarily occupied by orders of [José Santos Zelaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Santos_Zelaya), President of Nicaragua, who later renamed the territory "[Zelaya Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelaya_Department)".[^cite_note-85]

Throughout the late 19th century, the United States and several European powers considered various schemes to link the Pacific Ocean to the Atlantic by building a [canal across Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_Canal).[^cite_note-86]

### United States occupation (1909–1933)

See also: [United States occupation of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_occupation_of_Nicaragua)

In 1909, the United States supported the forces rebelling against President Zelaya. U.S. motives included differences over the proposed [Nicaragua Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_Canal), Nicaragua's potential to destabilize the region, and Zelaya's attempts to regulate foreign access to Nicaraguan natural resources. On 18 November 1909, U.S. warships were sent to the area after 500 revolutionaries (including two Americans) were executed by order of Zelaya. The U.S. justified the intervention by claiming to protect U.S. lives and property. Zelaya resigned later that year.

In August 1912, the President of Nicaragua, [Adolfo Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_D%C3%ADaz), requested the secretary of war, General [Luis Mena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mena_\(Nicaraguan_politician\)), to resign for fear he was leading an insurrection. Mena fled Managua with his brother, the chief of police of Managua, to start an insurrection. After Mena's troops captured steam boats of an American company, the U.S. delegation asked President Díaz to ensure the safety of American citizens and property during the insurrection. He replied he could not, and asked the U.S. to intervene in the conflict.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

[U.S. Marines](17349325.md) occupied Nicaragua from 1912 to 1933,[^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 111, 197 </sup> except for a nine-month period beginning in 1925. In 1914, the [Bryan–Chamorro Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan%E2%80%93Chamorro_Treaty) was signed, giving the U.S. control over a proposed canal through Nicaragua, as well as leases for potential canal defenses.[^cite_note-89] After the U.S. Marines left, another [violent conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_civil_war_\(1926%E2%80%9327\)) between Liberals and Conservatives in 1926 resulted in the return of U.S. Marines.[^cite_note-90]



Rebel leader [Augusto César Sandino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_C%C3%A9sar_Sandino) (center) in June 1929

From 1927 to 1933, rebel general [Augusto César Sandino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_C%C3%A9sar_Sandino) led a sustained guerrilla war against the regime and then against the [U.S. Marines](17349325.md), whom he fought for over five years.[^cite_note-91] When the Americans left in 1933, they set up the *[Guardia Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(Nicaragua\))* (national guard),[^cite_note-SY-92] a combined military and police force trained and equipped by the Americans and designed to be loyal to U.S. interests.

After the U.S. Marines withdrew from Nicaragua in January 1933, Sandino and the newly elected administration of President [Juan Bautista Sacasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Sacasa) reached an agreement that Sandino would cease his guerrilla activities in return for amnesty, a land grant for an agricultural colony, and retention of an armed band of 100 men for a year.[^cite_note-93] However, due to a growing hostility between Sandino and National Guard director [Anastasio Somoza García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasio_Somoza_Garc%C3%ADa) and a fear of armed opposition from Sandino, Somoza García ordered his assassination.[^cite_note-SY-92][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

Sacasa invited Sandino for dinner and to sign a peace treaty at the Presidential House on the night of 21 February 1934. After leaving the Presidential House, Sandino's car was stopped by National Guard soldiers and they kidnapped him. Later that night, Sandino was assassinated by National Guard soldiers. Later, hundreds of men, women, and children from Sandino's agricultural colony were murdered.[^cite_note-SomozaDynasty-96]

### Somoza dynasty (1936–1979)



President [Anastasio Somoza García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasio_Somoza_Garc%C3%ADa) (left) with [Dominican](8060.md) President [Rafael Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Trujillo) in 1952

Nicaragua has experienced several military dictatorships, the longest being the hereditary dictatorship of the [Somoza family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somoza_family), who ruled for 43 nonconsecutive years during the 20th century.[^cite_note-97] The Somoza family came to power in 1937 partly as a result of a U.S.-engineered pact in 1927 that stipulated the formation of the *Guardia Nacional* to replace the marines who had long reigned in the country.[^cite_note-98] Somoza García slowly eliminated officers in the national guard who might have stood in his way, and then deposed Sacasa and became president on 1 January 1937, in a [rigged election](382165.md).[^cite_note-SY-92]

In 1941, during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Nicaragua declared war on [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) (8 December), [Germany](21212.md) (11 December), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) (11 December), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria) (19 December), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_\(1920%E2%80%931946\)) (19 December) and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) (19 December). Only Romania reciprocated, declaring war on Nicaragua on the same day (19 December 1941).[^cite_note-99] No soldiers were sent to the war, but Somoza García confiscated properties held by [German Nicaraguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Nicaraguan) residents.[^cite_note-100] In 1945, Nicaragua was among the first countries to ratify the United Nations Charter.[^cite_note-101]

On 29 September 1956,[^cite_note-102] Somoza García was shot to death by [Rigoberto López Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigoberto_L%C3%B3pez_P%C3%A9rez), a 27-year-old Liberal Nicaraguan poet. [Luis Somoza Debayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Somoza_Debayle), the eldest son of the late president, was appointed president by the congress and officially took charge of the country.[^cite_note-SY-92] He is remembered by some as moderate, but after only a few years in power died of a heart attack. His successor as president was [René Schick Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Schick_Guti%C3%A9rrez), whom most Nicaraguans viewed "as nothing more than a puppet of the Somozas".[^cite_note-103] Somoza García's youngest son, [Anastasio Somoza Debayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasio_Somoza_Debayle), often referred to simply as "Somoza", became president in 1967.

An [earthquake in 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Nicaragua_earthquake) destroyed nearly 90% of Managua, including much of its infrastructure.[^cite_note-104] Instead of helping to rebuild the city, Somoza Debayle siphoned off relief money. The mishandling of relief money also prompted [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates) star [Roberto Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Clemente) to personally fly to Managua on 31 December 1972, but he died *en route* in an airplane accident.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] Even the economic elite were reluctant to support Somoza, as he had acquired monopolies in industries that were key to rebuilding the nation.[^cite_note-107]

The Somoza family was among a few families or groups of influential firms which reaped most of the benefits of the country's growth from the 1950s to the 1970s. When Somoza was deposed by the Sandinistas in 1979, the family's worth was estimated to be between \$500 million and \$1.5 billion.[^cite_note-108]

### Nicaraguan Revolution (1961–1990)

Main articles: [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution) and [History of Nicaragua (1979–1990)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nicaragua_\(1979%E2%80%931990\))



Celebrations of the 10th anniversary of the [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution) in [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua) in 1989

In 1961, [Carlos Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Fonseca) looked back to the historical figure of Sandino, and along with two other people, one of whom was believed to be Casimiro Sotelo, who was later assassinated, founded the [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN).[^cite_note-SY-92] After the 1972 earthquake and Somoza's apparent corruption, the ranks of the Sandinistas were flooded with young disaffected Nicaraguans who no longer had anything to lose.[^cite_note-Grinnell2007-109]

On December 27, 1974, a group of the FSLN, in an attempt to kidnap U.S. ambassador Turner Shelton, held some Managuan partygoers hostage after killing the party's host, former agriculture minister Jose Maria Castillo, until the Somoza government met their demands for a large ransom and free transport to [Cuba](5042481.md). Somoza granted the demand, and then subsequently sent his national guard out into the countryside to look for the kidnappers, who were described by opponents as terrorists.[^cite_note-110]

On 10 January 1978, [Pedro Joaquín Chamorro Cardenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Joaqu%C3%ADn_Chamorro_Cardenal), the editor of the national newspaper *[La Prensa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Prensa_\(Managua\))* and ardent opponent of Somoza, was assassinated.[^cite_note-AC-111] It is alleged that the planners and perpetrators of the murder were at the highest echelons of the Somoza regime.[^cite_note-AC-111]

The Sandinistas forcefully took power in July 1979, ousting Somoza, and prompting the exodus of the majority of Nicaragua's middle class, wealthy landowners, and professionals, many of whom settled in the United States.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] The Carter administration decided to work with the new government, while attaching a provision for aid forfeiture if it was found to be assisting insurgencies in neighboring countries.[^cite_note-115] Somoza fled the country, and eventually ended up in [Paraguay](23420.md), where he was assassinated in September 1980, allegedly by members of the Argentinian Revolutionary Workers' Party.[^cite_note-116]

In 1980, the [Carter administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Jimmy_Carter) provided \$60 million in aid to Nicaragua under the Sandinistas, but the aid was suspended when the administration obtained evidence of Nicaraguan shipment of arms to El Salvadoran rebels.[^cite_note-117] Most people sided with Nicaragua against the Sandinistas.[^cite_note-118]

#### Contras (1981–1990)

Main article: [Contras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras)



A group of [Contras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras) in the former [Zelaya Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelaya_Department) on Nicaragua's west coast on the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md). Women [commonly joined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role_of_women_in_the_Nicaraguan_Revolution) the Contras as combatants.

[^cite_note-119]

In response to the Sandinistas, various rebel groups collectively known as the "[Contras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras)" were formed to oppose the new government. The [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ronald_Reagan) ultimately authorized the [CIA](5183633.md) to [help the Contra rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_and_state-sponsored_terrorism#Nicaragua) with funding, weapons, and training.[^cite_note-CA-120] The Contras operated from camps in the neighboring countries of Honduras to the north and Costa Rica to the south.[^cite_note-CA-120]

They engaged in a systematic campaign of terror among rural Nicaraguans to disrupt the social reform projects of the Sandinistas. Several historians have criticized the Contra campaign and the [Reagan administration's support for the Contras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Ronald_Reagan_administration), citing the brutality and numerous human rights violations of the Contras, alleging that health centers, schools, and cooperatives were destroyed by rebels,[^cite_note-121] and that murder, rape, and torture occurred on a large scale in Contra-dominated areas.[^cite_note-122] The U.S. also carried out a campaign of economic sabotage, and disrupted shipping by planting underwater mines in Nicaragua's port of [Corinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinto,_Nicaragua),[^cite_note-123] an action [condemned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_v._United_States) by the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) as illegal.[^cite_note-124] The court also found that the U.S. encouraged acts contrary to humanitarian law by producing the manual *[Psychological Operations in Guerrilla Warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_Operations_in_Guerrilla_Warfare)* and disseminating it to the Contras.[^cite_note-125] The manual, among other things, advised on how to rationalize killings of civilians.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-126] The U.S. also sought to place economic pressure on the Sandinistas, and the Reagan administration imposed a full trade embargo.[^cite_note-127]

The Sandinistas were also accused of human rights abuses including torture, disappearances and mass executions.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] The [Inter-American Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-American_Commission_on_Human_Rights) investigated abuses by Sandinista forces, including an execution of 35 to 40 [Miskitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people) in December 1981,[^cite_note-130] and an execution of 75 people in November 1984.[^cite_note-131]

In the [Nicaraguan general elections of 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_general_election,_1984), which were judged by at least one visiting 30-person delegation of NGO representatives to have been free and fair,[^cite_note-132] the Sandinistas won the parliamentary election and their leader [Daniel Ortega](8778.md) won the presidential election.[^cite_note-133] The Reagan administration criticized the elections as a "sham" based on the claim that [Arturo Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Cruz), the candidate nominated by the [Coordinadora Democrática Nicaragüense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinadora_Democr%C3%A1tica_Nicarag%C3%BCense), comprising three right wing political parties, did not participate in the elections. However, the administration privately argued against Cruz's participation for fear that his involvement would legitimize the elections, and thus weaken the case for American aid to the Contras.[^cite_note-134]

In 1983 the U.S. Congress prohibited federal funding of the Contras, but the Reagan administration illegally continued to back them by covertly selling arms to [Iran](14653.md) and channeling the proceeds to the Contras in the [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair), for which several members of the Reagan administration were convicted of felonies.[^cite_note-135] The [International Court of Justice](14918.md), in regard to the case of [Nicaragua v. United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_v._United_States) in 1986, found, "the United States of America was under an obligation to make reparation to the Republic of Nicaragua for all injury caused to Nicaragua by certain breaches of obligations under customary international law and treaty-law committed by the United States of America".[^cite_note-icj-cij-136] During the war between the Contras and the Sandinistas, 30,000 people were killed.[^cite_note-137]

### Post-war (1990–2018)



In 1990, after the [Contra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras) war, [Violeta Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_Chamorro) became the first woman president democratically elected in the history of the [Americas](29833.md).



Flooding in Lake Managua after [Hurricane Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch) in 1998



[Nicaraguan protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932020_Nicaraguan_protests) in May 2018

In the [1990 Nicaraguan general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Nicaraguan_general_election), a coalition of anti-Sandinista parties from both the left and right of the political spectrum led by [Violeta Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_Chamorro), the widow of Pedro Joaquín Chamorro Cardenal, defeated the Sandinistas. The defeat shocked the Sandinistas, who had expected to win.[^cite_note-138]

Exit polls of Nicaraguans reported Chamorro's victory over [Ortega](8778.md) was achieved with a 55% majority.[^cite_note-139] Chamorro was the first woman president of Nicaragua. Ortega vowed he would govern *desde abajo* (from below).[^cite_note-140] Chamorro came to office with an economy in ruins, primarily because of the financial and social costs of the Contra War with the Sandinista-led government.[^cite_note-141] In the [1996 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Nicaraguan_general_election), Daniel Ortega and the Sandinistas of the FSLN lost again, this time to [Arnoldo Alemán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnoldo_Alem%C3%A1n) of the [Constitutional Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalist_Liberal_Party_\(Nicaragua\)) (PLC).

In [the 2001 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Nicaraguan_general_election), the PLC again defeated the FSLN, with Alemán's Vice President [Enrique Bolaños](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Bola%C3%B1os) succeeding him as president. However, Alemán was convicted and sentenced in 2003 to 20 years in prison for [embezzlement](209411.md), [money laundering](19390.md), and corruption;[^cite_note-142] liberal and Sandinista parliament members combined to strip the presidential powers of President Bolaños and his ministers, calling for his resignation and threatening [impeachment](15328.md). The Sandinistas said they no longer supported Bolaños after U.S. Secretary of State [Colin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Powell) told Bolaños to distance from the FSLN.[^cite_note-143] This "slow motion *coup d'état*" was averted partially by pressure from the Central American presidents, who vowed not to recognize any movement that removed Bolaños; the U.S., the OAS, and the [European Union](9317.md) also opposed the action.[^cite_note-144]

Nicaragua briefly participated in the [Iraq War](5043324.md) in 2004 as part of the [Plus Ultra Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_Ultra_Brigade), a military contingent of mixed personnel.[^cite_note-AP_2004-04-29-145]

Before [the general elections on 5 November 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Nicaraguan_general_election), the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Nicaragua) passed a bill further restricting [abortion in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Nicaragua).[^cite_note-146] As a result, Nicaragua is one of five countries in the world where abortion is illegal with no exceptions.[^cite_note-147]

Legislative and presidential elections took place on 5 November 2006. Ortega returned to the presidency with 37.99% of the vote. This percentage was enough to win the presidency outright, because of a change in electoral law which lowered the percentage requiring a runoff election from 45% to 35% (with a 5% margin of victory).[^cite_note-148] [Nicaragua's 2011 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nicaraguan_general_election) resulted in the re-election of Ortega, with a landslide 62.46% of the vote. In 2014 the National Assembly approved changes to the constitution allowing Ortega to run for a third successive term.[^cite_note-149]

In November 2016, [Ortega was elected for his third consecutive term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nicaraguan_general_election) (his fourth overall). International monitoring of the elections was initially prohibited, and as a result the validity of the [elections](1442381.md) has been disputed, but observation by the [OAS](22788.md) was announced in October.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] Ortega was reported by Nicaraguan election officials as having received 72% of the vote. However, the [Broad Front for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_for_Democracy) (FAD), having promoted boycotts of the elections, claimed that 70% of voters had abstained (while election officials claimed 65.8% participation).[^cite_note-152]

In April 2018, [demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932020_Nicaraguan_protests) were held to oppose a decree increasing taxes and reducing benefits in the country's pension system. Local independent press organizations documented at least 19 dead and over 100 missing in the ensuing conflict.[^cite_note-153] A reporter from NPR spoke to protestors who explained that while the initial issue was the pension reforms, the uprisings that spread across the country reflected many grievances about the government's time in office, and that the fight is for President Ortega and his vice president, his wife, to step down.[^cite_note-154]

24 April 2018 marked the day of the greatest march in opposition of the Sandinista party. On 2 May 2018, university student leaders made a public announcement giving the government seven days to set a date and time for a dialogue that was promised to the people due to the recent events of repression. The students also scheduled another peaceful protest march on that same day. As of May 2018, estimates of the death toll were as high as 63, many of them student protesters, and the wounded totaled more than 400.[^cite_note-155]

Following a working visit from 17 to 21 May, the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights adopted precautionary measures aimed at protecting members of the student movement and their families after testimonies indicated the majority of them had suffered acts of violence and death threats for their participation.[^cite_note-156] In the last week of May, thousands who accuse Mr. Ortega and his wife of acting like dictators joined in resuming anti-government rallies after attempted peace talks have remained unresolved.[^cite_note-157] Open suppression of political dissent and more militarized policing began in April 2018, but the onset of repression was gradual.[^cite_note-158] On 12 October 2024, Nicaragua broke ties with [Israel](9282173.md) in response to the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md), condemning Israel's leaders as "fascist" and "[genocidal](75668427.md)".[^cite_note-159]

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Nicaragua) and [Climate of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Nicaragua)

See also: [Volcanoes of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanoes_of_Nicaragua)



Topographic map



A [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) map of Nicaragua

Nicaragua occupies a landmass of 130,967 km<sup>2</sup> (50,567 sq mi), which makes it slightly larger than England. Nicaragua has three distinct geographical regions: the Pacific lowlands – fertile valleys which the Spanish colonists settled, the [Amerrisque Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerrisque_Mountains) (North-central highlands), and the Mosquito Coast ([Caribbean lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Lowlands)).

The low plains of the Caribbean Coast are 97 km (60 mi) wide in areas. They have long been exploited for their natural resources.

On the Pacific side of Nicaragua are the two largest freshwater lakes in Central America—[Lake Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Managua) and [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua). Surrounding these lakes and extending to their northwest along the [rift valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift_valley) of the [Gulf of Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Fonseca) are fertile lowland plains, with soil highly enriched by [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash) from nearby [volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) of the central highlands. Nicaragua's abundance of biologically significant and unique [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem) contribute to [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica)'s designation as a [biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot). Nicaragua has made efforts to become less dependent on fossil fuels, and it expects to acquire 90% of its energy from renewable resources by 2020.[^cite_note-bbc-160][^cite_note-161]

Nicaragua was one of the few countries that did not enter an [INDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intended_Nationally_Determined_Contributions) at [COP21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COP21).[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] Nicaragua initially chose not to join the Paris Climate Accord because it felt that "much more action is required" by individual countries on restricting global temperature rise.[^cite_note-bbc-160] In October 2017, Nicaragua made the decision to join the agreement.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] It ratified this agreement in November 2017.[^cite_note-167]

Nearly one fifth of Nicaragua is designated as [protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_Nicaragua) like national parks, nature reserves, and biological reserves. The country had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 3.63/10, ranking it 146th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-168] [Geophysically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysical), Nicaragua is surrounded by the [Caribbean Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Plate), an [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_crust) [tectonic plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_plate) underlying Central America, and the [Cocos Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_Plate). Since Central America is a major [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) zone, Nicaragua hosts most of the [Central American Volcanic Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Volcanic_Arc). On 9 June 2021, Nicaragua launched a new volcanic supersite research in strengthening the monitoring and surveillance of the country's 21 active volcanoes.

### Pacific lowlands



Nicaragua is known as "the land of lakes and volcanoes"; pictured is [Concepción volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concepci%C3%B3n_\(volcano\)), seen from [Maderas volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maderas).



Peñas Blancas, part of the [Bosawás Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaw%C3%A1s_Biosphere_Reserve) and located northeast of [Jinotega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinotega) in northeastern Nicaragua, is the second-largest rainforest in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) after the Amazonian Rainforest in [Brazil](3383.md).

In the west of the country, these lowlands consist of a broad, hot, fertile plain. Punctuating this plain are several large volcanoes of the [Cordillera Los Maribios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Los_Maribios) mountain range, including [Mombacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombacho) just outside Granada, and [Momotombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momotombo) near León. The lowland area runs from the [Gulf of Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Fonseca) to Nicaragua's Pacific border with Costa Rica south of [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua). Lake Nicaragua is the largest freshwater lake in Central America (20th largest in the world),[^cite_note-169] and is home to some of the world's rare freshwater sharks ([Nicaraguan shark](277607.md)).[^cite_note-170] The Pacific lowlands region is the most populous, with over half of the nation's population.[^cite_note-171]

The eruptions of western Nicaragua's 40 volcanoes, many of which are still active, have sometimes devastated settlements but also have enriched the land with layers of fertile ash. The geologic activity that produces vulcanism also breeds powerful earthquakes. Tremors occur regularly throughout the Pacific zone, and earthquakes have nearly destroyed the capital city, Managua, more than once.[^cite_note-gr1-172][^cite_note-173]

Most of the Pacific zone is *[tierra caliente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_caliente)*, the "hot land" of tropical [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America) at elevations under 610 meters (2,000 ft). Temperatures remain virtually constant throughout the year, with highs ranging between 29.4 and 32.2 °C (85 and 90 °F). After a dry season lasting from November to April, rains begin in May and continue to October, giving the Pacific lowlands 1,016 to 1,524 millimeters (40 to 60 in) of precipitation.[^cite_note-gr1-172]

Good soils and a favorable climate combine to make western Nicaragua the country's economic and demographic center. The southwestern shore of Lake Nicaragua lies within 24 kilometers (15 mi) of the Pacific Ocean. Thus the lake and the San Juan River were often proposed in the 19th century as the longest part of a canal route across the Central American isthmus. Canal proposals were periodically revived in the 20th and 21st centuries.[^cite_note-gr1-172][^cite_note-174] Roughly a century after the opening of the [Panama Canal](24849.md), the prospect of a Nicaraguan [ecocanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocanal) remains a topic of interest.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178]

In addition to its beach and resort communities, the Pacific lowlands contains most of Nicaragua's Spanish colonial architecture and artifacts. Cities such as León and [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua) abound in colonial architecture; founded in 1524, Granada is the oldest colonial city in the Americas.[^cite_note-179]

### North-central highlands



The [Somoto Canyon National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somoto_Canyon_National_Monument) in [Somoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somoto) in the [Madriz Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madriz_Department) in northern Nicaragua

Northern Nicaragua is the most diversified region producing coffee, cattle, milk products, vegetables, wood, gold, and flowers. Its extensive forests, rivers and geography are suited for ecotourism.

The central highlands are a significantly less populated and economically developed area in the north, between Lake Nicaragua and the Caribbean. Forming the country's [tierra templada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_templada), or "temperate land", at elevations between 610 and 1,524 meters (2,000 and 5,000 ft), the highlands enjoy mild temperatures with daily highs of 23.9 to 26.7 °C (75 to 80 °F). This region has a longer, wetter rainy season than the Pacific lowlands, making erosion a problem on its steep slopes. Rugged terrain, poor soils, and low population density characterize the area as a whole, but the northwestern valleys are fertile and well settled.[^cite_note-gr1-172]

The area has a cooler climate than the Pacific lowlands. About a quarter of the country's agriculture takes place in this region, with coffee grown on the higher slopes. [Oaks](39696.md), [pines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), [moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss), [ferns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern) and [orchids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchidaceae) are abundant in the [cloud forests](58903.md) of the region.

Bird life in the forests of the central region includes [resplendent quetzals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resplendent_quetzal), [goldfinches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_goldfinch), [hummingbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hummingbird), [jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay) and [toucanets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_toucanet).

### Caribbean lowlands



The dividing line between the Caribbean lowlands and the rest of Nicaragua. The three regions in the east make up 57% of the land area but only 15% of the population ([Río San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_San_Juan_Department), [North Caribbean Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region) and [South Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region)).

This large [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) region is irrigated by several large rivers and is sparsely populated. The area has 57% of the territory of the nation and most of its mineral resources. It has been heavily exploited, but much natural diversity remains. The [Rio Coco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_River) is the largest river in Central America; it forms the border with Honduras. The Caribbean coastline is much more sinuous than its generally straight Pacific counterpart; lagoons and deltas make it very irregular.

Nicaragua's [Bosawás Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaw%C3%A1s_Biosphere_Reserve) is in the region of [Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast), part of which is located in the municipality of [Siuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siuna); it protects 7,300 square kilometers (1,800,000 acres) of Mosquitia's forest – almost 7% of the country's area – making it the largest rainforest north of the [Amazon](48139.md) in Brazil.[^cite_note-180]

The municipalities of [Siuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siuna), [Rosita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosita,_Nicaragua), and [Bonanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonanza,_Nicaragua), known as the "Mining Triangle", are located in the region known as the [North Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region), in [Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast). Bonanza still contains an active gold mine owned by HEMCO. Siuna and Rosita do not have active mines but panning for gold is still very common in the region.

Nicaragua's tropical east coast is very different from the rest of the country. The climate is predominantly tropical, with high temperature and high humidity. Around the area's principal city of Bluefields, English is widely spoken along with the official Spanish. The population more closely resembles that found in many typical Caribbean ports than the rest of Nicaragua.[^cite_note-181]

A great variety of birds can be observed including [eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle), [toucans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toucan), [parakeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakeet) and [macaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaw). Other animal life in the area includes different species of [monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey), [anteaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anteater), white-tailed [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer) and [tapirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapir).[^cite_note-182]

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Fauna of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Nicaragua)



[Guardabarranco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardabarranco) ("ravine-guard") is Nicaragua's national bird.

Nicaragua is home to a rich variety of plants and animals. Nicaragua is located in the middle of the Americas and this privileged location has enabled the country to serve as host to a great biodiversity. This factor, along with the weather and light altitudinal variations, allows the country to harbor 248 species of amphibians and reptiles, 183 species of mammals, 705 bird species, 640 fish species, and about 5,796 species of plants.

The region of great forests is located on the eastern side of the country. Rainforests are found in the [Río San Juan Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_San_Juan_Department) and in the autonomous regions of [RAAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region) and [RAAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caribbean_Coast_Autonomous_Region). This biome groups together the greatest biodiversity in the country and is largely protected by the [Indio Maíz Biological Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indio_Ma%C3%ADz_Biological_Reserve) in the south and the [Bosawás Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaw%C3%A1s_Biosphere_Reserve) in the north. The Nicaraguan jungles, which represent about 9,700 square kilometers (2.4 million acres), are considered the lungs of Central America and comprise the second largest-sized rainforest of the Americas.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

There are currently 78 protected areas in Nicaragua, covering more than 22,000 square kilometers (8,500 sq mi), or about 17% of its landmass. These include [wildlife refuges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_refuge) and [nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve) that shelter a wide range of [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem). There are more than 1,400 animal species classified thus far in Nicaragua. Some 12,000 species of plants have been [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_classification) thus far in Nicaragua, with an estimated 5,000 species not yet classified.[^cite_note-185]

The [bull shark](277607.md) is a species of shark that can survive for an extended period of time in fresh water. It can be found in [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua) and the [San Juan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_\(Nicaragua\)), where it is often referred to as the "Nicaragua shark".[^cite_note-186] Nicaragua has recently banned freshwater fishing of the Nicaragua shark and the [sawfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawfish) in response to the declining populations of these animals.[^cite_note-187]

Politics of Nicaragua takes place in a framework of a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) republic, whereby the [President of Nicaragua](302862.md) is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md), and of a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) is exercised by the government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) is vested in both the government and the [national assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Nicaragua). The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) makes up the third branch of government.

Since [Daniel Ortega](8778.md)'s election in 2006, liberal democratic norms and individual rights in practice have deteriorated, a process known as [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding).[^cite_note-188] Parties other than the ruling [FSLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) have been repressed through arbitrary arrest and detention of opposition candidates and activists. Most government jobs *de facto* require membership in the FSLN. Opposition media has been repressed through arrests of journalists and seizure of broadcasting and printing materials.[^cite_note-189]

Between 2007 and 2009, Nicaragua's major political parties discussed the possibility of going from a presidential system to a parliamentary system. It was argued that the reason for this proposal was to find a legal way for President Ortega to stay in power after January 2012, when his second and last government period was expected to end. Ortega was [re-elected to a third term in November 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nicaraguan_general_election), and [a fourth in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nicaraguan_general_election); both elections were tainted by credible reports of large-scale fraud, voter intimidation, and politically motivated arrests of opposition party leaders. Independent observers were barred from the polls. The [OAS](22788.md), United States, and European Union all described the 2021 election as a "sham" due to these issues.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]

The [constitutional amendments of 2025](11109678.md#Constitutional_amendments_of_2025) established sweeping government changes.[^cite_note-\:2-192] The reform defined Nicaragua as a revolutionary socialist state with the flag of the [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) as a national symbol, disallowed transgressions against the "principles of security, peace, and wellbeing established in the Constitution", and declared opponents to these as "traitors to the homeland", whether inside or outside national territory. This reform also increased presidential powers by establishing a co-presidency capable of "coordinating" with other "state organs" such as the [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), and [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)). Among other changes, the reform increases the presidential term from 5 to 6 years, establishes a voluntary civilian police as "an auxiliary body in support of the National Police", and limits any speech or religious practices that "violate public order" and constitutional principles.[^cite_note-193]

The reform has been widely criticized by the OAS and opposition figures as officializing repressive practices, eliminating checks and balances, and establishing a "bicephalous dictatorship".[^cite_note-\:2-192] It has been condemned as "the most flagrant violation of human rights" since "it is about the constitution".[^cite_note-\:2-192] The OAS further criticized the reform as "illegitimate" and as an "aberrant form of institutionalization a matrimonial dictatorship".[^cite_note-\:2-192] Other outlets, including the Uruguayan Diálogo Político, have condemned the reforms as installing a totalitarian government.[^cite_note-Lira-8] The [V-Dem Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Institute) described Nicaragua as an electoral [autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy) with [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding).[^cite_note-j496-194]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Nicaragua)

Nicaragua pursues an independent foreign policy. [Nicaragua is in territorial disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_disputes_of_Nicaragua) with Colombia over the [Archipelago de San Andrés y Providencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago_de_San_Andr%C3%A9s_y_Providencia) and [Quita Sueño Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quita_Sue%C3%B1o_Bank) and with Costa Rica over a boundary dispute involving the [San Juan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_\(Nicaragua\)).

Since 2019, [human rights in Nicaragua](63105642.md) have been rated [not free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World).

On 12 October 2022, Nicaragua voted against condemning [Russia](25391.md) for the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-196]

### Military

Main article: [Nicaraguan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Armed_Forces)

The [Nicaraguan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Armed_Forces) consist of various military contingents. Nicaragua has an [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Army), [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Navy) and an [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Air_Force). There are roughly 14,000 active duty personnel, which is much less compared to the numbers seen during the [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution). Although the army has had a rough military history, a portion of its forces, which were known as the [national guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(Nicaragua\)), became integrated with what is now the [National Police of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polic%C3%ADa_Nacional_\(Nicaragua\)). In essence, the police became a *[gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie)*. The National Police of Nicaragua are rarely, if ever, labeled as a *[gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie)*. The other elements and manpower that were not devoted to the national police were sent over to cultivate the new Army of Nicaragua.

The age to serve in the armed forces is 17 and [conscription](5735.md) is not imminent. As of 2006, the military budget was roughly 0.7% of Nicaragua's expenditures.

### Law enforcement

Main article: [Law enforcement in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Nicaragua)



[National Police of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_of_Nicaragua)

The [National Police of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_of_Nicaragua) Force (in Spanish: La Policía Nacional Nicaragüense) is the national police of Nicaragua. The force is in charge of regular police functions and, at times, works in conjunction with the Nicaraguan military, making it an indirect and rather subtle version of a gendarmerie. However, the Nicaraguan National Police work separately and have a different established set of norms than the nation's military. According to a recent U.S. Department of State report, corruption is endemic, especially within law enforcement and the judiciary, and arbitrary arrests, torture, and harsh prison conditions are the norm.[^cite_note-Nicaragua-197]

Nicaragua has one of the lowest intentional homicide rates in Central America, according to the [United Nations Development Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Program), with a homicide rate of 11 per 100,000 inhabitants as of 2021.[^cite_note-Intentional_homicides_(per_100,000_people)_-_Nicaragua-198]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Nicaragua)



Nicaraguan [GDP](12594.md) per capita, 1920 to 2018



[Coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) is one of the Nicaragua's largest exports. It is grown in [Jinotega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinotega), [Esteli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteli), [Nueva Segovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Segovia_Department), [Matagalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matagalpa), and [Madriz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madriz_Department), and exported worldwide through North America, Latin America, Europe, Asia, and Australia. [Nestlé](160227.md) and [Starbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starbucks) buy Nicaraguan coffee.

Nicaragua is one of the poorest countries in [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas),[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201] [only ahead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_American_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)_per_capita) of [Haiti](13373.md) in terms of nominal per capita GDP. Its gross domestic product (GDP) in [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) (PPP) in 2023 was estimated at US\$56.7 billion.[^cite_note-202] In 2020, agriculture represented 15.5% of GDP, the highest percentage in Central America.[^cite_note-203] Remittances account for over 15% of its GDP. Almost one billion dollars are sent annually to the country by Nicaraguans living abroad.[^cite_note-204] The economy grew at a rate of about 4% in 2011.[^cite_note-cia-15] By 2019, given restrictive taxes and a civil conflict, it recorded a negative annual growth rate of −3.9%.

Nicaragua is known for its high-quality, high demand, and fine-flavor [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), and is the largest producer and exporter of cocoa in Central America, producing between 10,000 and 14,000 metric tons annually.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] In 2015, Nicaragua was recognized as 100% fine cocoa origin by the International Cocoa Organization.[^cite_note-auto-207] In addition, 85% of Nicaragua's cocoa production is high-quality and fine-flavor, with 98% of its producers being small-scale farmers.[^cite_note-auto1-208] Furthermore, Nicaraguan cacao and chocolate makers have won over 150 international awards from the two most prestigious organizations, the International Chocolate Awards and the Academy of Chocolate.[^cite_note-auto-207][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212] 37% of Nicaraguan cocoa is exported to European markets.[^cite_note-auto1-208][^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

According to the World Bank, Nicaragua ranked as the 123rd out of 190 best economy for starting a business.[^cite_note-215] In 2007, Nicaragua's economy was labelled "62.7% free" by the conservative [think tank](37101.md) [Heritage Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Heritage_Foundation), with high levels of fiscal, government, labor, investment, financial, and trade freedom.[^cite_note-216] It ranked as the 61st [freest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_Economic_Freedom), and 14th (of 29) in the Americas. Nicaragua was ranked 130th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]

Nicaragua is primarily an agricultural country; 29% of its workforce is employed in agriculture in 2020[^cite_note-219] and 60% of its total exports, which annually yield approximately US\$300 million.[^cite_note-220] Nearly two-thirds of the coffee crop comes from the northern part of the central highlands, in the area north and east of the town of Estelí.[^cite_note-gr1-172] Tobacco, grown in the same northern highlands region as coffee, has become an increasingly important cash crop since the 1990s, with annual exports of leaf and cigars in the neighborhood of \$200 million per year.[^cite_note-221]

Soil erosion and pollution from the heavy use of pesticides have become serious concerns in the cotton district. Yields and exports have both been declining since 1985.[^cite_note-gr1-172] Today most of Nicaragua's bananas are grown in the northwestern part of the country near the port of Corinto; sugarcane is also grown in the same district.[^cite_note-gr1-172] [Cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), a root crop somewhat similar to the potato, is an important food in tropical regions. Cassava is also the main ingredient in tapioca pudding.[^cite_note-gr1-172]

Nicaragua's agricultural sector has benefited because of the country's strong ties to [Venezuela](32374.md). It is estimated that Venezuela will import approximately \$200 million in agricultural goods.[^cite_note-222] In the 1990s, the government initiated efforts to diversify agriculture. Some of the new export-oriented crops were peanuts, [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame), melons, and onions.[^cite_note-gr1-172]

Fishing boats on the Caribbean side bring shrimp as well as lobsters into processing plants at Puerto Cabezas, Bluefields, and Laguna de Perlas.[^cite_note-gr1-172] A turtle fishery thrived on the Caribbean coast before it collapsed from [overexploitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overexploitation).[^cite_note-gr1-172]

Mining is becoming a major industry in Nicaragua,[^cite_note-223] contributing less than 1% of gross domestic product (GDP). Restrictions are being placed on lumbering due to increased environmental concerns about destruction of the rain forests. But lumbering continues despite these obstacles; indeed, a single hardwood tree may be worth thousands of dollars.[^cite_note-gr1-172]

During the war between the Contras and the government of the [Sandinistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) in the 1980s, much of the country's infrastructure was damaged or destroyed.[^cite_note-224] Transportation throughout the nation is often inadequate. For example, it was until recently impossible to travel all the way by highway from Managua to the Caribbean coast. A new road between Nueva Guinea and Bluefields was completed in 2019 and allows regular bus service to the capital.[^cite_note-225] The Centroamérica power plant on the [Tuma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuma_River) in the Central highlands has been expanded, and other hydroelectric projects have been undertaken to help provide electricity to the nation's newer industries.[^cite_note-gr1-172] Nicaragua has long been considered as a possible site for a [new canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_Canal) that could supplement the Panama Canal, connecting the Caribbean Sea (and therefore the Atlantic Ocean) with the Pacific Ocean.

Nicaragua's minimum wage is among the lowest in the Americas and in the world.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229] Remittances are equivalent to roughly 15% of the country's gross domestic product.[^cite_note-cia-15] Growth in the *maquila* sector slowed in the first decade of the 21st century with rising competition from Asian markets, particularly China.[^cite_note-gr1-172] Land is the traditional basis of wealth in Nicaragua, with great fortunes coming from the export of staples such as coffee, cotton, beef, and sugar. Almost all of the upper class and nearly a quarter of the middle class are substantial landowners.

A 1985 government study classified 69.4 percent of the population as poor on the basis that they were unable to satisfy one or more of their basic needs in housing, sanitary services (water, sewage, and garbage collection), education, and employment. The defining standards for this study were very low; housing was considered substandard if it was constructed of discarded materials with dirt floors or if it was occupied by more than four persons per room.

Rural workers are dependent on agricultural wage labor, especially in coffee and cotton. Only a small fraction hold permanent jobs. Most are migrants who follow crops during the harvest period and find other work during the off-season. The "lower" peasants are typically smallholders without sufficient land to sustain a family; they also join the harvest labor force. The "upper" peasants have sufficient resources to be economically independent. They produce enough surplus, beyond their personal needs, to allow them to participate in the national and world markets.

The urban lower class is characterized by the informal sector of the economy. The informal sector consists of small-scale enterprises that utilize traditional technologies and operate outside the legal regime of labor protections and taxation. Workers in the informal sector are self-employed, unsalaried family workers or employees of small-enterprises, and they are generally poor.

Nicaragua's informal sector workers include tinsmiths, mattress makers, seamstresses, bakers, shoemakers, and carpenters; people who take in laundry and ironing or prepare food for sale in the streets; and thousands of peddlers, owners of small businesses (often operating out of their own homes), and market stall operators. Some work alone, but others labor in the small talleres (workshops/factories) that are responsible for a large share of the country's industrial production. Because informal sector earnings are generally very low, few families can subsist on one income.[^cite_note-Nicaragua_–_SOCIETY-230]

Like most Latin American nations Nicaragua is also characterized by a very small upper-class, roughly 2% of the population, that is very wealthy and wields the political and economic power in the country that is not in the hands of foreign corporations and private industries. These families are oligarchical in nature and have ruled Nicaragua for generations and their wealth is politically and economically horizontally and vertically integrated.

Nicaragua is currently a member of the [Bolivarian Alliance for the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_Alliance_for_the_Americas), also known as ALBA. ALBA has proposed creating a new currency, the [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUCRE_\(currency\)), for use among its members. In essence, this means that the Nicaraguan córdoba will be replaced with the Sucre. Other nations that will follow a similar pattern include: [Venezuela](32374.md), Ecuador, [Bolivia](3462.md), Honduras, [Cuba](5042481.md), Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica) and [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md).[^cite_note-231]

Nicaragua is considering construction of a canal linking the Atlantic to the Pacific Ocean, which President Daniel Ortega has said will give Nicaragua its "economic independence".[^cite_note-232] Scientists have raised concerns about environmental impacts, but the government has maintained that the canal will benefit the country by creating new jobs and potentially increasing its annual growth to an average of 8% per year.[^cite_note-233] The project was scheduled to begin construction in December 2014,[^cite_note-234] however the [Nicaragua Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_Canal) has yet to be started.[^cite_note-235]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Nicaragua)



A [Royal Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Caribbean_International) [cruise ship](314855.md) docked near the beach at [San Juan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_del_Sur) in southern Nicaragua



[2,100-year-old human footprints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_footprints_of_Acahualinca), called "Huellas de Acahualinca" and preserved in volcanic mud near [Lake Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Managua)



[Apoyo Lagoon Natural Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoyo_Lagoon_Natural_Reserve), a nature reserve located between the departments of [Masaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaya) and [Granada](38264.md)



The [Solentiname Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solentiname_Islands), tropical islands in [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua), which are home to 76 bird species and are a growing ecotourism destination

By 2006, tourism became the second-largest industry in Nicaragua.[^cite_note-236] Previously, tourism had grown about 70% nationwide during a period of 7 years, with rates of 10%–16% annually.[^cite_note-C2-237] The increase and growth led to the income from tourism to rise more than 300% over a period of 10 years.[^cite_note-238] The growth in tourism has also positively affected the agricultural, commercial, and finance industries, as well as the construction industry. President [Daniel Ortega](8778.md) has stated his intention to use tourism to combat poverty throughout the country.[^cite_note-Nica-239] The results for Nicaragua's tourism-driven economy have been significant, with the nation welcoming one million tourists in a calendar year for the first time in its history in 2010.[^cite_note-240]

Every year about 60,000 U.S. citizens visit Nicaragua, primarily business people, tourists, and those visiting relatives.[^cite_note-241] Some 5,300 people from the U.S. reside in Nicaragua. The majority of tourists who visit Nicaragua are from the U.S., Central or South America, and Europe. According to the Ministry of Tourism of Nicaragua (INTUR),[^cite_note-242] the colonial cities of [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua) and [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua) are the preferred spots for tourists. Also, the cities of [Masaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaya), [Rivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivas,_Nicaragua) and the likes of [San Juan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_del_Sur), [El Ostional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ostional), the [Fortress of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_of_the_Immaculate_Conception), [Ometepe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ometepe_Island), the [Mombacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombacho) volcano, and the [Corn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Islands) among other locations are the main tourist attractions. In addition, [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism), [sport fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_fishing) and [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing) attract many tourists to Nicaragua.

According to the *TV Noticias* news program, the main attractions in Nicaragua for tourists are the beaches, the scenic routes, the architecture of cities such as León and Granada, [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism), and [agritourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agritourism) particularly in northern Nicaragua.[^cite_note-C2-237] As a result of increased tourism, Nicaragua has seen its [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) increase by 79.1% from 2007 to 2009.[^cite_note-243]

Nicaragua is referred to as *"the land of lakes and volcanoes"* due to the number of lagoons and lakes, and the chain of volcanoes that runs from the north to the south along the country's Pacific side.[^cite_note-Brierley-29][^cite_note-Wallace-30][^cite_note-CNNTravel-244] Today, only 7 of the 50 volcanoes in Nicaragua are considered active. Many of these volcanoes offer some great possibilities for tourists with activities such as [hiking](147313.md), [climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climbing), [camping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camping), and [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_swimming) in crater lakes.

The [Apoyo Lagoon Natural Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoyo_Lagoon_Natural_Reserve) was created by the eruption of the Apoyo Volcano about 23,000 years ago, which left a huge 7 km-wide crater that gradually filled with water. It is surrounded by the old crater wall.[^cite_note-245] The rim of the lagoon is lined with restaurants, many of which have [kayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaks) available. Besides exploring the forest around it, many water sports are practiced in the lagoon, most notably [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaking).[^cite_note-246]

Sand skiing has become a popular attraction at the [Cerro Negro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Negro) volcano in [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua). Both dormant and active volcanoes can be climbed. Some of the most visited volcanoes include the [Masaya Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaya_Volcano), [Momotombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momotombo), [Mombacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombacho), [Cosigüina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosig%C3%BCina) and [Ometepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ometepe)'s [Maderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maderas) and [Concepción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concepci%C3%B3n_\(volcano\)).

[Ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) aims to be ecologically and socially conscious; it focuses on local culture, wilderness, and adventure. Nicaragua's ecotourism is growing with every passing year.[^cite_note-247] It boasts a number of ecotourist tours and perfect places for adventurers. Nicaragua has three eco-regions (the Pacific, Central, and Atlantic) which contain volcanoes, tropical rainforests, and agricultural land.[^cite_note-248] The majority of the eco-lodges and other environmentally-focused touristic destinations are found on Ometepe Island,[^cite_note-249] located in the middle of Lake Nicaragua just an hour's boat ride from Granada. While some are foreign-owned, others are owned by local families.

According to a 2014 research published in the journal *Genetics and Molecular Biology*, European ancestry predominates in 69% of Nicaraguans, followed by African ancestry in 20%, and lastly [indigenous ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) in 11%.[^cite_note-253] A Japanese research of "Genomic Components in America's demography" demonstrated that, on average, the ancestry of Nicaraguans is 58–62% European, 28% Native Nicaraguan, and 14% African, with a very small Near Eastern contribution.[^cite_note-254]

Non-genetic data from the *[CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_World_Factbook)* establish that from Nicaragua's 2016 population of 5,966,798, around 69% are [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), 17% [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Latin_Americans), 5% Native Nicaraguan, and 9% [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Latin_Americans) and other races.[^cite_note-cia-15] This fluctuates with changes in migration patterns. The population is 58% urban as of 2013.[^cite_note-255]

The capital [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua) is the biggest city, with an estimated population of 1,042,641 in 2016.[^cite_note-256] In 2005, over 5 million people lived in the Pacific, Central and North regions, and 700,000 in the Caribbean region.[^cite_note-CN-257]

There is a growing expatriate community,[^cite_note-258] the majority of whom move for business, investment or retirement from across the world, such as from the United States, Canada, Taiwan, and European countries; the majority have settled in Managua, [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua) and [San Juan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_del_Sur).

Many [Nicaraguans live abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Diaspora), particularly in Costa Rica, the United States, Spain, Canada, and other Central American countries.[^cite_note-259]

Nicaragua has a population growth rate of 1.5% as of 2013.[^cite_note-260] This is the result of one of the highest [birth rates](515898.md) in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md): 17.7 per 1,000 as of 2017.[^cite_note-261] The death rate was 4.7 per 1,000 during the same period according to the United Nations.[^cite_note-262]

The majority of the Nicaraguan population is composed of mestizos, roughly 69%, while 17% of Nicaragua's population is white,[^cite_note-ciaarchive-263] with the majority of them being of Spanish descent, while others are of German, Italian, English, Danish or French ancestry.

#### Europeans



Italian immigrant family in Nicaragua (1904)

European immigration to Nicaragua began in 1524 when [Francisco Hernández de Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Hern%C3%A1ndez_de_C%C3%B3rdoba_\(founder_of_Nicaragua\)) founded the cities of [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua) and [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua). From 1527 to 1821, Nicaragua was a province in the [Captaincy General of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala), thus most of the immigrants were from [Spain](26667.md) during this period.[^cite_note-264] In the late 19th century, the Nicaraguan government started to offer land to European immigrants from Germany and nearby countries in an attempt to promote their [coffee industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Nicaragua).[^cite_note-explore-nica-265] The [fincas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finca) were located mostly in rural [Matagalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matagalpa_Department) and [Jinotega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinotega_Department) departments, where the high altitudes are suitable for [coffee production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production). These settlers eventually grew to be dominant with the majority of large fincas in these two departments owned by people with a foreign migration background by 1909.[^cite_note-explore-nica-265] People from other parts of Europe also settled in Nicaragua after independence, specifically people from [Italy](14532.md), although they were concentrated in urban environments. Today, the majority of white and mestizo Nicaraguans are of Spanish descent, about 75% of the total European admixture.[^cite_note-266]

#### Black Creoles



[Miskito people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people) changing a bus tire between [Bilwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilwi) and [Krukira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krukira), Nicaragua

About 9% of Nicaragua's population is [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Latin_Americans) and mainly resides on the country's Caribbean (or Atlantic) coast. The black population is mostly composed of black English-speaking Creoles who are the descendants of escaped or shipwrecked slaves; many carry the name of [Scottish settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_settlers) who brought slaves with them, such as [Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_\(surname\)), [Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_\(surname\)), [Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downs_\(surname\)), and [Hodgson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgson). Although many Creoles supported Somoza because of his close association with the United States, they rallied to the Sandinista cause in July 1979, only to reject the revolution soon afterwards in response to a new phase of "westernization" and imposition of central rule from Managua.[^cite_note-267] There is a smaller number of [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_people), a people of mixed [West African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African), [Carib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Carib) and [Arawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawak) descent. In the mid-1980s, the government divided the [Zelaya Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelaya_Department) – consisting of the eastern half of the country – into two autonomous regions and granted the black and indigenous people of this region limited self-rule within the republic.

#### Indigenous population

The remaining 5% of Nicaraguans are indigenous, the descendants of the country's original inhabitants. Nicaragua's [pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian) population consisted of many indigenous groups. In the western region, the Nahuas ([Nicarao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_people)) were present along with other groups such as the Chorotega people and the [Subtiabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtiaba) (also known as Maribios or Hokan Xiu). The central region and the Caribbean coast of Nicaragua were inhabited by indigenous peoples who were [Macro-Chibchan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macro-Chibchan_languages) groups that had migrated to and from South America in ancient times, primarily what is now [Colombia](5222.md) and [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269]

These groups include the present-day [Matagalpas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_people), [Miskitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people), [Ramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_people), as well as Mayangnas and Ulwas who are also known as [Sumos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumo_people).[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-Brief-55]<sup>: 20 </sup> In the 19th century, there was a substantial indigenous minority, but this group was largely assimilated culturally into the mestizo majority. The [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna) and [Miskito Sambu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_Sambu) are also present, mainly on the Caribbean Coast. They are of mixed African and Indigenous descent, also known as [Zambos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambos)..[^cite_note-271]

Relative to its population, Nicaragua has not experienced large waves of immigration. The number of immigrants in Nicaragua, from other Latin American countries or other countries, never surpassed 1% of its total population before 1995. The 2005 census showed the foreign-born population at 1.2%, having risen a mere 0.06% in 10 years.[^cite_note-CN-257]

In the 19th century, Nicaragua experienced modest waves of immigration from Europe. In particular, families from Germany, Italy, Spain, France and Belgium immigrated to Nicaragua, particularly the departments in the Central and Pacific region.

Also present is a small Middle Eastern-Nicaraguan community of [Syrians](8862873.md), [Armenians](387816.md), [Jewish Nicaraguans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_of_Nicaragua), and [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) in Nicaragua. This community numbers about 30,000. There is an East Asian community mostly consisting of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese). The [Chinese Nicaraguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Nicaraguan) population is estimated at 12,000.[^cite_note-273] The Chinese arrived in the late 19th century but were unsubstantiated until the 1920s.

### Diaspora

Main article: [Nicaraguan diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_diaspora)

The [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution) forced many Nicaraguans to start lives outside of their country. Many people emigrated during the 1990s and the first decade of the 21st century due to the lack of employment opportunities and poverty. The majority of the [Nicaraguan Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Diaspora) migrated to the United States and [Costa Rica](5551.md). Today one in six Nicaraguans live in these two countries.[^cite_note-274]

The diaspora has seen Nicaraguans settling around in smaller communities in other parts of the world, particularly Western Europe. Small communities of Nicaraguans are found in France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom. Communities also exist in Australia and New Zealand. Canada, Brazil and Argentina host small groups of these communities. In Asia, Japan hosts a small Nicaraguan community.

Due to extreme poverty at home, many Nicaraguans are now living and working in neighboring [El Salvador](9356.md), a country that has the [US dollar](18717338.md) as its currency.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276]

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Nicaragua)



A sign in Bluefields in English (top), [Nicaraguan Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Spanish) (middle), and [Miskito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_language) (bottom)

[Nicaraguan Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Spanish) has many indigenous influences and several distinguishing characteristics. For example, some Nicaraguans have a tendency to replace [/s/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_alveolar_sibilant) with [/h/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_glottal_fricative) when speaking. Although Spanish is spoken throughout, the country has great variety: vocabulary, accents and colloquial language can vary between towns and departments.[^cite_note-277]

[Nicaraguan Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Sign_Language) emerged in the 1970s and 1980s among deaf children as the first special education schools brought them together, and its emergence became of particular interest to linguists as an opportunity to directly observe the creation of a language.[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280]

On the [Caribbean](18956035.md) coast, indigenous languages, [English-based creoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-based_creole_languages), and Spanish are spoken. The [Miskito language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_language), spoken by the [Miskito people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people) as a first language and some other indigenous and Afro-descendants people as a second, third, or fourth language, is the most commonly spoken indigenous language. The indigenous [Misumalpan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misumalpan_languages) of Mayangna and Ulwa are spoken by the respective peoples of the same names. Many Miskito, Mayangna, and Sumo people also speak [Miskito Coast Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_Coast_Creole), and a large majority also speak Spanish. Fewer than three dozen of nearly 2,000 [Rama people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_people) speak their [Chibchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibchan_languages) [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_language) fluently, with nearly all Ramas speaking [Rama Cay Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_Cay_Creole) and the vast majority speaking Spanish. Linguists have attempted to document and revitalize the language over the past three decades.[^cite_note-281]

The [Garifuna people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_people), descendants of indigenous and Afro-descendant people who came to Nicaragua from Honduras in the early twentieth century, have recently attempted to revitalize their [Arawakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawakan_languages) [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_language). The majority speak Miskito Coast Creole as their first language and Spanish as their second. The Creole or Kriol people, descendants of enslaved Africans brought to the [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) during the British colonial period and European, Chinese, Arab, and British West Indian immigrants, also speak Miskito Coast Creole as their first language and Spanish as their second.[^cite_note-282]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Nicaragua)



[León Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n_Cathedral_\(Nicaragua\)), one of Nicaragua's [World Heritage Sites](44940.md)

Religion plays a significant role in Nicaraguan culture and is afforded special protections in its [constitution](11109678.md). [Religious freedom](46006.md) (which has been guaranteed since 1939) and [religious tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tolerance) are officially promoted by the government, but, in recent years, the [Catholic Church](606848.md) and the regime led by [Daniel Ortega](8778.md) have been in open conflict. The latter has been accused of using the police to harass clergy (including bishops),[^cite_note-283] closing down Catholic media outlets, and arresting members of the clergy (including [Bishop Rolando Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolando_Jos%C3%A9_%C3%81lvarez_Lagos) of the [Diocese of Matagalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Matagalpa)).

Nicaragua has no official [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion). Catholic bishops are expected to lend their authority to important state occasions, and their pronouncements on national issues are closely followed. They can be called upon to mediate between contending parties at moments of political crisis.[^cite_note-LOCR-284] In 1979, Miguel D'Escoto Brockman, a priest who had embraced [Liberation Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Theology), served in the government as foreign minister when the Sandinistas came to power. The largest denomination, and traditionally the religion of the majority, is the [Catholic Church](606848.md). It came to Nicaragua in the 16th century with the Spanish conquest and remained, until 1939, the [established faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion).

The number of practicing Catholics has been declining, while membership of [evangelical Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Protestant) groups and [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (LDS Church) has been growing rapidly since the 1990s. There is a significant LDS missionary effort in Nicaragua. There are two missions and 95,768 members of the LDS Church (1.54% of the population).[^cite_note-285] There are also strong [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) and [Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church) communities on the Caribbean coast in what once constituted the sparsely populated [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) colony. It was under British influence for nearly three centuries. [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) was brought to the [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) mainly by British and German colonists in forms of [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) and the [Moravian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church). Other kinds of Protestant and other [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination) were introduced to the rest of Nicaragua during the 19th century.

Popular religion revolves around the saints, who are perceived as intercessors between human beings and God. Most localities, from the capital of Managua to small rural communities, honor [patron saints](68055.md), selected from the Catholic calendar, with annual *fiestas*. In many communities, a rich lore has grown up around the celebrations of patron saints, such as Managua's Saint Dominic (Santo Domingo), honored in August with two colorful, often riotous, day-long processions through the city. The high point of Nicaragua's religious calendar for the masses is neither Christmas nor Easter, but La Purísima, a week of festivities in early December dedicated to the [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception), during which elaborate altars to the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_\(mother_of_Jesus\)) are constructed in homes and workplaces.[^cite_note-LOCR-284]

[Buddhism](3267529.md) has increased with a steady influx of immigration.[^cite_note-286]

Although [Jews](25955086.md) have been living in Nicaragua since the 18th century, the [Jewish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Nicaragua) is small, numbering less than 200 people in 2017. Of these, 112 were recent converts who claimed [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) Jewish ancestry.[^cite_note-Dolsten-287]

As of 2007, approximately 1,200 to 1,500 Nicaraguan residents practiced [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Nicaragua), most of them [Sunnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnis) who are [resident aliens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_aliens) or [naturalized citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized) from [Palestine](241405.md), [Libya](17633.md), and [Iran](14653.md) or natural-born Nicaraguan descendants of the two groups.[^cite_note-288]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Nicaragua)



Primary school student in Nicaragua holding the national flag

The adult literacy rate in 2005 was 78.0%, the lowest [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) rate in Central America.[^cite_note-unescolit-289]

Primary education is free in Nicaragua. A system of private schools exists, many of which are religiously affiliated and often have more robust English programs.[^cite_note-290] As of 1979, the educational system was one of the poorest in Latin America.[^cite_note-NE-291] One of the first acts of the newly elected Sandinista government in 1980 was an extensive and successful literacy campaign, using secondary school students, university students and teachers as volunteer teachers: it reduced the overall illiteracy rate from 50.3% to 12.9% within only five months.[^cite_note-NLC-292] This was one of a number of large-scale programs which received international recognition for their gains in [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy), health care, education, [childcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childcare), [unions](17626.md), and [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform).[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294] The Sandinistas also added a leftist ideological content to the curriculum, which was removed after 1990.[^cite_note-gr1-172] In September 1980, [UNESCO](21786641.md) awarded Nicaragua the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) sponsored [Nadezhda Krupskaya award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Nadezhda_K._Krupskaya_literacy_prize) for the literacy campaign.[^cite_note-295]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Nicaragua)

See also: [Healthcare in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Nicaragua)

Although Nicaragua's health outcomes have improved over the past few decades with the efficient utilization of resources relative to other Central American nations, [healthcare in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Nicaragua) still confronts challenges responding to its populations' diverse healthcare needs.[^cite_note-\:0-296]

The [Nicaraguan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nicaragua) guarantees universal free [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care) for its citizens.[^cite_note-\:1-297] However, limitations of current delivery models and unequal distribution of resources and medical personnel contribute to the persistent lack of quality care in more remote areas of Nicaragua, especially among rural communities in the Central and Atlantic region.[^cite_note-\:0-296] To respond to the dynamic needs of localities, the government has adopted a decentralized model that emphasizes community-based preventive and primary medical care.[^cite_note-298]

### Gender equality

Main article: [Gender equality in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality_in_Nicaragua)

Nicaragua's gender equality ranks high among countries in [Latin America](18524.md).[^cite_note-\:02-299] When it came to global rankings regarding gender equality, the World Economic Forum ranked Nicaragua at number twelve in 2015,[^cite_note-\:02-299] and in its 2020 report Nicaragua ranked number five, behind only northern European countries.[^cite_note-300]

Nicaragua was among the many countries in Latin America and the Caribbean to ratify the [Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Discrimination_Against_Women), which aimed to promote women's rights.[^cite_note-301]

In 2009, a Special Ombudsman for Sexual Diversity position was created within its Office of the Human Rights Ombudsman. And, in 2014, the Health Ministry banned discrimination based on gender identity and sexual orientation.[^cite_note-302] Nevertheless, discrimination against LGBTQ individuals is common, particularly in housing, education, and the workplace.[^cite_note-Nicaragua-197]

[The Human Development Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Report) ranked Nicaragua 106 out of 160 countries in the [Gender Inequality Index (GII)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_Inequality_Index) in 2017. It reflects gender-based inequalities in three dimensions – reproductive health, empowerment, and economic activity.[^cite_note-303]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Nicaragua)



[El Güegüense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_G%C3%BCeg%C3%BCense), a [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama), was the first literary work of post-Columbian Nicaragua and is regarded as one of [Latin America](18524.md)'s most distinctive colonial-era expressions and as Nicaragua's signature folkloric masterpiece combining music, dance, and theater.

[Nicaraguan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Nicaragua) has strong folklore, music and religious traditions, deeply influenced by [European culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Europe) but also including [Indigenous Nicaraguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas#Nicaragua) sounds and flavors. Nicaraguan culture can further be defined in several distinct strands. The Pacific coast has strong folklore, music and religious traditions, deeply influenced by [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) Europeans. It was colonized by Spain and has a similar culture to other Spanish-speaking Latin American countries. The indigenous groups that historically inhabited the Pacific coast have largely been assimilated into the [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) culture.

The Caribbean coast of Nicaragua was once a British protectorate. English is still predominant in this region and spoken domestically along with Spanish and [indigenous languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Nicaragua). Its culture is similar to that of Caribbean nations that were or are British possessions, such as [Jamaica](15660.md), Belize, the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands), etc. Unlike on the west coast, the indigenous peoples of the Caribbean coast have maintained distinct identities, and some still speak their native languages as first languages.

### Music

Main article: [Music of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Nicaragua)



Nicaraguan women wearing the [Mestizaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizaje) costume, which is a traditional costume worn to dance the Mestizaje dance. The costume demonstrates the indigenous influence upon Nicaraguan clothing.

[^cite_note-304]

[Nicaraguan music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Nicaragua) is a mixture of indigenous and Spanish influences. Musical instruments include the [marimba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marimba) and others common across Central America. The marimba of Nicaragua is played by a sitting performer holding the instrument on his knees. He is usually accompanied by a bass [fiddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle), guitar and guitarrilla (a small guitar like a [mandolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandolin)). This music is played at social functions as a sort of background music.

The marimba is made with hardwood plates placed over bamboo or metal tubes of varying lengths. It is played with two or four [hammers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammer). The Caribbean coast of Nicaragua is known for a lively, sensual form of [dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_music) called *[Palo de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_de_Mayo)* which is popular throughout the country. It is especially loud and celebrated during the Palo de Mayo festival in May. The [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_people) community (Afro-Amerindian) is known for its popular music called *[Punta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_\(music\))*.

Nicaragua has international influence in music. [Bachata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachata_\(music\)), [Merengue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merengue_music), [Salsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_music) and [Cumbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbia_music) have gained prominence in cultural centers such as Managua, León and [Granada](38264.md). Cumbia dancing has grown popular with the introduction of Nicaraguan artists, including Gustavo Leyton, on [Ometepe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ometepe_Island) and in Managua. [Salsa dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_dancing) has become extremely popular in Managua's nightclubs. With various influences, the form of salsa dancing varies in Nicaragua. New York style and Cuban Salsa (Salsa Casino) elements have gained popularity across the country.

### Dance

Further information: [Dance in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_in_Nicaragua)

Dance in Nicaragua varies depending upon the region. Rural areas tend to have a stronger focus on movement of the hips and turns. The dance style in cities focuses primarily on more sophisticated footwork in addition to movement and turns. Combinations of styles from the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) and the United States can be found throughout Nicaragua. [Bachata dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachata_\(dance\)) is popular in Nicaragua. A considerable amount of Bachata dancing influence comes from Nicaraguans living abroad, in cities that include Miami, Los Angeles and, to a much lesser extent, New York City. [Tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_\(dance\)) has also surfaced recently in cultural cities and ballroom dance occasions.

### Literature

Main article: [Literature of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_of_Nicaragua)



[Rubén Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Dar%C3%ADo), founder of [Latin America](18524.md)'s [modernismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernismo) literary movement

The origin of [Nicaraguan literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_of_Nicaragua) can arguably be traced to [pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian) times. The myths and [oral literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_literature) formed the cosmogenic view of the world of the indigenous people. Some of these stories are still known in Nicaragua. Like many Latin American countries, the Spanish conquerors have had the most effect on both the culture and the literature. Nicaraguan literature has historically been an important source of poetry in the Spanish-speaking world, with internationally renowned contributors such as [Rubén Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Dar%C3%ADo) who is regarded as the most important literary figure in Nicaragua. He is called the "Father of Modernism" for leading the *[modernismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernismo)* literary movement at the end of the 19th century.[^cite_note-NI-305] Other literary figures include [Carlos Martinez Rivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Martinez_Rivas), [Pablo Antonio Cuadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Antonio_Cuadra), Alberto Cuadra Mejia, Manolo Cuadra, Pablo Alberto Cuadra Arguello, Orlando Cuadra Downing, Alfredo Alegría Rosales, [Sergio Ramirez Mercado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ramirez_Mercado), [Ernesto Cardenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Cardenal), [Gioconda Belli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioconda_Belli), [Claribel Alegría](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claribel_Alegr%C3%ADa) and [José Coronel Urtecho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Coronel_Urtecho), among others.[^cite_note-306]

The satirical drama *[El Güegüense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_G%C3%BCeg%C3%BCense)* was the first literary work of post-Columbian Nicaragua. It was written in both [Nicarao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_language) and Spanish.[^cite_note-Campbell1997-64]<sup>: 21 </sup> It is regarded as one of Latin America's most distinctive colonial-era expressions and as Nicaragua's signature folkloric masterpiece. El Güegüense is a work of resistance to Spanish colonialism that combined music, dance and theater.[^cite_note-NI-305] The [theatrical](20913771.md) play was written by an anonymous author in the 16th century, making it one of the oldest [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_culture) theatrical/dance works of the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md). In 2005 it was recognized by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as "a patrimony of humanity".[^cite_note-307] After centuries of popular performance, the play was first published in a book in 1942.[^cite_note-308]

### Media

Main article: [Mass media in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Nicaragua)

For most Nicaraguans radio and TV are the main sources of news. There are more than 100 radio stations and several TV networks. Cable TV is available in most urban areas.[^cite_note-309]

The Nicaraguan print publications include [La Prensa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Prensa_\(Managua\)), [El Nuevo Diario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Nuevo_Diario), [Confidencial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidencial), Hoy, and Mercurio. Online news publications include Confidencial and [The Nicaragua Dispatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nicaragua_Dispatch). [Independent media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_media) have been dismantled according to the [World Press Freedom Index](8264708.md), preventing [freedom of the press in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_press_in_Nicaragua).[^cite_note-310]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Nicaraguan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_cuisine)



[Vigorón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigor%C3%B3n), a Nicaraguan dish served with boiled yuca and chicharrones (fried pork with skin) and topped with a cabbage salad



[Gallo pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo_pinto), a traditional Nicaraguan dish made with rice and beans

[Nicaraguan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_cuisine) is a mixture of Spanish food and dishes of a pre-Columbian origin.[^cite_note-NICA-311] Traditional cuisine changes from the Pacific to the Caribbean coast. The Pacific coast's main staple revolves around local fruits and corn, the Caribbean coast cuisine makes use of seafood and the coconut.

As in many other Latin American countries, [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) is a [staple food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_food) and is used in many of the widely consumed dishes, such as the [nacatamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacatamal), [güirila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCirila), and *[indio viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indio_viejo)*. Maize is also an ingredient for drinks such as [pinolillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinolillo) and [chicha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicha) as well as sweets and desserts. In addition to corn, rice and beans are eaten very often.

[Gallo pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo_pinto), Nicaragua's [national dish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_dish), is made with white rice and small red beans that are cooked individually and then fried together. The dish has several variations including the addition of [coconut milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_milk) or grated [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) on the Caribbean coast. Most Nicaraguans begin their day with gallo pinto. Gallo pinto is most usually served with *carne asada*, a salad, fried cheese, [plantains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_bananas) or maduros.

Many of Nicaragua's dishes include indigenous fruits and vegetables such as [jocote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocote), [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), [tamarindo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarind), pipian, banana, [avocado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avocado), [yuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), and herbs such as [cilantro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriander), [oregano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregano) and [achiote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bixa_orellana).[^cite_note-NICA-311]

Traditional street food snacks found in Nicaragua include "[quesillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quesillo)", a thick tortilla with soft cheese and cream, "tajadas" (deep-fried plantain chips), "maduros" (a sautéed ripe plantain), and "fresco" (fresh juices such as hibiscus and tamarind commonly served in a plastic bag with a straw).[^cite_note-312]

Nicaraguans have been known to eat [guinea pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_pig),[^cite_note-313] known as *cuy*. Tapirs, iguanas, turtle eggs, armadillos and boas are also sometimes eaten, but because of extinction threats to these wild creatures, there are efforts to curb this custom.[^cite_note-NICA-311]

### Sports



[Dennis Martínez National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Mart%C3%ADnez_National_Stadium), Nicaragua's main outdoor stadium

[Baseball](3850.md) is the most popular sport in Nicaragua. Although some professional Nicaraguan baseball teams have recently folded, the country still enjoys a strong tradition of American-style baseball.

Baseball was introduced to Nicaragua during the 19th century. In the Caribbean coast, locals from Bluefields were taught how to play baseball in 1888 by Albert Addlesberg, a retailer from the United States.[^cite_note-BB-314] Baseball did not catch on in the Pacific coast until 1891 when a group of mostly college students from the United States formed "La Sociedad de Recreo" (Society of Recreation) where they played various sports, baseball being the most popular.[^cite_note-BB-314]

Nicaragua has had its share of [MLB](38776.md) players, including shortstop [Everth Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everth_Cabrera), pitcher [Vicente Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Padilla), and pitcher [Jonathan Loáisiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Lo%C3%A1isiga), but the most notable is [Dennis Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Mart%C3%ADnez), who was the first baseball player from Nicaragua to play in [Major League Baseball](38776.md).[^cite_note-315] He became the first Latin-born pitcher to throw a [perfect game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_game_\(baseball\)), and the 13th in the major league history, when he played with the [Montreal Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos) against the [Dodgers](18213.md) at Dodger Stadium in 1991.[^cite_note-316]

[Boxing](4243.md) is the second-most popular sport in Nicaragua.[^cite_note-317] The country has had world champions such as [Alexis Argüello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Arg%C3%BCello) and [Ricardo Mayorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Mayorga) as well as [Román González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rom%C3%A1n_Gonz%C3%A1lez_\(boxer\)). Recently, [football](10568.md) has gained popularity. The [Dennis Martínez National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Mart%C3%ADnez_National_Stadium) has served as a venue for both baseball and football. The first ever national football-only stadium in Managua, the [Nicaragua National Football Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_National_Football_Stadium), was completed in 2011.[^cite_note-318]

[Nicaragua's national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua's_national_basketball_team) had some recent success as it won the silver medal at the [2017 Central American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Central_American_Games).[^cite_note-319] They took part in the [FIBA AmeriCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_AmeriCup) for the first time when Nicaragua hosted in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIBA_AmeriCup). The women's national basketball team will make their debut at the [FIBA Women's AmeriCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Women's_AmeriCup) in [2029](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2029_FIBA_Women's_AmeriCup)

Nicaragua featured national teams in [beach volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_volleyball) that competed at the [2018–2020 NORCECA Beach Volleyball Continental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932020_NORCECA_Beach_Volleyball_Continental_Cup) in both the women's and the men's sections.[^cite_note-320]
