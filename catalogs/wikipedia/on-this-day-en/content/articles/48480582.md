The **Ignacy Jan Paderewski Pomeranian Philharmonic** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Państwowa Filharmonia Pomorska imienia Ignacego Jana Paderewskiego*) is an orchestra in [Bydgoszcz](74593.md), [Poland](22936.md). It has been at its present site since 16 November 1953. It bears the name of Polish pianist and composer [Ignacy Jan Paderewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski). The Pomeranian Philharmonic is the musical center of [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) and also features an outdoor art gallery. It is registered on the [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) Heritage List.

## Location

The seat of the Pomeranian Philharmonic is located in downtown Bydgoszcz, in the center of the "Music District". It is surrounded by [Jan Kochanowski Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kochanowski_Park), [music schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_Schools_Group_in_Bydgoszcz), and an outdoor gallery of sculptures of composers and virtuoso musicians.

## Characteristics

The Pomeranian Philharmonic is a cultural institution which bodies are under the authority of leading [Polish Ministry of Culture and National Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_and_National_Heritage_\(Poland\)) and [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship). Performances include symphonic concerts, chamber music, and [recitals](236918.md#Recital) by virtuosos from all over the world. On stage Philharmonic concerts are accompanied by Polish or foreign groups, to play at the occasion of jubilees, commemorations and congresses organized by universities and private companies.[^cite_note-WA-1] The repertoire includes music of all periods, from medieval to contemporary works.

The acoustics of the concert hall of the Pomeranian Philharmonic is famous for its quality: concert recordings of classical and contemporary music are often performed there.[^cite_note-WA-1]

In 2010, the Pomeranian Philharmonic has carried out in their hall 360 concerts, gathering an audience of 79200 people.[^cite_note-2]

The first director of the Pomeranian Philharmonic was, from 1953 to 1991, [Andrzej Szwalbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Szwalbe), who made the orchestra of Bydgoszcz a recognized scene in Europe. Since 1991, director is Eleonora Harendarska.

## History

### Municipal Symphony Orchestra

The first initiative related to set up a Pomeranian Philharmonic was the creation of a local Symphony Orchestra able to perform at a high artistic level: in 1922, one the section of Bydgoszcz's Music Society had an orchestra section under the direction of Zygmunt Urbanyi. However, despite strenuous efforts, no symphony ensemble has been created in the 1920s. In 1924, the Pomeranian Philharmonic Society was an entity consisting of bands from [Bydgoszcz](74593.md), [Grudziądz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudzi%C4%85dz) and [Toruń](31258.md). In 1925, an initiative to establish an Orchestra of the Pomeranian Philharmonic Society, composed of professional musicians, failed for public foundings reasons.[^cite_note-PA-3] In this situation, the Bydgoszcz Music Society, until 1930, was the only body organizing regular performances:

- outstanding Polish and foreign artists,

- local military bands,

- Bydgoszcz Conservatoire of Music, conducted by Wilhelm von Winterfeld,

- bands from other cities, such as the orchestra of the [Grand Theatre of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theatre,_Pozna%C5%84).

  [^cite_note-BLMX-4]

In January 1936, on the initiative of voluntary musicians, the Bydgoszcz symphony orchestra has been created, soon directed by Wilhelm von Winterfeld. The inaugural concert of the **Municipal Symphony Orchestra** has been held on May 8, 1936 "Strzelnicy Hall" in Toruńska street.[^cite_note-PA-3] The following year, the orchestra was led by Alfons Rezler, a professor of the Municipal Conservatoire of Music, providing the basis for its organization. In 1938, the Bydgoszcz's Music Society took over the patronage of the orchestra,[^cite_note-ŁE-5] and the Municipality provided a considerable assistance to the team. [World War II](32927.md) and the years of occupation have destroyed these achievements: Alfons Rezler died during the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), many musicians were killed or fled abroad.[^cite_note-BLMX-4]

### Pomeranian Symphony Orchestra

In 1946, to celebrate the 600th anniversary of the foundation of Bydgoszcz, a new formation, close to the pre-war *Symphony Orchestra of the Music Society of Bydgoszcz* was created by Arnold Rezler, Jerzy Jasieński, Felicja Krysiewicz and chaired by Witold Miller.[^cite_note-PT-6] It gave its first concert on May 12, 1946, in the hall of the [Pomorski House of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Arts_House_in_Bydgoszcz) in [Gdańska Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84ska_Street,_Bydgoszcz). From 1946 to 1949, the Music society of Bydgoszcz set up the foundations of the organisation and the material, with few interference of the city.[^cite_note-DA-7] During this period the orchestra was called *Pomeranian Symphony Orchestra'* of the Bydgoszcz Music Society.

Public concerts were held four times a month, with additional ones in schools of Bydgoszcz and of the Voivodeship. Already at that time, initiatives to invite eminent conductors ([Witold Rowicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Rowicki) and [Bohdan Wodiczko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohdan_Wodiczko)[^cite_note-KB1-8] and soloists started ([Stanisław Szpinalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Szpinalski), [Zbigniew Drzewiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zbigniew_Drzewiecki), [Regina Smendzianka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Smendzianka), [Kazimierz Wiłkomirski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Wi%C5%82komirski), [Wanda Wiłkomirska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Wi%C5%82komirska)).[^cite_note-DA-7] Conducting Orchestra, among others, .

In 1949, Mieczyslaw Tomaszewski was nominated as new director: he organized three series of concerts related to [Frédéric Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin) (for the centenary of the composer's death), and presented a series of concerts "Music for All" in 26 cities of the Voivodeship. The orchestra was nationalized in November 1950.[^cite_note-PT-6] On September 1, 1951, Andrzej Szwalbe became director of the Pomeranian Philharmonic Orchestra. He soon championed the project of the construction of a specific building for the Philharmonic, to move out of the [Pomorski House of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Arts_House_in_Bydgoszcz) in [Gdańska Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84ska_Street,_Bydgoszcz). Main conductors at this time were, among others Edward Bury, Tadeusz Wilczak or [Robert Satanowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Satanowski).[^cite_note-ŁE-5] The inaugural concert of the new symphony orchestra of the State Pomeranian Philharmonic took place on January 9, 1953, in the hall of the Polish Theatre.[^cite_note-BLM-9]

In September 1955, the orchestra of the National Pomeranian Philharmonic, numbering 65 musicians, was augmented by disbanded musicians from the Bydgoszcz Polish Radio Orchestra. In the same year the name of the institution was changed to **Ignacy Jan Paderewski Pomeranian Philharmonic**,[^cite_note-KB1-8] earning the esteem of more and more talented graduates of music schools. In 1971, the philharmonic counted more than 100 people. Many of the orchestra musicians began working in music schools of Bydgoszcz and [Toruń](31258.md).[^cite_note-BLM-9]

On November 15, 1958, the orchestra performed its first concert in the brand new building of the Pomeranian Philharmonic in Bydgoszcz.

### Pomeranian Philharmonic

In September 1951, Andrzej Szwalbe was appointed director of the Pomeranian Philharmonic Orchestra, which was nationalized in December 1952. On January 1, 1953, the orchestra was renamed **National Pomeranian Philharmonic**. By February 1953, there were attempts to have a new philharmonic concert hall constructed.[^cite_note-ŁE-5] Thanks to the efforts of the Social Committee for the Philharmonic Building, the [cornerstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerstone) laying ceremony of the edifice at 16 Libelta street took place on June 26, 1954. The concert hall's construction was completed in the autumn of 1958. The new building was spacious, conveniently located, and, most importantly, [acoustically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics) sound. On November 16, 1958 a gala concert marking the opening of the new building was held, led by Zbigniew Chwedczuk.[^cite_note-ŁE-5]

Thanks to Andrzej Szwalbe's work, 1961 witnessed the first musical event on a national scale, the "Ignacy Jan [Paderewski International Piano Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderewski_International_Piano_Competition?action=edit\&redlink=1)" ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *[Międzynarodowy Konkurs Pianistyczny im. Ignacego Jana Paderewskiego](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzynarodowy%20Konkurs%20Pianistyczny%20im.%20Ignacego%20Jana%20Paderewskiego)*). Each year since then, a "Bydgoszcz Music Festival" has been organized.[^cite_note-ŁE-5]

## Philharmonic building

### History

Pomeranian Philharmonic building has been built in the Polish vogue style in the 1940s and 1950s, which is a mix of [functionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Style_\(architecture\)), [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture), [Renaissance architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture), [Baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) and [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo#Architecture). The external architecture reveals itself especially [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture), displaying seriousness and moderation to illustrate the social importance of the institutions housed there.[^cite_note-WAH-13] The originator and effective executor of the building was director of the Philharmonic [Andrzej Szwalbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Szwalbe). On March 24, 1953, the Presidium of the Provincial Council in consultation with the Central Board of Opera, Philharmonic Hall and Musical Institutions in [Warsaw](32908.md) adopted a resolution allowing the construction of the building. Initial draft were prepared by a team led by the architect Jan Kossowki, who at the time already worked on several projects in Bydgoszcz ([Freedom Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Monument,_Bydgoszcz), buildings in [Jagiellońska street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiello%C5%84ska_street_in_Bydgoszcz) and [Gdańska Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84ska_Street,_Bydgoszcz)). Many locations were identified: [People Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiello%C5%84ska_street_in_Bydgoszcz#Ludowy_Park), [Jan Kochanowski park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kochanowski_Park_in_Bydgoszcz), [Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) square or [Leszek the White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leszek_the_White) square. The selected area was the location of former tennis courts at Słowackiego street.[^cite_note-WAH-13] The jury, chaired by professor Piotr Biegański who co-led the reconstruction [Old](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Old_Town) and [New Town of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_New_Town), elected the team headed by:

- architect Stefan Klajbor (who realized a building in [Jagiellońska street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiello%C5%84ska_street_in_Bydgoszcz)),

- designer Bogdan Piestrzyński,

- acoustics designer Witold Straszewicz of [Warsaw University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_of_Technology).

  [^cite_note-WAH-13]

Construction began in February 1954, under the supervision of Zbigniew Arciszewski from [Gdansk University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk_University_of_Technology).[^cite_note-14] The construction was financed by the *"Social Reconstruction Fund of the Capital"*. The building site was twice inspected by a delegation of Soviet builders from Warsaw's [Palace of Culture and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science) in September 1954 and January 1955. The building was erected using Polish building materials, with the help of Polish engineering and manpower. The interior decor was designed by a team of artists from [Toruń](31258.md)–Stefan Zarębski, Joseph Kozlowski and Jozef Kowalczyk–, wood [panelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panelling) prepared by Bydgoszcs's Furniture Factory, railings by Blacksmith craftmen from [Gdańsk](12099.md) and [chandelier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandelier) by [Szklarska Poręba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szklarska_Por%C4%99ba) glassworks which provided chandeliers for [Warsaw Palace of Culture and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science).[^cite_note-WAH-13]

A particular attention has been paid to concert hall acoustic. The main hall has an [amphitheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheater) slope. Over the stage, acoustic reflectors are placed on the back wall, and the whole interior is paneled in order to obtain balanced reverberation time through the entire frequency spectrum. Acoustic effects of this arena amazes even professionals. In a letter to the then [Minister of Foreign Affairs](1161355.md) [Adam Rapacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Rapacki), Andrzej Szwalbe stated:

"acoustics were able to reach excellence. There is nothing to be ashamed of with regards to the East or the West \[...] "

### Architecture

The building is an 18m high regular structure with dimensions of 35m by 45m, reminding willingly the features of the [Warsaw Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_National_Philharmonic_Orchestra) building rebuilt at the same period (1955). The main elevation is mounted on a pedestal. The central part of the frontage displays a two-storey high [colonnade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnade), joined at its bottom by [baluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluster) railings. Facades are topped with [attic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attic_style) [cornices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornice) with wire mesh walls. [Terraces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_\(building\)) are available between each façade [colonnades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnade).

Inside are two concert halls:

- the main one called "[Arthur Rubinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rubinstein) Hall" with an 880 seats capacity

- a smaller one called "[Mikołaj Zieleński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko%C5%82aj_Ziele%C5%84ski) hall" with 195 seats.

Early in the 1950s, live broadcast occurred thanks to [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) radio resources, airing both intimate recordings, as well as great symphony orchestra concerts with a choir, with up to 300 people.[^cite_note-BJ-15] Around the buildings are displayed sculptures, part of the outdoor monument gallery.[^cite_note-WAH-13]

The Pomeranian Philharmonic building has been registered on the **[Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) Heritage List** Nr.601376 Reg.A/269 on January 27, 1978 and December 18, 1981.[^cite_note-16]

### Acoustics

The main concert hall of the Pomeranian Philharmonic is classified as acoustically one of the best in Europe, as confirmed by well-known artists and music critics.[^cite_note-17] In the background of the main concert hall is set up a mechanical [organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_organ) produced by the firm [Rieger–Kloss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rieger%E2%80%93Kloss) from [Krnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krnov), Czech Republic in 1977.[^cite_note-18] The chamber hall is equipped with an organ made by the establishment of Stefan Truszczyński from [Włocławek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82oc%C5%82awek). There is also a large collection of concert keyboards, [pianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano) and [harpsichords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsichord).[^cite_note-BJ-15]

Its excellent [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics) gives to the Pomeranian Philharmonic a fame among well-known artists, being regularly the site of professional recording for music publishers. In this location has been realised Poland's first 24-karat [gold](12240.md) audio [Compact Disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Disc).

### Interior

The Pomeranian Philharmonic has a large collection of art works that are exhibited in the [lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobby_\(room\)) and halls: sculptures, paintings and graphic/textile art works.[^cite_note-PMG-19]

The Gallery of Composers Portraits provides an overview of contemporary sculptures. Works have been realized by authors such as: Adam Myjak, Kazimierz Gustaw Zemła, [Barbara Zbrożyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Zbro%C5%BCyna), [Marian Konieczny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Konieczny), Alfons Karny, Ryszard Wojciechowski or Michał Kubiak. Among the artists portrayed are [Frédéric Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin), [Ignacy Jan Paderewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski), [Mieczysław Karłowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieczys%C5%82aw_Kar%C5%82owicz), [Karol Szymanowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Szymanowski), [Ludomir Różycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludomir_R%C3%B3%C5%BCycki), [Józef Koffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Koffler), Artur Malawski, [Bolesław Szabelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_Szabelski), [Witold Małcużyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Ma%C5%82cu%C5%BCy%C5%84ski), [Wojciech Kilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Kilar), [Henryk Górecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_G%C3%B3recki), [Arthur Rubinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rubinstein), [Tadeusz Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Baird), [Stefan Kisielewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Kisielewski), [Krzysztof Penderecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Penderecki), [Witold Lutosławski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Lutos%C5%82awski).

In the first floor lobby are paintings and graphics of contemporary Polish authors: [Jerzy Nowosielski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Nowosielski), [Zdzisław Beksiński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zdzis%C5%82aw_Beksi%C5%84ski), Jan Tarasin, [Władysław Hasior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Hasior) and others.

The Gallery of tapestries includes about 30 [tapestries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapestry) woven from 1983 to 1992 by the traditional manufactories of [Gdańsk](12099.md) and [Zakopane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakopane). Their authors are painters like Mieczysław Olszewsk, [Kiejstut Bereźnicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiejstut_Bere%C5%BAnicki), [Kazimierz Ostrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Ostrowski), Jerzy Zabłocki, [Tadeusz Brzozowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Brzozowski_\(painter\)), Kazimierz Śramkiewicz. ### Collections

The Piano Collection of the Pomeranian Philharmonic comprises 54 instruments. These are historical instruments, mainly from the 19th century: pianos, [clavichords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clavichord), [player pianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_piano) and [harmonichords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonichord) coming from [Paris](22989.md), [Berlin](3354.md), [Leipzig](17955.md), [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), [London](17867.md), [Vienna](55866.md), [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), [Warsaw](32908.md), [Kraków](16815.md), [Gdańsk](12099.md), [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw), [Kalisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalisz) and [Bydgoszcz](74593.md). Purchases of these instruments began in 1970, under the direction of Andrzej Szwalbe. The first instrument was apiano winger, from [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad) firm "Gebauhr" (ca 1875). The oldest exhibit is a table piano for children, produced by [Vienna](55866.md) company "Nurnberg Amberberga" (ca 1800). One of the instrument of 1890 has been donated by composer [Zygmunt Noskowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygmunt_Noskowski). Other exhibits worth mentioning are: a Parisian [harmonichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonichord), an English [player piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_piano), table pianos playing a record of Janczarski (drums and bells), pianos from Bydgoszcz factory "Bruno Sommerfeld" located from 1905 to 1945 in [Dworcowa Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dworcowa_Street_in_Bydgoszcz).[^cite_note-ŁE-5] In 1985, pianos were moved to [Ostromecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostromecko) Palace, but due to renovation, they were stored in warehouses. In 1999 a concept exhibition have been realized in the musical instruments museum in Bydgoszcz.[^cite_note-21]

The collection in Ostromecko Palace is now reopened as *"The Andrzej Szwalbe Collection"*.[^cite_note-22]

The [Harpsichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsichord) Collection has been established in 1976, by director Andrzej Szwalbe who was inspired by the activity of the band *"Capella Bydgostiensis"* specialized in playing on [Early music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_music) instruments. In the 1970s, four harpsichords, replicas of 16th-18th centuries French and [Flemish](10878.md) historical instruments, were brought from the United States. In the 1980s harpsichordists from across Poland played on these instruments, documenting their recording with the [Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio). Later on, Bydgoszcz collection ushered in a new era for the executive practice of harpsichord in Poland.[^cite_note-23]

## Sculpture gallery

The Sculpture gallery of composers and virtuosos consists of busts and monuments located in the interiors and surroundings of the Pomeranian Philharmonic building.

### History

This gallery has been created with the initiative of Andrzej Szwalbe, the longtime manager of the Pomeranian Philharmonic.[^cite_note-GEP-24]

The ensemble comprises two parts:

- Interior gallery, inside the building;

- Outdoor gallery with portraits, busts and statues of world music greatest composers world music, placed in the Jan Kochanowski Park around the building of the Philharmonic.

The nature and objectives of the whole gallery were designed by Andrzej Szwalbe. In the course of several years he held the unveiling of monuments with participation of the living artists in the halls and corridors of the Pomeranian Philharmonic, generally associated with special concerts. Gallery is a review of Polish sculpture from the late 1950s to the 1990s: each work reveals specific characteristics of its sculptor and the original psychological approach of each character.
