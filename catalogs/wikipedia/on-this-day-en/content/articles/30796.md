**Tyre**[^cite_note-1] is a city in [Lebanon](17771.md), and one of the [oldest continuously inhabited cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities) in the world.[^cite_note-2] It was one of the earliest [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) [metropolises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis) and the legendary birthplace of [Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa_\(consort_of_Zeus\)), her brothers [Cadmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmus) and [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_\(son_of_Agenor\)), and [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage)'s founder [Dido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dido) (Elissa). The city has many ancient sites, including the [Tyre Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Hippodrome), and was added as a whole to the list of [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) in 1984.[^cite_note-3] The historian [Ernest Renan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Renan) described it as "a city of ruins, built out of ruins".[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Tyre is the fifth-largest city in Lebanon after [Beirut](37428.md), [Tripoli](268796.md), [Sidon](28366.md), and [Baalbek](58207.md).[^cite_note-6] It is the capital of the [Tyre District](11166788.md) in the [South Governorate](1283570.md). There were approximately 200,000 inhabitants in the Tyre urban area in 2016, including many [refugees](45547.md), as the city hosts three of the twelve [Palestinian refugee camps](400347.md) in Lebanon: [Burj El Shimali](22194140.md), [El Buss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Buss_refugee_camp), and [Rashidieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidieh).[^cite_note-Maguire-2016-7]

## Territory

[Map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special\:Map/11/33.28/35.239/en)

Tyre juts out from the coast of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), and is located about 80 km (50 mi) south of [Beirut](37428.md). It originally consisted of two distinct urban centres: Tyre itself, which was on an island just 500 to 700 metres offshore; and the associated settlement of [Ushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushu) on the adjacent mainland, later called *Palaetyrus* ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Παλαίτυρος), meaning "Old Tyre" in [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek).[^cite_note-8] The fortified city was on top of a rock from which its name was inherited, as *S'r* is the Phoenician word for "rock". It had two ports, the "Sidonian port" to the north, still partly existing today, and the "Egyptian port" to the south which very recently has perhaps been discovered.[^cite_note-Goiran_Brocard_Evolution_of_Sea_Level-9]



Rocky islets off Tyre

From [prehistoric times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) onwards, all settlements in the Tyre area profited from the abundance of [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) supplies, especially from the nearby [springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(hydrology\)) of Rashidieh and [Ras Al Ain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_al-Ain,_Lebanon) in the South. In addition, there are the springs of Al Bagbog and Ain Ebreen in the North as well as the [Litani River](1920925.md), also known as Alqasymieh.[^cite_note-10] The present city of Tyre covers a large part of the original island and has expanded onto and covers most of the [causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causeway) built by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 332 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era).[^cite_note-\:1-11] This [isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus) increased greatly in width over the centuries because of extensive silt depositions on either side. The part of the original island not covered by the modern city of Tyre is mostly of an archaeological site showcasing remains of the city from ancient times.

Four municipalities contribute to Tyre city's 16.7-square-kilometre (6+7⁄16 sq mi) built-up area, though none are included in their entirety: Sour municipality contains the heart of the city, excluding the Natural and Coastal Reserve; Burj El Shimali to the East without unpopulated agricultural lands; Abbasiyet Sour to the North without agricultural lands and a dislocated village; and [Ain Baal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Baal) to the South-East, also without agricultural lands and dislocated villages. Tyre's urban area lies on a fertile coastal plain, which explains the fact that as of 2017 about 44% of its territory was used for intra-urban agriculture, while built-up land constituted over 40%.[^cite_note-Maguire-2016-7]

In terms of [geomorphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology) and [seismicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismicity), Tyre is close to the Roum Fault and the [Yammouneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yammoune) [Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)). Though it has suffered a number of devastating earthquakes over the [millennia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium), the threat level is considered to be low in most places and moderate in a few others. However, a [tsunami](31161.md) following an earthquake and subsequent [landslides](37208.md) and floods pose major natural risks to the Tyrian population.[^cite_note-Maguire-2016-7]

Vast reserves of natural gas are estimated to lie beneath Lebanese waters, much of it off Tyre's coast, but [exploitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation_of_natural_resources) has been delayed by [border disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_dispute) with Israel.[^cite_note-UN_OCHA-2013-12]

## Etymology

Early names of Tyre include [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language) *Ṣurru*, [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_languages) *Ṣūr* (𐤑𐤓‎), and [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) *Ṣōr* (צוֹר).[^cite_note-Woodhouse-13] In [Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages), the name of the city possibly means 'rock'[^cite_note-14] after the rocky formation on which the town was originally built.

The predominant form in [Classical Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greek) was *Týros* (Τύρος), which was first seen in the works of [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) but may have been adopted considerably earlier.[^cite_note-Woodhouse-13] It gave rise to [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *Tyrus*, which entered [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) during the [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English) period as *Tyre*.[^cite_note-15] The [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym) for Tyre is *Tyrian*, and the inhabitants are *Tyrians*.[^cite_note-16]

## Climate



A [22° halo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22%C2%B0_halo) over Al Mina site, 2019

Tyre has a [Hot-summer mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (classified as *Csa* under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), characterized by six months of drought from May to October. On average, it has 300 days of [sun](26751.md) a year and a yearly temperature of 20.8[°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (69.5 °F). The average maximum temperature reaches its highest at 30.8 °C (87.5 °F) in August and the average minimum temperature its lowest at 10 °C (50 °F) in January. On average, the mean annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) reaches up to 645 mm (25½"). The temperature of the [sea water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater) reaches a minimum of 17 °C (63°F) in February and a maximum of 32 °C (90°F) in August. At a depth of 70 metres (200') it is constantly at 17–18 °C (63 to 64°F).[^cite_note-Yacoub-2011-17]

Meanwhile, [rising sea levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) due to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) threaten [coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) to Tyre's peninsula and bay areas.[^cite_note-18]

## History

Main article: [History of Tyre, Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tyre,_Lebanon)



[Ary Renan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ary_Renan)'s 1878 sketch of Tyre

The ancient city of Tyre is located along the coast of [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) in modern Lebanon. The site has been occupied since the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age).[^cite_note-20] The city became a prominent Phoenician city-state between the 9th and 6th centuries BC, settling prestigious colonies around the Mediterranean Sea, such as [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) and [Leptis Magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptis_Magna).[^cite_note-21] It came under Babylonian rule in 572 BC, before being conquered by [siege](26905.md) by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 332 BC. Eight thousand Tyrians died in the massacre that followed, against only 400 Macedonian deaths; 30,000 surviving Tyrians were sold into [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery).[^cite_note-22]

Monumental archaeological remains dated from the subsequent [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), and [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) periods led to its inscription on its archaeological remains on the [UNESCO](21786641.md) World's Heritage list in 1984.

The Roman historian [Justin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_\(historian\)) wrote that the city’s founders arrived from the nearby city of [Sidon](28366.md) in the quest to establish a new harbour. The Greek historian [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) (c. 484–425 BC) visited Tyre around 450 BC at the end of the [Greco-Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Persian_Wars) (499–449 BC), and wrote in his *[Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_\(Herodotus\))* that according to the priests there, the city was founded 2300 years earlier (around 2750 BC),[^cite_note-23] as a walled place upon the mainland, now known as *Paleotyre* (Old Tyre).&#x20;



1881 sketch of the skyline of Tyre

The Phoenician Tyrians' international trade network was based on its two harbours which are mentioned by ancient writers.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The northern harbour opened toward the Phoenician city of [Sidon](28366.md) and has been therefore referred to as the "Sidonian Harbour" by 19th and 20th century scholars, but it was referred to as the "Port of Astronoe" during Late Antiquity.[^cite_note-26] The southern harbour opened toward [Egypt](8087628.md) and was referred to as the "Egyptian Harbour". The location of the two harbours has been the subject of speculations since the 17th Century.[^cite_note-Renan-27] The submarine excavation of a large, 4-6th Century BC breakwater north of the city,[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] and the discovery of 250 BC to 500 AD harbour sediments behind this breakwater[^cite_note-30] demonstrated the existence of a northern harbour repeatedly, if not permanently, throughout Antiquity under the modern harbour of Tyre.

The location of the southern harbour is more elusive. Renan envisioned it as an extensive structure now located offshore, south of the former island. Subsequent diving surveys identified submerged man-made structures on the seafloor within 150 m of the former island.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Antoine Poidebard, who was the first to have them explored by divers in 1939, saw in these structures former breakwaters enclosing a harbour with two entrances.[^cite_note-33] The geographic area enclosed within these structures is therefore often referred to as the "Southern Harbour". These structures have also been interpreted as a polder-like area protecting an urban district.[^cite_note-34] A Phoenician-style breakwater was recently found within this area, but excavation is needed to confirm its age ascription.[^cite_note-Goiran_Brocard_Evolution_of_Sea_Level-9] Harbour sediments found behind the structure suggest that the breakwater was part of the Egyptian harbour.[^cite_note-\:0-35][^cite_note-36] Harbour sediments found near Hiram's Tower, further north, mark an early location of the Sidonian Harbour.[^cite_note-\:0-35]

The development of Tyre was profoundly affected by the construction of a [causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causeway) built by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 332 BC to seize the city.[^cite_note-\:1-11] This reportedly 750 metre (820 yard) long[^cite_note-37] and 60 metre (200 ft.) wide causeway was laid over a submarine [shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal) less than 5.4 metres (18 ft.) deep.[^cite_note-38] This shoal was interpreted as a sandbank (also called a 'salient'), formed by the accretion of sand in the lee of the island, under the effects of the refraction and diffraction of waves around the island. The causeway interrupted longshore sand transport, forcing sand to accumulate along the causeway, rapidly creating an emerged sandy isthmus (or [tombolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombolo)), linking the island to the mainland.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

This sandy isthmus rapidly inflated during the centuries following the construction of the causeway. By early Imperial Roman times, monumental buildings had been built over most of its surface. Their layout implies that the isthmus was by then nearly as wide as today. Therefore, the isthmus had completely reshaped the eastern coast of Tyre Island within 6–10 centuries after the construction of the causeway, spurring a radical transformation of the city.

## Coast Nature Reserve



A sea turtle off Tyre's Southern bay



A [green sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sea_turtle) diving through submerged antiquities (Quay of the Spring)

Tyre enjoys a reputation of having some of the cleanest beaches and waters of Lebanon.[^cite_note-Carter-2004-41][^cite_note-42] However, a UN HABITAT profile found that "seawater is also polluted due to wastewater discharge especially in the port area".[^cite_note-Maguire-2016-7] There is still also considerable pollution by solid waste.[^cite_note-Rahhal-2018-43]



A [Palestine sunbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_sunbird) on *[Tecoma capensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecoma_capensis)* near Al Mina

The [Tyre Coast Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Coast_Nature_Reserve) (TCNR) was decreed in 1998 by the [Ministry of Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Works_and_Transport_\(Lebanon\)). It is 3.5 km (2.2 mi) long and covers over 380 hectares (940 acres). The TCNR is within the best preserved stretch of sandy coastline in southern Lebanon and divided into two section zones: a 1.8 km (1 mile) sand lined beach, 500 meters (yards) wide-ranging from the Tyre Rest House in the north to the Rashidieh Refugee Camp in the South, and a stretch of 2 km (1¼ miles) with agriculture lands of small family farms and the springs of Ras El Ain with three constantly flowing [artesian wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artesian_aquifer), ranging from Rashidieh to the village of Chaetiyeh in the South.[^cite_note-Yacoub-2011-17]

The former is divided into two zones: one for tourism that features a public beach of some 900 metres (yards) and restaurant tents during the summer season hosting up to 20,000 visitors on a busy day, and another 900 metres (yards) of conservation zone as a sanctuary for sea turtles and migrating birds.[^cite_note-Rahhal-2018-43]

Due to its diverse [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna), the reserve was designated a [Ramsar Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention) in 1999 according to the international [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) for the conservation and sustainable use of [Wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland), since it is considered "the last [bio-geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeography) [ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem) in Lebanon". It is an important nesting site for [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration), the endangered [Loggerhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggerhead_sea_turtle) and [green sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sea_turtle), the [Arabian spiny mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_spiny_mouse) and many other creatures (including [wall lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacertidae), [common pipistrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_pipistrelle), and [European badger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_badger)).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] Also, there are frequent sighting of dolphins in the waters off Tyre.[^cite_note-46] Altogether, the TCNR includes:

> 275 [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) distributed over 50 families. In addition, the reserve is home to seven regionally and nationally [threatened species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened_species), 4 [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) and 10 [rare species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_species), whilst 59 species are restricted to the Eastern Mediterranean area. It is also worthy to indicate that, several [bio-indicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioindicator) species as well as 25 [medicinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicinal_plants) species were recognized. TCNR encloses flora species belonging to the various habitats: the sandy shore, rocky shore, [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_zone) and [Freshwater ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_ecosystem). A wide number of [Gramineae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae), [Fabaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabaceae), [Asteraceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteraceae) and [Umbellifereae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apiaceae) families dominate the floristic resources.[^cite_note-Yacoub-2011-17]

However, the biodiversity of the TCNR is threatened as shown by a strong decrease in the numbers of the caspian [terrapin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrapin) *[Mauremys caspica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_turtle)*, the green [toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toad) *[Bufo viridis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_green_toad)* and the [tree frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_frog) *[Hyla savigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyla_savignyi)*. Also, since the 2000s, the North American camphorweed *[Heterotheca subaxillaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotheca_subaxillaris)* has invaded the TCNR as a [neophyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neophyte_\(botany\)) from Haifa across the Blue Line.[^cite_note-Yacoub-2011-17]

During the 2006 war, turtle breeding areas were affected when the IDF bombed the conservation site.[^cite_note-47]

The [oil spill](158705.md) which devastated the coast north of [Ashkelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkelon) in February 2021 also contaminated Tyre's beaches.[^cite_note-48]

## Historical and cultural heritage



Sign marking Tyre according to the 1954 [Hague Convention for the Protection of Cultural Property in the Event of Armed Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Convention_for_the_Protection_of_Cultural_Property_in_the_Event_of_Armed_Conflict). Note the ruins of the Mamluk House (left) which has been rehabilitated since.

See also: [Tyre ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_ruins)

Arguably the most lasting Phoenician legacy for the Tyrian population has been the linguistic mark that the [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language) and [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language) languages have left on the Arabic spoken in the region of Tyre.[^cite_note-49] Most notably, the widely used term "Ba'ali" – which is used especially to describe vegetables and fruits from rain-fed, untreated agricultural production – originates from the Baal religion.[^cite_note-Badawi-2008-50] The Tyrian municipality of [Ain Baal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Baal) is apparently also named after the Phoenician deity.[^cite_note-51] The most visible part of ancient and medieval history on the other side have been the archaeological sites though:

The first archaeological excavations were by Ernest Renan in 1860 and 1861.[^cite_note-Renan-27] He was followed in the 1870s by [Johann Nepomuk Sepp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Nepomuk_Sepp). His most notable work was excavating at the [Cathedral of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Cross,_Tyre) in an attempt to find the bones of [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa).[^cite_note-52] More work was undertaken in 1903 by the Ottoman archaeologist [Theodore Makridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Makridi), curator of the Imperial Museum at [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople). Important findings like fragments of marble sarcophagi were sent to the Ottoman capital.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]



Rusty relics from Chehab's excavations at Al Mina site

An archaeological survey of Tyre was done by a French team under the leadership of Denyse Le Lasseur in 1921.[^cite_note-55] It was followed by another mission between 1934 and 1936 that included aerial surveys and diving expeditions. It was led by the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) missionary [Antoine Poidebard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Poidebard), a pioneer of [aerial archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_archaeology).[^cite_note-56]

Large-scale excavations started in 1946 under the leadership of [Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) [Maurice Chéhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Chehab) (1904–1994), "the father of modern Lebanese archaeology" who for decades headed the Antiquities Service in Lebanon and was the curator of the [National Museum of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Beirut). His teams uncovered most remains in the [Al Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Necropolis)/[Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Hippodrome) and the City Site/Roman baths.[^cite_note-Boschloos-2016-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

During the 1960s, [Honor Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_Frost) (1917–2010) – the [Cyprus](5593.md)-born pioneer of [underwater archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_archaeology) initiated several investigations "aimed at identifying and documenting the significant archaeological potential for harbour facilities within coastal Tyre". Based on the results, she suggested that the Al Mobarakee Tower may actually date back to Hellenistic times.[^cite_note-Noureddine-2018-60]

All those works stopped though soon after the 1975 beginning of the Civil War and many records were lost.[^cite_note-Boschloos-2016-57]

In 1984, the [United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organisation (UNESCO)](21786641.md) declared Tyre a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in an attempt to halt the damage being done to the archaeological sites by the armed conflict and by anarchic urban development.[^cite_note-Carter-2004-41]

In the late 1980s, "clandestine excavations" took place in the [Al-Bass cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Necropolis), which "flooded the antiquities market".[^cite_note-61]

Regular excavation activities only started again in 1995 under the supervision of Ali Khalil Badawi.[^cite_note-62] Shortly afterwards, an Israeli bomb destroyed an apartment block in the city and evidence for an early church was revealed underneath the rubble. Its unusual design suggests that this was the site of the Cathedral of Paulinus which had been inaugurated in 315 CE.[^cite_note-63]

In 1997, the first Phoenician cremation cemetery was uncovered in the [al-Bass site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Necropolis), near the Roman necropolis.[^cite_note-64] Meanwhile, Honor Frost mentored local Lebanese archaeologists to conduct further underwater investigations, which in 2001 confirmed the existence of a human-made structure within the northern harbour area of Tyre.[^cite_note-Noureddine-2018-60]

In 2003, Randa Berri, president of the National Association for the Preservation of South Lebanon's Archaeology and Heritage and wife of [Nabih Berri](592775.md), veteran leader of the [Amal Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Movement) and longtime Speaker of the Parliament of Lebanon, patronized a plan to renovate Khan Sour / Khan Al Askaar, the former Ma'ani palace, and convert it into a museum.[^cite_note-65] As of 2019, nothing was done in that regard and the ruins have kept on crumbling.

The hostilities of the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) put the ancient structures of Tyre at risk. This prompted [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s Director-General to launch a "Heritage Alert" for the site.[^cite_note-66] Following the cessation of hostilities in September 2006, a visit by conservation experts to Lebanon observed no direct damage to the ancient city of Tyre. However, the bombardment had damaged frescoes in a Roman funerary cave at the [Tyre Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_Necropolis). Additional site degradation was also noted, including "the lack of maintenance, the decay of exposed structures due to lack of rainwater regulation and the decay of porous and soft stones".[^cite_note-icomos118-67]



Archaeologists from the University of Lyon at the Al Mina/City site in 2019

Since 2008, a [Lebanese/French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese-French) team under the direction by Pierre-Louis Gatier of the [University of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lyon) has been conducting archaeological and topographical work. When international archeological missions in [Syria](7515849.md) came to a halt after 2012 due to the war there, some of them instead started excavations in Tyre, amongst them a team headed by [Leila Badre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leila_Badre), director of the [Archeological Museum of the American University of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_the_American_University_of_Beirut) ([AUB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_of_Beirut)), and Belgian archaeologists.[^cite_note-Boschloos-2016-57]

Threats to Tyre's ancient [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage) include development pressures and the illegal antiquities trade.[^cite_note-68] A highway, planned for 2011, was expected to be built in areas that are deemed archaeologically sensitive.[^cite_note-69] A small-scale geophysical survey indicated the presence of archaeological remains at proposed construction sites. The sites have not been investigated. Despite the relocation of a proposed traffic interchange, the lack of precise site boundaries confuses the issue of site preservation.[^cite_note-icomos118-67]



The ruins of Khan Rabu

A 2018 study of Mediterranean world heritage sites found that Tyre's City site has "the highest risk of coastal erosion under current climatic conditions, in addition to 'moderate' risk from extreme sea levels."[^cite_note-70] Further coastal inspection was conducted in 2019, leading to a new hypothesis about the local relative sea level rise and to discovery of yet unreported submerged coastal structures.[^cite_note-Goiran_Brocard_Evolution_of_Sea_Level-9]

Like many of the cities in the Levant and in [Lebanon](17771.md), the architecture since the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) in the 1970s has been of poor quality, which tend to threaten the cultural heritage in the built environment before the war.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-\:2-72] Meanwhile, historical buildings from the Ottoman period like Khan Rabu and Khan Sour / Khan Ashkar have partly collapsed after decades of total neglect and lack of any maintenance whatsoever.[^cite_note-\:2-72]

In 2013, the International Association to Save Tyre (IAST) made headlines when it launched an online [raffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffle) in association with [Sotheby's](541267.md) to fund the artisans' village Les Ateliers de Tyr at the outskirts of the city. Participants could purchase tickets for 100 euros to win the 1914 *Man in the Opera Hat* painting by [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso).[^cite_note-73] The proceeds totaled US\$5.26 million. The painting was won by a 25-year-old fire-safety official from [Pennsylvania](23332.md).[^cite_note-74] IAST president [Maha al-Khalil Chalabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_al-Khalil_Chalabi) is a daughter of feudal lord and politician [Kazem el-Khalil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazem_al-Khalil).[^cite_note-75] In September 2017, she opened "Les Atelier", which is located in the middle of an orange grove covering an area of 7.300 m<sup>2</sup> at the northeastern outskirts of Tyre.[^cite_note-76]

During the [2024 Israeli invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon), [UNESCO](21786641.md) gave enhanced protection to 34 cultural sites in Lebanon including the Tyre archaeological sites to safeguard them from [damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_cultural_heritage_during_the_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

## Cultural life

The first cinema in Tyre opened in the late 1930s when a café owner established makeshift film screenings.[^cite_note-Stoughton-2016-95] Hamid Istanbouli – a fisherman by profession, who was also a traditional storyteller (*hakawati*) and thus interested in cinema – projected films on the wall of a Turkish hammam.[^cite_note-Hélou-2016-96] In 1939 the Roxy opened, followed in 1942 by the "Empire":[^cite_note-Al-Ghorba-2018-97]

> By the mid-1950s there were four cinemas in Tyre, and four more soon opened in nearby [Nabatieh](43794798.md). Many also hosted live performances by famous actors and musicians, serving as community spaces where people from different backgrounds came together.[^cite_note-Stoughton-2016-95]

In 1959, the "Cinema Rivoli of Tyre" opened and quickly became one of the prime movie theatres of the country. According to [UNIFIL](787766.md), it was visited "by celebrity who's whos of the time, including [Jean Marais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Marais), [Brigitte Bardot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Bardot), [Rushdi Abaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushdy_Abaza) and Omar Hariri."[^cite_note-98] In 1964, the "Dunia" opened,[^cite_note-99] two years later followed by the "Al Hamra Cinema",[^cite_note-Al-Ghorba-2018-97] which became a venue for some of the Arab world's most famous performers, like [Mahmoud Darwish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Darwish), [Sheikh Imam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Imam), [Ahmed Fouad Negm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Fouad_Negm), [Wadih el-Safi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadih_El_Safi), and [Marcel Khalife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Khalife).[^cite_note-Stoughton-2016-95]

Meanwhile, two Tyrian artists had a major impact on the development of Lebanese music: [Halim el-Roumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halim_el-Roumi) (1919–1983) and Ghazi Kahwaji (1945–2017). Some sources claim that the famous musician, composer, singer and actor el-Roumi was born in Tyre to Lebanese parents. However, others suggest that he was born in [Nazareth](21702.md) and moved to Tyre from Palestine.[^cite_note-100] For some time, he worked as a teacher at the Jafariya High School there. In 1950 he became director of [Radio Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Lebanon)'s music department,[^cite_note-101] where he discovered the singer [Fairuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairuz) and introduced her to the [Rahbani brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahbani_brothers).[^cite_note-102] Roumi composed music for and with them in close collaborations.[^cite_note-103]

Kahwaji was Lebanon's first [scenographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenographer) and for three decades the artistic general director for the [Rahbani brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahbani_brothers) and [Fairuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairuz). He used this prominent position to promote "against [confessionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessionalism_\(religion\)) and [fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamentalism)".[^cite_note-Sarhan-2013-104] Kahwaji, who was also a professor at the [Lebanese University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_University) (LU) and the [Saint Joseph University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph_University) in Beirut,[^cite_note-105] published between 2008 and 2010 the [sarcastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcasm) three-volume book series "Kahwajiyat" about [social injustice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice) in the Arab world.[^cite_note-Sarhan-2013-104]

By then, cultural life in Tyre had been severely affected by armed conflict as well. In 1975, the commercial "Festivals de Tyr" – organised by Maha al-Khalil Chalabi, the daughter of feudal landlord and politician Kazem al-Khalil – were supposed to debut but stopped at the outbreak of the Civil War.[^cite_note-106]

Some cinemas were damaged by Israeli bombardment in 1982 and all of them eventually closed down, the last ones in 1989:[^cite_note-Stoughton-2016-95] the Hamra and the AK2000.[^cite_note-Al-Ghorba-2018-97]

In the mid-nineties though, first the idea of a commercial Tyre International Festival was revived. It has been organised since then annually in the ancient site of the Roman hippodrome, featuring international artists like [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John) and [Sarah Brightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Brightman),[^cite_note-107] as well as Lebanese stars [Wadih El Safi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadih_El_Safi), [Demis Roussos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demis_Roussos), [Kadim Al-Saher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadim_Al-Saher), [Melhem Barakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melhem_Barakat), [Julia Boutros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Boutros), and [Majida El Roumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majida_El_Roumi),[^cite_note-Badawi-2008-50] the daughter of Halim el-Roumi.

In 2006, the "Centre de Lecture et d'Animation Culturelle" (C.L.A.C.) was opened by Tyre's municipality as the first public library of the city, with support from the Lebanese Ministry of Culture and the French Embassy in Beirut. It is located in the historical building of the "Beit Daoud" next to the "Beit El Medina", the former Mamluk House, in the old town.[^cite_note-108]

In 2014, the NGO Tiro Association for Arts rehabilitated the defunct cinema Al Hamra under the leadership of "Palestinian-Lebanese street theater performer, actor, comedian, and theater director"[^cite_note-109] [Kassem Istanbouli](https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85\:Kassem%20istanbouli) (\*1986). His grandfather was one of the founders of cinema in Tyre and his father used to repair cinema projectors.[^cite_note-Hélou-2016-96]

In 2018, the Istanbouli Theatre troupe rehabilitated and moved to the Rivoli Cinema,[^cite_note-110] which had been closed since 1988,[^cite_note-111] to establish the non-commercial Lebanese National Theater as a free cultural space with free entrance and a special focus on training children and youth in arts. It also runs the "Mobile Peace Bus", which is decorated with [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) of Lebanese cultural icons, to promote arts in the villages of the neighbouring countryside.[^cite_note-112] Istanbouli has argued:

> In Tyre, we have 400 shops for [shisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hookah), one library, and one theatre. But if there are places, people will come.[^cite_note-113]

In 2019, the film *Manara* (Arabic for "lighthouse") by Lebanese director [Zayn Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayn_Alexander), who shot the movie at the Al Fanar resort in Tyre, won the Laguna Sud Award for Best Short Film at the [Venice Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Days) Strand festival.[^cite_note-114]

## Education



The Jafariya School (2019)



The IUL (2009)

There are many universities in Lebanon.

The Jafariya School was founded in 1938 by Imam Abdul Hussein Sharafeddin.[^cite_note-115] It soon expanded thanks mainly to donations from rich émigrés and thus was upgraded in 1946 to be a Secondary School, the first in Southern Lebanon (see above). It has remained one of the main schools in Tyre ever since.[^cite_note-Leichtman-2015-116]

An important role in the Tyrian education landscape is played by the charity organisation of the vanished Imam [Musa al-Sadr](2432466.md), which has been headed since his disappearance in 1978 by his sister [Rabab al-Sadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabab_al-Sadr).[^cite_note-117] While the foundation operates in various parts of the country, its main base is a compound on the southern entry of the Tyre peninsula close to the sea. A major focus is its [Orphanages](387703.md), but it also runs adult educational and [vocational training](56551.md) programmes, especially for young women, in addition to health and development projects.[^cite_note-118]

Musa Sadr also laid the groundwork for establishing the Islamic University of Lebanon (IUL) which was finally licensed in 1996 and opened a branch on the seafront, in Tyre. Its board of trustees is dominated by representatives of the Supreme Shiite Council, founded by Sadr in 1967.[^cite_note-119]

The Lebanese Evangelical School in Tyre with a history of more than 150 years is arguably the largest school in town. [Collège Élite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_%C3%89lite_\(Tyre\)), a French international school opened in 1996, is another one of a host of private schools in Tyre. The Cadmous College - a pre-kindergarten to grade 12 school, run by the Maronite missionaries - has about 10% Christian and 90% Muslim pupils.[^cite_note-120]

In August 2019, the 17-year-old Ismail Ajjawi – a Palestinian resident of Tyre and graduate of the UNRWA '[Deir Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Yassin)' High School in the [El-Buss refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Buss_refugee_camp)[^cite_note-121] – made global headlines when he scored top-results to earn a scholarship to study at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), but was [deported](412990.md) upon arrival in [Boston](24437894.md) despite valid visa.[^cite_note-122] He was readmitted ten days later to start his studies in time.[^cite_note-123]

An accurate statistical accounting is not possible, since the government of Lebanon has released only rough estimates of population numbers since 1932.[^cite_note-125]

The Lebanese nationality population of Tyre is predominantly Shia Muslim with a small but noticeable Christian community. In 2010, it was estimated that Christians accounted for 15% of Tyre's population.[^cite_note-126] In 2017, the [Maronite Catholic Archeparchy of Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Tyre) counted about 42,500 members. The Melkite Greek Catholic Archeparchy of Tyre – which not only covers the [District of Tyre](11166788.md) in the [South Governorate](1283570.md) but also neighbouring areas in the [Nabatieh Governorate](4981449.md) – registered 2,857 members in that year.[^cite_note-127]

### Refugees

The city of Tyre has become home to more than 60,000 Palestinian refugees who are mainly Sunni Muslims with some Christian families. Tyre hosted Shias from the seven villages that were depopulated in 1948, they settled in suburbs like Shabriha. As of June 2018, there were 12,281 registered persons in the Al Buss camp,[^cite_note-128] 24,929 in Burj El Shimali[^cite_note-129] and 34,584 in [Rashidieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidieh).[^cite_note-UNRWA-2023-130] In the ramshackle "gathering" of Jal Al Bahar next to the coastal highway, the number of residents was estimated to be around 2,500 in 2015.[^cite_note-131]

In all camps, the number of refugees from Syria and Palestinian refugees from Syria increased in recent years.[^cite_note-UNRWA-2023-130] Tensions developed since these new arrivals would often accept work in the citrus and banana groves "for half the daily wage" that local Palestinian refugees used to earn.[^cite_note-132]

In early 2019, some 1,500 Syrian refugees were evicted from their informal settlements around the Litani river for allegedly polluting the waters which are already heavily contaminated.[^cite_note-133]

### Foreign workers



Avenue Du Senegal

Tyre is known as "Little West [Africa](5334607.md)". Many families in Tyre have relatives in the Western Africa diaspora, especially in [Senegal](27278.md), [Sierra Leone](27308.md), [Liberia](17791.md), [Ivory Coast](57744.md) and [Nigeria](21383.md). In Senegal, most immigrants originated from Tyre. Member of the Tyrian communities there are "primarily second, third and fourth generation migrants, many of whom have never been to Lebanon." One of Tyre's main promenades is called "Avenue du Senegal".[^cite_note-Leichtman-2015-116]

As there were an estimated 250,000 foreign workers – mostly [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian) women – under the discriminatory [Kafala system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafala_system) of sponsorship in Lebanon by 2019,[^cite_note-134] there is also a large community of African migrants in Tyre. They are mainly Ethiopian women who work as domestic servants. Some of them celebrate church service at the Greek-Catholic Cathedral of Saint Thomas, which has devoted a chapel on its compound to Tyre-born [Saint Frumentius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Frumentius), the first bishop of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. In April 2014 one Ethiopian made headlines in an apparent suicide in Tyre:

> Media reports said the woman had fled last week from her employer's home. Security forces later detained the Ethiopian and returned her to her employer[^cite_note-135]

## Economy



A [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_458) with a number plate from [Lagos](85232.md), Nigeria, in Tyre

The economy of urban Tyre mostly depends on tourism, contracting services, the construction sector, and [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) from Tyrians in the diaspora, especially in West Africa.[^cite_note-Maguire-2016-7]

UNIFIL contributes greatly to the purchasing power in the Tyrian economy as well, both through spending by its individual members as well as through "quick-impact projects" like gravelling road, rehabilitating public places etc.[^cite_note-UN_OCHA-2013-12]

As of 2016, Olive trees were reported to comprise 38% of Tyre's [agricultural land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_land), but producers lacked a [collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective) [marketing strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_strategy). While [Citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) reportedly comprised 25% of the agricultural land, 20% of its [harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest) ended up [wasted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste).[^cite_note-136]



Barbour boatbuilders

Tyre houses one of the nation's major ports, though much smaller than the ports of Beirut, Tripoli, and also Sidon/Saida. Its cargo traffic has been limited to the periodical import of used cars. One day after the [2020 Beirut explosion](64752725.md) which devastated the [Port of Beirut](2301960.md) and much of the national capital on 4 August the national government reportedly decided to use the Port of Tyre as a back-up for the Port of Tripoli.[^cite_note-137]

In the harbour area, the Barbour family of shipbuilders continues to build wooden boats.[^cite_note-138] Tyre is thus one of only a few cities in the Mediterranean that has kept this ancient tradition, although the Barbour business has been struggling to survive as well. By 2004, there were "over 600 fishermen \[..] striving to make ends meet in Tyre alone".[^cite_note-139]

Lebanon's General Directorate of Land Registry and Cadastre (GDLRC) recorded for Tyre a 4.4 percent growth rate for land transactions between 2014 and 2018, the highest rate in the country during that period.[^cite_note-140] This increase in real estate prices has been largely attributed to the inflow of remittances from diaspora Tyrians.[^cite_note-Maguire-2016-7]

Off the Tyrian coast, block 9 has been awarded for [deepwater drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_drilling) of natural gas to a consortium of French company [TotalEnergies](804161.md), Italy-based [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni), and Russian [Novatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novatek).[^cite_note-141]

## Sports



The faded sign of Tadamon SC on the roof of the Municipal Stadium

[Tadamon Sour Sporting Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadamon_Sour_SC), or simply Tadamon (meaning "Solidarity"), nicknamed "The Ambassador of the South", was founded in 1946 and is thus the historically most established [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_team) of Tyre. They play their home matches at the [Tyre Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sour_Stadium) and have won one [Lebanese FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_FA_Cup) (2000–01) and two [Lebanese Challenge Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Challenge_Cup) (2013 and 2018). Tadamon's traditional rivals, [Salam Sour Sports Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salam_Sour_SC), are also based in Tyre.

According to BBC reports, Tadamon SC was stripped of its [Lebanese Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Premier_League) championship title in 2001 following [match-fixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_fixing_in_association_football) allegations.[^cite_note-142]

In the same year the club scored arguably one of its biggest transfers when [Roda Antar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roda_Antar) from its own youth teams was loaned to Germany's [Hamburger SV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_SV) for two seasons. After eight years in Germany with Hamburg, [SC Freiburg](1438835.md) and [1. FC Köln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_K%C3%B6ln) he played another six years in the [Chinese Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Super_League) and then returned to Tadamon for one final season before retirement.

A number of Lebanese Premier League professional [footballers](10669.md), who have also played for the [Lebanon national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team), originate from Tyre, namely [Rabih Ataya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabih_Ataya),[^cite_note-GSA-2022a-143] and [Nassar Nassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassar_Nassar).[^cite_note-GSA-2022b-144]
