During his reign, [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) ("The Pilgrim Pope") made 146 pastoral visits within Italy and 104 foreign trips, more than all previous popes combined. In total he logged more than 1,167,000 km (725,000 mi). He consistently attracted large crowds on his travels, some among the largest ever assembled. While some of his trips (such as to the United States and [Israel](9282173.md)) were to places that were previously visited by [Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) (the first pope to travel widely), many others were to countries that no pope had previously visited.

## Travels outside Italy

### 1970s



Millions cheer Pope John Paul II during his first visit to Poland as pontiff



[Pope John Paul II](23805.md), during his first U.S. visit in 1979, at [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_\(1923\)), New York City

Pope John Paul II's first foreign journey was a three-country visit to the Dominican Republic, Mexico and the Bahamas in January 1979. Some 18 million people were believed to have greeted the Pope during his stay in Mexico.

The Pope's second foreign visit was to his homeland, Poland, in June 1979.[^cite_note-5] This was possibly the most significant of all his trips as it, according to some historians, set in train a series of events that led to the establishment of the [Solidarity trade union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(Polish_trade_union\)), which was a key movement in the fall of Communism in eastern Europe. During his visit to Poland, John Paul visited [Warsaw](32908.md), [Gniezno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gniezno), [Kraków](16815.md), [Nowy Targ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowy_Targ), [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz), and [Jasna Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasna_G%C3%B3ra). The nine-day tour attracted millions of faithful.

The [Pope's visit to Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II's_visit_to_Ireland) on 29 September drew immense crowds. 1,250,000 people, one quarter of the population of the island of Ireland, one third of the population of the Republic of Ireland, attended the opening Mass of the visit in [Dublin](8504.md)'s [Phoenix Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Park). Over 250,000 attended a Liturgy of the Word in [Drogheda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drogheda) later that evening. Hundreds of thousands lined the streets of Dublin that night for a motorcade from Dublin Airport to the [Presidential Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ras_an_Uachtar%C3%A1in) in the Phoenix Park.

The following day, Sunday 30 September, included Masses in [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway) (300,000), [Knock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock_Shrine) (450,000) and a stop over at the monastic ruins of [Clonmacnois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonmacnois) (20,000). The final day of the visit began with a visit to the National Seminary in [Maynooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynooth) (attended by 80,000). The final Mass of the visit was at Greenpark Racecourse in [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick) in the south of the country before 400,000 people which was more than had been expected.

John Paul II made his first [visit to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_visits_to_the_United_States) in October 1979. He arrived in [Boston](24437894.md) on 1 October. The next two days were spent in New York City, where he addressed the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md). There he condemned all uses of [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camps) and [torture](47702.md) on the 40th anniversary year of [World War II](32927.md)'s start in 1939 and subsequent establishment of such camps by both the invading German Nazis and Soviet Communists, with Communism's camps and tortures continuing after the war's end in 1945. The pope later spoke to students gathered at [Madison Square Garden](75129.md), and conducted Mass at the [original Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_\(1923\))[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] for 75,000 people[^cite_note-8] as well as at [Shea Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Stadium) to an audience of over 52,000.[^cite_note-9] He arrived in [Philadelphia](50585.md) on 3 October and [Des Moines, Iowa](9163.md) on the next day before arriving in [Chicago](6886.md). There he celebrated Mass in [Grant Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Park_\(Chicago\)), met with civic leaders and [Chicago's Polish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Polonia). Chicago was the largest Catholic archdiocese in the United States at the time and the home of the largest [Polish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_diaspora) outside of Poland.[^cite_note-10] He concluded his pilgrimage to the U.S. in Washington, D.C. where he became the first Pope to visit the [White House](33057.md). He was greeted warmly by President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter), and they met privately in the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office).&#x20;

### 1980s

On 3 June 1980, he made a pilgrimage to [Lisieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisieux) in northern France, the home town of [St. Therese of the Child Jesus of the Holy Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_of_Lisieux).[^cite_note-12] In 1997 he declared St. Therese the third woman [Doctor of the Church](340345.md).[^cite_note-13] His 1980 visit to France was the first by a pope since 1814 and his journey to [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in November 1980 was the first since 1782.[^cite_note-LifeTribute-14]



The Pope with U.S. President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) and First Lady [Nancy Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Reagan), 1982

On 18 February 1981, he beatified several martyrs, including those later [canonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization), [St. Lorenzo Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Ruiz) and [Magdalene of Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_of_Nagasaki), in [Manila](184334.md). This was the first beatification to be held outside [Vatican City](32408.md). He became the first reigning pope to [travel to the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II's_visit_to_the_United_Kingdom) in 1982, where he met [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II), the [Supreme Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Governor_of_the_Church_of_England) of the [Church of England](5955.md). This trip was in danger of being cancelled due to the then current [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) (Spanish: *Guerra de las Malvinas/Guerra del Atlántico Sur*), against which he spoke out during the visit. In a dramatic symbolic gesture, he knelt in prayer alongside [Archbishop of Canterbury](2345.md) [Robert Runcie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_Robert_Runcie_of_Canterbury), in the See of the Church of England, [Canterbury Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral), founded by St [Augustine of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_of_Canterbury). They prayed at the site of the martyrdom of St. [Thomas Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Becket), meant as a show of friendship between the Roman Catholic and Anglican churches. Pope John Paul II was the first Pontiff to visit Scotland, where about 300,000 of the [Roman Catholic minority in that country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Scotland) celebrated Mass with the Pope at [Bellahouston Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellahouston_Park). On this visit the Pope faced protest from Protestant pastor [Jack Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Glass) and his followers. This visit had to be balanced for fairness with an unscheduled trip to [Argentina](18951905.md) that June.[^cite_note-15]

Throughout his trips, he stressed his devotion to the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary) through visits to various [shrines to the Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrines_to_the_Virgin_Mary), notably [Knock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock,_County_Mayo) in [Ireland](14560.md), [Fatima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_F%C3%A1tima) in Portugal, [Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe) in Mexico, [Aparecida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparecida) in Brazil and [Lourdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes) in France.

In 1984, John Paul became the first Pope to visit [Puerto Rico](23041.md). Stands were specially erected for him at [Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mu%C3%B1oz_Mar%C3%ADn_International_Airport) in [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico), where he met with governor [Carlos Romero Barceló](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Romero_Barcel%C3%B3), and at [Plaza Las Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Las_Am%C3%A9ricas_\(Puerto_Rico\)).

The pope made a pastoral trip to [Singapore](27318.md) in 1986, and was received by the Prime Minister [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew) in the [Istana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Istana). Following that, the Pope made pastoral speeches concerning the Catholic doctrines in the [National Stadium of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stadium_of_Singapore), which was viewed by a large audience.

### 1990s



Plaque in the [Almudena Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almudena_Cathedral), Madrid, marking a 1993 visit by John Paul II

The Pope's foreign travel programme for 1994 was suspended due to a fall resulting in hip-replacement surgery. Visits to Belgium, the United States, and Lebanon were cancelled as a result. The visits to Belgium and the United States took place in 1995, while the visit to Lebanon was delayed until 1997.



Pastoral visit of Pope John Paul II to Bosnia and Herzegovina

There was a plot to assassinate the Pope during his visit to [Manila](184334.md) in January 1995, as part of [Operation Bojinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Bojinka), a mass terrorist attack that was developed by [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) members [Ramzi Yousef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramzi_Yousef) and [Khalid Sheik Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_Sheik_Mohammed). A [suicide bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber) dressed as a [priest](23707.md) and planned to use the disguise to get closer to the Pope's motorcade so that he could kill the Pope by detonating himself. Before 15 January, the day on which the men were to attack the Pope during his [Philippine](23440.md) visit, an apartment fire brought investigators led by [Aida Fariscal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aida_Fariscal) to Yousef's laptop computer, which had terrorist plans on it, as well as clothes and items that suggested an assassination plot. Yousef was arrested in Pakistan about a month later, but Khalid Sheik Mohammed was not arrested until 2003. During this trip to the Philippines, on 15 January 1995, the Pope offered Mass to an estimated crowd of 4–5 million in [Luneta Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luneta_Park), Manila, the largest papal crowd ever.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] On 19 September 1996, the Pope traveled to [Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Laurent-sur-S%C3%A8vre), France to meditate and pray at the adjacent tombs of Saint [Louis de Montfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Montfort) and Blessed [Marie Louise Trichet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Louise_Trichet). On 22 March 1998, during his second visit to [Nigeria](21383.md), he beatified the Nigerian monk [Cyprian Michael Iwene Tansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian_Michael_Iwene_Tansi).[^cite_note-22] In 1999, John Paul II made a final trip to the United States, this time celebrating Mass in [St. Louis](27687.md) in the [Trans World Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_World_Dome). Over 104,000 people attended the 27 January Mass, making it the biggest indoor gathering in the United States.[^cite_note-23]

### 2000s

In 2000, he became the first modern Catholic pope to visit [Egypt](8087628.md), where he met with the [Coptic Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_of_the_Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria) and the [Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Alexandria).

In May 2001, the Pope took a pilgrimage that would trace the steps of his co-namesake, [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus), across the Mediterranean, from Greece to [Syria](7515849.md) to [Malta](19137.md). John Paul II became the first Pope to visit Greece in 1291 years. The visit was controversial, and the Pontiff was met with protests and snubbed by Eastern Orthodox leaders, none of whom met his arrival.

In [Athens](1216.md), the Pope met with Archbishop [Christodoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_Christodoulos_of_Athens), the head of the [Greek Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Greece) in Greece. After a private 30 minute meeting, the two spoke publicly. Christodoulos read a list of "13 offences" of the Roman Catholic Church against the Orthodox Church since the [Great Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East-West_Schism), including the [pillaging of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Constantinople_\(1204\)) by Crusaders in 1204. He also bemoaned the lack of any apology from the Roman Catholic Church, saying that "until now, there has not been heard a single request for pardon" for the "maniacal crusaders of the 13th century".

The Pope responded by saying, "For the occasions past and present, when sons and daughters of the Catholic Church have sinned by action or omission against their Orthodox brothers and sisters, may the Lord grant us forgiveness", to which Christodoulos immediately applauded. John Paul also said that the sacking of Constantinople was a source of "deep regret" for Catholics.

Later, John Paul and Christodoulos met on a spot where [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus) had once preached to Athenian Christians. They issued a "common declaration", saying, "We shall do everything in our power, so that the Christian roots of Europe and its Christian soul may be preserved. ... We condemn all recourse to violence, proselytism and fanaticism, in the name of religion." The two leaders then said the [Lord's Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord's_Prayer) together, breaking an Orthodox taboo against praying with Catholics.

He was the first Catholic Pope to visit and pray in a [Mosque](19894.md), in [Damascus](8914.md), [Syria](7515849.md). He visited the [Umayyad Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Mosque), where [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) is believed to be interred.

In September 2001 amid post-[September 11](5058690.md) concerns, he travelled to [Kazakhstan](16642.md), with an audience of largely Muslims, and to [Armenia](10918072.md), to participate in the celebration of the 1700 years of [Christianity](5211.md) in that nation.[^cite_note-NewYorkTimes3-27] The Pope's final visit was to the [Marian Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrines_to_the_Virgin_Mary) of [Lourdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes) in the south of France.
