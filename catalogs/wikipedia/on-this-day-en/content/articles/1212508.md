The **Eswatini national football team**, nicknamed *Sihlangu Semnikati* (King's Shield),[^cite_note-3] represents [Eswatini](27451.md), formerly known as **Swaziland**, in international [football](10568.md) and is controlled by the [Eswatini Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eswatini_Football_Association). It has never qualified for the [World Cup](11370.md) or the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) finals. Eswatini's best performance in an international tournament is a third place finish in the [2016 COSAFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_COSAFA_Cup).

On 8 June 2008, they achieved their first win in a World Cup qualifier since 1992, beating 2006 finalists [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_national_football_team) 2–1 on home soil. The team's best recent performance came in [2017 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) where Eswatini finished second in Group L above [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team) and [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team).

## History

### Beginnings

The team played its first international match against [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team), winning 2–0.[^cite_note-4] For the first decade, the national team only played [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team) and [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team), failing to register a single win from 1969 until 1984, when they beat [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho_national_football_team) 3–1 in a friendly at home. Following the Lesotho win, Eswatini entered the [1986 African Cup of Nations qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) for the first time, losing 1–8 on aggregate to [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team). The team then entered the [1987 All-Africa Games qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1987_All-Africa_Games_%E2%80%93_Men's_qualification) however they lost 2–9 on aggregate against [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team).&#x20;

The first time that Eswatini progressed past the first round of a qualifying competition was for the [1990 African Cup of Nations qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), beating [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_national_football_team) 3–1 on penalties after drawing the tie 2–2. In the second round they faced [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team) who knocked them out again, 1–3 after two legs. Eswatini next entered the [1990 SADCC Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_SADCC_Tournament?action=edit\&redlink=1), advancing past the group stage on goal difference ahead of [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team), before losing to [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team) on penalties (5–3) in the semi-finals following a 4–4 draw after extra-time. In the [1992 African Cup of Nations qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), Eswatini beat [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) (2–1) during the preliminary group stage but finished in third and failed to progress.&#x20;

Eswatini entered the [1994 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) for the first time, but ended up in second place behind [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) thereby not advancing to the final round. For the [1998 World Cup first qualification round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), Eswatini lost twice to [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon_national_football_team) (0–1 and 0–2) and were eliminated. They failed to make it past the first qualifying round for the inaugural [1997 COSAFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_COSAFA_Cup), where they lost 0–4 to [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_national_football_team), and in the [1998 COSAFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_COSAFA_Cup) where they lost to an own-goal in extra-time against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team) (0–1). Eswatini returned to the [2000 African Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) matches after missing the previous three tournaments but they fell 2–3 against [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_national_football_team) and were eliminated. They managed to qualify for the [1999 COSAFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_COSAFA_Cup) after beating [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_national_football_team) 3–1 in the qualifiers, then beat [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals 4–3 on penalties after drawing 1–1 in regular time, with [Bongani Mdluli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongani_Mdluli?action=edit\&redlink=1) having scored the equaliser for Eswatini in the 89th minute. In the semi-finals they lost to [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) on penalties, 2–4.

### 21st century

Eswatini were eliminated by [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team) in the [2002 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), losing 1–8 on aggregate. They then lost to [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_football_team) in the [2002 African Cup of Nations qualification round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) 3–5. Their next success in a competition came in the [2002 COSAFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_COSAFA_Cup) where they qualified ahead of [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) (2–1) and beat [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team) (2–0) to advance to the semis. There they lost to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) (1–4) who would go on to win the tournament. In the [2004 AFCON qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), Eswatini finished third in their group, two points behind [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) and three behind [DR Congo](1030243.md) therefore missing out on qualification.

In the following tournaments, Eswatini failed to progress past the first qualifying round. They finished bottom of their qualifying groups until [2017 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification) where they finished in second on goal-difference ahead of [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team) but were three points off of qualifying, behind [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team). In the [2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), Eswatini thrashed [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_national_football_team) 8–1 over two legs, but were beaten 0–2 by [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) to end their hopes of qualification.
