The Western and Central factions of [chimpanzees](7844.md) of the Ngogo hill region in [Kibale National Park](2435189.md), [Uganda](31816.md), have been engaged in a violent conflict since 2015.[^cite_note-nbc-1] The conflict has been characterized by one-sided violence, including killing, brutal attacks, and mutilations, by the Western faction against the Central faction.[^cite_note-nyt_apr09-2] Because the Ngogo chimpanzees formerly constituted a single peaceful community that violently split apart, this conflict has been described as a "[civil war](7085.md)".[^cite_note-3] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* said the conflict was the bloodiest among chimpanzees ever recorded.[^cite_note-nyt_apr09-2]

This is the second major conflict between chimpanzees that has been observed by [primatologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primatologists), the first being the [Gombe Chimpanzee War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe_Chimpanzee_War) of the 1970s in [Tanzania](30118.md).

## Background

See also: [Chimpanzee violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee_violence)

The community of chimpanzees living in the densely forested Ngogo region of [Kibale National Park](2435189.md) had been documented to have killed 21 members of neighbouring groups between 1999 and 2008.[^cite_note-4]

By 2015, the community consisted of about 200 members that had been living in relative cohesion for 20 years.[^cite_note-5] Though various [cliques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique) existed, they generally cooperated and defended their territory together from other chimpanzee communities. [Social cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_cohesion) began to break down, however, after several individuals of the Ngogo community, who had helped to bridge the gaps between the cliques, died from disease in 2014.[^cite_note-6] Furthermore, a new [alpha male](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_male) rose in the community, furthering tension.[^cite_note-wsj-7][^cite_note-8]

## Conflict

The main [evolutionary pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_pressure) behind male chimpanzees' canine tooth size is [socio-behavioral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_ecology): displays of dominance, intimidation and defense.

In 2015, tension among the Ngogo chimpanzees caused low-level violence, with two rival factions emerging.[^cite_note-wsj-7] These were described as the Western and Central Ngogo chimpanzees by researchers.[^cite_note-nbc-1] By 2018, the division had become complete, and the violence escalated in intensity.[^cite_note-nbc-1][^cite_note-wsj-7] The Western faction, despite being numerically inferior, launched coordinated lethal raids into the Central Ngogo chimpanzees' territory, seeking out and killing rival adult males. The raiders often [ambushed](1905.md) isolated chimpanzees, overwhelming their targets with numbers.[^cite_note-nbc-1] From 2021, the Western raiders also began to target and kill infants.[^cite_note-wsj-7] The Western faction's attacks were so successful that the conflict has been described as a "one-sided rout", with the Westerners growing in numbers from 76 to 108, while the population of the Central faction suffered a "stepwise decline".[^cite_note-nbc-1]

By 2026, at least 28 chimpanzees, including 19 infants, had been killed by the western Ngogo chimpanzees during the conflict.[^cite_note-nbc-1] All casualties had been among the members of the Central faction.[^cite_note-nyt_apr09-2]

## Media coverage

The Ngogo community was the primary subject of the 2023 [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) docuseries *[Chimp Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimp_Empire)*. Directed by [James Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Reed_\(filmmaker\)) and narrated by [Mahershala Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahershala_Ali), the series brought global attention to the "Western" and "Central" factions just as their conflict was escalating. Reed also directed the film *Rise of the Warrior Apes* before the split between the two factions.[^cite_note-11]

In April 2026, major international outlets including the [BBC](19344654.md), *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, and *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported on a study published in the journal *[Science](193513.md)*. These reports highlighted the war as the first rigorously documented "permanent fission" and subsequent "civil war" in wild chimpanzees without human intervention.[^cite_note-12]
