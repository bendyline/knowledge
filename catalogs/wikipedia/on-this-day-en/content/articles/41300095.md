**Ángel Heladio Aguirre Rivero** (born 21 April 1956) is a Mexican politician who twice served as [governor of Guerrero](8498269.md), as substitute governor from 1996 to 1999 and as elected governor from 2011 to 2014.[^cite_note-lat-1] He represented [Guerrero](195816.md) in the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Republic_\(Mexico\)) from 2006 to 2010 and sat twice in the [Chamber of Deputies](411807.md).[^cite_note-sil-lx-2]

Born in [Ometepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ometepec), Guerrero, Aguirre studied economics at the [National Autonomous University of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Autonomous_University_of_Mexico) and rose through the [Institutional Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_Revolutionary_Party) (PRI). In 1996, the state congress named him substitute governor following the resignation of [Rubén Figueroa Alcocer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Figueroa_Alcocer). He left the PRI in 2010 and won the 2011 gubernatorial election for a coalition led by the [Party of the Democratic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_the_Democratic_Revolution) (PRD). His second term was cut short by the [Iguala mass kidnapping](44077808.md) of 43 [Ayotzinapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayotzinapa_Rural_Teachers'_College) students in September 2014; after weeks of protests he stepped down on 23 October 2014.[^cite_note-lat-1] In August 2026 the [Attorney General's Office](2586359.md) arrested him on charges of concealing evidence in the Iguala case.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

## Political career

Ángel Aguirre Rivero took a degree in economics from the [National Autonomous University of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Autonomous_University_of_Mexico) (UNAM), where he also served as professor in the same faculty. In 1991, he was elected to the [Chamber of Deputies](411807.md) for [Guerrero's 6th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_federal_electoral_district_of_Guerrero) for the 1991–1994 term[^cite_note-4] and, from 1993 to 1996, he was the president of the PRI in the state of [Guerrero](195816.md).

On 12 March 1996, Governor of Guerrero [Rubén Figueroa Alcocer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Figueroa_Alcocer) resigned on account of the [Aguas Blancas massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguas_Blancas_massacre), where peasants were murdered by agents of the state police at the ford of Aguas Blancas in the municipality of [Coyuca de Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyuca_de_Ben%C3%ADtez); the same day, the [Congress of Guerrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Guerrero) appointed Aguirre as interim governor, where he served until the end of Figueroa's term on 31 March 1999.

In the [2003 mid-terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Mexican_legislative_election) he was returned to the [Chamber of Deputies](411807.md) to represent [Guerrero's 8th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_federal_electoral_district_of_Guerrero) during the [59th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIX_Legislature_of_the_Mexican_Congress)[^cite_note-5] and, in the [2006 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Mexican_general_election), he successfully ran for one of Guerrero's seats in the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_\(Mexico\)), both times on the PRI ticket.[^cite_note-sil-lx-2][^cite_note-6]

On 25 August 2010, Aguirre announced that he was resigning his membership in the PRI and intended to pursue the PRD's candidacy for governor of Guerrero.[^cite_note-7] He subsequently won the 30 January 2011 election[^cite_note-8] and took office on 1 April.[^cite_note-9]

He resigned the governorship in October 2014 in the aftermath of the [Iguala mass kidnapping](44077808.md) of 26 September 2014.[^cite_note-lat-1][^cite_note-10]

On 6 August 2026, the [Attorney General's Office](2586359.md) announced his apprehension being accused of hiding evidence on the case of the Iguala mass kidnapping.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-11]
