The **International Chopin Piano Competition** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Międzynarodowy Konkurs Pianistyczny im. Fryderyka Chopina*), also known as the **Chopin Competition**, is a piano [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_competition) in [Warsaw](32908.md), Poland, held first in 1927 and every five years since 1955. The competition is one of the founding members of the [World Federation of International Music Competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_of_International_Music_Competitions). It is entirely dedicated to the works of [Frédéric Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin), and is one of the few competitions dedicated entirely to the works of a single composer.[^cite_note-1] It is organized by the [Fryderyk Chopin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin_Institute).[^cite_note-2]

Described as the "[Olympics](22576.md) of the piano world" by *[The New York Times](30680.md)*,[^cite_note-3] the Chopin Competition is considered one of the most prestigious [competitions in classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_classical_music_competitions), known for launching pianists' international careers.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Past winners have included [Maurizio Pollini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Pollini) ([1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VI_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition)), [Martha Argerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Argerich) ([1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition)), [Krystian Zimerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krystian_Zimerman) ([1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IX_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition)), [Yundi Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yundi) ([2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XIV_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition)), [Rafał Blechacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafa%C5%82_Blechacz) ([2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XV_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition)), and [Seong-Jin Cho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seong-Jin_Cho) ([2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition)). The most recent winner is American pianist [Eric Lu](48574279.md), who won the [XIX competition](77046446.md) in 2025. Chinese pianist Yundi Li is the youngest winner[^cite_note-6] and the youngest juror in the competition's history.[^cite_note-7]

## History



[3rd Chopin Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/III_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition) (1937). Among members of the jury (sitting on the left) [Heinrich Neuhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Neuhaus), [Emil von Sauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_von_Sauer), [Guido Agosti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Agosti), and [Wilhelm Backhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Backhaus)

The competition was initiated by Polish pianist and pedagogue [Jerzy Żurawlew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_%C5%BBurawlew), who began seeking funds for a piano competition in 1925, influenced by [Aleksander Michałowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_Micha%C5%82owski). Żurawlew recalled later: "Young people at that time, not long after the end of the [Great War](4764461.md), were taking a keen interest in sports. They were dyed-in-the-wool realists in their outlook on life. I would often hear that Chopin was excessively romantic, that he enervated the soul and weakened the psyche. Some went so far as to discourage the inclusion of Chopin as required repertoire in music schools. All that showed a fundamental lack of understanding, which I found very painful ... As I watched young people's enthusiasm for sporting achievement, I finally hit upon a solution: a competition! Here was a format to bring tangible advantages to young performers of Chopin in the form of monetary prizes and an international performing career."[^cite_note-8]

Gathering funds for the competition proved to be a difficult task. As Żurawlew remembered later: "I met with utter incomprehension, indifference and even aversion. The opinion among musicians was unanimous: Chopin is so great that he can defend himself. At the Ministry, it was announced that there were no funds for it \[...] and that the whole idea was unfeasible". In this difficult situation, help arrived from Henryk Rewkiewicz—a businessman, music lover, and board member of The Warsaw Music Society, who personally guaranteed to cover the entire deficit expected to arise from the first Competition.[^cite_note-9]

Many years later Jerzy Żurawlew wrote, "I was greatly helped by my friend Henryk Rewkiewicz, director of the Match Monopoly, who offered 15,000 złoty—a substantial sum at the time—for the Competition".[^cite_note-10] Ultimately, things picked up with the election of Polish president [Ignacy Mościcki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Mo%C5%9Bcicki), who became the Chopin Competition's patron.[^cite_note-Institute-11]

Subsequent editions were organized in [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition) and [1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/III_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition); the postwar fourth and fifth editions were held in [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IV_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition) and [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition). In 1957 the competition became one of the founding members of the [World Federation of International Music Competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_of_International_Music_Competitions) in Geneva.[^cite_note-12] The prewar editions of the competition as well as three editions after [World War II](32927.md) (1955, 1960, 1965) were held in winter, close to Chopin's birthdate, 22 February. But because jurors and competitors often fell ill in this period, the organizers moved the competition to October, the month in which Chopin died.[^cite_note-13]

The [1980 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition) was marked by controversy over the elimination of [Ivo Pogorelić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Pogoreli%C4%87), who was seen as one of the favourites, in the third round. This prompted juror [Martha Argerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Argerich) to resign in protest, calling Pogorelić a "genius". Her action was supported by two other jurors, who called it "unthinkable that such an artist should not make it to the finals". Other judges spoke out about their disapproval of what they considered Pogorelić's eccentricities.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] After [Stanislav Bunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Bunin) won in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XI_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition), the jury withheld first prize in both [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition) and [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XIII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition). First prize was not awarded again until [Yundi Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yundi) won in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XIV_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition).[^cite_note-los-17]

Traditionally, on 17 October—the day of Chopin's death—a solemn mass is celebrated at the [Holy Cross Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Cross_Church,_Warsaw) in Warsaw, during which [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)'s *[Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_\(Mozart\))* is performed in accordance with Chopin's wishes.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In 2018, the Chopin Institute organized the inaugural [I International Chopin Competition on Period Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_International_Chopin_Competition_on_Period_Instruments). The [XVIII International Chopin Piano Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVIII_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition), originally scheduled for 2020, was postponed to 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-20]

## In popular culture

The Chopin Competition is a major plot device in the Japanese manga series *[Forest of Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_Piano)*, serialized from 1998 to 2015 and adapted as an [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime) from 2018 to 2019. It follows the story of Kai Ichinose, who ultimately wins the Chopin Competition.[^cite_note-32] Creator [Makoto Isshiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Isshiki) was inspired to write the series when she watched a documentary showing [Stanislav Bunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Bunin) winning the [XI International Chopin Piano Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XI_International_Chopin_Piano_Competition).[^cite_note-MediaFestival-33] The 2023 documentary film *[Pianoforte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pianoforte_\(2023_film\))*, directed by Jakub Piątek, depicts the competition through exclusive behind-the-scenes footage and premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-34]
