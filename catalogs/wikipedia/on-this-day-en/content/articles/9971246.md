# Terrorism in Australia

**Terrorism in Australia** deals with [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) activities in Australia as well as steps taken by the Australian government to counter the threat of terrorism. In 2004 the Australian government has identified [transnational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnationalism) terrorism as a threat to Australia and to Australian citizens overseas.[^cite_note-dfat-trans-nat-terror-1] Australia has experienced acts of modern terrorism since the 1960s, while the federal parliament, since the 1970s, has enacted legislation seeking to target terrorism.

*Terrorism* is defined as "an action or threat of action where the action causes certain defined forms of harm or interference and the action is done or the threat is made with the intention of advancing a political, religious and ideological or group cause".[^cite_note-2]

The 2020s have seen a notable resurgence in terrorism in Australia. The current *Australian National Terrorism Threat Level* is set to probable. In 2024, the [Australian](1222540.md) government [accused](36971672.md) the [government](50913538.md) of [Iran of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_and_state-sponsored_terrorism) state sponsored [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Iran), for allegedly [organising two arson attacks](80881934.md) in Melbourne, against a kosher restaurant and [a synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Melbourne_synagogue_attack). On 14 December 2025, two gunmen motivated by the [Islamic State](9087364.md) committed [a mass shooting](81839099.md) at [Bondi Beach](368567.md), killing 15 people at a [Hanukkah](7388.md) celebration.[^cite_note-3] This terrorist attack specifically motivated the [National Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cabinet_\(Australia\)) to agree on further [gun restrictions in Australia](450955.md).[^cite_note-4]

## Background

Further information on extremism in Australia: [Far-right politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_Australia), [Far-right terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_terrorism_in_Australia), [Islamic radicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Australia#Issues), and [Islamic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_terrorism_and_Australia)

Multiple ideologies promote terrorism in Australia including religions extremists, far-right racially motivated groups, and [Incels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incels).[^cite_note-MWM_10_January_2026-5] The current main terrorism threats within Australia are [far-right terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_terrorism_in_Australia) and the self-described [Islamic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_terrorism_and_Australia) movement ([also known as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_Islamic_State) ISIL or ISIS). Various right wing extremist groups have posed tangible terrorism threats in Australia over several decades. The [Australian Security Intelligence Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation) (ASIO) in the 1970s monitored Safari 8, the Legion of the Frontiersmen of the Commonwealth and the Australian Youth Coalition as terrorist threats, and from the 1980s through to the early 2000s sporadic terrorist activities were undertaken by [National Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Action_\(Australia\)) and the Australian Nationalist Movement.[^cite_note-6] The deadliest terror attacks involving Australians have been overseas. The greatest number of Australian victims occurred in the 2002 Bali bombings, targeting Australian tourists in Indonesia.[^cite_note-Pearlman_16_Dec_2025-7] The deadliest terror attack ever committed by an Australian was [far right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_Australia), the [Christchurch mosque massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch_mosque_massacre) that killed 51 people in [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_New_Zealand) in 2019.[^cite_note-Pearlman_16_Dec_2025-7][^cite_note-WION_15_December-8] A number of incidents relating to the [Islamic State](9087364.md) group [have involved Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Islamic_State_and_Australia).[^cite_note-ANS_26_July_2014-9]

Researcher Kristy Campion argues that the far-right in Australia is itself diverse and that government must not rely on stereotypes. Campion argues for a three type model. 1) ethnocentric types who attack others based culture, race or religion; 2) anti-government types who attack government institutions and officials; 3) religious extremists.[^cite_note-10]

Terrorists attacks have occurred in Australia over the country's history,[^cite_note-13] including the following notable incidents.

### Soviet Embassy bombing (1971)

The Embassy of the USSR was attacked by a crude bomb on 17 January 1971 in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra).[^cite_note-14]

### Sydney Yugoslav General Trade and Tourist Agency bombing (1972)

Main article: [Sydney Yugoslav General Trade and Tourist Agency bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Yugoslav_General_Trade_and_Tourist_Agency_bombing)

The Sydney Yugoslav General Trade and Tourist Agency bombing occurred in [Haymarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket,_New_South_Wales), [Sydney](27862.md) on 16 September 1972; the attack injured sixteen people. The perpetrators of the attack were believed to be Croatian separatists.

### Sydney Hilton Hotel bombing (1978)

Main article: [Sydney Hilton Hotel bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Hilton_Hotel_bombing)

The Sydney Hilton Hotel bombing occurred on 13 February 1978; a bomb exploded outside the Hilton Hotel in [George Street, Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Street,_Sydney), which was hosting the first [Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting). Two garbage collectors and a police officer were killed and nine others were injured.

### Family Court of Australia attacks (1980–1985)

Main article: [Family Court of Australia attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Court_of_Australia_attacks)

On 23 June 1980, [David Opas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Opas), a judge of the Family Court, was shot dead outside his home in [Woollahra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woollahra). In March 1984, a bomb destroyed the [Belrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belrose) home of fellow judge [Richard Gee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gee_\(judge\)), who survived. In April, a bomb exploded in the Family Court building in [Parramatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parramatta). In July, the wife of judge [Ray Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Watson_\(judge\)) was killed when a bomb exploded on their doorstep in [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich,_New_South_Wales). In July 1985 a bombing at a [Jehovah's Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witness) hall in [Casula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casula) killed the minister, Graham Wykes and injured 13 others.[^cite_note-famcourt-15]

On 29 July 2015, 68-year-old Leonard John Warwick was arrested at [Campbelltown, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbelltown,_New_South_Wales). He was charged with 32 offences, including four counts of murder, one of attempted murder, and 13 counts of burning or maiming with an explosive substance.[^cite_note-daily-tele-court-30-jul-15-16][^cite_note-revenge-17] On 3 September 2020, Warwick was sentenced to life in prison without the possibility of parole.[^cite_note-18] He died in prison on 14 February 2025.[^cite_note-abc-warwick-death-19]

### Iwasaki resort bombing (1980)

At 2am on 29 November 1980, the day of the [Queensland State election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Queensland_state_election), a [nitropril and fuel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANFO) bomb was detonated with [gelignite](226403.md) at the Iwasaki resort construction site north of [Yeppoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeppoon).[^cite_note-smhIwasaki1980-20][^cite_note-Lee2015-21] The blast created a seven-metre-wide crater in a block of holiday units under construction.[^cite_note-smhIwasaki1980-20] Premier [Joh Bjelke-Petersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joh_Bjelke-Petersen) and MP [Ben Humphreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Humphreys) labelled the incident a terrorist attack,[^cite_note-Humphreys1980-22][^cite_note-smhIwasaki1980-20][^cite_note-Gistitin2014-23] and a caller to [Rockhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockhampton)'s *[The Morning Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Morning_Bulletin)* newspaper claimed responsibility on behalf of the "Queensland Republican Army".[^cite_note-smhIwasaki1981-24] The media saw the motivation behind the attack as increasing resentment over Japanese land ownership in Queensland, while Petersen claimed that it was a racist attack.[^cite_note-Gistitin2014-23] Two men, Eric John Geissman and Kerry Geissman, were committed to trial on 15 May 1981.[^cite_note-smhIwasaki1981-24] Both were found not guilty after questions were raised regarding police misconduct in the investigation, a [common problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzgerald_Inquiry) in Queensland at the time.[^cite_note-Gistitin2014-23]

### Sydney Turkish Consul General assassination (1980)

On 17 December 1980, Sydney Turkish Consul General [Şarık Arıyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Ear%C4%B1k_Ar%C4%B1yak) and his security attaché Engin Sever were assassinated by two people on motorcycles wielding firearms in Sydney. The [Justice Commandos of the Armenian Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Commandos_of_the_Armenian_Genocide) claimed responsibility but the culprits were never identified and no charges were laid. The Consul General was gunned down despite having taken precautions in the form of not travelling in the official consulate Mercedes Benz vehicle and instead being chauffeured in the trailing security attaché's car.[^cite_note-25] In December 2019, it was announced that [New South Wales](21654.md) [Joint Counter Terrorism Team](341221.md) had established Strike Force Esslemont to re-investigate the murders.[^cite_note-ata-esslemont-26] The reward, for information that leads to an arrest and conviction, was increased from A\$250,000 to A\$1 million.[^cite_note-smh-reward-increase-27] This was the first ever million-dollar reward offered in Australia for an act of terrorism.[^cite_note-nswpolice-reward-esslemont-28]

In August 2020 police Marine Area Command divers searched [Greenwich Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich,_New_South_Wales) in relation to the assassination.[^cite_note-abc-divers-turk-consul-kill-29]

### Jack van Tongeren and the ANM (1980s, 2004)

Main article: [Jack van Tongeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_van_Tongeren)

Throughout the 1980s, the [Western Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian) neo-Nazi group "The Australian Nationalist Movement", led by [Jack van Tongeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_van_Tongeren), engaged in a series of bombings of Asian restaurants and businesses, political violence, murder of a suspected informant and other acts to intimidate the Asian population. Van Tongeren was eventually imprisoned for a long period of time until his release in the early to mid-2000s. He resumed his activities until he was re-arrested in 2004 as part of Operation Atlantic, prompting a judge to order him to leave the state.[^cite_note-30]

In February 2004 three Chinese restaurants in [Perth](24355.md) were firebombed in the early hours of the morning.[^cite_note-31] The police also identified a plot to harm Jim McGinty and his family, two police officers and an ethnic community leader, and raided Van Tongeren's home.[^cite_note-32] After two separate man-hunts, the second after van Tongeren skipped bail, Van Tongeren was found and arrested in the [Boddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boddington,_Western_Australia) area south-east of Perth.[^cite_note-33] Van Tongeren was found guilty of planning to bomb Chinese restaurants in Perth and of organising a destructive hate campaign in 2007, but was given a 2-year suspended sentence on account of ill health.[^cite_note-abc.net.au-34] His right-hand man, John Van Blitterswyk, was given a 2-year, 4-month sentence.[^cite_note-35]

### 1981 alleged Croatian nationalist bombings in Sydney

This section is an excerpt from [Croatian Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Six).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Croatian_Six\&action=edit)]

[The Croatian Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Six) ([Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Hrvatska šestorka*, consisting of Max Bebić, Vic Brajković, Tony Zvirotić, Joe Kokotović, his brother Ilija Kokotović and Mile Nekić)[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_dijaspora-36] were six [Croatian-Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian-Australian) men sentenced to 15 years jail in 1981 for a [conspiracy](7230.md) to bomb several targets in [Sydney](27862.md),[^cite_note-37] including a [Yugoslavian](34244.md) [travel agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_agent), the former [Elizabethan Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethan_Theatre) in Newtown and a major [water supply](684928.md) line in St Marys in western Sydney.[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_fix-38] The trial was one of the longest in Australian legal history,[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_fix-38] occupying 172 sitting days and with 111 witnesses giving testimonies.[^cite_note-39] An appeal for these convictions and sentences failed,[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_framed-40] and the men were subsequently imprisoned for 10 years before being released in 1991.[^cite_note-41]

Media investigations since the trial, such as for the ABC's *[Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners_\(Australian_TV_program\))* programme and *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* newspaper, suggested that much of the evidence on which the six were charged was fabricated and that the men were set up as part a [sting operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_operation) by the Yugoslav foreign [intelligence service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_service), [UDBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDBA). Intelligence sources later confirmed that Dr Georgi Trajkovski, the Yugoslav Consul General in Melbourne, was a UDBA operative and a key player in the Croatian Six set up.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Disgraced former detective [Roger Rogerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Rogerson), one of the arresting officers, later admitted that [planting evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planting_evidence) during the 1970s and 80s was part of police culture.[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_ABC_2021-44] Australian [investigative journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_journalist) Chris Masters revealed in 1991 that chief witness of the case, known by the name of Vice Vrkez, was actually UDBA's agent Vitomir Misimović, who infiltrated in Croatian community in Australia.[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_dijaspora-36]

### Israeli consulate and Hakoah Club bombing (1982)

The [bombing of the Israeli Consulate and Hakoah Club in Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Israeli_Consulate_and_Hakoah_Club_bombings) occurred on 23 December 1982. The two bombings occurred on the same day within five hours of each other. The initial case led to a single arrest though charges were later dropped. In 2011, the NSW police and Australian federal police reopened the case citing new leads.[^cite_note-jewishnews1982-45][^cite_note-smh1982-46][^cite_note-newsau1982-47][^cite_note-abc1982-48]

### Turkish consulate bombing (1986)

The [Melbourne Turkish consulate bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Turkish_consulate_bombing) occurred on 23 November 1986; a [car bomb](155282.md) exploded in a carpark beneath the [Turkish](11125639.md) Consulate in [South Yarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yarra), [Melbourne](17306237.md), killing the bomber who failed to correctly set up the explosive device. Levon Demirian, a Sydney resident with links to the [Armenian Revolutionary Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), was charged over the attack and served 10 years.[^cite_note-49]

### Attempted assassination of Eddie Funde (1989)

In 1989, two [Skinhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinhead) youths, inspired by the Australian-based [neo-Nazi](54361.md) [National Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Action_\(Australia\)) group, fired shotguns at the home of [African National Congress](2503.md) representative Eddie Funde in an assassination attempt. Head of National Action, [James Saleam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Saleam), was sentenced to three-and-a-half years in jail for ordering the assassination.[^cite_note-50] The trial judge described the assassination as "an act of naked political terrorism".[^cite_note-51]

### Perth French Consulate bombing (1995)

In 1995, terrorists [firebombed the French Consulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_French_consulate_bombing_in_Perth) in Perth.[^cite_note-latimes-perth-52]

### Abortion clinic attack (2001)

Main article: [Peter James Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_James_Knight)

On 16 July 2001, [Peter James Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_James_Knight), described as an "obsessive anti-abortionist" who lived alone in a makeshift camp in rural New South Wales, attacked the East Melbourne Family Planning clinic, a privately run clinic providing [abortions](765.md), carrying a rifle, and large quantities of kerosene and lighters. He shot and killed a security guard at the clinic before his capture and arrest. He was charged and convicted of murder, and was sentenced to life imprisonment with a non-parole period of 23 years.[^cite_note-53] Australian terrorism academic Clive Williams listed the attack amongst incidents of politically motivated violence in Australia.[^cite_note-54]

### Holsworthy Barracks terror plot

Main article: [Holsworthy Barracks terror plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holsworthy_Barracks_terror_plot)

Five arrested for an Islamist terrorist plot uncovered in August 2009 targeting Holsworthy Barracks—an Australian Army training area with automatic weapons. Given lengthy prison terms, the judge called them an ongoing threat to society as long as they were unrepentant of their jihadist attitudes. Connected with the Somali-based terrorist group [al-Shabaab](8726463.md).[^cite_note-55]

### Endeavour Hills stabbings (2014)

Main article: [2014 Endeavour Hills stabbings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Endeavour_Hills_stabbings)

On 24 September 2014, an 18-year-old man, Numan Haider, was shot and killed by police outside [Endeavour Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endeavour_Hills) police station. Victoria Police Assistant Commissioner Luke Cornelius said Haider had been asked to come to the police station to discuss behaviour "which had been causing some concern". When the man arrived outside the station, he stabbed the two officers as they went to meet him. The two stabbed officers, one from Victoria Police and one from the Australian Federal Police, were working together as part of a joint operation on counter-terrorism between the AFP and Victoria Police.[^cite_note-age-end-hills-56] Haider was found to be carrying two knives and an [Islamic State](9087364.md) flag.[^cite_note-57]

### Lindt Cafe siege (2014)

Main articles: [Lindt Cafe siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindt_Cafe_siege), [Australia–Iran relations](36971672.md), [Islam in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Australia), and [Military history of Australia § 21st century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Australia#21st_century)

On 15 December 2014, [Man Haron Monis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Haron_Monis), took 17 people hostage inside a Lindt chocolate café in [Martin Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Place), Sydney. He forced hostages to hold up a [jihadist black flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Standard#Jihadist_black_flag) against a window of the café. In the early hours of 16 December, police breached the café and fatally shot Monis following the escape of several hostages. Two hostages also died, while another four people, including a police officer, were injured in the incident.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

Nick O'Brien, associate professor of counter terrorism at Charles Sturt University has said [Islamic State's magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabiq_\(magazine\)) claim that the Sydney siege gunman is a righteous [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) should not be lightly dismissed.[^cite_note-60] Dr David Martin Jones, Senior Lecturer at the School of Government, University of Tasmania has said not to underestimate the politically destabilising intent of Monis's lone-actor violence, as it is a considered tactic and a strategic goal of ISIL.[^cite_note-61]

He had a long history of proven violence and violent threats within Australia.[^cite_note-Iran_says_it_requested_extradition-62] He had Shia family heritage but he had become a self-appointed Sunni cleric. He was a religious eccentric whose ideas had been rejected by the local Muslim community.[^cite_note-Iran_says_it_requested_extradition-62] He had been wanted on criminal changes in Iran, primarily related to fraud. Iran had notified Australia and Interpol several years earlier.[^cite_note-\:3-63] They requested his extradition 14 years before the attack.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] He was not extradited because Australia did not have an extradition treaty with Iran.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-\:3-63] After the attack the Iranian ambassador said that the attack would not have happened if Monis had been extradited.[^cite_note-\:3-63] The criminal charges were financial, but a foreign ministry told the ABC that Iran had also repeatedly discussed his psychological state with Australian officials.[^cite_note-67]

#### Debate about terrorism categorisation

The designation of the 2014 Lindt Cafe siege, also known as the Sydney hostage crisis or the [Martin Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Place) siege, has been subject to debate among terrorism experts and news commentators. Initially, during the early stages of the incident, the Australian government and NSW authorities did not label the event as a terrorist attack;[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] however, as the siege continued, NSW police authorised the engagement of the state's counterterrorism task force, treating the incident as an act of terrorism.[^cite_note-70] Commentators have debated whether the perpetrator of the attack, [Man Haron Monis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Haron_Monis), was in fact a terrorist and whether his actions can be classified as an act of terrorism. One terrorism expert described Monis's actions as those of a "[lone-wolf terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_wolf_attack) ... driven by a desire for attention and to be in the spotlight."[^cite_note-Newscom-71][^cite_note-Fraser-72] Another wrote in an [opinion column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_column) that the attack "was very different from first-generation or second-generation terrorist attacks—but it was terrorism, and terrorism of a brutal and more unpredictable sort."[^cite_note-Wesley-73] Scott Stewart supervisor of the analysis of terrorism and security issues for [Statfor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statfor) said that this hostage-incident exhibits many of the elements associated with grassroots terrorism.[^cite_note-74] By contrast, criminologist Mark Lauchs stated that the event "was not about religion and neither was it a terrorist attack."[^cite_note-Newscom-71] Media outlets have also provided conflicting designations for Monis; John Lehmann, editor of *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Telegraph_\(Sydney\))*, wrote how Monis filled the criteria of an Islamic State terrorist,[^cite_note-75] while a columnist for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* wrote how the designation of a terrorist is misplaced and would only serve the interests of ISIL.[^cite_note-76] On 15 January 2015, Australia's Treasurer [Joe Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Hockey) declared the siege in Sydney's Martin Place as a terrorist incident for insurance purposes.[^cite_note-77]

The difference between terrorism and terrorising acts was noted in one analysis as "enormously important"—in Monis's case, terrorism "was clearly an element, but he was coming to the end of his rope with a variety of legal processes; there was clearly some mental instability."[^cite_note-Feneley3-78] One argument was that the gunman's lack of ties to any movement did not preclude his being a terrorist as it is "an inclusive club".[^cite_note-Maley3-79]

### Parramatta shooting (2015)

Main article: [2015 Parramatta shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Parramatta_shooting)

On 2 October 2015, an Iranian-born Iraqi-Kurdish youth shot dead 58-year-old Curtis Cheng, an accountant who worked for the [New South Wales Police Force](563673.md), outside the [Parramatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parramatta) Police headquarters.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] The terrorist then shot at [special constables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Constable#Australia) guarding the building, and was shot dead by them. NSW Police Commissioner [Andrew Scipione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Scipione) said "We believe that his actions were politically motivated and therefore linked to terrorism." Four other 'alleged Islamic State members' were arrested and charged in relation to the shooting.[^cite_note-abc-fourth-charged-83]

### Minto stabbing (2016)

On 10 September 2016, Islamist Ihsas Khan attacked a man in a park in [Minto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minto,_New_South_Wales), New South Wales. The victim was chased and repeatedly stabbed or slashed with a knife. Khan was charged with attempted murder and with committing a terrorist act.[^cite_note-smh-going_to_die-84] Crown prosecutor Peter Neil said there was evidence to suggest Khan had been planning to attack a civilian at random on 11 September to coincide with the anniversary of the [Al Qaeda attacks on the United States](5058690.md).[^cite_note-Sydney_terrorism_accused_Ihsas_Khan_wanted_police_to_kill_him,_court_hears-85]

### Queanbeyan stabbing (2017)

Main article: [2017 Queanbeyan stabbing attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Queanbeyan_stabbing_attacks)

On 7 April 2017, a pair of 15- and 16-year-old boys entered a service station in the city of [Queanbeyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queanbeyan), New South Wales and stabbed the attendant, 29-year-old Zeeshan Akbar of Pakistani descent, who died at the scene. Two other men were also attacked and injured nearby. Elsewhere a man was [carjacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carjacked), struck with a hammer and stabbed. The 16-year-old's mother had told police that she believed her son had been radicalised in recent weeks and that he sympathised with Islamic State and had also posted concerning posts on Facebook. ## Notable terror plots

### Bob Hawke assassination plot

In 1975, the Palestinian [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_Organization) terrorist group and the Australian branch of the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) (PFLP) terrorist group plotted to assassinate future Australian Prime Minister [Bob Hawke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hawke), then Australian Labor Party president, along with a number of notable Jewish journalists seen as being pro-Israel. A Black September member visited Australia under the guise of a journalist and was provided with materials from Australian PFLP members and returned to Israel; the Black September member who intended to carry out the attack was killed by Israeli forces before he could return to Australia.[^cite_note-114]

### 1981 alleged Croatian nationalist bombings in Sydney

This section is an excerpt from [Croatian Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Six).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Croatian_Six\&action=edit)]

[The Croatian Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Six) ([Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Hrvatska šestorka*, consisting of Max Bebić, Vic Brajković, Tony Zvirotić, Joe Kokotović, his brother Ilija Kokotović and Mile Nekić)[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_dijaspora-36] were six [Croatian-Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian-Australian) men sentenced to 15 years jail in 1981 for a [conspiracy](7230.md) to bomb several targets in [Sydney](27862.md),[^cite_note-115] including a [Yugoslavian](34244.md) [travel agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_agent), the former [Elizabethan Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethan_Theatre) in Newtown and a major [water supply](684928.md) line in St Marys in western Sydney.[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_fix-38] The trial was one of the longest in Australian legal history,[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_fix-38] occupying 172 sitting days and with 111 witnesses giving testimonies.[^cite_note-116] An appeal for these convictions and sentences failed,[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_framed-40] and the men were subsequently imprisoned for 10 years before being released in 1991.[^cite_note-117]

Media investigations since the trial, such as for the ABC's *[Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners_\(Australian_TV_program\))* programme and *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* newspaper, suggested that much of the evidence on which the six were charged was fabricated and that the men were set up as part a [sting operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_operation) by the Yugoslav foreign [intelligence service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_service), [UDBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDBA). Intelligence sources later confirmed that Dr Georgi Trajkovski, the Yugoslav Consul General in Melbourne, was a UDBA operative and a key player in the Croatian Six set up.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] Disgraced former detective [Roger Rogerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Rogerson), one of the arresting officers, later admitted that [planting evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planting_evidence) during the 1970s and 80s was part of police culture.[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_ABC_2021-44] Australian [investigative journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_journalist) Chris Masters revealed in 1991 that chief witness of the case, known by the name of Vice Vrkez, was actually UDBA's agent Vitomir Misimović, who infiltrated in Croatian community in Australia.[^cite_note-Croatian_Six_dijaspora-36]

### Faheem Khalid Lodhi

Main article: [Faheem Khalid Lodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faheem_Khalid_Lodhi)

[Faheem Khalid Lodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faheem_Khalid_Lodhi) is an Australian architect accused of an October 2003 plot to bomb the national electricity grid or Sydney defence sites in the cause of violent jihad. He was convicted by a New South Wales Supreme Court jury in June 2006 on terrorism-related offences,[^cite_note-120] namely:

- Preparation for terrorist attack, by seeking information for the purpose of constructing explosive devices

- Seeking information and collecting maps of the Sydney electricity supply system and possessing 38 aerial photos of military installations in preparation for terrorist attacks

- Possessing terrorist manuals detailing how to manufacture poisons, detonators, explosives and incendiary devices

In his judgement, Justice Anthony Whealy illustrated that Lodhi's behaviour breached the rules under the *Anti Terrorism Act 2004* (Cth),[^cite_note-121] Crimes Act 1914 (Cth), the Criminal Code and the Crimes (Internationally Protected Persons) Act 1976 (Cth)[^cite_note-Regina_v_Lodhi-122]

His intended targets were the national electricity supply system, the [Victoria Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Barracks,_Sydney), [HMAS *Penguin* naval base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Penguin_\(naval_base\)), and [Holsworthy Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holsworthy_Barracks). Justice Anthony Whealy commented at sentencing that Lodhi had "the intent of advancing a political, religious or ideological cause, namely violent jihad" to "instill terror into members of the public so that they could never again feel free from the threat of bombing in Australia."[^cite_note-123]

Accordingly, Whealy said the sentence to be imposed "must be a substantial one to reflect the important principles of deterrence and denunciation. In relation to count 2 the appropriate sentence, in my view, is one of imprisonment for a term of 20 years. The sentence is to commence on 22 April 2004 and to expire on 21 April 2024."[^cite_note-Regina_v_Lodhi-122]

### Neil Prakash

Main article: [Neil Prakash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Prakash)

Neil Prakash is a former Buddhist from Melbourne who became a jihadist and changed his name to Abu Khaled al-Cambodi.[^cite_note-124]

He was linked to a number of domestic terror threats, including an alleged Anzac Day terror plot in Melbourne and the shooting [death of NSW police worker Curtis Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Parramatta_shooting) in western Sydney in 2015. Later, he fled to Syria and became a senior recruiter for the Islamic State in Iraq and Syria, appearing in propaganda videos and magazines with the intention of recruiting people to commit acts of terrorism.

Prakash was reportedly killed by a targeted US air strike in northern Iraq in May 2016.[^cite_note-125] Australian Prime Minister [Malcolm Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Turnbull) commented: "Neil Prakash's death is a very, very positive development in the war against Daesh and the war against terror".[^cite_note-126] The *[Herald Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Sun)* later reported that Australian security officials were "almost certain" that Prakash had not been killed in the airstrike and had continued to act as a recruiter for the group.[^cite_note-127] In November 2016, Australian counter-terrorism officials confirmed that Prakash was still alive, and had been arrested after attempting to enter Turkey from Syria.[^cite_note-notdead-128] On 16 March 2019, he was convicted in Turkey of membership in a terrorist organisation and sentenced to seven-and-a-half years in prison.[^cite_note-abc-sentenced-2019-03-16-129]

In early December 2022 Prakash was extradited back to Australia and charged in [Melbourne Magistrates' Court](3296758.md) with terrorism-related offences. He was due to reappear in court in late February 2023.[^cite_note-abc-returned-3-dec-22-130]

### Australians joining external conflicts

See also: [Foreign fighters in the Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_fighters_in_the_Croatian_War_of_Independence), [Foreign fighters in the Syrian and Iraqi Civil Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_fighters_in_the_Syrian_and_Iraqi_Civil_Wars), and [Collaboration with the Islamic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_the_Islamic_State)

The [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL), proscribed by the government as a terrorist organisation,[^cite_note-166] has targeted [Australian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Muslims) for recruitment.[^cite_note-Lloyd,_Peter-167] Making use of [social media](5897742.md), recruiters target those vulnerable to radicalisation,[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-ISISevolution-169] and encourage local jihad activities.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Some of those targeted have been minors, including a 17-year-old who was arrested in Melbourne in May 2015 for plotting to detonate [home-made bombs](265112.md),[^cite_note-her-sun-ied-172] and sentenced to 7 years' jail.[^cite_note-age-mhk-ied-sentence-173] In June 2014, the government claimed that roughly 150 Australians had been recruited to fight in the conflicts in Syria and Iraq.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-Bourke-175] A list released in April 2015 showed that most were young males who have come from a range of occupations, including students.[^cite_note-176] It was also reported at the time that 20 Australians had been killed fighting overseas for terror groups,[^cite_note-177] with 249 suspected jihadists prevented from leaving Australia.[^cite_note-178] The [Australian Border Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Border_Force) Counter-Terrorism Unit, tasked with stopping jihadists from leaving the country,[^cite_note-179] had cancelled more than 100 passports by the end of March 2015.[^cite_note-ISISevolution-169] Several jihadists have expressed the desire to return to Australia,[^cite_note-180] but Prime Minister [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott) has said that any who do would be prosecuted on their arrival.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

In May 2016, in what became known as the '[Tinnie terror plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinnie_terror_plot)', five men were arrested as they towed a boat in the Northern Territory. A sixth was arrested in Melbourne. The group was charged with "making preparations for incursions into foreign countries to engage in hostile activities." They had intended to go by sea to help [Islamic insurgents in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_conflict_in_the_Philippines) overthrow their government. All six men were jailed. As of April 2020 two of them had been released.[^cite_note-9news-release-thorne-183] The leader, [Musa Cerantonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Cerantonio) was sentenced to seven years.[^cite_note-abc-cerantonio-sentenced-184]

On 1 September 2016 Hamdi Alquidsi was sentenced to 8 years' jail (6 years minimum) by Justice Christine Adamson. In July he was found guilty of seven counts of "supporting engagement in armed hostilities" in Syria. Two men who he helped travel to Syria were killed there, two others have returned to Australia. The fate of another two is unknown, while the last never left. Alqudsi will be eligible for release in July 2022.[^cite_note-abc-alquidsi-6years-185]

### September 2014 AFP raids (Operation Appleby)

#### Raids in Sydney and Brisbane (18 September 2014)

In the early hours of 18 September 2014, large teams of [Australian Federal Police](341221.md) (AFP) and other security agencies conducted search operations in both Sydney and Brisbane. Australian Prime Minister [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott) has alluded to an alleged plot aimed at conducting a random act of terrorism as the reason for the police action.[^cite_note-smh-raids-18-sep14-186] This action is described as the largest in Australian history to date. One man arrested, from [Guildford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guildford,_New_South_Wales), allegedly conspired to commit a "horrifying" terrorist act with a man believed to be the most senior Australian Islamic State leader.[^cite_note-smh-raids-18-sep14-186]

#### Melbourne raids (30 September 2014)

On 30 September 2014 there were more raids in [Melbourne](17306237.md). The AFP executed seven search warrants in [Broadmeadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadmeadows), [Flemington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemington,_Victoria), [Kealba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kealba), [Meadow Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadow_Heights) and [Seabrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabrook,_Victoria). ### February 2015 Sydney raid

On 10 February 2015 two men were arrested in [Fairfield, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield,_New_South_Wales), and charged with "Acts done in preparation, for, or planning terrorists acts".[^cite_note-nsw-cops-12feb15-188][^cite_note-abc-pm-murder-189] On the morning of 10 February police were informed the two were planning a terrorist attack. They were quickly placed under surveillance and tracked. When they purchased a [hunting knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_knife) from a store about 3\:00 pm, NSW Joint Counter Terrorism Team (JCTT) forces decided to intervene and soon after 4\:00 pm the men were arrested.[^cite_note-abc-pm-warn-190]

The men were a 24-year-old student from Iraq, and a 25-year-old nurse who moved from Kuwait in 2012. The two were unknown to police until the tip-off.[^cite_note-abc-pm-warn-190] The men's residence, a vehicle and places of work were searched.[^cite_note-nsw-cops-12feb15-188] They were found with a machete, a hunting knife, a homemade [Islamic State flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_flag) and "a video which depicted a man talking about carrying out an attack", according to NSW Police [Deputy Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Commissioner) (Specialist Operations) Catherine Burn.[^cite_note-abc-pm-murder-189] One of the men arrested appeared in the video.[^cite_note-abc-pm-murder-189]

Burn also said: "We will allege that both of these men were preparing to do this act yesterday" and "We believe that the men were potentially going to harm somebody, maybe even kill somebody ...".[^cite_note-abc-pm-murder-189]

The JCTT investigation has been given the code name Operation Castrum.[^cite_note-nsw-cops-12feb15-188]

## Threats

### Far-right terrorism

Main article: [Far-right terrorism in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_terrorism_in_Australia)

In the wake of the 2019 [Christchurch mosque terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch_mosque_shootings) at [Al Noor Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Noor_Mosque,_Christchurch) and [Linwood Islamic Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linwood_Islamic_Centre) in [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch), New Zealand, in which 51 people were murdered, by Australian [Brenton Harrison Tarrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenton_Harrison_Tarrant) from [Grafton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafton,_New_South_Wales), a self-described "[Ethno-nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism)", "[Eco-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-fascist)", "[Kebab removalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebab_removalist)" "[racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racist)", New South Wales Police Commissioner Michael Fuller identified the likelihood of right wing [lone wolf terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_wolf_terrorist) attacks in Australia as "an emerging risk" and indicated he will consider boosting resources into the anti-terrorism squad focused on right-wing extremists.[^cite_note-210] Recent examples of such right wing extremist 'lone wolves' include white supremacist Michael Holt, jailed for weapons and child pornography offences after threatening to carry out a mass shooting at a shopping centre on the NSW Central Coast,[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212] and Phillip Galea, convicted of terrorism charges relating to planned bombings of left wing organisations in Melbourne.[^cite_note-Percy-158]

In 2015 the [New South Wales Police Force](563673.md)'s Counter Terrorism and Special Tactics Command assessed that members of the anti-government [sovereign citizen movement](1814025.md) posed a potential terrorist threat. At the time there were reported to be 300 members of this movement in the state. While there had been no incidents of violence associated with sovereign citizens in Australia at that time, the NSW Police were concerned that members of the movement were becoming radicalised and could commit attacks such as those made by sovereign citizens in the United States.[^cite_note-213]

### Islamic State

Main article: [Islamic State terrorism and Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_terrorism_and_Australia)

A number of incidents relating to the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL) terrorist group [have involved Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Islamic_State_and_Australia) and garnered the attention of the Australian public. In 2014, two Australian Islamic extremists made a propaganda video encouraging Australians to join the [ISIL](9087364.md),[^cite_note-Lloyd,_Peter-167] According to the Australian government, up to 150 Australians "have been or are currently overseas fighting with extremists in Iraq and Syria".[^cite_note-Bourke-175] Some of their activities are thought to be [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-214] Australian man, [Khaled Sharrouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Sharrouf), posted a picture of himself, and another of his son, holding a decapitated soldier's head.[^cite_note-215]

In 2015 it was reported that more than 20 Australians who have fought with [ISIL](9087364.md) have returned and are being monitored by security agencies. The [Attorney-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_for_Australia) Senator [George Brandis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Brandis) has expressed concern that those fighting jihad, then returning from the Middle East, represent, "the most significant risk to Australia's security that we have faced in many years."[^cite_note-221] The [Australian Security Intelligence Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation) (ASIO) is concerned that Australians fighting jihad may return home to plan terror attacks.[^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223] In October 2014, ISIL published an online video in which a teenage Australian Jihadi, Abdullah Elmir, threatened the United States and Australia, naming U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and Australian Prime Minister [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott) as targets.[^cite_note-224]

On 18 April 2015, at least 200 police officers conducted a series of raids in Melbourne, frustrating a terror plot planned for [ANZAC Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZAC_Day). According to witnesses, some years later, some the officers committed some abuses during the raid; they had thrown a suspect into a fridge, causing a deep cut, made repeated racial slurs to an Aboriginal man and assaulted him. The Australian federal police spent years denying these accusations.[^cite_note-232]

On 6 September 2016 ISIL published a new online magazine in which they urged "lone wolves" to carry out attacks:

## Anti-terrorism legislation

Main articles: [Australian anti-terrorism legislation, 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_anti-terrorism_legislation,_2004) and [Australian Anti-Terrorism Act 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Anti-Terrorism_Act_2005)

### 20th century legislation

Prior to the 1960s, there had not been any act in Australia that could accurately be deemed "terrorism" in the modern political and strategic sense of the word. Politically motivated violent incidents were rare, usually isolated, and for the most part driven by issues arising from political legislation, greed, or individuals being singled out, such as the attempted assassination of Australian Labor Party Leader [Arthur Calwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Calwell) in 1965 over his [Vietnam War](32611.md) stance. Likewise the [1968 attack on the US Consulate in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_attack_on_the_US_Consulate_in_Melbourne?action=edit\&redlink=1) was also regarded to be an isolated incident protesting the US involvement in Vietnam. The two exceptions to this state of affairs would be the [assassination attempt on the Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred,_Duke_of_Saxe-Coburg_and_Gotha#Entering_the_Royal_Navy) in 1868 by an Irish Nationalist named [Henry O'Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_O'Farrell), who was later executed for his crime, and [an attack in Broken Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Broken_Hill) in 1915 by Afghan supporters of the Sultan of Turkey.

Although it had known sporadic acts through its history, and examples of modern terrorism for almost a decade, Australia did not introduce terrorism specific laws into Parliament until the late 1970s. In 1977, after a three-year inquiry into Australia's intelligence services, Justice Robert Hope delivered his [Royal Commission on Intelligence and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_Intelligence_and_Security) (RCIS). The RCIS recommended amongst other things that the [Australian Security Intelligence Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation) (ASIO) areas of investigation be widened to include terrorism. A further Protective Security Review by Justice Hope in 1978 following the [Sydney Hilton bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Hilton_bombing) designated ASIO as the government agency responsible for producing national [threat assessments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threat_assessment) in the field of terrorism and politically motivated violence.

### 21st century legislation

See also: [ANZUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS), [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes), and [Australia–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93United_States_relations)

Successive governments have reviewed and altered the shape of both legislation and the agencies that enforce it to cope with the changing face, threat and scope of terrorism. It was not until after the attacks of 11 September 2001, however, that Australian policy began to change to reflect a growing threat against Australia and Australians specifically. Until then the view held from the 1960s had been that terrorist actions in Australia were considered as a problem imported from conflicts overseas and concerned with foreign targets on Australian soil.

#### Listed Terrorist organisations

Main article: [Organisations listed as terrorist organisations under Australia's Criminal Code](10680841.md)

The *Security Legislation Amendment (Terrorism) Act 2002* was passed by the [Australian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Parliament) and inserted a range of terrorist organisation offences into the *[Criminal Code Act 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law_of_Australia)* (Cth). For example, the Act made it an offence to provide support to a terrorist organisation.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-ANS1-236]

Since November 2016, there have been 20 organisations designated as terrorist organisations. Organisations can be so designated by a court or a government department. All but one of those organisations are [Islamist](15012.md). Identification of an organisation as a terrorist organisation may result from a prosecution for a terrorist offence, or from a listing determined by the [Attorney-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Australia).[^cite_note-237] The Australian list does not correspond to similar lists of other countries or international organisations.

#### 2004 and 2005

In 2004, the [Howard government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_government) introduced into Parliament three anti-terrorism bills, collectively called the [Australian anti-terrorism legislation, 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_anti-terrorism_legislation,_2004), and in 2005 the *Australian Anti-Terrorism Act 2005* was passed.

Before 2006, the last legislation to be brought into effect was the *Anti-Terrorism Act (No. 2) 2005*.[^cite_note-238]

The *[Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Money_Laundering_and_Counter-Terrorism_Financing_Act_2006?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (Cth) came into law on 12 December 2006,[^cite_note-parlinfo-terror-finance-239] and extends the regulatory regime imposed by the *[Financial Transaction Reports Act 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Transaction_Reports_Act_1988)*.

#### Shooting policy

In November 2015 it was stated that [New South Wales police](563673.md) were being retrained to "...shoot terrorists on sight rather than try to contain them and negotiate...".[^cite_note-abc-shoot-terrorist-247]

#### Stripping citizenship from Australians accused of terrorism

In August 2018 five Australians who had travelled to fight with the Islamic State were stripped of Australian citizenship and barred from reentering Australia. This was possible because they had another citizenship, because international law does not permit the measure being used on individuals with only one citizenship. The five brought the total to six for Australians stripped of citizenship for acts "contrary to their allegiance to Australia".[^cite_note-248]

## Social impact

### Croatian Australians

See also: [Terrorism in Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Yugoslavia), [Croatian Revolutionary Brotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Revolutionary_Brotherhood), [Ustaše in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e_in_Australia), [Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e), and [Wog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wog)

In the late 20th century [Croatian Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Australians) were stereotyped as terrorists, and fascist extremists. This based on events that mostly occurred overseas, and continuing animosity from Croatians siding with Germany in WWII.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] Many of them had engaged in non-violent activism for Croatian independence. There were some incidents credibly linked to the movement, such as the [Sydney Yugoslav General Trade and Tourist Agency bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Yugoslav_General_Trade_and_Tourist_Agency_bombing). However a later alleged plot, by the *[Croatian Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Six)* was based on evidence fabricated by [NSW police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSW_police) and a [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Australians) man, pretending to be Croatian who was working for [Yugoslav](34244.md) intelligence.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255]

### Muslim community

See also: [Islamophobia in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamophobia_in_Australia), [2005 Cronulla riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Cronulla_riots), and [Islam in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Australia)

Researchers have observed that [Muslims in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_in_Australia) have become a "stigmatised minority", subject to increased surveillance by state authorities as well as public discourse that constructs Muslims as a potential terrorist threat. Researchers term this the "suspect community" thesis. Researchers study how Muslims perceive themselves as a suspect community and how this influences their support for counter-terrorism efforts.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257]

### Jewish community

See also: [Antisemitism in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_Australia)

Terrorism in Australia has impacted the Australian Jewish community. After the [2001 terrorist attacks in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/911_attacks), the Australian Jewish community became a prime target of [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) terror cells.[^cite_note-258] Notable terrorist incidents impacting the Jewish community in Australia include the [1982 bombing of a Jewish centre in Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Israeli_Consulate_and_Hakoah_Club_bombings), the [2024 firebombing of a Melbourne synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Melbourne_synagogue_attack), and the [Bondi Beach shooting](81839099.md).
