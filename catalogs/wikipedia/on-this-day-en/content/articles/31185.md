The **tonne** ([/tʌn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/06/En-us-tonne.ogg/En-us-tonne.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/06/En-us-tonne.ogg/En-us-tonne.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> or [/tɒn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); symbol: **t**) is a [unit of mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_mass) equal to 1,000 [kilograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram). It is also referred to as a ***metric ton*** in the United States to distinguish it from the [non-metric units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton) of the *[short ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton)* and the *[long ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_ton)*. One tonne is equivalent to approximately 2,204.6 [pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_avoirdupois_pound), 1.102 short tons, and 0.984 long tons. The exact corresponding SI [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_unit_of_measurement) is the **megagram** (symbol **Mg**), a less common way to express the same mass.

## Symbol and abbreviations

The [BIPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Weights_and_Measures) symbol for the tonne is t, adopted at the same time as the unit in 1879.[^cite_note-bippy-1] Its use is also official for the metric ton in the United States, having been adopted by the United States [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology) (NIST).[^cite_note-fedreg-2][^cite_note-3] It is a symbol, not an abbreviation, and should not be followed by a [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_\(punctuation\)). Use of lower case is significant, and use of other letter combinations can lead to ambiguity. For example, T, MT, mT, are the SI symbols for the [tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_\(unit\)), megatesla, and millitesla, respectively, while Mt and mt are SI-compatible symbols for the megatonne (one teragram) and millitonne (one kilogram). If describing [TNT equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_equivalent) units of energy, one megatonne of TNT is equivalent to approximately 4.184 [petajoules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petajoule).

## Origin and spelling

In English, *tonne* is an established spelling alternative to *metric ton*.[^cite_note-4] In American English and British English, tonne is usually pronounced the same as ton ([/tʌn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), but the final "e" can also be pronounced, i.e. "tunnie" ([/ˈtʌni/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)).[^cite_note-5] In Australian English, the common and recommended pronunciation is [/tɒn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] In the United States, *metric ton* is the name for this unit used and recommended by NIST;[^cite_note-fedreg-2] an unqualified mention of a *ton* typically refers to a [short ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton) of 2000 lb (907.2 kg) and to a lesser extent to a [long ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_ton) of 2240 lb (1016 kg), with the term *tonne* rarely used in speech or writing. Both terms are acceptable in [Canadian English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_English).

*Ton* and *tonne* are both derived from a [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) word in general use in the [North Sea](21179.md) area since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) (cf.  [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) and [Old Frisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Frisian) *tunne*, [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German) and [Medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) *tunna*, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) *tonne*) to designate a large cask, or *tun*.[^cite_note-8] A full tun, standing about a metre high, could easily weigh a tonne. See also the common German word *[Mülltonne](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/M%C3%BClltonne)* (lit. 'garbage [drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_\(container\))').

The spelling *tonne* pre-dates the introduction of the SI in 1960; it has been used with this meaning in France since 1842,[^cite_note-9] when there were no metric prefixes for multiples of 10<sup>6</sup> and above, and is now used as the standard spelling for the metric mass measurement in most [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)-speaking countries.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] In the United States, the unit was originally referred to using the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) words *millier* or *tonneau*,[^cite_note-14] but these terms are now obsolete.[^cite_note-FR1998-15] The British imperial and United States customary units are comparable to the *tonne* and the spelling of *ton* in English is the same, though they differ in mass.

### French pre-metric tonne

Prior to metrification the French tonne consisted of 2000 French pounds (livres). Therefore a French tonne of that era weighed 979 kg compared to an English ton of 1016 kg. Prior to the use of tonne for this weight tonneau (plural: tonneaux) was used.[^cite_note-16]

## Derived units

As a non-SI unit, the use of SI metric prefixes with the tonne does not fall within the SI standard. For multiples of the tonne, it is more usual to speak of thousands or millions of tonnes. Kilotonne, megatonne, and gigatonne are more usually used for the energy of nuclear explosions and other events in [equivalent mass of TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_equivalent), often loosely as approximate figures. When used in this context, there is little need to distinguish between metric and other tons, and the unit is spelled either as *ton* or *tonne* with the relevant prefix attached.[^cite_note-22]

## Alternative usages

### Metric ton units

A **metric ton unit** (mtu) can mean 10 kg (22 lb) within metal trading, particularly within the United States. It traditionally referred to a metric ton of ore containing 1% (i.e. 10 kg) of metal.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The following excerpt from a mining geology textbook describes its usage in the particular case of tungsten:

> &#x20;Tungsten concentrates are usually traded in metric tonne units (originally designating one tonne of ore containing 1% of WO<sub>3</sub>, today used to measure WO<sub>3</sub> quantities in 10 kg units. One metric tonne unit (mtu) of tungsten (VI) contains 7.93 kilograms of tungsten.

— Walter L Pohl, *Economic Geology: Principles and Practices*, English edition, 2011, p. 183.

In the case of [uranium](31743.md), *MTU* is sometimes used in the sense of *metric ton of uranium* (1000 kg \[2200 lb]).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Use of mass as proxy for energy

Main articles: [TNT equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_equivalent) and [Tonne of oil equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne_of_oil_equivalent)

The *tonne of [trinitrotoluene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinitrotoluene) (TNT)* is used as a proxy for energy, usually of explosions (TNT is a common [high explosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_explosive)). Prefixes are used: kiloton(ne), megaton(ne), gigaton(ne), especially for expressing [nuclear weapon yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_yield), based on a [specific combustion energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_value) of TNT of about 4.2 [MJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megajoule)/[kg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram) (or one [thermochemical calorie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie) per [milligram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milligram)). Hence, 1 t TNT = approx. 4.2 [GJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigajoule), 1 [kt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiloton) TNT = approx. 4.2 [TJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terajoule), 1 Mt TNT = approx. 4.2 [PJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petajoule).

The SI unit of energy is the [joule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule). One tonne of TNT is approximately equivalent to 4.2 gigajoules.

In the [petroleum industry](994887.md), the [tonne of oil equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne_of_oil_equivalent) (toe) is a [unit of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_energy): the amount of energy released by burning one tonne of [crude oil](23195.md), approximately 42 GJ. There are several slightly different definitions. This is ten times as much as a tonne of TNT because atmospheric oxygen is used.

### Unit of force

Like the [gram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) and the kilogram, the tonne gave rise to a (now obsolete) force unit of the same name, the tonne-force, equivalent to about 9.8 [kilonewtons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_\(unit\)#Kilonewtons). The unit is also often called simply "tonne" or "metric ton" without identifying it as a unit of force.
