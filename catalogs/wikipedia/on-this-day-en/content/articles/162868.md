The **Scandinavian Airlines System** (**SAS**),[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-5] commonly known as **Scandinavian Airlines**, is the joint [flag carrier airline](465816.md) of [Denmark](76972.md), [Norway](21241.md), and [Sweden](5058739.md). It is part of [SAS Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Group) and is headquartered in [Solna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_Municipality), Sweden.

Operating a wide European and intercontinental network, the airline's principal hub is [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport), supported by two other major hubs at [Stockholm Arlanda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Arlanda_Airport) and [Oslo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Airport).[^cite_note-6] Including its subsidiaries [SAS Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Link) and [SAS Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Airlines_Connect), operates a fleet of over 130 aircraft to over 150 destinations.[^cite_note-FR24Norwegian-7] As of 2024, SAS serves approximately 25 million passengers annually, making it the largest airline in Denmark and Sweden and one of the largest in Europe.[^cite_note-8]

The airline was founded in 1946 as a [consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium) to [pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pooling_\(resource_management\)) the [transatlantic operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_flight) of the Swedish, Norwegian, and Danish national airlines, which were fully merged in 1951. Long considered an icon of Scandinavian cooperation, SAS was one of the five founding members of the [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) in 1997.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In October 2023, as part of [Chapter 11 bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_11_bankruptcy) restructuring, [SAS Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Group) underwent a major change in ownership. A consortium including [Air France–KLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_France%E2%80%93KLM) and the [Government of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Denmark) acquired stakes in the airline.[^cite_note-AFKLM-11][^cite_note-12] As a result of this transition, SAS departed Star Alliance and joined the [SkyTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTeam) alliance in September 2024.[^cite_note-allianceexit-13] In July 2025, the Franco-Dutch airline group announced an agreement to increase its holding to 60.5%.[^cite_note-AirFranceKLM-14]

## History



The airline's original emblem, displaying each Scandinavian flag as coats of arms, with surmounting [crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_\(heraldry\))

### Founding



A privately preserved [Douglas DC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) wearing SAS' late 1940s-style markings

The airline was founded on 1 August 1946, when [Svensk Interkontinental Lufttrafik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Intercontinental_Airlines) AB (an airline owned by the Swedish [Wallenberg family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallenberg_family)), [Det Danske Luftfartselskab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Air_Lines) A/S, and [Det Norske Luftfartselskap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Air_Lines) AS (the [flag carriers](465816.md) of Denmark and Norway) formed a partnership to handle the [combined air traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-haul#Long-haul_flights) of the three [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) countries.[^cite_note-15] The first president of SAS was [Per Norlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Norlin).[^cite_note-official_pres-16] On 17 September 1946, operations started under the new entity and the first international service was conducted between Stockholm and New York.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] Within a half-year, SAS set a new record for carrying the heaviest single piece of air cargo across the Atlantic on a scheduled passenger airliner, by shipping a 1,400-pound electrical panel from New York to the [Sandvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandvik) company in Sweden.[^cite_note-18]

In 1948, the Swedish flag carrier AB [Aerotransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerotransport) joined SAS and quickly coordinated its European operations between both carriers. Three years later, the companies formally merged to form the SAS Consortium.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] When established, ownership of the airline was divided between SAS Danmark (28.6%), SAS Norge (28.6%), and SAS Sverige (42.8%), all of which were owned 50% by private investors and 50% by their governments.[^cite_note-19]

### Transpolar route

During 1954, SAS became the first airline to commence scheduled flights on a [polar route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_route), flying [Douglas DC-6Bs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-6) from Copenhagen to Los Angeles with stops in Søndre Strømfjord (now [Kangerlussuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangerlussuaq)) in Greenland and Winnipeg in Canada, pioneering a commercial route that marked a milestone in transpolar aviation history.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] By the summer of 1956, traffic on the route had justified the frequency to be increased to three flights per week. The service proved relatively popular with Hollywood celebrities and members of the film industry, and the route turned out to be a publicity coup for SAS. Thanks to a tariff structure that allowed free transit to other European destinations via Copenhagen, this trans-polar route gained increasing popularity with American tourists throughout the 1950s.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17]

In 1957, SAS was the first airline to offer around-the-world service over the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole) via a second polar route served by [Douglas DC-7Cs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-7) flying from Copenhagen to Tokyo via [Anchorage International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Stevens_Anchorage_International_Airport) in Alaska.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] The flight via Alaska was a compromise solution since the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) would not allow SAS, among other air carriers, to fly across [Siberia](29639.md) between Europe and Japan, and Chinese airspace was also closed.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17]

### Jet era



A Scandinavian Airlines flight attendant serving passengers in the 1960s



Radisson Blu Royal Hotel in central [Copenhagen](5166.md), originally SAS Royal Hotel, designed by [Arne Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Jacobsen) and built in 1960

In 1959, SAS entered the [jet age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_propulsion), having procured a number of [French](5843419.md)-built [Sud Aviation Caravelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sud_Aviation_Caravelle) as the company's first jetliner.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17]

In addition to modern airliners, SAS also adopted innovative operating practices and systems to improve the customer experience. In 1965, it was the first airline to introduce an electronic [reservation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_reservation_system).[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] During 1971, SAS introduced its first [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) jumbo jet into service.[^cite_note-20] Prior to the delivery of its first 747s, SAS had formed the KSS maintenance consortium with [KLM](16882.md) and [Swissair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swissair) in 1969 to provide a maintenance pool and standardize aircraft specifications for the three airlines' 747 fleets. The consortium later incorporated [UTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_de_Transports_A%C3%A9riens) and was renamed into KSSU to jointly acquire and maintain [McDonnell Douglas DC-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10) widebody trijets.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In 1982, SAS was recognised as the most punctual airline operating in Europe at that time.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17]

During its first decades, the airline built two large hotels in central Copenhagen, *SAS Royal Hotel* (5 stars) and the even larger *SAS Hotel Scandinavia* (4 stars, with a casino on the 26th floor).[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] In 1980, SAS opened its first hotel outside of Scandinavia, the *SAS Kuwait Hotel*. By 1989, SAS's hotel division owned a 40 percent share in the [Intercontinental Hotels Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Hotels_Group).[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] Following the [deregulation of commercial aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_deregulation) in Europe and the competitive pressures from new rivals, SAS experienced economic difficulties (as did many incumbent [flag carrier](465816.md) airlines) this heavily contributed to the airline's decision to sell its hotel chain to the [Radisson Hotel Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radisson_Hotel_Group) during 1992.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17]



The company logo in the 1980s was made up of stripes in the colors of the flags of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden.



SAS operated flights to [Greenland](12118.md) for more than 50 years until March 2003. The route reopened in spring 2007 until January 2009. Pictured: a [Boeing 767-300ER](4165.md) at [Kangerlussuaq Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangerlussuaq_Airport) (2001).

### Consolidation, acquisitions, and partnerships

During the early 1990s, SAS unsuccessfully tried to merge itself with [KLM](16882.md), along with [Austrian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Airlines) and Swissair, in a proposed combined entity commonly called [Alcazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazar_\(airline\)).[^cite_note-fail-23][^cite_note-swissair-24] However, months of negotiations towards this ambitious merger ultimately collapsed due to multiple unsettled issues; this strategic failure heavily contributed to the departure of Carlzon that same year and his replacement by Jan Reinås.[^cite_note-official_pres-16] The airline marked its 50th year of operation on 1 August 1996 with the harmonization and name of SAS's parent company to *SAS Danmark A/S, SAS Norge ASA* and *SAS Sverige AB*.[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] During May 1997, SAS became a founding member of the global [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) network, joining with airlines such as [Air Canada](145623.md), [Lufthansa](97733.md), [Thai Airways International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Airways_International), and [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines).[^cite_note-A_new_alliance-25][^cite_note-5_Airlines-26]

In June 2001, the ownership structure of SAS was changed, with a holding company being created in which the holdings of the governments changed to Sweden (21.4%), Norway (14.3%), and Denmark (14.3%), while the remaining 50 percent of shares were publicly held and traded on the [stock market](52328.md).[^cite_note-official_milestones-17] During 2004, SAS was again restructured, being divided into four separate companies: *SAS Scandinavian Airlines Sverige AB, SAS Scandinavian Airlines Danmark A/S, SAS Braathens AS,* and *SAS Scandinavian International AS*. *SAS Braathens* was re-branded *SAS Scandinavian Airlines Norge AS* in 2007.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-official_milestones-17]

### 2009–2021: Restructuring

With the growth of [budget airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_airlines) and decreasing fares in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), the business experienced financial hardship. By 2009, competitive pressures had compelled the airline to launch a cost-cutting initiative. In the first step of which, the business sold its stakes in other companies, such as [British Midland International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Midland_International), Spanair, and [airBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic), and began to restructure its operations.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] During January 2009, an agreement to divest more than 80 percent of the holdings in Spanair was signed with a [Catalan](6822.md) group of investors led by Consorci de Turisme de Barcelona and Catalana d'Inciatives.[^cite_note-31] These changes reportedly reduced the airliner's expenses by around 23 per cent between 2008 and 2011.[^cite_note-tradeunion_2012-32]

In November 2012, the company came under heavy pressure from its owners and banks to implement even heavier cost-cutting measures as a condition for continued financial support. Negotiations with the respective [trade unions](17626.md) took place for more than a week and exceeded the original deadline; in the end, an agreement was reached between SAS and the trade unions that would increase the work time, cutting employee's salaries by between 12 and 20 percent, along with reductions to the [pension](43613.md) and retirement plans; these measures were aimed at keeping the airline as an operating concern. SAS was criticized for how it handled the negotiations, having reportedly denied facilities to the union delegations.[^cite_note-tradeunion_2012-32]

During 2017, SAS announced that it was forming a new airline, [Scandinavian Airlines Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Airlines_Connect), operating out of [Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport) and [Málaga Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Airport) to fly European routes on its parent's behalf using nine Airbus A320neos.[^cite_note-33] SAS sought to replace its own aircraft with cheaper ones crewed and based outside Scandinavia to compete better with other airlines.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] The Swedish Pilots Union expressed its dissatisfaction with the operational structure of the new airline, suggesting it violated the current labour-agreements.[^cite_note-36] The Swedish Cabin Crew Union also condemned the new venture and stated that SAS established the airline to "not pay decent salaries" to cabin crew.[^cite_note-37]

In 2018, SAS announced that it had placed an order for 50 [Airbus A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo) narrow-body jetliners to facilitate the creation of a single-type fleet. That same year, in June, the Norwegian government divested its stake in the airline.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-official_milestones-17] As part of an environmental initiative launched by [San Francisco International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_International_Airport) (SFO), SAS flights operating out of SFO since December 2018 have been supplied with [sustainable aviation fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_aviation_fuel) from [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) and [SkyNRG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyNRG).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] On 27 June 2018, the Norwegian government announced that it had sold all its shares in SAS.

In July 2021, the European Commission approved a Swedish and Danish aid measure of approximately US\$356 million to support SAS.[^cite_note-41] In September 2021, SAS announced that it would establish two operating subsidiaries; SAS Connect and SAS Link, with its existing [SAS Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Airlines_Connect) subsidiary to be rebranded as the new SAS Connect, while SAS Link would initially operate the airline's Embraer E195 aircraft, and the operations of both companies to begin by early 2022.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

### 2022–2024: Sweden's exit, Air France-KLM entry and alliance shift

Following little progress with SAS's restructuring plan, SAS Forward, the Swedish government announced on 7 June 2022 that Sweden, which owns 21.8% of the company, would not inject new capital into SAS and that it did "not aim to be a long-term shareholder in the company".[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] In early July 2022, nearly 1,000 SAS pilots went on a 15-day strike over wages and cost-cutting measures, severely disrupting operations.[^cite_note-47] A day after the strike began, the airline filed for [Chapter 11 bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_11,_Title_11,_United_States_Code) protection in the United States on 5 July 2022.[^cite_note-48]

In September 2022, SAS announced it was returning at least ten aircraft to lessors, including five long-haul aircraft - amongst them two barely two year old Airbus A350s. This measure is a result of the closure of Russian airspace for flights to Asia which caused a severe drop in demand and efficiency.[^cite_note-fleetreturn-49] As of November 2022, SAS announced it was searching for a buyer for one of their [Airbus A350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350) aircraft.[^cite_note-auto-50]

In October 2023, it was announced that [Air France–KLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_France%E2%80%93KLM) would be investing alongside the Danish government and two investment firms (Castlelake and Lind Invest) in SAS, with the airline group buying up to 20% of SAS shares following the airline's ongoing Chapter 11 process in the United States. With the investment (if approved by the EU Commission, and respective US and Swedish courts),[^cite_note-51] SAS left Star Alliance and joined [SkyTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTeam) alongside Air France–KLM.[^cite_note-AFKLM-11][^cite_note-52]

On 19 March 2024, US Bankruptcy Court approved the new restructuring plan and investment, allowing SAS to exit Chapter 11 by mid 2024.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] It also applied for company reorganization in Sweden on 27 March.[^cite_note-55] SAS announced it would leave [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) by 31 August 2024, becoming a SkyTeam member the next day, as confirmed on 29 April.[^cite_note-allianceexit-13][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] On 12 June, Stockholm District Court announced that it would hold a hearing for the company reorganization on 12 July,[^cite_note-58] eventually approving it on 19 July.[^cite_note-59] On 28 June, European Commission announced that it approved the restructuring plan.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

In September 2024, Scandinavian Airlines announced it would terminate its wet-leasing contract with [Xfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordica_\(airline\)) by November 2024.[^cite_note-sascancellation-62] On 17 September 2024, SAS announced a partnership with [Braathens Regional Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braathens_Regional_Airlines) (BRA), designating them as a subcontractor to operate flights from [Stockholm Arlanda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Arlanda_Airport) starting in 2025, with BRA announcing it would cease operating its own domestic flight network from [Stockholm Bromma Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Bromma_Airport).[^cite_note-63] This collaboration, valued at approximately SEK 6 billion over seven years, aims to strengthen SAS's domestic network in Sweden, increasing daily departures to destinations such as [Visby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby_Airport), [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6teborg_Landvetter_Airport), [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6_Airport), and [Luleå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lule%C3%A5_Airport).[^cite_note-64]

On 18 September 2024, just a month after the completion of the restructuring, SAS announced expansion of the route network from Copenhagen Airport with 15 new destinations from Summer 2025. Following the new ownership structure, the airline will focus on positioning the airport as the airline's main hub for international travel.[^cite_note-65]

On 4 July 2025, Air France-KLM announced its intention to increase its stake in SAS’s share capital from 19.9% to 60.5%.[^cite_note-66]

### Head office



The current head office, the SAS Frösundavik Office Building as seen in 2007

Scandinavian Airlines' head office is located in the SAS Frösundavik Office Building in [Frösundavik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%B6sundavik?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Solna Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_Municipality), [Stockholm County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_County).[^cite_note-fastighetsvarlden-90] Between 2011 and 2013, the head office was located at [Stockholm Arlanda Airport (ARN)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm-Arlanda_Airport) in [Sigtuna Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigtuna_Municipality), Sweden.[^cite_note-91] The SAS Cargo Group A/S head office is in [Kastrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastrup), [Tårnby Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A5rnby_Municipality), Denmark.[^cite_note-92]

The SAS Frösundavik Office Building,[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] was designed by [Niels Torp Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Torp) and built between 1985 and 1987. The move from Solna to Arlanda was completed in 2010.[^cite_note-95] A previous SAS head office was located on the grounds of [Bromma Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromma_Airport) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Municipality).[^cite_note-96] In 2013, SAS announced that it once again would relocate to Frösundavik.[^cite_note-fastighetsvarlden-90]

In contrast to most other businesses and private individuals in Sweden, airlines are exempt from the Swedish [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax). Had SAS paid the Swedish carbon tax level of SEK 1180 (EUR 114) per tonne (as of 2019)[^cite_note-98] for all of its emissions, it would have had significant impact on recent profit levels. Since 2012, [airlines are included](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Emission_Trading_Scheme#Aviation_emissions) in the [EU ETS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_ETS). In January 2013, the price for extra emission rights on top of the granted were approximately [EUR 6.3 per tonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Emission_Trading_Scheme#Phase_III). In May 2017, the price was EUR 4.9 per tonne.[^cite_note-99]

### Future fleet plans

#### Short haul



SAS [Airbus A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo) in the airline's current livery

On 20 June 2011, SAS announced an order for 30 new [A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320neo) aircraft as part of its fleet harmonization plan.[^cite_note-airwaysmag-145] SAS' stated goal is to have an all-Airbus fleet at its bases in Stockholm and Copenhagen by 2019, with a mixed A320neo and A320ceo fleet operation at both bases. The base in Oslo was then operate mostly Boeing 737-800 aircraft, with a few 737-700s also being retained at the time. The older, smaller 737-600s were disposed of in 2019.[^cite_note-airbusflaade-146] The first order of A320neos was delivered in October 2016.[^cite_note-147]

In April 2018, SAS announced an order of 50 more A320neos to replace all 737NGs and older A320ceos in service as part of its goal to have an all-Airbus fleet by 2023.[^cite_note-sas_a320neo-132] The last Boeing 737 has been phased out of the fleet on 19 November 2023. This [Boeing 737-700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation), registered LN-RRB and named "Dag Viking", was operating as SAS Flight 737. As of now, the aircraft is currently operated by [Canadian North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_North) as C-FHNH.

#### Long haul



Airbus A340-313

On 25 June 2013, SAS and Airbus signed a memorandum of understanding stating that SAS intends to buy twelve new-generation aircraft, including six options. The agreement consists of eight A350-900s with six options and four A330-300Es. The first new long-haul aircraft to enter service will be the A330-300E, which was originally planned to replace the aging A340-300s in 2015 as leasing agreements on these aircraft expire. Instead, SAS renewed the leasing agreements to be able to expand its long-haul fleet and used the new A330-300Es to add more long-haul destinations to its network.

The first six of eight Airbus A350-900s for SAS were delivered to the airline in 2019 and were to be operating long-haul routes from 2020. The A350 will first fly on the [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) and [Chicago](248449.md) route, with the airline planning [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Capital_International_Airport), [New York](248492.md), [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport), [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Pudong_International_Airport), [Hong Kong](248457.md) and [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_International_Airport) when more A350 aircraft are delivered.[^cite_note-148]

### Livery



Airbus A319 OY-KBO, named Christian Valdemar Viking in honor of [Prince Christian of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Christian_of_Denmark), wearing a special retro livery



An [Airbus A320-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320-200) in the former SAS livery

In September 2019, SAS unveiled new livery for its fleet, initially implemented on new A350 and A320neo aircraft,[^cite_note-livery-149] with all aircraft repainted in the following five years. The fuselage is light grey with the "SAS" logotype in silver displayed prominently across the height of the front section. The vertical stabilizer and adjacent parts of the fuselage are blue, with the SAS logo in white shown on the stabilizer. The blue area on the rear fuselage extends towards the front in a curved line. The horizontal stabilizers are beige (except for the ATR 72 aircraft, where they are blue). Winglets are blue as well. The engine casings are beige with a vertical blue stripe at the front and bear the word "Scandinavian" in blue. "Scandinavian" in large blue letters is also displayed on the underbelly of the aircraft.[^cite_note-livery-149]

The previous livery, designed by SthlmLab (Stockholm Design Lab), had been introduced in 1998. SAS aircraft look predominantly white; however, the fuselage is in a very light beige (Pantone Warm Gray 2/Pantone 9083C) with "Scandinavian" above the windows in silver lettering (Pantone 877) and "Airlines" below the windows in white. The typeface used is [Rotis Semi Serif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotis#Rotis_Semi_Serif). The vertical stabilizer (and winglets) are painted blue (Pantone 2738C) with the classic white SAS logo on it. It is a variant of the traditional SAS logotype, slimmed slightly and stylized by the design company Stockholm Design Lab as part of the SAS livery change. The engine casing is painted in scarlet (Pantone Warm Red/Pantone 179C) with the word Scandinavian in white, the [thrust reversers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_reversers) in the color of the fuselage. All other text is painted in Pantone Warm Gray 9. The design also features stylized versions of the Scandinavian flags. All aircraft are named, traditionally after Vikings.

To celebrate its 60th anniversary in 2006, SAS painted its first Airbus A319-100, with registration OY-KBO and name "Christian Valdemar Viking", in [retro livery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrojet);[^cite_note-150] it remained in service as of 2025.[^cite_note-151]

To celebrate its 80th anniversary in 2026, SAS painted an A330-300, with registration LN-RKR in a special livery. The body is painted in SAS blue, the number 80 on the vertical stabiliser, large SAS wordmark in silver on the forward fuselage, and the Scandinavian flags as stripes around the fuselage, similar to their logo in the 1980s.[^cite_note-152]

## Cabin

### SAS Business

On long-haul flights, business class, called *SAS Business*, is offered and features wide sleeper seats. On the [A330s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330) and [A350s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350), seating is 1-2-1 on seats that convert into 196–202-centimetre (77–80 in) flat beds, with power sockets and a 15-inch (380 mm) entertainment screen. On the [A321LRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A321LR), business class has alternating 2-2 and 1-1 seating, all convertible to flat beds.[^cite_note-153]

### SAS Plus

Plus is SAS' premium economy class. On the A330s, seating is 2-3-2, 2-4-2 on the A350s and 2-2 on the A321LR. The seats offered on SAS Plus are wider than those in the SAS Go section.

On European flights, SAS Plus tickets are refundable and include a meal, a double checked-in baggage allowance, and access to lounges and fast track security at the airport. The SAS Plus passengers are seated at the front of the aircraft and passengers can choose their seat at booking for free, but the seats there are otherwise the same as the SAS Go seats. The two-class system was introduced in June 2013, when [business class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_class) was eliminated from intra-European flights.[^cite_note-154]

### SAS Go

SAS Go, or economy class, offers 3-3 seating on intracontinental flights, 2-4-2 on the A330s and 3-3-3 on the A350s.

SAS offers free coffee and tea to GO passengers on short-haul services, except very short flights like [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_Airport,_Flesland)-[Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_Airport,_Sola) or [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Arlanda_Airport)-[Visby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby_Airport). Meals are served to all passengers on long-haul flights.

### SAS Go Light

SAS Go Light is a variant of SAS Go with no checked luggage included. Tickets are sold in the same booking class as SAS Go and are otherwise identical. As of 14 December 2017, SAS Go Light is available on both European and long-haul flights. SAS Go Light is aimed at competing with [low-cost carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_carriers) for those who travel with hand luggage only. Extra luggage allowance for Star Alliance Gold, and [EuroBonus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBonus) Silver, Gold, and Diamond members does not apply on SAS Go Light tickets and is only valid for EuroBonus Pandion members.

## Services

### EuroBonus

Main article: [EuroBonus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBonus)

SAS's frequent-flyer program is called EuroBonus. Members earn points on all SAS flights, Widerøe routes with no SAS competition (except Public Service Operations) as well as on Skyteam flights. Around 50 percent of SAS' total revenues are generated by EuroBonus members. By August 2015, the EuroBonus program had in excess of four million members.[^cite_note-155]

### Hovercraft

Between 1984 and 1994, SAS operated a [hovercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovercraft) service between [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) in Sweden and [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) in Denmark. Travellers could check in for their flights in Malmö and the hovercraft were operated as connecting flights. The service was operated using a handful of [British Hovercraft Corporation AP1-88s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hovercraft_Corporation_AP1-88), which took an average journey time of 45 minutes to traverse the 27 km (17 mi) route across the [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund); within its first year of operation, hovercraft reportedly carried roughly 100,000 passengers.[^cite_note-popmech_hover-156] Due to the level of demand experienced, SAS examined the prospects for introducing larger hovercraft, capable of carrying up to 200 passengers, for the service.[^cite_note-popmech_hover-156] However, in 1994, the hovercraft were replaced by [catamarans](195952.md). These vessels were in turn discontinued during 2000 due to the opening of the [Öresund bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96resund_bridge), which provided a competing rail link between Malmö and Copenhagen airport.[^cite_note-157]

### Wi‑Fi

During May 2018, SAS launched a new high-speed [Wi‑Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi%E2%80%91Fi) internet access system supplied by [Viasat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasat_\(American_company\)). The new system was announced to enable passengers to stream movies on board. Prior to this, SAS offered Internet access on board on its long haul aircraft and a small number of Boeing 737s. Wi‑Fi internet access is free for Eurobonus Gold and Diamond members and those with Business Class tickets. Otherwise, it can be purchased with EuroBonus points or for a fee.

In March 2026, SAS announced they will be introducing a new generation of onboard connectivity with [Starlink](45111627.md), and in partnership with vendor [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_\(company\)). Introduction for SAS main fleet takes place during 2026 and this will expand the free Wi-Fi access offer to all Eurobonus members and 3 customers.[^cite_note-158] [^cite_note-159]

### Non-aviation related incidents

#### Controversial advertising campaign

On 10 February 2020, SAS released 2 minutes and 45 seconds long commercial on [YouTube](3524766.md) titled "What is truly Scandinavian?"[^cite_note-160] which tells a story about company's values and highlighting the ideas and inventions that [globalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalism) brought to [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), which caused an outrage among right-wing groups due to its perceived denigration of Scandinavian culture.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] On 12 February 2020, SAS Group, a parent company of SAS, released a statement that they would continue with the advertising campaign despite the outrage.[^cite_note-165]

On 13 February 2020, 3 days after the commercial was published, SAS offices in [Adelgade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelgade), [Copenhagen](5166.md) and advertising agency \&Co which produced the commercial received [bomb threats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_threat).[^cite_note-\:0-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-\:2-169][^cite_note-\:3-170] Later, a shorter 45 second version of the same commercial was republished on [Facebook](7529378.md) by SAS and official version on YouTube made private.[^cite_note-\:0-166][^cite_note-\:2-169][^cite_note-\:3-170]

#### Responses

[Norwegian Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_\(airline\)) quickly reacted to the controversy by publishing the message "Fortunately, nobody can take away the cheese slicer from us" ([Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language): *Heldigvis kan ingen ta fra oss ostehøvelen*) and an image on Facebook of a [cheese slicer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_knife), which is a Norwegian invention.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]
