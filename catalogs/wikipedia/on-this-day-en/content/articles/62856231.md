**Luka Berulava** ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): ლუკა ბერულავა; born 27 November 2002) is a [Georgian](48768.md) [pair skater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skater). With current partner [Anastasia Metelkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Metelkina), he is an Olympic silver medalist ([2026](82110766.md)), World silver medalist ([2026](82193351.md)), European champion ([2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Figure_Skating_Championship)), two-time [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Figure_Skating_Championships) medalist (2024, 2025), [2024–25 Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024-25_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final) bronze medalist, a three-time [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating) medalist, and a four-time [Challenger Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Challenger_Series) medalist. On the junior level, the pair are two-time [World Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships) champions ([2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships), [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships)), the [2023–24 Junior Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023-24_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final) champion, and a two-time [ISU Junior Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix) gold medalists. He and Metelkina are the first Georgian athletes to ever win a medal at a Winter Olympic Games.

Berulava first emerged on the international scene to represent Georgia at the [2020 Winter Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2020_Winter_Youth_Olympics), where he and his partner [Alina Butaeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alina_Butaeva) won two medals − bronze in pairs and gold in the team event. He then formed a new partnership with [Karina Safina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karina_Safina). Safina/Berulava were the first Georgian pair medalists on the [ISU Junior Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix) circuit, and the first Georgians to win a [World Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships) title in any discipline when they won gold in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships).[^cite_note-ISU210906-1] At the senior level they won a bronze medal at the [2021 CS Nebelhorn Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CS_Nebelhorn_Trophy), achieved notable fourth-place finishes at both the [2022 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Figure_Skating_Championships) and the [2022 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_Figure_Skating_Championships), and represented Georgia at the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics).

## Personal life

Berulava was born on 27 November 2002 in Moscow, Russia into a Georgian family.[^cite_note-ISU-1920-2][^cite_note-YOG2020-3] Berulava stated that “I was born in Moscow because my family had to leave Georgia [during the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sukhumi_\(September_1993\)), but we always spoke [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language) at home. It's my first language.”[^cite_note-GS240922-4][^cite_note-5]

He is the [godfather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godparent) of fellow Georgian skater, [Nika Egadze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nika_Egadze).[^cite_note-6]

## Career

### Early career

Berulava began learning to skate in 2005.[^cite_note-ISU-1920-2] In 2019, he teamed up with [Alina Butaeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alina_Butaeva) to compete in [pair skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skating). The pair decided to train in [Perm](389777.md), coached by Pavel Sliusarenko and Alexei Menshikov.[^cite_note-ISU-1920-2] Despite training at facilities in Russia, at international competitions Berulava has only ever represented [Georgia](48768.md). Berulava has stated that he dreamed of competing for Georgia since he was a child and immediately accepted when the Georgian Figure Skating Federation made him an offer.[^cite_note-7]

#### 2019–20 season: Debut of Butaeva/Berulava, Youth Olympics medals

The two made their debut for Georgia in September 2019, placing eighth at an [ISU Junior Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix) (JGP) event in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_in_Poland)[^cite_note-poland-8] and then sixth at [JGP Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_in_Croatia).[^cite_note-croatia-9] Their results earned a spot for Georgia in the pairs' event at the [2020 Winter Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Winter_Youth_Olympics).[^cite_note-qualification-10] In November, they took bronze in the junior pairs' category at the [Volvo Open Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Open_Cup) in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), Latvia.[^cite_note-volvo-11] In December, they won the junior event at the [Golden Spin of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_CS_Golden_Spin_of_Zagreb).[^cite_note-ISU-ABLB-12]

In January 2020, the pair competed at the [2020 Winter Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2020_Winter_Youth_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Pair_skating) in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland. They finished third in the short program behind two pairs from Russia,[^cite_note-short_olympics-13] then did likewise in the free skating program. Their combined score put them in third place with a bronze medal.[^cite_note-bronze-14] This was Georgia's first medal in the [Winter Youth Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Youth_Olympic_Games), and their tenth medal in the [Youth Olympic Games](11315968.md) overall.[^cite_note-medals-15] The pair also received a gold medal for their participation in the [team event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2020_Winter_Youth_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_trophy) as part of Team Courage, alongside [Arlet Levandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlet_Levandi) from Estonia, [Ksenia Sinitsyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksenia_Sinitsyna) from Russia, and ice dancers [Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utana_Yoshida)/[Nishiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shingo_Nishiyama) from Japan.[^cite_note-team-16] Butaeva/Berulava finished the season at the [2020 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships), where they placed seventh.[^cite_note-GS200305-17]

### Pair skating with Karina Safina

#### 2021–22 season: Debut of Safina/Berulava, Beijing Olympics, and World Junior champions

After the end of his partnership with Butaeva, Berulava formed a new partnership with Russian pair skater [Karina Safina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karina_Safina). With international junior competition resuming following the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) causing the 2020–21 junior season to be cancelled, Safina/Berulava made their [Junior Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021-22_ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix) debut at the [2021 JGP Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_in_Slovakia). they won the silver medal behind gold medal-winning Russian team [Mukhortova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Mukhortova?action=edit\&redlink=1)/[Evgenyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Evgenyev?action=edit\&redlink=1), and ahead of third place Russian team [Kostiukovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polina_Kostiukovich)/[Briukhanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Briukhanov?action=edit\&redlink=1). Their placement marked Georgia's first [Junior Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix) medal in pair skating.[^cite_note-ISU210906-1]

Safina/Berulava next made their senior international debut at the [2021 CS Nebelhorn Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CS_Nebelhorn_Trophy) to attempt to [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Qualification) a berth for Georgia in the pairs event at the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics). The team set a new personal best to win the short program by a narrow margin over German team [Hase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva_Fabienne_Hase)/[Seegert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Seegert). They fell to third in the free program after losing their forward inside death spiral and one of their lifts, and ultimately finished third overall to successfully qualify for an Olympic spot in their discipline for Georgia. Berulava remarked afterwards, "we would like to have done it in a better, nicer way."[^cite_note-GS210924-18] Their performance, along with Georgian ice dance team [Kazakova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Kazakova_\(figure_skater\))/[Reviya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Reviya)'s podium placement, also qualified a spot for Georgia in the Olympic team event.

Safina/Berulava returned to the Junior Grand Prix circuit in October for their second assignment, the [2021 JGP Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_in_Austria) in [Linz](55856.md). They placed third in both segments to finish third overall behind Russian teams [Khabibullina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Khabibullina)/[Knyazhuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Knyazhuk) and Mukhortova/Evgenyev. Due to the unique qualification process for the 2021–22 season, the team did not advance to the [2021–22 Junior Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final), despite two podium finishes.[^cite_note-ISU-KSLB-19]

In December, Safina/Berulava faced domestic rivals [Metelkina](67224137.md)/[Parkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Parkman) for the first time in international competition at the [2021 CS Golden Spin of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CS_Golden_Spin_of_Zagreb). Safina/Berulava set a new personal best to win the short program but fell to eighth in the free program standings after errors on their side-by-side jumping passes and losing their forward inside death spiral. The team placed seventh overall, while Metelkina/Parkman advanced onto the podium and took home the silver medal.[^cite_note-ISU-KSLB-19] Metelkina/Parkman initially received the nod for the Georgian pairs berth at the [2022 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_Figure_Skating_Championships); however, Safina/Berulava replaced them after they withdrew from the event on 9 January. At Europeans, Safina/Berulava placed sixth in the short program after falling on their side-by-side triple Salchows. They delivered a stronger performance in the free skate, albeit still struggling with their side-by-side jumps, to climb to fourth in the segment and fourth overall, the best of the non-Russian competitors.[^cite_note-GS220114-20]

The day after the pairs free skate at the European Championships, Safina/Berulava were officially named to the [Georgian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics) for the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics) by the Georgian Figure Skating Federation.[^cite_note-olympics-announcement-21] Safina/Berulava made their Olympic debut in the [team event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_event) before the opening ceremony on February 3. They cleanly skated their short program to place sixth in the segment out of nine and earn five points towards Team Georgia's combined score.[^cite_note-GS220204-22] However, despite scoring 22 team points overall to tie for fifth place with Team China going into the free skate, Team Georgia lost the tie-breaker and did not advance.[^cite_note-GS220207-23] In the [pairs event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Pair_skating), Safina/Berulava were ninth in the short program and eighth in the free skate, for ninth overall.[^cite_note-GS220218-24][^cite_note-GS220219-25]

Days after the Olympics concluded, [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) ordered an [invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), as a result of which the [International Skating Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Skating_Union) banned all Russian and Belarusian skaters from competing at the [2022 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Figure_Skating_Championships). As well, the [Chinese Skating Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Skating_Association) opted not to send athletes to compete in [Montpellier](73369.md). As those countries' athletes comprised the entirety of the top five pairs at the Olympics, this greatly impacted the field.[^cite_note-CBC220322-26] Safina and Berulava relocated to train in Italy. They placed fourth in the short program at the World Championships with a clean skate.[^cite_note-GS220323-27] They were fourth in the free skate to finish fourth overall, the only error in the latter segment being Safina doubling out on a triple Salchow attempt.[^cite_note-GS220324-28]

Due to the pandemic, the [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships) could not be held in their scheduled location of [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia,_Bulgaria), and as a result, they were moved to [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn) and held in mid-April, rather than their traditional early March timeframe.[^cite_note-ISU220304-29] As Russian pair teams were also banned from attending this event, Safina/Berulava entered as heavy favourites for the gold medal.[^cite_note-ISU220301-30][^cite_note-IFS2022-31] They won the short program with a clean skate and a 67.77 score.[^cite_note-GS220414-32] They also won the free skate, taking the title by a margin of almost twenty points. Berulava reflected that "it was a long and busy season with many competitions and to end it with a gold medal is really nice."[^cite_note-GS220415-33]

#### 2022–23 season: Grand Prix debut & injury struggles

Safina and Berulava were assigned to make their senior [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022-23_ISU_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating) debut in the fall. Despite dealing with an injury in the lead-up to their first event, the [2022 Grand Prix de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Grand_Prix_de_France), they placed third in the short program, with Safina saying, "I am more pleased than not pleased with our performance today because nothing really worked in practice for me. We have still a lot of work ahead of us, and I think I've done the maximum of what I can do at the moment."[^cite_note-ISU221104-34] They struggled in the free skate, placing sixth in that segment and dropping to fifth overall.[^cite_note-GS221106-35] The team later withdrew from their second assignment, the [2022 Grand Prix of Espoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Grand_Prix_of_Espoo).

After missing most of the season, Safina/Berulava returned to compete at the [2023 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Figure_Skating_Championships), but struggled at the event and came nineteenth.[^cite_note-ISU-KSLB-19] The following month, Safina announced that Berulava had opted to end their partnership.[^cite_note-KS230407-36] He subsequently confirmed this, expressing "deep gratitude to Karina."[^cite_note-LB230407-37]

### Pair skating with Anastasiia Metelkina

#### 2023–24 season: Debut of Metelkina/Berulava, European silver medal, and World Junior Champions



Metelkina and Berluava at the [2024 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Figure_Skating_Championships)

On June 5, it was announced that Berulava had former a new partnership with [Anastasiia Metelkina](67224137.md) and that they would train in [Perm, Russia](389777.md) under Pavel Sliusarenko, Egor Zukroev, and [Maxim Trankov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Trankov).[^cite_note-metelkina-38][^cite_note-ISU-2324-39] Metelkina/Berulava opted to make their competitive debut on the [Junior Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023-24_ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix), winning the gold medal at the [2023 JGP Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_in_Turkey) in [Istanbul](3391396.md). Despite two falls in their free skate, their margin of victory over American silver medalists [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Flores)/[Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Wang) was almost 27 points.[^cite_note-ISU230911-40] Two weeks later they earned the gold at their second event, the [2023 JGP Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_in_Hungary) in [Budapest](36787.md), despite difficulties on their jump elements. Metelkina/Berulava's results qualified them to the [Junior Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023-24_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final) in December; they said they planned on getting senior-level experience in the meantime.[^cite_note-ISU230925-41]

Metelkina/Berulava made their senior debut at the [Warsaw Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CS_Warsaw_Cup), winning the gold medal.[^cite_note-ISU-AMLB-42] They then entered the Junior Grand Prix Final as heavy favourites to take the title, and won by a 34-point margin over Canadian silver medalists [Kemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ava_Kemp)/[Elizarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohnatan_Elizarov). They were the first Georgian pair team to win the Junior Grand Prix Final gold.[^cite_note-GS231209-43]

Entering the [2024 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Figure_Skating_Championships) in [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas) as among the favourites, Metelkina/Berulava took first place in the short program, winning a gold small medal. Metelkina erred on both of her jump sequences in the free skate, and they came fifth in that segment, dropping to second place overall. Berulava remarked that "not everything worked out. But it's only our first season together and silver is also a medal."[^cite_note-GS240111-44]

Having medaled at a senior championship event before returning to juniors, Metelkina/Berulava were heavy favourites at the [2024 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships) in [Taipei](57648.md), and won the short program by a margin of 9.20 points. They struggled in the free skate, with errors on all four jumping elements, but still finished first in that segment as well, and claimed the gold medal. Both vowed to work harder in training after the difficulties in the free program.[^cite_note-GS240229-45]

Making their senior [World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Figure_Skating_Championships) debut as a team at the [2024 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Figure_Skating_Championships) in [Montreal](7954681.md), Metelkina/Berulava were fifth in the short program. They were only 0.08 points behind fourth-place Germans [Hase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva_Fabienne_Hase)/[Volodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Volodin), thus narrowly missing participation in the final flight of the free skate. In that segment, they struggled with several errors, including an aborted lift, which saw them come tenth in the free skate and drop to seventh overall. Speaking afterward, a "despondent" Berulava said: "Right now, I don't have words to comment on this performance."[^cite_note-GS240322-46]

#### 2024–25 season: First Grand Prix gold, European bronze medal, and second World Junior gold medal



Metelkina and Berluava performing their short program at the [2025 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Figure_Skating_Championships)

Metelkina/Berulava did not compete on the [Challenger circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024-25_ISU_Challenger_Series) at the start of the season, as he explained their belief that they had begun the prior season "too early," as a result of which "we ended up feeling exhausted by the time the most important tournaments came by."[^cite_note-GS240922-4] They began the [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024-25_ISU_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating) at the [2024 Skate America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Skate_America), where they came third in the short program but dropped to fourth overall after a fourth-place free skate that featured multiple jump errors and a fall in their death spiral element. They missed the bronze medal by a fraction of a point, but Metelkina said that the "short margin to the bronze medal is not what really matters. The mistakes are what we have to improve and work on."[^cite_note-GS241020-47] At their second event, the [2024 NHK Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NHK_Trophy), Metelkina/Berulava won the gold medal, defeating reigning World champions [Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riku_Miura)/[Kihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryuichi_Kihara) in the process. This was the first Grand Prix medal of any colour for a Georgian pair team, of which Berulava said they were "just so proud."[^cite_note-GS241109-48] Shortly afterward, they competed at and won the [2024 CS Warsaw Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CS_Warsaw_Cup).[^cite_note-ISU-AMLB-42]



Metelkina and Berluava after their free skate at the [2025 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Figure_Skating_Championships)

The team's results at their Grand Prix events qualified them for the [Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024-25_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final) in [Grenoble](50972.md). They were third in the short program with a new personal best score of 72.26 points. They were second in the free skate with the lone error being an underrotated jump by Metelkina, remaining third overall and taking the bronze medal.[^cite_note-GS241207-49] The following month at [2025 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_European_Figure_Skating_Championships), Metelkina/Berulava entered as podium favourites, but came ninth in the short program after multiple errors, including an invalid death spiral. They rebounded in the free skate, coming third in that segment and rising to third overall for their second bronze medal at a major event of the season.[^cite_note-GS250130-50]

Metelkina/Berulava opted to return to the junior level to compete at the [2025 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships) in [Debrecen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrecen), for which they were subject to some criticism in skating circles. Berulava defended the decision, noting that it was allowed by the ISU's rules, and citing a desire to gain competitive experience and [World Standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_World_Standings_and_Season's_World_Ranking) points.[^cite_note-GS250130-50] They won both segments of the competition by a wide margin, taking their second consecutive World Junior title (and Berulava's third) by a margin of almost 27 points.[^cite_note-GS250301-51]

At the [2025 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Figure_Skating_Championships), they finished fourth in the short program, sixth in the free skate, and fourth overall.[^cite_note-GS032825P-52] "Actually, we’re happy with the fourth place, considering what we did," said Berulava. "It was a long season, but due to the good preparation by our coaches, we are not feeling tired and exhausted. We still feel ready to go, and we’re actually really hoping to make the World Team Trophy.”[^cite_note-GS032825P-52]

The team capped off the season with [2025 World Team Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ISU_World_Team_Trophy_in_Figure_Skating) where they placed third in both the short program and free skate, with Team Georgia taking sixth place overall. At this event, they earned a new personal best for both the short program and total score.[^cite_note-GS041825-53][^cite_note-GS041925-54] “It feels easier to skate in this event as there is so much support,” said Metelkina. “We hope to get into the Olympic team event and this competition was a good practice. It was very important for us to be here.”[^cite_note-GS041925-54]

#### 2025–26 season: Milano Cortina Olympic Pairs silver, World silver, and European champions



Metelkina and Berulava doing their opening pose for their short program at [2025 Skate America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Skate_America)

Metelkina/Berulava opened the 2025–26 season in September at [2025 CS Kinoshita Group Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CS_Kinoshita_Group_Cup) where they earned the silver medal. The following month, they took gold at [2025 CS Trialeti Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CS_Trialeti_Trophy).

Two weeks later, the team competed at [2025 Cup of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cup_of_China) where they won their second individual Grand Prix gold.[^cite_note-GS102625P-55] "Today was a rather difficult day," said Berulava after the free skate. "I'm glad we fought for this gold medal and didn't give up after the mistake on the jump."[^cite_note-GS102625P-55]

The following month, Metelkina/Berulava took silver at [2025 Skate America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Skate_America).[^cite_note-GS111525P-56] They placed first in the short program, but made costly mistakes in the free skate where they finished fourth. “Unfortunately, our performance today did not work out, but we will work hard,” said Berulava. “We want to improve, and we also will work on the mental side." After medaling in both Grand Prix events, they qualified for the 2025-26 [Grand Prix of Figure Skating Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final). “Now we will take a deep breath after this, and we will work on our mistakes,” Metelkina summed up. “And I'm also very pleased that the Final is in Japan. We always skate very well in Japan."[^cite_note-GS111525P-56]



Metelkina and Berulava performing a [death spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_spiral_\(figure_skating\)) during their free skate at [2025 Skate America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Skate_America)

In December, Metelkina/Berulava finished fourth at the [2025–26 Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Grand_Prix_of_Figure_Skating_Final). They placed third in the short program and fourth in the free skate after a shaky lift. "The skate was OK, we made some mistakes," Berulava acknowledged. "After the Final we will go home and will train hard to win the Europeans."[^cite_note-GS120525P-57]

The following month, the duo won the gold at the [2026 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_European_Figure_Skating_Championships), becoming the first Georgian Pairs team to do so. "We are crazy happy to have won the European championships!" said Metelkina. "We’ve had the bronze, the silver, and now we finally got the gold medal. We couldn't be happier right now!"[^cite_note-GS011526P-58] The team will compete next at the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md). "Right now, the situation is very interesting," said Berulava of the upcoming Olympics. "It's fascinating because there's not a clear leader. There are six top teams and all together, and everyone is aiming for the gold medal. Each of us, each athlete, we all have the same goal."[^cite_note-GS011526P-58] Later in the month, Berulava and ice dancer, [Diana Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Davis), were announced to have been selected as the flag bearers for the opening ceremony at the upcoming Olympic Games.[^cite_note-59]

On 6 February, Metelkina/Berulava placed second in the short program in the [2026 Winter Olympics Figure Skating Team Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_event). "It was a calm skate, a good warm up," said Metelkina. "We are confident and our coaches and our federation has confidence in us, too. We can do it all and practices were excellent. We had a very good week of training before the Olympic Games, therefore we went out calmly and did what we know we can do."[^cite_note-GS020626P-60] Two days later, they placed second in the free skate and Team Georgia finished in fourth place overall.[^cite_note-SS-AMLB-61]



Berulava and Metelkina after their performance at the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md)

On 15 February, Metelkina/Berulava competed in the short program of the [Pairs](82110766.md) event. They placed second in the short program despite Metelkina stepping out of the throw triple flip. "I'm always a maximalist, so I'm not satisfied that I made a mistake on an element that is usually the most consistent for me,” said Metelkina. “I will try to do everything clean tomorrow. I'm absolutely satisfied with what my partner did today. I am also grateful about the great support my partner gave me."[^cite_note-62] The following day, the pair skated a clean free program aside from Metelkina stepping out of the throw triple loop, placing second in that segment and winning the silver medal overall.[^cite_note-SS-AMLB-61] With this result, Metelkina/Berulava made history as the first Georgian athletes to medal at a Winter Olympic Games.[^cite_note-GS021626P-63] "It is a huge honor, and I am incredibly happy that our names are now written as the first Georgians to win a medal at the Olympic Winter Games. It's a huge honor. It was a long path for us, and we are unbelievably happy about it," said Berulava following the result. "I hope that after us, a big team will develop in Georgia — that we will have second, third, fourth pairs, and that there will be competition inside the country. After the end of my career, I want to take care of this, because I love figure skating and I want to do everything to develop pair skating in Georgia."[^cite_note-64]

Berulava and alpine skier, [Nino Tsiklauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nino_Tsiklauri), were subsequently selected as Georgia's flag bearers for the Olympic closing ceremony.[^cite_note-65]

On March 26, 2026, Metelkina/Berulava made history by becoming the first Georgian pair team to earn a world medal (silver) at the [2026 World Figure Skating Championships](82193351.md). The pair placed second in the short program and fourth in the free skate. “I’m very happy because this is our first medal at the World Championships and also that we ended the season on a high note,” said Metelkina. “There’s a medal and there are a lot of emotions. This season, I really want to thank everybody who was part of our preparation—our coaches, our federation. We tried our best and we will continue to try our best.”[^cite_note-GS032626P-66]
