# Tourism in Brazil



[Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), the most visited destination in [Brazil](3383.md) by foreign tourists for leisure trips, and second place for business travel



[São Paulo](390875.md) is the most visited city in Brazil, being the number one city for those looking for [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_tourism), events, [gastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culinary_tourism), [cultural tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_tourism) and a vibrant [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife).



Sancho Bay, [Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha), elected the most beautiful beach in the world by [TripAdvisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TripAdvisor)

[^cite_note-praias-1]

**Tourism in [Brazil](3383.md)** is a growing sector and key to the economy of several regions of the country. It had 9.28 million visitors in 2025, an increase of 32% compared to 2024,[^cite_note-\:1-2] ranking in terms of the international tourist arrivals as the second main destination in Latin America after [Mexico](3966054.md).[^cite_note-UNTourism2026-3] Revenues from international tourists reached US\$7.9 billion (R\$41.5 billion), an increase of 7.1% over 2024, according to data from the foreign sector statistics report released by the Central Bank (Bacen).[^cite_note-4]

Brazil offers for both domestic and international tourists an ample range of options, with natural areas being its most popular tourism product, a combination of [leisure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leisure) and [recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation), mainly sun and beach, and [adventure travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_travel), as well as historic and [cultural tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_tourism). Among the most popular destinations are beaches at [Rio de Janeiro](229419.md) and [Santa Catarina](256305.md), business trips to [São Paulo](390875.md) city, cultural and historic tourism in [Minas Gerais](222651.md), the [Iguazu Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iguazu_Falls) and the [Pantanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantanal) in the [Center-West Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center-West_Region,_Brazil).[^cite_note-dadosefatos.turismo.gov.br-5][^cite_note-6]

In terms of the 2024 [Travel and Tourism Competitiveness Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_and_Tourism_Competitiveness_Report) (TTCI), which is a measurement of the factors that make it attractive to develop business in the travel and tourism industry of individual countries, Brazil ranked in the 26th place at the world's level, third in the Americas, after Canada and United States.[^cite_note-TTCI2024-7] Brazil’s main competitive advantages are its natural resources, which ranked 3rd on this criterion out of all countries considered in the Americas, and ranked 2nd for its cultural resources, due to its many [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-8] The 2013 TTCI report also notes Brazil's main weaknesses: its ground transport infrastructure remains underdeveloped (ranked 129th), with the quality of roads ranking in the 121st place, and quality of air transport infrastructure in 131st; and the country continues to suffer from a lack of price competitiveness (ranked 126th), due in part to high and increasing ticket taxes and airport charges, as well as high and rising prices more generally. Safety and security improved significantly between 2008 and 2013, moving from 128th to 73rd place,[^cite_note-TTCI2013-9] before slipping to 106th by 2017.[^cite_note-TTCI2017-10]

According to the [World Tourism Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_Organization), international travel to Brazil began to grow fast since 2000, particularly during 2004 and 2005. However, in 2006 a slow down took place, and international arrivals have had almost no growth both in 2007 and 2008.[^cite_note-WTO2007-14][^cite_note-EMBRATUR09-17][^cite_note-WTO2008-28][^cite_note-29] In spite of this trend, revenues from international tourism continued to rise, from US\$3.9 billion in 2005 to US\$4.9 billion in 2007, a US\$1 billion increase despite 333,000 less arrivals. This favorable trend is the result of the strong devaluation of the [American dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_dollar) against the [Brazilian real](34201323.md), which began in 2004, but on the other hand, making Brazil a more expensive international destination.[^cite_note-Boletim0207-30] This trend changed in 2009, when both visitors and revenues fell as a result of the [2008–2009 economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession).[^cite_note-WTO2010-19] By 2010, the industry recovered, and arrivals grew above 2006 levels to 5.16 million international visitors, and receipts from these visitors reached US\$5.9 billion.[^cite_note-UNWTO2011-21] In 2012, the historical record was reached with 5.6 million visitors and US\$6.6 billion in receipts.[^cite_note-WTO2013Highlights-20]

Despite continuing record breaking of international tourism revenues, the number of Brazilian tourists travelling overseas has been growing steadily since 2003, resulting in a net negative [foreign exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market) balance, as more money is spent abroad by Brazilian than receipts from international tourist visiting Brazil. Tourism expenditures abroad grew from US\$5.76 billion in 2006, to US\$8.21 billion in 2007, a 42.45% increase, representing a net deficit of US\$3.26 billion in 2007, as compared to US\$1.45 billion in 2006, a 125% increase from the previous year.[^cite_note-Boletim0308-31] This trend is caused by Brazilians taking advantage of the stronger [Real](34201323.md) to travel and making relatively cheaper expenditures abroad.[^cite_note-Boletim0308-31] Brazilian traveling overseas in 2006 represented 3.9% of the country's population.[^cite_note-FIPE006-32]

In 2005, tourism contributed with 3.2% of the country's revenues from exports of goods and services, and represented 7% of direct and indirect employment in the Brazilian economy.[^cite_note-BID2006-33] In 2006, direct employment in the sector reached 1.87 million people.[^cite_note-IPEA08-34] Domestic tourism is a fundamental market segment for the industry, as 51 million traveled throughout the country in 2005,[^cite_note-FIPE007-35] and direct revenues from Brazilian tourists reached US\$21.8 billion,[^cite_note-36] 5.6 times more receipts than international tourists in 2005.

In 2005, [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), [Foz do Iguaçu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foz_do_Igua%C3%A7u), [São Paulo](390875.md), [Florianópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florian%C3%B3polis), and [Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia) were the most visited cities by international tourists for leisure trips. The most popular destinations for business trips were São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and [Porto Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Alegre).[^cite_note-EMBRATUR06-37] In 2006, Rio de Janeiro and [Fortaleza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza) were the most popular destinations by national visitors.[^cite_note-38]

## 2025 record arrivals

The year 2025 marked a record-breaking number of international visitors,[^cite_note-41] as announced by Embratur and the Ministry of Tourism. In 2025, Brazil registered 9,287,196 international arrivals from 205 countries. This represented a 37% increase compared to the previous record set in 2024, far surpassing the influx of tourists recorded during major mega-events hosted by the country, such as the 2014 FIFA World Cup and the 2016 Rio Olympics.

The most significant growth in arrivals came from Argentina, which saw a 72% year-over-year (YoY) increase. With over 3.3 million tourists, Argentines formed the largest demographic group to visit Brazil, followed by visitors from Chile, the United States, Paraguay, and Uruguay. Brazil also registered a substantial rise in tourists from Europe and Asia, with an overall increase of more than 20% from these regions. The United Kingdom, France, Portugal, Spain, Germany, Italy, and China represented the largest source markets outside the Americas.[^cite_note-\:0-40]

## Paleontological tourism

Main article: [Paleorrota Geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleorrota_Geopark)



[Lençóis Maranhenses National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len%C3%A7%C3%B3is_Maranhenses_National_Park), in [Maranhão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranh%C3%A3o)

Geopark Paleorrota is the main area of [geotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) in [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md) and one of the most important in Brazil. With 83,000 [km<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (32,000 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)) inside 281,000 km<sup>2</sup> (108,000 sq mi) of the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil), where many fossils of the [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) and [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic) period, with ages ranging between 210 and 290 million years ago, when there were only the continent [Pangaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangaea).

In the region [Metropolitan Porto Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Porto_Alegre) there are 5 museums to visit. In Paleorrota Geopark there are 7 museums, the [Palaeobotanical Garden in Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeobotanical_Garden_in_Mata) and the [Paleontological Sites of Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontological_Sites_of_Santa_Maria) to be visited. The [BR-287](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-287), nicknamed *[Highway of Dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_of_Dinosaurs)*, crosses 17 of 41 municipalities of the geopark.

## Domestic tourism

Domestic tourism is a key market segment for the tourism industry in Brazil. In 2005, 51 million Brazilian nationals made ten times more trips than foreign tourists and spent five times more money than their international counterparts.[^cite_note-FIPE007-35] The main destination states in 2005 were [São Paulo](229422.md) (27.7%), [Minas Gerais](222651.md) (10.8%), [Rio de Janeiro](229419.md) (8.4%), [Bahia](199070.md) (7.4%), and [Santa Catarina](256305.md) (7.2%). The top three states by trip origin were [São Paulo](229422.md) (35.7%), [Minas Gerais](222651.md) (13.6%).[^cite_note-FIPE007-35]

In terms of tourism revenues, the top earners by state were São Paulo (16.4%) and Bahia (11.7%). For 2005, the three main trip purposes were visiting friends and family (53.1%), sun and beach (40.8%), and cultural tourism (12.5%).[^cite_note-FIPE006-32]
