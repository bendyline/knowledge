The **Zulu Kingdom** ([/ˈzuːluː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ZOO-loo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_language): *KwaZulu*), sometimes referred to as the **Zulu Empire**, was a monarchy in [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa). During the 1810s, [Shaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaka) established a standing army that consolidated rival clans and built a large following which ruled a wide expanse of Southern Africa that extended along the coast of the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) from the [Tugela River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugela_River) in the south to the [Pongola River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pongola_River) in the north, centred on the present [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md) [province of South Africa](53215.md).

A civil war in the mid-19th century erupted which culminated in the 1856 [Battle of Ndondakusuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ndondakusuka) between the brothers [Cetshwayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetshwayo) and Mbuyazi. In 1879, a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) force invaded Zululand, beginning the [Anglo-Zulu War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Zulu_War). After an initial Zulu victory at the [Battle of Isandlwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Isandlwana) in January, the British regrouped and defeated the Zulus in July during the [Battle of Ulundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ulundi), ending the war. The area was absorbed into the [Colony of Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Natal) and later became part of the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa). The current Zulu king is [Misuzulu Sinqobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misuzulu_Sinqobile), who serves as the monarch of South Africa's [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md) province.

## History

### Background

Main article: [Mfecane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfecane)

The founding of the Zulu Kingdom was precipitated by the [Mfecane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfecane), a period of widespread instability and [state formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_formation) in Southern Africa, caused by pre-existing trends of political centralisation which were catalysed by the effects of international trade, environmental instability, and European colonial expansion.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

### Rise under Shaka

Main article: [Shaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaka)

Further information: [Zulu royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_royal_family)



Drawing of King Shaka (c. 1824)

[Shaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaka) was the [illegitimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegitimate) son of [Senzangakhona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senzangakhona), Chief of the Zulus. He was born c. 1787. He and his mother, [Nandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_\(mother_of_Shaka\)), were exiled by Senzangakhona, and found refuge with the [Mthethwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mthethwa_Paramountcy). Shaka fought as a warrior under [Dingiswayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dingiswayo), leader of the Mthethwa Paramountcy. When Senzangakona died, Dingiswayo helped Shaka become king of the Zulu. After Dingiswayo's death at the hands of [Zwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwide_kaLanga), king of the [Ndwandwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndwandwe), around 1816, Shaka assumed leadership of the entire Mthethwa alliance.[^cite_note-4]

Shaka expanded the Zulu Kingdom through war and diplomacy.[^cite_note-\:02-5] Shaka's clan at first numbered no more than a few thousands, but eventually grew in size to 45,000 after absorbing neighbouring clans. His military reforms included new battle techniques, training, and tough discipline, as well as the replacement of long throwing spears in exchange for the more effective short stabbing spears. Conscripted men were segregated from the rest of Zulu society to be trained as an organised standing army called the *amabutho*.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

The alliance under his leadership survived Zwide's first assault at the [Battle of Gqokli Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gqokli_Hill) (1818). Within two years, Shaka had defeated Zwide at the [Battle of Mhlatuze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mhlatuze_River) (1820) and broken up the Ndwandwe alliance. The [Ngoni people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngoni_people) fled as far north as Tanzania and Malawi.[^cite_note-7] By 1822, Shaka had conquered an empire covering an area of around 80,000 square miles (210,000 km<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-Gluckman-1] covering Pongola to the Tugera Rivers.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

An offshoot of the Zulu, the amaNdebele, better known to history as the [Matabele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ndebele_people), created an even larger empire under their king [Mzilikazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzilikazi), including large parts of the [highveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highveld) and modern-day [Zimbabwe](34399.md).[^cite_note-8]

### Dingane's reign



King Dingane

Shaka was succeeded by [Dingane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dingane), his half-brother, who conspired with Mhlangana, another half-brother, and Mbopa, an [induna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induna), to murder him in 1828. Following this assassination, Dingane murdered Mhlangana, and took over the throne. One of his first royal acts was to execute all of his royal kin. In the years that followed, he also executed many past supporters of Shaka in order to secure his position. One exception to these purges was [Mpande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpande), another half-brother, who was considered too weak to be a threat at the time.[^cite_note-ULWAZI-9]

#### Clashes with Voortrekkers



Military innovations such as the *[assegai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assegai)*, the age-grade regimental system and [encirclement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encirclement) tactics helped make the Zulu one of the most powerful clans in southern and south-eastern Africa.

In the [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony), increasing tensions between some Dutch Settlers and the [British colonial authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) led to a wave of [Boer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boers) (also known as Voortrekkers) migration northwards from 1836 onwards in order to establish autonomous Boer states independent of British control. The Boers settlers began moving across the [Orange River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River) northwards. While travelling they first collided with the Ndebele kingdom, and then with Dingane's Zulu kingdom.[^cite_note-10] In October 1837, the [Voortrekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voortrekker) leader [Piet Retief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Retief) visited Dingane at his royal kraal to negotiate a land deal for the voortrekkers. In November, about 1,000 Voortrekker wagons began descending the [Drakensberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakensberg) mountains from the [Orange Free State](180208.md) into what is now [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md).

Dingane asked that Retief and his party retrieve some cattle stolen from him by a local chief as part of the treaty for land for the Boers. This Retief and his men did, returning on 3 February 1838. The next day, a treaty was signed, wherein Dingane ceded all the land south of the [Tugela River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugela_River) to the [Mzimvubu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzimvubu_River) to the Voortrekkers. Celebrations followed. On 6 February, at the end of the celebrations, Retief's party were invited to a dance, and asked to leave their weapons behind. At the peak of the dance, Dingane leapt to his feet and yelled *"Bambani abathakathi!"* ([isiZulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IsiZulu) for "Seize the wizards").

Retief and his men were overpowered, taken to the nearby hill kwaMatiwane, and executed. Some allege that they were killed for withholding some of the cattle they recovered, but it is likely that the deal was a plot to overpower the Voortrekkers. Dingane's army then attacked and [massacred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weenen_massacre) a group of 250 Voortrekker men, women and children camped nearby. The site of this massacre is today called [Weenen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weenen), ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) for "to weep"). The remaining Voortrekkers elected a new leader, [Andries Pretorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andries_Pretorius), who led a successful defence of the Voortrekker position from the Zulu forces and Dingane at the [Battle of Blood River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blood_River) on 16 December 1838, when 15,000 Zulu [impis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impi) (warriors) attacked a group of 470 Voortrekker settlers led by Pretorius.

### Mpande's reign



King Mpande, drawing by [George French Angas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_French_Angas)

Following his defeat, Dingane burned his royal household and fled north. [Mpande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpande), the half-brother who had been spared from Dingane's purges, defected with 17,000 followers, and, together with Pretorius and the Voortrekkers, went to war with Dingane. Dingane was assassinated near the modern [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland) border. Mpande then took over rulership of the Zulu nation.

Following the campaign against Dingane, in 1839 the Voortrekkers, under Pretorius, formed the [Boer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer) republic of [Natalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Republic), south of the Tugela, and west of the British settlement of Port Natal (now [Durban](313650.md)). Mpande and Pretorius maintained peaceful relations. However, in 1842, war broke out between the British and the Boers, resulting in the British annexation of Natalia. Mpande shifted his allegiance to the British, and remained on good terms with them.

In 1843, Mpande ordered a purge of perceived dissidents within his kingdom. This resulted in numerous deaths, and the fleeing of thousands of refugees into neighbouring areas (including the British-controlled [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal_Province)). Many of these refugees fled with cattle. Mpande began raiding the surrounding areas, culminating in the invasion of [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland) in 1852. However, the British pressured him into withdrawing, which he did shortly.[^cite_note-ULWAZI-9]

### Cetshwayo's reign

At this time, a battle for the succession broke out between two of Mpande's sons, [Cetshwayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetshwayo) and Mbuyazi. This culminated in 1856 with the [Battle of Ndondakusuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ndondakusuka), which left Mbuyazi dead. Cetshwayo then set about usurping his father's authority. When Mpande died of old age in 1872, Cetshwayo took over as ruler.

#### British Conquest

Main article: [Anglo-Zulu War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Zulu_War)



The [Battle of Isandlwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Isandlwana), 1879

On 11 December 1878, with the intent of instigating a war with the Zulu, [Sir Henry Bartle Frere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bartle_Frere), on his own initiative and without the approval of the British government, presented an ultimatum to the Zulu king Cetshwayo in terms with which he could not possibly comply:[^cite_note-11] that the Zulu army be disbanded and the Zulus accept a British resident. British forces crossed the Tugela river at the end of December 1878. Initially, the British suffered a heavy defeat at the [Battle of Isandlwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Isandlwana) on 22 January 1879 where the Zulu army killed more than 1,000 British soldiers in a single day.

The Zulu deployment at Isandhlwana showed the well-organized tactical system that had made the Zulu kingdom successful for many decades. This constituted the worst defeat the British army had ever suffered at the hands of a native African fighting force. The defeat prompted a redirection of the war effort, and the British, though outnumbered, began winning small engagements and later larger setpiece encounters. The fighting culminated in the [Siege of Ulundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ulundi), the Zulus' capital city, and the subsequent defeat of the Zulu Kingdom.

#### Division and the death of Cetshwayo



King Cetshwayo (c. 1875)

Cetshwayo was captured a month after his defeat, and then exiled to [Cape Town](6653.md). The British passed rule of the Zulu kingdom onto 13 "kinglets", each with his own subkingdom. Conflict soon erupted between these subkingdoms, and in 1882, Cetshwayo was allowed to visit England. He had audiences with [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) and other famous personages before being allowed to return to Zululand to be reinstated as king.[^cite_note-ULWAZI-9]

In 1883, Cetshwayo was put in place as king over a buffer reserve territory, much reduced from his original kingdom. Later that year, however, Cetshwayo was attacked at Ulundi by [Zibhebhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zibhebhu_kaMaphitha), one of the 13 kinglets. Cetshwayo was wounded and fled. Cetshwayo died in February 1884, possibly poisoned. His son, [Dinuzulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinuzulu), then 15, inherited the throne.[^cite_note-ULWAZI-9]

The academic Roberto Breschi notes that Zululand had a flag from 1884 to 1897 but this is pure conjecture as A.P. Burgers notes in his book.[^cite_note-12] It consisted of three horizontal bands in equal width of gold, green and red.

### Dinuzulu's reign and exile



King Dinuzulu (c. 1883)

Dinuzulu made a pact with the [Boers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boers) of his own, promising them land in return for their aid. The Boers were led by [Louis Botha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Botha). Dinuzulu and the Boers defeated Zibhebhu in 1884. They were granted about half of Zululand individually as farms, and formed the independent [Republic of Vryheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vryheid). This alarmed the British who wanted to prevent the Boers access to a harbour. The British then annexed Zululand in 1887. Dinuzulu became involved in later conflicts with rivals. In 1906 Dinuzulu was accused of being behind the [Bambatha Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambatha_Rebellion). He was arrested and put on trial by the British for "high treason and public violence". In 1909, he was sentenced to ten years' imprisonment on [St Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Helena) island. When the Union of South Africa was formed, Louis Botha became its first prime minister, and he arranged for his old ally Dinuzulu to return to South Africa and live in exile on a farm in the Transvaal, where he died in 1913.[^cite_note-ULWAZI-9]

Dinuzulu's son [Solomon kaDinuzulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_kaDinuzulu) was never recognised by South African authorities as the Zulu king, only as a local chief, but he was increasingly regarded as king by chiefs, by political intellectuals such as [John Langalibalele Dube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langalibalele_Dube) and by ordinary Zulu people. In 1923, Solomon founded the organisation Inkatha YaKwaZulu to promote his royal claims, which became moribund and then was revived in the 1970s by [Mangosuthu Buthelezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosuthu_Buthelezi), chief minister of the KwaZulu bantustan. In December 1951, Solomon's son [Cyprian Bhekuzulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian_Bhekuzulu) kaSolomon was officially recognised as the Paramount Chief of the Zulu people, but real power over ordinary Zulu people lay with South African government officials working through local chiefs who could be removed from office for failure to cooperate.[^cite_note-ULWAZI-9]

### KwaZulu Bantustan

[KwaZulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu) was a [bantustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantustan) in [South Africa](17416221.md), intended by the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) government as a semi-independent homeland for the [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_people) people. The capital was moved from [Nongoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nongoma) to [Ulundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulundi) in 1980.

It was led until its abolition in 1994 by Chief [Mangosuthu Buthelezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosuthu_Buthelezi) of the [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_people) royal family and head of the [Inkatha Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkatha_Freedom_Party) (IFP). It was merged with the surrounding South African province of [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_Province) to form the new province of [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md).

The name *KwaZulu* translates roughly as *Place of [Zulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_people)*, or more formally *Zululand*.

### Contemporary Zululand

Main articles: [Zulu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_people) and [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md)

The Zulu Kingdom is currently part of South Africa's [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md) (of which the [King of the Zulu Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Zulu_Nation) is the monarch), one of the country's nine provinces, under the leadership of [King MisuZulu ka Zwelithini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misuzulu_Zulu). A large portion of the territory is made up of wildlife reserves and a major contributing source of income is derived from tourism – the area is known for its [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) covered hills. It is home to a [WWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature) [Black Rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Rhinoceros) reintroduction project known as "The Black Rhino Range Expansion Project" within the *[Zululand Rhino Reserve (ZRR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manyoni_Private_Game_Reserve)*. The ZRR is a 20,000 hectare reserve consisting of 15 individually owned farms that have lowered their fences in order to further conservation. The [Zulu royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_royal_family) still fulfils many important ceremonial duties.

The cause of what became known as the Mfecane was once attributed to the rise of the Zulu kingdom. But recent scholarship indicates that the Zulu kingdom under Shaka kaSenzangakhona was a product rather than a cause of the Mfecane. In reality the conflicts and wars of the late 18th and early 19th centuries took the form of struggles and competition between large political formations such as the Ngwane, Ndwandwe, and Mthethwa. The leaders of these political formations were engaged in increasing their power through military conquest of people and acquisition of land, while at the same time incorporating defeated enemies into their ranks. Shaka kaSenzangakhona's Zulu kingdom emerged from these competitive struggles with the status of an empire.
