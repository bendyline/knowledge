**Ostriches** are large [flightless birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightless_bird). Two living species are recognised: the [common ostrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ostrich), native to large parts of [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md), and the [Somali ostrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_ostrich), native to the [Horn of Africa](62142.md).

They are the heaviest and largest living birds, with adult common ostriches weighing anywhere between 63.5–145 kg (140–320 lb), and laying the largest [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg) of any living land animal.[^cite_note-world-2] With the ability to run at 70 km/h (43 mph),[^cite_note-Doherty-3] they are the fastest birds on land. They are farmed worldwide, with significant industries [in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_farming_in_the_Philippines) and [in Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_farming_in_Namibia). [South Africa](17416221.md) produces about 70% of global ostrich products,[^cite_note-4] with the industry largely centered around the town of [Oudtshoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudtshoorn). [Ostrich leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_leather) is a lucrative commodity, and the large feathers are used as [plumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plume_\(feather\)) for the decoration of ceremonial headgear. [Ostrich eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_egg) and [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_meat) have been used by humans for millennia. [Ostrich oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_oil) is another product that is made using ostrich fat.&#x20;

Ostriches belong to the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) ***Struthio*** in the order [Struthioniformes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struthioniformes), part of the infra-class [Palaeognathae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeognathae), a diverse group of flightless and weakly-flying birds also known as [ratites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratite) that includes the [cassowaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassowary), [emus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu), [rheas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhea_\(bird\)), [kiwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiwi_\(bird\)), [tinamous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinamou), and the extinct [elephant birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_bird) and [moa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moa).

The common ostrich was historically native to the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), and ostriches were present across Asia as far east as China and Mongolia during the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene) and possibly into the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_climatic_optimum).

## Taxonomic history

The genus *Struthio* was first described by [Carl Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus) in 1758. The genus was used by Linnaeus and other early taxonomists to include the [emu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu), [rhea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhea_\(bird\)), and [cassowary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassowary), until they each were placed in their own genera.[^cite_note-Gray-1] The Somali ostrich (*Struthio molybdophanes*) has recently become recognised as a separate species by most authorities, while others are still reviewing the evidence.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-BLI-6]

## Evolution

[Struthionidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struthionidae) is a member of the [Struthioniformes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struthioniformes), a group of [paleognath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleognath) birds which first appeared during the Early [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene), and includes a variety of flightless forms which were present across the Northern Hemisphere (Europe, Asia and North America) during the Eocene epoch. The closest relatives of Struthionidae within the Struthioniformes are the [Ergilornithidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergilornithidae), known from the late Eocene to early [Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliocene) of Asia. It is therefore most likely that Struthionidae originated in Asia.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

The earliest fossils of the genus *Struthio* are from the early [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) \~21 million years ago of Namibia in Africa, so it is proposed that genus is of African origin. By the middle to late Miocene (5–13 mya) they had spread to and become widespread across Eurasia.[^cite_note-8] While the relationship of the African fossil species is comparatively straightforward, many Asian species of ostrich have been described from fragmentary remains, and their interrelationships and how they relate to the African ostriches are confusing. In India, Mongolia and [China](5405.md), ostriches are known to have become extinct only around, or even after, the end of the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period); images of ostriches have been found prehistoric Chinese pottery and [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-janz-10][^cite_note-r1-11][^cite_note-12]

## Distribution and habitat

Today, ostriches are only found naturally in the wild in [Africa](5334607.md), where they occur in a range of open arid and semi-arid habitats such as [savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) and the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel), both north and south of the equatorial forest zone.[^cite_note-Donegan_2002-13] The [Somali ostrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_ostrich) occurs in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md), having evolved isolated from the common ostrich by the geographic barrier of the [East African Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Rift). In some areas, the common ostrich's [Masai subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masai_ostrich) occurs alongside the Somali ostrich, but they are kept from interbreeding by behavioural and ecological differences.[^cite_note-Freitag-14] The [Arabian ostriches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_ostrich) in [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor) and [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) were hunted to extinction by the middle of the 20th century, and in [Israel](9282173.md) attempts to introduce [North African ostriches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_ostrich) to fill their ecological role have failed.[^cite_note-15] Escaped common ostriches in Australia have established [feral](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/feral) populations.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Diet

Ostriches are omnivores although plants make up the majority of their diet. They primarily eat plant foods including leafy greens, roots, grasses, fruit and succulents. They will also eat small animals such as lizards, frogs and mice, as well as insects such as grasshoppers or locusts[^cite_note-19].&#x20;

Ostriches do not have teeth, and like other birds, swallow sand and pebbles to grind food in their stomachs.
