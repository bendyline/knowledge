**North Carolina** ([/ˌkærəˈlaɪnə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/62/En-us-North_Carolina.ogg/En-us-North_Carolina.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/62/En-us-North_Carolina.ogg/En-us-North_Carolina.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*KAYR-ə-LIE-nə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [state](18618239.md) in the [Southeastern](816925.md) and [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_states) regions of the [United States](3434750.md). It is bordered by [Virginia](32432.md) to the north, the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the east, [South Carolina](27956.md) to the south, [Georgia](48830.md) to the southwest, and [Tennessee](30395.md) to the west. The state is the [28th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) and [ninth-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) of the [United States](12610470.md). Along with South Carolina, it makes up the [Carolinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinas) region of the [East Coast](89126.md). At the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the state had a population of 10,439,388.[^cite_note-2020CensusQuickFacts-10] [Raleigh](57845.md) is the state's [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) and [Charlotte](57447.md) is [its most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_North_Carolina) and one of the fastest growing cities in the United States.[^cite_note-11] The [Charlotte metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_metropolitan_area), with an estimated population of 2,883,370 in 2024,[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-12] is the [most populous metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_statistical_areas) in North Carolina, the [21st-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the United States, and the largest banking center in the nation after New York City.[^cite_note-13] The [Research Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle), with an estimated population of 2,368,947 in 2023, is the second-most populous combined metropolitan area in the state, [31st-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) in the United States,[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-12] and is home to the largest research park in the United States, [Research Triangle Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle_Park).

## Overview

The earliest evidence of human occupation in North Carolina dates back 10,000 years, found at the [Hardaway Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardaway_Site). North Carolina was inhabited by tribes of Native Americans prior to the arrival of Europeans. [King Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) granted eight lord proprietors a colony they named Carolina after the king and which was established in 1670 with the first permanent settlement at Charles Town (now [Charleston, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina)). Because of the difficulty of governing the entire colony from Charles Town, the colony was eventually divided and North Carolina was established as a [royal colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_colony) in 1729 and was one of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies). The [Halifax Resolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Resolves) resolution adopted by North Carolina on April 12, 1776, was the first formal call for independence from Great Britain among the American Colonies during the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution).[^cite_note-14]

On November 21, 1789, North Carolina became the 12th state to ratify the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). In the run-up to the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), North Carolina declared its [secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_in_the_United_States) from the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) on May 20, 1861, becoming the tenth of eleven states to join the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America).[^cite_note-15] Following the Civil War, the state was restored to the Union on July 4, 1868.[^cite_note-16] On December 17, 1903, [Orville and Wilbur Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orville_and_Wilbur_Wright) successfully piloted the world's first controlled, sustained flight of a powered, [heavier-than-air aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) at [Kitty Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Hawk,_North_Carolina) in North Carolina's [Outer Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Banks). North Carolina often uses the slogan "First in Flight" on state [license plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/License_plates) to commemorate this achievement, alongside a newer alternative design bearing the slogan "First in Freedom" in reference to the [Mecklenburg Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Declaration) and Halifax Resolves.

North Carolina is defined by a wide range of elevations and landscapes. From west to east, North Carolina's elevation descends from the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) to the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)) and [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain). North Carolina's [Mount Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mitchell) at 6,684 ft (2,037 m) is the highest point in North America east of the [Black Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hills), in South Dakota.[^cite_note-17] Most of the state falls in the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) zone; however, the western, mountainous part of the state has a [subtropical highland climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate).[^cite_note-18]

## History

Main article: [History of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_North_Carolina)

### Native Americans, lost colony, and permanent settlement

See also: [Native Americans in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), [Joara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joara), [Roanoke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Island), and [Fort Raleigh National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Raleigh_National_Historic_Site)



Ceremony of [Secotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secotan) warriors in North Carolina. Watercolor painted by English colonist [John White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_White_\(colonist_and_artist\)) in 1585.

North Carolina was inhabited for at least 10,000 years by succeeding [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric) [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) cultures. The [Hardaway Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardaway_Site) saw major periods of occupation dating to 10,000 years BCE. Before 200 AD, people were building [earthwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworks_\(archaeology\)) [platform mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_mounds) for ceremonial and religious purposes. Succeeding peoples, including those of the [South Appalachian Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Appalachian_Mississippian_culture), established by 1000 AD in the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)) and mountain region, continued to build this style of mounds. In contrast to some of the larger centers of the classic Mississippian culture in the area that became known as the western Carolinas, northeastern Georgia, and southeastern Tennessee, most of the larger towns had only one central platform mound. Smaller settlements had none, but were close to more prominent towns. This area became known as the homelands of the historic [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee) people, who are believed to have migrated over time from the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) area.

In the 500–700 years preceding European contact, the Mississippian culture built elaborate cities and maintained far-flung regional trading networks. Its largest city was [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia), which had numerous mounds for different purposes, a highly stratified society, and was located in present-day southwestern Illinois near the Mississippi River. Starting in 1540, the Native polities of the Mississippian culture fell apart and reformed as new groups, such as the [Catawba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_people), due to a series of destabilizing events known as the "[Mississippian shatter zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_shatter_zone)". Introduction of colonial trading arrangements and hostile native groups from the north such as the Westo Indians hastened changes in an already tenuous regional hierarchy.[^cite_note-19] As described by anthropologist [Robbie Ethridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Ethridge), the Mississippian shatter zone was a time of great instability in what is now the American South, caused by the instability of Mississippian chiefdoms, high mortality from new Eurasian diseases, conversion to an agricultural society and the accompanying population increase, and the emergence of Native "militaristic slaving societies".[^cite_note-20]

Historically documented tribes in the North Carolina region include various [Algonquian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_peoples), such as the [Chowanoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowanoc), [Roanoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_\(tribe\)), [Pamlico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamlico) and [Machapunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machapunga), who were the first encountered by the English; [Iroquoian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquoian_peoples) such as the [Meherrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meherrin), [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee), and [Tuscarora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscarora_\(tribe\)); and others, such as the [Cheraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheraw), [Coree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coree), [Waxhaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxhaw_people), [Saponi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saponi), [Waccamaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waccamaw), [Cape Fear Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_Indians), [Yuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuchi) and [Catawba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_\(tribe\)).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The first documented European exploration of the coast of present-day North Carolina was undertaken by the Florentine navigator [Giovanni da Verrazzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Verrazzano), who sailed in the service of the French Crown. In 1524, during his expedition along the Atlantic coast of North America, Verrazzano visited and explored the region between present-day Cape Fear and Pamlico Sound, providing the first known European description of that area. The primary record of this voyage is the letter Verrazzano sent to [King Francis I of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_I_of_France), preserved in several manuscript copies, including the Cèllère Codex, which describes in detail the geography, Indigenous peoples, and natural features of the explored coast.

In the late 16th century, the first Spanish explorers traveling inland recorded meeting [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) people at [Joara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joara), a regional [chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdom) near what later developed as [Morganton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morganton,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-25] Records of [Hernando de Soto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_de_Soto) attested to his meeting with them in 1540. In 1567, Captain [Juan Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pardo_\(explorer\)) led an expedition to claim the area for the Spanish colony and to establish another route to reach silver mines in Mexico.[^cite_note-26] Pardo made a winter base at Joara, which he renamed *Cuenca*.[^cite_note-\:3-27][^cite_note-28] His expedition built [Fort San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_San_Juan_\(Joara\)) and left a contingent of 30 Spaniards there, while Pardo traveled further.[^cite_note-\:3-27] His forces built and garrisoned five other forts. He returned by a different route to [Santa Elena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Santa_Elena) on [Parris Island, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parris_Island,_South_Carolina), then a center of [Spanish Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida). In the spring of 1568, natives killed all but seventy four of the Spaniards and burned the six forts in the interior, including Fort San Juan.[^cite_note-29] Although the Spanish never returned to the interior, this effort marked the first European attempt at colonization of the interior of what became the United States. A 16th-century journal by Pardo's scribe Bandera, and [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) findings since 1986 at Joara, have confirmed the settlement.[^cite_note-richards-30][^cite_note-31]

### Anglo-European settlement



[Sir Walter Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Raleigh), namesake of the state capital of North Carolina, [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh)

In 1584, [Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I) granted a charter to [Sir Walter Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Walter_Raleigh), for whom the state capital is named, for land in present-day North Carolina (then part of the territory of [Virginia](32432.md)).[^cite_note-32] It was the second American territory that the English attempted to colonize. Raleigh established two colonies on the coast in the late 1580s, but both failed. The colony established in 1587 saw 118 colonists 'disappear' when [John White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_White_\(colonist_and_artist\)) was unable to return from a supply run during battles with the [Spanish Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armada). The fate of the "[Lost Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Colony)" of [Roanoke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Island) remains one of the most widely debated mysteries of American history. Two native Chieftains, [Manteo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteo_\(Native_American_leader\)) and [Wanchese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanchese_\(Native_American_leader\)), of which the former helped the colonists and the latter was distrustful, had involvement in the colony and even accompanied Raleigh to England on a previous voyage in 1585. Manteo was also the first Indigenous North American to be baptized by English settlers. Upon White's return in 1590, neither native nor Englishman were to be found. Popular theory holds that the colonists either traveled away with or assimilated into local native culture.[^cite_note-33] [Virginia Dare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Dare), the first English person to be born in North America, was born on Roanoke Island on August 18, 1587; the surrounding [Dare County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dare_County) is named for her.

As early as 1650, settlers from the [Virginia colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_colony) had moved into the [Albemarle Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_Sound) region. By 1663, King [Charles II of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) granted a [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter) to start a new colony on the North American continent; this would generally establish North Carolina's borders. He named it *Carolina* in honor of his father, [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England).[^cite_note-34] By 1665, a second charter was issued to attempt to resolve territorial questions. This charter rewarded the [Lords Proprietors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_Proprietors), eight Englishmen to whom King Charles II granted joint ownership of a tract of land in the state. All of these men either had remained loyal to the Crown or aided Charles's restoration to the English throne after [Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cromwell). In 1712, owing to disputes over governance, the Carolina colony split into North Carolina and [South Carolina](27956.md). North Carolina became a crown colony in 1729.[^cite_note-35]

[Most of the English colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery_in_North_Carolina) had arrived as [indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servant), hiring themselves out as laborers for a fixed period to pay for their passage. In the early years the line between indentured servants and African [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave) or laborers was fluid. Some Africans were allowed to earn their freedom before slavery became a lifelong status. Most of the [free colored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_people_of_color) families formed in North Carolina before the Revolution were descended from unions or marriages between free whites and enslaved or free Africans or African-Americans. If the mothers were free, their children were born free. Many had migrated or were descendants of migrants from colonial Virginia.[^cite_note-36] As the flow of indentured laborers to the colony decreased with improving economic conditions in [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain), planters imported more slaves, and the state's legal delineations between free and slave status tightened, effectively hardening the latter into a racial caste. Conditions for both slaves and workers worsened as the ranks of the former eclipsed the latter and expansion of farming operations into former Indigenous territories lowered prices. Unable to establish deep water ports such as at Charles Town and Norfolk, the economy's growth and prosperity was thus based on cheap labor and slave plantation systems, devoted primarily to the production of tobacco, then later cotton and textiles.[^cite_note-\:2-37]

In [1738–1739](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1738%E2%80%931739_North_Carolina_smallpox_epidemic), smallpox caused high fatalities among the Native Americans, who had no [immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunity_\(medical\)) to the new disease (it had become [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) over centuries in Europe).[^cite_note-38] According to the historian Russell Thornton, "The 1738 epidemic was said to have killed one-half of the [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee), with other tribes of the area suffering equally."[^cite_note-39]

### Colonial period

After the Spanish in the 16th century, the first permanent European settlers of North Carolina were English colonists who migrated south from [Virginia](32432.md). Virginia had grown rapidly and land was less available. [Nathaniel Batts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Batts) was documented as one of the first of these Virginian migrants. He settled south of the [Chowan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowan_River) and east of the [Great Dismal Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Dismal_Swamp) in 1655.[^cite_note-40] By 1663, this northeastern area of the [Province of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Carolina), known as the [Albemarle Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_Settlements), was undergoing full-scale English settlement.[^cite_note-41] During the same period, the English monarch Charles II gave provincial land grants to the [Lords Proprietors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_Proprietors), the group of noblemen who had helped restore him to the throne in 1660. These grants were predicated on an agreement that the Lords would use their influence to bring in colonists and establish ports of trade. This new Province of Carolina was named in honor and memory of his father, Charles I (Latin: *Carolus*).

Lacking a viable coastal port city due to geography, towns grew at a slower pace and remained small. By the late 17th century, Carolina was essentially two colonies, one centered in the Albemarle region in the north and the other located in the south around Charleston.[^cite_note-\:2-37] In 1705 South Carolinian [John Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lawson_\(explorer\)) purchased land on the Pamlico River and laid out [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_North_Carolina), North Carolina's first town. After returning to England, he published the book A New Voyage to Carolina, which became a travelogue and a marketing piece to encourage new colonists to Carolina. Lawson encouraged Baron [Christoph von Graffenried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_von_Graffenried), the leader of a group of Swiss and German Protestants, to immigrate to Carolina. Von Graffenried purchased land between the Neuse and the Trent Rivers and established the town of [New Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Bern). After an attack on New Bern in which hundreds were killed or injured, Lawson was caught then executed by [Tuscarora Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscarora_Indians). A large revolt happened in the state in 1711, known as [Cary's Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary's_Rebellion). In 1712, North Carolina became a separate colony, and in 1729 it became a royal colony, with the exception of the [Earl Granville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carteret,_2nd_Earl_Granville) holdings.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-42]

In June 1718, *[Queen Anne's Revenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne's_Revenge)*, the flagship of pirate [Blackbeard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbeard), ran aground at Beaufort Inlet, North Carolina, in present-day [Carteret County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carteret_County). After the grounding, her crew and supplies were transferred to smaller ships. In November 1718, after appealing to the governor of North Carolina, who promised safe-haven and a pardon, Blackbeard was killed in an ambush by troops from Virginia.[^cite_note-43] In 1996, Intersal, Inc., a private maritime research firm, discovered the remains of a vessel likely to be the *Queen Anne's Revenge*, which was added to the U.S. [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

North Carolina became one of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) and with the territory of [South Carolina](27956.md) was originally known as the [Province of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_North_Carolina). The northern and southern parts of the original province separated in 1712, with North Carolina becoming a royal colony in 1729. Originally settled by small farmers, sometimes having a few slaves, who were oriented toward [subsistence agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture), the colony lacked large cities or towns. [Pirates](50715.md) menaced the coastal settlements, but by 1718 piracy in the Carolinas was on the decline. Growth was strong in the middle of the 18th century, as the economy attracted [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch-Irish_American), [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) immigrants. A majority of the North Carolina colonists generally supported the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), although there were some [Loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalist_\(American_Revolution\)). Loyalists in North Carolina were fewer in number than in some other colonies such as Georgia, South Carolina, Delaware, and New York.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

During colonial times, Bath was the first provincial capital after the formation of North Carolina from the province of Carolina in 1712, followed by [Edenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edenton) beginning in 1722, followed by [New Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Bern) becoming the capital in 1766. Construction of [Tryon Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryon_Palace), which served as the residence and offices of the provincial governor [William Tryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tryon), began in 1767 and was completed in 1771. In 1788, [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh) was chosen as the site of the new capital, as its central location protected it from coastal attacks. Officially established in 1792 as both county seat and state capital, the city was named after Sir [Walter Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Raleigh), sponsor of [Roanoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony), the "lost colony" on [Roanoke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Island).[^cite_note-49] The population of the colony more than quadrupled from 52,000 in 1740 to 270,000 in 1780 from high immigration from Virginia, [Maryland](18858.md) and [Pennsylvania](23332.md), plus immigrants from abroad.[^cite_note-50]

North Carolina did not have any printer or print shops until 1749, when the North Carolina Assembly commissioned [James Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Davis_\(printer\)) from Williamsburg Virginia to act as their official printer. Before this time the laws and legal journals of North Carolina were handwritten and were kept in a largely disorganized manner, prompting the hiring of Davis. Davis settled in New Bern, married, and in 1755 was appointed by [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin) as North Carolina's first postmaster. In October of that year the North Carolina Assembly awarded Davis a contract to carry mail between [Wilmington, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina) and [Suffolk, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk,_Virginia). He was also active in North Carolina politics as a member of the Assembly and later as the Sheriff. Davis also founded and printed the *[North-Carolina Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Carolina_Gazette)*, North Carolina's first newspaper, printed in his printing house in New Bern.[^cite_note-powell34-35-51][^cite_note-52]

Differences in the settlement patterns of eastern and western North Carolina, or the [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain) and uplands, affected the political, economic, and social life of the state from the 18th until the 20th century. Eastern North Carolina was settled chiefly by immigrants from rural England and Gaelic speakers from the [Scottish Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlands). The [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)) upcountry and western mountain region of North Carolina was settled chiefly by [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_people), English, and German Protestants, the so-called "[cohee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohee)". Arriving during the mid-to-late 18th century, the Scots-Irish, people of Scottish descent who migrated to and then emigrated from what is today Northern Ireland, were the largest non-English immigrant group before the Revolution; English indentured servants were overwhelmingly the largest immigrant group before the Revolution.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-census.gov-54][^cite_note-ReferenceA-55][^cite_note-56]

### Revolutionary War



[Halifax Resolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Resolves) plaque inside the [North Carolina State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_Capitol)

During the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), the English and Gaelic speaking Highland Scots of eastern North Carolina tended to remain loyal to the British Crown, because of longstanding business and personal connections with Great Britain. The English, Welsh, Scots-Irish, and German settlers of western North Carolina tended to favor American independence from Britain. British loyalists dubbed the [Mecklenburg County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_County,_North_Carolina) area to be 'a hornet's nest' of radicals, birthing the name of the future Charlotte NBA team. On April 12, 1776, the colony became the first to instruct its delegates to the [Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress) to vote for independence from the British Crown, through the [Halifax Resolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Resolves) passed by the [North Carolina Provincial Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Provincial_Congress). The date of this event is memorialized on the [state flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_North_Carolina) and [state seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_North_Carolina). Throughout the Revolutionary War, fierce [guerrilla warfare](12720.md) erupted between bands of pro-independence and pro-British colonists. In some cases the war was also an excuse to settle private grudges and rivalries.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

North Carolina had around 7,800 [Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_\(American_Revolution\)) join the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) under General [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington); and an additional 10,000 served in local militia units under such leaders as General [Nathanael Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathanael_Greene).[^cite_note-59] There was some military action, especially in 1780–81. Many Carolinian frontiersmen had moved west over the mountains, into the [Washington District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_District,_North_Carolina) (later known as [Tennessee](30395.md)), but in 1789, following the Revolution, the state was persuaded to relinquish its claim to the western lands. It ceded them to the national government so the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) could be organized and managed nationally.[^cite_note-60]

[A major American victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kings_Mountain) in the war took place at [King's Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Mountain) along the North Carolina–South Carolina border; on October 7, 1780, a force of 1,000 Patriots from western North Carolina (including modern-day [Tennessee](30395.md)) and [southwest Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Virginia) overwhelmed a force of some 1,000 British troops led by Major [Patrick Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Ferguson). Most of the soldiers fighting for the British side in this battle were Carolinians who had remained loyal to the Crown (they were called "Tories" or Loyalists). The American victory at King's Mountain gave the advantage to colonists who favored American independence, and it prevented the British Army from recruiting new soldiers from the Tories.[^cite_note-61]



1st Maryland Regiment holding the line at the [Battle of Guilford Court House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guilford_Court_House), 1781

The road to [Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorktown,_Virginia) and America's independence from [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) led through North Carolina. As the [British Army](4887.md) moved north from victories in [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina) and [Camden, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden,_South_Carolina), the Southern Division of the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) and local militia prepared to meet them. Following General [Daniel Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Morgan)'s victory over the British Cavalry Commander [Banastre Tarleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banastre_Tarleton) at the [Battle of Cowpens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cowpens) on January 17, 1781, southern commander [Nathanael Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathanael_Greene) led British Lord [Charles Cornwallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cornwallis) across the heartland of North Carolina, and away from the latter's base of supply in Charleston, South Carolina. This campaign is known as "The Race to the Dan" or "The Race for the River".[^cite_note-autogenerated1-42]

In the [Battle of Cowan's Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cowan's_Ford), Cornwallis met resistance along the banks of the [Catawba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_River) at Cowan's Ford on February 1, 1781, in an attempt to engage General Morgan's forces during a tactical withdrawal.[^cite_note-62] Morgan had moved to the northern part of the state to combine with General Greene's newly recruited forces. Generals Greene and Cornwallis finally met at the [Battle of Guilford Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guilford_Courthouse) in present-day [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro) on March 15, 1781. Although the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) troops held the field at the end of the battle, their casualties at the hands of the numerically superior Continental Army were crippling. Following this "[Pyrrhic victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhic_victory)", Cornwallis chose to move to the Virginia coastline to get reinforcements, and to allow the [Royal Navy](26061.md) to protect his battered army. This decision would result in Cornwallis' eventual defeat at [Yorktown, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorktown,_Virginia), later in 1781. The Patriots' victory there guaranteed American independence. On November 21, 1789, North Carolina became the twelfth state to ratify the [U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Constitution).

### Antebellum period

Main articles: [Constitutional Convention (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(United_States\)), [Admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union), and [List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union)

&#x20;After 1800, cotton and tobacco became important export crops. The eastern half of the state, especially the Coastal Plain region, developed a slave society based on a [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South) system and [slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave) labor. Planters owning large estates wielded significant political and socio-economic power in antebellum North Carolina. They placed their interests above those of the generally non-slave-holding [yeoman farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeoman_farmer) of North Carolina. While slaveholding was slightly less concentrated compared to some other Southern states, according to the 1860 census, more than 330,000 people, or 33% of the population out of 992,622 people in total, were enslaved African Americans.[^cite_note-census-63] They lived and worked chiefly on plantations in the Piedmont and Coastal Plain regions of the state. In addition, 30,463 [free people of color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_people_of_color) lived in the state.[^cite_note-census-63] They were also mainly concentrated in the eastern coastal plain, especially at port cities such as Wilmington and New Bern, where a variety of jobs were available. Most were descendants from free African Americans who had migrated along with neighbors from [Virginia](32432.md) during the 18th century. The majority were the descendants of unions in the working classes between white women, indentured servants or free, and African men, indentured, slave or free.[^cite_note-64]&#x20;



Map of the roads and railroads of North Carolina, 1854

After the American Revolution, [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) and [Mennonites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mennonite) worked to persuade slaveholders to free their slaves. Some were inspired by their efforts and the language of the Revolution to arrange for [manumission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manumission) of their slaves. The number of free people of color rose markedly in the first couple of decades after the Revolution.[^cite_note-65] Many [free people of color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_people_of_color) migrated to the frontier, along with their European-American neighbors, where the social system was looser. By 1810, nearly three percent of the free population consisted of free people of color, who numbered slightly more than 10,000. The western areas of North Carolina were mainly white families of [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) descent, especially [Scotch-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch-Irish_Americans), who operated small subsistence farms. In the early national period, the state became a center of [Jeffersonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffersonian_democracy) and [Jacksonian democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonian_democracy), with a strong [Whig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_\(United_States\)) presence, especially in the western part of the state. After [Nat Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_Turner)'s slave uprising in 1831, North Carolina and other southern states reduced the rights of free blacks. In 1835, the legislature withdrew their right to vote.

In mid-century, the state's rural and commercial areas were connected by the construction of a 129 mi (208 km) wooden plank road, known as a "farmer's railroad", from [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina) in the east to [Bethania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethania,_North_Carolina) (northwest of [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem)).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-42] On October 25, 1836, construction began on the [Wilmington and Raleigh Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington_and_Raleigh_Railroad)[^cite_note-66] to connect the port city of [Wilmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina) with the state capital of [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh). In 1840, the [state capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_Capitol) building in Raleigh was completed, and still stands today.

In 1849, the North Carolina Railroad was created by act of the legislature to extend that railroad west to [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro), [High Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Point,_North_Carolina), and [Charlotte](57447.md). During the Civil War, the Wilmington-to-Raleigh stretch of the railroad was vital to the Confederate war effort; supplies shipped into Wilmington were moved by rail through Raleigh to the Confederate capital of [Richmond, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia).[^cite_note-67]

### American Civil War

Main articles: [Ordinance of Secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_of_Secession), [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America), and [North Carolina in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_in_the_American_Civil_War)

Further information: [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War)



Union troops capture [Fort Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Fisher), 1865

In 1860, North Carolina was a slave state, in which one-third of the state's total population were African-American slaves. The state did not vote to join the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) until President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) called on it to invade its sister state,[^cite_note-68] [South Carolina](27956.md), becoming the last or possibly penultimate state to officially join the Confederacy. The title of "last to join the Confederacy" has been disputed; although Tennessee's informal secession on May 7, 1861, preceded North Carolina's official secession on May 20,[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] the Tennessee legislature did not formally vote to secede until June 8, 1861.[^cite_note-71]

Around 125,000 troops from North Carolina served in the Confederate Army, and about 15,000 North Carolina troops (both black and white) served in [Union Army regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Carolina_Union_Civil_War_units), including those who left the state to join Union regiments elsewhere.[^cite_note-72] Over 30,000 North Carolina troops died from combat or disease during the war.[^cite_note-73] Elected in 1862, Governor [Zebulon Baird Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebulon_Baird_Vance) tried to maintain state autonomy against Confederate President [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis) in [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia). The state government was reluctant to support the demands of the national government in Richmond, and the state was the scene of only small battles. In 1865, Durham County saw the largest single surrender of Confederate soldiers at [Bennett Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Place), when [Joseph E. Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_E._Johnston) surrendered the Army of Tennessee and all remaining Confederate forces still active in North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia, and Florida, totalling 89,270 soldiers.[^cite_note-74]



[Bennett Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Place) historic site in [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina)

Confederate troops from all parts of North Carolina served in virtually all the major battles of the [Army of Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Northern_Virginia), the Confederacy's most famous army. The largest battle fought in North Carolina was at [Bentonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bentonville), which was a futile attempt by Confederate General [Joseph Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_E._Johnston) to slow Union General [William Tecumseh Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tecumseh_Sherman)'s advance through the Carolinas in the spring of 1865.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-42] In April 1865, after losing the [Battle of Morrisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Morrisville), Johnston surrendered to Sherman at [Bennett Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Place), in what is today [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina). North Carolina's port city of [Wilmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina), was the last Confederate port to fall to the Union, in February 1865, after the Union won the nearby [Second Battle of Fort Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Fort_Fisher), its major defense downriver.

The first Confederate soldier to be killed in the Civil War was Private [Henry Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lawson_Wyatt) from North Carolina, in the [Battle of Big Bethel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Big_Bethel) in June 1861. At the [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg) in July 1863, the 26th North Carolina Regiment participated in [Pickett/Pettigrew's Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickett's_Charge) and advanced the farthest into Union lines of any Confederate regiment. During the [Battle of Chickamauga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chickamauga), the 58th North Carolina Regiment advanced farther than any other regiment on Snodgrass Hill to push back the remaining Union forces from the battlefield. At [Appomattox Court House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appomattox_Court_House_National_Historical_Park) in Virginia in April 1865, the 75th North Carolina Regiment, a cavalry unit, fired the last shots of the Confederate [Army of Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Northern_Virginia) in the Civil War. The phrase "First at Bethel, Farthest at Gettysburg and Chickamauga, and Last at Appomattox", later became used through much of the early 20th century.[^cite_note-75]

After secession, some North Carolinians refused to support the Confederacy. Some of the yeoman farmers chiefly in the state's mountains and western Piedmont region remained neutral during the Civil War, with others covertly supporting the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) cause during the conflict.[^cite_note-76] Approximately [15,000 North Carolinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Carolina_Union_Civil_War_units) (both black and white) from across the state enlisted in the [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army). Numerous slaves also escaped to Union lines, where they became essentially free.

### Reconstruction era through late 19th century

Main article: [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era)



[William Woods Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Woods_Holden), a Unionist who served as the 38th and 40th [Governor of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_North_Carolina), and during the [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era)

Following the collapse of the Confederacy in 1865, North Carolina, along with other former Confederate States (except Tennessee), was put under direct control by the [U.S. military](32212.md) and was relieved of its [constitutional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_North_Carolina) and representation within the [United States Congress](31756.md) in what is now referred to as the [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era). To earn back its rights, the state had to make concessions to Washington, one of which was ratifying the [Thirteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment). Congressional Republicans during Reconstruction, commonly referred to as "[radical Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Republicans)", constantly pushed for new constitutions for each of the Southern states that emphasized equal rights for African-Americans. In 1868, a constitutional convention restored the state government of North Carolina. Though the [Fifteenth Amendment](31667.md) was also adopted that same year, it remained [in most cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws) ineffective for almost a century, not to mention paramilitary groups and their [lynching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching_in_the_United_States) with impunity.[^cite_note-77]

The elections in April 1868 following the constitutional convention led to a narrow victory for a Republican-dominated government, with 19 African-Americans holding positions in the [North Carolina State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_Legislature). In attempt to put the reforms into effect, the new Republican Governor [William W. Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_W._Holden) declared martial law on any county allegedly not complying with law and order using the passage of the [Shoffner Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoffner_Act).

A [Republican Party](32070.md) coalition of black freedmen, northern [carpetbaggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpetbagger) and local [scalawags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalawag) controlled state government for three years. The white conservative Democrats regained control of the state legislature in 1870, in part by [Ku Klux Klan](16779.md) violence and terrorism at the polls, to suppress black voting. Republicans were elected to the governorship until 1876, when the [Red Shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Shirts_\(Southern_United_States\)), a paramilitary organization that arose in 1874 and was allied with the [Democratic Party](5043544.md), helped suppress black voting. More than 150 black Americans were murdered in electoral violence in 1876.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

&#x20;Post–Civil War-debt cycles pushed people to switch from subsistence agriculture to commodity agriculture. Among this time the notorious Crop-Lien system developed and was financially difficult on landless whites and blacks, due to high amounts of usury. Also due to the push for commodity agriculture, the free range was ended. Prior to this time people fenced in their crops and had their livestock feeding on the free range areas. After the ending of the free range people now fenced their animals and had their crops in the open.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]&#x20;



Segregated drinking fountain during the [Jim Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow) era in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_North_Carolina), 1938

Democrats were elected to the legislature and governor's office, but the [Populists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(United_States\)) attracted voters displeased with them. In 1896 a biracial, Populist-Republican Fusionist coalition gained the governor's office and passed laws that would extend the voting franchise to blacks and poor whites. The Democrats regained control of the legislature in 1896 and passed laws to impose [Jim Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow) and [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) of public facilities. Voters of North Carolina's [2nd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_2nd_congressional_district) elected a total of four African-American [congressmen](31756.md) through these years of the late 19th century.

Political tensions ran so high a small group of white Democrats in 1898 planned to take over the [Wilmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina) government if their candidates were not elected. In the [Wilmington massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington_massacre), white Democrats led around 2,000 of their supporters that attacked the black newspaper and neighborhood, killed an estimated 60 to 300 people, and ran off the white Republican mayor and aldermen. They installed their own people and elected [Alfred M. Waddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_M._Waddell) as mayor, in the only successful coup d'état in [United States history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coups_and_coup_attempts_by_country#United_States).[^cite_note-82]

In 1899, the state legislature passed a new constitution, with requirements for [poll taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax) and [literacy tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_test) for voter registration which [disenfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disenfranchisement_after_the_Reconstruction_Era) most black Americans in the state.[^cite_note-83] Exclusion from voting had wide effects: it meant black Americans could not serve on juries or in any local office. After a decade of [white supremacy](33178.md), many people forgot North Carolina had ever had thriving middle-class black Americans.[^cite_note-84] Black citizens had no political voice in the state until after the federal [Civil Rights Act of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964) and [Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965) were passed to enforce their constitutional rights. It was not until 1992 that another African American was elected as a U.S. Representative from North Carolina.

### Early through mid-20th century



First successful flight of the *[Wright Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer)*, near [Kitty Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Hawk,_North_Carolina), 1903

After the reconstruction era, North Carolina had become a one-party state, dominated by the [Democratic Party](5043544.md). The state mainly continued with an economy based on tobacco, cotton textiles and commodity agriculture. Large towns and cities remained in few numbers. However, a major industrial base emerged in the late 19th and early 20th century, in the counties of the [Piedmont Triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Triad), based on cotton mills established at the [fall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Seaboard_fall_line). Railroads were built to connect the new industrializing cities.[^cite_note-85]

The state was the site of [the first successful controlled, powered and sustained heavier-than-air flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer), by the [Wright brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers), near [Kitty Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Hawk,_North_Carolina) on December 17, 1903.



Map of [Research Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle), with points representing [NC State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NC_State_University), [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), and [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill)

In the first half of the 20th century, many African Americans left the state to go North for better opportunities, in the [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_\(African_American\)). Their departure changed the demographic characteristics of many areas.

North Carolina was hard hit by the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States), but the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) programs of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) for cotton and tobacco significantly helped the farmers. After [World War II](32927.md), the state's economy grew rapidly, highlighted by the growth of such cities as Charlotte, Raleigh, and Durham in the Piedmont region.

[Research Triangle Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle_Park), established in 1959, serves as the largest [research park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park) in the United States. Formed near Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill, the [Research Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle) metro is a major area of universities and advanced scientific and technical research. By holding a 1956 meeting which helped lead to the creation of the park, Governor [Luther H. Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_H._Hodges) launched the transformation of his state from one of the poorest in the Union to one of the most prosperous.[^cite_note-Audretsch_Page_105-86] In contrast, South Carolina's leaders lacked such vision, explaining why that state was and still is among the poorest in the country.[^cite_note-Audretsch_Page_105-86]

The [Greensboro sit-ins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro_sit-ins) in 1960 played a crucial role in the Civil Rights Movement to bring full equality to American blacks. By the late 1960s, spurred in part by the increasingly leftward tilt of national Democrats, conservative whites began to vote for Republican national candidates and gradually for more Republicans locally.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

### Late 20th century to present



[North Carolina Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Museum_of_History) in Raleigh, 2008

Since the 1970s, North Carolina has seen steady increases in population growth. This growth has largely occurred in [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) located within the [Piedmont Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Crescent), in places such as Charlotte, Concord, Greensboro, Winston-Salem, Durham and Raleigh.[^cite_note-89] The Charlotte metropolitan area has experienced large growth mainly due to its finance, banking, and tech industries.[^cite_note-90]

By the 1990s, Charlotte had become a major regional and national banking center. Towards Raleigh, [North Carolina State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State), [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), and [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), have helped the [Research Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle) area attract an educated workforce and develop more jobs.[^cite_note-91]

In 1988, North Carolina gained its first professional sports franchise, the [Charlotte Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA). The hornets team name stems from the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), when British General Cornwallis described Charlotte as a "hornet's nest of rebellion".[^cite_note-92] The [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Panthers) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) became based in Charlotte as well, with their first season being in 1995. The [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) moved to [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh) in 1997, with their colors being the same as the [NC State Wolfpack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NC_State_Wolfpack), who are also located in Raleigh.

By the late 20th century and into the early 21st century, economic industries such as technology, [pharmaceuticals](560876.md), banking, [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [vehicle parts](163778.md), and tourism started to emerge as North Carolina's main economic drivers. This marked a shift from the state's former main industries of [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivation_of_tobacco), [textiles](2162735.md), and furniture. Factors that played a role in this shift were globalization, the state's higher education system, national banking, the transformation of agriculture, and new companies moving to the state.[^cite_note-93]

North Carolina is bordered by [South Carolina](27956.md) to the south, [Georgia](48830.md) to the southwest, [Tennessee](30395.md) to the west, [Virginia](32432.md) to the north, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) places North Carolina in the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_States) division of the [southern](179553.md) region.[^cite_note-Census_Regions-94] It has a total area of 53,819.16 square miles (139,391.0 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 48,617.91 square miles (125,919.8 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 5,201.25 square miles (13,471.2 km<sup>2</sup>) (9.66%) is water.[^cite_note-2010TotalArea-95]

North Carolina consists of three main geographic regions: the [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain), occupying the eastern portion of the state; the central [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)) region, and the mountain region in the west, which is part of the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains). The coastal plain consists of more specifically defined areas known as the [Outer Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Banks), a string of sandy, narrow [barrier islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island) separated from the mainland by sounds or inlets, including [Albemarle Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_Sound) and [Pamlico Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamlico_Sound), the native home of the [venus flytrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_flytrap), and the inner coastal plain, where [longleaf pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longleaf_pine) trees are native.

So many ships have been lost off [Cape Hatteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Hatteras) that the area is known as the "[Graveyard of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graveyard_of_the_Atlantic)"; more than a thousand ships have sunk in these waters since records began in 1526. The most famous of these is the *[Queen Anne's Revenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne's_Revenge)* (flagship of the pirate [Blackbeard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbeard)), which went aground in Beaufort Inlet in 1718.[^cite_note-96]

The coastal plain transitions to the Piedmont region along the [Atlantic Seaboard fall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Seaboard_fall_line), the elevation at which waterfalls first appear on streams and rivers. The Piedmont region of central North Carolina is the state's most populous region, containing the six largest cities in the state by population.[^cite_note-97] It consists of gently rolling countryside frequently broken by hills or low mountain ridges. Small, isolated, and deeply eroded mountain ranges and peaks are located in the Piedmont, including the [Sauratown Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauratown_Mountains), [Pilot Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_Mountain_\(North_Carolina\)), the [Uwharrie Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uwharrie_Mountains), [Crowder's Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowder's_Mountain), [King's Pinnacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Pinnacle), the [Brushy Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brushy_Mountains_\(North_Carolina\)), and the [South Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Mountains_\(North_Carolina\)). The Piedmont ranges from about 300 feet (100 m) in elevation in the east to about 1,500 feet (500 m) in the west.

The [western section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_Carolina) of the state is part of the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains) of the larger [Appalachian Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountain) range. Among the subranges of the Blue Ridge Mountains located in the state are the [Great Smoky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains) and the [Black Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mountains_\(North_Carolina\)).[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-visitnc-99] The Black Mountains are the highest in the eastern United States, and culminate in [Mount Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mitchell) at 6,684 feet (2,037 m), the highest point east of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River).[^cite_note-visitnc-99][^cite_note-usgs-100]



[Cullasaja Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cullasaja_Falls) in [Macon County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macon_County,_North_Carolina)

North Carolina has 17 major river basins. The five basins west of the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains) flow to the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), while the remainder flow to the Atlantic Ocean.[^cite_note-Rivers-101] Of the 17 basins, 11 originate within the state of North Carolina, but only four are contained entirely within the state's border—the [Cape Fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_River), the [Neuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuse_River), the [White Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Oak_River), and the [Tar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tar_River)–[Pamlico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamlico_Sound) basin.[^cite_note-102]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_North_Carolina)

See also: [Climate change in North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_North_Carolina)



[Graveyard Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graveyard_Fields) in the fall

Elevation above sea level is most responsible for temperature change across the state, with the mountainous regions being coolest year-round. The climate is also influenced by the Atlantic Ocean and the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream), especially in the coastal plain. These influences tend to cause warmer winter temperatures along the coast, where temperatures only occasionally drop below the freezing point at night. The coastal plain averages around 1 inch (2.5 cm) of snow or ice annually, and in many years, there may be no snow or ice at all.[^cite_note-NC_Climate-103]

The Atlantic Ocean exerts less influence on the climate of the Piedmont region, which has hotter summers and colder winters than along the coast, though winters are still mild.[^cite_note-NC_Climate-103]

North Carolina experiences severe weather both in summer and in winter, with summer bringing threat of [hurricanes](8282374.md), [tropical storms](8282374.md#tropical_storm), heavy rain, and flooding.[^cite_note-104] Destructive hurricanes that have hit North Carolina include [Hurricane Fran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Fran), [Hurricane Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Florence), [Hurricane Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Floyd), [Hurricane Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hugo), and [Hurricane Hazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hazel), the latter being the strongest storm ever to make landfall in the state, as a [Category 4](255313.md) in 1954. [Hurricane Helene](77945659.md) ranks as the most destructive of the 21st century.[^cite_note-HURDAT-105][^cite_note-106]

North Carolina averages fewer than 20 tornadoes per year, many of them produced by hurricanes or tropical storms along the coastal plain. Tornadoes from thunderstorms are a risk, especially in the eastern part of the state. The western Piedmont is often protected by the mountains, which tend to break up storms as they cross over; the storms will often re-form farther east. A phenomenon known as "[cold-air damming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold-air_damming)" often occurs in the northwestern part of the state, which can weaken storms but can also lead to major ice events in winter.[^cite_note-107]

In April 2011, [the worst tornado outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_outbreak_of_April_14%E2%80%9316,_2011) in North Carolina's history occurred. Thirty confirmed tornadoes touched down, mainly in the Eastern Piedmont and Sandhills, killing at least 24 people.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] In September 2019 [Hurricane Dorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian) hit the area.

### Parks and recreation



The [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains) of the [Shining Rock Wilderness Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shining_Rock_Wilderness)

North Carolina provides a large range of recreational activities, from swimming at the beach to [skiing](28478.md) in the mountains. North Carolina offers [fall colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_colors), freshwater and saltwater fishing, hunting, [birdwatching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdwatching), [agritourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agritourism), [ATV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-terrain_vehicle) trails, [ballooning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_ballooning), [rock climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_climbing), [biking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biking), hiking, skiing, [boating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boating) and sailing, [camping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camping), [canoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoeing), [caving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caving) (spelunking), gardens, and [arboretums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum). North Carolina has [theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park), [aquariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_aquarium), museums, [historic sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_site), [lighthouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse), elegant theaters, [concert halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_hall), and [fine dining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_dining).[^cite_note-NC_Beach-117][^cite_note-NC_Visit-118]

North Carolinians enjoy outdoor recreation using numerous local bike paths, 34 [state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Carolina_state_parks), and 14 national parks. [National Park Service](161535.md) units include the [Appalachian National Scenic Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_National_Scenic_Trail), the [Blue Ridge Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Parkway), [Cape Hatteras National Seashore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Hatteras_National_Seashore), [Cape Lookout National Seashore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Lookout_National_Seashore), [Carl Sandburg Home National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sandburg_Home_National_Historic_Site) at [Flat Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_Rock,_Henderson_County,_North_Carolina), [Fort Raleigh National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Raleigh_National_Historic_Site) at [Manteo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteo,_North_Carolina), [Great Smoky Mountains National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains_National_Park), [Great Smoky Mountains Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains_Railroad), [Guilford Courthouse National Military Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilford_Courthouse_National_Military_Park) in [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro), [Moores Creek National Battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moores_Creek_National_Battlefield) near [Currie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currie,_North_Carolina) in [Pender County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pender_County), the [Overmountain Victory National Historic Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overmountain_Victory_National_Historic_Trail), [Old Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Salem) National Historic Site in [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem), the [Trail of Tears National Historic Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_of_Tears_National_Historic_Trail), and [Wright Brothers National Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Brothers_National_Memorial) in [Kill Devil Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Devil_Hills).

National forests include [Uwharrie National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uwharrie_National_Forest) in central North Carolina, [Croatan National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatan_National_Forest) in [Eastern North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_North_Carolina), [Pisgah National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisgah_National_Forest) in the western mountains, and [Nantahala National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantahala_National_Forest) in the southwestern part of the state.

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) determined the population of North Carolina was 10,439,388 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] Based on numbers in 2012 of the people residing in North Carolina 58.5% were born there; 33.1% were born in another state; 1.0% were born in Puerto Rico, U.S. island areas, or born abroad to American parent(s); and 7.4% were foreign-born.[^cite_note-128] Between 2021 and 2024, the state's population increased from 10,551,162 to 11,046,024.[^cite_note-\:4-129] North Carolina recorded the fourth-largest population gain nationally between 2023 and 2024, and it remains the ninth-most populous state in the country.[^cite_note-\:5-130]

State population estimates indicate notable age-structure shifts between 2021 and 2024. The population of young children increased modestly, with 604,897 residents aged 0–4 in 2024 compared with 582,801 in 2021.[^cite_note-\:4-129] School-aged cohorts also grew, including 648,697 individuals aged 5–9 and 665,306 aged 10–14.[^cite_note-\:4-129] Adolescents and young adults remained a sizable demographic, with 730,298 residents aged 15–19 and 750,306 residents aged 20–24 recorded in 2024.[^cite_note-\:4-129] Working-age adults constitute the state's largest population groups. In 2024, the 30-34 age group contained 764,029 residents, followed by 735,094 aged 35–39.[^cite_note-\:4-129] Older adults represent a growing proportion of the state. Individuals aged 70–74 rose from 516,446 to 532,540 in 2024, while the 75-79 population increased substantially from 326,449 to 394,209 across the same period.[^cite_note-\:4-129] The number of residents aged 85 and older also grew, reaching 181,248 in 2024.[^cite_note-\:4-129]

Statistics show that annual births have remained stable, but slightly declining over the past decade. Births decreased from 122,302 in 2010 to 118,292 in 2023.[^cite_note-\:6-131] Deaths increased from 78,604 in 2010 to 118,040 in 2021 during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) before declining to 104,855 in 2023.[^cite_note-\:6-131] Since 2022, annual births have averaged around 121,000, while deaths have remained near 109,000, resulting in a modest natural increase.[^cite_note-\:6-131] This natural increase added approximately 12,000 residents between July 2023 and July 2024, accounting for 8% of total population growth during that period.

Migration is the primary factor driving recent population change. Between April 2020 and July 2024, North Carolina gained an estimated 573,000 residents through net migration, representing 95% of total population growth of that period.[^cite_note-\:5-130] Domestic migration continues to account for the majority of this increase.[^cite_note-\:5-130] Between July 2023 and July 2024, the state gained 82,000 residents through net state-to-state migration, the second-highest figure in the nation after Texas.[^cite_note-\:5-130] International migration, which declined sharply during the height of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), has rebounded. While it represented less than 10% of net migration in 2020–2021, it accounted for 46% of net migration in 2023–2024, with 70,000 net international arrivals during the year.[^cite_note-\:5-130]

Population growth is heavily concentrated in the state's largest metropolitan counties. In 2020, Wake County reported 1,135,576 residents, and Mecklenburg County reported 1,118,967, making them the two most populous counties in North Carolina.[^cite_note-132]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 9,382 [homeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeless) people in North Carolina.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

The top countries of origin for North Carolina's immigrants were [Mexico](3966054.md), [India](14533.md), [Honduras](13394.md), [China](5405.md) and [El Salvador](9356.md), as of 2018.[^cite_note-135]

In 2020, North Carolina like much of the U.S. experienced a decline in its non-Hispanic white population; at the 2020 census, non-Hispanic whites were 62.2%, Blacks or African Americans 20.5%, American Indian and Alaska Natives 1.2%, Asians 3.3%, Native Hawaiian and other Pacific Islanders 0.1%, people from other race 5.9%, and multiracial Americans 6.8%.[^cite_note-\:0-147]

The majority of the Black American population is concentrated in the urban areas and northeastern part of the state.[^cite_note-148]

North Carolina's Hispanic population has grown rapidly. The Hispanic population more than doubled in size between 1990 and 2000. Many of North Carolina's Hispanic residents are of [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) descent. Many of North Carolina's newer Latino residents came from Mexico largely to work in agriculture, manufacturing, or on one of North Carolina's military installations.[^cite_note-149]

The most common ancestries in North Carolina are African-American, American, German, English, and Irish.[^cite_note-150]

North Carolina has the eighth-largest Native American population in the country.[^cite_note-151] The state is home to eight Native American tribes and four urban Native American organizations.[^cite_note-152]

North Carolina residents since the colonial era have historically been overwhelmingly [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant)—first [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican), then [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) and [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist). In 2010, the Southern Baptist Convention was the single largest Christian denomination, with 4,241 churches and 1,513,000 members. The second largest in 2024 was the [Catholic Church](606848.md) which is organised into two dioceses. In the west is the [Diocese of Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Charlotte) which includes Charlotte and has 530,000 members in 196 [parishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_\(Catholic_Church\)). In the east is the [Diocese of Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Raleigh) with a total of 80 parishes and nearly 500,000 Catholics.[^cite_note-157] The third largest was the [United Methodist Church](32304.md), with 660,000 members and 1,923 churches. The fourth largest was the [Presbyterian Church (USA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(USA\)), with 186,000 members and 710 congregations; this denomination was brought by Scots-Irish immigrants who settled the backcountry in the colonial era.[^cite_note-158] In 2020, the Southern Baptists remained the largest with 1,324,747 adherents, though Methodists and others were collectively overtaken by non/interdenominational Protestants numbering 1,053,564.[^cite_note-159]

In 1845, the Baptists split into regional associations of the Northern United States and Southern U.S., over the issue of slavery. These new associations were the Northern Baptist Convention (today the [American Baptist Churches USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Baptist_Churches_USA)) and [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention). By the late 19th century, the largest Protestant denomination in North Carolina were [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists). After [emancipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation), black Baptists quickly set up their own independent congregations in North Carolina and other states of the South, as they wanted to be free of white supervision.[^cite_note-brooks-160][^cite_note-raboteau-161][^cite_note-162] Black Baptists developed their own state and national associations, such as the [National Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baptist_Convention_USA,_Inc.).[^cite_note-raboteau-161] Other primarily African American Baptist conventions which grew in the state since the 20th century were the [Progressive National Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_National_Baptist_Convention) and [Full Gospel Baptist Church Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Gospel_Baptist_Church_Fellowship).

[Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodists) (the second-largest group among North Carolinian Protestants) were divided along racial lines in the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) and [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church). The Methodist tradition tends to be strong in the northern Piedmont, especially in populous [Guilford County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilford_County). Other prominent Protestant groups in North Carolina as of the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center)'s 2014 study were [non/interdenominational Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) and [Pentecostalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism). The [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God_USA) and [Church of God in Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_God_in_Christ) are the largest Pentecostal denominations operating in the state, while notable minorities include [Oneness Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneness_Pentecostals) primarily affiliated with the [United Pentecostal Church International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Pentecostal_Church_International).

The state also has a special history with the [Moravian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church), as settlers of this faith (largely of German origin) settled in the [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem) area in the 18th and 19th centuries. [Historically Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterians) have had a strong presence in [Charlotte](57447.md) and in [Scotland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_County,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-163]

&#x20;A wide variety of non-Christian faiths are practiced by other residents in the state, including: [Judaism](15624.md), [Islam](6037917.md), [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD), [Buddhism](3267529.md), and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). ## Economy

Main article: [Economy of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_North_Carolina)

According to the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, North Carolina's [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) was \$893.762 billion and its per capita personal income was \$68,511 in 2025.[^cite_note-165] Based on American Community Survey 2010–2014 data, North Carolina's median household income was \$46,693. It ranked forty-first out of fifty states plus the District of Columbia for median household income. North Carolina had the fourteenth highest poverty rate in the nation at 17.6%, with 13% of families that were below the poverty line.[^cite_note-166] As of May 2025, the unemployment rate was 3.7%.[^cite_note-167]



Charlotte is home to the headquarters of [Bank of America](347756.md) (foreground) and [Truist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truist) (background), two of the [largest banks in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_banks_in_the_United_States). Bank of America is the largest company headquartered in North Carolina.

[^cite_note-168]

The state has a very diverse economy because of its great availability of hydroelectric power,[^cite_note-169] its pleasant climate, and its wide variety of soils. The state ranks third among the South Atlantic states in population, but leads the region in industry and agriculture.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] North Carolina leads the nation in the production of tobacco.[^cite_note-172]

Charlotte, the state's largest city, is a major textile and trade center. According to a Forbes article written in 2013, employment in the "Old North State" has gained many different industry sectors. Science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM) industries in the area surrounding North Carolina's capital have grown 17.9 percent since 2001. Raleigh ranked the third best city for technology in 2020 due to the state's growing technology sector.[^cite_note-173] In 2010, North Carolina's total gross state product was \$424.9 billion,[^cite_note-174] while the state debt in November 2012, according to one source, totaled \$2.4 billion,[^cite_note-175] while according to another in 2012 it was \$57.8 billion.[^cite_note-176]

In 2025, 99.6% of North Carolina businesses were [small businesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_businesses) and employed 1.8 million people, or 44.2% of the state's work force.[^cite_note-177]

North Carolina is the leading U.S. state in production of [flue-cured tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flue-cured_tobacco) and [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato), and comes second in the farming of pigs and hogs, [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout), and turkeys.[^cite_note-ncgov-178][^cite_note-179] In the three most recent [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA) surveys (2002, 2007, 2012), North Carolina also ranked second in the production of [Christmas trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree).[^cite_note-ncgov-178][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

North Carolina has 15 metropolitan areas,[^cite_note-2023OMB-123] and was chosen as the third-best state by Forbes Magazine in 2010 and the second-best state by Chief Executive Magazine in 2011 for business.[^cite_note-182] Since 2000, there has been a clear division in the economic growth of North Carolina's urban and rural areas. While North Carolina's urban areas have enjoyed a prosperous economy with steady job growth, low unemployment, and rising wages, many of the state's rural counties have suffered from job loss, rising levels of poverty, and population loss as their manufacturing base has declined. According to one estimate, one-half of North Carolina's 100 counties have lost population since 2010, primarily due to the poor economy in many of North Carolina's rural areas. However, the population of the state's urban areas is steadily increasing.[^cite_note-Urban_-183] In 2025, *[CNBC](223441.md)* ranked North Carolina as America's number one state for business based on factors such as workforce, business friendliness, and economy, among others.[^cite_note-184]

[Metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) and [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) acts such as [Corrosion of Conformity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrosion_of_Conformity), [Between the Buried and Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Between_the_Buried_and_Me), and Nightmare Sonata are native to North Carolina.

North Carolina is the home of more *[American Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Idol)* finalists than any other state: [Clay Aiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Aiken) (season two), [Fantasia Barrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasia_Barrino) (season three), [Chris Daughtry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Daughtry) (season five), [Kellie Pickler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellie_Pickler) (season five), [Bucky Covington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucky_Covington) (season five), [Anoop Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoop_Desai) (season eight), [Scotty McCreery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotty_McCreery) (season ten), and [Caleb Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Johnson) (season thirteen). North Carolina also has the most *American Idol* winners with Barrino, McCreery, and Johnson.

In the mountains, the [Brevard Music Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brevard_Music_Center) hosts choral, operatic, orchestral, and solo performances during its annual summer schedule.

North Carolina has five professional opera companies: [Opera Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Carolina) in Charlotte, NC Opera in Raleigh, Greensboro Opera in Greensboro, Piedmont Opera in Winston-Salem, and [Asheville Lyric Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville_Lyric_Opera) in Asheville. Academic conservatories and universities also produce fully staged operas, such as the A. J. Fletcher Opera Institute of the [University of North Carolina School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_School_of_the_Arts) in Winston-Salem, the Department of Music of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and UNC Greensboro.

Among others, there are three high-level symphonic orchestras: [NC Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NC_Symphony) in Raleigh, [Charlotte Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Symphony), and Winston-Salem Symphony. The NC Symphony holds the North Carolina Master Chorale. The [Carolina Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Ballet) is headquartered in Raleigh, and there is also the [Charlotte Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Ballet).

The state boasts three performing arts centers: the [Durham Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Performing_Arts_Center) in Durham, the Martin Marietta Center for the Performing Arts in Raleigh, and the [Blumenthal Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumenthal_Arts) Center in Charlotte. They feature concerts, operas, recitals, and traveling Broadway musicals.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195]

### Cuisine and agriculture



2008 [Lexington Barbecue Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_Barbecue_Festival)

A culinary staple of North Carolina is pork [barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbecue). There are strong regional differences and rivalries over the sauces and methods used in making the barbecue. The common trend across Western North Carolina is the use of premium grade [Boston butt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_butt). Western North Carolina pork barbecue uses a tomato-based sauce, and only the pork shoulder (dark meat) is used. Western North Carolina barbecue is commonly referred to as Lexington barbecue after the [Piedmont Triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Triad) town of [Lexington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_North_Carolina), home of the [Lexington Barbecue Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_Barbecue_Festival), which attracts more than 100,000 visitors each October.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] Eastern North Carolina pork barbecue uses a vinegar-and-red-pepper-based sauce and the "[whole hog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_hog_barbecue)" is cooked, thus integrating both white and dark meat.[^cite_note-198]

[Krispy Kreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krispy_Kreme), an international chain of doughnut stores, was started in North Carolina; the company's headquarters are in [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem). [Pepsi-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi-Cola) was first produced in 1898 in New Bern. A regional soft drink, [Cheerwine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheerwine), was created and is still based in the city of Salisbury. Despite its name, the hot sauce [Texas Pete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Pete) was created in North Carolina; its headquarters are also in Winston-Salem. The [Hardee's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardee's) fast-food chain was started in [Rocky Mount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mount,_North_Carolina). Another fast-food chain, [Bojangles'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojangles'), was started in [Charlotte](57447.md), and has its corporate headquarters there. A popular North Carolina restaurant chain is [Golden Corral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Corral). Started in 1973, the chain was founded in [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina), with headquarters located in [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh). Popular [pickle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickled_cucumber) brand [Mount Olive Pickle Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olive_Pickle_Company) was founded in [Mount Olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olive,_North_Carolina) in 1926. Fast casual burger chain [Hwy 55 Burgers, Shakes & Fries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwy_55_Burgers,_Shakes_&_Fries) also makes its home in Mount Olive. [Cook Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Out_\(restaurant\)), a popular fast-food chain featuring burgers, hot dogs, and milkshakes in a wide variety of flavors, was founded in Greensboro in 1989 and has begun expanding outside North Carolina. In 2013 *Southern Living* named Durham–Chapel Hill the South's "Tastiest City".

Over the last decade, North Carolina has become a cultural epicenter and haven for internationally prize-winning wine (Noni Bacca Winery), internationally prized cheeses (Ashe County), "L'institut International aux Arts Gastronomiques: Conquerront Les Yanks les Truffes, January 15, 2010" international hub for truffles (Garland Truffles), and beer making, as tobacco land has been converted to grape orchards while state laws regulating [alcohol by volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_by_volume) (ABV) in beer allowed a jump from six to fifteen percent. The Yadkin Valley in particular has become a strengthening market for grape production, while [Asheville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville) recently won the recognition of being named "Beer City USA". Asheville boasts the largest number of [breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing_in_North_Carolina#Breweries) per capita of any city in the United States. Recognized and marketed brands of beer in North Carolina include Highland Brewing, Duck Rabbit Brewery, Mother Earth Brewery, Weeping Radish Brewery, Big Boss Brewing, Foothills Brewing, Carolina Brewing Company, Lonerider Brewing, and White Rabbit Brewing Company.

North Carolina has large grazing areas for beef and dairy cattle. Truck farms can be found in North Carolina. A truck farm is a small farm where fruits and vegetables are grown to be sold at local markets. The state's shipping, commercial fishing, and lumber industries are important to its economy. Service industries, including education, health care, private research, and retail trade, are also important. [Research Triangle Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle_Park), a large industrial complex located in the Raleigh-Durham area, is one of the major centers in the country for electronics and medical research.[^cite_note-199]

Tobacco was one of the first major industries to develop after the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). Many farmers grew some tobacco, and the invention of the cigarette made the product especially popular. Winston-Salem is the birthplace of [R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._J._Reynolds_Tobacco_Company) (RJR), founded by [R. J. Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._J._Reynolds) in 1874 as one of sixteen tobacco companies in the town. By 1914 it was selling 425 million packs of Camels a year. Today it is the second-largest tobacco company in the U.S. (behind [Altria Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altria_Group)). RJR is an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Reynolds American Inc., which in turn is 42% owned by [British American Tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_American_Tobacco).[^cite_note-200]

USS *North Carolina* on permanent display in Wilmington

Several ships have been named after the state, most famously [USS *North Carolina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_North_Carolina_\(BB-55\)) in the [Pacific Ocean theater of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean_theater_of_World_War_II). ### Armed forces installations



Troopers of the 82nd Airborne Division training at [Fort Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bragg), March 2011

[Fort Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bragg), near [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina) and [Southern Pines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pines), is a large and comprehensive military base and is the headquarters of the [XVIII Airborne Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVIII_Airborne_Corps), [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division), and the [U.S. Army Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Special_Operations_Command). Serving as the air wing for Fort Bragg is [Pope Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Field), also located near Fayetteville.

Located in [Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_North_Carolina), [Marine Corps Base Camp Lejeune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Camp_Lejeune), combined with nearby bases [Marine Corps Air Station (MCAS) Cherry Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCAS_Cherry_Point), [MCAS New River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCAS_New_River), [Camp Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Geiger), [Camp Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Gilbert_H._Johnson), [Stone Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Bay) and Courthouse Bay, makes up the largest concentration of Marines and sailors in the world. MCAS Cherry Point is home of the [2nd Marine Aircraft Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Marine_Aircraft_Wing). Located in [Goldsboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsboro,_North_Carolina), [Seymour Johnson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Johnson_Air_Force_Base) is home of the [4th Fighter Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Fighter_Wing) and [916th Air Refueling Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/916th_Air_Refueling_Wing). One of the busiest air stations in the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) is located at the [Coast Guard Air Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Air_Station_Elizabeth_City) in [Elizabeth City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_City). Also stationed in North Carolina is the [Military Ocean Terminal Sunny Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Ocean_Terminal_Sunny_Point) in [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_North_Carolina).

On January 24, 1961, a B-52G [broke up in midair and crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Goldsboro_B-52_crash) after suffering a severe fuel loss, near [Goldsboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsboro,_North_Carolina), dropping two [nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_bombs) in the process without detonation.[^cite_note-203] In 2013, it was revealed that three safety mechanisms on one bomb had failed, leaving just one low-voltage switch preventing detonation.[^cite_note-204]

[Biltmore Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biltmore_Estate), [Asheville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville)



[Cape Hatteras Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Hatteras_Lighthouse), located in North Carolina's [Outer Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Banks)

Gantt Center for African-American Arts + Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_B._Gantt_Center_for_African-American_Arts_+_Culture), [Levine Museum of the New South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levine_Museum_of_the_New_South), [McColl Center for Art + Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McColl_Center_for_Art_+_Innovation), [Mint Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_Museum), and the [NASCAR Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Hall_of_Fame).

Every year the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) attract several million tourists to the western part of the state,[^cite_note-207] including the historic [Biltmore Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biltmore_Estate). The scenic [Blue Ridge Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Parkway) and [Great Smoky Mountains National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains_National_Park) are the two most visited national park and unit in the United States with more than 25 million visitors in 2013.[^cite_note-208] The City of [Asheville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville) is consistently voted as one of the top places to visit and live in the United States, known for its rich art deco architecture, mountain scenery and outdoor activities.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

In [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh), many tourists visit the capital, African American Cultural Complex,[^cite_note-211] [Contemporary Art Museum of Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_Art_Museum_of_Raleigh), [Gregg Museum of Art & Design at NCSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Museum_of_Art_&_Design), [Haywood Hall House & Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haywood_Hall), [Marbles Kids Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marbles_Kids_Museum), [North Carolina Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Museum_of_Art), [North Carolina Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Museum_of_History), [North Carolina Museum of Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Museum_of_Natural_Sciences), [North Carolina Sports Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Sports_Hall_of_Fame), [Raleigh City Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh_City_Museum), [J. The [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) NHL hockey team is also located in the city.

In the [Conover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conover,_North_Carolina)–[Hickory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickory,_North_Carolina) area, attractions include [Hickory Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickory_Motor_Speedway), RockBarn Golf and Spa,[^cite_note-212] home of the [Greater Hickory Classic at Rock Barn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Hickory_Classic_at_Rock_Barn); [Catawba County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_County) Firefighters Museum,[^cite_note-213] the SALT Block,[^cite_note-214] and [Valley Hills Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Hills_Mall).

The [Piedmont Triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Triad), or center of the state, is home to [Krispy Kreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krispy_Kreme), [Mayberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayberry), [Texas Pete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Pete), the [Lexington Barbecue Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_Barbecue_Festival), and [Moravian cookies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_spice_cookies). The internationally acclaimed [North Carolina Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Zoo) in [Asheboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheboro) attracts visitors to its animals, plants, and a 57-piece art collection along five miles (8 km) of shaded pathways in the world's largest-land-area natural-habitat park. [Seagrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrove), in the central portion of the state, attracts many tourists along [Pottery Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery_Highway) (NC Hwy 705). [MerleFest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MerleFest) in [Wilkesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkesboro,_North_Carolina) attracts more than 80,000 people to its four-day music festival. North Carolina is home to multiple water parks, such as [Wet 'n Wild Emerald Pointe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_'n_Wild_Emerald_Pointe) in [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro) and [Carolina Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Harbor) in Charlotte.[^cite_note-215]

The [Outer Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Banks) and surrounding beaches attract millions of people to the Atlantic beaches every year.[^cite_note-216]

The mainland northeastern part of the state, having recently adopted the name the [Inner Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Banks), is also known as the Albemarle Region, for the [Albemarle Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_Settlements), some of the first settlements on North Carolina's portion of the [Atlantic Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coastal_Plain). The region's historic sites are connected by the [Historic Albemarle Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Albemarle_Tour).

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_North_Carolina)

See also: [Vehicle registration plates of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_North_Carolina) and [List of North Carolina railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Carolina_railroads)



Most common license plate design in the state; includes the *[Wright Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer)* in the background



[LYNX](2173439.md) light rail car in Charlotte



State-owned *[Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(train\))* train in High Point

Transportation systems in North Carolina consist of air, water, road, rail, and public transportation including intercity rail via Amtrak and light rail in Charlotte. North Carolina has the second-largest state highway system in the country as well as the largest ferry system on the [East Coast](89126.md).[^cite_note-217]

North Carolina's airports serve destinations throughout the United States and international destinations in Canada, Europe, Central America, and the Caribbean. In July 2022, [Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport), which serves as the second busiest hub for [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines), ranked as the 11th busiest airport in the United States with [Raleigh-Durham International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh-Durham_International_Airport), a focus-city for [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) and formerly a hub for American Airlines and [Midway Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_Airlines_\(1993-2003\)), ranked as the 37th busiest airport in the United States.[^cite_note-218]

North Carolina has a growing passenger rail system with [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) serving most major cities. Charlotte is also home to North Carolina's only light rail system known as [LYNX](2173439.md).[^cite_note-219]

## Education

Main article: [Education in North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_North_Carolina)

### Primary and secondary education

See also: [List of school districts in North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_North_Carolina) and [List of high schools in North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_North_Carolina)

Elementary and secondary public schools are overseen by the [North Carolina Department of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Department_of_Public_Instruction). The [North Carolina Superintendent of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Superintendent_of_Public_Instruction) is the secretary of the [North Carolina State Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_Board_of_Education), but the board, rather than the superintendent, holds most of the legal authority for making public education policy. In 2009, the board's chairman also became the "chief executive officer" for the state's school system.[^cite_note-220] North Carolina has 115 public school systems, each of which is overseen by a local school board.[^cite_note-SchoolQF-221][^cite_note-Dpi-222] A county may have one or more systems within it. The largest school systems in North Carolina are the [Wake County Public School System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_County_Public_School_System), [Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte-Mecklenburg_Schools), [Guilford County Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilford_County_Schools), [Winston-Salem/Forsyth County Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem/Forsyth_County_Schools), and [Cumberland County Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_County_Schools).[^cite_note-223] In total there are 2,425 public schools in the state, including over 200 [charter schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_school_\(North_Carolina\)).[^cite_note-224] North Carolina Schools were segregated until the *[Brown v. Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education)* trial and the release of the [Pearsall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearsall_Plan).

Previously the [SAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT) was the dominant university entrance examination students took<sup>\[Vague?]</sup>. In 2004 76% of NC high school students took the SAT. In 2012 state law changed which required 11th grade students to take the [ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_\(test\)). The SAT testing rate fell to 46% in 2019. Because students now can take that test for free, the ACT became the dominant university entrance examination. This also caused SAT average scores to rise, as in 1996 North Carolina was 48th nationally in SAT scores, but the profile of students taking the SAT has gotten smaller.[^cite_note-225] In 2024, North Carolina's high school graduation rate was 87%, though just 36% of students met the state's benchmarks for "college and career readiness."[^cite_note-226]

## Health

The residents of North Carolina [have a lower life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_life_expectancy) than the U.S. national average of life expectancy. According to the [Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Health_Metrics_and_Evaluation), in 2014, males in North Carolina lived an average of 75.4 years compared to the national average of 76.7 years. Females in North Carolina lived an average of 80.2 years compared to the national average of 81.5 years. Male life expectancy in North Carolina between 1980 and 2014 increased by an average of 6.9 years, slightly higher than the male national average of a 6.7-year increase. Life expectancy for females in North Carolina between 1980 and 2014 increased by 3.2 years, lower than the female national average of a 3.9-year increase.[^cite_note-234]

Using 2017–2019 data, the [Robert Wood Johnson Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_Johnson_Foundation) calculated that life expectancy for North Carolina counties ranged from 71.4 years for [Swain County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swain_County) to 82.3 years for [Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County,_North_Carolina). Life expectancy for the state was 78.1 years.[^cite_note-Robert_Wood_Johnson-235] The Foundation estimated that life expectancy for the United States as a whole in 2021 was 79.2 years.[^cite_note-236]

The government of North Carolina is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial. These consist of the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Council_of_State) (led by the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_North_Carolina)), the [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) legislature (called the [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_General_Assembly)), and the state court system (headed by the [North Carolina Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Supreme_Court)). The [state constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_North_Carolina) delineates the structure and function of the state government. Most municipalities in North Carolina operate under [council–manager governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government).[^cite_note-240]

North Carolina's party loyalties have undergone a series of important shifts in the last few years: While the 2010 midterms saw [Tarheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarheel) voters elect a bicameral [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Republican_Party) majority legislature for the first time in more than a century, North Carolina has also become a moderately Republican leaning Southern [swing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state) in presidential races Since Southern Democrat [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter)'s comfortable victory in the state [in 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), the state had consistently leaned Republican in presidential elections until Democrat [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) narrowly won the state [in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), though Republicans have carried the state in every presidential election since. In the 1990s, Democrat [Bill Clinton](3356.md) came within a point of winning the state [in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina) and also only narrowly lost the state [in 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina). In [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), Republican [George W. Bush](3414021.md) easily won the state by more than 13 points.

By [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), demographic shifts, population growth, and increased liberalization in densely populated areas such as the [Research Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle), [Charlotte](57447.md), [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro), [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem), [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina), and [Asheville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville), propelled Barack Obama to victory in North Carolina, the first Democrat to win the state since 1976. In [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), North Carolina was again considered a competitive swing state, with the Democrats even holding their [2012 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Democratic_National_Convention) in Charlotte. However, Republican [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney) ultimately eked out a two-point win in North Carolina, the only 2012 swing state Obama lost, and one of only two states (along with [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election_in_Indiana)) to flip from Obama in 2008 to the GOP in 2012. Furthermore, Republican Donald Trump carried the state in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina), and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Carolina).

In 2012, the state elected a Republican governor ([Pat McCrory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_McCrory)) and lieutenant governor ([Dan Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Forest)) for the first time in more than two decades, while also giving the Republicans veto-proof majorities in both the State House of Representatives and the State Senate. Several U.S. House of Representatives seats flipped control in 2012, with the Republicans holding nine seats to the Democrats' four. In the [2014 midterm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_North_Carolina) elections, Republican [David Rouzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rouzer) won the state's [7th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_7th_congressional_district) seat, increasing the congressional delegation party split to 10–3 in favor of the GOP, a split they retained in subsequent elections until 2020, when it narrowed to 8–5 in favor of the GOP. In 2016, despite Donald Trump winning the state, North Carolina voters elected Democrat Roy Cooper as governor, in part due to an unpopular law passed by former governor Pat McCrory known as [HB2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HB2). Cooper went on to win re-election in 2020, despite Donald Trump narrowly winning the state again against Joe Biden.

As a result of the 2020 census, North Carolina gained another seat in the [118th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/118th_United_States_Congress), for a total of 14.[^cite_note-241] After the 2024 elections, the state was represented by 10 Republicans and 4 Democrats.

In a 2020 study, North Carolina was ranked as the 23rd easiest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-242]

### Gerrymandering

See also: [Gerrymandering in the United States](42223515.md) and [North Carolina's congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_congressional_districts)

The state has been sued for racially gerrymandering the districts, which resulted in minority voting power being diluted in some areas, resulting in skewed representation. In 2000, the [U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_North_Carolina) ruled that the [12th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_12th_congressional_district) was an illegal racial gerrymander.[^cite_note-243] This was again appealed, now as *[Easley v. Cromartie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easley_v._Cromartie)*. The [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) reversed the Eastern District of North Carolina in 2001 and ruled that the 12th district boundaries were not racially based but was a [partisan gerrymander](12987.md). They said this was a political question that the courts should not rule upon.[^cite_note-\:1-244]

In 2015, federal courts again ordered redistricting.[^cite_note-stohr-245] Two lawsuits challenging the state congressional district map were led by "two dozen voters, the state Democratic Party, the state chapter of the League of Women Voters, and the interest group Common Cause".[^cite_note-stohr-245] They contend that the redistricting resulted in deliberate under-representation of a substantial portion of voters. This case reached the U.S. Supreme Court in March 2019, which also heard a related partisan gerrymandering case from Maryland.[^cite_note-stohr-245]

On February 4, 2022, the North Carolina Supreme Court struck down the congressional and state legislative district maps drawn by the GOP-controlled [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_General_Assembly) on terms of partisan gerrymandering in a 4–3 ruling.[^cite_note-246] Later that month, a panel of three former judges chosen by the Wake County Superior Court drew and approved a remedial congressional map after the new map proposed by the General Assembly was struck down by the North Carolina Supreme Court. Though, the state legislative maps proposed by the General Assembly were allowed to be used. The General Assembly would then redraw all three maps for the [2024 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_elections) in the state.[^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248]

On April 28, 2023, the North Carolina Supreme Court—after Republicans gained a majority in the court following the [2022 judicial elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_North_Carolina_judicial_elections)—overturned the same ruling in a 5–2 decision, which cleared the way for gerrymandering in the next redistricting cycle.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250] New maps were approved by the General Assembly on October 25, 2023. All three new maps heavily favor the GOP, with allegations of racial bias made against the maps as well.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

In November 2023, a lawsuit was filed against the [North Carolina Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Senate) district map—specifically the [1st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_1st_Senate_district) and [2nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_2nd_Senate_district) Senate districts—in the Eastern District of North Carolina, arguing the map violates Section 2 of the [Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965).[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256] In December 2023, two lawsuits were filed in the [Middle District of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_District_of_North_Carolina), with the first challenging the [1st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_1st_congressional_district), [6th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_6th_congressional_district), [12th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_12th_congressional_district), and [14th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_14th_congressional_district) congressional districts in the map,[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] and the second, challenging multiple specific districts in all three maps, as racial gerrymanders.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261] Both lawsuits were consolidated together in March 2024.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263]

On January 26, 2024—regarding the lawsuit currently in the Eastern District of North Carolina—a preliminary injunction to block the current North Carolina Senate district map was denied, citing the [*Purcell* principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purcell_principle).[^cite_note-264] The ruling was appealed to the [U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Fourth_Circuit), which upheld the lower court's decision on March 28, 2024. The lawsuit will continue in the district court, but no ruling will be made until after the 2024 elections.[^cite_note-265]

North Carolina is home to four [major league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) sports franchises: the [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Panthers) of the [National Football League](21211.md), the [Charlotte Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md), and [Charlotte FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_FC) of [Major League Soccer](71802.md) are based in Charlotte, while the Raleigh-based [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) play in the [National Hockey League](21809.md). The Panthers and Hurricanes are the only two major professional sports teams that have the same geographical designation while playing in different metropolitan areas. The Hurricanes are the only major professional team from North Carolina to have won a league championship, having captured the Stanley Cup in [2006](5444393.md) and [2026](80157147.md). North Carolina is also home to two other top-level professional teams—the Charlotte Hounds of [Major League Lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Lacrosse) and the [North Carolina Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Courage) of the [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League).

While North Carolina has no [Major League Baseball](38776.md) team, it does have numerous [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball) teams, with the highest level of play coming from the [Triple-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-A_\(baseball\)) [Charlotte Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Knights) and [Durham Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Bulls). Additionally, North Carolina has minor league teams in other team sports including soccer and [ice hockey](14790.md), most notably [North Carolina FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_FC) and the [Charlotte Checkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Checkers), both of which play in the second tier of their respective sports.

In addition to professional team sports, North Carolina has a strong affiliation with [NASCAR](21114.md) and stock-car racing, with [Charlotte Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Motor_Speedway) in [Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_North_Carolina) hosting races every year. Charlotte also hosts the [NASCAR Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Hall_of_Fame), while Concord is the home of several top-flight racing teams, including [Hendrick Motorsports](2089777.md), [RFK Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFK_Racing), and [Haas Factory Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_Factory_Team). Numerous other tracks around North Carolina host races from other racing circuits as well.

Golf is a popular summertime leisure activity, and North Carolina has hosted several important professional golf tournaments. [Pinehurst Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinehurst_Resort) in [Pinehurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinehurst,_North_Carolina) has hosted a [PGA Championship](224616.md), [Ryder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryder_Cup), two [U.S. Opens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_\(golf\)), and one [U.S. Women's Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Women's_Open). The [Wells Fargo Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo_Championship) is a regular stop on the [PGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour) and is held at [Quail Hollow Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quail_Hollow_Club) in Charlotte, and Quail Hollow has also played host to the PGA Championship. The [Wyndham Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyndham_Championship) is played annually in Greensboro at [Sedgefield Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedgefield_Country_Club).

[College sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_athletics_in_the_United_States) are also popular in North Carolina, with 18 schools competing at the [Division I](669931.md) level. The [Atlantic Coast Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Conference) (ACC) is headquartered in [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro), and both the [ACC Football Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACC_Football_Championship_Game) (Charlotte) and the [ACC men's basketball tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACC_men's_basketball_tournament) (Greensboro) were most recently held in North Carolina. Additionally, the city of [Charlotte](57447.md) is home to the [National Junior College Athletics Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Junior_College_Athletic_Association)'s (NJCAA) headquarters.[^cite_note-266] [College basketball](768856.md) is very popular in North Carolina, buoyed by the [Tobacco Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_Road_\(rivalry\)) rivalries between ACC members [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_men's_basketball), [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Blue_Devils_men's_basketball), [North Carolina State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NC_State_Wolfpack_men's_basketball), and [Wake Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_Demon_Deacons_men's_basketball). The [ACC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACC_Championship_Game) and the [Duke's Mayo Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke's_Mayo_Bowl) are held annually in Charlotte's [Bank of America Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_America_Stadium), featuring teams from the ACC and the [Southeastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Conference). Additionally, the state has hosted the NCAA Men's Basketball [Final Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Four_\(college_basketball\)) on two occasions, in Greensboro in 1974 and in Charlotte in 1994.
