The **Malian Air Force** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Armée de l'air du Mali*) is the primary aerial warfare branch of [Malian Armed Forces](19135.md). It was established in 1961. In the following years, the Malian Air Force received significant support from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), which provided both equipment and training to the force.[^cite_note-3] Since 2022, it has received support from Russia.

## History

The Malian Air Force ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Armée de l'air du Mali*) was founded in 1961 with French-supplied military aid. This included [MH.1521 Broussard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH.1521_Broussard) utility monoplane followed by two [C-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47_Skytrain) transports. In 1962, the Soviet Union would then supply the Malian Air Force with four [Antonov AN-2 Colt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-2) biplane transports and four [Mi-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-4) light helicopters.[^cite_note-WorldAircraftInformationFiles-4]

In the mid-1960s, the Soviets would further deliver five [MiG-17F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-17) fighters and a single [MiG-15UTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-15) fighter trainer to equip a squadron based at [Bamako–Sénou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako%E2%80%93S%C3%A9nou_International_Airport) initially with Soviet pilots. Two [Ilyushin Il-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-14) transports and a [Mil Mi-8](957233.md) helicopter were delivered in 1971 followed by two [Antonov An-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-24) transports.

In 1974, 12 MiG-21Bis were obtained from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), with a pair of two-seat MiG-21UMs to follow a couple of years later. These initial Fishbeds served alongside the four remaining [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17)Fs and saw combat on two occasions during the [Agacher Strip War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agacher_Strip_War) in 1974 against [Upper Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Upper_Volta), and again in 1985 with the same country, now renamed [Burkina Faso](3470.md). In 2005, another three MiG-21MFs were delivered from the [Czech Republic](5321.md), reinforcing the surviving jets. By 2010, the Fishbeds were only flown on ceremonial occasions. By January 2012, only one MiG-21MF and one MiG-21UM remained operational until they were grounded for lack of spare parts, ammunition, and pilots a few months later. In January 2013, the [Nigerian Air Force](538869.md) sent a technical team to [ Bamako–Sénou International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako%E2%80%93S%C3%A9nou_International_Airport), with the aim of refurbishing the MiG-21s, but the project was abandoned. Other jets withdrawn from service were six [L-29 Delfins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-29_Delf%C3%ADn), which were used for training.[^cite_note-Mali's_Air_Force-5]

In June 2015 the Malian government ordered [Super Tucano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Tucano) light attack aircraft from the Brazilian company [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer).[^cite_note-6] Four were paid for and were delivered in 2018.[^cite_note-7] One of these crashed in [Sévaré](8445305.md) two years later, killing both pilots.[^cite_note-8]

In December 2020, the [Malian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malian_government) ordered 4 [Mi-171](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-171) helicopters. They were delivered by [Russia](25391.md) on 30 September 2021.[^cite_note-devdiscourse-9] Russia has assisted Mali with reviving the Malian Air Force, which as of 2023 has received at least four Aero L-39 jet trainers, one [Mi-8T Hip](957233.md) transport helicopter and two Mi-8MTs, two Mi-24 attack helicopters, two Sukhoi Su-25 ground attack jets, and a single Airbus C-295.[^cite_note-Aerospace-10]

Both of the Su-25s were lost.[^cite_note-Aerospace-10] In September 2023, the Malian government has lost their last [Su-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-25) due to a crash caused by a missile launched by [Azawad rebels.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) However the pilot safely ejected.[^cite_note-military.africa-11] Additional losses during the [Mali War](36856978.md) include three L-39s, multiple Mi-24s and Mi-8s, and a Su-24. Some of the helicopters were operated by Russia's [Wagner Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) or [Africa Corps](78298390.md).[^cite_note-Aerospace-10]
